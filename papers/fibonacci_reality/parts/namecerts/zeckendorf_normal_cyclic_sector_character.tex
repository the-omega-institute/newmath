\subsection{Cyclic Zeckendorf-normal sectors and their rotation character}
\label{sec:zeckendorf-normal-cyclic-sector-character}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
Fix a positive integer $n$. The basic finite carrier is the subset $C_n \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ consisting of all binary words $w=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ for which no two cyclically adjacent coordinates are both equal to $1$. The cyclic adjacency relation contains the ordinary neighboring pairs $(x_i,x_{i+1})$ for $1 \leq i<n$ and also the boundary pair $(x_n,x_1)$. Thus the predicate defining membership in $C_n$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
w\in C_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
& w=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in \{0,1\}^n, \\
& \text{and there is no cyclic adjacent pair }(x_i,x_j)\text{ with }x_i=x_j=1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, $w\in C_n$ exactly when $x_i x_{i+1}=0$ for every $1\leq i<n$ and $x_nx_1=0$. This is the cyclic Zeckendorf-normal carrier of window length $n$.

\paragraph{Lucas count.}
Let the finite sequence $L_n$ be determined by $L_0=2$, $L_1=1$, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{n+2}=L_{n+1}+L_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
The enumerative claim for the carrier is the exact finite identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
|C_n|=L_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first six positive window lengths this gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
|C_1|&=L_1=1, \\
|C_2|&=L_2=3, \\
|C_3|&=L_3=4, \\
|C_4|&=L_4=7, \\
|C_5|&=L_5=11, \\
|C_6|&=L_6=18.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are finite cardinalities of explicitly described subsets of $\{0,1\}^n$. No limiting argument, continuum parameter, or external interpretation is part of the count.

\paragraph{Rotation successor.}
Define the cyclic rotation map $\rho:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n$ by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1},x_n)=(x_n,x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}).
\end{aligned}
$$
This map preserves the cyclic adjacency predicate because every adjacent cyclic pair is sent to another adjacent cyclic pair. Hence $\rho(C_n)=C_n$, so $\rho$ restricts to a permutation of the finite set $C_n$. Applying the rotation $n$ times returns every coordinate to its original position, and therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho^n(w)=w
\end{aligned}
$$
for every $w\in C_n$.

\paragraph{Deterministic rotation kernel.}
The same rotation can be recorded as a finite transition matrix on $C_n$. Define $K_n:C_n\times C_n\to\{0,1\}$ by
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_n(w,u)=
\begin{cases}
1, & u=\rho(w),\\
0, & u\ne\rho(w).
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
Each row of $K_n$ has exactly one entry equal to $1$, and each column has exactly one entry equal to $1$, because $\rho$ is a permutation of $C_n$. Thus $K_n$ is a finite permutation matrix encoding the forced rotation step on the admissible carrier. The identity $\rho^n(w)=w$ says that $K_n^n$ is the identity matrix on the finite state set $C_n$.

\paragraph{Cyclic sectors.}
Partition $C_n$ into orbits under repeated application of $\rho$. For $w\in C_n$, its cyclic sector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
[w]_{\rho}=\{\rho^k(w):0\leq k<n\}\subseteq C_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
The collection of these sets is a partition of $C_n$: every admissible word belongs to its own orbit, two orbits are either identical or disjoint, and all orbit elements remain admissible because rotation preserves the defining predicate. These sectors are the cyclic classes of the finite carrier.

\paragraph{Sector character.}
A sector character is any function $\chi_n:C_n\to A$ into a set $A$ that is constant on the rotation orbits. Its defining condition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi_n(\rho(w))=\chi_n(w)
\end{aligned}
$$
for every $w\in C_n$. Since every element of a sector has the form $\rho^k(w)$ for some $k$, this condition implies that $\chi_n$ has one value on the whole sector $[w]_{\rho}$. Conversely, assigning one value to each cyclic sector defines such a function on $C_n$. The character is therefore an invariant of the orbit partition rather than of a chosen coordinate origin.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
The finite witnesses are explicit. The carrier witness is the decidable predicate on $\{0,1\}^n$ checking all cyclic adjacent pairs, including $(x_n,x_1)$. The counting witness is the equality $|C_n|=L_n$ together with the recurrence $L_{n+2}=L_{n+1}+L_n$ and the displayed initial values. The rotation witness is the coordinate shift $\rho$, whose preservation proof is the observation that cyclic adjacent pairs are mapped to cyclic adjacent pairs. The sector witness is the orbit partition generated by $\rho$. The character witness is the equality $\chi_n(\rho(w))=\chi_n(w)$, which makes $\chi_n$ constant on each orbit.

\paragraph{Boundary of the construction.}
The construction asserts only finite combinatorial facts about cyclically admissible binary words, their Lucas cardinalities, their rotation permutation, and functions constant on rotation sectors. It does not assert any physical-constant identification, global flow law, external dependency, metrological comparison, or interpretation beyond this finite construction unless a separate certificate supplies additional assumptions and data.
