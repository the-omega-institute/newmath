\subsection{A width-six forced-window observation}
\label{sec:window6-three-rigidity-window-observation}

\paragraph{Carrier and predicate.}
The finite carrier is the set of all binary words of length six,
$\{0,1\}^6$.  An element is written as
$w=(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4,w_5,w_6)$ with each coordinate in $\{0,1\}$.  The admissibility predicate is the local forced-window rule
that no adjacent pair is simultaneously equal to one.  Thus
$w$ is admissible exactly when there is no index $i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ such that $w_i=w_{i+1}=1$.  The admissible class is
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_6=\{w\in\{0,1\}^6 : \text{there is no } i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}\text{ with } w_i=w_{i+1}=1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a purely finite definition: the rule refers only to the six displayed coordinates and the five adjacent coordinate pairs inside the word.

\paragraph{Exact enumeration.}
The full binary cube has one independent binary choice in each of the six coordinates, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\{0,1\}^6|=2^6=64.
\end{aligned}
$$
The admissible words are exactly the words selected by the no-adjacent-ones predicate.  They are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&000000,\ 000001,\ 000010,\ 000100,\ 000101,\ 001000,\ 001001,\ 001010,\ 010000,\ 010001,\ 010010,\\
&010100,\ 010101,\ 100000,\ 100001,\ 100010,\ 100100,\ 100101,\ 101000,\ 101001,\ 101010.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each displayed word has length six and avoids the factor $11$.  Conversely, scanning the five adjacent positions of any length-six binary word either finds such a factor or places the word in the displayed list.  Therefore the admissible class has cardinality
$$
\begin{aligned}
|X_6|=21.
\end{aligned}
$$
If $F_0=0$, $F_1=1$, and $F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n$, then the initial values give
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_2&=1,\quad F_3=2,\quad F_4=3,\quad F_5=5,\\
F_6&=8,\quad F_7=13,\quad F_8=21,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the same count may be recorded as $|X_6|=21=F_8$.

\paragraph{Partition of the cube.}
The predicate partitions $\{0,1\}^6$ into two disjoint finite classes: the admissible class $X_6$ and its complement, consisting of all words that contain at least one adjacent pair $11$.  The exact sizes are
$$
\begin{aligned}
|X_6|&=21,\\
|\{0,1\}^6\setminus X_6|&=64-21=43.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the complete partition is admissible $21$ and non-admissible $43$.  The identity $64-21=43$ is the whole complement count; no limiting argument, density statement, or asymptotic estimate is involved.

\paragraph{Finite audit map and maximum-fiber relation.}
Let the width-six audit map be the finite map whose fibers are being counted in this window record, and let $c_{\max}$ denote its maximum fiber multiplicity.  In first-principles finite terms, if the audit map is written as $a$ on its finite domain, then a fiber over a value $y$ is the finite set $\{w : a(w)=y\}$, and $c_{\max}$ is the largest cardinality of such a fiber.  The supplied finite bound for this record is
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{\max}<21.
\end{aligned}
$$
Combining this maximum-fiber bound with the exact admissible count and the full cube count gives the strict three-term separation
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{\max}<|X_6|=21<2^6=64.
\end{aligned}
$$
The right inequality is witnessed by the explicit counts $21$ and $64$.  The left inequality is witnessed by the stated maximum-fiber bound for the width-six audit map.  The middle term is witnessed by the full enumeration of the no-adjacent-ones words above.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
A finite checker can recover the admissible witness set by scanning each of the $64$ words in $\{0,1\}^6$ and rejecting exactly those with some adjacent pair equal to $11$.  The accepted words are precisely the $21$ displayed elements of $X_6$.  A separate finite check of the audit map fibers supplies the inequality $c_{\max}<21$.  These two finite checks are independent in form: one counts words selected by a local adjacency predicate, while the other bounds the largest fiber of the audit map.  Their conjunction is exactly the asserted separation.

\paragraph{Boundary of the assertion.}
The construction proves only the finite statement for the width-six binary window: an admissible class of size $21$ lies strictly between the maximum audit-map fiber multiplicity and the full cube of size $64$.  No physical-constant identification, global flow law, external dependency, metrological comparison, continuum limit, spectral interpretation, edge-flux statement, or broader physical reading follows from this finite construction unless an additional certificate supplies that further content.
