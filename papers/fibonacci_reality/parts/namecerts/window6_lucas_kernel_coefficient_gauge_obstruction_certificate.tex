\subsection{A six-window coefficient gauge obstruction from the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$}
\label{sec:window6-lucas-kernel-coefficient-gauge-obstruction-certificate}

\paragraph{Carrier.}
Let
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite carrier is the set $\{0,1\}^6$ of all binary words $w=(w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4,w_5)$ of length six.  It has exactly $2^6=64$ elements.  The parity map is the function $p:\{0,1\}^6\to\{0,1\}$ defined by
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(w)&\equiv w_0+w_1+w_2+w_3+w_4+w_5 \pmod 2.
\end{aligned}
$$
This gives a two-part partition
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{0,1\}^6 &= E\sqcup O,\\
E&=\{w\in\{0,1\}^6:p(w)=0\},\\
O&=\{w\in\{0,1\}^6:p(w)=1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Flipping the first coordinate is a bijection from $E$ to $O$, hence $|E|=|O|=32$.

\paragraph{Finite matrix data.}
The certificate uses only the integer powers of $M$ through the sixth forced window.  Direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^0&=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},&
M^1&=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},&
M^2&=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\\
M^3&=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix},&
M^4&=\begin{pmatrix}5&3\\3&2\end{pmatrix},&
M^5&=\begin{pmatrix}8&5\\5&3\end{pmatrix},\\
M^6&=\begin{pmatrix}13&8\\8&5\end{pmatrix}.&&
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding trace row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{tr}(M^0),\operatorname{tr}(M^1),\operatorname{tr}(M^2),\operatorname{tr}(M^3),\operatorname{tr}(M^4),\operatorname{tr}(M^5),\operatorname{tr}(M^6))
=(2,1,3,4,7,11,18).
\end{aligned}
$$
It satisfies the finite recurrence identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(M^{n+2})=\operatorname{tr}(M^{n+1})+\operatorname{tr}(M^n)
\end{aligned}
$$
for $0\leq n\leq 4$, as checked by the five equalities $3=1+2$, $4=3+1$, $7=4+3$, $11=7+4$, and $18=11+7$.

\paragraph{Coefficient gauge.}
For $w\in\{0,1\}^6$, define its coefficient vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
c(w)=(c_0(w),c_1(w),c_2(w),c_3(w),c_4(w),c_5(w),c_6(w))
\end{aligned}
$$
by
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_0(w)&=1,\\
c_i(w)&=w_{i-1}\quad \text{for }1\leq i\leq 6.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated trace-weight is the integer
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{6}c_i(w)\operatorname{tr}(M^i)
=2+w_0+3w_1+4w_2+7w_3+11w_4+18w_5.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite readout rule from the carrier into integer data.  No limiting process, probability model, or analytic continuation is involved.

\paragraph{Forced-window relation.}
Define a relation $R\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\times\mathbb{Z}$ by
$$
\begin{aligned}
(w,t)\in R \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad t=T(w).
\end{aligned}
$$
Every word has exactly one related integer, so $R$ is the graph of $T$ and contains exactly $64$ ordered pairs.  The smallest value is $T(0,0,0,0,0,0)=2$, and the largest value is $T(1,1,1,1,1,1)=46$.  The parity of the trace-weight is
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(w)&\equiv w_0+w_1+w_4 \pmod 2,
\end{aligned}
$$
because $2$, $4$, and $18$ are even while $1$, $3$, $7$, and $11$ are odd.

\paragraph{Kernel relation.}
The coefficient-gauge kernel is the finite equivalence relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
K=\{(u,v)\in\{0,1\}^6\times\{0,1\}^6:T(u)=T(v)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
It is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive because equality of integers has those three properties.  A collision is present: for
$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=(1,1,0,0,0,0),\\
v&=(0,0,1,0,0,0),
\end{aligned}
$$
one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(u)&=2+1+3=6,\\
T(v)&=2+4=6.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $u\neq v$ but $(u,v)\in K$.

\paragraph{Obstruction.}
The obstruction is the failure of the trace-weight gauge to be injective on the six-window carrier.  If an injective finite naming by this gauge existed, then distinct words would have distinct values of $T$.  The displayed pair $u=(1,1,0,0,0,0)$ and $v=(0,0,1,0,0,0)$ contradicts that condition.  The exact coefficient identity behind the obstruction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(M^1)+\operatorname{tr}(M^2)=\operatorname{tr}(M^3),
\end{aligned}
$$
namely $1+3=4$.  In the gauge notation this says that the two different coefficient choices
$$
\begin{aligned}
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5,c_6)&=(1,1,1,0,0,0,0),\\
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5,c_6)&=(1,0,0,1,0,0,0)
\end{aligned}
$$
produce the same finite trace-weight.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
Given the data of the construction, a witness can be extracted without search over an infinite domain.  Compute the trace row through $M^6$, read the equality $1+3=4$ in positions $1$, $2$, and $3$, and place the two sides of that equality into the six available coefficient slots while keeping the constant coefficient $c_0=1$ fixed.  This yields the two words $u=(1,1,0,0,0,0)$ and $v=(0,0,1,0,0,0)$.  The verification then consists only of the two integer evaluations $T(u)=6$ and $T(v)=6$ together with the coordinate comparison $u\neq v$.

The construction proves a finite obstruction internal to the stated carrier, relation, matrix powers, and integer trace gauge.  It does not identify any physical constant, assert a global evolution law, import an outside dependency, compare against measurements, or attach an interpretation beyond this finite certificate unless a separate certificate supplies the missing assumptions.
