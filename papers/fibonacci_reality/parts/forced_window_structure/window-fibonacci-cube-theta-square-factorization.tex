\subsection{Finite forced-window square profile for Fibonacci cubes}
\label{sec:window-fibonacci-cube-theta-square-factorization}

\paragraph{Carrier and admissible words.}
Fix an integer $m \geq 1$. The finite vertex set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_m=\{w\in \{0,1\}^m : \text{there is no }k\text{ with }1\leq k<m\text{ and }w_k=w_{k+1}=1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The graph $\Gamma_m$ has vertex set $X_m$. Two vertices $u,v\in X_m$ are joined by an edge exactly when they differ in one coordinate. Thus the edge relation is the restriction of the Hamming-distance-one relation on $\{0,1\}^m$ to the admissible set $X_m$. The number of admissible words is the Fibonacci count
$$
\begin{aligned}
|X_m|=F_{m+2},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $F_1=1$, $F_2=1$, and $F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n$. In particular $|X_6|=F_8=21$.

\paragraph{Coordinate edge classes.}
For each coordinate $i$ with $1\leq i\leq m$, let $\Theta_i$ be the set of all graph edges whose two endpoints differ exactly in coordinate $i$. Equivalently, $\Theta_i$ is the finite relation consisting of unordered pairs $\{u,v\}$ with $u,v\in X_m$, $u_k=v_k$ for every $k\neq i$, and $u_i\neq v_i$. These classes partition the edge set of $\Gamma_m$ because every Hamming-distance-one edge has a unique flipped coordinate.

It is useful to record the same data as coordinate kernels. Let $K_i$ be the $0$-$1$ matrix indexed by $X_m\times X_m$ and defined by
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_i(u,v)=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u,v\in X_m,\ u_k=v_k\text{ for }k\neq i,\text{ and }u_i\neq v_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then $K_i$ is symmetric, has zero diagonal, and records precisely the edges in $\Theta_i$. No limiting object or infinite graph is used here; all relations are finite.

\paragraph{Square-counting predicate.}
For $1\leq i<j\leq m$, define $S_m(i,j)$ to be the number of base words $w\in X_m$ satisfying $w_i=0$, $w_j=0$, and such that the four words
$$
\begin{aligned}
w,
\qquad w+e_i,
\qquad w+e_j,
\qquad w+e_i+e_j
\end{aligned}
$$
all lie in $X_m$. Here $e_i$ denotes the word with a single $1$ in coordinate $i$ and $0$ elsewhere, and addition means flipping the displayed zero coordinate from $0$ to $1$. The four admissible words then form the coordinate square obtained by toggling $i$ and $j$. Since the two diagonal pairs differ in two coordinates, the square has no diagonal Hamming-distance-one edge; hence it is an induced square inside $\Gamma_m$.

Equivalently, the finite witness predicate for a base word is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{i,j}(w)=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
w_i=w_j=0\text{ and }w,w+e_i,w+e_j,w+e_i+e_j\in X_m,
\end{aligned}
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_m(i,j)=\sum_{w\in X_m} Q_{i,j}(w).
\end{aligned}
$$
This definition extracts one witness for each induced square: the unique vertex of that square whose $i$-th and $j$-th coordinates are both $0$.

\paragraph{Adjacent coordinates.}
If $j=i+1$, then a simultaneous flip of the two zero coordinates would produce a word with adjacent $1$ entries in positions $i$ and $i+1$. Therefore the fourth corner $w+e_i+e_{i+1}$ is not in $X_m$. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_m(i,i+1)=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a purely finite obstruction coming from the defining no-adjacent-$1$ predicate.

\paragraph{Separated coordinates and factorization.}
Assume now that $j>i+1$. A base word contributing to $S_m(i,j)$ must have zeros at $i$ and $j$, and the flips force the neighbors of $i$ and $j$ to be zero whenever those neighbors exist. Thus the remaining free positions split into three independent admissible blocks: a left block before the forced zero zone around $i$, a middle block between the forced zero zones around $i$ and $j$, and a right block after the forced zero zone around $j$. The no-adjacent-$1$ condition does not couple these three blocks because the separating coordinates are fixed to zero.

Counting binary strings with no adjacent $1$ entries in each block gives the product identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_m(i,j)=F_i\,F_{j-i-1}\,F_{m-j+1}\qquad (j>i+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Together with the adjacent-coordinate vanishing, this gives the complete coordinate-resolved induced-square profile:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_m(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
0, & j=i+1,\\
F_i\,F_{j-i-1}\,F_{m-j+1}, & j>i+1.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality has also been checked by exhaustive enumeration for every $m=2,3,4,5,6,7,8$ and every coordinate pair $1\leq i<j\leq m$.

\paragraph{The six-window profile.}
For $m=6$, the graph has $21$ vertices. The nonzero coordinate-pair square counts are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&S_6(1,3)=3,
&&S_6(1,4)=2,
&&S_6(1,5)=2,
&&S_6(1,6)=3,\\
&S_6(2,4)=2,
&&S_6(2,5)=1,
&&S_6(2,6)=2,\\
&S_6(3,5)=2,
&&S_6(3,6)=2,
&&S_6(4,6)=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
All adjacent-coordinate pairs have count zero. Summing the displayed nonzero values gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
3+2+2+3+2+1+2+2+2+3=22,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the coordinate-resolved induced-square total for $\Gamma_6$ is $22$.

\paragraph{Local scope of the construction.}
The construction above proves only a finite combinatorial statement about admissible binary words, coordinate-flip edge classes, and induced squares in the graph $\Gamma_m$. No physical-constant identification, no global flow law, no external dependency, no metrological comparison, and no interpretation beyond this finite construction follows from the square profile alone. Any later numerical comparison would require a separate certificate specifying the comparison rule and the data to which it is applied.
