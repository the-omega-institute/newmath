\subsection{Forced-window necklace determinant bridge}
\label{sec:window6-clock-gauge-invariant-necklace-spectral-determinant-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The construction starts from the weighted two-state golden transfer matrix
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The variable $x$ records period and the variable $y$ records the weight carried by the upper-right transition. The spectral determinant of this carrier is computed directly from the $2\times 2$ matrix:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(I-xT(y))=1-x-yx^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
All later objects in this subsection are finite formal consequences of this matrix identity inside the $x$-adic envelope truncated through degree $10$, together with the divisor-sector data attached to $10$. No ambient infinite shift, physical model, or external interpretation is used as an input.

\paragraph{Primitive necklace polynomials.}
For each positive integer $d$, define the primitive necklace polynomial by the Mobius trace formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_d(y)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(r)\operatorname{tr}\left(T(y^r)^{d/r}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
This formula separates the trace power sums of the matrix into primitive period contributions. Equivalently, for every $g$ with $1\leq g\leq 10$, the trace power sum decomposes as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(T(y)^g)=\sum_{d\mid g} d\,P_d\left(y^{g/d}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite certificate only needs the divisor sector $d\mid 10$. In that sector the primitive coefficients are
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1(y)&=1,\\
P_2(y)&=y,\\
P_5(y)&=y+y^2,\\
P_{10}(y)&=y+3y^2+5y^3+2y^4.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are exactly the coefficients used by the trace-cycle-index kernel and by the finite divisor-sector Euler product below.

\paragraph{Euler product inside the finite envelope.}
The formal zeta expression associated to the primitive data is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z(x,y)=\prod_d\prod_s\left(1-x^d y^s\right)^{-[y^s]P_d(y)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The logarithm of the determinant has the finite Newton expansion
$$
\begin{aligned}
-\log\det(I-xT(y))=\sum_{g=1}^{10}\operatorname{tr}(T(y)^g)\frac{x^g}{g}
\end{aligned}
$$
through degree $10$. Substituting the primitive decomposition of the traces into this expansion gives the same logarithm as the Euler product through $x^{10}$. Hence, inside the stated finite formal envelope,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log Z(x,y)&=-\log\det(I-xT(y)),\\
Z(x,y)&=\frac{1}{\det(I-xT(y))}=\frac{1}{1-x-yx^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an exact polynomial and formal power-series identity at the declared truncation level, not a numerical fit.

\paragraph{The $10$-sector forced window.}
The divisor-sector Euler factor for $10$ is obtained by keeping only the primitive factors with $d\mid 10$ and using the coefficients displayed above. It is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-x^2y)(1-x^5y)(1-x^5y^2)(1-x^{10}y)(1-x^{10}y^2)^3(1-x^{10}y^3)^5(1-x^{10}y^4)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lifted exponent table attached to this sector is the finite set
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{&(1,0)\mapsto 1,\ (2,5)\mapsto 1,\ (5,2)\mapsto 1,\ (5,4)\mapsto 1,\\
&(10,1)\mapsto 1,\ (10,2)\mapsto 3,\ (10,3)\mapsto 5,\ (10,4)\mapsto 2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The table is a record of the same primitive coefficients, lifted into the forced-window sector. It is not a second source of arithmetic data; it is the finite row form of the divisor-sector Euler factors.

\paragraph{Specialization at unit weight.}
At $y=1$, the carrier becomes the ordinary two-state golden transfer matrix and the determinant specializes to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(I-xT(1))=1-x-x^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently the reciprocal determinant has the finite Fibonacci expansion through degree $10$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{1-x-x^2}=\sum_{g\geq 0}F_{g+1}x^g
\end{aligned}
$$
when read only up to $x^{10}$. The logarithmic power sums specialize to the Lucas sums through the same degree:
$$
\begin{aligned}
-\log(1-x-x^2)=\sum_{g=1}^{10}L_g\frac{x^g}{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the unit-weight check agrees with both the reciprocal determinant and the Newton power-sum side of the construction.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
A finite native computation records the determinant calculation, the Newton necklace power sums, the $10$-sector Euler exponents, and the unit-weight Lucas power sums. The extraction is purely algebraic: it evaluates the $2\times 2$ determinant, computes trace powers through period $10$, applies the Mobius primitive formula, and reads off Euler-factor exponents from the resulting primitive polynomials. Every displayed coefficient is therefore recoverable from the stated matrix carrier and the divisor-sector restriction.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
