\subsection{Cyclic Zeckendorf-Normal Sector Character}
\label{sec:zeckendorf-normal-cyclic-sector-character}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
Fix a positive integer $n$. The carrier is the finite set $C_n$ of binary words $w=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ in $\{0,1\}^n$ satisfying one cyclic exclusion rule: no adjacent pair is allowed to have two entries equal to $1$, and the boundary pair $(x_n,x_1)$ is counted as adjacent. Thus $w\in C_n$ exactly when every consecutive cyclic pair avoids $(1,1)$. In explicit predicate form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
w\in C_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
& w=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\{0,1\}^n,\\
& \text{for every } i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\},\ (x_i,x_{i+1})\ne(1,1),\\
& \text{and } (x_n,x_1)\ne(1,1).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the cyclic Zeckendorf-normal carrier of window length $n$. The construction is finite at every stage: the ambient set has $2^n$ words, and $C_n$ is obtained by applying the stated adjacency predicate to those words.

\paragraph{Lucas count.}
Let $L_0=2$, let $L_1=1$, and for all $n\geq 0$ set $L_{n+2}=L_{n+1}+L_n$. The finite counting assertion attached to the carrier is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|C_n|=L_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first windows this gives the exact values
$$
\begin{aligned}
|C_1|&=L_1=1,\\
|C_2|&=L_2=3,\\
|C_3|&=L_3=4,\\
|C_4|&=L_4=7,\\
|C_5|&=L_5=11,\\
|C_6|&=L_6=18.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are asserted only as the finite cardinalities of the cyclic admissibility predicate above. The recurrence is part of the same finite combinatorial description and does not add any analytic or physical interpretation.

\paragraph{Rotation map and finite kernel.}
Define the rotation successor $\rho:C_n\to C_n$ by cyclically shifting coordinates one step, for example
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)=(x_n,x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The chosen direction is immaterial for the orbit partition as long as it is fixed. The preservation witness is local: $\rho$ sends each cyclic adjacent pair to another cyclic adjacent pair, so the forbidden pair $(1,1)$ cannot appear after rotation if it was absent before rotation. Hence $\rho$ permutes $C_n$. Repeating the step $n$ times returns every coordinate to its starting position, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho^n(w)=w
\end{aligned}
$$
for every $w\in C_n$.

The same data may be expressed as a finite transition kernel on the carrier. For $u,v\in C_n$, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_n(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
1, & v=\rho(u),\\
0, & v\ne\rho(u).
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $\rho$ is a permutation of $C_n$, each row and each column of $K_n$ has exactly one entry equal to $1$. Thus $K_n$ is the permutation matrix of the cyclic rotation step on the admissible finite carrier.

\paragraph{Sector partition.}
The cyclic sectors are the orbits of $C_n$ under repeated application of $\rho$. For a word $w\in C_n$, its sector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{w,\rho(w),\rho^2(w),\ldots,\rho^{n-1}(w)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The identity $\rho^n(w)=w$ ensures that this displayed set is closed under the rotation step. The family of all such orbit sets partitions $C_n$: every admissible word belongs to its own orbit, and two orbits are either identical or disjoint because $\rho$ is a permutation.

\paragraph{Sector character.}
A sector character is a finite label assigned to admissible words with the defining invariance condition that it is constant along each cyclic sector. Written as a function $\chi:C_n\to S$ into some finite label set $S$, the condition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(\rho(w))=\chi(w)
\end{aligned}
$$
for every $w\in C_n$. Since every sector is generated by iterating $\rho$, this one-step equality implies that all words in the same sector have the same label. Conversely, assigning one label to each orbit determines such a character. The witness for invariance is therefore the orbit construction itself: the rotation successor preserves admissibility, the finite iteration closes after $n$ steps, and the character is defined to be constant on the resulting orbit blocks.
