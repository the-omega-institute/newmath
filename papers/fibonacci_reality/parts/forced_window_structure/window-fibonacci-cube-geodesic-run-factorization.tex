\subsection{Forced-window geodesic counts in Fibonacci cubes}
\label{sec:window-fibonacci-cube-geodesic-run-factorization}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
For each integer $m$ with $2 \leq m \leq 7$, let
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_m=\{w\in \{0,1\}^m : \text{there is no } i \text{ with } 1\leq i<m \text{ such that } w_i=w_{i+1}=1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The graph $\Gamma_m$ has vertex set $X_m$. Two vertices $x,y\in X_m$ are adjacent exactly when they differ in one coordinate. Equivalently, the edge relation is the symmetric finite relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_m=\{(x,y)\in X_m\times X_m : \#\{i: x_i\neq y_i\}=1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated adjacency matrix is the $X_m\times X_m$ matrix $M_m$ with
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_m(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
1, & (x,y)\in E_m,\\
0, & (x,y)\notin E_m.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite enumeration used here gives $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ for $m=2,3,4,5,6,7$. Thus the ordered endpoint set checked in this window is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigcup_{m=2}^7 \{m\}\times X_m\times X_m,
\end{aligned}
$$
and its cardinality is $1864$.

\paragraph{Difference runs and local factors.}
For endpoints $u,v\in X_m$, define the differing-coordinate set
$$
\begin{aligned}
D(u,v)=\{i:1\leq i\leq m \text{ and } u_i\neq v_i\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Write $D(u,v)$ as the disjoint union of its maximal consecutive intervals. If these intervals have lengths $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$, set
$$
\begin{aligned}
d=\ell_1+\cdots+\ell_r.
\end{aligned}
$$
The coefficient sequence used by the finite rule is the explicitly listed row
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0=1,\quad A_1=1,\quad A_2=1,\quad A_3=2,\quad A_4=5,\quad A_5=16,\quad A_6=61,\quad A_7=272.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the checked forced-window range, the geodesic count between ordered endpoints is the finite product law
$$
\begin{aligned}
N(u,v)=\frac{d!}{\ell_1!\cdots \ell_r!}\prod_{j=1}^{r} A_{\ell_j}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $N(u,v)$ denotes the number of shortest paths from $u$ to $v$ in $\Gamma_m$. The multinomial factor records the possible interleavings of independent consecutive difference blocks, while the product records the internal count assigned to each block length.

\paragraph{Shortest-path kernel.}
The verification is finite and graph-internal. For each $m$, breadth-first search in $\Gamma_m$ gives a distance value $\delta_m(u,v)$ and a shortest-path count $B_m(u,v)$ for every ordered pair $u,v\in X_m$. The checked equality is
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_m(u,v)=\frac{d!}{\ell_1!\cdots \ell_r!}\prod_{j=1}^{r} A_{\ell_j}
\end{aligned}
$$
for every ordered endpoint pair with $2\leq m\leq 7$, where $\ell_1,
\ldots,\ell_r$ are extracted from $D(u,v)$ as above. Thus the witness is not a sampled statistic: it is the complete finite table of ordered endpoints in the six graph sizes $m=2,3,4,5,6,7$.

\paragraph{Concrete endpoint witnesses.}
In $\Gamma_4$, take $u=0101$ and $v=1000$. The differing coordinates split into consecutive run lengths $(2,1)$. Hence $d=3$, and the formula gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
N(0101,1000)=\frac{3!}{2!1!}A_2A_1=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
In $\Gamma_6$, take the opposite alternating endpoints $010101$ and $101010$. Their differing set is one consecutive interval of length $6$, so the distance is $6$ and
$$
\begin{aligned}
N(010101,101010)=A_6=61.
\end{aligned}
$$
These two endpoint checks display both mechanisms in the rule: interleaving across separated runs and internal multiplicity inside one forced run.

\paragraph{Window-six aggregate.}
The per-pair law also determines the complete distance profile for $m=6$ after summing over all ordered endpoint pairs in $X_6\times X_6$. Since $|X_6|=21$, there are $441$ ordered endpoint pairs. Grouping by shortest-path distance gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
[21,76,216,448,602,424,122].
\end{aligned}
$$
The entries are listed in increasing distance order from $0$ through $6$. This profile is an aggregate consequence of the same endpoint-level law; it is not an additional fitting condition.

\paragraph{Certificate content and boundary.}
The construction consists only of the finite sets $X_m$, the finite relations $E_m$, the adjacency matrices $M_m$, the breadth-first search kernels $(\delta_m,B_m)$, the run partition of $D(u,v)$, and the explicit coefficient row $A_0,\ldots,A_7$. Within the stated window, the exhaustive equality between $B_m(u,v)$ and the run-factor formula is the claimed mathematical content. No physical-constant identification, global flow law, external dependency, metrological comparison, or interpretation beyond this finite construction follows from the window computation unless a separate certificate supplies the additional premises.
