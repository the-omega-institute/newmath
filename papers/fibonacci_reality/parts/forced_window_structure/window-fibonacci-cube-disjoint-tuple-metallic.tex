\subsection{Finite forced-window tuple counts on Fibonacci cubes}
\label{sec:window-fibonacci-cube-disjoint-tuple-metallic}

\paragraph{Carrier.}
For each integer $m\geq 0$, let
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_m=\{w\in \{0,1\}^m: \text{there is no }j\text{ with }1\leq j<m\text{ and }w_j=w_{j+1}=1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite graph $\Gamma_m$ used here has vertex set $X_m$.  No edge relation is needed for the count; only the finite no-adjacent-ones carrier is used.  The basic vertex count is $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$, and this identity was checked for $m=0,1,\ldots,14$ in the finite rows under discussion.

\paragraph{Tuple predicate.}
Fix $k\geq 1$.  Define $D^{(k)}_m$ to be the number of ordered $k$-tuples $(w^{(1)},\ldots,w^{(k)})\in X_m^k$ such that their supports are pairwise disjoint.  Equivalently, for every coordinate $j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, at most one of the symbols $w^{(1)}_j,\ldots,w^{(k)}_j$ is equal to $1$.  Thus every coordinate is partitioned into one of $k+1$ local classes: unused, or used by exactly one chosen component.  This is an ordered tuple count, so assigning a coordinate to component $i$ and assigning it to component $i'$ are different choices when $i\neq i'$.

\paragraph{Finite transition kernel.}
For fixed $k$, the transfer states are the binary vectors $\{0,1\}^k$.  A state records which tuple components used the previous coordinate.  If $s,d\in\{0,1\}^k$, the transition $s\to d$ is allowed exactly when
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^k d_i &\leq 1,\\
d_i=1 &\Longrightarrow s_i=0\quad\text{for every }i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first condition enforces pairwise disjointness at the current coordinate.  The second condition enforces the no-adjacent-ones rule inside each component word.  Let $A_k$ be the $2^k\times 2^k$ matrix indexed by $\{0,1\}^k$ whose entry from $s$ to $d$ is $1$ when this transition is allowed and $0$ otherwise.  The finite transfer count is obtained by summing all admissible state paths of length $m$, with the first coordinate allowed to be unused or assigned to one component.  Direct brute-force enumeration of ordered tuples agrees with this transfer count for $k=1,2,3,4$ and $m=0,1,\ldots,5$.

\paragraph{Initial rows and recurrence.}
The empty window has exactly one tuple, so $D^{(k)}_0=1$.  A one-coordinate window is either unused or assigned to one of the $k$ ordered components, so $D^{(k)}_1=k+1$.  In the verified finite range $k=1,2,3,4,5$ and $m=2,3,\ldots,14$, the rows satisfy the unified second-order identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
D^{(k)}_m=kD^{(k)}_{m-1}+D^{(k)}_{m-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated characteristic polynomial is
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-kx-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $k=1$ slice recovers the vertex-count row $D^{(1)}_m=F_{m+2}$.  The finite prefixes recorded for the next slices are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(D^{(2)}_0,\ldots,D^{(2)}_7)&=(1,3,7,17,41,99,239,577),\\
(D^{(3)}_0,\ldots,D^{(3)}_6)&=(1,4,13,43,142,469,1549),\\
(D^{(4)}_0,\ldots,D^{(4)}_6)&=(1,5,21,89,377,1597,6765),\\
(D^{(5)}_0,\ldots,D^{(5)}_6)&=(1,6,31,161,836,4341,22541).
\end{aligned}
$$
The $k=3$ prefix is the median-zero triple anchor in this finite family.  The data therefore place the Fibonacci vertex count, the $k=2$ second-order row, and the $k=3$ anchor inside one forced-window construction indexed by $k$.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
A counted tuple can be read as a word of length $m$ over the alphabet $\{0,1,\ldots,k\}$, where $0$ means that the coordinate is unused and $i$ means that the coordinate is used by component $i$.  The local rule is: two adjacent letters may not be equal to the same nonzero value.  From such an alphabet word one extracts the ordered tuple by setting $w^{(i)}_j=1$ exactly when the $j$th alphabet letter is $i$, and setting all other component entries at that coordinate to $0$.  Conversely, every admissible ordered tuple determines exactly one such alphabet word.  This gives a finite, reversible certificate for each counted object, independent of any limiting process or numerical fitting.
