\subsection{Finite forced-window closed-neighborhood transfer}
\label{sec:window-fibonacci-cube-closed-neighborhood-transfer}

\paragraph{Carrier and adjacency relation.}
For each integer $m \geq 2$, let
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_m=\{w=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)\in \{0,1\}^m : w_iw_{i+1}=0\text{ for every }1\leq i<m\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $X_m$ is the finite set of binary words of length $m$ in which adjacent ones are forbidden. Define a finite graph $\Gamma_m$ with vertex set $X_m$ by joining two distinct words when their Hamming distance is one. Let $A(\Gamma_m)$ be the adjacency matrix of this graph in the chosen prefix order, and set
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_m=I+A(\Gamma_m).
\end{aligned}
$$
The matrix $N_m$ is therefore the closed-neighborhood matrix: its $(u,v)$ entry is $1$ exactly when $u=v$ or $u$ and $v$ differ in one coordinate, and is $0$ otherwise. The finite vertex count is governed by the same recursion as the admissible words: the checked values satisfy $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ for $m=2,3,4,5,6,7,8$.

\paragraph{Forced prefix partition.}
The first coordinate gives a canonical split. Every admissible word either begins with $0$, after which the remaining suffix is any element of $X_{m-1}$, or begins with $10$, after which the remaining suffix is any element of $X_{m-2}$. Hence, in prefix order,
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_m=0X_{m-1}\,\sqcup\,10X_{m-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
With respect to this ordered disjoint union, the closed-neighborhood matrix has the block form
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_m=\begin{pmatrix}N_{m-1}&J_m\\ J_m^T&N_{m-2}\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
for the checked range $m=3,4,5,6,7$. Here $J_m$ is the rectangular $0$-$1$ matrix whose row is indexed by a word in $0X_{m-1}$ and whose column is indexed by a word in $10X_{m-2}$, with entry $1$ exactly when the two represented length-$m$ words differ in one coordinate. This definition uses only the finite carrier and the Hamming-distance-one relation.

\paragraph{Integral transfer matrix.}
The forced split exposes a smaller integral remainder. For the checked range $m=4,5,6,7,8$, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_m=\begin{pmatrix}
N_{m-3}&J_{m-1}^T\\
-N_{m-2}J_{m-1}&I-N_{m-2}^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The identity-link transfer asserts that the Smith data of $N_m$ reduce, over the integers, to an identity block together with this remainder. In the finite window recorded here, the nontrivial Smith invariant factors of $N_m$ and $R_m$ agree for $m=4,5,6,7,8$, and the determinant relation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(N_m)=(-1)^{F_m}\det(R_m)
\end{aligned}
$$
for $m=4,5,6,7,8$. The sign is therefore controlled by the Fibonacci index $F_m$, while the torsion-relevant invariant factors are carried by $R_m$.

\paragraph{Finite numerical witnesses.}
The exact determinant values in the checked window are
$$
\begin{aligned}

\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}

In particular, the sixth closed-neighborhood matrix gives the exact finite anchor
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
This is a statement about the integer Smith normal form of a concrete finite matrix indexed by the words in $\{0,1\}^6$ with no adjacent ones. It is not introduced as a fitted physical value.

\paragraph{Witness extraction procedure.}
A witness for any listed $m$ is obtained by the following finite operations. First enumerate $X_m$ in prefix order. Second form the Hamming-distance-one graph on this list. Third construct $N_m=I+A(\Gamma_m)$. Fourth split the ordered vertex list as $0X_{m-1}\sqcup 10X_{m-2}$ and read off the off-diagonal block $J_m$. Fifth form $R_m$ from the displayed block formula. Sixth compute determinant and Smith invariant factors over $\mathbb{Z}$. These steps recover the stated determinant transfer, the agreement of nontrivial Smith factors in the checked window, and the $m=6$ cokernel $\mathbb{Z}/144\mathbb{Z}$.
