\subsection{Golden-Ratio Characteristic Recurrence}\label{sec:binet-golden-ratio-characteristic-recurrence}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Carrier.}
The finite carrier for this construction is the two-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
C=\{\phi,\psi\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi&=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},\\
\psi&=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The carrier is not introduced as a fitted numerical parameter set. It is the root set of the characteristic equation
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2=x+1,
\end{aligned}
$$
equivalently of the polynomial
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-x-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the predicate on a candidate number $r$ is the first-principles root condition
$$
\begin{aligned}
r \text{ is admitted exactly when } r^2=r+1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The construction is finite because only the two named candidates are present in the carrier, and each candidate is checked against the same quadratic predicate.

\paragraph{Root witnesses and finite relations.}
The two carrier elements are certified by direct substitution. Substituting $\phi$ into $x^2-x-1$ gives zero, and substituting $\psi$ into $x^2-x-1$ gives zero. The elementary relations recorded on the carrier are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi+\psi&=1,\\
\phi\psi&=-1,\\
\phi-\psi&=\sqrt{5},\\
\phi^2&=\phi+1,\\
\psi^2&=\psi+1.
\end{aligned}
$$
These relations are the complete local algebraic data used here. The finite partition of the carrier separates the dominant and subdominant roots:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{dom}}&=\{\phi\},\\
C_{\mathrm{sub}}&=\{\psi\},\\
\phi&>1,\\
|\psi|&<1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The inequality $|\psi|<1$ is the only asymptotic ingredient needed in the record: it implies that $\psi^n$ tends to zero as $n$ increases.

\paragraph{Recurrence kernel.}
The recurrence condition is stated without adding any further carrier. A sequence $A_0,A_1,A_2,\ldots$ passes the local recurrence test when
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=0,\\
A_1&=1,\\
A_n&=A_{n-1}+A_{n-2} \quad \text{for } n\geq 2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, for $n\geq 2$ the checking expression is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_n-A_{n-1}-A_{n-2}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the only kernel-like test used in the chapter: it is a finite recurrence constraint applied at each index, with the two initial values fixing the sequence.

\paragraph{Closed form.}
For the Fibonacci sequence $F_0,F_1,F_2,\ldots$ determined by the preceding recurrence test, the exact identity is
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_n=\frac{\phi^n-\psi^n}{\sqrt{5}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The witness for this identity is internal to the displayed recurrence: the expression
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\phi^n-\psi^n}{\sqrt{5}}
\end{aligned}
$$
has value $0$ at $n=0$, has value $1$ at $n=1$, and satisfies the same relation $F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}$ because both $\phi$ and $\psi$ satisfy $r^2=r+1$. Therefore the closed form is fixed by the same initial data and recurrence rule.

\paragraph{Forced finite window.}
The finite window explicitly recorded here is the index set $\{5,6,7,8\}$. On that window the exact counts are
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_5&=5,\\
F_6&=8,\\
F_7&=13,\\
F_8&=21.
\end{aligned}
$$
These four values are not statistical estimates. They are the forced outputs of the recurrence initialized by $F_0=0$ and $F_1=1$, and the same values are obtained by substituting $n=5,6,7,8$ into the closed form. The dominant-root partition explains why the quantity $\phi^n/\sqrt{5}$ controls the nearest-integer value: since $|\psi|<1$, the correction term $\psi^n/\sqrt{5}$ is bounded and tends to zero. Consequently, $F_n$ is the nearest integer to $\phi^n/\sqrt{5}$.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter asserts only the finite algebraic carrier $\{\phi,\psi\}$, its quadratic root predicate, the listed relations, the recurrence test, the closed form for $F_n$, and the exact window counts $F_5=5$, $F_6=8$, $F_7=13$, and $F_8=21$. It does not assert any physical-constant identification, global flow law, external dependency, metrological comparison, or interpretation beyond this finite construction. In particular, $\phi$ is used here as a proved algebraic root of $x^2-x-1$, not as a fitted physical constant, and no conclusion involving a fine-structure constant, the number $137$, or any measurement convention follows from the recurrence facts alone.
