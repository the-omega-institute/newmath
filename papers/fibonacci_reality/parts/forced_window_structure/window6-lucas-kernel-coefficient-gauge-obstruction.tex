\subsection{A finite Lucas kernel for the forced positions 7, 17, and 27}\label{sec:window6-lucas-kernel-coefficient-gauge-obstruction}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Carrier.}
The carrier is the integer companion matrix
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ 1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
It realizes multiplication by the residue class of $x$ in the quadratic integer algebra $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2-x-1)$. Equivalently, the defining reduction rule is $x^2=x+1$, and the matrix $M$ is the two-dimensional integer representation of that rule in the ordered basis $(x,1)$. The finite window used here is the ordered set of exponents
$$
\begin{aligned}
W=(7,17,27).
\end{aligned}
$$
A coefficient row for this window is a triple $(a,b,c)\in \mathbb{Z}^3$, read as the integer matrix expression
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_W(a,b,c)=aM^7+bM^{17}+cM^{27}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predicate certified by the packet is the finite annihilation predicate
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_W(a,b,c) \quad\text{means}\quad R_W(a,b,c)=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
No limiting process, real approximation, external measurement, or analytic continuation is part of this predicate; it is only a statement about three integer matrices and one integer coefficient triple.

\paragraph{Lucas number and integer powers.}
The integer $123$ used in the coefficient row is obtained from the Lucas recurrence
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_0&=2,\\
L_1&=1,\\
L_{n+2}&=L_{n+1}+L_n.
\end{aligned}
$$
Direct recurrence evaluation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{10}=123.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding companion-matrix powers are computed by ordinary integer matrix multiplication. The two powers needed to display the base relation are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{10}&=\begin{pmatrix}89&55\\ 55&34\end{pmatrix},\\
M^{20}&=\begin{pmatrix}10946&6765\\ 6765&4181\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting these matrices gives the exact identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{20}-123M^{10}+I=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $I$ is the $2\times 2$ identity matrix. Written in the quotient algebra $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2-x-1)$, this is the finite polynomial relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{20}-123x^{10}+1=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the numerical coefficient $123$ is not fitted from a real value. It is the tenth Lucas number and is verified by the recurrence above.

\paragraph{Forced-window relation.}
Multiplying the base matrix identity on the left by $M^7$ gives another exact integer matrix identity:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{27}-123M^{17}+M^7=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is precisely the annihilation statement for the ordered window $(7,17,27)$ with coefficient triple
$$
\begin{aligned}
k=(1,-123,1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_W(1,-123,1)&=1\cdot M^7-123\cdot M^{17}+1\cdot M^{27}\\
&=M^7-123M^{17}+M^{27}\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality is symmetric under reordering of summands because the entries lie in the ordinary integer matrix ring. The witness is therefore finite: the carrier matrix $M$, the recurrence value $L_{10}=123$, and the directly powered matrices $M^7$, $M^{17}$, and $M^{27}$ suffice to check the displayed equality by integer arithmetic.

\paragraph{Kernel direction and coefficient partition.}
The subgroup of coefficient triples generated by the certified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
K=\{t(1,-123,1):t\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subseteq \mathbb{Z}^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
For every $t\in\mathbb{Z}$, integer linearity gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_W\bigl(t,-123t,t\bigr)&=tR_W(1,-123,1)\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently, if a coefficient triple $(a,b,c)$ is used only through the matrix expression $R_W(a,b,c)$, then replacing it by
$$
\begin{aligned}
(a,b,c)+t(1,-123,1)
\end{aligned}
$$
leaves the resulting matrix unchanged. The finite construction therefore partitions $\mathbb{Z}^3$ into cosets of the rank-one subgroup $K$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(a,b,c)\sim(a',b',c')
\quad\text{when}\quad
(a',b',c')-(a,b,c)\in K.
\end{aligned}
$$
The packet certifies this indeterminacy direction, not a distinguished representative of each coset. In particular, the three positions $7$, $17$, and $27$ determine a valid annihilating coefficient row only modulo the integer span of $(1,-123,1)$ when rows are compared through the realized matrix expression.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
The witness can be extracted without any appeal to real numbers. First, compute $L_{10}$ from the Lucas recurrence and obtain $123$. Second, compute powers of the companion matrix $M$ by repeated integer multiplication. Third, verify the displayed identity $M^{20}-123M^{10}+I=0$. Fourth, multiply that identity by $M^7$ to obtain $M^{27}-123M^{17}+M^7=0$. The extracted certificate is the row $(1,-123,1)$ together with the window $(7,17,27)$ and the matrix carrier $M$. Every equality in the construction is an equality of $2\times 2$ integer matrices.
