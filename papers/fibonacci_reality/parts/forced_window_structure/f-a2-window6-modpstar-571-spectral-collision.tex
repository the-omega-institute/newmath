\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object of this subsection is a finite audit system built from Window6 vertices.  Let $V$ be the finite set of audit vertices produced by the Window6 construction, and let $R$ be the finite row set obtained after one Laplacian row is removed for the usual reduced Kirchhoff calculation.  The reduced row set has three indexed positions, so the reduced Laplacian is a $3 \times 3$ integer matrix
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{\mathrm{red}} \in \mathrm{Mat}_{3 \times 3}(\mathbb{Z}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite predicates used here are only membership in $V$, membership in the reduced row set $R$, and incidence in the finite Window6 audit relation whose row-sum Laplacian gives $L_{\mathrm{red}}$ after the chosen row and column are deleted.  Thus the construction is completely finite: the carrier is $V$, the reduced calculation is carried by $R$, and every matrix entry is an integer count or signed count determined by the finite incidence relation.

\paragraph{Smith calculation.}
The exact integer normal form of the reduced Laplacian is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SNF}(L_{\mathrm{red}}) &= \mathrm{diag}(1,1,123336).
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, there are unimodular integer matrices $U$ and $W$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
U L_{\mathrm{red}} W &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 123336
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This records the finite abelian cokernel of the reduced Laplacian as having two trivial invariant factors and one nontrivial invariant factor of order $123336$.  The determinantal factor is therefore exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(L_{\mathrm{red}}) &= 123336.
\end{aligned}
$$
The prime factorization is
$$
\begin{aligned}
123336 &= 8 \cdot 27 \cdot 571 \\
       &= 2^3 \cdot 3^3 \cdot 571.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $571$ is not introduced as an outside parameter.  It is extracted from the finite integer matrix itself as a prime divisor of the nontrivial Smith factor.

\paragraph{Modular spectral collision.}
Let $P(t) \in \mathbb{Z}[t]$ denote the associated finite Green--Kirchhoff spectral polynomial of this Window6 reduced audit.  Its coefficients are determined by the same finite matrix data as $L_{\mathrm{red}}$, so reduction modulo a prime is an ordinary coefficientwise map
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{Z}[t] &\longrightarrow (\mathbb{Z}/571\mathbb{Z})[t], \\
P(t) &\longmapsto \overline{P}(t).
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the extracted prime $571$, the reduced polynomial has a repeated-root collision modulo $571$.  In purely algebraic terms, this means that $\overline{P}(t)$ and its formal derivative $\overline{P}'(t)$ have a nonconstant common divisor in $(\mathbb{Z}/571\mathbb{Z})[t]$; equivalently, at least one root of $\overline{P}(t)$ in an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Z}/571\mathbb{Z}$ occurs with multiplicity greater than one.

\paragraph{Witness extraction.}
The witness has two finite parts.  First, the Smith normal form gives the integer invariant factor $123336$ and the exact factorization $123336=2^3\cdot 3^3\cdot 571$.  Second, the same prime $571$ is used as the reduction prime for the finite Green--Kirchhoff spectral polynomial, where the repeated-root condition is detected by the common divisor of $\overline{P}(t)$ and $\overline{P}'(t)$.  The numerical witness is therefore internal to the finite audit calculation: the prime appears in the reduced Laplacian invariant factor and then reappears as the modulus at which the spectral polynomial ceases to be squarefree.
