\section{Latent Claim Certificate Protocol}
\label{sec:latent-claim-certificate}

BEDC-JEPA changes the learning objective by making distinctions and gaps part
of the learned world state.  A second question remains even when the carrier
is fixed: what may be claimed about the contents of a fixed latent carrier?
The protocol in this section answers that question in fail-closed form.  It
does not assign semantic names to coordinates.  It tests whether a declared
operational predicate is supported by a source split, a test surface, and a
calibrated singleton claim.

\begin{definition}[BEDC-certified latent claim]
Let $H:\mathcal O\to\mathcal Z$ be a fixed latent carrier, let
$p_k:\Omega\to\{0,1\}$ be an operational predicate with a declared source
split $S_k$ and test surface $T_k$, and fix a risk level $\alpha\in(0,1)$.
A latent-claim procedure is BEDC-certified for $p_k$ when it fits a shallow
readout over $H(o)$ on the declared source split, forms split-conformal
prediction sets $\Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))$ on the test surface, and emits the
claim $p_k(o)=y$ only in the singleton case
$$
  \Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))=\{y\}.
$$
If the prediction set is non-singleton, the case is recorded as coverage debt.
If the source split or test surface is absent, the candidate is recorded as
source debt.  If the claim fails under declared perturbations or transfers, it
is recorded as stability debt.
\end{definition}

The content of a latent carrier is therefore not a list of coordinate names.
It is the algebra of operational predicates that survive the source, test,
calibration, and stability criteria, together with the ledger rows for predicates
that do not survive them.

\begin{proposition}[Singleton conformal claims bound unlogged error]
Assume the split-conformal prediction sets for predicate $p_k$ satisfy
$$
  \Pr[
    y_k \notin \Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))
  ]
  \le \alpha
$$
on the declared exchangeable test surface.  If the latent-claim procedure
emits a claim only when $\Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))$ is a singleton, then the
event
$$
  E_{\mathrm{UER},k}
  =
  \{
    |\Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))|=1
    \wedge
    \hat y_k(o)\ne y_k
  \}
$$
satisfies
$$
  \Pr[E_{\mathrm{UER},k}]
  \le \alpha .
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the event $|\Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))|=1$, the procedure emits the unique
label in the conformal set.  If the emitted label is wrong, then the true label
$y_k$ is not in that singleton set.  Hence
$$
  E_{\mathrm{UER},k}
  \subseteq
  \{
    y_k \notin \Gamma_{\alpha,k}(H(o))
  \}.
$$
The claimed bound follows from the conformal coverage assumption.
\end{proof}

This proposition is narrow by design.  It does not prove that every latent
coordinate is interpretable, that the readout is causal, or that a missing
source trace may be replaced by a probe score.  It only states that, within
the declared split-conformal surface, singleton claims cannot create more
unlogged error than the calibrated miscoverage budget.  All other candidates
remain in the gap ledger.
