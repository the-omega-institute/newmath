\section{Autoresearch Boundary Synthesis}
\label{sec:autoresearch-boundary-synthesis}

The LeWorldModel instantiation is now governed by an autoresearch ledger rather
than by isolated experiment rows.  A finding enters the paper boundary only
after a deterministic runner produces an executed verdict, the mechanical gates
clear reproducibility, criterion, provenance, and overclaim checks, and an
adversarial review records the verdict as sound or sound-but-scoped.  The
current ledger has no pending adversarial queue.  The authoritative
claim-verdict rows, adversarial-review ledger, and cannot-claim list are the
autoresearch evidence pointers listed in \autoref{sec:lewm-instantiation}.

The boundary that survives that gate has five parts.  First, horizon-indexed
detection is real: native and integrated ledgers read future failure surfaces
from the fixed LeWorldModel carrier, and shuffled-label guards rule out a
simple capacity artifact.  This does not become a stable strongest-probe
superiority or a calibrated high-coverage selective claim; the strongest-paired
and selective-sweep gates remain scoped or fail-closed.

Second, prediction parity is still negative.  Local native dynamics, teacher
transition imitation, recurrent sequence conditioning, multi-step residual
dynamics, and the completed A100 uniform/decay rows all remain far outside the
LeWorldModel next-latent parity threshold.  The transition-split upper bound
separates held-out generalization from residual fit, but it still leaves a
large prediction gap.  A BEDC-native state that fails this gate is a monitor or
diagnostic ledger, not yet a replacement world model.

Third, compute-value is a distinct variable from failure probability.  The
fixed-export structured assignment row closes when the model predicts
option-conditioned compute cost and the allocator enforces an exact per-episode
budget.  Independent PushT and reacher carriers preserve scoped
compute/control closure, while cube remains open.  The survival matrix records
the same distinction: a variable can reduce option-error readout loss without
becoming a budget-feasible control variable.

Fourth, the state-generation allocation boundary is now localized rather than
merely negative.  Episode-level allocation losses move toward the gate, and
geometry-conditioned state features reduce hard-regime damage, but the
hard-only interval remains open.  Internal scorer uncertainty, cluster
coverage, calibration transforms, generic assignment-value learners,
hard-preserving transforms, train-defined proxies, and learned residual-benefit
selectors do not repair the hard slice.  An episode-level budget-transfer audit
keeps this boundary sharp: under fixed residual-benefit scores, the
priority-residual row has hard budget-three episode mean $-0.00523$ with CI high
$+0.00334$, while the raw row has $-0.00556$ with CI high $+0.00369$, and the
hard budget-four slice turns harmful.  Anchor-level hard movement therefore
cannot be promoted into episode budget-magnitude transfer closure.  The matching
oracle ceiling separates the mechanism from the score: with the same exact
episode-budget constraints, an option-error oracle has hard budget-two, -three,
and -four means $-0.02512$, $-0.04300$, and $-0.05986$, with CI highs
$-0.01139$, $-0.02302$, and $-0.03088$.  The hard allocation problem is
therefore not vacuous; the missing object is a learned option-conditioned
compute-value score that approaches this ceiling without reading the evaluation
error.  A learned-oracle gap audit quantifies the distance: the best hard
budget-three learned score captures only $0.1293$ of the oracle headroom, with
mean regret $0.03744$, and budget-four capture can reverse to $-0.1519$.  A
candidate-pool audit over $22$ fixed learned score artifacts does not change the
boundary: the best mean hard oracle-headroom capture is only $0.0242$, its
budget-three capture is $0.1216$, and budget-four remains negative at
$-0.0629$.  Direct train/cal oracle-value target transforms also do not close
the gap.  Across option-error, per-anchor oracle-regret, uniform-relative-cost,
and tail-weighted option-error targets, the best direct row is the
uniform-relative-cost target with mean hard oracle-headroom capture $-0.1986$,
budget-three capture $-0.3610$, budget-four capture $-0.1285$, and mean regret
$0.0513$, worse than the best fixed-candidate mean capture $0.0242$.  The
predicted-rollout mechanism row is informative but still bounded: adding
transition, curvature, validity, and option-interaction summaries moves the
best mean hard oracle-headroom capture to $0.1119$, with budget-three capture
$0.1234$, budget-four capture $0.0960$, and mean regret $0.0380$.  This
improves over the direct target baseline and shows that rollout-internal
mechanism information matters, but it still captures only a small part of the
oracle ceiling.  A forced-replacement exact-budget target pushes this direction
slightly further: the best replacement row has mean hard oracle-headroom
capture $0.1261$ and budget-three capture $0.1741$, improving the passive
mechanism row, but budget-four capture reverses to $-0.0744$ and mean regret is
$0.0393$.  Naive multi-budget pooling does not repair this stress failure: a
single scorer with budget multiplier tokens has mean capture $-0.5408$,
budget-three capture $-0.4282$, budget-four capture $-0.2151$, and mean regret
$0.0613$.  Budget-aware supervision is therefore useful evidence, not
allocation closure, and budget conditioning must be more structured than a
pooled token.  A shared mechanism trunk with budget-specific heads makes this
stress boundary more precise: it improves over pooled budget-token conditioning
in mean capture and repairs the budget-four sign to $0.0959$, but its mean
hard oracle-headroom capture remains $-0.3271$, budget-three capture remains
$-0.4197$, budget-two capture is $-0.6576$, and mean regret is $0.0523$.  The
matching assignment-aware minimax policy row tests a stronger policy-level
failure mode: direct exact-budget oracle-assignment supervision with
worst-budget calibration has mean capture $-0.4869$, budget-two capture
$-0.3996$, budget-three capture $-0.8183$, budget-four capture $-0.2428$, and
mean regret $0.0626$.  Adding shallow episode-coupled context does not repair
the boundary: non-label episode rank, size, position, and budget-depth
competition features with episode-batched training give mean capture $-0.5590$,
budget-two capture $-1.3448$, budget-three capture $-0.4180$, budget-four
capture $0.0859$, and mean regret $0.0582$.  The current pressure is therefore
not another failure-score ranker, another selection rule over existing score
families, a simple oracle-target transform over the same carrier, feature
appending alone, a single-budget replacement target, naive budget-token pooling,
a simple shared-trunk budget-head factorization, local oracle-assignment
imitation with worst-budget selection, or shallow episode-coupled context; it
is held-out episode budget-magnitude transfer under explicit environment-state
and option-conditioned mechanisms.

A global assignment transfer diagnostic makes the hard-regime split explicit.
Among $19$ fixed score families and mixtures, selecting by non-hard
oracle-headroom capture chooses the minimax budget-policy score with non-hard
mean capture $0.4081$.  The same fixed score does not transfer to the held-out
hard episodes: hard mean capture is $-0.4869$, with budget-two, -three, and
-four captures $-0.3996$, $-0.8183$, and $-0.2428$, and hard mean regret
$0.0626$.  Non-hard assignment success therefore cannot be promoted into
hard-regime compute-value closure.

Fixed non-label descriptors also fail to explain this transfer gap strongly
enough to justify descriptor reweighting.  After fixing the non-hard selected
minimax score, $33$ descriptors from score geometry, scorer disagreement,
predicted rollout geometry, and two-rooms environment state are tested against
selected-vs-oracle episode regret.  The strongest descriptor is agent
$x$-position mean with absolute Spearman correlation only $0.2824$.  This
keeps the hard-regime mechanism open: existing descriptors can name the
boundary but do not yet supply a compute-value rule.

Fifth, the state-generation contract is a route constraint, not a completed
model claim.  The aligned export and candidate packet close the data-substrate
and measurement-path checks, but the candidate still misses prediction parity
and does not close allocation.  The next BEDC-native world-model step must keep
the prediction-parity gate explicit while training internal variables that
survive failure, claim, compute-value, and action/control readouts under the
same budget-feasible selection rule.  The compute-value target must be a
mechanism-bearing state variable, not merely a supervised transform of
available option errors, a passive rollout-feature descriptor, or a
single-budget replacement surrogate that fails budget-stress transfer.  It also
cannot be a single pooled scorer that treats budget as another passive token, or
a shared-trunk budget-head factorization that repairs one stress slice while
losing mean and budget-three oracle-headroom capture.  It also cannot be local
exact-budget oracle-assignment imitation unless that imitation survives the
hard-slice budget transfer gate, nor can it be shallow set-context scoring that
repairs budget-four while losing the mean and budget-two hard slices.
Selection on non-hard exact-budget episodes is also insufficient unless the
selected assignment rule transfers to the held-out hard regime.
Existing fixed non-label descriptors are not enough by themselves; the next
state-generation target needs perturbation or value labels for the hard regime,
not merely descriptor reweighting.

The hard-regime perturbation-value audit supplies that target without claiming
closure.  For each held-out hard episode and each exact-budget multiplier, the
oracle assignment is recomputed after forcing each anchor-depth option.  The
resulting forced-option delta labels are non-degenerate: the minimum label IQR
across budgets is $0.1276$, and the maximum near-zero fraction is $0.2000$.
The fixed non-hard selected minimax score is only weakly aligned with these
labels, with mean score-label Spearman correlation $0.1719$.  The boundary is
therefore sharper: the hard regime contains usable marginal compute-value
labels, but the existing selected score does not read them strongly.

Training on those labels improves the boundary without closing it.  A
budget-conditioned mechanism learner trained on forced-option perturbation
labels raises hard mean oracle-headroom capture from $-0.4869$ to $-0.0681$ and
reduces hard mean regret from $0.0626$ to $0.0454$.  Budget-three capture
becomes positive at $0.0787$, but budget-two and budget-four remain negative,
and the hard mean label Spearman is only $0.2635$ compared with the fixed-score
baseline $0.1719$.  The value-label route is therefore useful, but this learner
is still not an allocation-closed policy.

Non-hard episode/depth calibration also fails to explain the remaining hard
gap.  Among $34$ fixed transforms of the perturbation-value learner and the
minimax score, non-hard selection chooses a half mixture with mean capture
$0.4167$ and positive budget-two, -three, and -four capture on non-hard
episodes.  The same transform falls to hard mean capture $-0.3809$, hard
budget-three capture $-0.8038$, and hard mean regret $0.0581$, worse than the
base perturbation-value learner's hard mean capture $-0.0681$.  Thus the
remaining missing variable is not generic episode/depth calibration; it is a
hard-regime assignment mechanism.

The assignment-mechanism label audit identifies that mechanism more directly.
Held-out hard episodes have higher forced-delta concentration than non-hard
episodes: the top-decile forced-delta mass fraction shifts by $+0.1519$, and
the forced-delta IQR shifts by $+0.0713$.  The perturbation-value learner also
selects higher-cost forced options on hard episodes, with selected forced-delta
mean shifted by $+0.1149$, while selected-minus-oracle regret shifts by
$+0.0318$.  Depth-count mismatch alone is not the hard signature; concentrated
forced replacement value and costly selected forced options are.  This supplies
a concrete mechanism-label target for the next allocation learner.

The mechanism-aware target audit tests whether that target is strong enough in
principle.  Among $17$ candidate target families, the selected
mechanism-forced-delta target reaches hard mean oracle-headroom capture $1.000$,
with budget-two, budget-three, and budget-four capture all $1.000$, zero hard
regret, and label Spearman $1.000$.  The perturbation-value learner baseline is
still negative on mean hard capture at $-0.0681$, with budget-two capture
$-0.1556$, budget-three capture $0.0787$, and budget-four capture $-0.1275$.
The target therefore closes the hard exact-budget target gate on this export:
the missing state variable is not generic calibration but the assignment
mechanism carried by forced replacement value.  This is not a deployable
allocation policy, because the target is oracle-derived from forced-option
mechanism labels.  The next closure step is a non-leaky predictor of the same
mechanism target, evaluated under the same hard-slice budget transfer gate and
then repeated on an independent export.

A non-leaky mechanism-target learner narrows but does not close that step.  The
learner is trained from train/calibration forced-delta mechanism labels, while
its inputs are latent, environment, rollout-internal, option-depth, and budget
features rather than held-out evaluation option errors.  On the held-out hard
slice it raises mean capture from the perturbation-value learner baseline
$-0.0681$ to $-0.0050$, with budget-three capture improving from $0.0787$ to
$0.1081$.  The all-budget gate still fails: budget-two capture is $-0.1022$,
budget-four capture is $-0.0209$, and hard mean label Spearman drops from
$0.2635$ to $0.1922$.  The boundary is therefore no longer target definition.
The target ceiling is known; the open problem is learning that mechanism target
from deployable BEDC-native state features without leaking the forced-option
oracle.

Assignment-structured non-hard calibration also does not repair the gap.  A
transfer audit tests $12$ rank, episode-zscore, depth-offset, affine, and
mixture transforms of the non-leaky mechanism learner and perturbation-value
learner.  The oracle-derived target ceiling is reported only as a reference and
is excluded from selection.  Selection on non-hard episodes chooses the
perturbation-value learner, with non-hard mean capture $0.3094$, but this
transfers to hard mean capture $-0.0681$, budget-two capture $-0.1556$, and
budget-four capture $-0.1275$.  The raw mechanism learner remains better on the
hard mean at $-0.0050$, though still not closed.  Thus the remaining mechanism
gap is not a simple rank normalization, depth offset, affine correction, or
non-hard-selected mixture; it requires a learner whose internal state carries
hard-regime assignment mechanism information before the exact-budget selection
rule is applied.

Adding deployable set context is still insufficient in a linear learner.  A
set-context ridge learner uses random-projected latent state, environment
features, rollout-internal features, episode-local z-scores and ranks, option
depth, and budget tokens to predict the forced-delta mechanism target.  It
improves over the base perturbation-value learner on hard mean capture
($-0.0325$ versus $-0.0681$) and raises hard label Spearman to $0.2526$, but it
does not beat the scalar mechanism learner's hard mean capture $-0.0050$ and
does not close all budgets: budget-two capture is $0.0000$, budget-three is
$-0.0552$, and budget-four is $-0.0423$.  The target ceiling remains $1.000$ on
all hard budgets.  The hard boundary therefore requires more than deployable
set-context featurization with ridge supervision; the missing object is a
mechanism-bearing assignment state learned under the exact-budget combinatorial
constraint itself.

A shallow exact-budget surrogate does not supply that state.  The surrogate
uses non-hard exact-budget assignment regret to select among the scalar and
set-context mechanism sources and to fit small depth-offset templates before
held-out hard evaluation.  It selects the scalar mechanism source and learns
only a budget-two depth-offset template.  On the hard slice, mean capture stays
at $-0.0050$, with budget-two capture $-0.1022$, budget-three capture
$0.1081$, and budget-four capture $-0.0209$, identical to the scalar mechanism
learner and still below the all-budget gate.  The hard label Spearman also
drops to $0.1531$.  Thus direct contact with the exact-budget assignment rule
is necessary but not sufficient when applied as a post-hoc shallow offset; the
assignment mechanism must be learned inside the state/scoring model rather than
recovered after the fact by depth penalties.

An exact-budget pair-swap contrastive learner also fails as a shallow state
model.  The learner constructs train-time contrastive rows from oracle
exact-budget assignments: within a single episode and budget, two-anchor
replacements are sampled only when they preserve total depth, and the model is
trained to score the oracle pair below the feasible replacement.  Calibration
selects the regularization strength through exact-budget capture before any
hard-slice readout.  On the held-out hard slice, the selected contrastive model
has mean capture $-1.0873$, with budget-two capture $-2.0326$, budget-three
capture $-0.7923$, and budget-four capture $-0.4370$; hard label Spearman is
$0.0443$.  This is worse than the scalar mechanism learner's mean capture
$-0.0050$ and worse than the perturbation-value baseline's $-0.0681$.  Thus
direct pair-swap contact with the exact-budget combinatorics does not by itself
produce a usable assignment state.  The remaining object is not a post-hoc
pairwise reranker but a richer assignment-bearing world state whose internal
variables survive prediction, failure, and compute-value readouts jointly.

An oracle depth-count state is also not sufficient.  This ceiling audit supplies
the hard episode's oracle depth-count template for each budget, then asks whether
deployable within-count scores can choose the anchors for those counts.  The
source selected on non-hard episodes is the base perturbation-value scorer.  On
the held-out hard slice, oracle counts plus that scorer reach mean capture
$-0.5178$, with budget-two capture $-0.7532$, budget-three capture $-0.5602$,
and budget-four capture $-0.2398$; this is worse than the scalar mechanism
learner's hard mean capture $-0.0050$.  Therefore the missing state is not
merely the episode-level budget-count ledger.  It must encode anchor--depth
compatibility inside the count state: which anchors should receive which depths
under the exact-budget constraint, rather than only how many anchors of each
depth should be selected.

A shallow anchor--depth compatibility learner does not recover that state from
the current deployable features.  The learner is trained on train option-error
compatibility costs using latent, environment, rollout-internal, episode-local
set-context, depth, and budget features; calibration selects the ridge
regularization and tail weight through exact-budget capture.  On the held-out
hard slice, the selected learner has mean capture $-0.2288$, with budget-two
capture $-0.5841$, budget-three capture $0.0333$, and budget-four capture
$-0.1356$; hard label Spearman is $0.1427$.  This is better than the oracle
depth-count-within-count audit but still worse than the scalar mechanism
learner's hard mean capture $-0.0050$.  Thus the compatibility variable is the
right level of missing mechanism, but the current shallow deployable feature
map does not recover it under hard exact-budget transfer.  The next state model
must represent anchor--depth compatibility as an internal assignment-bearing
world-state variable, not as a linear readout over fixed rollout features.

Assignment-level hinge contact is still insufficient with the same fixed
features.  A structured assignment-energy learner constructs train rows from
whole exact-budget assignments: the oracle assignment is contrasted against
feasible uniform-depth, shallow-depth, deep-depth, and random-depth competitors,
and the linear energy is trained so that the oracle assignment has lower total
energy.  Calibration selects the regularization through exact-budget capture.
On the held-out hard slice, the selected energy model has mean capture
$-0.2458$, with budget-two capture $-0.5717$, budget-three capture $-0.3041$,
and budget-four capture $0.1383$; hard label Spearman is $-0.1197$.  This does
not improve over the scalar mechanism learner and does not improve over the
shallow compatibility learner.  The negative result separates assignment-level
loss contact from assignment-bearing state: exposing the exact-budget hinge is
not enough if the representation remains a fixed shallow rollout feature map.
