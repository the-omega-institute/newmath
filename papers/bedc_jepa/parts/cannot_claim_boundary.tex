\section{Cannot-Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:cannot-claim-boundary}

The present paper does not claim that BEDC-JEPA has been trained at scale.  It
does not claim autonomous intelligence, natural-language semantic grounding,
or full pixel-control world modeling.  It does not claim that a post-hoc
QualityEvidenceEnvelope is already a new model objective.

The current artifact claims an evaluated small-world comparison, a grid-pixel
learned-transition benchmark, a MiniGrid-style visual planning benchmark, a
two-object counterfactual intervention benchmark, and seeded four-slot and
six-slot distractor benchmarks.  It also claims a boundary-world
gradient-trained objective artifact under a fixed-latent protocol and a
public V-JEPA2-AC Giant checkpoint adapter evaluation on CUDA.  It does not
claim public benchmark superiority, a robotics benchmark result, large-scale
real-world world modeling, formal neural-network verification, or a general
autonomous-intelligence result.  The artifact also claims a native public
MiniGrid DoorKey S0/S1/S2/S3 study with a seed sweep and an audited planning
penalty sweep, plus a CUDA retraining loss-term ablation for the boundary-gated
torch objective and a fixed-carrier V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid latent-claim
certificate artifact.  It also claims a fixed-checkpoint V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid
latent-prediction contact artifact.  The reproducibility artifacts cover the
declared study.
The public AC Giant study is an adapter comparison on the declared
boundary-gated tiny world, not a reproduction of the checkpoint's native
training or evaluation setting.  The public MiniGrid study is a native
benchmark evaluation for BEDC readback and planning-debt accounting; it is not a
native V-JEPA2-AC benchmark reproduction.  Its seed sweep supports unlogged
error and gap-AUROC directions but does not establish public MiniGrid debt
improvement.  Its debt-decomposition artifact attributes the single-run public
debt result to a silent-debt versus coverage-debt tradeoff, not to an established
public total-debt improvement.  Its certified-coverage and ablation artifacts
are local public MiniGrid readout analyses; they do not claim public MiniGrid
full retraining.  The retraining loss-term artifact is scoped to the
boundary-gated torch objective: it supports the gap-loss term as a mechanism
for gap ranking and certified coverage under unchanged latent recovery, but it
does not establish an independent unlogged-loss effect in that setting.  It
also leaves stability-term and intervention-term retraining as source-debt
rows because their required supervision surfaces are not present in the
boundary-gated torch objective.  The V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid certificate artifact
is a fixed-carrier readback study: it records coverage-gap and source-debt
outcomes under the latent-claim certificate protocol, not native V-JEPA2-AC
rollout reproduction or certified semantic grounding.  The V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid
latent-prediction artifact records fixed-checkpoint encoder/predictor contact
on the public MiniGrid stream, not official V-JEPA2-AC benchmark reproduction
or public benchmark superiority.  The object-interaction
evidence is still
rendered slot-world evidence rather than natural video or robot control.  The
LeJEPA-style
Gaussian-OU runner remains a calibration baseline.  These experiments are
scoped evidence records for BEDC-JEPA, not a complete model family.

\paragraph{Required next evidence.}
The next manuscript-bearing evidence must include:
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
1. & \text{a native V-JEPA2-AC benchmark reproduction or external baseline run},\\
2. & \text{stronger public MiniGrid calibration and risk-success Pareto summaries},\\
3. & \text{a public pixel-world benchmark comparison},\\
4. & \text{a public object-interaction benchmark with natural clutter or control}.
\end{array}
$$
The four-system ablation remains mandatory in each setting because it tests
whether BEDC-JEPA is more than a reporting layer.

\paragraph{Name boundary.}
Operational names in this paper are not free natural-language labels.  A name
is admissible only when it is attached to a distinction with a declared source,
test surface, stability condition, and gap policy.  The first such name is the
inside-boundary distinction of the boundary-gated OU world.
