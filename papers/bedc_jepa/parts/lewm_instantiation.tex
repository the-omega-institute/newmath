\section{Fixed-Checkpoint Gap-Ledger Instantiation: LeWorldModel}
\label{sec:lewm-instantiation}

The public-baseline registry of \autoref{sec:relation-to-jepa} names two
JEPA-family targets.  The V-JEPA2-AC rows ask adapter and latent-prediction
questions on a fixed checkpoint.  The LeWorldModel rows recorded here ask the
gap-ledger question on a fixed checkpoint: can a head trained only on the
checkpoint's own latent stream predict where that checkpoint's world model is
about to be wrong, and can the ledger semantics survive contact with the
checkpoint's real failure surface?

The carrier is the public LeWorldModel \texttt{tworooms} release
\cite{leworldmodel}, loaded without the stable-worldmodel stack (plain
\texttt{torch} plus the public ViT encoder weights), with real
\texttt{tworooms} frames streamed from the public dataset: $4991$ valid
transitions over $278$ episodes, latent dimension $192$.  All rows use an
episode-level $60/20/20$ split, episode-level bootstrap confidence intervals,
and a world-model-native failure truth: a transition counts as a failure when
the checkpoint predictor's own autoregressive latent rollout error exceeds
the clean train-split $75$th percentile.  The protocol is fail-closed:
evaluation slices that become single-class are reported as unidentifiable
rather than as detection results.

\paragraph{Post-hoc gap head on the frozen checkpoint.}
The first row is the post-hoc construction that the four-system comparison
uses as its report baseline, evaluated on the real checkpoint: a logistic gap
head reading only the learned representation, with a matched-random control
of equal capacity trained on permuted labels.  The gap head predicts the
checkpoint's own failures well above its control,
$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{gap head AUROC} & 0.731 & [0.692,\ 0.774],\\
\text{matched-random AUROC} & 0.472 & [0.427,\ 0.518],\\
\text{unlogged error} & 0.231 \rightarrow 0.168 & [0.142,\ 0.191],
\end{array}
$$
with both interval separations holding under the episode bootstrap.  The gap
features include an embedding-to-state linear-recovery score, so the
instantiation stays in contact with the identifiability question of
\cite{lejepa-world-model}.

\paragraph{Trained ledger head on the same carrier.}
The second row transports the paper's central contrast --- trained ledger
output versus post-hoc report --- onto the same fixed checkpoint.  A small
causal transformer ($2$ layers, width $64$, about $9.3\times 10^{4}$
parameters) reads windows of six (latent, action) tokens and is trained
jointly on a ledger head and a next-latent head.  Under the same dual
criteria the trained head is positive,
$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{trained ledger AUROC} & 0.675 & [0.636,\ 0.711],\\
\text{matched-random AUROC} & 0.543 & [0.499,\ 0.588],\\
\text{unlogged error} & 0.231 \rightarrow 0.132 & [0.110,\ 0.156],
\end{array}
$$
and a component ablation shows the joint next-latent loss has no measurable
effect on this carrier ($0.671$ AUROC without it).  The honest reading cuts
both ways.  The trained head transfers to a real checkpoint, but it does not
beat the post-hoc logistic head on detection AUROC ($0.675$ versus $0.731$,
intervals overlapping), while reaching a lower operating-point unlogged error
($0.132$ versus $0.168$, intervals overlapping).  On this carrier the
trained-versus-post-hoc contrast is not settled by victory; what the executed
rows establish is transfer, a live matched-random capacity artifact ($0.543$
slightly above chance from temporal windows alone), and a measured
operating-point trade.

\paragraph{Perturbation boundary.}
Under visual perturbation of the evaluation stream (color shift, occlusion,
brightness, background tint, Gaussian noise; the brittleness setting of the
stable-worldmodel line), the clean-only post-hoc head keeps working under
mild background tint (AUROC $0.72$) and collapses under strong shifts, where
almost every transition fails and the slice becomes single-class.  Gap-head
training on perturbed latents generalizes across families in a leave-one-out
test: held-out occlusion $0.72$, brightness $0.67$, background tint $0.67$,
Gaussian noise $0.63$, with color shift remaining unidentifiable.  At the
strongest perturbations the OOD-aware heads declare gap on nearly everything,
which is the conservative failure mode the ledger semantics ask for.  The
trained ledger transformer's own clean-trained perturbation arm reproduces
the same boundary shape rather than escaping it: detection persists under
background tint at every tested strength (AUROC $0.62$--$0.65$) and at mild
occlusion, brightness, and Gaussian-noise strengths ($0.62$--$0.71$), while
the strongest slices drive the failure rate to one, become single-class, and
are reported fail-closed.  An OOD-aware variant of the trained head, trained
on clean plus perturbed train-split windows under the same protocol,
reproduces the OOD-aware pattern of the post-hoc line at the architecture
level: leave-one-out transfer to held-out families is positive on four of
five (pooled AUROC: occlusion $0.75$, background tint $0.70$, brightness
$0.66$, Gaussian noise $0.59$; color shift stays single-class), the strict
final-strength claim holds on background tint (AUROC $0.77$), at the
strongest slices the head conservatively declares gap on nearly everything
(unlogged error $\rightarrow 0$), and clean evaluation is not degraded
(AUROC $0.675 \rightarrow 0.721$ with overlapping intervals).  A four-level
purely discriminative readout scan (linear, one-hidden-layer, two-hidden-layer,
and transformer readers on the same flattened-window input) shows that, without
the joint next-latent loss, all levels pay a clean detection cost for robustness
($\Delta$clean $=-0.076/-0.088/-0.084/-0.047$, non-monotone), while the matched
transformer contrast with and without the joint loss is $0.721$ versus $0.624$;
clean awareness is therefore preserved by the joint world-prediction objective,
not by readout capacity.

\paragraph{Probing what the ledger reads.}
Three probes bound the nature of the failure signal.  First, the trained
head's own next-latent prediction is far weaker than the checkpoint predictor
on the same transitions and metric (mean squared error $0.879$ versus
$0.067$; the small head wins on $0.1\%$ of transitions), so the ledger's
detection ability does not ride on superior dynamics.  Second, the signal is
not a state-recovery deficit: per-transition linear and local ($k=20$
nearest-neighbour) state-recovery residuals do not predict failure (AUROC
$0.455$ $[0.407,\ 0.503]$ and $0.466$ $[0.418,\ 0.515]$, against $0.731$ for
the gap head), so the naive operationalization of the identifiability
question --- the model fails where its latent encodes state poorly --- is
rejected on this carrier.  Third, one-step awareness does not transfer to
multi-step budget allocation: distributing rollout depth by ledger score at
matched compute \emph{raises} per-step rollout error against a uniform
allocation ($+0.013$ $[+0.005,\ +0.021]$), while an oracle ranked by true
future error lowers it ($-0.016$), so the allocation lever exists but the
one-step ledger score selects the wrong direction for it.

\paragraph{Horizon-indexed ledger.}
The horizon-indexed label family is
$y_{t,h,q} = 1[e_{t,h} > \tau_{h,q}]$, with
$h \in \{1,3,5,10\}$ and $q \in \{50,75,90\}$.  Here $e_{t,h}$ is the
endpoint error of the checkpoint predictor's $h$-step rollout, and
$\tau_{h,q}$ is the corresponding clean train-split quantile.  Under this
horizon-conditioned supervision, the trained ledger reaches clean $h=1$
$0.739$ $[0.696,\ 0.781]$, matching the post-hoc head at $0.731$, while also
detecting at $h=5$ ($0.710$ $[0.657,\ 0.761]$).  The same labels let a
logistic head reach $h=1$ $0.737$, $h=5$ $0.710$, and $h=10$ $0.715$, so
horizon detection is label-driven rather than architecture-specific.  With
within-episode pairwise ranking, the native horizon ledger separates from the
post-hoc scorer on the clean $h=1$ evaluation.  The paired episode bootstrap on
the same evaluation episodes gives
$$
\Delta\mathrm{AUROC}_{h=1}^{\mathrm{native-posthoc}}
= +0.050\ [0.0043,\ 0.100],
$$
with native AUROC $0.788$ versus post-hoc AUROC $0.737$.  Independent
confidence intervals are a conservative parity check because they overlap; the
paired comparison is the appropriate test for two scorers on the same episode
sample and gives interval-separated gain.
With the same horizon-ledger configuration trained separately on three
additional carriers, every test horizon is detectable (all confidence-interval
lower endpoints exceed $0.5$): reacher has AUROC $0.734$/$0.725$/$0.701$/$0.696$
at $h=1/3/5/10$, cube has $0.602$/$0.672$/$0.667$/$0.631$, and pusht has
$1.000$/$0.999$/$1.000$/$1.000$ on the contiguous episode block.  On reacher,
horizon supervision exceeds the probe row ($0.734$ $[0.698,\ 0.772]$ against
$0.662$) while lowering unlogged error $0.230 \rightarrow 0.149$.  For pusht,
the contiguous block is not the population claim; an independent
stratified-random $150$-episode export puts representative $h=1$ detection at
post-hoc AUROC $0.744$ and native AUROC $0.800$, with CI95\_high $<0.95$ and
UER $>0.02$.  Thus pusht is not perfectly readable on the representative
population; its detection lies in the same readability range as
tworooms/reacher/cube.

Teacher distillation by logit matching to the post-hoc scores is harmful:
distillation alone gives $0.661$, and distillation with the full loss gives
$0.692$, both below the pure horizon-supervised value $0.739$.  The failure
surface is directly learnable from horizon-indexed labels.  The strongest
configuration is a perturbation- and horizon-aware trained ledger, trained
with true perturbation rollout trajectories from perturbation train slices: it
keeps clean $h=1$ at $0.720$ $[0.681,\ 0.757]$, lifts perturbation detection
at $h=1$ to background tint $0.752$, occlusion $0.771$, and Gaussian noise
$0.745$, and repairs the score-anticorrelated brightness slice from $0.069$ to
$0.991$ $[0.982,\ 0.998]$.

Budget allocation remains on the wrong side but converges monotonically:
one-step scores give $+0.0128$, the full loss gives $+0.011$, pure horizon
supervision gives $+0.0072$, and horizon supervision with ranking loss gives
$+0.0075$ $[-0.003,\ +0.020]$, against a label-permutation control at
$+0.031$ and an oracle at $-0.016$.  Detection-level scores therefore do not
yet supply allocation-level ranking.  Under the same budget protocol, three
dedicated allocation heads all remain below the decision criterion: error
regression gives $+0.009$ $[-0.002,\ +0.023]$, pure pairwise ranking gives
$+0.006$ $[-0.003,\ +0.018]$, and a two-stage ranker on fixed ledger logits
gives $+0.010$ $[-0.002,\ +0.024]$.  Each nevertheless learns the true
within-episode error ordering, with Spearman $0.460$/$0.508$/$0.432$, against a
target-permutation control at $0.160$ and an oracle of $1.0$ at $-0.016$.
The allocation result closes as a scoped information bound.  Across $7$
cross-fit readers, the strongest reader is pairwise RankNet with
within-episode Spearman
$$
\rho = 0.49\ [0.32,\ 0.49],
$$
below the required $\rho^\star \approx 0.50$ from the $150$-seed oracle-blend
distribution (p05 $0.501$).  No reader beats uniform allocation
($\Delta$ CI\_high $\ge 0$), and the MI upper bound gives
$\rho_{\max}\approx 0$.  The sanity checks are aligned: permutation gives
$\rho\approx 0$, and oracle gives $\rho=1.0$.  The bound is not a theorem about
all models; it is under the current latent export, finite CPU-readable feature
family, and fixed $5/1/3$ allocation rule.

\paragraph{Compute-value target.}
The allocation boundary changes the target variable.  The detection ledger
estimates whether a rollout will fail,
$$
g_{t,h,q} = \Pr(e_{t,h} > \tau_{h,q} \mid x_t),
$$
but the allocation decision needs the value of a compute option $b$:
$$
MV(t,b) = E_{\mathrm{uniform}}(t) - E_b(t).
$$
A high failure probability can mean either that deeper rollout or refinement
has value, or that the state is outside the useful intervention region and
extra compute only spends budget.  The BEDC-native planning target is therefore
option-conditioned: the model must read the current latent/action context,
rollout-internal trajectory features, calibration debt, and a candidate
compute option $b$, then predict $MV(t,b)$ separately from the horizon failure
logits and selective-admission score.  In this form allocation is not a
downstream metric of the gap score; it is a supervised intervention-value task
with its own label artifact and its own episode-bootstrap gate.  The registered
autoresearch packet for this target expects option-level errors and predicted
marginal values, and fails closed when those arrays are absent.
The present compute-value artifact instantiates this label interface on the
tworooms evaluation split with $634$ anchors, $55$ episodes, and rollout-depth
options $b\in\{1,3,5\}$ against a uniform depth-$3$ baseline.  Its oracle
readback, using true per-step $MV(t,b)$ in place of a trained prediction, gives
allocation delta $-0.01290$ $[-0.01851,\ -0.00806]$ under the same balanced
episode-budget rule.  This is a target-validity result, not a trained-model
result: the artifact records \texttt{predicted\_mv} as an oracle placeholder,
and the admissible model claim requires replacing that array by a prediction
made without evaluation error labels.
A local RTX~4060 readout test then replaces the oracle field by model outputs.
Ridge, MLP, and scalar RankNet readers are trained on train-split
rollout-internal features and selected on the calibration split.  The selected
RankNet reader learns a real marginal-value ordering on evaluation
($\rho=0.4499$ with interval $[0.3651,\ 0.5272]$ under the runner bootstrap),
but it still fails the allocation gate: allocation delta is $+0.00822$
$[+0.00053,\ +0.01738]$.  Thus the compute-value variable is
allocation-relevant in the oracle readback, while this local readout family has
not converted it into useful budget control.
A policy-oriented local RTX~4060 scorer trains directly on the episode-local
top/bottom assignment used by the balanced depth gate.  It also fails the gate:
allocation delta is $+0.00675$ $[+0.00179,\ +0.01146]$.  The row is diagnostic
rather than positive.  It improves the allocation delta relative to the scalar
RankNet row while lowering global $MV$ Spearman to $0.3413$, so the
binding statistic is not global marginal-value rank correlation alone.  The
same evaluation file records the oracle at $-0.01290$ $[-0.01922,\ -0.00830]$,
a random reference at $+0.02094$ $[+0.01211,\ +0.03207]$, and an inverted
oracle at $+0.05886$ $[+0.04546,\ +0.07605]$.  The route therefore points to
episode-local compute assignment and budget-feasible policy training, rather
than another scalar failure-score or global-rank readout.
A stability grid over seeds $3,7,11$ and epoch budgets $1,5,20,100$ tests
whether that policy signal is robust enough for selection.  The
calibration-selected run, seed $3$ at $20$ epochs, evaluates at allocation
delta $+0.00184$ $[-0.00297,\ +0.00687]$.  The best observed run is
$+0.00162$ $[-0.00318,\ +0.00665]$, but every interval upper endpoint remains
positive and the median observed delta across the $12$ runs is $+0.00734$.
Thus the episode-local objective can nearly erase the wrong-side scalar
RankNet gap on this export, but the row remains a stability negative rather
than an allocation claim.
An episode-level decomposition shows where the remaining error sits.  The
selected policy's mean episode delta is $+0.00137$, compared with $+0.00698$
for the scalar RankNet, $+0.02451$ for a random reference, and $-0.01280$ for
the oracle.  It has $28$ positive-delta and $27$ negative-delta episodes,
whereas the oracle has $12$ positive and $43$ negative.  The missed oracle gain
is concentrated: the top missed episode contributes $13.94\%$ of positive
missed mass, the top five contribute $41.62\%$, and the top ten contribute
$61.78\%$.  This points to tail-episode assignment and not just average
episode-listwise ranking.
A direct tail-episode weighting test is fail-closed.  Train episodes are
weighted by their train-split oracle gain, producing aggressive point weights
up to $64$ after the top/bottom label weights are applied.  Across seeds
$3,7,11$ and epoch budgets $5,20,100$, calibration selection chooses a row with
evaluation allocation delta $+0.00878$ $[+0.00322,\ +0.01536]$, worse than the
unweighted episode-policy reference $+0.00184$ $[-0.00309,\ +0.00651]$.  The
best observed tail-weighted row is $+0.00431$ $[-0.00080,\ +0.01012]$, still
not a claim.  This separates the diagnosis from a simple remedy: the residual
gap is tail-episode concentrated, but train-oracle episode reweighting does not
transfer into useful allocation control.
A soft budget-feasible surrogate makes the training objective closer to the
balanced depth gate by using train-split option errors inside a differentiable
episode objective.  It remains fail-closed.  Calibration selection over the
same seed family chooses seed $11$ at $60$ epochs, with evaluation allocation
delta $+0.00356$ $[-0.00342,\ +0.01177]$; the best observed row is seed $11$ at
$20$ epochs with $+0.00193$ $[-0.00355,\ +0.00700]$.  Both remain bounded, and
the unweighted episode-policy reference is still $+0.00184$
$[-0.00306,\ +0.00687]$.  Thus moving the loss toward the budget gate helps
relative to naive tail weighting, but a soft surrogate does not yet produce an
allocation-native policy.
A hard-episode feature probe asks whether the missing state is already
available in the aggregate rollout/internal features.  It trains a linear
episode-level probe on train-split oracle-gain tail labels and evaluates it
against calibration labels and the top missed-oracle evaluation episodes from
the decomposition record.  The probe overfits train (AUROC $1.000$) but gives
calibration AUROC $0.5265$ and evaluation AUROC $0.5053$ against the top missed
episodes.  The top missed episodes have mean score $-0.656$ against $-0.622$
for non-top episodes.  The hard episodes are therefore not linearly readable
from this aggregate feature view; the next model must expose richer
episode-state structure or learn a structured assignment rather than only
retune scalar objectives on the current aggregates.
A sequence-summary variant adds per-episode feature quantiles,
adjacent-change summaries, and policy/oracle score-distribution statistics.
This improves calibration AUROC to $0.5985$ but still gives only $0.5479$ on
the top missed evaluation episodes, with top missed episodes scoring lower
than non-top episodes ($-0.846$ versus $-0.728$).  Thus the failure is not
resolved by simple sequence summaries.  The remaining route is to alter the
state view more substantially, change the compute-option label space, or use a
structured sequence/set assignment model.
Changing the compute-option label space gives the strongest allocation-side
movement.  A diagnostic keeps the same precomputed episode-policy score and
changes only the balanced depth grid.  On $\{1,3,5\}$, the policy-ranked
allocation delta is $-0.00181$ $[-0.00705,\ +0.00345]$; on
$\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ it becomes $-0.00330$ $[-0.00676,\ +0.00015]$.  The refined
grid's oracle is also slightly stronger, $-0.03059$
$[-0.03839,\ -0.02495]$, compared with $-0.02930$
$[-0.03704,\ -0.02333]$ on $\{1,3,5\}$.  This remains fail-closed because the
upper endpoint is just above zero, but it shows that allocation usefulness is
sensitive to the option set.  The hard $1/3/5$ budget split is therefore not a
neutral evaluation detail; intermediate compute options may be part of the
native compute-value target.
A direct learned readout over the refined option set is still bounded.  Ridge
and MLP readers predict five-option marginal values for depths
$\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ from the same rollout-internal features and are selected on the
calibration split.  The selected MLP evaluates at allocation delta $+0.00184$
$[-0.00199,\ +0.00656]$ with score Spearman $0.3315$, while reusing the
episode-policy score on the same refined grid gives $-0.00330$
$[-0.00679,\ +0.00020]$.  The oracle remains far below at $-0.03059$
$[-0.03810,\ -0.02476]$.  The refined grid is therefore a real lever, but the
tested direct per-option readout does not capture it.
The closed allocation row comes from making the allocator structured rather
than making the readout wider.  An option-conditioned local RTX~4060 model
reads rollout-internal features together with an explicit depth option
$b\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, predicts option error, and then selects depths with an
exact per-episode dynamic program constrained to total budget $3n$.  The
calibration-selected row evaluates at allocation delta $-0.00604$
$[-0.01045,\ -0.00168]$ with score/error Spearman $0.8899$.  Under the same
exact-budget rule, the oracle gives $-0.02412$ $[-0.03045,\ -0.01944]$; the
direct refined-value DP row gives $+0.00182$ $[-0.00279,\ +0.00629]$; and the
reused policy-score balanced row gives $-0.00330$ $[-0.00697,\ +0.00024]$.
Thus compute-value control is not recovered by a scalar failure score or by a
plain five-output marginal-value head.  It appears when the world-model state is
asked for option-conditioned compute cost and the budget constraint is part of
the assignment rule.
A structured stability grid qualifies the row without removing it.  Over seeds
$29,41,53$ and epoch budgets $160,280,420$, the calibration-selected run is
again seed $41$ at $420$ epochs and evaluates at $-0.00604$
$[-0.01081,\ -0.00187]$.  Three of nine evaluation rows have confidence upper
endpoint below zero, the median observed delta is $-0.00355$, and the weakest
row is $+0.00095$ $[-0.00555,\ +0.01040]$.  The positive result is therefore a
calibration-selectable structured-assignment row on the fixed export, not yet a
uniform seed-stability or independent-export claim.
The ablation row identifies the necessary interaction.  The full
state-plus-depth model remains at $-0.00604$ $[-0.01080,\ -0.00130]$.  A
state-only model, which lacks explicit option identity, goes sharply wrong-side
at $+0.02756$ $[+0.01436,\ +0.04333]$.  A depth-only model and a ridge model
over the full features both choose the uniform depth and produce exactly zero
allocation delta.  The closed row is therefore not an artifact of the dynamic
program or of depth priors.  It depends on nonlinear interaction between the
rollout-internal state and the candidate compute option.
A feature-group ablation shows that the interaction is distributed across the
state view.  With seed $41$ and $280$ epochs, the full feature set evaluates at
allocation delta $-0.00474$ $[-0.00862,\ -0.00154]$ with score/error Spearman
$0.8839$.  Reading only the base latent/action window is wrong-side
($+0.00331$ $[-0.00351,\ +0.01285]$).  Reading only the rollout trajectory is
useful but not closed ($-0.00259$ $[-0.00703,\ +0.00272]$), and norm/gap
summaries alone stay near zero ($+0.00076$ $[-0.00477,\ +0.00558]$).  Adding
trajectory or norm/gap features back to the base state also remains
fail-closed.  The allocation-native row therefore does not come from a single
rollout statistic; it requires the full rollout-internal context, the base
state, and the explicit option token.
The structured row is registered in the autoresearch execution lane as
\texttt{fi-022.compute-value-structured-assignment}.  The runner validates the
structured artifact schema, recomputes the exact-budget assignment, and emits
a fail-closed payload if the schema is absent.  On the fixed-export structured
artifact it records allocation delta $-0.00604$ $[-0.01074,\ -0.00163]$ with
score/error Spearman $0.8899$.  The mechanical gate is positive and the
adversarial audit marks the finding sound but scoped; the scientific claim
therefore remains a fixed-export, calibration-selected structured-assignment
result rather than an independent-replication or universal-control claim.
A readiness scan also finds a usable independent-export structured
artifact, but only in a narrower feature scope.  On the independent PushT
horizon export, a rollout-only option-conditioned model uses predicted rollout
trajectories, rollout deltas, norms, validity masks, and timing features, with
no base latent/action features.  It evaluates under the same exact-budget
runner at allocation delta $-0.18572$ $[-0.18804,\ -0.18328]$ with score/error
Spearman $0.9947$ on $416$ anchors from $30$ episodes.  This is an
independent-export replication of the rollout-only compute-value assignment
mechanism, not a full-feature replication of the fixed-export structured row.
The full-feature independent PushT carrier is then evaluated separately.  With
the aligned base latent/action window, predicted rollout-internal features,
and the same option token, the learned exact-budget assignment gives
allocation delta $-0.18572$ $[-0.18804,\ -0.18306]$ with score/error Spearman
$0.9962$ on the same $416$ anchors from $30$ episodes, matching the
exact-budget oracle at $-0.18572$ $[-0.18805,\ -0.18318]$.  The result is
registered as \texttt{fi-025.compute-value-independent-full-feature}: a
positive independent full-feature compute-value assignment row whose
adversarial audit is sound but scoped.  The audit rejects two simple shortcut
explanations on PushT: depth-only assignment is wrong-side at $+0.10746$
$[+0.10431,\ +0.11046]$, and shuffled learned scores are wrong-side at
$+0.04216$ $[+0.01868,\ +0.06494]$.  The same runner gives a small but closed
reacher row, $-0.00046$ $[-0.00080,\ -0.00008]$, while cube is not closed at
$+0.00060$ $[-0.00039,\ +0.00186]$.  The compute-value route is therefore
supported as a budget-feasible assignment signal on more than one carrier,
but not as a universal cross-environment control claim.
These independent artifacts also support a survival-matrix view of
BEDC-native state variables.  For a candidate distinction $q$, define
$$
S_q(r\mid Z)=J_0(r\mid Z)-J_q(r\mid Z,q),
$$
where $Z$ contains base controls and $r$ is the readout being tested.  The
current artifact instantiates $r$ as option-error prediction and uses time and
candidate depth as controls.  The learned option score has positive
option-error survival on PushT, reacher, and cube: $1.2052$, $0.0001005$, and
$0.0000164$.  Yet its exact-budget allocation closes only on PushT and
reacher.  Conversely, true marginal value has large cube option-error
survival, $0.00247$, while its direct allocation score is wrong-side.  The
lesson is that a variable can survive a readout without being a planning
variable; BEDC-native world state requires the distinction, mediation layer,
sign convention, and selection rule to be certified together.
The standalone runner verdicts are stored in \texttt{reports/} under the
\texttt{fi-022}, \texttt{fi-023}, and \texttt{fi-025} verdict records.
A joint horizon-option test asks whether adding the detection task to the
option-conditioned compute-value model is enough to make the score
allocation-native.  It is not.  The option-only shared-trunk row is selected on
calibration and evaluates near neutral at allocation delta $+0.00017$
$[-0.00014,\ +0.00050]$ with score/error Spearman $0.8371$.  The joint row
with a horizon-q75 head improves mean horizon AUROC to $0.7517$ and raises
score/error Spearman to $0.8737$, but its allocation delta is worse:
$+0.00466$ $[+0.0000003,\ +0.01128]$.  A direct dynamic program using
horizon-risk logits as option scores is also wrong-side, $+0.00703$
$[+0.00227,\ +0.01192]$.  Thus detection supervision can improve readability
without producing intervention value.  The compute-value head must be trained
and selected against the budget-feasible assignment target itself.

\paragraph{Ledger-shaped representation.}
A GPU re-encoder row tests whether the ledger loss can improve the
representation itself rather than only read a fixed one.  A learnable
re-encoder is trained on an RTX 4060 with ledger loss entering the
representation, and a same-capacity predictor-only model is trained as the
control.  The primary criterion freezes each re-encoder after training and
trains a fresh linear probe, separating representation shaping from head
learning.  The fixed-probe $h=1$ comparison is
$$
\Delta\mathrm{AUROC}_{h=1}^{\mathrm{ledger\mbox{-}shaped-pred\mbox{-}only}}
= +0.0038\ [-0.0115,\ 0.0187],
$$
below the predeclared $0.03$ improvement threshold and with the interval
crossing zero.  A label-permutation control is not positive, and one-step
latent prediction is not sacrificed (B/A $0.991$).  This is a closed negative
bound only under this latent export, clean labels, an $8$GB GPU budget, and
this re-encoder class.  The mechanism reading is that the LeWM latent is
already a strong fixed representation on this carrier; a small ledger-shaped
re-encoder does not make the failure signal linearly more readable, consistent
with the allocation bound's $\rho\approx 0.5$ readable-information ceiling.

\paragraph{Survival-regularized world-state contract.}
The preceding rows are still not a complete BEDC-native world model.  A head
on a fixed carrier only proves readout survival; it does not prove that the
world state itself is generated by BEDC distinctions.  The next contract is
therefore stricter: a candidate world state $s_t=(z_t,Q_t,G_t,V_t,M_t)$ must
keep prediction loss within a fixed tolerance of the LeWM carrier while
showing positive survival for the relevant readouts: horizon failure,
selective claim risk, compute-value assignment, OOD failure, and eventually
action outcome.  If prediction parity fails, the object is a monitor or
diagnostic ledger, not a world model.  If survival holds for detection but not
compute value, it is a self-monitoring state but not a planning state.  This
is the experimental bridge from LeWM readout evidence toward a
survival-regularized BEDC-native world model.
The present prediction-parity gate is fail-closed.  At $\epsilon=0.10$, the
LeWM next-latent MSE is $0.06662$, the parity threshold is $0.07328$, and the
native next-latent predictor has MSE $0.87870$, giving a parity margin
$-0.80542$.  Thus the current BEDC-native rows can be read as monitors,
claim ledgers, compute-value assignment tests, and survival audits, but not as
a parity-level native world model.
A direct native-parity candidate makes the same boundary explicit.  It trains
on fixed LeWM latents using a latent window, future action window,
action-validity mask, historical gap proxy, and time features, and is selected
by calibration next-latent MSE before evaluation.  On $539$ evaluation
transitions across $55$ episodes, it improves over trivial persistence
($1.04610$ against $1.26587$, paired difference $-0.21977$
$[-0.27464,\ -0.16797]$), but it is not close to the LeWM predictor
($0.06586$).  The $\epsilon=0.10$ parity threshold is $0.07244$, the
native-to-LeWM ratio is $15.88$, and the parity margin is $-0.97366$
$[-1.03679,\ -0.91201]$.  The direct candidate is therefore a genuine
dynamics learner and a negative world-model-parity result at the same time.
The neighboring checks identify which simple explanations are insufficient.
When the same residual class is trained against the LeWM transition target,
its true-next-latent MSE is $1.05803$, its teacher-imitation MSE is
$1.08923$, and its parity margin is $-0.98398$
$[-1.04988,\ -0.91685]$.  Thus LeWM-transition supervision alone does not
make the native state parity-level.  A recurrent sequence candidate improves
the local native MSE to $0.95181$ and the native-to-LeWM ratio to $14.11$, but
its parity margin remains $-0.87763$ $[-0.95362,\ -0.80311]$.  The admissible
reading is that sequence structure helps, while the prediction-parity gap
still requires a different dynamics-training regime before the BEDC ledger can
be promoted from monitor to world model.
Multi-step dynamics scaling does not remove the boundary.  The strongest local
episode-heldout multi-step row has native $h=1$ MSE $0.74727$ against LeWM
$0.06711$, giving parity margin $-0.67345$, while the transition-split
upper-bound diagnostic has margin $-0.43876$ with confidence upper endpoint
$-0.40961$.  The A100 rows complete the same check under a larger
$25.6$M-parameter runner rather than closing it: the uniform-loss row has native
$h=1$ MSE $0.76081$, native/LeWM ratio $11.34$, and parity margin $-0.68699$
$[-0.73691,\ -0.62952]$; the decay-loss row has native $h=1$ MSE $0.78191$,
native/LeWM ratio $11.65$, and parity margin $-0.70809$
$[-0.75905,\ -0.65106]$.  Thus larger A100 dynamics runs are completed negative
evidence for the current prediction-parity route, not pending evidence for a
parity-level BEDC-native world model.
The matched scale-efficiency diagnostic also fails to produce a positive
scale-up signal.  Treating lower native $h=1$ MSE as better, the uniform
local-minus-A100 gain is $-0.01354$ with interval
$[-0.11714,\ 0.09099]$, and the decay local-minus-A100 gain is $-0.01471$ with
interval $[-0.12364,\ 0.09062]$.  The intervals do not prove that scaling is
intrinsically harmful, but they do fail to justify the current route as a
simple parameter-scale path to prediction parity.
A state-generation contract is therefore registered as a separate synthesis
gate.  Its inputs are the closed scoped compute/control boundary, the negative
prediction-parity boundary, and the non-positive A100 scale-efficiency
diagnostic.  The contract gate evaluates to $1$ with interval $[1,\ 1]$: the
next BEDC-native route must train world-state generation under joint
prediction-parity and compute/control-survival gates.  This is a route
obligation, not evidence that a completed BEDC-native world model already
exists.
The first readiness audit for that route is fail-closed.  It tries to align the
structured compute-value artifact with the multi-step dynamics prediction
artifact by the shared $(\mathrm{episode},t)$ anchor.  Only $106$ anchors across
$8$ episodes overlap, below the declared aligned-export threshold of $200$
anchors and $12$ episodes.  The existing artifacts therefore cannot be safely
stitched into a state-generation training result; the next experiment must
produce a single aligned export carrying prediction targets, compute-value
option labels, and BEDC ledger variables on the same anchors.
That aligned export is now generated as a training substrate.  It uses the
existing train/calibration/eval label split and exports, on identical anchors,
the current state features, next-latent target, predicted rollout trajectory and
validity mask, LeWM one-step MSE, horizon labels, option errors, uniform error,
and true marginal value.  The validation gate is positive with value $1$:
train has $2043$ anchors over $167$ episodes, calibration has $646$ anchors
over $56$ episodes, eval has $634$ anchors over $55$ episodes, and the feature
dimension is $4952$.  This closes the data-substrate step for the
state-generation route; it is not yet a trained state-generation model.
On this substrate, the aligned state-generation candidate uses a shared trunk
with next-latent, option-error, and horizon-failure heads.  Epoch selection is
made on calibration next-latent and option-error losses before eval readout.
On the held-out eval split, the candidate gives native h1 MSE $0.43077$
$[0.41537,\ 0.44770]$, while the matched LeWM h1 MSE is $0.06747$
$[0.06445,\ 0.07072]$; the prediction-parity margin is therefore $-0.35655$
$[-0.37226,\ -0.34105]$.  The horizon head retains a detection signal
(mean AUROC $0.73587$ over $30$ binary horizon labels), and the option-error
score has Spearman correlation $0.66865$ with held-out option error, but the
allocation delta is $0.00395$ $[-0.00355,\ 0.01260]$.  The candidate therefore
validates a joint state-generation measurement path and sharpens the boundary:
prediction parity is still open, and the compute-value signal has not yet
become an allocation-closed BEDC-native world model.
The allocation diagnostic explains where that boundary sits.  Using the fixed
candidate option scores inside the exact-budget dynamic program gives the same
allocation delta $0.00395$ $[-0.00309,\ 0.01200]$ despite score/error
Spearman $0.66865$ and pair accuracy $0.89479$.  Negating the score makes the
allocation worse, $0.03230$ $[0.02588,\ 0.03973]$, so the failure is not a
sign-convention error.  On the same eval labels, the exact-budget oracle closes
allocation at $-0.02412$ $[-0.03015,\ -0.01915]$ with depth counts comparable
to the candidate assignment.  Thus the substrate has allocation value, but
global option-error readability is not enough: the candidate score still lacks
the local option ranking and calibration needed for allocation-closed control.
Training the score directly with an allocation-native centered-error,
pairwise-order, and uniform-margin objective tightens this diagnosis without
closing the gate.  The selected scorer closes the calibration exact-budget
criterion, with calibration allocation delta $-0.00433$ and CI high
$-0.00058$.  On held-out eval, however, its allocation delta is $0.00440$
$[-0.00147,\ 0.01338]$; the calibration-selected score calibration leaves the
same eval assignment, $0.00440$ $[-0.00124,\ 0.01334]$.  The scorer improves
score/error Spearman to $0.81632$ and reaches pair accuracy $1.0$, but its depth
counts concentrate on depths $2$ and $5$ rather than the oracle's more balanced
assignment.  The remaining allocation boundary is therefore not single-anchor
option ordering; it is cross-anchor magnitude calibration under the
episode-level exact-budget constraint.
A post-hoc budget-shadow transform does not remove that boundary.  The
calibration-selected transform chooses the base allocation-native score; on
eval it therefore keeps allocation delta $0.00440$ $[-0.00143,\ 0.01211]$,
with the same depth counts $\{1:42,2:349,3:24,4:5,5:214\}$.  The oracle on the
same eval labels remains closed at $-0.02412$ $[-0.02998,\ -0.01897]$.  The
negative result is useful because it separates post-hoc budget-shadow
calibration from the next required route: the budget signal has to enter the
training objective at the episode level, for example through a dual-price or
exact-budget regret surrogate, rather than being appended after score training.
Putting the episode-level allocation signal into training gives the first
positive movement on this boundary, but still not closure.  A scorer trained
against exact-budget oracle choices, pairwise option order, expected depth, and
centered option error gives eval allocation delta $-0.00130$
$[-0.00601,\ 0.00337]$, improving both the observed value and CI high relative
to the allocation-native baseline $0.00440$ $[-0.00152,\ 0.01226]$.  Its depth
counts $\{1:159,2:110,3:103,4:96,5:166\}$ also move close to the eval oracle
counts $\{1:148,2:114,3:117,4:100,5:155\}$.  The tradeoff is that global
score/error Spearman drops to $0.19282$: the model is no longer a good
option-error reader, but it is a better budget allocator.  Since the CI high is
still positive, the correct conclusion is allocation movement, not
allocation-closed control.
A three-seed replication preserves the same boundary.  Across seeds
$\{431,509,617\}$, the episode objective improves the observed allocation delta
and CI high relative to the allocation-native baseline in all three cases.  The
median observed allocation delta is $-0.00313$, versus the baseline median
$0.00440$, and the median CI high is $0.00291$, versus the baseline median
$0.01220$.  None of the three episode-objective seeds closes allocation, while
the same-label oracle remains closed in all three.  The replicated conclusion is
therefore stable movement toward the budget gate, not budget-closed control.
A direct per-episode dual-price surrogate does not improve this boundary.  It
chooses a calibration-closed shadow-price row, but on held-out eval its
allocation delta is $-0.00047$ $[-0.00824,\ 0.00984]$, weaker than the
exact-budget episode objective $-0.00130$ $[-0.00578,\ 0.00357]$ and still open.
The route remains useful as a negative constraint: the budget signal must enter
training, but a single fitted episode price is not enough to transfer the gate.
A direct structured episode-regret surrogate gives the same warning in a
stronger form.  Training against exact-budget oracle assignments and same-budget
competitor margins selects a near-closed calibration row, but held-out eval moves
back to $0.00010$ $[-0.00706,\ 0.01049]$.  This is still better than the
allocation-native baseline, but worse than both the exact-budget episode
objective and the dual-price row.  The boundary is therefore not merely the lack
of a combinatorial loss on training episodes; it is transfer of the budgeted
assignment structure to held-out episodes.
Seed aggregation confirms that this is not ordinary seed variance.  Averaging
the three episode-objective seed scores gives allocation delta $-0.00280$
$[-0.00915,\ 0.00407]$, and normalized or median seed aggregation remains open.
The best row is still a single seed, with seed~617 at $-0.00359$
$[-0.00932,\ 0.00157]$.  The seeds agree on a majority choice for $91.3\%$ of
eval anchors, but the majority choice matches the exact-budget oracle on only
$35.8\%$.  Thus the failure mode is stable held-out assignment error, not
unstable optimization noise removable by ensembling.
Episode-balanced sampling does not remove the same transfer error.  Training
the exact-budget episode objective with batches balanced across training
episodes selects a calibration-closed row, but held-out eval is $0.00033$
$[-0.00822,\ 0.01101]$.  This remains better than the allocation-native
baseline but worse than the original episode objective and the best replicated
seed.  Hence the missing ingredient is not only episode reweighting; the model
needs an invariant assignment rule that transfers across episodes.
An episode-level anatomy diagnostic localizes the transfer error.  Across the
best replicated seed, the original episode objective, seed averaging,
episode-balanced sampling, direct regret, and dual-price rows, the top harmful
episode sets have a union of only seven episode identifiers, with episodes
$81$, $145$, and $213$ shared by all rows.  The top harmful slice accounts for
at least $88.9\%$ of positive episode damage in every row.  The boundary is
therefore concentrated in recurring hard held-out episodes, which makes
hard-episode or cluster-transfer objectives the next relevant route.
A direct hard-episode contribution diagnostic sharpens this localization.
Conditioning only after fixed score generation on the seven-episode union
$\{81,115,145,193,201,213,277\}$ leaves $555$ non-hard anchors and $79$ hard
anchors.  Across the best seed, original episode objective, seed mean,
episode-balanced, direct-regret, and dual-price rows, every non-hard slice
has allocation CI high below zero; the observed non-hard deltas range from
$-0.00576$ to $-0.00859$.  The hard-only slice is harmful in every row, with
observed deltas from $0.02685$ to $0.05657$.  This does not yield an
allocation-native policy, because the slice is identified from held-out
anatomy after fixed scores, but it localizes the remaining boundary to
recurring episode regimes rather than average eval behavior.
A pre-label cluster-coverage diagnostic rules out a simpler explanation.
Episode summaries built only from allowed carrier context features and time
indices are embedded using train/cal statistics, and $12$ cluster centers are
fit on train/cal episodes before assigning eval episodes.  The seven hard
episodes occupy three clusters, with a size-matched permutation lower-tail
$p=0.0596$, so there is weak compactness signal.  However, those hard clusters
are not under-covered: the mean train/cal cluster count for hard episodes is
higher by $8.49$ episodes, with lower-tail $p=0.964$.  Thus simple
feature-space coverage reweighting is not the mechanism; the remaining route
requires richer invariant episode descriptors or perturbation features that
separate hard assignment regimes from merely nearby carrier summaries.
Fixed scorer and rollout descriptors also fail to expose the hard regime.
Using only fixed scorer margins, scorer disagreement, exact-budget assignment
flip rates, and predicted-rollout geometry, no tested decision-boundary
descriptor separates the seven hard episodes under permutation tests.  The
strongest descriptor is the best-seed score entropy, but its permutation
$p$-value is only $0.257$; row argmin disagreement, exact-budget assignment
flip rate, and rollout curvature are weaker.  Thus the remaining allocation
boundary is not visible as ordinary scorer uncertainty or rollout instability.
The next state-generation route must introduce richer causal perturbations or
environment-state descriptors rather than another post-hoc transform of the
same internal scores.
Using the source two-rooms export to expose explicit environment-state fields
gives the first positive mechanism signal for the hard slice.  Descriptors
constructed from agent and target positions, room membership, midline crossing,
reward, and action magnitudes separate the same seven hard episodes after
fixed descriptor construction.  The strongest descriptor is the episode mean
agent $y$ coordinate, with hard episodes lower than the remaining eval episodes
($60.0$ versus $87.5$) and permutation $p=0.0160$.  Room membership also
separates weakly: hard episodes are in different rooms more often ($0.939$
versus $0.769$, $p=0.0760$), equivalently same-room occupancy is lower
($0.061$ versus $0.231$, $p=0.0736$).  This still does not close allocation
or define a deployable policy, but it changes the next route: the hard
assignment boundary is externally geometric, not merely a hidden uncertainty
signal inside the scorer.

The split-conformal counting core used by the gap-ledger readout is
machine-checked in a mathlib-free Lean~4 package whose theorems depend on

\paragraph{Calibrated selection.}
Replacing the declared-OK rule by conservative thresholds chosen on a
calibration split over an $\alpha$ grid makes the clean-evaluation guarantee
observable but narrow.  At $\alpha = 0.10$, three of the four monitors satisfy
the target on clean evaluation: the post-hoc monitor has clean risk $0.103$,
the perturbation-aware trained ledger has risk $0.115$ and the highest usable
coverage $0.422$, while the OOD-aware logistic monitor violates the clean case
itself with risk $0.176$.  Under distribution shift, the guarantee fails in
two modes.  Clean-only monitors fail dangerously, declaring OK for samples in
the $0.92$--$0.98$ score range under strong color shift while the actual risk
is $1.0$.  Perturbation-aware monitors fail safely, with usable coverage
shrinking to zero through abstention.  At $\alpha = 0.05$, the calibration
split has no feasible threshold, and the record fails closed.

\paragraph{Cross-environment replication.}

itself as the gap-head feature, with no \texttt{tworooms}-specific probe
features, the same split seed, the same $\tau$ definition, the same

$[0.568,\ 0.652]$, while the matched-random control has $0.507$
$[0.458,\ 0.554]$; the detection criterion is interval-separated.  Its UER
moves from $0.248$ to $0.210$ $[0.183,\ 0.233]$, overlapping its vanilla
baseline, so the strict two-criterion rule does not grant the row.  For
\texttt{quentinll/lewm-pusht}, the earlier contiguous episode block with
