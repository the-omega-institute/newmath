\section{Fixed-Checkpoint Gap-Ledger Instantiation: LeWorldModel}
\label{sec:lewm-instantiation}

The public-baseline registry of \autoref{sec:relation-to-jepa} names two
JEPA-family targets.  The V-JEPA2-AC rows ask adapter and latent-prediction
questions on a fixed checkpoint.  The LeWorldModel rows recorded here ask the
gap-ledger question on a fixed checkpoint: can a head trained only on the
checkpoint's own latent stream predict where that checkpoint's world model is
about to be wrong, and can the ledger semantics survive contact with the
checkpoint's real failure surface?

The carrier is the public LeWorldModel \texttt{tworooms} release
\cite{leworldmodel}, loaded without the stable-worldmodel stack (plain
\texttt{torch} plus the public ViT encoder weights), with real
\texttt{tworooms} frames streamed from the public dataset: $4991$ valid
transitions over $278$ episodes, latent dimension $192$.  All rows use an
episode-level $60/20/20$ split, episode-level bootstrap confidence intervals,
and a world-model-native failure truth: a transition counts as a failure when
the checkpoint predictor's own autoregressive latent rollout error exceeds
the clean train-split $75$th percentile.  The protocol is fail-closed:
evaluation slices that become single-class are reported as unidentifiable
rather than as detection results.

\paragraph{Post-hoc gap head on the frozen checkpoint.}
The first row is the post-hoc construction that the four-system comparison
uses as its report baseline, evaluated on the real checkpoint: a logistic gap
head reading only the learned representation, with a matched-random control
of equal capacity trained on permuted labels.  The gap head predicts the
checkpoint's own failures well above its control,
$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{gap head AUROC} & 0.731 & [0.692,\ 0.774],\\
\text{matched-random AUROC} & 0.472 & [0.427,\ 0.518],\\
\text{unlogged error} & 0.231 \rightarrow 0.168 & [0.142,\ 0.191],
\end{array}
$$
with both interval separations holding under the episode bootstrap.  The gap
features include an embedding-to-state linear-recovery score, so the
instantiation stays in contact with the identifiability question of
\cite{lejepa-world-model}.

\paragraph{Trained ledger head on the same carrier.}
The second row transports the paper's central contrast --- trained ledger
output versus post-hoc report --- onto the same fixed checkpoint.  A small
causal transformer ($2$ layers, width $64$, about $9.3\times 10^{4}$
parameters) reads windows of six (latent, action) tokens and is trained
jointly on a ledger head and a next-latent head.  Under the same dual
criteria the trained head is positive,
$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{trained ledger AUROC} & 0.675 & [0.636,\ 0.711],\\
\text{matched-random AUROC} & 0.543 & [0.499,\ 0.588],\\
\text{unlogged error} & 0.231 \rightarrow 0.132 & [0.110,\ 0.156],
\end{array}
$$
and a component ablation shows the joint next-latent loss has no measurable
effect on this carrier ($0.671$ AUROC without it).  The honest reading cuts
both ways.  The trained head transfers to a real checkpoint, but it does not
beat the post-hoc logistic head on detection AUROC ($0.675$ versus $0.731$,
intervals overlapping), while reaching a lower operating-point unlogged error
($0.132$ versus $0.168$, intervals overlapping).  On this carrier the
trained-versus-post-hoc contrast is not settled by victory; what the executed
rows establish is transfer, a live matched-random capacity artifact ($0.543$
slightly above chance from temporal windows alone), and a measured
operating-point trade.

\paragraph{Perturbation boundary.}
Under visual perturbation of the evaluation stream (color shift, occlusion,
brightness, background tint, Gaussian noise; the brittleness setting of the
stable-worldmodel line), the clean-only post-hoc head keeps working under
mild background tint (AUROC $0.72$) and collapses under strong shifts, where
almost every transition fails and the slice becomes single-class.  Gap-head
training on perturbed latents generalizes across families in a leave-one-out
test: held-out occlusion $0.72$, brightness $0.67$, background tint $0.67$,
Gaussian noise $0.63$, with color shift remaining unidentifiable.  At the
strongest perturbations the OOD-aware heads declare gap on nearly everything,
which is the conservative failure mode the ledger semantics ask for.  The
trained ledger transformer's own clean-trained perturbation arm reproduces
the same boundary shape rather than escaping it: detection persists under
background tint at every tested strength (AUROC $0.62$--$0.65$) and at mild
occlusion, brightness, and Gaussian-noise strengths ($0.62$--$0.71$), while
the strongest slices drive the failure rate to one, become single-class, and
are reported fail-closed.  An OOD-aware variant of the trained head, trained
on clean plus perturbed train-split windows under the same protocol,
reproduces the OOD-aware pattern of the post-hoc line at the architecture
level: leave-one-out transfer to held-out families is positive on four of
five (pooled AUROC: occlusion $0.75$, background tint $0.70$, brightness
$0.66$, Gaussian noise $0.59$; color shift stays single-class), the strict
final-strength claim holds on background tint (AUROC $0.77$), at the
strongest slices the head conservatively declares gap on nearly everything
(unlogged error $\rightarrow 0$), and clean evaluation is not degraded
(AUROC $0.675 \rightarrow 0.721$ with overlapping intervals).

\paragraph{Probing what the ledger reads.}
Three probes bound the nature of the failure signal.  First, the trained
head's own next-latent prediction is far weaker than the checkpoint predictor
on the same transitions and metric (mean squared error $0.879$ versus
$0.067$; the small head wins on $0.1\%$ of transitions), so the ledger's
detection ability does not ride on superior dynamics.  Second, the signal is
not a state-recovery deficit: per-transition linear and local ($k=20$
nearest-neighbour) state-recovery residuals do not predict failure (AUROC
$0.455$ $[0.407,\ 0.503]$ and $0.466$ $[0.418,\ 0.515]$, against $0.731$ for
the gap head), so the naive operationalization of the identifiability
question --- the model fails where its latent encodes state poorly --- is
rejected on this carrier.  Third, one-step awareness does not transfer to
multi-step budget allocation: distributing rollout depth by ledger score at
matched compute \emph{raises} per-step rollout error against a uniform
allocation ($+0.013$ $[+0.005,\ +0.021]$), while an oracle ranked by true
future error lowers it ($-0.016$), so the allocation lever exists but the
one-step ledger score selects the wrong direction for it.
The split-conformal counting core used by the gap-ledger readout is
machine-checked in a mathlib-free Lean~4 package whose theorems depend on
zero axioms (no \texttt{propext}, no \texttt{Classical.choice}); the full
$\alpha$-statistical miscoverage bound (rank quantile plus exchangeability)
is recorded as open rather than claimed.

\paragraph{Boundary of this instantiation.}
One public checkpoint (\texttt{tworooms}), one latent export, episode
bootstrap rather than independent world seeds, and no claim of benchmark
superiority.  These rows are an external baseline run beyond the
fixed-checkpoint MiniGrid row, in gap-ledger form; they do not execute
LeWorldModel's official benchmark setting and do not produce the
latent-prediction and rollout-or-planning fields of the public-baseline
comparison record, so the comparison record carries no LeWorldModel score
row.
