\paragraph{Writeback discipline.}
The paper follows the same separation discipline as the BioReality writeback
pipeline: runtime records are not copied into the manuscript body.  They are
first compiled into a durable paper-writeback packet with source, pattern,
classifier, stability, metrics, ledger, not-claimed, artifact, and fact-owner
fields.  The manuscript writes only the claims admitted by that packet and
keeps unsupported candidates as ledger or cannot-claim rows.  In the current
packet, the admitted latent operational claim is
\texttt{door\_key\_context\_visible}; the source-gap predicates
\texttt{has\_key}, \texttt{door\_open\_or\_unlocked}, and
\texttt{goal\_reachable\_with\_current\_state} remain outside the admitted
latent-meaning claim boundary.

\paragraph{Source specification.}
The source is \texttt{boundary-gated-ou-world}.  Its latent dimension is $2$,
with sample count $768$, $\rho=0.84$, radius $1.0$, and gap width $0.14$.
The nonlinear observation is \texttt{sinusoidal-parabolic-shear}.

\paragraph{Pattern specification.}
The pattern is \texttt{latent-plus-distinction-plus-gap}.  It asks for three
readbacks from the same world process: continuous state recovery, operational
inside-boundary distinction, and boundary gap detection.

\paragraph{Public JEPA-family checkpoint evaluation.}
The public JEPA-family checkpoint boundary includes a CUDA evaluation of the
public V-JEPA2-AC Giant checkpoint.  The adapter loads
\texttt{vjepa2\_ac\_vit\_giant} from the public V-JEPA2 hub with
\texttt{pretrained=False}, because the observed hub source points its default
\texttt{VJEPA\_BASE\_URL} at \texttt{localhost}.  The run then loads the
public checkpoint through the artifact pointer recorded in
\texttt{reports/bedc\_jepa\_public\_ac\_giant\_adapter.json}.
The CUDA environment records \texttt{torch} \texttt{2.5.1+cu121}, CUDA
runtime \texttt{12.1}, and one \texttt{NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU}
with $8585216000$ bytes of device memory.

The adapter maps the same boundary-gated tiny world into a two-frame
$256 \times 256$ RGB video plus seven-float action and state contracts, runs
the V-JEPA2-AC encoder and action-conditioned predictor, and trains only
linear readout heads for the declared distinction and gap labels.  The public
AC Giant adapter records train count $64$, test count $64$, distinction
accuracy $0.718750$, gap AUROC $0.562842$, unlogged error $0.250000$,
certified coverage $0.968750$, and debt $0.290557$.  The matching
BEDC-objective row records distinction accuracy $0.988281$, gap AUROC
$0.999223$, unlogged error $0.000000$, certified coverage $0.912760$, and
debt $0.007459$.  The public AC Giant minus BEDC deltas are therefore
$-0.269531$ for distinction accuracy, $-0.436381$ for gap AUROC,
$0.250000$ for unlogged error, $0.055990$ for certified coverage, and
$0.283098$ for debt.

This supplies V-JEPA2-AC Giant checkpoint evidence for the declared adapter
scope.  It does not establish public benchmark superiority, large-scale
real-world world-modeling, or formal neural-network proof claims.

\paragraph{Debt items.}
The current debt items are scale debt and public-baseline debt.  Scale debt
means the learned-transition and object-intervention benchmarks are small.
Public-baseline debt means the executed public JEPA-family comparison is an
adapter-scope checkpoint evaluation, not a native V-JEPA2-AC task comparison.
The public MiniGrid studies have executable DoorKey contracts.  The transition
study samples Gymnasium image observations and discrete actions.  The
benchmark study computes a public DoorKey readback/gap metric contract with
distinction accuracy, gap AUROC, unlogged error, certified coverage, and
scope-relative debt.  The public MiniGrid external result records an executed
32-sample DoorKey study with distinction accuracy $1.000000$, gap AUROC
$1.000000$, unlogged error $0.000000$, certified coverage $0.375000$, and
debt $0.062500$.  The native public MiniGrid study runs
\texttt{MiniGrid-DoorKey-8x8-v0} with the same public image/action stream for
S0/S1/S2/S3.  It records train count $128$, test count $128$, S0 unlogged
error $0.164063$, S3 unlogged error $0.039063$, S0 debt $0.165625$, S3 debt
$0.163102$, S0 gap AUROC $0.500000$, and S3 gap AUROC $0.590738$.  Its
audited two-step planning sweep records high-gap state rate $0.843750$ and
success $0.968750$ at $\lambda_g=0$, versus high-gap state rate $0.156250$
and success $0.562500$ at the best recorded risk point $\lambda_g=0.5$.
The five-seed native MiniGrid sweep records mean S0-minus-S3 unlogged-error
reduction $0.067188$, mean S3-minus-S0 gap AUROC gain $0.185662$, unlogged
error win rate $0.600000$, and gap AUROC win rate $1.000000$.  It also records
mean S0-minus-S3 debt change $-0.041377$ and debt win rate $0.400000$, so the
public MiniGrid debt direction is not established.  The same sweep records mean
high-gap planning reduction $0.393750$ and mean success reduction $0.337500$,
which treats gap-aware planning as a risk-success tradeoff rather than a free
planning improvement.  The reproducibility artifact set covers the declared
evidence scope, but it does not establish the external baseline comparison.
The public debt decomposition explains the single-run native MiniGrid debt
result: S3 reduces silent debt from $0.065625$ to $0.015625$, while
coverage debt rises from $0.000000$ to $0.065625$.  Thus the public setting
supports the statement that BEDC-JEPA reduces silent false claims, but the
current threshold policy pays for that reduction with abstention/coverage
debt.  The certified-coverage sweep records a best S3 threshold $0.80$ with
debt $0.141227$ and certified coverage $1.000000$, while the
risk-constrained planning artifact records that tight risk budgets can avoid
high-gap selected states only by producing a large no-certified-plan rate.
These artifacts classify the public MiniGrid weakness as calibration and
coverage debt, not as an established public total-debt result.

\paragraph{External evaluation status.}
The evidence audit records three evaluated scopes for the declared study:
public checkpoint evaluation, native public MiniGrid readback, and a
reproducibility artifact set.  The local torch objective seed sweep, local
visual planning benchmark, cluttered object-counterfactual benchmark, public
MiniGrid execution, public native MiniGrid S0/S1/S2/S3 benchmark and seed
sweep, and public V-JEPA2-AC Giant CUDA checkpoint adapter evaluation satisfy
their declared evidence criteria.  The current artifact set is therefore suitable
for external evaluation as a bounded evidence artifact with public checkpoint and
native public benchmark evidence, not as a public benchmark superiority claim.
The reproduction-command artifact records the MiniGrid export command, the
public native MiniGrid benchmark command, the AC Giant CUDA checkpoint
evaluation command pair, the V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid latent-prediction command,
and the evidence-audit command needed to reproduce the artifact-level checks.
The older baseline external result remains an unevaluated import scope for
external public benchmark metrics.  The CUDA adapter record and the MiniGrid
latent-prediction record are the executed checkpoint artifacts for the selected
V-JEPA 2-AC candidate.  The native V-JEPA2-AC evaluation-scope record remains
necessary because these artifacts are fixed-checkpoint contact protocols, not
the checkpoint's official benchmark reproduction.

\paragraph{Quality-backend projection.}
The quality-backend candidate is a non-canonical projection of the same
BEDC-JEPA evidence records into a backend-style quality interface.  It exposes
the world-state contract, the S0/S1/S2/S3 readback pattern, selected metric
cells, and ledger rows for source, classifier, stability, generalization, and
mechanism debt.  Its forbidden surfaces explicitly exclude model-runner
execution, canonical writer execution, terminal verdict projection, and raw
benchmark regeneration.  Thus the candidate is an audit projection over the
declared evidence scope, not a new experiment, not a canonical BEDC backend,
and not a claim of global model quality.

\paragraph{Latent Claim Certificate Protocol.}
The latent-claim certificate artifacts instantiate the protocol in
Section~\ref{sec:latent-claim-certificate}.  The current certificate artifact
applies the fail-closed rule to the native MiniGrid feature carrier for the
declared DoorKey readback predicate.  It records source gaps for operational
names whose required source traces are not present in the current evidence
record: \texttt{has\_key}, \texttt{door\_open\_or\_unlocked}, and
\texttt{goal\_reachable\_with\_current\_state}.  The conformal audit therefore
does not assert that every latent distinction is interpretable.  It asserts
only that each candidate distinction ends in one of two states: a certified
singleton operational claim under the declared risk level, or an explicit
gap ledger entry with coverage, source, or stability debt.
