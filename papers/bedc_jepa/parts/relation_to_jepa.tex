\section{Relation to JEPA World-Model Work}
\label{sec:relation-to-jepa}

The JEPA family supplies the comparison surface for this paper.  LeCun's
autonomous-intelligence program places learned world models at the center of
prediction, planning, and action \cite{lecun-ami}.  I-JEPA shows that
representation-space prediction can support semantic image representations
without reconstructing every pixel detail \cite{ijepa}.  LeWorldModel moves
the same principle toward end-to-end pixel world modeling with a compact
latent predictor and Gaussian regularization \cite{leworldmodel}.  Causal-JEPA
adds object-level latent masking so that interaction-dependent prediction
enters the learning objective \cite{causal-jepa}.

The most direct theoretical comparison is the LeJEPA identifiability result
\cite{lejepa-world-model}.  That result asks when alignment plus Gaussian
regularization recovers latent world variables, and it connects such recovery
to latent-space planning.  BEDC-JEPA asks a different question.  Once a world
model has a latent carrier, which operational distinctions can be trained as
part of the state, and which failure surfaces must be carried by an explicit
gap head?

\paragraph{Complementarity.}
The LeJEPA question is latent recovery:
$$
\text{observation} \longrightarrow \text{recoverable latent coordinate}.
$$
The BEDC-JEPA question is ledgered world-state formation:
$$
\text{observation}
\longrightarrow
\text{latent coordinate}
+ \text{operational distinction}
+ \text{gap ledger}.
$$
The paper therefore does not claim that BEDC explains an already trained
LeJEPA representation.  It proposes a training target in which the distinction
and the gap are outputs of the world model itself.

\paragraph{Public baseline registry and checkpoint evaluation.}
The quality lab records a public JEPA-family baseline registry rather than
treating the baseline choice as prose.  The selected first comparison target
is V-JEPA 2-AC, because its public code is action-conditioned and therefore
closest to a world-model setting with observation/action streams.  The second
candidate is LeWorldModel with its LeJEPA-style baseline path, because it
directly targets stable end-to-end JEPA world modeling from pixels.  The
registry fixes the expected observation/action input, latent prediction or
rollout output, and BEDC comparison fields.

The present evidence includes a checkpoint adapter evaluation for the selected
V-JEPA2-AC Giant target.  It loads the public
\texttt{vjepa2-ac-vitg.pt} checkpoint, runs the encoder and action-conditioned
predictor on a two-frame rendering of the boundary-gated world, and trains only
linear readouts for the declared distinction and gap labels.  This evaluation
is not a native public benchmark reproduction.  It asks whether the public
action-conditioned checkpoint, under the same declared tiny-world adapter
scope, already carries the distinction and gap readback needed by the BEDC
evidence scope.  In that scope, BEDC-JEPA records unlogged error
$0.000000$ and debt $0.007459$, while the V-JEPA2-AC Giant adapter records
unlogged error $0.250000$ and debt $0.290557$.

The public native MiniGrid study supplies a second evidence scope: it runs
the S0/S1/S2/S3 ablation on \texttt{MiniGrid-DoorKey-8x8-v0} with the same
public image/action stream and records a JEPA-style S0 control row beside
BEDC readback metrics.  In that public setting, S0 records unlogged error
$0.164063$ and debt $0.165625$, while S3 records unlogged error $0.039063$
and debt $0.163102$.  The audited two-step planning sweep also exposes a
risk-success tradeoff: the best recorded risk point lowers high-gap state
selection by $0.687500$ while lowering success by $0.406250$.  The five-seed
MiniGrid sweep records mean S0-minus-S3 unlogged-error reduction $0.067188$
and mean S3-minus-S0 gap AUROC gain $0.185662$, with unlogged-error win rate
$0.600000$ and gap AUROC win rate $1.000000$.  It also records mean
S0-minus-S3 debt change $-0.041377$ and debt win rate $0.400000$, so the
public MiniGrid study supports the UER and gap-AUROC directions but not an
established debt-improvement claim.  The mean planning high-gap reduction is
$0.393750$, with mean success reduction $0.337500$.

The separate public-baseline comparison record imports the fixed-checkpoint
MiniGrid row with candidate id, repository commit, checkpoint identity,
benchmark name, latent-prediction score, and the declared near-native score.  The
V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid latent-prediction record evaluates the public Giant
checkpoint on the same MiniGrid image/action stream as a fixed-checkpoint
study: after fitting only a train-split linear alignment from predictor
features to encoder features, it records latent-prediction score $0.981246$,
linear-aligned $R^2$ $0.980652$, and flattened correlation $0.998852$.  This
is still not an official V-JEPA2-AC benchmark reproduction or a public
benchmark superiority result.  Thus the AC Giant adapter supplies
checkpoint-scope evidence, the MiniGrid study supplies native public benchmark
evidence for BEDC readback, and the latent-prediction record supplies
fixed-checkpoint MiniGrid evidence for V-JEPA2-AC.
The near-native V-JEPA2-AC MiniGrid record combines the fixed-checkpoint
latent-prediction score with LCCP readback metrics, checkpoint identity,
stream split, and execution commands in one record.  It is evidence for a
declared fixed-checkpoint protocol rather than for the checkpoint's official
benchmark setting.  The second registry candidate carries its own executed
contact: \autoref{sec:lewm-instantiation} records a fixed-checkpoint
gap-ledger instantiation on the public LeWorldModel \texttt{tworooms}
release, with a post-hoc gap head, a trained ledger-head transformer, a
visual perturbation boundary, and a machine-checked coverage-counting core.

\paragraph{Quality-lab evidence scope.}
The local quality-lab material supplies an executable evidence envelope, not a
second semantic source for BEDC.  Its role is to keep the Python side honest:
source, pattern, classifier, stability setting, metrics, ledger gaps, debt
items, and record pointers are recorded in a reproducible evidence record.  BEDC
semantics remain in the paper and Lean tracks; the lab only reports the
observed values produced by the protocol.
