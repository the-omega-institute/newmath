\section{Autoresearch loop: gated, adversarially verified findings}
\label{sec:bedc-jepa-autoresearch}

The instantiation studies were extended by an autoresearch loop that treats
every candidate finding as untrusted until two independent layers clear it.  An
open hypothesis is compiled into a deterministic runner; the runner is executed
twice at a fixed seed and must produce byte-identical output; the resulting
verdict passes through mechanical gates (anchor reproduction, two-run
reproducibility, a confidence-interval criterion, a measured-scope overclaim
guard, and an executed-provenance check); and only then does an independent
adversarial review judge whether the experiment is methodologically sound.  A
finding is authoritative only when the mechanical gates and the adversarial
review both clear it, and is held back otherwise (fail-closed).

The two layers are not redundant.  The mechanical gates are necessary but not
sufficient: one candidate placed the label-defining future error inside its own
feature vector, passed every mechanical gate with an apparent
$\mathrm{AUROC} \approx 0.98$, and was demoted only when the adversarial review
identified the leakage; once the leaked variable was excluded the same
hypothesis returned an honest unidentifiable result.  The pipeline, its gates,
the verified runners, and the verdict records are at
\path{experiments/lewm_gap_ledger/autoresearch/}; the authoritative findings are
summarized at
\path{experiments/lewm_gap_ledger/autoresearch/findings/verified_findings.md}
and the adversarial verdicts at
\path{experiments/lewm_gap_ledger/autoresearch/findings/adversarial_verdicts.jsonl}.

\paragraph{Quality-lab claim interface.}
The overclaim screen for the autoresearch loop consumes the quality-lab
forbidden positive claim term list as its single term source, covering
\texttt{full-lejepa}, \texttt{global-quality},
\texttt{full-tensor-namecert}, and \texttt{llm-behavior}.  The loop keeps its
own runner and adversarial-review path, but exports its authoritative findings
as canonical claim-verdict pointer rows for quality-lab consumption at
\path{experiments/lewm_gap_ledger/autoresearch/findings/canonical_claim_verdicts.jsonl}.
Thus the relationship is a consumer relation to the quality-lab claim-verdict
bus, not a second verdict system with its own positive-claim vocabulary.

\paragraph{Verified findings on the fixed carriers.}
The current verified findings on the fixed carriers are recorded in
\path{experiments/lewm_gap_ledger/autoresearch/findings/verified_findings.md},
which is the single ledger source for the number, status, evidence pointer, and
claim text of those findings.  The paper text does not cache those counts; it
uses the ledger as the authority for the current positive, bounded, and
fail-closed findings.

\paragraph{Native-predictor derisk.}
A small BEDC-native next-latent predictor trained on fixed tworooms latents
beats a trivial persistence baseline (evaluation mean squared error $0.880$
against $1.467$, paired interval below zero), so the recipe learns genuine
dynamics.  It remains about $12.6\times$ the LeWorldModel predictor's own
next-latent error ($0.880$ against $0.070$), and enlarging the native predictor
narrows that gap by only $0.011$.  The signal is therefore that closing the
prediction-axis gap is not a matter of scale alone for this recipe: the
objective or architecture is the binding factor, which bounds what a larger
compute budget alone would buy.

\paragraph{Three-tier BEDC-native readout evaluation.}
Across the verified findings the BEDC-native ledger separates into three tiers
against a fixed LeWorldModel carrier.  For \emph{detection}, the outcome is
config dependent across two distinct paired comparisons.  In one paired
episode-bootstrap comparison a native horizon-indexed ledger config exceeds its
post-hoc scorer by $+0.050\ [0.004,\ 0.100]$.  In a separate hardening
comparison against the strongest post-hoc probe -- a logistic gap head at
$\mathrm{AUROC} \approx 0.744$ -- a standard horizon-ledger config does not
establish superiority (native $0.737$ against $0.744$, paired gap interval
crossing zero).  The integrated A100 matrix then moves the detection frontier:
the full rollout-internal, prediction, tail-risk, and OOD-gated row reaches
$h=1$ AUROC $0.78165$, exceeding the recorded strongest-probe scalar $0.74399$,
and its paired comparison against the matched in-runner current-latent post-hoc
reference gives $+0.04734\ [0.00319,\ 0.09463]$.  This is scalar hardening past
the strongest-probe record plus an interval-separated paired gain against the
runner reference; it is not a direct paired interval against the $0.744$ scalar.
The matrix identifies rollout-internal features as the lever, and its pred-only
control collapses to $h=1$ $0.48784$ with paired $\Delta=-0.24648$
$[-0.30930,\ -0.18052]$.  For \emph{selective prediction},
the split-conformal rule on the native score holds realized
failure-among-admitted risk below the target $\alpha = 0.10$ only at a
conservative admitted coverage near $10\%$; at usable coverage the guarantee is
marginal.  For \emph{allocation}, no post-hoc transform of the fixed latent --
neither a readout head nor a tail-weighted re-encoder -- and no small
from-scratch encoder brings the allocation delta against a uniform budget below
zero, with the readable rank signal saturating near $\rho \approx 0.5$.  Scaling
the from-scratch encoder on a GPU across a $1200\times$ range of capacity (from
$0.14$M to $165$M parameters), every run with a verified healthy non-collapsed
latent of effective rank $54$--$112$, leaves the allocation rank correlation
flat in $\rho \in [0.26,\ 0.39]$ -- never approaching that ceiling, and
flat-to-decreasing along the matched-batch series -- so the bound is
encoder-capacity-invariant rather than a small-budget artifact
(\path{experiments/lewm_gap_ledger/encoder_scale_derisk/}).  Training the full
integrated objective end to end -- a single encoder shaped jointly by
next-latent prediction, a horizon-indexed detection head, and the tail-weighted
allocation loss, behind an out-of-distribution fail-closed gate -- does not move
this delta below zero either.  In the A100 integrated matrix, the full row has
allocation delta $+0.00786$ $[-0.00243,\ +0.02061]$ and
$\rho=0.421$ $[0.321,\ 0.513]$; its selective admission risk is $0.1165$ at
$\alpha=0.10$, so neither selective admission nor allocation passes its
criterion.  Detection is therefore an internalization that hardens past the
strongest-probe scalar under the integrated row, selective prediction is a
conservative-coverage result, and allocation remains bounded when it is treated
as a downstream failure-score or integrated-detector ranking problem.

The bounded allocation frontier is not treated as a request for another
failure-score ranker.  The research loop carries a separate compute-value axis:
a valid evidence packet must name candidate compute options $b$ and provide
direct option-level labels
$$
MV(t,b) = E_{\mathrm{uniform}}(t) - E_b(t),
$$
with the allocation head trained and evaluated against that marginal value
rather than against $P(e_{t,h}>\tau_{h,q})$.  The corresponding runner accepts
only an artifact with episode ids, option errors, uniform errors, and predicted
marginal values; absent those arrays it emits a fail-closed verdict.  Thus the
automation continues the BEDC-native world-model program by asking for
intervention-value evidence, not by retrieving one named experiment or
relabeling detection as planning.
The first artifact for this axis is deliberately an oracle readback: it writes
the option-level label matrix for rollout depths $1$, $3$, and $5$ on $634$
evaluation anchors from $55$ episodes, and fills \texttt{predicted\_mv} with
the true per-step marginal value only to test whether the target itself can
drive the balanced allocation gate.  That readback gives allocation delta
$-0.01290$ $[-0.01851,\ -0.00806]$ with $MV$ Spearman $1.0$.  It is therefore
evidence that the missing variable is allocation-relevant, while the trained
compute-value model remains unclaimed until \texttt{predicted\_mv} is produced
by a model that has not read evaluation errors.
The local 4060 readout supplies that stricter test for a small model family.
Ridge, MLP, and scalar RankNet readers are trained without evaluation labels
and selected on calibration.  RankNet is selected, reaches evaluation
$MV$ Spearman $0.4499$ $[0.3651,\ 0.5272]$, but remains wrong-side on
allocation: $+0.00822$ $[+0.00053,\ +0.01738]$.  The signal is therefore
partly readable, but the readout still does not supply an allocation-native
policy.
A policy-oriented local RTX~4060 run trains the scorer directly on the
episode-balanced top/bottom assignment induced by the same marginal-value
labels.  It slightly improves the allocation delta relative to the scalar
RankNet row, $+0.00675$ $[+0.00179,\ +0.01146]$ versus $+0.00822$
$[+0.00065,\ +0.01656]$, but it remains on the wrong side.  Its global
$MV$ Spearman is lower ($0.3413$), even though the allocation delta is better,
so global marginal-value rank correlation is not a sufficient model-selection
statistic for the balanced budget gate.  The oracle under the identical gate
still gives $-0.01290$ $[-0.01922,\ -0.00830]$, while a random reference gives
$+0.02094$ $[+0.01211,\ +0.03207]$ and an inverted oracle gives $+0.05886$
$[+0.04546,\ +0.07605]$.  The target has usable intervention signal; the open
model problem is stable episode-local assignment, not merely per-anchor
compute-value readability.
A stability grid over seeds $3,7,11$ and epoch budgets $1,5,20,100$ keeps that
reading.  Selecting by calibration allocation delta chooses seed $3$ at $20$
epochs and gives evaluation allocation delta $+0.00184$
$[-0.00297,\ +0.00687]$, close to the decision boundary but still fail-closed.
Across all $12$ runs, no evaluation interval has upper endpoint below zero; the
observed deltas range from $+0.00162$ to $+0.01024$, with median $+0.00734$.
This supports episode-local policy training as the right pressure point, but
also shows that the present scorer is not stable enough to claim
allocation-native control.
An episode decomposition of the selected stability scorer localizes the
remaining gap.  Averaged by episode, the policy has delta $+0.00137$ against
$+0.00698$ for the scalar RankNet and $-0.01280$ for the oracle.  The policy
improves the scalar RankNet episode mean by $0.00560$, but it still misses the
oracle by $0.01417$ on average.  The missed-oracle mass is tail concentrated:
the worst episode accounts for $13.94\%$ of the positive missed mass, the worst
five for $41.62\%$, and the worst ten for $61.78\%$.  Thus the next allocation
objective should be tail-episode-aware, not only globally listwise.
The direct tail-episode weighting test is negative.  It weights train episodes
by their train-split oracle gain before fitting the same policy scorer, using a
$3\times 3$ seed/epoch grid and calibration allocation selection.  The selected
row evaluates at $+0.00878$ $[+0.00322,\ +0.01536]$, worse than the unweighted
episode-policy reference $+0.00184$ $[-0.00309,\ +0.00651]$; the best observed
tail-weighted row is still $+0.00431$ $[-0.00080,\ +0.01012]$.  This rejects
naive train-oracle episode weighting as the missing step.  The tail signal must
enter through a budget-feasible objective or a transferable hard-episode state,
not through simple episode reweighting alone.
A soft budget-feasible surrogate is also bounded.  The surrogate uses
train-split option errors inside a differentiable balanced-depth objective and
keeps calibration-only model selection.  It improves over the naive
tail-weighted row but does not close allocation: the selected run gives
$+0.00356$ $[-0.00342,\ +0.01177]$, and the best observed run gives $+0.00193$
$[-0.00355,\ +0.00700]$.  The unweighted episode-policy reference remains
$+0.00184$ $[-0.00306,\ +0.00687]$.  This suggests that a soft surrogate is not
enough; the missing step is likely a sharper discrete/listwise assignment
objective or additional state that predicts transferable hard episodes.
A hard-episode state probe tests the second possibility directly.  A linear
episode-level probe over aggregated rollout/internal features fits
train-split oracle-gain tail labels perfectly, but it does not transfer:
calibration AUROC is $0.5265$ and evaluation AUROC against the top missed
episodes is $0.5053$.  The top missed episodes have mean score $-0.656$,
essentially the same as non-top episodes at $-0.622$.  Thus the hard-episode
state is not linearly readable from this aggregate feature view; allocation
progress likely requires richer episode-state features or a genuinely
structured assignment model, not just a better scalar probe on the same
aggregates.
A richer sequence-summary probe gives only a small movement.  It augments the
episode aggregates with per-episode quantiles, adjacent-change summaries, and
score-distribution summaries.  Calibration AUROC rises to $0.5985$, but
evaluation AUROC against the top missed episodes is only $0.5479$, and the top
missed episodes still receive lower mean score ($-0.846$) than non-top episodes
($-0.728$).  The hard-episode state is therefore not recovered by simple
sequence summaries either; the next useful experiment should change the state
view or the option set rather than add another scalar probe.
The option-set diagnostic identifies the first clear allocation-side lever.
Using the same precomputed policy score and only changing the balanced option
grid, the coarse $\{1,3,5\}$ grid gives policy-ranked allocation delta
$-0.00181$ $[-0.00705,\ +0.00345]$, while the refined $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ grid
gives $-0.00330$ $[-0.00676,\ +0.00015]$.  The refined-grid oracle is also
slightly stronger, $-0.03059$ $[-0.03839,\ -0.02495]$ versus $-0.02930$
$[-0.03704,\ -0.02333]$.  This is not yet a closed allocation claim, but it is
the strongest sign that the hard boundary is partly an option-space problem:
the score is more useful when the budget allocator has intermediate depths
rather than only the hard $1/3/5$ split.
A direct refined-option readout does not yet turn that diagnostic into a
learned improvement.  Ridge and MLP readers are trained to predict
five-option marginal values for depths $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and selected on
calibration.  The selected MLP has evaluation allocation delta $+0.00184$
$[-0.00199,\ +0.00656]$ with score Spearman $0.3315$, while the reused
episode-policy score on the same refined grid remains better at $-0.00330$
$[-0.00679,\ +0.00020]$.  Thus the refined option space is useful, but a
small direct per-option value readout has not learned the right assignment
signal.  The next step is option-conditioned or structured assignment learning,
not just a wider output head.
A structured assignment row gives the compute-value axis its first closed
allocation-side model result on this fixed export.  The model reads the same
rollout-internal features plus an explicit depth option, predicts option error
for each depth in $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, and then uses an exact per-episode dynamic
program with budget $\sum b = 3n$ before any evaluation errors are read.  The
learned structured row evaluates at allocation delta $-0.00604$
$[-0.01045,\ -0.00168]$ with score/error Spearman $0.8899$.  The exact-budget
oracle is still lower at $-0.02412$ $[-0.03045,\ -0.01944]$, while the direct
refined-value DP row remains $+0.00182$ $[-0.00279,\ +0.00629]$ and the reused
policy-score balanced row is $-0.00330$ $[-0.00697,\ +0.00024]$.  The positive
reading is therefore not that failure detection solved allocation, but that
allocation becomes learnable when the model predicts option-conditioned
compute cost and the allocator enforces the budget as a discrete assignment
constraint.
A seed/epoch stability grid keeps this positive row as calibration-selectable
but not yet uniformly stable.  Across seeds $29,41,53$ and epoch budgets
$160,280,420$, all calibration rows are below zero, the selected run is again
seed $41$ at $420$ epochs, and its evaluation delta is $-0.00604$
$[-0.01081,\ -0.00187]$.  Three of the nine evaluation rows have upper
confidence endpoint below zero; the median observed evaluation delta is
$-0.00355$, while the weakest row is $+0.00095$ with upper endpoint
$+0.01040$.  This supports option-conditioned exact-budget assignment as the
right allocation-native direction, but it also makes stability and independent
export replication the next claim boundary.
An ablation localizes the lever.  With the same seed and epoch budget, the full
state-plus-depth row remains closed at $-0.00604$ $[-0.01080,\ -0.00130]$.
Removing explicit depth conditioning while keeping the state gives a wrong-side
row, $+0.02756$ $[+0.01436,\ +0.04333]$.  Removing the state while keeping only
depth information collapses to the uniform-depth assignment, $0.00000$
$[0.00000,\ 0.00000]$; a ridge reader over the full feature set also collapses
to the same zero row.  Thus the positive allocation result is not created by
the dynamic program alone, by a depth prior, or by a linear readout.  It
requires a nonlinear state--option interaction that predicts option-conditioned
compute cost before the exact-budget assignment is applied.

The integrated A100 closure checks are now finite and keep the same bounded
reading.  The direct paired comparison against the strongest post-hoc scalar
has native $h=1$ AUROC $0.78165$ against $0.74399$, but the paired delta
$+0.03767$ has interval $[-0.01262,\ 0.08607]$, so direct strongest-probe
superiority remains fail-closed.  The calibration-only selective sweep has no
threshold with realized eval risk at or below its $\alpha$ value: at
$\alpha=0.10$ it admits $16.25\%$ of anchors with risk $0.1165$, and at larger
coverage the risk rises further.  The shuffled-label permutation guard is
healthy and collapses detection to $h=1$ AUROC $0.50918$, supporting that the
finite detection row is not a simple shuffled-label capacity artifact.  These
closures therefore strengthen the boundary rather than promote the row into an
allocation or high-coverage selective claim.

This loop is the continuation mechanism for the article: each hypothesis it
clears is appended to the verified-findings record above, and nothing reaches
that record without passing both the mechanical gates and the adversarial
review.
