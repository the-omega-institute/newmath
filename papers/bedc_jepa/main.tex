\begin{abstract}
Joint-embedding predictive architectures learn world models by predicting
target representations rather than reconstructing observations.  Recent
identifiability results show that, under explicit Gaussian and transition
assumptions, such objectives can recover latent world variables up to a linear
or orthogonal gauge.  This paper proposes a different learning target:
BEDC-JEPA, a world-model principle in which the learned state includes
continuous latent coordinates, operational distinction heads, and explicit gap
ledger heads.  The goal is not to attach a post-hoc report to a representation,
but to train the model to know which distinctions are stable enough to claim
and where its own world-state predictions require a ledger entry.  The same
logic yields a fail-closed latent-claim certificate protocol for frozen
carriers: operational claims are emitted only when a declared source split,
test surface, and split-conformal singleton certificate support them; otherwise
the candidate is ledgered as coverage, source, or stability debt.  A
boundary-gated Ornstein--Uhlenbeck world gives the first executable study:
a continuous latent trajectory, an inside/outside operational distinction, and
a boundary gap band.  A four-system comparison separates latent-only
prediction, a post-hoc probe, a post-hoc BEDC report, and a trained BEDC-JEPA
gap readback.  The evidence record includes frozen-latent torch objectives,
learned-transition pixel grids, MiniGrid-style visual planning, object-level
counterfactual contact, cluttered distractor settings, a public V-JEPA2-AC
Giant CUDA checkpoint adapter evaluation, and a native
\texttt{MiniGrid-DoorKey-8x8-v0} S0/S1/S2/S3 study with a seed sweep and
planning penalty sweep.  Under fixed or declared latent carriers, the trained
BEDC-JEPA systems reduce unlogged distinction failures in the controlled
settings; the public MiniGrid study supports the UER direction while exposing
weak debt reduction and a risk-success planning tradeoff.  The result is a
ledgered world-state objective with public checkpoint and native public
benchmark evidence, not a public benchmark superiority claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
BEDC-JEPA is not a formalization of an existing JEPA paper and not a post-hoc
model card.  The object of study is a BEDC-native world-model objective:
$$
\text{world state} = z_t + d_t + g_t .
$$
Here $z_t$ is a continuous latent state, $d_t$ is a vector of operational
distinctions, and $g_t$ is a vector of gap-ledger states.  A distinction is not
a natural-language label.  It is a stable predicate with transition,
intervention, or planning consequences inside a declared scope.  A gap state is
not an after-the-fact explanation.  It is a model output indicating that the
current prediction or distinction claim is not certified.
