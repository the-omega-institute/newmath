\section{Introduction}

The source theory proposes a dynamic mathematical ontology kernel
$$
  \K_{\PZG}
$$
whose objects are not only static data, but generated histories, standard codes, decoders, normalizers, finite readings, and ledger states.  The PZG part of the name is literal: \emph{prime axes} provide the coordinate directions, while \emph{Zeckendorf words} provide the per-axis digit system.  In the formalized carrier, a PZG table is a finite-support map
$$
  p \longmapsto w_p
$$
where the support is restricted to primes and each $w_p$ is a legal Zeckendorf representation.  Decoding maps each $w_p$ to a natural exponent and then uses unique factorization to recover an element of $\Npos$.

The goal of this Lean line is not to certify the full speculative reach of the source document.  It is narrower and more valuable as a logical audit: identify the finite and algebraic claims that can be stated cleanly, prove them in Lean, and keep analytic or self-referential frontier claims outside the checked-result column until they have genuine declarations and proofs.  This paper therefore distinguishes three classes.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Checked theorem or definition}: a declaration in \lean{UnifiedTheory/**.lean} that can be referenced by name.
  \item \textbf{Encoded statement}: a Lean \lean{Prop} used to record a target without pretending that it is proved.
  \item \textbf{Outlook}: mathematical plans or analytic programs that are not yet formalized.
\end{enumerate}

The development is deliberately mathlib-based.  It uses mathlib's APIs for natural-number factorization, Zeckendorf representations, golden-ratio identities, pigeonhole arguments, and Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares.  This is separate from the mathlib-free BEDC core in the main repository: no file under the BEDC core imports this Lean project, and this project does not alter the BEDC no-mathlib invariant.

\subsection{Prime Axes and Euclid Escape}

The prime-axis state space is
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PrimeExp},
$$
a finite-support natural exponent vector whose support consists of primes.  The declaration
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PrimeExp.equivPNat}
$$
has type
$$
  \Npos \simeq \lean{UnifiedTheory.PrimeExp}.
$$
This is the formalized prime-axis statement: positive natural numbers are equivalent to finite prime exponent vectors.  The proof is a mathlib wrapper around \lean{Nat.factorizationEquiv}, so the paper should not claim that this Lean file rederives the full elementary proof of unique factorization from scratch.

The exponent reading is
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.vp},
$$
and the multiplicative additivity theorem is
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.vp_mul}.
$$
It says
$$
  v_p(mn)=v_p(m)+v_p(n)
$$
for $m,n\in\Npos$.  This is the checked content behind theorem 4.4's ``multiplication is coordinatewise addition on prime axes'' slogan.

The group-ification of this free structure to the positive rationals (source theorem 18.10) is checked on its free half: \lean{UnifiedTheory.primes_mul_indep} proves that for a formal integer exponent vector $a:\mathbb{N}\to_0\mathbb{Z}$ with prime support, $\prod_p p^{a_p}=1$ in $\mathbb{Q}$ forces $a=0$.  Equivalently the map $a\mapsto\prod_p p^{a_p}$ is injective, so the primes are multiplicatively independent and $(\mathbb{Q}_{>0},\times)$ is free (no relations) on the prime axes.  The proof applies the $p$-adic valuation $v_q$ to the product, which splits as a sum of valuations (\lean{UnifiedTheory.padicValRat_prod}); distinct-prime terms vanish (\lean{UnifiedTheory.padicValRat_distinct_prime}), the $p=q$ term returns $a_q$ via the integer-power valuation (\lean{UnifiedTheory.padicValRat_zpow}), and $v_q(1)=0$ forces $a_q=0$.  The surjective half is also checked: \lean{UnifiedTheory.primes_generate} proves that every positive rational is such a product, taking the exponent vector $a=v(\mathrm{num})-v(\mathrm{den})$ from the factorizations of the coprime numerator and denominator and splitting the product by \lean{Finsupp.prod_add_index} into $\mathrm{num}/\mathrm{den}=q$.  Together these two halves are source theorem 18.10: $(\mathbb{Q}_{>0},\times)$ is the free abelian group on the prime axes, the group-ification of theorem 4.4.

Euclid escape is represented by
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.euclidEscape}
$$
and proved by
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.exists_prime_not_mem}.
$$
For every finite set $S$ of primes, the theorem produces a prime divisor of
$$
  \prod_{p\in S}p+1
$$
that is not in $S$.  This is the formalized version of theorem 4.5: no finite window of prime axes exhausts the prime directions.

$$
says that a \lean{PhiInt} is fixed by conjugation exactly when its $\phiG$-coefficient is zero.  This is the algebraic reason the deficit integrality proof works.

The embedding compatibility theorem

$$
identifies the shrinking real face with the expanding real face after conjugation, while
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.toRealPlus_mul}
$$
states that the expanding embedding is multiplicative.  The Fibonacci decomposition of powers is
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.phiPow_ab},
$$
which says that the $b$-coordinate of $\phiG^n$ is the Fibonacci number $F_n$.

The kernel's cascade cancellation rests on a one-page golden algebra, checked in Lean.  From $\phiG^2=\phiG+1$ one gets $\phiG^3=2\phiG+1$, $\phiG^4=3\phiG+2$, the identity $\phiG^4+\phiG^2-1=2\phiG^3$, \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenRatio_pow4_add_sq_sub_one}, the second-order golden cancellation $\phiG^2+\phiG^3=\phiG^4$, \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenRatio_sq_add_cube}, and the Zeckendorf $11\to100$ analytic shadow $\phiG^4-\phiG^3-\phiG^2=0$, \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenRatio_pow4_sub_cube_sub_sq}.  The conjugate satisfies $\psiG=-1/\phiG$, \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenConj_eq_neg_inv}, and the constant $C_0$ splits into two half-goldens $\phiG/2=\sqrt5/2-1/(2\phiG)$, \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenRatio_half_eq_sqrt5_half_sub}.  The one-line Binet identity seeding the $\bar r$-chain, $F_{k+1}/\phiG=F_k-\psiG^{k+1}$, is \lean{UnifiedTheory.fib_succ_div_goldenRatio}.

The two-dimensional lift is forced at the outset: \lean{UnifiedTheory.no_geometric_fib_ratio} proves there is no constant ratio $\Lambda$ with $F_{k+2}=\Lambda\,F_{k+1}$ for all $k$ (the consecutive-Fibonacci ratios $1,2,3/2,\dots$ are non-constant), so no single geometric weight reproduces the Zeckendorf decode --- this is source theorem 6.2, the structural no-go that motivates the double-sided eigenline lift.  The double-sided scalar identities are then explicit.  The constant $c^\ast$ from source 6.145 is \lean{UnifiedTheory.cstar_eq_sqrt5_phi}, namely $\phiG^2+1=\sqrt{5}\,\phiG$; Binet's difference formula \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenPow_sub_goldenConjPow} proves $\phiG^n-\psiG^n=\sqrt{5}\,F_n$, so the source definition 6.4 length $\ell(z)=(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)/\sqrt{5}$ is an integer Fibonacci-weighted quantity, not an irrational golden-weighted one.  The product formula \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenPow_mul_goldenConjPow}, $\phiG^n\psiG^n=(-1)^n$, is the double-sided orientation sign of the alternating $\bar r$-chain.  The sum completes the triple of symmetric functions of the golden-rotation eigenvalues: \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldenPow_add_goldenConjPow} proves the integer Lucas trace $\phiG^n+\psiG^n=2F_{n+1}-F_n$.  Finally, \lean{UnifiedTheory.floorPhiMul_eq} proves $\lfloor(v+1)\phiG\rfloor=(v+1)+\lfloor(v+1)/\phiG\rfloor$, and \lean{UnifiedTheory.beattyShiftRead_eq_add_floor} proves $S(v)=v+\lfloor(v+1)/\phiG\rfloor$; hence shift decode and the fiber coordinate $a+b=\lfloor(v+1)/\phiG\rfloor$ are floors of one golden rotation, as in source Corollary~6.48$'$.

\subsection{Golden-Weight Generating Function and Finite Euler Product}

The golden weight $\Omega_\varphi$ is additive over coprime factors, so its generating function $n\mapsto x^{\Omega_\varphi(n)}$ is a multiplicative arithmetic function \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldGenAF}, checked in \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldGenAF_isMultiplicative}.  Its local factor at a prime power is $x^{S(v)}$, because $\Omega_\varphi(p^v)=S(v)$:
$$
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldWeight_prime_pow}\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldGenAF_prime_pow}.
  \end{gathered}
$$
Consequently, for every $n\neq 0$ the generating function factors as the finite Euler product
$$
  x^{\Omega_\varphi(n)} \;=\; \prod_{p^k \,\|\, n} x^{S(k)},
$$
checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldGenAF_euler}.  This is the algebraic Euler factorization of the golden weight over prime axes.  The golden weight is additive over coprime factors but not completely additive ($S(2)=3\neq 2\,S(1)=4$), so the generating function is multiplicative yet \emph{not} completely multiplicative: at $x=2$, $\mathrm{goldGenAF}\,2\,(2\cdot 2)=2^{S(2)}=8$ while $(\mathrm{goldGenAF}\,2\,2)^2=(2^{S(1)})^2=16$, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldGenAF_not_completelyMultiplicative}.  Hence the local Euler factor is genuinely non-geometric and the golden L-series does not reduce to a shift or power of $\zeta$.  The analytic Dirichlet series $\sum_n x^{\Omega_\varphi(n)}\,n^{-s}$---convergence on a half-plane, meromorphic continuation, and any relation to $\zeta$---is not formalized and remains an open leaf.

\subsection{Golden Weight, Squarefreeness, and Primality}

Because $S(v)\ge 2$ for $v\ge 1$ (with $S(1)=2$ and $S$ monotone), the golden weight is bounded below by twice the number of distinct prime factors, $2\,\omega(n)\le\Omega_\varphi(n)$, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.two_mul_primeFactors_card_le_goldWeight}.  Equality holds exactly for squarefree $n$, since $\Omega_\varphi(n)=2\,\omega(n)$ forces every prime exponent to equal $1$:
$$
  \Omega_\varphi(n) = 2\,\omega(n) \iff n\ \text{squarefree},
$$
checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldWeight_eq_two_mul_primeFactors_card_iff_squarefree}.  On the prime-power axis the golden weight reads primality: for $n$ a prime power, $\Omega_\varphi(n)=2$ iff $n$ is prime, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.isPrimePow_goldWeight_eq_two_iff_prime} (as $S(v)=2\iff v=1$).  Thus weight $2$ is the first-order prime channel of the golden weight.

The next weight value detects prime squares: for nonzero $n$, $\Omega_\varphi(n)=3$ iff $n=p^2$ for some prime $p$, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldWeight_eq_three_iff_prime_sq}.  The bound $2\,\omega(n)\le\Omega_\varphi(n)$ forces $\omega(n)=1$ (a prime power), and then $S(v)=3\iff v=2$ pins the exponent to $2$; this rules out semiprimes ($\omega=2$, so $\Omega_\varphi\ge 4$), cubes ($S(3)=5$), and non-prime squares.  The golden weight also evaluates cleanly on factorials: by Legendre's factorial factorization,
$$
  \Omega_\varphi(N!) = \sum_{p\,\mid\,N!} S\!\Big(\sum_{i\ge 1}\big\lfloor N/p^{\,i}\big\rfloor\Big),
$$
checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.goldWeight_factorial}, the inner sum being the $p$-adic valuation of $N!$.

\subsection{Finite Readings and Two-Square Classification}

The finite-reading pigeonhole theorem is
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.Reading.reading_has_fiber}.
$$
Given a finite carrier, a finite class set, and a map into that class set, if the class set has smaller cardinality than the carrier, two distinct elements share the same reading.  This proves theorem 8.5 at the level of finite sets: a finite reading with too few values has a nontrivial fiber.

The two-square classifier includes the prime-level theorem
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.Reading.prime_sq_add_sq_iff}.
$$
For a prime $p$, it states
$$
  (\exists a\,b:\N,\ a^2+b^2=p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\bmod 4\ne 3.
$$
The easy obstruction is proved in the file from the fact that squares modulo $4$ are $0$ or $1$, and the existence direction uses mathlib's theorem for primes that are sums of two squares.  This captures the prime-classifier part of chapter 9.

The general classifier is checked:
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.Reading.sq_add_sq_iff_factorization}.
$$
It states that a natural number $n$ is a sum of two squares exactly when every prime factor $p\equiv3\pmod4$ appears in the factorization of $n$ with even exponent.  This is theorem 9.10 in its ordinary arithmetic form.  The proof is a mathlib-based wrapper around the standard sum-of-two-squares factorization theorem; the Lean file records the kernel-facing reading theorem, not an elementary reproof of Fermat's theorem.

The constructive input behind the $p\equiv1\pmod4$ branch is source theorem 9.5, checked here as an \emph{explicit} certificate rather than a mere existence claim.  The factorial pairing \lean{UnifiedTheory.factorial_pairing} proves $(p-1)!\equiv(-1)^{m}(m!)^2$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p$ for odd prime $p$ and $m=(p-1)/2$, by reflecting the upper half $\{m{+}1,\dots,p{-}1\}$ onto the lower half via $k\mapsto p-k$ and using $\overline{p-k}=-\bar k$ to extract $(-1)^{m}$.  Combined with Wilson's lemma and $m$ even for $p\equiv1\pmod4$, this yields \lean{UnifiedTheory.neg_one_sq_witness}: the element $m!$ is an \emph{explicit} square root of $-1$ modulo $p$.  This strengthens mathlib's existence-only \lean{ZMod.exists_sq_eq_neg_one_iff} to a named witness, in keeping with the kernel's certificate discipline.

\subsection{Weil Explicit-Formula Small-Support Reduction}

We formalize the support-vanishing mechanism of the Weil explicit formula abstractly, not the full explicit formula.  An explicit-formula functional is modeled by \lean{UnifiedTheory.WeilFunctional}: an archimedean carrier together with the explicit prime-power side
$$
  \operatorname{primeSide}(g) \;=\; \sum_{p}\ \sum_{k\ge 0}\ (\log p)\,\bigl(g((k{+}1)\log p) + g(-(k{+}1)\log p)\bigr),
$$
which is \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide}.  When the test function $g$ is supported in the open window $(-\log 2,\ \log 2)$, every prime-power frequency satisfies $(k{+}1)\log p \ge \log 2$ and so falls outside the window; hence the prime side vanishes term by term, \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide_eq_zero_of_smallSupport} (unconditional).  On such test functions the functional therefore reduces to its archimedean part, \lean{UnifiedTheory.WeilFunctional.eval_smallSupport}, and is nonnegative whenever the archimedean part is, \lean{UnifiedTheory.WeilFunctional.eval_nonneg_of_smallSupport}.  The archimedean positivity is supplied as an explicit hypothesis: it is the Connes--Consani archimedean positivity, an open leaf that this development does not prove.

The reduction is non-vacuous: the single-point witness $g=\mathbf 1_{\{\log 2\}}$ gives $\operatorname{primeSide}(g)=\log 2$, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide_witness} and \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide_not_identically_zero}, so the prime side genuinely detects prime-power logarithms and $\log 2$ is the exact lower boundary of detectable frequencies.

Widening the window by one arithmetic threshold makes the finite-packet structure explicit.  Since $2$ is the only prime power below $3$, a test function supported in $(-\log 3,\ \log 3)$ leaves exactly one surviving prime term:
$$
  \operatorname{primeSide}(g) = \log 2\,\bigl(g(\log 2)+g(-\log 2)\bigr),
$$
checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide_eq_single_of_support_log3}.  On this chamber the functional equals its archimedean part plus a single explicit prime shift, \lean{UnifiedTheory.WeilFunctional.eval_log3_chamber}---the first nonzero finite prime side (the window $(-\log 2,\log 2)$ had none), and the semi-local finite-packet shape.  Positivity is still not asserted: the archimedean term stays an open leaf.

The chamber structure extends to every window width.  For a test function supported in the open window $(-\log N,\ \log N)$ with $N\ge 2$, every prime $p\ge N$ has smallest frequency $\log p\ge\log N$ outside the window, so it contributes nothing to the prime side, \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide_inner_vanishes_of_ge}.  Consequently the outer sum collapses to a finite sum over the primes below $N$:
$$
  \operatorname{primeSide}(g) =
  \sum_{\substack{p<N\\ p\ \mathrm{prime}}}\ \sum_{k\ge 0}
  (\log p)\,\bigl(g((k{+}1)\log p)+g(-(k{+}1)\log p)\bigr),
$$
checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.primeSide_eq_sum_of_support}.  On bounded support the Weil prime side is therefore a genuine finite object---the finite-packet cutoff---with the arithmetic thresholds $\log 2,\log 3,\log 4,\dots$ marking where each new prime power enters.  This is the geometric prime side only; positivity is not asserted and the archimedean term remains an open leaf.

The prime-side frequencies connect the two layers.  Under the canonical indexing $(p,k)\mapsto p^{k+1}$ of prime powers, the Weil frequency $\log(p^{k+1})=(k{+}1)\log p$ carries the golden label $\Omega_\varphi(p^{k+1})=S(k+1)$, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.primePower_frequency_goldLabel}, so the explicit-formula and golden-weight layers meet on prime powers.  Moreover the frequency map $(p,k)\mapsto (k{+}1)\log p$ is injective, checked as \lean{UnifiedTheory.weilFreq_injective}: the prime side is indexed by distinct prime powers with no frequency collisions and no duplicate $(p,k)$ data.

The count of surviving frequencies is a step function of the window.  Writing $\mathrm{ppChamber}(N)=\{q<N: q\ \text{a prime power}\}$ for the prime powers below $N$, the set is monotone in $N$, \lean{UnifiedTheory.ppChamber_mono}, and its cardinality increases by exactly one precisely at prime-power boundaries, \lean{UnifiedTheory.ppChamber_card_succ} and \lean{UnifiedTheory.ppChamber_gains_iff}: the finite prime side acquires a new frequency exactly at each prime power, never between them and never two at once.

This formalizes the support-vanishing reduction only; unconditional Weil positivity, the explicit formula's zero side, and RH are not claimed.

\subsection{Heart-Electrode Residual-Identity Foundation}

The elementary closed foundation of the heart-chapter residual spectral identity is now separated from the analytic frontier.  The audible-window Mellin transform is \lean{UnifiedTheory.gTilde}; in source H.2 it is
$$
  \tilde g(s)=\frac{4\sinh^2(s/2)}{s^2},
$$
with removable center \lean{UnifiedTheory.gTilde_zero}, $\tilde g(0)=1$, and limit theorem \lean{UnifiedTheory.tendsto_gTilde_zero}.  The window is moreover entire: \lean{UnifiedTheory.gTilde_entire} proves $\tilde g$ analytic on all of $\mathbb{C}$, the removable singularity at $s=0$ discharged by Riemann's theorem because the punctured-neighbourhood limit equals the defined value $1$, so the closed Mellin window carries no pole to obstruct the spectral sum.  This is the closed Mellin-window part of the slogan ``station two empty, station three fully audible.''

The audible window is also nonvanishing on the open right half-plane: \lean{UnifiedTheory.gTilde_ne_zero_of_re_pos} proves $\tilde g(s)\ne0$ whenever $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, since the only zeros of $4\sinh^2(s/2)/s^2$ are those of $\sinh(s/2)$ at $s\in2\pi i\mathbb{Z}$ (the imaginary axis). A subsequent literature-review round of the source volume corrects the lineage of the two headline statements, and we record that attribution honestly. What is genuinely new to the volume, and not a re-labelling of these classics, is the window-\emph{family} refinement with per-window computable constants and, checked here, the ``no-miss'' mechanism: the right-half-plane nonvanishing \lean{UnifiedTheory.gTilde_ne_zero_of_re_pos} is exactly what guarantees no off-line zero can hide from the electrode family, a step with no counterpart in the cited literature.  The full criterion and lower bound themselves require Landau's oscillation ($\Omega$) theorem, which is not in mathlib (a candidate contribution in its own right), so they remain open leaves; the numerical certificate behind H.8, the double-contour value $\hat R(\rho_1)\approx3.35\times10^{-8}\ne0$, is empirical, and the ``tax-reduction'' claim H.9 (that the window family needs only a polynomial shadow of the Gonek--Hejhal bound $\sum_{\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2}\ll T$ for absolute convergence) depends on that zero-moment input and stays open.   \operatorname{Converges}(\sigma)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \int_{u\ge u_0} \left|f(u)e^{-\sigma u}\right|\,du<\infty,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.Converges}.
$$
This is only the absolute-convergence layer; no oscillation theorem is hidden in the definition.

The first checked theorem is monotonicity in the real exponent:
$$
  \operatorname{Converges}(\sigma)\ \wedge\ \sigma\le\sigma'
  \Longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Converges}(\sigma'),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.Converges.mono}.
$$
The proof is the direct right-ray comparison
$$
  \left|f(u)e^{-\sigma' u}\right|
  \le
  e^{-(\sigma'-\sigma)u_0}\left|f(u)e^{-\sigma u}\right|,
  \qquad u\ge u_0,
$$
so moving right in the half-plane cannot destroy absolute convergence.

The convergence abscissa is then the lower envelope
$$
  \sigma_c
  =
  \inf\{\sigma\in\mathbb R:\operatorname{Converges}(\sigma)\},
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.abscissa}.
$$
Its half-plane implication is checked as
$$
  \sigma_c<\operatorname{Re}s
  \Longrightarrow
  \int_{u\ge u_0}\left|f(u)e^{-su}\right|\,du<\infty,
  \qquad
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.converges\_of\_abscissa\_lt},\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.integrableOn\_integrand}.
  \end{gathered}
$$
The reverse direction used at a Landau boundary is also part of the same envelope:
$$
  \operatorname{Converges}(\sigma)\Longrightarrow\sigma_c\le\sigma,
  \qquad
  \sigma<\sigma_c\Longrightarrow\neg\operatorname{Converges}(\sigma),
  \qquad
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.abscissa\_le\_of\_converges},\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.not\_converges\_of\_lt\_abscissa}.
  \end{gathered}
$$

On the open half-plane, differentiation under the integral sign is formalized with the derivative written explicitly:
$$
  F'(s_0)
  =
  \int_{u\ge u_0} f(u)(-u)e^{-s_0u}\,du,
  \qquad
  0\le u_0,\quad \sigma_c<\operatorname{Re}s_0,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.hasDerivAt\_F}.
$$
The derivative integrand is itself integrable,
$$
  \int_{u\ge u_0}\left|f(u)(-u)e^{-su}\right|\,du<\infty,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.integrableOn\_derivIntegrand},
$$
because after choosing
$$
  \sigma_c<\sigma_2<\sigma_1<\operatorname{Re}s_0,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.exists\_real\_between},
$$
the polynomial factor is absorbed by $u\le\varepsilon^{-1}e^{\varepsilon u}$ with $\varepsilon=\sigma_1-\sigma_2$.  The global holomorphy statement on the half-plane is
$$
  F\ \hbox{is complex-differentiable on}\ \{s:\sigma_c<\operatorname{Re}s\},
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.differentiableOn\_F}.
$$

The derivative recursion is expressed by multiplying the weight by $-u$:
$$
  (\operatorname{mulNegU}\mathcal D).f(u)=(-u)f(u),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.mulNegU}.
$$
The shift preserves the relevant half-plane,
$$
  \sigma_c(\mathcal D)<\operatorname{Re}s
  \Longrightarrow
  \sigma_c(\operatorname{mulNegU}\mathcal D)<\operatorname{Re}s,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.abscissa\_mulNegU\_lt},
$$
and the first derivative is exactly the shifted transform:
$$
  F_{\mathcal D}'(s)=F_{\operatorname{mulNegU}\mathcal D}(s),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.hasDerivAt\_F'}.
$$

Iterating gives the full derivative tower:
$$
  F_{\mathcal D}^{(n)}(s)
  =
  F_{(\operatorname{mulNegU}^{[n]}\mathcal D)}(s),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.iteratedDeriv\_F}.
$$
The corresponding weight identity is checked separately:
$$
  \big(\operatorname{mulNegU}^{[n]}\mathcal D\big).f(u)
  =
  (-u)^n f(u),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.f\_iterate},
$$
with the half-plane bound at every stage recorded as
$$
  \sigma_c(\mathcal D)<\operatorname{Re}s
  \Longrightarrow
  \sigma_c(\operatorname{mulNegU}^{[n]}\mathcal D)<\operatorname{Re}s,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.abscissa\_iterate\_lt}.
$$

The sign ledger needed by Landau's argument is also checked, but only as a sign ledger.  If $f(u)\ge0$ on the ray, then on the real half-plane
$$
  0\le
  (-1)^n
  \int_{u\ge u_0}
    \big(\operatorname{mulNegU}^{[n]}\mathcal D\big).f(u)e^{-\sigma u}\,du,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.sign\_control}.
$$
Equivalently, after identifying the real-axis transform,
$$
  0\le (-1)^n\,\operatorname{Re}\big(F^{(n)}(\sigma)\big),
  \qquad
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.sign\_control\_iteratedDeriv\_re},\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.F\_ofReal}.
  \end{gathered}
$$

The open leaf is the Landau $\Omega$-oscillation theorem itself: for $f\ge0$, one still has to prove that analytic continuation across the real abscissa contradicts the strict-left nonconvergence just displayed.  There is no Lean theorem \texttt{landau\_nonneg} in this layer.  The missing steps are the Taylor-radius bridge at the boundary and the Tonelli exchange that turns a nonnegative Taylor series back into absolute convergence of the original Laplace integral.
The per-term infrastructure toward that Tonelli exchange is machine checked: each Taylor term $(\sigma_1-\sigma)^n/n!\cdot u^n f(u)e^{-\sigma_1u}$ is integrable on $[u_0,\infty)$, via \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.iter\_weight\_integrable} and \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.taylor\_term\_integrable}; and the terms are machine checked to sum pointwise to the original integrand $f(u)e^{-\sigma u}$, via \lean{UnifiedTheory.LaplaceData.taylor\_series\_pointwise}, so the Tonelli bridge only still has to interchange that pointwise sum with termwise integration in \texttt{converges\_of\_summable\_taylor}.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\varphi$}{phi}-Coordinate System (Golden Coordinate Ring, L0)}

The golden coordinate system starts with a concrete coordinate ring rather than an abstract field extension.  Its base carrier is the integer-pair model
$$
  \mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\varphi]=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\varphi,
  \qquad
  \varphi^2=\varphi+1,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt}.
$$
An element is written $a+b\varphi$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$.  This is the natural $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ integral coordinate, not the smaller-looking $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$ coordinate, and the Lean layer gives it a concrete commutative ring structure by the multiplication rule forced by $\varphi^2=\varphi+1$.

The L0 norm is the integer form
$$
  N(a+b\varphi)=a^2+ab-b^2,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.norm}.
$$
It is tied to conjugation inside the ring by
$$
  N(x)=x\,\sigma(x)
  \quad\hbox{under the integer embedding,}
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.norm\_eq\_mul\_conj},
$$
and it is multiplicative:
$$
  N(xy)=N(x)N(y),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.norm\_mul}.
$$
Thus the first invariant of the coordinate ring is not a declared axiom; it is proved from the integer-pair multiplication.

The Galois conjugation is the map
$$
  \sigma(a+b\varphi)=(a+b)-b\varphi.
$$
The checked algebra states that it is involutive and packages it as both a ring homomorphism and a ring automorphism:
$$
  \sigma^2=\operatorname{id},
  \qquad
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.conj\_involutive},\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.conjHom},\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.conjAut}.
  \end{gathered}
$$
This is the coordinate-system reason the expanding and shrinking golden faces are paired by a single algebraic operation.

The basic unit calculation is also closed at L0:
$$
  \varphi\in\mathcal O^\times,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.isUnit\_phi}.
$$
Together with the norm of the generator,
$$
  N(\varphi)=-1,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.norm\_phi},
$$
the powers satisfy
$$
  N(\varphi^n)=(-1)^n,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.norm\_phiPow}.
$$
This is the finite unit calculus needed before any ideal-theoretic splitting statement can be asked.

Layer L1 adds a multiplicative coordinate axis.  The degree coordinate is
$$
  \deg_\times(x)=\left\lfloor\log_\varphi |x_+|\right\rfloor,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.degMul},
$$
where $x_+$ is the expanding real embedding.  On the golden powers it lands exactly on the integer grid:
$$
  \deg_\times(\varphi^n)=n,
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.degMul\_phiPow}.
$$

The digit coordinate is delegated to the existing Zeckendorf-word API:
$$
  \operatorname{zeckDigits}(n)=\operatorname{encode}(n),
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.zeckDigits}.
$$
Its round-trip theorem is

  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.decode\_zeckDigits}.
$$
This identifies the GCS digit axis with the finite Zeckendorf axis already used by the PZG coding.

The geometric coordinate is named as the circle coordinate

  \operatorname{geomCoord}(n)=\{n\varphi\},
  \qquad
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PhiInt.geomCoord}.
$$
This definition gives the axis on which a three-distance theorem should live, but the three-gap theorem is not formalized here and is not available as a ready-made theorem in the imported mathlib surface.

The arithmetic core currently checked for the prime-family side is the mod-$5$ splitting criterion for the discriminant:

  \Longleftrightarrow

Equivalently, for these primes the square classes are $p\equiv\pm1\pmod5$.  This is the discriminant-$5$ reason the golden coordinate ring naturally sees mod-$5$ residue classes rather than Fermat's mod-$4$ dichotomy.

\paragraph{Conjecture R (the middle-form window): a dimension-group adjudication.}
The GCS source outline's Conjecture R, the representation theorem for the middle-form window, asserts that every quasiperiodic structure $X$ with discrete scale invariance at scale $\varphi$ and bounded remainder has, in GCS coordinates, a standard section $\tau_X$ such that $X\cong\tau_X$ preserving spectrum and bounded-remainder order, hence preserving the topological conjugacy class.  The literature adjudication is three-layered: orbit equivalence, spectrum, and topological conjugacy.

The anchor is that the GCS coordinate ring
$$
  \bigl(\mathbb Z[\varphi],\,\mathbb Z[\varphi]\cap[0,\infty),\,1\bigr)
$$
is precisely the ordered group called the Fibonacci dimension group in the Cortez--Durand--Petite orbit-equivalence framework (Cortez, Durand and Petite, 2014, arXiv:1408.2112).  It is the stationary dimension group of the Fibonacci substitution, computed as the inductive limit
$$
  \varinjlim\bigl(\mathbb Z^2\xrightarrow{M}\mathbb Z^2\xrightarrow{M}\cdots\bigr),
  \qquad
  M=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}.
$$
Durand--Host--Skau (1999, Ergodic Theory Dynam.\ Systems 19, 953--993) place primitive substitution minimal systems in the stationary Bratteli--Vershik and dimension-group setting.  For Fibonacci, the substitution is left-proper and unimodular with $\det(M)=-1$, so the inductive limit is the integral group $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ itself, with no localization.  The canonical unit is the image of $(1,1)$, namely $\varphi^2$ under the Perron--Frobenius coordinate; multiplication by the positive unit $\varphi^{-2}\in\mathbb Z[\varphi]^\times$ is an order automorphism and normalizes the distinguished unit to $1$.

\textbf{Orbit-equivalence layer: CLOSED.}  Giordano--Putnam--Skau (1995, J. reine angew. Math.\ 469, 51--111) prove that two minimal Cantor systems are strongly orbit equivalent exactly when their ordered dimension groups with order unit are isomorphic.  Therefore
$$
  \bigl(\mathbb Z[\varphi],\,\mathbb Z[\varphi]\cap[0,\infty),\,1\bigr)
$$
is the complete invariant of the strong orbit-equivalence class containing the golden-Sturmian system.  The bridge is concrete: $\varphi=[1;1,1,\dots]$, so the continued-fraction tail is all $1$; bounded partial quotients give linear recurrence in the sense of Durand (2000, Ergodic Theory Dynam.\ Systems 20, 1061--1078); and self-similarity here is the stronger single-substitution source of the stationary Bratteli--Vershik model, not merely linear recurrence.  The exact Sturmian scope is the noble-slope class: the slope is $\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb Z)$-equivalent to $\varphi$ exactly when its continued-fraction expansion is eventually all $1$, by the Effros--Shen rank-two continued-fraction classification (Effros and Shen, 1980, Indiana Univ. Math. J.\ 29, 191--204) together with Serret's continued-fraction theorem (Serret, 1877, \emph{Cours d'algebre superieure}).

\textbf{Spectrum layer: CLOSED for the concrete golden system, by independent input.}  The golden system is a two-letter unimodular Pisot substitution, hence has pure discrete spectrum by Hollander--Solomyak (2003, Ergodic Theory Dynam.\ Systems 23, 533--540).  In the geometric tiling model, Baake--Gahler (2024, arXiv:2311.05387) identify the Fibonacci chain as a regular model set with unique ergodicity; the geometric tiling flow has eigenvalue group $\mathbb Z[\tau]/\sqrt5$, with $\tau=\varphi$, hence a $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$-module.  This spectral closure is not a consequence of the coordinate ring alone.  Strong orbit equivalence preserves only rational eigenvalues; irrational $\varphi$-spectrum can vary inside a strong orbit-equivalence class, as shown by Ormes (1997, J. Anal. Math.\ 71, 103--133) and by Cortez--Durand--Petite (2014, arXiv:1408.2112).  The spectrum lives in the infinitesimal and eigenvalue data, $\mathrm{Inf}(K^0)$ and $E(X,T)$, and the pure-point conclusion for the golden case comes separately from Pisot theory.  The real failure mode is randomization, where random Fibonacci systems can acquire mixed spectrum, not the symbolic-versus-geometric choice of tile lengths; the symbolic and geometric Fibonacci systems are both pure point.

\textbf{Topological-conjugacy layer: CLOSED for the golden case.}  Three convergence theorems close the golden conjugacy classification.  First, Durand--Leroy (2022, Discrete Analysis, Paper No.\ 7; arXiv:1806.04891) prove that topological conjugacy of minimal substitution subshifts is decidable, with Fibonacci as a running example.  Second, Sturmian rigidity makes the golden-slope class a single rigid point up to the standard flip: for Sturmian systems, the slope is the complete conjugacy invariant, so $\Omega_\alpha\cong\Omega_\beta$ only for $\alpha=\beta$ or $\alpha=1-\beta$ in the two-letter coding convention.  This is the classical Morse--Hedlund/Sturmian rotation classification (Morse and Hedlund, 1940, Amer. J. Math.\ 62, 1--42) specialized to the golden slope.  Third, Clark--Sadun (2006, Ergodic Theory Dynam.\ Systems 26, 69--86; arXiv:math/0306214) prove for tiling spaces that asymptotically negligible shape change is exactly the deformation direction producing topological conjugacy; in the Pisot substitution case, the conjugacy-preserving shape deformations form the finite-dimensional Galois-contraction subspace $|\lambda|<1$, the same Pisot gap $|\lambda_2|=1/\varphi$ seen above.  Thus Conjecture R, stated honestly as $X\cong\tau_X$ preserving spectrum and bounded-remainder order, is a theorem for the golden-Sturmian case, not an open problem.

The verdict is two-sided.  For the golden-Sturmian class, all three layers are closed: the GCS ring coordinatizes that world completely.  But once the correct scope is stated, this closure is close to tautological, since the two-letter $\varphi$-self-similar basic object is precisely the Fibonacci system.  The title-level canonicity slogan that $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ is the coordinate system for all $\varphi$-self-similar mathematics is false.  The golden-mean shift of finite type, forbidding $11$, has entropy $\log\varphi>0$ and is not minimal, yet Krieger's dimension-group construction for shifts of finite type (Lind and Marcus, 1995, \emph{An Introduction to Symbolic Dynamics and Coding}, Cambridge Univ.\ Press, Ch.\ 7) gives the same ordered inductive-limit group from the matrix $M$; $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ cannot separate that heterogeneous SFT object from the Fibonacci substitution world.  Conversely, rank greater than two golden-inflation systems, including three-or-more-letter and higher-dimensional cut-and-project systems with Perron--Frobenius eigenvalue $\varphi$, have dimension-group rank greater than $2$ and are not $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$.  This matches the strong-form boundary of the GCS outline itself: GCS is not the unique self-similar coordinate system and not a universal substrate; it is the native coordinate at the golden lattice origin, not an exclusive foundation for all self-similar mathematics.

The genuine open residue is not golden and not $\varphi$-specific.  \textbf{Open proposition O-7}: in the general minimal-Cantor conjugacy problem, even in effectively presented substitutive regimes where Durand--Leroy give a decision procedure, there is no known single clean complete algebraic invariant with the elegance of the Giordano--Putnam--Skau dimension group for strong orbit equivalence.  This is a gap between a decision procedure and a compact complete conjugacy invariant; it is registered as a precise open proposition, not a theorem, and it is not an openness claim about the golden-Sturmian case.

\section{Scope and Honesty}
\label{sec:scope}

The checked column has precise boundaries.  The Zeckendorf shrinking-window and Beatty-floor routes are
$$
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.deficitTrichotomy_holds}\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.cDef_mem}.
  \end{gathered}
$$
They are independent mechanisms; the bridge
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.S_eq_shifted_zeck}
$$
is no longer a checked theorem under a separate bridge hypothesis.  The assumption
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.ShiftedZeckendorfBeattyBridge}.
$$
has been discharged by \lean{UnifiedTheory.minusWindow_holds}, so the Zeckendorf shrinking-window and Beatty-floor routes now meet in one unconditional theorem.  At the project-ledger level, the front-line conditional count is now zero; this does not promote any analytic outlook item into the checked column.
The $n$-ary theorem \lean{UnifiedTheory.Cn_abs_le} is the symmetric estimate $|C_n|\le m-1$; sharper asymmetric endpoint counts are not claimed.  The normalization theorem
$$
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.PZGTable.decode_normAdd}
$$
is canonical normalization through the PZG bijection, not termination of a concrete carry-rewrite algorithm.

The zeta results include
$$

along with completed functional equations and reflected completed-zeta zeros.  These facts give prime-axis analyticity in $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$, symmetry about the critical line, and exclusion of projected nontrivial zeros from $\operatorname{Re}(s)\ge1$.  They do not prove RH.

The golden-weight layer contains the finite Euler factorization

This is an algebraic product over the finite set of prime axes dividing one natural number.  It is not a formalization of the analytic Dirichlet series $\sum_n x^{\Omega_\varphi(n)}n^{-s}$, its convergence domain, continuation, or any $\zeta$-relation.

is an equivalence with \lean{RiemannHypothesis}.  The theorem \lean{UnifiedTheory.onticZero_re} proves that an already ontic zero lies on the critical line.  The missing RH-strength step is still the universal upgrade from projected zeros to ontic zeros; there is no unconditional declaration of type \lean{RiemannHypothesis}.

The checked self-code layer contains Cantor barriers, Kleene fixed points, and the Gödel sequence encoder. \section{Outlook}

All items in this section are outside the present checked-result column and are \textbf{not yet formalized}.  The analytic program still lacks a PZG heat-trace derivation, Mellin/theta bridge, the full Weil explicit formula with its zero side, tail-budget analysis, and a positivity or spectral argument upgrading projected zeros to ontic zeros.  The checked Weil contribution is the support-vanishing reduction of the prime side under a small-support condition; Weil archimedean positivity, unconditional small-support positivity, and RH remain open leaves.  The analytic Dirichlet series attached to the golden weight, including convergence, continuation, and any relation to $\zeta$, also remains an open leaf.  RH itself, O-5, O-6, and O-7 remain open leaves.

The source theory's quasicrystal zeta object $Z_{\mathrm{qc}}$, its Euler product or continuation theory, natural-boundary claims, diagonal feedback program, and pullback-line claim 6.32 remain outlook.  The finite trace-map declarations
$$
  \begin{gathered}
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.cassini_fricke}\\
  \lean{UnifiedTheory.cassini_fricke_ne_zero}
  \end{gathered}
$$
supply algebraic input for such a program, but they do not build $Z_{\mathrm{qc}}$.

The intended research workflow is correspondence search followed by theorem extraction: prime-axis exponent vectors with Euler products, Beatty--Wythoff and Zeckendorf coding, $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ two-square norms with $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ deficit traces, Hecke--Mahler series with golden word factors, and hidden profinite fibers with rigidity.  This manuscript reports only the correspondences backed by actual Lean declarations.
