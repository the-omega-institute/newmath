\title{Sharp Dyadic Truncations of the Riemann Xi:\\
Numerical Evidence Against a Laguerre--Polya Route and the Located-Zero Alternative}
\author{Technical note}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
This note records a modest outcome about one constructive route around the
Riemann xi function.  The attractive route is to truncate the de
Bruijn--Newman Fourier representation sharply at dyadic cutoffs, prove every
truncation belongs to the Laguerre--Polya class, and pass to the locally
uniform limit.  The bridge itself is sound, but its universal premise is
not established, and small sharp cutoffs provide numerical evidence against it:
argument-principle computations and Newton residual checks locate non-real
zero candidates.  These data are numerical evidence, not a rigorous
certificate; an airtight negative theorem would require interval-arithmetic
validation.  A separate alternative is numerically viable: locate an
individual zeta zero by a Krawczyk or interval-Newton certificate.  That
alternative still has a precise missing analytic ingredient, namely an
Euler--Boole complex tail bound for eta-series evaluation in the critical
strip.  The note makes no claim about the truth of RH.
\end{abstract}

\section{The tempting route}

Write $\xi(s)$ for the classical completed Riemann xi function and write
$\Xi(z)=\xi(1/2+iz)$ for the de Bruijn--Newman variable.  In that variable,
use the Fourier form
$$
\Xi(z)=\int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cos(zu)\dd u,
$$
where $\Phi$ is the standard even-kernel side of the theta-derived
representation after the conventional change of variables.  The exact
normalization of $\Phi$ is not important for the point of this note; what
matters is that the integral defines an even entire function and that the
Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to all zeros of $\Xi$ being real,
equivalently to $\Xi$ being in the Laguerre--Polya class.

For a positive cutoff $a_K$, define the sharp truncation
$$
\Xi_K(z)=\int_0^{a_K}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\dd u .
$$
The motivating idea was:
$$
  \forall K,\ \Xi_K\in \LP
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Xi\in \LP
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \RH .
$$
This is a legitimate conditional bridge.  The functions $\Xi_K$ converge to
$\Xi$ locally uniformly as $a_K\to\infty$, and the Laguerre--Polya class is
closed under locally uniform limits of real entire functions, allowing the
usual Hurwitz argument for zeros.  The bridge is also completely unforgiving:
all of the difficulty is in the premise that every sharp truncation is
Laguerre--Polya.

This note records numerical evidence against the premise for the sharp cutoff
family.  It does not produce a theorem about RH, and it does not shift any
zero of the true $\Xi$ function.  It identifies the exact certificate needed
to turn the observed obstruction into an airtight theorem.

\section{Numerical evidence against the premise}

Let $\kappa_K$ denote the number of non-real zero pairs of $\Xi_K$ visible in
the audited upper half-plane boxes for the chosen cutoff convention.  Since
every Laguerre--Polya entire function has only real zeros, one rigorously
certified non-real zero of one $\Xi_K$ would be enough to refute the all-$K$
premise.

\begin{evidence}[Computational non-real zero candidates]
\label{ev:numerical-candidates}
The script
$$
\texttt{papers/bedc/experiments/dyadic\_truncation\_lp\_refutation.py}
$$
computes $\Xi_K$ from the finite incomplete-gamma expression for the sharp
cutoff integral, counts zeros in a rectangle by the argument principle, and
then refines the center of the counted rectangle by Newton iteration.  Its
output gives two small-cutoff numerical witnesses.

\begin{itemize}
\item For the square-root cutoff convention at $K=1$,
$a_K=0.980258143468547\ldots$.  The box
$[28,32]\times[0.5,1.2]$ has boundary winding $1$, stable under the
double-sampled check.  Newton refinement gives
$z\approx30.2985057832+0.8632292908i$, with residual on the order of
$10^{-45}$.
\item For the power cutoff convention at $K=1$,
$a_K=1.386294361119890\ldots$.  The box
$[64,68]\times[0.9,1.7]$ has boundary winding $1$, again stable under the
double-sampled check.  Newton refinement gives
$z\approx66.3839283664+1.3116251915i$, with residual on the order of
$10^{-58}$.
\end{itemize}

The displayed coordinates are therefore not being used as assumptions; they
are the output of a count-then-refine procedure.  The winding counts and
Newton residuals are not strict certificates.
\end{evidence}

The evidence block is deliberately not stated as a rigorous analytic theorem.
It is a reproducible numerical indication of a route failure.  The negative
logical point that an interval certificate would establish is small but
decisive:
$$
  \exists K\ \kappa_K>0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \neg(\forall K,\ \Xi_K\in\LP).
$$
Thus a certified non-real zero for one sharp truncation would block the
implication ``all sharp dyadic truncations are $\LP$, hence $\RH$'' by making
the antecedent false.  An airtight theorem from Numerical
evidence~\ref{ev:numerical-candidates} requires interval-arithmetic validation:
either an
interval-Newton or Krawczyk proof of a unique zero in a certified complex box
$B\subset\{\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$, or a strict argument-principle enclosure
showing that
$$
  0\notin \Xi_K(\partial B),
  \qquad
  \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial B}\frac{\Xi_K'(z)}{\Xi_K(z)}\dd z=1
$$
with rigorous interval bounds.  Until that interval certificate is supplied,
the candidates in this section are numerical evidence, not a theorem.

\subsection{Observed bifurcation mechanism}

The numerical obstruction is not surprising.  A boundary-corrected truncation
can be organized as a homotopy
$$
  F_t(z)=Q_K(z)+tP_K(z),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1,
$$
where $Q_K$ is the Robin-type reference characteristic function with simple
real zeros, and $P_K$ carries the boundary perturbation.  In the large-cutoff
regime, the first Robin zeros cluster near the origin.  Their first-order
motions under the perturbation have opposite signs for adjacent zeros, so a
pair is numerically seen, and generically expected, to move toward collision.
This is an explanatory mechanism, not a proof of finite-time collision:
higher-order terms can change the direction, zeros can interact with other
zeros, and the tracked pair need not be the eventual bifurcation pair.

The honest weak conclusion is conditional.  If $F_0=Q_K$ has only simple real
zeros, if $F_1=\Xi_K$ has a non-real zero, and if $F_t$ is a continuous
homotopy through real entire functions, then the real-zero property must fail
somewhere along $t\in[0,1]$.  In the present note, the endpoint non-real zero
is still numerical evidence.  A rigorous bifurcation theorem would require a
certified solution of
$$
  F_t(x)=0,\qquad \partial_xF_t(x)=0
$$
in an interval box $(x,t)\in X\times T$, together with zero-count checks on
the two sides of the bifurcation parameter.

This is the suspected failure of a no-bifurcation sub-route.  The endpoint
$\Xi_K$ is not
protected by the same mechanism that protects the smooth de Bruijn--Newman
heat deformation.  A proof that every sharp cutoff stays in $\LP$ would have
to prevent exactly these births of non-real pairs; the observed evidence
indicates where such a proof would have to break for the sharp family.

\subsection{Charge cancellation is not a reduction}

One can rephrase the attempted salvage as a statement about bifurcation
charges.  Let the charge record the signed births and deaths of non-real zero
pairs along a cutoff or homotopy parameter.  Asking for total cancellation at
each endpoint amounts to asking that the final truncation have no non-real
zeros:
$$
  \sum \text{charge}(K)=0
  \quad\text{for every }K
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \kappa_K(1)=0
  \quad\text{for every }K .
$$
In words, the charge-cancellation condition is just the all-$K$
Laguerre--Polya premise in a different coordinate system.  It is a strong
sufficient premise for deriving RH through the truncation bridge, not an
equivalent reformulation of RH: RH does not imply that every sharp cutoff is
Laguerre--Polya.  It relocates the burden; it does not reduce it.  Numerical
evidence~\ref{ev:numerical-candidates} indicates that this condition is false
for the sharp-cutoff family.

\section{Why sharp cutoff is a poor preservation object}

The structural reason is simple.  The de Bruijn--Newman theory studies a
smooth deformation of the Fourier kernel, often written schematically as
$e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)$ in the Fourier-side normalization.  That deformation has a
real-zero preservation and monotonicity theory, encoded by the
de Bruijn--Newman constant $\Lambda$.  The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to
$\Lambda\leq0$, while Rodgers and Tao proved $\Lambda\geq0$; hence RH is
equivalent to $\Lambda=0$ in that framework.

A sharp domain cutoff is a different operation.  It multiplies the kernel by
an indicator of $[0,a_K]$, introducing a hard boundary rather than a heat-flow
deformation.  There is no reason for this operation to preserve
Laguerre--Polya membership, and the small-cutoff computations give concrete
evidence that it does not.

This belongs to a familiar pattern.  Several finite approximant routes to RH
look plausible because they converge to a limiting object, but the relevant
zero property is not stable in the approximants in the needed direction.
Classical examples include the failure of Turan-style partial-sum routes, the
Szego-curve behavior of partial sums of $e^z$ whose zeros are complex although
the limit is zero-free, and Jensen-polynomial hyperbolicity tests that are too
coarse to decide RH.  A reported PF$_5$ failure for a related total-positivity
route fits the same warning: finite positivity or real-root tests can encode
interesting shadows of the xi/zeta structure without becoming a proof route
to RH.

The lesson is not that truncations are useless.  They are useful numerical and
conceptual probes.  The narrower lesson is that the sharp-cutoff
Laguerre--Polya bridge has no proved universal premise, and the current
numerics point directly against that premise.

\section{The located-zero alternative}

The viable replacement is local rather than global.  Instead of trying to
show that every approximant has only real zeros, one can try to locate one
specific zero by a constructive fixed-point certificate.

Let $c$ be a numerical approximation to the first nontrivial zero on the
critical line, near
$$
  c=\frac12+14.1347\ldots i .
$$
Choose
$$
  a=\frac{1}{\zetaR'(c)}
$$
from a high-precision numerical derivative, and define the Newton-like map
$$
  N(z)=z-a\,\zetaR(z).
$$
For a closed complex box or ball $B$ around $c$, a Krawczyk or Banach
certificate has the form
$$
  N(B)\subseteq B,
  \qquad
  \sup_{z\in B}|1-a\zetaR'(z)|<1 .
$$
The first condition traps a fixed point of $N$, hence a zero of $\zetaR$,
inside $B$.  The second condition gives uniqueness in the box.

Our numerical checks support this route for the first zero.  With
$\zetaR(s)$ evaluated through
$$
  \eta(s)=\sum_{n\geq1}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-s},
  \qquad
  \zetaR(s)=\frac{\eta(s)}{1-2^{1-s}},
$$
the evaluator agrees with \texttt{mpmath.zeta} at the tested points, and the
Krawczyk contraction test succeeds with a comfortable numerical margin in a
box around $\rho_1=1/2+14.1347\ldots i$.  This is evidence for a
constructive location certificate for one near-line zero.

It is important not to overstate this.  Locating one zero near the critical
line is not RH.  It does not say that all nontrivial zeros lie on the line,
that zeros are simple, or that a zero-free region has been proved.  It is a
local certificate for one zero, once all interval bounds in the computation
are made rigorous.

The same distinction matters for the phrase ``on the critical line.''  A
Krawczyk proof in a complex box only proves that a zero lies in that box.  To
force $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$, one should either isolate a real zero of the
Hardy $Z$-function, or use a box symmetric about $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$
together with the functional-equation symmetry
$\rho\mapsto 1-\overline{\rho}$; uniqueness in the symmetric box then forces
$\rho=1-\overline{\rho}$.

\section{A constructive 0-axiom winding-certificate layer}

There is a second way to organize the same finite-height verification
program.  Around a rectangle $R$, sample $\zetaR$ on the boundary
$\partial R$, replace the sampled boundary trace by a rational polygon, and
compute the winding number of that polygon about the origin.  Together with a
Krawczyk or interval-Newton enclosure, this is the usual argument-principle
shape of a certificate: the boundary winding supplies the zero count, while
the local fixed-point box supplies the location of the zero.

This route has two logically separate halves.  The first half is topological
and combinatorial: define the winding number of an oriented rational polygon
as a signed crossing count, and prove that the count is well defined under
the elementary changes a certificate generator must make.  The second half is
analytic: prove that the rational polygon faithfully tracks
$\zetaR(\partial R)$, and prove the constructive argument-principle theorem
which identifies the boundary winding with the number of zeros inside $R$.
Only the first half is complete at present.

The completed BEDC layer lives in the mathlib-free namespace
$$
  \texttt{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.RationalPolygonWinding}
$$
and its sibling modules.  It is Lean-kernel verified with no project axioms,
no \texttt{sorry}, and no forbidden Lean standard-library axioms such as
\texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}, or \texttt{propext} in the
strict axiom-purity audit.  The core definitions are
\texttt{computedWinding} and \texttt{computedWindingUp}.  The first is the
ordinary integer-valued signed count of crossings of the positive real ray by
the directed edges of a rational polygon.  The second is the same count in
the BEDC integer carrier \texttt{IntegerUp}, so that the certificate layer
can stay inside the propext-free algebraic development.

The verified properties are exactly the structural properties needed by the
topological side of an argument-principle certificate.

\begin{itemize}
\item Stability under certified perturbation is proved by
\begin{quote}
\small\path|computedWinding_stable_of_edgewiseMarginPreserving|
\end{quote}
If each edge has rational margin from the Boolean crossing discriminants,
then a vertexwise rational perturbation preserving those margins leaves
\texttt{computedWinding} unchanged.

\item The zero-enclosure base case is proved by
\begin{quote}
\small
\path|computedWinding_zero_of_all_im_pos|\par
\path|computedWinding_zero_of_all_im_neg|
\end{quote}
A polygon whose vertices all lie in the open upper half-plane, or all lie in
the open lower half-plane, has winding count $0$ for this ray-crossing
definition.

\item Boundary refinement is supported by
\begin{quote}
\small
\path|edgeCrossing_collinear_split|\par
\path|sumEdgeCrossingsUp_subdivide_one|
\end{quote}
Inserting a rational point on an edge, with the appropriate collinearity and
between-endpoint witnesses, does not change the crossing contribution.  This
is the formal permission to refine a sampled boundary polygon without
changing the certificate's topological value.

\item The choice of starting vertex is irrelevant by
\begin{quote}
\small\path|computedWindingUp_cyclic_rotate_one|
\end{quote}
Rotating a nonempty vertex list by one step preserves the \texttt{IntegerUp}
winding count.

\item Orientation behaves as expected by
\begin{quote}
\small
\path|edgeCrossingUp_reverse|\par
\path|edgeCrossingUp_reverse_cancel|
\end{quote}
Reversing one directed edge negates its crossing contribution, and traversing
the same edge in both directions has net contribution $0$.

\item Box-cover additivity is represented in the current BEDC checkout by
\begin{quote}
\small
\path|cover_winding_additive_from_edge_decomposition|\par
\path|pair_cover_winding_additive_from_edge_decomposition|\par
\path|split_parent_winding_eq_child_sum|
\end{quote}
Once a parent boundary is decomposed into the oriented cell boundaries of a
finite rational cover, the parent winding is the sum of the cell windings.
For a binary split this is the algebraic skeleton behind recursive
subdivision: shared interior edges are traversed in opposite directions and
cancel.
\end{itemize}

In short, for rational boundary polygons the winding number is defined as a
finite combinatorial object, is stable under certified rational
perturbations, vanishes in the half-plane base cases, is invariant under edge
subdivision and cyclic rotation, is antisymmetric under orientation reversal,
and is additive under box subdivision.  That is the topological half of the
argument-principle certificate layer.  It is not, by itself, a zero-counting
theorem for $\zetaR$.

Two analytic bridges remain open before this can become a finite-height
zeta-zero certificate.

First, one needs a located interval evaluator for $\zetaR$ on the boundary
$\partial R$.  The intended evaluator goes through
$$
  \eta(s)=\sum_{n\geq1}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-s},
  \qquad
  n^{-s}=\exp(-s\log n),
$$
with
$$
  n^{-it}=\cos(t\log n)-i\sin(t\log n),
$$
and with a strict Euler--Boole complex tail bound in the critical strip, of
the kind isolated in Obligation~\ref{obl:eta-tail}.  The BEDC/Loning rational
analysis base already contains 0-axiom material for rational series
enclosures of the relevant elementary functions, such as the rational
atanh/log, exponential, and trigonometric power-series bounds.  The missing
bridge is to connect those rational enclosures to located values of the real
transcendental functions by constructive continuity moduli for
$\exp$, $\log$, $\sin$, and $\cos$.

Second, one still needs the argument principle itself in the same
mathlib-free constructive setting.  The required theorem is not a slogan that
``winding equals zeros.''  It must be a discrete Rouche or Brouwer-degree
argument, plus an error theorem saying that a rational polygon with a
certified tube around $\zetaR(\partial R)$ has the same winding as the true
boundary trace and therefore counts the zeros in $R$.

These two bridges are the exact bottleneck for turning the present topology
layer into a Box14 certificate for the first zeta zero in
$[0,1]\times[14,15]$.  They are multi-week constructive analysis work, not a
completed finite-height verification and not progress on RH.  The winding
certificate layer and the located-zero Krawczyk route are complementary legs
of the same program: both are already reduced to the same located-$\zetaR$
analytic bridge.

\section{The open analytic obligation}

The missing piece is not philosophical.  It is a concrete analytic estimate.

\begin{obligation}[Euler--Boole eta tail bound in the critical strip]
\label{obl:eta-tail}
Use Euler--Boole summation, the alternating Euler--Maclaurin formula, to
produce a rigorous complex tail bound for the eta evaluation used in the
Krawczyk box:
$$
  R_N(s)=\sum_{n>N}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-s},
  \qquad s=\sigma+it
$$
uniformly for the small complex box $B$ around the target zero, with
$0<\sigma<1$.  The bound must also cover the $s$-derivative, since the
Krawczyk test needs an interval enclosure of $\zetaR'(B)$.  The enclosure must
be strong enough to prove
$$
  N(B)\subseteq B,
  \qquad
  \sup_{z\in B}|1-a\zetaR'(z)|<1 .
$$
\end{obligation}

The elementary Leibniz bound $(N+1)^{-\sigma}$ cannot simply be used for the
complex tail.  The terms
$$
  (-1)^{n-1}n^{-s}=(-1)^{n-1}n^{-\sigma}e^{-it\log n}
$$
rotate in phase and are not an alternating sequence on one real line.  The
concrete, rigorously provable, 0-axiom-formalizable tail bound that would
complete the located-zero certificate is the following.

Let
$$
  B=\{s=\sigma+it:\sigma_-\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_+,\ t_-\leq t\leq t_+\},
  \qquad 0<\sigma_-<\sigma_+<1,
$$
and set $X=N+1$ and $f_s(x)=x^{-s}$.  Define the Euler polynomials by
$$
  \frac{2e^{xz}}{e^z+1}
  =
  \sum_{\nu\geq0}E_\nu(x)\frac{z^\nu}{\nu!}.
$$
For every integer $L\geq1$, Euler--Boole summation gives
$$
  R_N(s)
  =
  (-1)^N
  \left[
  \frac12\sum_{\nu=0}^{2L-1}
  \frac{E_\nu(0)}{\nu!}\,
  f_s^{(\nu)}(X)
  +
  \mathcal E_L(s)
  \right],
$$
with
$$
  f_s^{(\nu)}(X)=(-1)^\nu(s)_\nu X^{-s-\nu},
  \qquad
  E_{2j}(0)=0\quad(j\geq1).
$$
The remainder has the uniform bound
$$
  |\mathcal E_L(s)|
  \leq
  C_L\,
  \frac{|(s)_{2L}|\,X^{1-\sigma-2L}}
  {\sigma+2L-1},
  \qquad
  C_L=\frac{2(1-2^{-2L})\zetaR(2L)}{\pi^{2L}}.
$$
Hence the whole box $B$ is covered by
$$
  |\mathcal E_L(B)|
  \leq
  C_L\,
  \frac{P_{2L}(B)\,X^{1-\sigma_- -2L}}
  {\sigma_-+2L-1},
$$
where
$$
  P_{2L}(B)=
  \prod_{j=0}^{2L-1}
  \sqrt{(\sigma_+ + j)^2+T^2},
  \qquad
  T=\max(|t_-|,|t_+|).
$$
For the derivative tail, differentiate the finite endpoint terms using
$$
  \partial_s f_s^{(\nu)}(X)
  =
  f_s^{(\nu)}(X)
  \left(
  \sum_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\frac1{s+j}
  -
  \log X
  \right),
$$
and use the safe box remainder bound
$$
  |\partial_s\mathcal E_L(B)|
  \leq
  C_L P_{2L}(B)X^{1-\sigma_- -2L}
  \left[
  \frac{A_{2L}(B)+\log X}{\sigma_-+2L-1}
  +
  \frac{1}{(\sigma_-+2L-1)^2}
  \right],
$$
with
$$
  A_{2L}(B)=\sum_{j=0}^{2L-1}\frac1{\sigma_-+j}.
$$
Finally,
$$
  \zetaR(s)=\frac{\eta(s)}{D(s)},
  \qquad
  D(s)=1-2^{1-s},
$$
and
$$
  \zetaR'(s)=
  \frac{\eta'(s)D(s)-\eta(s)D'(s)}{D(s)^2},
  \qquad
  D'(s)=(\log2)\,2^{1-s}.
$$
This is the exact analytic gap between the present numerical success and a
strict constructive proof of one located zero.  It is not a hidden RH
obligation.

\section{Scope}

The result of this note is the following.

\begin{proposition}[Scope of the note]
The sharp-cutoff Laguerre--Polya bridge is a valid conditional route, but its
all-$K$ premise has concrete numerical evidence against it from small sharp
cutoffs.  A rigorous refutation of that premise requires the
interval-arithmetic certificate described above.  The local Krawczyk route to
a single zero is numerically viable but incomplete until
Obligation~\ref{obl:eta-tail} is discharged.
Neither statement proves, disproves, or otherwise advances RH.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The logical part is elementary: a certified non-real zero of one $\Xi_K$ would
show $\Xi_K\notin\LP$, hence would refute the universal premise required by
the truncation bridge.  Numerical evidence~\ref{ev:numerical-candidates}
indicates such zeros, but its winding and residual data are not by themselves
strict certificates.  The Krawczyk discussion is a local fixed-point method
for one zero and depends on a still-open rigorous tail estimate.  Neither
argument contains a global zero classification for $\zetaR$ or $\Xi$.
\end{proof}

Thus the honest current state is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{sharp-cutoff LP route} &:\quad \text{numerically obstructed; certification open},\\
\text{one-zero Krawczyk route} &:\quad \text{numerically viable, analytically incomplete},\\
\text{RH} &:\quad \text{no claim made}.
\end{aligned}
$$
