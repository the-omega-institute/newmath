\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate}\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

The biological question is not whether a protein folds, functions, or is physically admissible. \paragraph{Residual basis.}
The residual coordinates tracked here are the nine elements
$$
\begin{aligned}

\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q),

\subsection{Translation-survival matrix for the nine residual coordinates}
\label{sec:translation-survival-b-star-q6-survival-matrix}

\paragraph{Object under test.}
This record concerns the survival of the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates when they are compared with translation-related readouts after the amino-acid quotient has already been imposed. The coordinate family is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and the antisymmetric $f_{3}$ stress coordinate. The intended finite matrix has one row for each of these nine residual coordinates and one column for each admissible translation readout, such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein abundance when the corresponding organism-matched measurement is actually attached. For a residual coordinate $q_i$ and a readout $r_j$, the statistic is not the raw codon-count association. It is the remaining readout improvement after the controls $Z$ have absorbed amino-acid composition, organism context, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, and the simpler $M$-density baseline.

\paragraph{Matrix statistic.}
Let $\ell_j^{\perp}$ denote the synonymous projection of the readout weight for column $j$, after the non-synonymous amino-acid component has been removed. The proxy coupling measured at the codon-count layer has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ij} &= q_i^{T}\Sigma\ell_j^{\perp},\\
S_{ij} &= S_{q_i}(r_j\mid Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\Sigma$ is the residual count covariance and $S_{ij}$ is the controlled survival score, equivalently the partial-$R^2$ or description-length improvement after the declared covariates have been fitted. A positive entry $S_{ij}>0$ would mean that the residual coordinate contributes information about the translation readout beyond the amino-acid, organism, compositional, length, and $M$-baseline explanation. A non-positive entry does not prove biological irrelevance of the coordinate; it only says that the tested readout and control set did not leave detectable residual survival for that entry.

\paragraph{Finite rows already computed.}
The current finite computation has a complete nine-coordinate proxy coupling row against a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum. The codon-count ambient space has $61$ sense-codon coordinates. The organisms loaded in that row are \textit{Homo sapiens}, \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12. The nine residual coordinates computed in the row are

$$

\paragraph{Survival claim status.}
The matrix conjecture is therefore open at the translation-realization layer. The current facts establish two bounded finite contacts: a nine-coordinate proxy coupling matrix with maximum $|A_{ij}|=1{,}882{,}464.1106377328$ in a $61$-codon count space, and a three-organism Leu CUN/UUR gate with Pearson and partial Pearson both equal to $1.0$ but with non-uniform signs. The entry must then survive amino-acid composition, organism context, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, and $M$-density baseline ablation. \paragraph{Local evidential boundary.}
The chapter does not infer ribosome-level realization from codon usage and tRNA gene-copy tables alone. It also does not treat tRNA gene copy as tRNA abundance, tRNA charging, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein output. Ribosome-profiling contact, if attached, can test translation-layer residual survival only under the stated controls; it is not protein-abundance evidence unless matched proteomics is separately supplied. No statement in this section promotes proxy coupling $A_{ij}$ to survival $S_{ij}$ without the explicit controlled partial-$R^2$ or description-length calculation.
