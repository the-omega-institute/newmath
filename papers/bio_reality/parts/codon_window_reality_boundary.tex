\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This record concerns a finite organism-level leucine coding contrast.  The measured coordinate is the signed residual between the CUN and UUR leucine codon blocks, paired with organism labels and with a tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate.  The biological reading is deliberately narrow: for each organism in the finite table, one asks whether the sign and magnitude of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ agree with the organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after controlling for amino-acid composition through synonymous-class comparison.  The row is therefore about an externally checkable codon-usage and tRNA-copy statistic, not about protein output as a whole.

\paragraph{Finite coding support.}
The code-table side of the record uses the finite set $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ with cardinality $20$.  In the checked row the listed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports $|M|=20$, so the CUN block is being handled as an explicit finite codon block inside a larger finite codon family rather than as an informal label.  This matters because the CUN/UUR contrast is only meaningful here after the codon families and their organism-level counts are fixed.

\paragraph{Organism rows and measured signs.}
The validation run loads three organism rows: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  Their reported CUN/UUR log-ratio values are

The corresponding signs are $(1,-1,1)$, and the row records $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$.  Thus the finite table does not support a uniform cross-organism direction for the CUN/UUR leucine contrast.  The yeast row reverses sign relative to the human and bacterial rows, so any admissible interpretation must be organism-conditioned.

\paragraph{tRNA-Leu pairing.}
The same checked row reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
r_{\partial}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}

Within this finite table, the result is therefore a passed organism-level test: the CUN/UUR log-ratio is non-uniform across organisms, and the sign-and-magnitude coordinate is aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate after the stated amino-acid composition control.  The test is intentionally modest.  It says that the cross-organism leucine codon-usage residual has a matching tRNA-copy contact in the three-row table; it does not say that the same direction must hold in every organism or under every annotation scheme.

\paragraph{What the contact can and cannot establish.}
The codon-usage contact is a per-organism summary of coding-sequence composition, and the tRNA contact is a genome-annotation summary of tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra.  Together they can test a translation-layer statistical relation at organism resolution: whether a codon-usage residual is paired with a tRNA-copy coordinate.  They do not observe ribosome kinetics, elongation timing, translation initiation, folding, stability, cellular function, or physical admissibility of a protein product.  Consequently no direct translation event, folded structure, physical feasibility claim, biological function, mechanistic law, or universal cross-organism principle follows from this row unless a different measurement supplies that contact.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite record is accepted as a bounded cross-organism translation-realization statistic.  Its positive content is the following: among the three loaded organisms, the CUN/UUR leucine log-ratio is not sign-uniform, and the controlled paired statistic against tRNA-Leu copy ratio is positive with reported partial Pearson value $1.0$ on $n=3$ rows.  Its negative content is equally important: the row is not evidence for protein structure, folding, function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, physical admissibility, or a universal biological rule.  It is a finite organism-level codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact, and it should be used only at that resolution.

\subsection{Residual codon topology after the amino-acid quotient}

\paragraph{Observed residual scope.}
The available codon-count contact covers three organisms: $Homo\ sapiens$, $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, and $Escherichia\ coli$ K-12. \begin{aligned}

This is a coordinate fact about organism-scoped codon-count tables. It says that the residual basis is not vacuous on the sampled tables. It does not, by itself, say that ribosomes use these residual directions, that proteins are more abundant because of them, or that a biochemical mechanism has been isolated.

\paragraph{Survival statistic.}
For a readout $r$ such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, the proposed test keeps amino-acid composition and context inside $Z$ and compares a baseline score to the score after adding one residual direction $q$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive value means only that, under the stated conditioning set and readout, the residual coordinate improves the specified predictive or explanatory score. The stronger probe against an $M$-only account uses an $M$-density baseline $\Delta_M$ and asks whether
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)>0
\end{aligned}
$$
for at least one $q\in B^*_{Q6}$ in at least one organism, or whether the controlled $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$ log-ratio changes sign across organisms while $\Delta_M$ does not. This formulation is intentionally conditional: $S_Q>0$ is not a universal biological law, and a single positive organism-readout pair would not establish a general principle.

\paragraph{Current readout status.}
The chapter has three relevant classes of contact: organism-level codon-usage summaries, organism-level tRNA gene-copy summaries, and organism-scoped tAI or stAI weights. These are sufficient to define the intended empirical interface, but the finite memory supplied here does not yet contain a curated organism-matched table joining codon usage, tRNA copy number, and tAI or stAI weights with amino-acid-composition-controlled estimates of $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$. Therefore the present status is a blocked null at the translation-readout level. The missing paired statistic blocks both the positive residual-survival claim and the claim that an $M$-only baseline is insufficient.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The nonzero residual fraction $7/9$, the exact thirteen-codon $R$ list, the $M$ face vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, and the quotient face vector $(10,14,6,1)$ support only the finite coordinate statement that the amino-acid-residual codon basis is computable and nontrivial on the three sampled organism tables. No conclusion about translation realization, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, protein function, or organism-independent universality follows from those coordinate facts alone. Any such conclusion requires a separate matched contact that supplies the relevant readout and the controlled survival statistic.
