\subsection{Direct protein-omics survival for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates with a translation boundary}\label{subsec:q6-direct-protein-omics-survival-translation-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether synonymous residual coordinates from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ codon-usage packet retain detectable predictive content for measured protein abundance after the amino-acid quotient has already been taken. The data object is a finite CDS-by-organism matrix whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns include residual codon-topology coordinates $q_i$, baseline controls, and protein-omics readouts $P_k$. The intended boundary is stricter than a direct association: a protein-omics signal may be reported as downstream survival, but it may not be called a translation mechanism unless matched translation readouts $T_j$ are present and an explicit $q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ mediation fit shows that the translation readout carries the signal.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates.}
The computed $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate family contains nine synonymous residual directions after amino-acid composition has been separated out: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. These are not protein states and are not translation measurements. They are finite sequence-derived coordinates that can be tested against measured readouts only after the baseline covariates have been projected away. In the direct protein-omics run the $q$-vectors were imported from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation-survival computation, so the protein-abundance test uses the same nine-coordinate residual basis rather than a post hoc protein-specific coordinate set.

\paragraph{Finite rows and controls.}
The protein-omics readout was $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts. Three organism-specific finite tables were used. In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $3739$ proteins were joined, with join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$. In \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $4129$ proteins were joined, with join hit rate $1.0$. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ proteins were joined, with join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$. For each organism, the residualization control block had $24$ columns, numerical rank $23$, and the residualized $B^{*}_{Q6}$ block retained rank $9$. The residual degrees of freedom after controls were $3716$, $4106$, and $5792$, respectively, so the direct protein-omics regressions are not an $n=3$ cross-organism saturation artifact.

\paragraph{Direct survival statistic.}
Let $X_Q$ denote the nine-column $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate block, let $Z$ denote the baseline control matrix, and let $Y_P$ denote the measured protein-abundance column for the organism under test. The statistic reported here is the protein-omics partial survival of $X_Q$ after residualizing both $X_Q$ and $Y_P$ against $Z$. In schematic form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{X}_Q &= (I-\Pi_Z)X_Q,\\
\widetilde{Y}_P &= (I-\Pi_Z)Y_P,\\
S^{QP} &= R^2(\widetilde{Y}_P \sim \widetilde{X}_Q).
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold was $10^{-12}$ for partial $R^2$. The observed direct protein-omics survival values were positive in all three finite organism tables: $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $R^2_{QP}=0.03209009170649934$ for \emph{M. smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}. In each organism, all nine residual coordinates were counted as surviving coordinates under the stated positive partial-$R^2$ criterion. The corresponding residual response energies after the control projection were $4020.333671802061$, $5729.194538214237$, and $2958.1865890925337$.

\paragraph{Baseline adequacy.}
The finite rank checks separate this record from a vacuous all-positive readout. The control ranks were $23$ rather than full column count $24$, while the residualized $q$ block still had rank $9$ in every organism. The run therefore had remaining coordinate variation after the controls, and each organism had thousands of residual degrees of freedom. The control projection explained energies $432.89050065533684$, $183.85037813566998$, and $882.6526210803958$ in the three organism tables. These numbers support the narrow claim that the nine synonymous residual directions retained direct association with measured protein abundance after the recorded baseline controls. They do not by themselves decide why that association exists.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
A separate modeled-translation computation supplied a bounded codon-adaptation contact rather than a measured ribosome or translation-efficiency contact. In that run, the readout was modeled tAI $w_{codon}$ from GtRNAdb tRNA gene copy and dos Reis wobble $s$-values. The finite modeled table covered $3$ organisms, all $61$ sense codons, and the same nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates. The local GtRNAdb payload contained all $20$ standard amino-acid families in \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast; the FASTA header counts were $89$, $432$, and $275$, with matched header counts $87$, $432$, and $275$. The modeled tAI computation had zero raw-$W$ fallback count $0$ for each organism and reported $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}=3801961.031607896$. This is evidence for a bounded modeled translation-readout contact, not for measured ribosome occupancy or translation-rate mediation.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The ready result is a direct cross-layer association from synonymous residual coordinates to measured protein abundance under the recorded baseline controls. It should be read as DNA-to-protein-omics predictive irreducibility in finite joined rows. It must not be rewritten as a translation mechanism, because the protein-omics run explicitly lacked perturbation, rescue, ribosome profiling, and matched transcript controls, and its positive entries were not mediation entries $M^{QTP}$. A translation-mechanism statement would require same-CDS measured translation columns $T_j$, matched mRNA controls, and a mediation comparison showing whether conditioning on $T_j$ removes, reduces, or leaves the $q_i \to P_k$ signal.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter only licenses the bounded protein-omics statement above. No claim about translation, folding, localization, post-translational modification, stability, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causality, or universality follows from $S^{QP}>0$. A separate contact at the relevant layer must supply the corresponding rows and statistic. In particular, modeled tAI weights are not observed ribosome dynamics, measured PAXdb abundance is not a translation-rate measurement, mRNA abundance is only a control covariate when present, and a direct residual-to-protein association is not a proof of a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object tested here is a finite organism-by-CDS readout surface in which the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are compared with organism-level modeled codon adaptation weights after amino-acid quotient residualization. $$

 n &= 40,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 23,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X) &= 9,\\
 \operatorname{df}_{\mathrm{res}} &= 17,\\
 R^{2}_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579.
\end{aligned}
$$
The declared positive-entry threshold for partial $R^2$ was $10^{-12}$, and the rank-degeneracy cutoff was $0.999999$.
