\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate as an organism-level translation statistic}
\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family as a finite organism-level readout, not as a direct observation of ribosome motion. \paragraph{Finite codon window.}

$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The CUN part used by the leucine gate is $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, while the UUR leucine alternatives are $\{\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.  The finite window therefore has enough internal codon structure to name the split, but that internal naming alone is not the biological result; the biological contact is supplied only when the codon-usage row is paired with organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy data.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The checked cross-organism row loaded three organisms: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  The reported CUN/UUR log ratios were

The amino-acid-composition-controlled partial correlation is also reported as $1.0$ with $n=3$ organism rows.  Thus the row supports a narrow statement: in this three-organism table, the sign and magnitude of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ align positively with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated control.  The result is a finite statistical contact at the codon-usage and tRNA-copy level, not a claim that the same direction must hold for all organisms or all growth conditions.

When these two contacts are paired, they can test whether the CUN/UUR leucine split has an organism-dependent translation-layer statistical correlate. The result is therefore not a codon-label fact by itself and not merely an identifier convention; it is a bounded organism-level relation between codon usage and tRNA-Leu copy spectra.
