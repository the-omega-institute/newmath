\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Packet and biological coordinate.}
This record isolates a cross-organism leucine coding question at the level of finite codon-usage and tRNA-copy summaries.  The biological coordinate is the synonymous leucine split between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ block and the $\mathrm{UUR}$ block, paired with an organism label and an amino-acid-composition control.  The measured statistic is the per-organism value
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{O} = \log\bigl(\#\mathrm{CUN}_{O}/\#\mathrm{UUR}_{O}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where the subscript $O$ ranges over the organisms for which both codon-usage and tRNA-Leu copy-number summaries are present.  The finite run used three organism rows: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  The associated log-ratio values were $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$, with signs $+1$, $-1$, and $+1$.  Thus the observed sign pattern is not uniform across the three rows.

\paragraph{Codon universe attached to the gate.}
The companion finite codon check identifies the packet's codon set as the union $\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ with cardinality $20$.  The listed elements are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This finite enumeration matters because the leucine gate is not treated as a free verbal analogy.  It is attached to a concrete codon window containing the four $\mathrm{CUN}$ codons $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, and $\mathrm{CUU}$, together with the $\mathrm{UUR}$ leucine codons $\mathrm{UUA}$ and $\mathrm{UUG}$ inside the same recorded set.  The count $|M|=20$ is the row-level check used here, not an inference about protein behavior.

\paragraph{Observed relation.}
The validation row reports a positive association between the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ log-ratio statistic and the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after amino-acid composition control.  For the three organism rows, the reported Pearson value is $1.0$, and the reported partial Pearson value after the stated control is also $1.0$ with $n=3$.  In the finite data supplied to this chapter, the relevant summary is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{sign}(\ell_O) &= (+1,-1,+1),\\
\operatorname{Pearson}(\ell_O,\tau_O) &= 1.0,\\
\operatorname{PartialPearson}(\ell_O,\tau_O\mid\mathrm{AA\ composition}) &= 1.0,\quad n=3,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\tau_O$ denotes the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate.  The result supports only the narrow claim that, within these three loaded rows, the non-uniform $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ sign and magnitude track the recorded tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate under the stated composition control.  The run status attached to this statistic is recorded as passed.

\paragraph{Scope of the biological reading.}
The chapter's admissible reading is deliberately small.  Codon-usage tables are sequence-composition summaries, and tRNA gene-copy counts are genome-annotation summaries.  Their pairing can test whether an organism-level codon-usage residual co-varies with an organism-level tRNA-copy coordinate.  That is enough to record a bounded translation-layer contact in the sense of codon allocation versus tRNA-copy availability, but it is not an observation of ribosome dynamics.  It is also not a protein-output measurement.  No direct translation event, folding conclusion, physical admissibility claim, biological function claim, molecular mechanism, or universal cross-organism law follows from these rows unless an additional contact of the appropriate kind is supplied.

\paragraph{Failure modes held outside the claim.}
The sign reversal is context-dependent: the finite row set contains both positive and negative $\log(\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR})$ values, so the chapter does not promote either sign direction to a general biological rule.  The raw log-ratio alone is also insufficient; the reported statistic is read with the amino-acid-composition control explicitly present.  Likewise, the identifier spelling and the codon labels themselves do not supply biological evidence.  The only accepted evidence in this chapter is the paired organism-level table: three organism labels, the three log-ratio values, the three signs, the $n=3$ controlled partial-correlation report, and the finite codon-set check $|M|=20$ for $\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$.

\subsection{Residual Q6 Basis After Amino-Acid Quotient}\label{sec:residual-q6-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Object.}
This record isolates the part of codon topology that remains after amino-acid identity has already been accounted for.  The carrier is not a protein-folding object, not a biochemical mechanism, and not a universal law.  It is the per-organism codon-count table projected to the amino-acid-residual basis $B^{*}_{Q6}$.  The nine coordinates retained in this basis are
$$
\begin{aligned}

&Ser_{UCR/AGY},\ Ser_{UCA/UCG},\ Thr_{ACR/ACY},\ f_{3}\text{-stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The point of the construction is to keep only synonymous residual structure: a coordinate may be biologically interesting only after the amino-acid sequence $y$ is included among the controls.  Thus a positive residual score is never read as a claim about amino-acid composition itself.

\paragraph{Finite codon-topology facts.}
The codon-side contact gives a concrete finite table, not a population estimate.  The residual computation was carried out on three organism-level codon-count summaries: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. This establishes that the quotient has remaining coordinates in the finite table, but it does not by itself establish translation efficiency, ribosome behavior, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, cellular function, mechanism, or universality.

\end{aligned}
$$
The same check reports no missing codons and no extra codons relative to that motif description.  Hence the residual coordinates above are not introduced as an open-ended feature list; they are tied to a concrete finite codon subset.

\paragraph{Quotient geometry.}
The baseline quotient geometry supplies the comparison object against which a residual coordinate must be tested.  The $M$-complex has $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$

\end{aligned}
$$
This matters because an $M$-density model can be locally adequate while still being unable to ask every synonymous question in $B^{*}_{Q6}$.  In particular, a statistic that sees only $M$-membership cannot separately test the $CUN/UUR$ leucine split or the $AAA/AAG$ lysine split.  The residual basis is therefore a stricter probe than the $M$ quotient alone, but the stricter coordinate system is still only a coordinate system until paired with an organism-matched readout.

\paragraph{Translation-layer test.}
For a readout $r$ chosen from tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, and for controls $Z$ containing the amino-acid sequence $y$, local context $c$, and simpler codon-feature baselines, the intended survival statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q).
\end{aligned}
$$
A residual coordinate $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$ survives only if adding that coordinate improves the controlled score.  The operational counterfactual is sharper when the $M$ baseline is included:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null form says that, for every organism, every accepted readout, and every residual coordinate $Q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$, adding the residual coordinate to the amino-acid-controlled $M$ baseline fails to improve the score.  A break can occur if at least one organism and at least one readout gives positive controlled survival for some residual coordinate, or if the $CUN/UUR$ leucine log-ratio reverses sign between organisms after amino-acid composition control while the $M$ baseline does not capture the reversal.

\paragraph{Contacts and current gap.}
Three empirical contact classes are relevant here.  Per-organism codon-usage resources can supply coding-sequence count or frequency summaries.  Organism-level tRNA gene-copy annotations can supply anticodon-family copy-number spectra, including leucine, arginine, isoleucine, serine, and threonine families.  tAI or stAI resources can supply bounded translation-adaptation weights.  These contacts are sufficient to define the kind of controlled statistic required by the conjecture, but the current finite record does not yet contain an organism-matched table joining codon usage, tRNA copy number, and tAI or stAI values with estimates of $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$.

Consequently the present status is a bounded conjectural measurement plan plus a finite codon-topology computation.  The computation shows that the post-amino-acid quotient is nontrivial in the three-organism codon-count contact, with $7$ nonzero residuals among $9$ coordinates.  It does not show that any coordinate changes actual translation, ribosome occupancy, or protein output.  It also does not say anything about folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biochemical mechanism, or a global biological rule.  Any such conclusion would require a separate matched contact at the relevant biological layer, not merely a nonzero residual coordinate.
