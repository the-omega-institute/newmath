\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Boundary}\label{sec:codon-window6-local-tile-boundary}

\paragraph{Claim.}

The biological contact is only the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label. The mathematical object is therefore a finite labelled cube with a local face-and-corner pattern, not a model of translation dynamics. In this setting the claim is that a distinguished local tile can be read in the standard-code labels, and that its boundary behavior is strong enough to deserve a code-layer packet. The packet is deliberately limited to code readback. It does not assert that DNA must obey this geometry, that ribosomal translation is determined by the tile, that a peptide folds, that a molecule is physically admissible, that a biological function is realized, or that the observed tile is a universal biological mechanism.

The distinguished relation $R$ has thirteen codons. In standard RNA notation its finite row is

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The curated standard-code table is the only reality contact used here. It supports codon-to-label readback and local split checks. It has no observation of ribosomal implementation, no folded-structure measurement, no admissibility assay, no function assay, and no universality theorem. The packet cannot say: this tile is translation itself, this tile determines protein folding, this tile supplies physical admissibility, this tile entails biological function, or this tile is a global law of biology. Any such stronger statement would require a separate contact of the relevant kind, not merely another reading of the codon label table.

\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ and $UUR$ blocks as an organism-indexed finite statistic, not as a free-standing biochemical mechanism. The carrier is the residual coordinate consisting of the signed quantity $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$, an organism label, an organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control on synonymous codon classes. The point of the construction is to ask whether the leucine block split changes direction across organisms and whether that change is aligned with an independently listed tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum. The admissible readout is therefore a paired table over organisms: one coordinate records the observed codon-usage imbalance, and the other coordinate records the annotated tRNA-Leu copy ratio.

\paragraph{Codon-set anchor.}
The finite codon anchor used by the packet is the verified set $M=WNR\cup CUN$, with cardinality $20$. In the recorded run this set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The four $CUN$ leucine codons appearing in this anchor are $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$, while the companion leucine block used in the ratio is $UUR=\{UUA,UUG\}$. This finite support is not itself a biological conclusion. It supplies the codon coordinates on which the organism-level count ratio is later evaluated.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The cross-organism validation loaded three organism rows: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$. The recorded $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
Their signs were
$$
\begin{aligned}
+1,\quad -1,\quad +1,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the sign was not uniform over the measured organisms. This is the minimal finite observation: at least within the three loaded rows, the CUN-versus-UUR imbalance is not a single organism-independent direction. The packet therefore passes the first counterexample check against a universal sign rule.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu statistic.}
The same run records a Pearson correlation of $1.0$ for the organism-level paired statistic and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu copy ratio after the stated amino-acid-composition control. The controlled statistic is reported with
$$
\begin{aligned}
n=3,\qquad r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite row set, the observed sign reversal is therefore accompanied by a positive controlled association with the tRNA-Leu copy coordinate. Because the sample has only three organism rows, the number should be read as an exact finite-row result for the loaded table rather than as an asymptotic population claim. No p-value is recorded for this packet, so no significance threshold is asserted here.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The admissible external contacts are two organism-level sequence resources: codon-usage summaries for coding sequences and genome-annotation summaries of tRNA-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family. These contacts can check whether an organism-indexed codon-usage imbalance is paired with an organism-indexed tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum. They do not measure ribosome transit, decoding kinetics, protein abundance, folding state, cellular phenotype, or fitness. Thus the present record is a bounded translation-layer statistic only in the narrow sense that it compares codon usage with a translation-related genomic annotation.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result does not license a stronger biological reading by itself. From the finite rows above one may report non-uniform $\log(CUN/UUR)$ signs and the recorded positive controlled association with tRNA-Leu copy ratio. One may not infer an actual ribosome-mediated translation event, a kinetic mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, protein function, organismal function, or a universal cross-organism law unless an additional contact supplies that specific readout. In particular, the negative yeast-side sign and the positive signs in the other two loaded organisms are context-dependent table entries, not a rule saying that one leucine block is globally preferred. The row is therefore retained as a finite organism-paired codon/tRNA observation with a strict boundary against mechanism, structure, function, and universality.

\subsection{Residual codon topology after amino-acid quotienting}
\label{sec:residual-basis-q6-topology-after-aa-quotient-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
The object considered here is the amino-acid-residual codon basis $B^{*}_{Q6}$ obtained from per-organism coding-sequence codon counts after amino-acid composition has been held fixed. The carrier is not the amino-acid sequence itself, and it is not a direct readout of folding, molecular function, or organismal fitness. It is the residual coordinate system left over after the amino-acid quotient has removed the part of codon usage explained by the translated amino-acid string. The nine named residual directions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{*}_{Q6}=\{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN/UUR},\ Leu_{UUA/UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR/AGY},\ Ser_{UCA/UCG},\ Thr_{ACR/ACY},\ f_{3}\text{-stress}_{\mathrm{antisym}}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A translation-layer test is therefore only licensed when a readout $r$ such as a codon adaptation weight, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance is paired with the same organism and with a context vector $Z$ that includes the amino-acid sequence $y$ and simpler codon-feature controls.

\paragraph{Finite codon-contact data.}
The finite contact used to establish that the coordinate is nontrivial consists of three per-organism codon-count tables: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli}$ K--12. In this finite panel the residual computation reports $9$ basis elements, of which $7$ exceed the stated variance threshold $0.01$. The resulting nonzero fraction is $0.7777777777777778$. This is a finite cross-organism contact, not a population estimate and not a universal biological law. Its role is to show that the amino-acid quotient does not collapse all $Q6$ residual directions in these organisms.

\paragraph{Motif compression and quotient geometry.}
The lower-level motif contact identifies the residual-relevant codon set $R$ exactly as
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,\ AGA,\ AGG,\ AUA,\ CUA,\ CUC,\ CUG,\ CUU,\\
&UAA,\ UAG,\ UCA,\ UGA,\ UUA\}.

The comparison reports no missing codons and no extra codons. The associated $M$ geometry has face vector

The quotient image has size $10$ and is represented by the rows

These finite rows are the reason the $M$-only coordinate is not sufficient as a test of all amino-acid-residual distinctions: membership in $M$ can describe a local codon class, but it cannot by itself distinguish contrasts such as $CUN$ versus $UUR$ leucine usage or $AAA$ versus $AAG$ lysine usage after amino-acid composition has been controlled.

\paragraph{Translation-layer survival statistic.}
For a residual coordinate $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$ and a readout $r$, the intended statistic compares a baseline model $J_0$ using $Z$ with a model $J_Q$ that also sees $q$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive value means that the residual coordinate improves the bounded readout under the specified controls. The sharper test against an $M$-density baseline is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
for at least one organism, at least one readout $r\in\{\mathrm{tAI},\ \mathrm{ribosome\ occupancy},\ \mathrm{protein\ abundance}\}$, and at least one residual coordinate $Q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$. Equivalently, the null model says that after controlling for amino-acid sequence and the $M$-density baseline, every residual coordinate satisfies $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)\leq 0$.

\paragraph{Present evidential status.}
The current finite rows support the existence of a nontrivial residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting: $9$ residual directions are computed, $7$ are nonzero above threshold in a three-organism panel, and the quotient geometry has the explicit $10$-row image displayed above. They do not yet supply an organism-matched statistic combining codon usage, relevant tRNA copy-number spectra, and a tAI or stAI readout under amino-acid-composition control. Thus the translation-realization claim remains a controlled conjecture: the geometry says where to test, while the translation readout must come from a separate matched contact.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
No translation-realization conclusion follows from nonzero codon-count residual variance alone. No ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, protein-output claim, or universal biological rule follows from the coordinate computation. A positive survival score would be conditional on the chosen organism, readout, and context vector $Z$; it would not by itself assert folding, function, or universality. Conversely, the present absence of a paired controlled statistic is a blocked null state, not evidence that the $M$-only coordinate is sufficient.

\subsection{Translation Survival Matrix for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-translation-survival-b-star-q6-survival-matrix-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Claim shape.}
This record asks whether the nine residual coordinates of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ survive after the amino-acid quotient when they are tested against translation-related readouts. The carrier is a finite matrix $S_{ij}$ whose rows are the nine residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \in \{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN/UUR},\ Leu_{UUA/UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR/AGY},\ Ser_{UCA/UCG},\ Thr_{ACR/ACY},\ f3\text{-stress}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
and whose columns range over organism-matched readouts such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, and protein abundance when those contacts are explicitly attached. For each coordinate-readout pair, the intended statistic is not the raw covariance and not the sign of a proxy association. It is the controlled survival score obtained after quotienting amino-acid composition and after conditioning on organism context, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the simpler $M$-density baseline. In the notation of this packet, the readout direction is first projected to its synonymous component $\ell_j^{\perp}$, the proxy coupling is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ij}=q_i^{T}\Sigma \ell_j^{\perp},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the survival entry $S_{ij}$ is the partial-$R^2$ or description-length improvement left after the full control set $Z$ is present.

\paragraph{Finite rows already attached.}
Two finite computations are currently attached to the packet. The first computation used a $61$-codon count space and computed all nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates listed above. Its break threshold for the proxy coupling was $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ for every coordinate against the available proxy readout. That break condition was not met: all $9$ coordinates had nonzero proxy entries, and the reported largest absolute coupling was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}|=1882464.1106377328.
\end{aligned}
$$
The organisms loaded in that finite row were Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K-12. The readout, however, was a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum, not a direct tAI, stAI, ribosome-profiling, translation-efficiency, or protein-abundance survival row. Thus the row is useful evidence that the residual coordinates are not numerically silent under the attached codon-usage and tRNA-copy proxy, but it does not by itself fill any $S_{ij}$ entry.

\paragraph{CUN/UUR Leu contact.}
The second finite computation isolates the cross-organism Leu CUN/UUR gate over the same three organisms. The value $n=3$ is too small to promote the statistic to a population-level estimate, and the attached row gives no $p$-value or non-trivial null distribution for an eight-or-more-organism version.

\paragraph{Survival test still open.}
The live conjecture is the following controlled alternative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\exists i,j\quad S_{ij}=S_{q_i}(r_j\mid Z)>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, length, and the $M$-density baseline. Equivalently, the conjecture says that the minimal model using amino-acid sequence, context, and the simpler codon baseline does not absorb every $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate on every translation-related readout. The presently attached rows do not establish this alternative. They establish only that the nine residual coordinates can be computed, that proxy couplings against a tRNA-copy spectrum exceed the $0.01$ silence threshold, and that a three-organism Leu CUN/UUR relation has a perfect finite-row correlation while retaining organism-level sign reversal.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No realized translation claim follows from the current proxy rows unless a direct translation contact supplies the missing readout and the controlled $S_{ij}$ statistic is computed. In particular, tRNA gene-copy ratio is not treated as tRNA abundance, tRNA charging, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein output. Ribosome profiling, if later attached, would test a bounded translation readout only after matched mRNA control and identifier alignment. Protein abundance, if later attached, would be a downstream abundance readout, not evidence of folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law. The present chapter therefore records a precise open survival-matrix test: proxy coupling is nonzero in the attached finite rows, but translation-readout survival remains unfilled until organism-matched tAI, stAI, ribosome-profiling, translation-efficiency, or protein-abundance entries are estimated under the stated controls.
