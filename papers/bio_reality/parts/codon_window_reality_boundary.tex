\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family as a bounded organism-level measurement problem.  The carrier is a paired residual: one coordinate records the signed codon-usage contrast $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, while the second coordinate records the organism-matched tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after controlling for amino-acid composition.  The organism label is not a biological mechanism; it is the index that prevents a human, yeast, or bacterial row from being collapsed into a single universal direction.  The admissible readback is therefore narrow: for each organism, the packet compares the sign and magnitude of the CUN-over-UUR usage contrast with the corresponding tRNA-Leu copy spectrum under a synonymous-class control.

\paragraph{Finite code-window support.}
The lower-level codon-window check fixes the finite set used by the packet.  The verified row reports $|M|=20$ and identifies $M$ with the union of the WNR window and the CUN window.  Explicitly, the twenty codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The CUN component appearing in this set is $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, while the UUR leucine component used by the contrast is $\{\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.  This finite support is a coordinate scaffold only.  It licenses a codon-label and codon-usage statistic; it does not license a protein-level conclusion.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The checked cross-organism table contains three organism rows: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  Their observed CUN/UUR log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.3514980212896148,\qquad -0.24869078488829677,\qquad 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated signs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
+1,\qquad -1,\qquad +1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the three measured organisms.  This matters because the packet is not asking whether CUN is globally preferred over UUR.  It asks whether the usage contrast changes with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy spectrum once the amino-acid composition background has been separated from the synonymous codon choice.

\paragraph{Controlled relation.}
For the three-row table, the recorded Pearson correlation between the codon-usage contrast and the tRNA-Leu ratio is $1.0$.  The amino-acid-composition-controlled statistic is also positive in the available finite sample: the partial Pearson value is $1.0$ with $n=3$.  In the packet notation, the measured relation can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{corr}\bigl(\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR}),\ \mathrm{tRNA\mbox{-}Leu\ copy\ ratio}\bigr)&=1.0,\\
\operatorname{pcorr}\bigl(\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR}),\ \mathrm{tRNA\mbox{-}Leu\ copy\ ratio}\mid \mathrm{AA\ composition}\bigr)&=1.0,\\
n&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore a passed finite-row translation-layer contact in the limited sense supplied by the two outside biological tables: codon usage per organism and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotation per organism.  The negative yeast-side sign and the positive human and bacterial signs are retained as the essential cross-organism feature; they prevent the statistic from being restated as a fixed sign law.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback is organism-level and paired.  A raw CUN/UUR sign by itself is not enough, and a tRNA-Leu copy count by itself is not enough.  The packet becomes informative only when both are present for the same organism row and the comparison is made after the amino-acid composition control.  On the supplied rows, the finite evidence supports the statement that the CUN/UUR leucine usage contrast is context-dependent and aligns with the paired tRNA-Leu copy ratio in this three-organism table.  It does not say that the same sign must occur in all organisms, nor does it say that every leucine-coding context is explained by gene-copy counts alone.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The conclusion is deliberately confined to codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact.  No translation process, folding behavior, physical admissibility, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this table unless a separate contact supplies that stronger observation.  In particular, the finite values $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$ are not protein-structure measurements; the signs $+1,-1,+1$ are not functional annotations; and the correlations $1.0$ and $1.0$ at $n=3$ are not a general theorem about all organisms.  The chapter records a bounded cross-organism usage relation, with the non-uniform sign and the paired tRNA-Leu statistic as its entire empirical content.

The conjectural test is conditional. \paragraph{Local boundary.}

Codon-count residuals alone also do not establish ribosome-mediated translation realization, protein abundance, or structure. \paragraph{Null interpretation.}

Until that paired statistic is computed, the absence of a positive $S_Q$ value is not evidence for the sufficiency of an $M$-only model.  It is only an absence of the required controlled comparison.  The next finite test is to compute, for each organism and each available readout, whether every $q\in B^*_{Q6}$ satisfies $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)\leq 0$, or whether at least one residual coordinate survives the $M$-baseline comparison.
