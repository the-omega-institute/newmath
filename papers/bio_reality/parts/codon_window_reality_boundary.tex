\begin{aligned}
S(q_i,t_j\mid Z)>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Delta(q_i,t_j,p_k\mid Z,m)>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, and $M$-density baseline, and $m$ denotes matched mRNA abundance control.  The first inequality says that the residual coordinate survives at a translation readout.  The second says that the residual-to-translation edge improves downstream matched protein-abundance readback after transcription-layer abundance has been accounted for.  A direct association
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow p_k
\end{aligned}
$$
without the intermediate surviving translation node is not admitted as protein realization.  It is only an uncontrolled cross-layer association and remains outside the support rule.

\paragraph{Separation from transcription-only signal.}
Matched mRNA abundance has a restricted role in this chapter.  It is a control for separating translation-layer signal from transcription-layer abundance, not itself a translation readout.  In a ribosome-profiling setting, translation efficiency may be written schematically as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{TE}=\frac{\mathrm{RPF}}{\mathrm{mRNA}},
\end{aligned}
$$
but the mRNA term alone does not observe ribosome occupancy, protein output, folding, function, mechanism, or a population law.  The same restriction applies to protein-abundance contacts: steady-state protein abundance is downstream readback, affected by turnover, ionization efficiency, sample preparation, and isoform aggregation.  It can participate in a mediated matrix only after the translation node has survived; it cannot replace that node.
