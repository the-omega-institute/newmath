Let $X_Q$ denote the nine-dimensional residual-coordinate block after amino-acid composition, organism-scoped terms, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched abundance controls have been accounted for where available.  Let $T$ denote per-protein modeled tAI and $Y$ denote log protein abundance.  The reported scalar is the fraction of the residual-to-protein association removed by conditioning on $T$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{R^2(Q\to P)-R^2(Q\to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to P)} .
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\mathrm{E.\ coli}$ K12 MG1655, $R^2(Q\to P)=0.10767526677985896$, $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.04167869969498872$, the indirect difference is $0.06599656708487024$, and $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$. For yeast, they are $0.29837624994140827$, $0.0151709093608293$, $0.28320534058057895$, and $0.9491551041216973$.  All three mediated fractions lie in $[0,1]$, so the finite rows support a bounded statistical mediation readback in which modeled tAI absorbs part of the residual-to-protein association.

The energy explained by $Q$ on $T$ is $0.256128714706293$, $0.026187928294862578$, and $5.671703404506426$.  The energy explained by $Q$ on $Y$ is $432.89050065533684$, $183.85037813566998$, and $882.6526210803958$. \paragraph{Verdict and boundary.}
The chapter records a finite, controlled statistical relation: the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be arranged with modeled per-protein tAI and matched protein abundance into a nondegenerate $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition.  The strongest finite-row effect occurs in yeast, where $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$; the weakest occurs in $\mathrm{M.\ smegmatis}$, where $M^{QTP}=0.08397602919861291$.
