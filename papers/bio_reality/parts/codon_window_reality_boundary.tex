\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate}
\label{subsec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Empirical packet.}
This chapter records a bounded cross-organism test for the leucine codon pair split between the CUN family and the UUR family.  The data object is not a protein, a folding trajectory, a ribosome movie, or a cellular phenotype.  It is a finite organism-indexed table whose coordinates are the sign and magnitude of $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$, a tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, an organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The intended biological reading is deliberately narrow: an organism-level codon-usage residual is paired with an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum, and the question is whether the leucine-family sign varies with that copy-number spectrum after the synonymous-class control is imposed.

\paragraph{Finite codon support.}
The underlying codon support recorded for the companion finite fact is the $20$-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
     &CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cardinality is $|M|=20$, and the same row reports that this set is the union named by the WNR/CUN readback.  For the leucine gate itself, the active comparison is between $CUN=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ and $UUR=\{UUA,UUG\}$ inside organism-level codon-usage summaries.  The chapter uses this finite support only as a coordinate discipline: it fixes which codon labels are being counted and prevents the organism-level statistic from drifting into a claim about protein output.

\paragraph{Observed organism rows.}
The cross-organism validation row loaded three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  Their recorded $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1,\;-1,\;1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  The recorded field $sign\_uniform=false$ is the important finite outcome: the leucine split is not being treated as a single universal direction attached to the codon alphabet alone.  It is an organism-indexed statistic whose direction can reverse when the organism row changes.

\paragraph{Controlled tRNA-Leu relation.}
The same validation row reports a Pearson coefficient of $1.0$ for the paired statistic and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio after the amino-acid-composition control.  The controlled row is explicitly finite: it records $n=3$ and $partial\_pearson=1.0$.  In symbolic form, the packet is asking for the sign and magnitude relation

to be compared against the organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the synonymous leucine composition effect has been controlled.  The row status is recorded as passed for this finite test, with experiment id $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$ and experiment run id $675fdddb3187$.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The ready claim is therefore a restricted translation-layer contact in the sense of codon-usage and tRNA-copy co-variation.  It says that the three loaded organism rows show non-uniform $CUN/UUR$ sign and a positive controlled association with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.  It does not say that the ribosome has been directly observed choosing one leucine family over the other in a mechanistic assay.  It also does not say that any encoded protein folds, becomes physically admissible, performs a function, or obeys a universal biological law.  Those stronger statements would require separate measurements of ribosome dynamics, protein structure, physical chemistry, phenotypic function, or broader comparative sampling.  The present row is a constrained organism-level usage-and-copy-number contact, not a promotion to those layers.

\paragraph{Null tests and failure modes.}
The proposed counterexample search is equally finite.  The packet would fail if all measured organisms had a uniform $\log(CUN/UUR)$ sign independent of the tRNA-Leu copy spectrum.  It would also fail if sign reversal appeared but had no positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio after the amino-acid-composition control.  The loaded row avoids both failures within its three-organism sample: the signs are $(1,-1,1)$, and the controlled partial correlation is recorded as $1.0$.  Because the row has only $n=3$, this is not a population-level estimate with robust uncertainty quantification.  It is a compact empirical contact showing that the codon-usage coordinate can be matched to organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number data without turning the result into a mechanism, a structure claim, a function claim, or a universal rule.

The chapter therefore keeps two facts together and no more.  First, the codon-label carrier used by the surrounding packet has the recorded $20$-member support $M$.  Second, the cross-organism leucine gate has a passed finite validation row over $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$, with non-uniform signs and a controlled positive association with tRNA-Leu copy ratio. \end{aligned}

\begin{aligned}

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The finite facts above support only a residual-coordinate formulation and a blocked empirical test.  They do not imply translation realization, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, protein abundance, or universality.  A nonzero codon-count residual is a coordinate-level observation.  A positive survival score, if later obtained, would still be conditional on $Z$, on the chosen readout, and on the organismal sampling frame.  Promotion to folding, function, mechanism, or a global biological rule would require a distinct contact that directly measures the promoted layer.
