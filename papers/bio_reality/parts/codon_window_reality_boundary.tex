\subsection{Translation-mediated protein abundance for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
This record tests whether synonymous residual coordinates in the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ packet can be read as a controlled cross-layer relation only after passing through a translation readout. The finite surface consists of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$, where $q_i$ ranges over the nine computed residual coordinates
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$; $t_j$ is a translation readout; and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout. The admissible readback is not a direct residual-to-protein arrow. It is a controlled mediation matrix in which the residual coordinate is first tested against a translation quantity and only then compared with protein abundance:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow P_k,\\
T_j &= \text{per-protein mean modeled tAI},\\
P_k &= \log_{10}(\text{PAXdb abundance ppm}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation quantity used here is per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as the codon-count-weighted mean of codon tAI weights derived from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights. The protein quantity is measured PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts. Amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance remain controls rather than promoted coordinates.

\paragraph{Modeled translation contact.}
The translation-side finite contact was computed for three organisms. The modeled tAI readout covered all 61 sense codons in \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with zero raw-weight fallback count equal to $0$ in all three modeled-tAI codon tables. The local tRNA payload contained all $20$ standard amino-acid families in each organism. Header counts were $89$ for \textit{E. coli} K-12, $432$ for human, and $275$ for yeast; matched header counts were $87$, $432$, and $275$, respectively. At the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$, the modeled $S_{ij}$ table had $81$ entries above threshold, with maximum absolute entry $3{,}801{,}961.031607896$ and the same value on the maximum absolute diagonal entry. This contact is therefore a nonzero modeled codon-adaptation readout, but it is not ribosome profiling, not observed translation efficiency, and not a protein-output measurement. The organism count is $3$, so the powered-survival requirement of at least $40$ matched species is not met for a population-level translation-survival claim.

\paragraph{Joined protein-abundance rows.}
The cross-layer computation joined CDS codon counts, modeled per-protein tAI, and measured protein abundance in three organism-scoped datasets. The usable row counts were $3739$ proteins for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $4129$ proteins for \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ proteins for \textit{S. cerevisiae}. The corresponding all-tRNA copy totals used for the modeled tAI computation were $90$, $49$, and $275$. The geometric-mean replacement count for zero raw codon weights was $1$ in \textit{E. coli}, $1$ in \textit{M. smegmatis}, and $0$ in yeast. Each organism retained a rank-sufficient residualized design: the control rank was $23$, the control column count was $24$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{XT})=10$. The residual degrees of freedom after controls were $3716$, $4106$, and $5792$, respectively.

\paragraph{Mediation decomposition.}
After residualizing the declared controls, the joint nine-dimensional $X_Q$ block was decomposed by conditioning on the modeled translation readout. In \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, the total residual-to-protein $R^2$ was $0.10767526677985896$, the direct $R^2$ after conditioning on $T$ was $0.04167869969498872$, and the indirect difference $c-c'$ was $0.06599656708487024$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$. In \textit{M. smegmatis}, the corresponding values were $0.03209009170649934$, $0.029395293228368185$, $0.0026947984781311544$, and $M^{QTP}=0.08397602919861291$. In yeast, they were $0.29837624994140827$, $0.0151709093608293$, $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$. All three mediated fractions lie in the unit interval.
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{E.\ coli} &= 0.612922252793667,\\
M^{QTP}_{M.\ smegmatis} &= 0.08397602919861291,\\
M^{QTP}_{S.\ cerevisiae} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
The residual energy audit is consistent with a finite statistical decomposition rather than a free narrative claim. In \textit{E. coli}, $X$ explained $432.89050065533684$ units of residual protein-abundance energy and $T$ explained $582.5912438239341$; the incremental improvement of $T$ given $X$ was $317.26302294928666$, while the direct improvement of $X$ given $T$ was $167.56227978068944$. In \textit{M. smegmatis}, the same quantities were $183.85037813566998$, $15.813547327443837$, $0.37452260494680445$, and $168.41135341317295$. In yeast, they were $882.6526210803958$, $941.8858618613751$, $104.11162139652299$, and $44.87838061554362$.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The finite result supports a bounded cross-layer statistical readback: for the tested organisms, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual block has a nonzero protein-abundance association whose decomposition changes after conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI. The strongest mediated fraction in this finite table is yeast, $0.9491551041216973$; the weakest is \textit{M. smegmatis}, $0.08397602919861291$. The result is therefore not a universal sign law, not a CUN/UUR population rule, and not an organism-independent biological principle. It is a finite, organism-scoped mediation table whose admissible interpretation is: modeled translation adaptation can absorb a positive portion of the residual-to-protein-abundance association under the stated controls.

\paragraph{Local scope restriction.}
No translation mechanism follows from this chapter by itself. Modeled tAI is not ribosome occupancy, not ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and not a perturbation experiment. Conditioning on modeled tAI in cross-sectional CDS/protein rows is a statistical mediation decomposition, not a causal effect. Protein abundance is not isolated here from mRNA abundance, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, pathway context, or sample-specific proteomics bias. Consequently this chapter does not claim folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism realization, selection, or universality. Any such claim requires a separate layer-matched contact and a new controlled statistic at that layer. A direct $q_i \to P_k$ association remains inadmissible as protein realization unless the same residual coordinate first survives the $q_i \to T_j$ translation-readout step under the declared controls.
