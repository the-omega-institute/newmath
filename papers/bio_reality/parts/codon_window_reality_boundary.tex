\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family as an organism-level measurement problem.  The carrier is the residual codon-usage coordinate
$\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, paired with an organism label and with the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio.  The intended readout is not a protein-level fact.  It is the sign and size of the CUN-versus-UUR usage imbalance, compared across organisms after controlling for amino-acid composition by restricting attention to synonymous leucine usage.

The finite codon-side anchor used by the packet is the recorded set $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ with cardinality $20$.  In the row audit this set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality check reports both $|M|=20$ and size $20$.  The leucine gate then isolates the CUN members $\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}$ against the UUR members $\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}$ and asks whether their relative usage is aligned with organism-specific tRNA-Leu copy-number structure.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The experiment row named $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$ loads three organisms: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  The reported CUN-over-UUR log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is explicitly non-uniform across the measured organisms.  In this finite table the ordinary Pearson coefficient between the CUN/UUR coordinate and the paired tRNA-Leu ratio is reported as $1.0$.  The amino-acid-composition-controlled statistic is also positive in the recorded row: the partial Pearson value is $1.0$ with $n=3$ organisms.  The experiment status is $\mathrm{passed}$.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the pass.}
The row supports a narrow organism-level translation-realization contact: for the three loaded organisms, the CUN/UUR leucine imbalance changes sign, and the measured magnitude is positively aligned with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after synonymous-class control.  The positive partial correlation is not being read as a biochemical law.  It is a finite paired statistic connecting two external summaries: codon-usage tables and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations.

The sign reversal matters because a single fixed direction would make the packet much less informative as a cross-organism gate.  Human and $E.\ coli$ K12 appear on the positive side of the log-ratio in the recorded rows, while yeast appears on the negative side.  The finite result is therefore not that CUN always dominates UUR, nor that UUR always dominates CUN.  The result is that the sign and magnitude are organism-sensitive in the three-row sample and, in that same sample, track the paired tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
This chapter does not infer ribosome motion, elongation kinetics, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a general law of organismal codon usage.  The codon-usage contact records sequence-composition summaries, and the tRNA contact records genome-annotation copy-number summaries.  Those contacts justify the bounded comparison above, but they do not by themselves observe translation events at the ribosome, structural outcomes in proteins, cellular function, or a universal mechanism.

Consequently the admissible claim is deliberately small.  The row licenses the statement that, in the three-organism finite table, $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ is non-uniform and positively paired with the recorded tRNA-Leu ratio under the stated synonymous-class control.  Any stronger statement would require a separate contact: ribosome profiling or stAI-like measurements for translation dynamics, structural assays for folding, biophysical tests for physical admissibility, functional assays for organismal role, and broader sampling with uncertainty estimates for claims of generality.

\paragraph{Audit status.}
The packet is ready as a finite BioReality chapter because its cited rows give concrete codon lists, organism names, scalar log-ratios, sign data, and correlation values.  Its strength is the explicit pairing between codon usage and tRNA-Leu copy spectra in a controlled three-row comparison.  Its main limitation is also explicit: $n=3$ and Pearson value $1.0$ should be read as an exact finite-row report, not as a population-level estimate with quantified uncertainty.

\paragraph{Question.}

$B^{*}_{Q6}$. $$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z) &= J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q),\\
Z &= (y,c,\hbox{simpler codon-feature baselines}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where positive survival means that the residual coordinate $q$ improves the readout model relative to the controlled baseline.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate facts.}
The finite row currently supporting the coordinate construction is explicit. The motif union $R$ is exactly the thirteen-codon set
$\{AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU, UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}$, with no missing and no extra codons in the recorded check. $$
The residual-basis computation was run on three organism-level codon-count contacts: human, budding yeast, and \emph{E. coli} K-12. Among the nine residual coordinates, seven were nonzero above the stated variance threshold $0.01$, giving nonzero fraction $7/9=0.7777777777777778$. This is a finite contact result, not a population estimate and not a universal frequency statement.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the residual basis.}
The result says that the amino-acid quotient does not erase all Q6 codon distinctions in the available organismal codon-count rows. This is already stronger than a statement about membership in the motif set $M$: an $M$-density description can see whether codons lie in the selected motif region, but it cannot by itself distinguish $AAA$ from $AAG$, nor can it test the $CUN/UUR$ leucine split. The residual basis is therefore the appropriate coordinate system for a controlled test of synonymous codon structure after amino-acid composition has been accounted for. The test remains local to the specified readout: it asks whether a residual coordinate contributes information beyond amino-acid sequence and baseline codon features, not whether Q6 topology has an unconditional biological role.

\paragraph{Required empirical contact.}
The available contacts identify the kinds of measurements needed for the next statistic. Per-organism codon-usage tables provide codon-count or frequency summaries for coding sequences. Genome annotation tables provide organism-level tRNA copy-number spectra, including anticodon-family information. tAI or stAI resources provide bounded codon-adaptation weights that may be used as translation-layer readouts when they remain separate from the coordinate construction. The current row does not yet contain an organism-matched table combining codon usage, tRNA copy number, and tAI or stAI values into a controlled estimate of $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$. Thus the present status is a blocked null at the readout stage: the coordinate and contact classes are in place, but the controlled statistic has not yet been computed.

\paragraph{Conjectural test.}
The falsifiable comparison is the following. If $M$-density alone is the relevant DNA-to-protein variable, then for every organism, every readout $r$ in the chosen translation-layer family, and every residual coordinate $Q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M) \leq 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
A break occurs if some residual coordinate has $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)>0$ in at least one organism on at least one admissible readout, or if a controlled leucine $CUN/UUR$ log-ratio reverses sign across organisms while the $M$-density baseline does not account for the reversal. Such a result would support bounded translation-layer survival of a synonymous residual coordinate. It would not, by itself, establish a ribosome-mediated mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The finite codon-count result does not establish translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, molecular mechanism, protein-output causality, or a universal biological law. None of those conclusions follows from nonzero residual variance, from the thirteen-codon motif list, from the $M$ and quotient face vectors, or from the three-organism contact alone. A higher claim requires a separate measurement at the corresponding layer. In the present form, the chapter records a precise residual coordinate, the finite organismal rows on which it is nontrivial, and the missing controlled statistic needed before a positive translation-realization conclusion can be drawn.
