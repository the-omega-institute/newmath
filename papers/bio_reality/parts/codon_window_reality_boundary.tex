\subsection{Residual $Q6$ Coordinates After Amino-Acid Quotienting}
\label{sec:bior-reality-residual-basis-q6-translation-realization}

\paragraph{Coordinate under test.}
The object considered here is not raw codon usage and not amino-acid composition. It is the residual codon coordinate left after the amino-acid sequence has already been accounted for. For each organism, the carrier is the per-organism coding-sequence codon-count table projected to the amino-acid residual basis $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$. The basis has nine named coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{\ast}_{Q6}=\{&K_{AAA},\ \operatorname{Arg}_{AGR},\ \operatorname{Ile}_{AUA},\ \operatorname{Leu}_{CUN/UUR},\ \operatorname{Leu}_{UUA/UUG},\\
&\operatorname{Ser}_{UCR/AGY},\ \operatorname{Ser}_{UCA/UCG},\ \operatorname{Thr}_{ACR/ACY},\ f_{3}\text{-stress antisymmetric}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The intended readout is conditional. For a residual coordinate $q$ and a measured readout $r$, the survival score is read as
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes the amino-acid sequence $y$, local context $c$, and simpler codon-feature baselines. The chapter therefore asks a restricted question: after amino-acid composition has been held fixed, does any residual $Q6$ coordinate improve a bounded organism-level readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance over the baseline model? A positive value of $S_Q$ is only meaningful relative to the specified $Z$ and the specified readout; it is not a free-standing biological law.

\paragraph{Testable survival claim.}
The bounded conjecture is the following. Let $\Delta_M$ denote the $M$-density baseline, and let $r$ be an organism-level readout from $\{\text{tAI},\text{ribosome occupancy},\text{protein abundance}\}$. \begin{aligned}

\begin{aligned}

\paragraph{Reality contacts and their limits.}
Three contact classes are relevant. Per-organism codon-usage tables provide sequence-composition readback. Organism-level tRNA gene-copy annotations provide anticodon-family spectra, including leucine, arginine, isoleucine, serine, and threonine families. tAI or stAI tables provide bounded codon-adaptation weights that can be used as modeled translation-layer readouts when kept separate from the coordinate construction. Consequently, the present chapter records a well-posed test and a nontrivial finite residual coordinate calculation, not a completed positive survival result.
