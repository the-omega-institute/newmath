\subsection{Window-six local tile boundary for the standard code}
\label{subsec:codon-window-six-local-tile-boundary}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The window-six codon display treats a codon as a point of a six-coordinate cube $Q_6$ and asks only whether the standard-code label table creates a small, locally organized boundary tile inside that finite coordinate system. The claim is deliberately at the code-read layer: the table supplies amino-acid or stop labels for codons, while the coordinate construction supplies adjacency, closure, spectrum, and relation data. The point is not that the coordinate cube is a biological cause. It is that the curated standard-code labels, when read against this six-bit display, isolate a thirteen-codon relation $R$ with a stable local shape.

\paragraph{Finite relation.}
The finite relation singled out by the row data has cardinality $13$ and consists exactly of
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same thirteen codons are reported by the punctured-cube check. Its cell count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$

The module partition also closes on the same thirteen codons. The reported modules are $\{AGA,AGG\}$ for the AGR pair, $\{AAA\}$ for the lysine-side singleton, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ for the CUN block, $\{AUA\}$ for the Ile/Met singleton, $\{UCA,UUA\}$ for the SSN-side pair, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$ for the stop/Trp-side triple. The module audit gives $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The external contact used here is only the curated standard genetic-code table. That table states which codon is assigned to which amino-acid or stop label. It does not observe ribosomal translation, folded structure, energetic admissibility, biological function, or evolutionary mechanism. Consequently the readback supported by this chapter is a codon-label readback and a local label-split check. The words ``amino acid'' and ``stop'' are used as labels supplied by the curated table, not as a license to infer a protein-level event.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
