For a readout $r$ such as organism-scoped tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, the intended controlled statistic compares a baseline model $J_0$ against a model $J_Q$ that adds one residual coordinate $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$. \paragraph{Current status of the translation claim.}
The present finite rows do not contain an organism-matched table joining codon usage, tRNA copy-number spectra, and tAI or stAI weights into a controlled estimate of $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$. \paragraph{Permitted contacts.}

Second, tRNA gene-copy annotations can supply anticodon-family spectra, including leucine, arginine, isoleucine, serine, and threonine families relevant to the named residuals. Each contact is noisy in a different way: codon-usage tables are sequence summaries, tRNA copy numbers are annotation summaries, and tAI or stAI weights are modelled translation-efficiency annotations. None of them alone observes a ribosome event or a protein product.

\paragraph{Local limit of the claim.}
This chapter does not infer translation behavior, ribosome mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, protein-output causality, or a universal biological law from nonzero residual variance. \subsection{Readback protocol for the residual basis}
\label{sec:residual-basis-q6-readback-protocol}

\paragraph{Per-coordinate reporting.}
For each organism and for each $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$, the report should state the sign and magnitude of $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$, the readout $r$, the conditioning set $Z$, and the baseline $\Delta_M$. The minimal acceptable table has one row per organism, residual coordinate, and readout. A row is interpretable only if amino-acid composition is controlled; otherwise the statistic confounds synonymous residual structure with the encoded protein sequence.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The conjecture is supported in the bounded sense if at least one controlled row has positive survival score, with the model comparison carried out against the $M$-density baseline rather than against an unconditioned codon-frequency model. It is broken for the chosen dataset and readout if all nine residual coordinates satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)\leq 0
\end{aligned}
$$
for every organism and permitted readout in the matched table. Even in that broken case, the result would be a statement about the tested readout and sampled organisms, not a universal theorem about all translation systems.

\paragraph{Why the $M$-only baseline is insufficient as the final coordinate.}
The finite quotient geometry reports $\pi_M$ with $10$ vertices and $f(\pi_M)=(10,14,6,1)$, while the residual basis explicitly contains contrasts that are invisible to an $M$-density-only readback. Thus the correct experiment is not whether $M$ is locally useful, but whether adding a named residual coordinate to an $M$-controlled baseline changes a translation-layer readout. The residual coordinates are therefore treated as testable synonymous contrasts rather than as replacements for amino-acid sequence, codon context, or external readout data.
