\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN block and the UUR block as an organism-level statistical contact, not as a molecular-mechanism claim. The admissible datum is the signed residual comparison of CUN usage against UUR usage, paired with organism labels and with a tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio. The intended readout is whether the sign and size of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ change across organisms in a way that tracks the available tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after amino-acid composition has been controlled. The biological object is therefore a finite cross-organism table of codon usage and tRNA-Leu annotation summaries for human, yeast, and an additional model organism, with the composition control preventing the raw leucine imbalance from being promoted into a broader biological assertion.

\paragraph{Finite row content.}

$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\

$$
The cross-organism validation row loaded three organisms: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$. For these three rows, the recorded CUN/UUR logarithmic residuals are

with corresponding signs $(1,-1,1)$. Thus the sign is explicitly non-uniform across the measured organisms. \paragraph{Empirical contacts.}
Two contacts support the admissible interpretation. The first contact is the per-organism codon-usage summary: external codon-usage resources report codon-count or frequency summaries over coding sequences, and those summaries provide the organism-level CUN and UUR readback used in the logarithmic residual. Within the finite table supplied here, the probe condition is met in its narrow statistical form. The paired controlled statistic is also positive in the stored row, with partial Pearson $1.0$ for $n=3$. The packet is therefore admissible as a translation-layer usage-and-copy-number association, where ``translation-layer'' means only that codon usage and tRNA-Leu copy spectra are being compared at organism resolution. The result should be read as a finite controlled association across the three named organisms, not as a universal law and not as a mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation event, ribosome-mediated process, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanistic explanation, or universal cross-organism rule follows from this table by itself. A stronger statement would require a separate contact that directly measures the relevant layer: for example, ribosome profiling or charged-tRNA availability for a process claim, protein structural evidence for folding, biochemical or phenotypic evidence for function, and a larger independently curated organism panel before any general law is considered. The mixed sign is therefore reported only as context-dependent usage variation paired with tRNA-Leu copy-number annotation under the stated amino-acid composition control.
