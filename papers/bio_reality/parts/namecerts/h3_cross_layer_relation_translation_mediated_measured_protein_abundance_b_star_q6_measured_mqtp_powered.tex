\subsection{Measured translation conditioning for the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-translation-conditioned-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer matrix whose rows are coding sequences joined across codon residual coordinates, measured translation efficiency, matched protein abundance, and transcript controls.  The coordinate block has nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual columns: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ computed as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance in the local ribosome-TE files.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from the local organism-matched proteomics abundance files, joined by protein identifier.  All reported coordinates, translation readouts, and protein-abundance readouts are residualized against the same control family: intercept, coding-sequence length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Rank and join audit.}
The measured TE--abundance--CDS join is rank-powered in all four organisms.  The three-way joined row counts are $13{,}140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3{,}415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $11{,}554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5{,}128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The reported CDS abundance joins are $14{,}410$, $3{,}739$, $19{,}053$, and $5{,}815$, respectively, while the valid measured-TE rows used in the same order are $13{,}140$, $3{,}415$, $11{,}554$, and $5{,}128$.  After residualizing by the control matrix, every organism has $rank(Z)=23$, $24$ control columns, $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, $rank(\widetilde T)=1$, and $rank(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)=10$, so the surface is not the all-saturated small-rank artifact.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $13{,}117$, $3{,}392$, $11{,}531$, and $5{,}105$ in the same organism order.

\paragraph{Measured mediation surface.}
Let $X_Q$ denote the joint nine-column $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block, let $T$ denote measured $\log_{10}(TE)$, and let $Y$ denote measured $\log_{10}$ protein abundance.  The experiment reports a joint statistical decomposition rather than a per-coordinate causal effect.  The finite scalar readback is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{meas} &= R^2(T \mid Z, X_Q)-R^2(T \mid Z),\\
S^{QP} &= R^2(Y \mid Z, X_Q)-R^2(Y \mid Z),\\
M^{QTP}_{meas} &= \hbox{the fraction of the direct } X_Q \to Y \hbox{ signal absorbed after conditioning on } T.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \emph{Danio rerio}, $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.04034217614654355$ and $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$, but the raw mediated fraction is $-0.04801486048721136$ and is clipped to $0.0$ for the unit-interval readback.  This is a translation-surviving and protein-omics-surviving surface without positive measured mediation.  For \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.009394266754822262$, $S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074$, and $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.04091158340950673$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.018837911344127926$, $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, and $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0027966485036786744$.  For \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436$, $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, and $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793069656345167$.

\paragraph{Energy decomposition.}
The raw energies clarify why the four organisms receive different readbacks.  In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, the measured translation readout explains substantial protein-abundance energy: $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$ units of $Y$ energy, $X_Q$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units of $Y$ energy, and the incremental $T$ improvement given $X_Q$ is $269.89558844115504$, while the direct $X_Q$ improvement given $T$ remains $351.63835021403213$.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, by contrast, $T$ explains only $16.418999670692678$ units of $Y$ energy while $X_Q$ explains $1092.0114819316218$ units, and the direct $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$; the measured translation readout therefore does not absorb the direct protein-abundance signal.  The bacterial and human panels are intermediate but small on measured mediation: \emph{E. coli} has $T$-on-$Y$ energy $35.56347808682994$, $X_Q$-on-$Y$ energy $393.78692134854015$, and direct $X_Q$-given-$T$ energy $377.67647487021645$, while human has $T$-on-$Y$ energy $2.0678517436514716$, $X_Q$-on-$Y$ energy $565.3850203147235$, and direct $X_Q$-given-$T$ energy $563.803837143658$.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite row surface separates four local cases.  A row is translation-null when $S^{QT}_{meas}\leq 0$; it is protein-omics-null when $S^{QP}\leq 0$; it is direct-prediction-only when $S^{QT}_{meas}>0$ and $S^{QP}>0$ but the measured mediated fraction is zero or negligible; and it is measured-translation-conditioned when all three quantities are positive with nontrivial absorption of the direct protein-abundance signal.  Under this rule, all four organisms are powered and positive on both $S^{QT}_{meas}$ and $S^{QP}$.  \emph{Danio rerio} is direct-prediction-only for this measured TE contact because the raw mediation is suppressive and the reported mediated fraction is $0.0$.  \emph{E. coli} and human have positive but small measured mediation.  \emph{S. cerevisiae} is the strongest measured-translation-conditioned case, with nearly half of the direct joint $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal assigned to the measured TE conditioning readback.

\paragraph{Adjacent residual checks.}
Two neighboring finite-row audits show why this chapter should not be promoted beyond measured translation conditioning.  A UniProtKB reviewed localization audit projects the modeled-translation complement abundance residual onto twelve residualized localization columns.  Its joined localization rows are $1{,}984$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $16{,}008$ for human, and $4{,}656$ for yeast, with localization absorption values $0.009508864544725801$, $0.009254913329269913$, and $0.011225105608516642$.  The corresponding degree-of-freedom-matched null $95$th percentiles are $0.011321328712802819$, $0.0013758521256775384$, and $0.004958116973149795$, so the localization contact is a descriptive downstream audit and not a translation mechanism.  A separate human protein-turnover audit joins $8{,}057$ abundance--turnover--CDS rows from a reported $19{,}053$ human abundance--CDS join, with a CDS hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$.  Its turnover absorption is only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ against a null mean of $0.0001262572958089998$ and a null $95$th percentile of $0.0004342285066808464$, so turnover does not explain the large human modeled-translation complement residual in this row set.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
This chapter supports a finite, organism-matched, cross-sectional statistical decomposition of the joint $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block through measured ribosome-TE and matched protein abundance.  It does not establish causal mediation, temporal order, synonymous-edit response, perturbation rescue, elongation or initiation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  The measured TE contact is condition-specific and comes from local ribosome-profiling studies; the protein abundance contact is steady-state proteomics; and the mRNA contact is a control used to form and separate TE rather than a translation-output claim by itself.  Any claim above the measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance layer requires its own layer-matched contact and a new controlled finite-row statistic.
