\subsection{Measured translation conditioning of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record uses a finite matched matrix whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns contain the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, a measured translation-efficiency readout $T$, measured protein abundance $P$, and the covariates used to remove ordinary composition effects.  The residual coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The controls are an intercept, $\log$ CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline.  In every measured-mediation organism the residualized design has $9$ $Q$ coordinates, rank $23$ for the control block, rank $9$ for the residualized $Q$ block, rank $1$ for the measured translation readout, and rank $10$ for the joint $Q,T$ block.  The readout $T$ is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ computed from ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling files; the readout $P$ is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance in ppm joined by protein identifier.

\paragraph{Matched rows and rank.}
The measured three-way joins are large enough to avoid the rank-saturation failure mode.  Danio rerio has $14410$ CDS rows reported and $13140$ rows joined to both measured TE and abundance.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3739$ reported CDS rows and $3415$ joined rows.  Homo sapiens has $19053$ reported CDS rows and $11554$ joined rows.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5815$ reported CDS rows and $5128$ joined rows.  The measured-TE contact itself has ribosome-TE join hit rates $0.9118667591950035$ for Danio rerio, $0.9133458143888741$ for Escherichia coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.8818572656921754$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The direct protein-abundance contact is also well joined in the broader PAXdb panel, for example $0.9978649586335735$ in Escherichia coli, $0.9779294769799313$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.9156038419146592$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

\paragraph{Measured survival before conditioning.}
Before the mediation step, the measured translation readout gives positive $Q\to T$ survival in all four measured-TE organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{meas}(Danio) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
S^{QT}_{meas}(E.\ coli) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
S^{QT}_{meas}(Human) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
S^{QT}_{meas}(Yeast) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding direct $Q\to P$ survival values in the measured mediation run are
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QP}(Danio) &= 0.04926578454614652,\\
S^{QP}(E.\ coli) &= 0.10941535951828074,\\
S^{QP}(Human) &= 0.04288464996165745,\\
S^{QP}(Yeast) &= 0.3146818503437042.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite rows do not merely record a protein-omics association, and they do not merely record a translation association.  They put the same residual coordinate system against both measured TE and measured abundance on the same joined CDS units.

\paragraph{Measured mediation scalar.}
The registered statistic is a joint nine-dimensional $Q$ mediation scalar with a per-coordinate audit.  It asks how much of the direct $Q\to P$ signal is removed when the measured translation readout is included.  The observed measured fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{meas}(Danio) &= 0,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(E.\ coli) &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(Human) &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(Yeast) &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw Danio value is suppressive, $-0.04801486048721136$, and is therefore clipped to the admissible unit interval at $0$.  The other three organisms show positive but very different measured attenuation.  Yeast is the strongest finite-row case: the $Q\to P$ signal is large, the measured $Q\to T$ signal is also large, and conditioning on measured TE absorbs about $47.93\%$ of the direct $Q\to P$ association.  Escherichia coli and human remain positive but weak on this specific measured-TE mediation scale.

\paragraph{Energy readback.}
The residual energy diagnostics show why the result should be read as a finite decomposition, not as a verbal mechanism.  In yeast, $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$ units of residual protein-abundance energy, while $Q$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units on $P$ and $103.10404057290246$ units on $T$; after $T$ is present, the remaining direct $Q$ improvement on $P$ is $351.63835021403213$.  In Escherichia coli, the analogous energies are $35.56347808682994$ for $T$ on $P$, $393.78692134854015$ for $Q$ on $P$, $8.637891149939946$ for $Q$ on $T$, and $377.67647487021645$ for the residual direct $Q$ improvement after $T$.  Human has $2.0678517436514716$ for $T$ on $P$ and $563.803837143658$ for the direct $Q$ improvement after $T$, so its measured-TE mediation fraction is correspondingly tiny.  Danio has a positive $Q\to T$ and $Q\to P$ surface but a suppressive raw mediation value, so it is reported as a non-mediated measured-readout outcome rather than hidden inside the positive cases.

\paragraph{Four-case relation.}
The same boundary is visible in the four-case discriminant computed with modeled tAI rather than measured ribosome TE.  In that separate classification run, Escherichia coli has seven case-D coordinates and two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop, while yeast has nine case-D coordinates and no positive-positive coordinates without mediation drop.  Those labels are useful as a finite diagnostic of whether $Q\to T$ and $Q\to P$ signs coexist and whether conditioning reduces the protein-abundance signal, but they are not a substitute for measured ribosome TE.  The present record therefore gives priority to the measured $TE=RPF/mRNA$ statistic above, while keeping the modeled-tAI classification as a comparison surface.

\paragraph{Relation to broader readout dictionary.}
The result is also consistent with the larger yeast reduction family.  In the joint measured-TE plus mRNA-stability decomposition, yeast has $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, giving a joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$ with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The single-mediator fractions are $0.4697188319693239$ for measured TE and $0.0626618595344251$ for mRNA stability, with the joint value close to their sum under the fixed tolerance.  A separate yeast readout-dictionary certificate with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $n=1800$, $5$ folds, and $120$ permutations gives coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  Thus the measured translation readout is a real reduction axis, but it does not exhaust the protein-abundance residual.

\paragraph{Localized supporting readouts.}
The $f3\_stress$ component has additional yeast evidence on ribosome dwell.  On $1257$ joined rows, the dwell-mediated abundance component has base held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full held-out $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, and held-out increment $0.01856473944057712$, above a deterministic permutation null $95\%$ value of $0.0013115362189692937$ with $120$ permutations.  The positional version identifies the $5'$ segment as dominant: the $5'$ window passes, the $3'$ window does not, and the leading segment by held-out increment is $5prime$.  In the structural-order positional run, the conclusion is $ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized$: the primary $50$-codon ramp signal is positive, with weighted ramp increment $0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, ramp sign-flip $p=0.0478515625$, and paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip $p=0.05859375$.  These numbers support a bounded translation-adjacent readout for the $f3\_stress$ coordinate, while still stopping short of a structural or functional claim.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row claim is ready as a measured statistical decomposition: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured ribosome TE and measured protein abundance on joined CDS rows, and in yeast a large fraction of the direct abundance association is absorbed by the measured translation readout.  Escherichia coli and human provide positive but small measured mediation fractions, while Danio is a suppressive/null mediation case despite positive marginal $Q\to T$ and $Q\to P$ surfaces.  Therefore the admissible statement is not that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ causes protein abundance, nor that it realizes a universal translation law, but that the finite matched matrix contains a non-vacuous measured $Q\to T\to P$ attenuation surface with organism-specific strength.

\paragraph{Local scope boundary.}
No stronger biological conclusion follows from these rows alone.  The computation is cross-sectional and condition-bound; it does not establish temporal ordering, synonymous-edit rescue, perturbation response, molecular causation, elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, protein turnover mechanism, localization, post-translational modification, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  A direct $Q\to P$ association remains only a protein-omics prediction unless the same finite row system also shows a surviving measured $Q\to T$ step and a reduction of the $Q\to P$ signal after conditioning on $T$.  Claims about structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, or a general biological rule require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.
