\subsection{Measured translation conditioning for the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance surface}
\label{sec:bq6-measured-translation-conditioned-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record studied here is a finite cross-layer matrix whose rows are coding sequences with matched codon counts, measured translation-efficiency readouts, matched mRNA controls, and matched protein-abundance measurements.  The coordinate side is the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  These nine coordinates are tested jointly as $X_Q$, after residualizing against a control block $Z$ consisting of an intercept, $\log$ CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The translation readout is $T=\log_{10}(TE)$, where $TE$ is measured ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA, and the protein readout is $Y=\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from the organism-matched protein-abundance table.  The finite joins are nondegenerate: $3415$ three-way rows for $E.\ coli$ K12 MG1655, $5128$ for $S.\ cerevisiae$, $11554$ for human, and $13140$ for zebrafish.  The corresponding reported CDS-abundance joins are $3739$, $5815$, $19053$, and $14410$ rows, respectively.

\paragraph{Rank and residualization check.}
Each organism has $24$ control columns and $rank(Z)=23$.  After projecting out $Z$, the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates have $rank(X_Q)=9$, the measured translation readout has $rank(T)=1$, and the combined block has $rank(X_Q,T)=10$ in every organism.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $3392$ for $E.\ coli$, $5105$ for yeast, $11531$ for human, and $13117$ for zebrafish.  Thus the measured-mediation surface is not an all-fit or rank-saturated three-row artifact.  The residual energies for $T$ and $Y$ are, respectively, $919.4854$ and $3599.0095$ in $E.\ coli$, $753.5781$ and $2146.0645$ in yeast, $2052.5947$ and $13183.8553$ in human, and $3588.3789$ and $22165.7179$ in zebrafish.  The rank tolerance is $10^{-10}$ and the degeneracy cutoff is $0.999999$.

\paragraph{Measured survival and conditioning.}
The measured translation-survival values $S^{QT}_{meas}$ are positive in all four organisms: $0.0093943$ in $E.\ coli$, $0.1368193$ in yeast, $0.0188379$ in human, and $0.0403422$ in zebrafish.  The direct protein-abundance survival values $S^{QP}$ are also positive: $0.1094154$, $0.3146819$, $0.0428846$, and $0.0492658$ in the same order.  The measured conditioning scalar is small in $E.\ coli$ and human, large in yeast, and suppressive in zebrafish.  Its raw values are $0.0409116$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.4793070$ for yeast, $0.00279665$ for human, and $-0.0480149$ for zebrafish; after truncating to the reported unit interval, the mediated fractions are $0.0409116$, $0.4793070$, $0.00279665$, and $0.0$.  In matrix language, the relevant comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{meas} &> 0,\\
S^{QP} &> 0,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas} &= \frac{\text{protein-abundance signal removed by conditioning on measured }T}{\text{direct }Q\to P\text{ signal before measured }T\text{ conditioning}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest organism-level case is yeast: $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.1368193$, $S^{QP}=0.3146819$, and $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793070$.  The weakest positive measured conditioning case is human: $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.0188379$, $S^{QP}=0.0428846$, and $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.00279665$.  Zebrafish remains translation-positive and protein-positive but not positively mediated by measured $TE$ under this scalar, because the raw conditioning fraction is negative and is reported as $0.0$ rather than promoted.

\paragraph{Direct and conditioned energy accounting.}
The finite rows also record the energy decomposition used to separate direct $Q\to P$ association from translation-conditioned association.  For yeast, $X_Q$ explains $675.3275$ units of protein-abundance residual energy before $T$ conditioning, while the remaining direct improvement given measured $T$ is $351.6384$ and the incremental improvement of $T$ given $X_Q$ is $269.8956$.  In $E.\ coli$, the corresponding values are $393.7869$, $377.6765$, and $19.4530$.  In human they are $565.3850$, $563.8038$, and $0.4867$.  In zebrafish they are $1092.0115$, $1144.4443$, and $68.8518$, a suppressive configuration rather than a mediation-positive one.  The $Q\to T$ energies are $8.6379$ in $E.\ coli$, $103.1040$ in yeast, $38.6666$ in human, and $144.7630$ in zebrafish; the direct $T\to P$ energies before adding $X_Q$ are $35.5635$, $593.5848$, $2.0679$, and $16.4190$.  These numbers make the four-organism panel a measured cross-sectional decomposition, not a proof of temporal ordering.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The per-organism readback separates four finite cases.  Case A is a translation-surviving but protein-null coordinate block; it is not the observed joint outcome here because $S^{QP}$ is positive in all four organisms.  Case B is protein-surviving but translation-null; it is also not the observed joint outcome because $S^{QT}_{meas}$ is positive in all four organisms.  Case C is direct protein prediction without meaningful measured-translation absorption; human is close to this boundary because the measured mediated fraction is only $0.00279665$, and zebrafish is suppressive because the raw fraction is negative.  Case D is measured translation-conditioned protein propagation; yeast is the clearest finite-row instance, with nearly half of the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal absorbed by measured $TE$ conditioning.  $E.\ coli$ supplies a weaker positive instance.  Therefore the present record supports a bounded measured-readout conclusion: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive both measured translation and protein-abundance readouts in a rank-powered panel, but the strength of measured translation conditioning is organism-specific.

\paragraph{Relation to residual protein-state searches.}
Two additional finite records delimit what remains after measured translation conditioning.  The localization projection record tests the modeled translation-complement abundance residual against twelve residualized localization columns.  It reports localization absorptions of $0.0095089$ in zebrafish, $0.0092549$ in human, and $0.0112251$ in yeast, with dof-matched null $95\%$ levels of $0.0113213$, $0.00137585$, and $0.00495812$, respectively.  Thus human and yeast exceed their reported null levels, while zebrafish does not.  The human turnover record tests the large human modeled-complement residual against log half-life on $8057$ joined rows; it reports $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490$, $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ norm squared $1262.2238$, $R^2_{Q,Tturn}=0.0046154$, and only $0.000003574$ absorption by turnover, below a dof-matched null $95\%$ level of $0.0004342$.  These two records do not alter the measured $TE$ mediation result; they only show that residual protein-state explanations split by layer and must be tested with their own finite rows.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is statistical and finite-row only.  Measured ribosome-seq $TE$, protein abundance, and CDS rows are cross-sectional and condition-specific; they do not establish causality, perturbation response, temporal ordering, synonymous-edit rescue, elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, turnover mechanism, localization mechanism, post-translational modification, folding, molecular function, physical admissibility, phenotype, or universality.  A positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ value says that the residual coordinate block carries measured translation-readout signal after the declared controls.  A positive $S^{QP}$ value says that the same block carries protein-abundance signal after the declared controls.  A positive $M^{QTP}_{meas}$ value says only that conditioning on measured $TE$ removes part of the direct protein-abundance association in the fitted cross-sectional decomposition.  Any claim beyond this measured translation-conditioned abundance surface requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
