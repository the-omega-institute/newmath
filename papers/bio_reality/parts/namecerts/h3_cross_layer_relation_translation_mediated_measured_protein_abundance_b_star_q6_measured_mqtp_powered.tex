\subsection{Measured translation conditioning of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests a nine-coordinate synonymous residual surface against a measured translation readout and a measured protein-abundance readout on the same coding-sequence rows.  The coordinate list is $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each organism, the design residualizes the codon-coordinate block $X_Q$, the measured translation variable $T$, and protein abundance $Y$ against a common control matrix $Z$ containing length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  The measured translation variable is $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$ from organism-local ribosome-profiling records, while the protein variable is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from matched proteomics abundance joined by protein identifier.

\paragraph{Row joins and rank.}
The measured three-way joins are large enough to avoid the rank-saturated all-ones boundary: Danio rerio has $13140$ joined CDS rows from $14410$ codon-abundance rows, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3415$ joined rows from $3739$ codon rows, Homo sapiens has $11554$ joined rows from $19053$ codon rows, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5128$ joined rows from $5815$ codon rows.  In all four organisms the residualized coordinate block has rank $9$, the translation readout has rank $1$, the joint $X_Q,T$ block has rank $10$, and the control matrix has rank $23$ with $24$ control columns.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$, respectively.  Thus the finite rows define a genuinely powered measured-readout matrix rather than a degenerate three-organism fit.

\paragraph{Measured survival and mediation scalars.}
The measured translation-survival and protein-abundance-survival entries are both positive in every organism, but the translation-conditioned fraction differs sharply by organism:
$$
\begin{aligned}
D.rerio &: S^{QT}_{meas}=0.04034217614654355,\quad S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0,\\
E.coli &: S^{QT}_{meas}=0.009394266754822262,\quad S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.04091158340950673,\\
H.sapiens &: S^{QT}_{meas}=0.018837911344127926,\quad S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0027966485036786744,\\
S.cerevisiae &: S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436,\quad S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw mediated fraction for Danio rerio is negative, $-0.04801486048721136$, and is therefore clipped to $0$ in the bounded readback.  The yeast row is the strongest finite instance: measured translation conditioning accounts for about $47.93\%$ of the direct codon-residual to protein-abundance association.  Escherichia coli shows a smaller but positive measured fraction, about $4.09\%$, and human shows a nearly null measured fraction, about $0.28\%$.

\paragraph{Energy decomposition.}
The same conclusion is visible in the residual-energy audit.  In yeast, $X_Q$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units of residual protein-abundance energy, while measured $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$ units on $Y$ and leaves an $X_Q$ improvement of $351.63835021403213$ after conditioning.  In human, by contrast, measured $T$ explains only $2.0678517436514716$ units on $Y$ and the $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ remains $563.803837143658$, so the measured translation readout barely absorbs the direct association.  Escherichia coli is intermediate, with $T$ explaining $35.56347808682994$ units on $Y$ and the $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ equal to $377.67647487021645$.  Danio has positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ but a suppressive mediation scalar: $T$ explains $16.418999670692678$ units on $Y$, while the $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$, larger than the direct $X_Q$ energy on $Y$ of $1092.0114819316218$.

\paragraph{Relation to direct protein-omics survival.}
A broader direct protein-abundance screen reports positive $S^{QP}$ in fifteen organisms, with all nine coordinates surviving at the stated positive threshold.  Representative direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in Escherichia coli, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.04613529807085213$ in Danio, and $0.06397514251295629$ in Sulfolobus solfataricus.  These entries justify treating the protein-abundance association as a real finite-row signal, but they do not by themselves identify translation as the absorbing route.  The present measured-readout test separates the direct $Q\to P$ association from the measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The modeled-tAI four-case audit gives a useful contrast but is not substituted for measured ribosome profiling.  On the modeled-tAI surface, Escherichia coli has seven case-$D$ coordinates and two positive-positive entries without a mediation drop; yeast has nine case-$D$ coordinates and no positive-positive entry lacking the mediation drop.  The measured-TE decomposition is stricter: yeast remains the clear translation-conditioned protein-abundance case, Escherichia coli remains weakly positive, human is almost entirely direct or non-translation residual, and Danio is suppressive under measured $TE$.  Thus the measured table supports a heterogeneous readback rather than a universal codon-to-protein law.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
The joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability partition also reports organism-specific fractions: yeast has joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$ with $TE$-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$ and stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$; Escherichia coli has joint fraction $0.2404669586626661$ with $TE$-only $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only $0.23052056324898387$; human has joint fraction $0.16890712099923358$ with $TE$-only $0.00549490864190343$ and stability-only $0.15314679870183337$.  Each reported joint partition has right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  A yeast $f3\_stress$ dwell audit gives $n=1257$, five folds, base $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, and an approximate $f3$--dwell $p$-value $7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  A separate yeast readout-dictionary compression check uses $n=1800$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, and reports coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ with unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  These neighboring rows are consistent with partial reducibility, not with complete translation explanation.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite matrix supports a measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance record with the following precise scope.  $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured $TE$ and measured protein abundance on powered rows, and yeast gives a strong positive measured mediation scalar.  Escherichia coli gives a weak positive measured scalar, human gives an almost null scalar, and Danio gives a suppressive raw scalar clipped to zero.  Therefore the valid conclusion is a bounded cross-layer statistical decomposition: measured translation readouts sometimes absorb part of the direct codon-residual to protein-abundance signal, but the effect is organism- and readout-dependent.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
No claim of translation mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causality, perturbation rescue, or universal biological law follows from this table.  The ribosome-profiling $TE$ rows are condition-specific and cross-sectional; PAXdb abundance is steady-state protein abundance; the decomposition is a variance-partition statistic rather than a temporal or causal mediation experiment.  Any stronger biological interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic for that layer.
