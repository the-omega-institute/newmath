\subsection{Measured translation conditioning for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein abundance}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-translation-conditioned-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record is a finite matched-row test of whether the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates retain a protein-abundance association after conditioning on a measured translation-efficiency readout.  The coordinate block is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The response $Y$ is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb protein abundance joined by protein identifier, and the measured translation readout $T$ is $\log_{10}(TE)$, where $TE$ is ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance in the local ribosome-profiling tables.  The calculation uses residualization against $24$ control columns: an intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The post-control design has $rank(Z)=23$, $rank(X_Q)=9$, $rank(T)=1$, and $rank(X_Q,T)=10$ in each organism-specific fit, so the reported surface is not the rank-degenerate $n=3$ boundary.

\paragraph{Matched rows.}
Four organism panels have simultaneous codon, measured-TE, and protein-abundance rows.  The three-way joins are $13140$ rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3415$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5128$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  These rows sit inside larger CDS-to-abundance contacts: direct PAXdb joins report hit rates $1.000000$ for danio, $0.9978649586$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.9779294770$ for human, and $0.9156038419$ for yeast.  The measured-TE contacts report ribosome-TE join rates $0.9118667592$ for danio, $0.9133458144$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.6064136881$ for human, and $0.8818572657$ for yeast.  Thus the mediation surface is tied to explicit finite row sets rather than to a named contact class alone.

\paragraph{Residual energy and rank checks.}
After the controls are removed, the residual degrees of freedom are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$ for danio, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast respectively.  The corresponding translation residual energies are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$, while the abundance residual energies are $22165.717891059892$, $3599.0095273849333$, $13183.855314669145$, and $2146.0645009193045$.  The $Q$ block explains translation energy $144.76301201119642$, $8.637891149939946$, $38.66659710161269$, and $103.10404057290246$ in the same organism order.  On protein abundance before conditioning on $T$, the $Q$ block explains $1092.0114819316218$, $393.78692134854015$, $565.3850203147235$, and $675.3275481062249$ units of residual energy.

\paragraph{Measured survival and mediation fractions.}
The measured translation-survival values are positive in all four panels:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.04034217614654355, & S^{QP}_{\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.04926578454614652,\\
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.009394266754822262, & S^{QP}_{\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.10941535951828074,\\
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.018837911344127926, & S^{QP}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.04288464996165745,\\
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.13681931343730436, & S^{QP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.3146818503437042.
\end{aligned}
$$
Conditioning on measured $T$ gives raw measured mediation fractions $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$ for danio, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast.  The reported clipped fractions are therefore $0.0$, $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$.  Yeast is the strong positive measured-TE case: nearly half of the joint $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association is removed when measured translation efficiency is included.  \emph{E. coli} and human show small positive measured fractions.  Danio is suppressive in the raw decomposition and is consequently read as zero measured mediation rather than as hidden support.

\paragraph{Direct signal versus measured translation absorption.}
The direct protein-omics surface is positive in a larger abundance-only panel: all nine coordinates survive against PAXdb abundance in each of fifteen organisms, with direct $R^2_{QP}$ values including $0.04613529807085213$ for danio, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast.  The measured-TE decomposition narrows that broad protein-omics fact.  A positive $Q \to P$ association alone is not counted as a translation-mediated result; the finite test requires both a surviving measured $Q \to T$ step and attenuation of the $Q \to P$ association after $T$ is present.  This is why yeast receives the clearest measured mediation reading, while danio is kept at a direct-or-confounded protein-abundance readback despite positive $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$.

\paragraph{Comparison with modeled translation and four-case labels.}
The modeled-tAI four-case discriminant remains useful only as a bounded contrast.  In the modeled-tAI table, \emph{E. coli} has seven case-D coordinates and two positive-positive entries without a mediation drop, whereas yeast has nine case-D coordinates.  Those labels do not substitute for measured ribosome-profiling TE.  In the measured-TE table, the decisive numbers are the measured $S^{QT}$ values and the measured mediation fractions above.  The measured result agrees with the qualitative yeast strength but sharply separates weak or suppressive readouts from a full mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Relation to mRNA stability.}
A related finite decomposition that conditions jointly on measured TE and mRNA stability gives joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The single-mediator fractions in that table separate TE-only from stability-only contributions: $0.01975521340963268$ and $0.23052056324898387$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.00549490864190343$ and $0.15314679870183337$ in human, and $0.4697188319693239$ and $0.0626618595344251$ in yeast.  This supports the local reading of the present chapter: measured TE is a major yeast mediator, while human and \emph{E. coli} protein-abundance residuals are more strongly controlled by other measured or unmeasured downstream variables.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is positive but bounded.  The rank-powered measured panel establishes a real $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ association with measured translation efficiency and measured protein abundance in the joined organisms, and it shows measured translation-conditioned absorption most strongly in yeast.  The admissible claim is a cross-sectional statistical decomposition of $Q \to P$ through measured $T$, not a causal pathway.  No translation mechanism, folding conclusion, physical admissibility statement, molecular-function claim, perturbation result, synonymous-edit rescue, or universal biological law follows from this surface unless an additional layer-specific measurement supplies that claim and is analyzed on matched rows under its own controls.
