\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite measured-readout test for the claim that a synonymous residual coordinate can pass from codon geometry to measured translation efficiency and then to measured protein abundance on the same coding-sequence units.  The tested carrier is a four-way finite table whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns contain the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, a measured translation-efficiency readout $T$, a measured protein-abundance readout $Y$, and the covariates used to remove composition and size effects.  The nine residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ computed as ribosome footprint divided by mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling files.  The abundance readout is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from the local proteomics abundance tables joined by protein identifier.  All reported fits residualize against the same control family: intercept, coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.

\paragraph{Rank and row support.}
The measured mediation run used four rank-powered organism joins.  Danio rerio joined $13140$ coding sequences out of $14410$ reported CDS rows with $13140$ valid measured-TE proteins and $14410$ valid abundance proteins.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 joined $3415$ rows out of $3739$ reported CDS rows, with $3415$ valid measured-TE proteins and $3747$ valid abundance proteins.  Homo sapiens joined $11554$ rows out of $19053$ reported CDS rows, with $11554$ valid measured-TE proteins and $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae joined $5128$ rows out of $5815$ reported CDS rows, with $5128$ valid measured-TE proteins and $6351$ valid abundance proteins.  In every organism the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ design has rank $9$, the control design has rank $23$, the translation column has residual rank $1$, and the combined $Q,T$ design has rank $10$.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$ for Danio, E. coli, human, and yeast respectively, so the measured mediation statistic is not an all-fit rank artifact.

\paragraph{Measured translation survival.}
The same finite rows also support a direct measured-TE survival readback.  The measured $Q \to T$ partial coefficients are positive in all four organisms: Danio has $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.04034217614654355$, E. coli has $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.009394266754822262$, human has $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.018837911344127926$, and yeast has $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436$.  The corresponding measured-TE join hit rates are $0.9118667591950035$ for Danio, $0.9133458143888741$ for E. coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for human, and $0.8818572656921754$ for yeast.  These numbers certify a bounded translation-readout association after the declared controls; they do not by themselves certify elongation kinetics, initiation control, perturbational rescue, or a causal path.

\paragraph{Direct abundance survival.}
The same residual basis survives directly against measured protein abundance.  In the four mediation organisms the controlled $Q \to Y$ entries are $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ for Danio, $0.10941535951828074$ for E. coli, $0.04288464996165745$ for human, and $0.3146818503437042$ for yeast.  A broader direct protein-omics run over fifteen organisms likewise found all nine coordinates surviving in each organism, with $R^2_{QP}$ values including $0.10767526677985896$ for E. coli, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.04613529807085213$ for Danio, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.02763998603083845$ for rat, and $0.06397514251295629$ for Sulfolobus.  This direct abundance surface is a prediction surface, not a mechanism surface: a positive $Q \to Y$ entry is kept separate from any claim that measured translation explains the entry.

\paragraph{Mediation scalar.}
The measured mediation statistic asks whether conditioning on measured $T$ removes or reduces the direct $Q \to Y$ signal.  The observed mediated fractions are heterogeneous.  Danio has raw $M^{QTP}_{meas}=-0.04801486048721136$ and clipped measured fraction $0.0$, so its positive $Q \to T$ and $Q \to Y$ entries do not form a positive measured mediation readback.  E. coli has raw and clipped measured fraction $0.04091158340950673$.  Human has raw and clipped measured fraction $0.0027966485036786744$.  Yeast has raw and clipped measured fraction $0.4793069656345167$.  Thus the finite table supports a measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation signal most strongly in yeast, weakly in E. coli and human, and not positively in Danio under the stated scalar rule.

\paragraph{Energy audit.}
The residual-energy decomposition explains why the four organisms should not be collapsed into a single verbal claim.  In Danio, $X$ explains $1092.0114819316218$ units of abundance energy, $T$ explains $16.418999670692678$, and the direct $X$ improvement after conditioning on $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$.  In E. coli the corresponding values are $393.78692134854015$, $35.56347808682994$, and $377.67647487021645$.  In human they are $565.3850203147235$, $2.0678517436514716$, and $563.803837143658$.  In yeast they are $675.3275481062249$, $593.5847863333478$, and $351.63835021403213$.  The yeast row is the one in which measured translation accounts for a large part of the abundance-facing residual signal; the human row is a clear example where measured translation survival exists but explains little of the abundance association.

\paragraph{Auxiliary readout checks.}
Related finite runs refine the interpretation.  A joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition reported joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$ in E. coli, $0.16890712099923358$ in human, and $0.5195182225621592$ in yeast, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In that same run, yeast is dominated by measured TE alone with $TE$-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$, whereas E. coli and human are dominated by stability-only fractions $0.23052056324898387$ and $0.15314679870183337$.  A modeled-tAI four-case discriminant classified yeast as $D=9$ with no positive-positive coordinate lacking a mediation drop, while E. coli had $D=7$ and $2$ positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell run in yeast had $n=1257$, $5$ folds, $120$ deterministic permutations, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, null $95\%$ value $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$ for the $f3$--dwell correlation.  Its positional companion classified the dominant segment as $5prime$, with the five-prime window passing and the three-prime body window not passing.  A compact readout-dictionary run with $n=1800$, $5$ folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $120$ permutations covered $0.24538211439730545$ of the yeast residual signal with measured TE, mRNA stability, and turnover, leaving $0.7546178856026946$ unexplained above a selection-aware null $95\%$ value of $0.029149007242956115$.  The irreducibility audit used the same $n=1800$, $5$-fold, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$ setting and records that the current source signal is real while at least one listed reduction family refutes a full irreducibility reading.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a measured, cross-sectional mediation decomposition for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals against measured protein abundance.  The strongest positive instance is yeast, where $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436$, $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, and $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793069656345167$ on $5128$ joined rows.  E. coli and human are positive but much smaller, while Danio is suppressive under the measured mediation scalar despite positive $Q \to T$ and $Q \to Y$ entries.  The correct conclusion is therefore a finite readout statement: measured translation can absorb part of the codon-residual abundance association in some rank-powered panels, especially yeast, but the residual basis is not exhausted by measured translation.  No translation mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbational mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the same controlled finite-row calculation is performed for that layer.
