\subsection{Measured translation conditioning for B-star-Q6 protein abundance}\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-measured-translation-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a rank-powered cross-layer surface whose rows are coding sequences with matched B-star-Q6 residual coordinates, measured translation-efficiency readouts, protein-abundance readouts, and transcript controls.  The active coordinate block has nine columns: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each organism first residualizes the coordinate matrix $Q$, the measured translation readout $T$, and the abundance readout $P$ against the same covariate family: intercept, coding length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the M-density baseline.  The residualized control rank is $23$ in each measured-mediation organism, with $24$ control columns before rank reduction.  The measured translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, where $TE$ is footprint divided by mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling table; the protein readout is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance in ppm, joined by protein identifier.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank.}
Four measured-TE abundance panels are usable.  Danio has $14410$ reported CDS-abundance joins and $13140$ three-way codon--TE--abundance rows.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3739$ reported CDS joins and $3415$ three-way rows.  Human has $19053$ reported CDS joins and $11554$ three-way rows.  Yeast has $5815$ reported CDS joins and $5128$ three-way rows.  All four panels have residual coordinate rank $9$, measured-TE rank $1$, joint coordinate-plus-TE rank $10$, and pass the declared nondegenerate rank test.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $13117$ for danio, $3392$ for E. coli, $11531$ for human, and $5105$ for yeast.

S^{QT}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.018837911344127926,\\

$$
The corresponding measured-TE join hit rates from the TE survival run are $0.9118667591950035$ for danio, $0.9133458143888741$ for E. coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for human, and $0.8818572656921754$ for yeast.  These numbers justify treating the translation readout as present on the same row surface, but they do not make the readout causal or condition-invariant.

\paragraph{Direct protein-abundance survival.}
The direct coordinate-to-protein-abundance surface is positive across the broader PAXdb panel.  In the four organisms used for measured translation mediation, the direct abundance values are $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ for danio, $0.10941535951828074$ for E. coli, $0.04288464996165745$ for human, and $0.3146818503437042$ for yeast in the measured-mediation run.  The independent direct protein-omics survival audit gives the same qualitative picture on the larger PAXdb surface: for example, yeast has $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, E. coli has $0.10767526677985896$, human has $0.04454565118019448$, and danio has $0.04613529807085213$, with all nine coordinates surviving by the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold.  Those direct associations are downstream prediction scores, not explanations of how protein levels are produced.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned mediation scalar.}
The measured mediation statistic compares the direct $Q \to P$ score with the residual direct score after conditioning on measured $T$.  The finite readback is sharply organism-specific:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}_{\mathrm{danio}} &= 0,\quad M^{raw}_{\mathrm{danio}}=-0.04801486048721136,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{\mathrm{Ecoli}} &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
Danio is therefore a suppressive or null measured-mediation case despite positive $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$.  E. coli and human show small positive measured fractions.  Yeast is the strong measured-TE mediation case: the coordinate block has $S^{QT}=0.13681931343730436$, $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, and nearly half of the direct coordinate-to-abundance association is absorbed by measured TE under this scalar decomposition.  The underlying energy diagnostics agree with this reading.  In yeast, $X$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units of residual abundance energy before conditioning, measured $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$ units of residual abundance energy, and the remaining direct $X$ improvement after $T$ is $351.63835021403213$.  In human, by contrast, $T$ explains only $2.0678517436514716$ units on $P$, while the direct $X$ improvement after $T$ remains $563.803837143658$.

\paragraph{Four-case interpretation.}
The measured-mediation row surface separates four possibilities: no translation and no protein signal, translation-only signal, direct protein-omics signal without a measured translation absorption, and measured translation-conditioned abundance absorption.  On the finite rows here, every reported organism has positive $S^{QT}$ and positive $S^{QP}$, but only yeast gives a large measured absorption fraction.  E. coli and human remain weak measured-mediation positives, and danio remains a positive-survival but null-measured-mediation boundary.  The appropriate statement is therefore not that B-star-Q6 universally realizes a translation mechanism.  The correct finite claim is narrower: on matched measured-TE and abundance rows, the coordinate block has nonzero translation and abundance readbacks in all four panels, and the strongest translation-conditioned abundance absorption occurs in yeast.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
These computations are cross-sectional variance decompositions on joined omics rows.  They do not establish temporal order, synonymous-edit rescue, perturbational causation, ribosome elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, turnover mechanism, localization mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  Protein abundance alone is not a structural or functional assay.  Measured TE is a condition-specific ribosome-footprint over mRNA readout, not a complete description of translation dynamics.  Any stronger biological conclusion requires a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic on the same claimed layer.
