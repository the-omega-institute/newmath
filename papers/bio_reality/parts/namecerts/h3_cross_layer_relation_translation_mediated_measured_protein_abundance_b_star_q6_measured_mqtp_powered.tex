\subsection{Measured translation conditioning of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance surface}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp-powered}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates remain merely direct predictors of measured protein abundance, or whether part of the abundance association is absorbed by an organism-matched measured translation-efficiency readout. The finite surface is built on nine residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. For each joined coding sequence row, the coordinate matrix $X_Q$ is residualized against a fixed control block $Z$ containing an intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline. The translation readout $T$ is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ computed as ribosome footprint divided by mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling files. The protein readout $Y$ is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from the matched local proteomics abundance file. The fitted object is therefore a finite three-readout matrix $X_Q,T,Y$ on the same CDS/protein rows, not a symbolic promotion from codon geometry alone.

\paragraph{Rank and row admissibility.}
The measured mediation run used four organisms with nondegenerate row counts after joining codon, measured TE, and abundance rows: $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The reported CDS joins before imposing all readouts were $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, respectively. In every organism the residualized coordinate design had $rank(X_Q)=9$, the measured translation column had $rank(T)=1$, the combined design had $rank(X_Q,T)=10$, and the control block had $rank(Z)=23$ from $24$ control columns. The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$. Thus the four rows are not the rank-degenerate $n=3$ all-fit boundary; they are powered finite surfaces with a nine-dimensional residual coordinate block and a measured translation column.

Those broader $Q \to P$ associations motivate the measured-conditioning test, but they are not themselves mediation evidence.

\paragraph{Measured mediation decomposition.}
The measured mediation statistic compares the direct $Q \to P$ fit with the fit after conditioning on measured $TE$. The finite-row outcome is organism-specific:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{Danio})_{raw} &= -0.04801486048721136, & M^{QTP,meas}_{clipped} &= 0,\\
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{human}) &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{yeast}) &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest measured translation-conditioned absorption is therefore in yeast, where measured $TE$ accounts for approximately $47.93\%$ of the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association. The \emph{E. coli} and human surfaces are positive but much smaller, at approximately $4.09\%$ and $0.28\%$. The \emph{Danio} surface is suppressive in the raw decomposition and is reported as zero after enforcing the unit-interval mediated fraction. In the underlying energy accounting, yeast has $X_Q$ explaining $675.3275481062249$ units on $Y$, measured $T$ explaining $593.5847863333478$ units on $Y$, and the residual direct $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ equal to $351.63835021403213$. By contrast, human has $X_Q$ explaining $565.3850203147235$ units on $Y$, measured $T$ explaining only $2.0678517436514716$, and a residual direct $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ of $563.803837143658$, which is why its measured mediation fraction is nearly zero despite positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ and $S^{QP}$.

\paragraph{Four-case interpretation.}
This measured run separates three notions that would otherwise be conflated. First, positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ says that the codon residual block predicts the measured translation-efficiency readout after the declared controls. Second, positive $S^{QP}$ says that the same residual block predicts measured protein abundance after the same family of controls. Third, positive $M^{QTP,meas}$ says that conditioning on measured $TE$ reduces the direct $Q \to P$ association. The supporting modeled-tAI four-case discriminant classified yeast as nine case-D coordinates, while the modeled-tAI \emph{E. coli} surface had seven case-D coordinates and two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop. That modeled-tAI result is not substituted for the measured ribosome-profiling result here; it is useful only as a contrast showing why the measured $TE$ conditioning must be read separately from modeled adaptation weights.

\paragraph{Joint-readout context.}
A related three-readout decomposition using measured $TE$ and mRNA stability reports joint mediation fractions of $0.2404669586626661$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ in human, and $0.5195182225621592$ in yeast, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. In that decomposition, yeast is again the high-mediation surface: the $TE$-only fraction is $0.4697188319693239$, the stability-only fraction is $0.0626618595344251$, and the single-mediator sum is within the stated descriptive tolerance of the joint fit. A separate yeast partial-readout dictionary certificate used $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $n=1800$, five folds, and $120$ permutations; it found selection-aware coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ over the readout dictionary $[measured\_te, mrna\_stability, turnover]$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ above a selection-aware null $95\%$ value of $0.029149007242956115$. The yeast $f3\_stress$ dwell test also passed, with held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ above null $95\%=0.0013115362189692937$ and $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. These surrounding finite-row records support the view that the yeast residual abundance surface is partly reducible to measured translation and dwell-related readouts, but still not exhausted by them.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The measured $Q \to T \to P$ surface is ready as a bounded cross-layer relation. It supports a finite statistical statement: in four powered joined panels, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has positive measured translation-readout survival and positive protein-abundance survival, and in yeast the measured translation readout absorbs a large fraction of the direct protein-abundance association. The same table also records weak measured mediation in \emph{E. coli}, very small measured mediation in human, and a suppressive raw decomposition in \emph{Danio}. No causal translation process, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbation mechanism, organismal phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universal law follows from these rows. Such claims would require their own layer-matched measurements and controls; the present chapter only reports the finite measured-TE conditioning of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance association.
