\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein abundance for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This BioReality record tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface reaches measured protein abundance through a measured translation-efficiency readout rather than only through a direct codon-to-protein association.  The finite carrier is a matrix surface over nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each row is joined to coding-sequence codon counts, measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency $T$, measured PAXdb protein abundance $P$, and the same control block $Z$ used throughout this surface: amino-acid composition, CDS length, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  The measured translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$ in the local ribosome-profiling files; the protein readout is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ joined by protein identifier.

\paragraph{Rank and row support.}
The measured three-way joins are rank-powered for all four organisms in the tested panel.  The joined row counts are $13140$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $3415$ for $Escherichia\ coli$ K-12 MG1655, $11554$ for $Homo\ sapiens$, and $5128$ for $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$.  In every organism the residualized control basis has $rank(Z)=23$, the residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate block has $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, the measured translation readout contributes $rank(\widetilde T)=1$, and the joint block has $rank(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)=10$.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$ respectively, so none of the four measured mediation entries is an all-fit rank artifact.  The finite rank statement is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
rank(Z)&=23,\\
rank(\widetilde X)&=9,\\
rank(\widetilde T)&=1,\\
rank(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)&=10
\end{aligned}
$$
for each organism in the measured panel.

\paragraph{Measured translation and protein association.}
The translation-side survival values $S^{QT}_{meas}$ are positive in all four joined panels: $0.04034217614654355$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $0.009394266754822262$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast.  The corresponding direct protein-abundance associations $S^{QP}$ are also positive: $0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$.  Thus the finite-row surface does not stop at a direct protein-omics correlation: it contains a measured $Q \to T$ readout and a measured $Q \to P$ readout on the same coding-sequence units.

\paragraph{Measured mediation decomposition.}
The mediation scalar is computed by conditioning the $Q \to P$ association on measured $T$, using the joint nine-dimensional $X_Q$ block with a per-coordinate audit.  The raw measured fractions are $-0.04801486048721136$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $0.04091158340950673$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The reported bounded fractions are therefore $0.0$, $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$.  In finite terms,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}_{Danio} &= 0,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{E.coli} &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{human} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{yeast} &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The yeast row is the strongest measured mediation entry in this panel: $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436$, $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, and nearly one half of the direct $Q \to P$ association is absorbed by measured $TE$ under the stated controls.  The $E.\ coli$ and human rows are positive but small, while the danio row is suppressive in raw form and is therefore read as zero measured mediation rather than promoted.

\paragraph{Energy audit.}
The decomposition is not inferred from signs alone.  For yeast, the $Q$ block explains $675.3275481062249$ units of protein-residual energy before conditioning, measured $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$ units on $Y$, and the direct $Q$ improvement after $T$ is $351.63835021403213$.  In $E.\ coli$, the corresponding values are $393.78692134854015$, $35.56347808682994$, and $377.67647487021645$.  In human they are $565.3850203147235$, $2.0678517436514716$, and $563.803837143658$.  In danio they are $1092.0114819316218$, $16.418999670692678$, and $1144.4442608870015$.  This energy pattern explains the finite verdict: the same measured translation contact is present everywhere, but only yeast shows a large measured translation-conditioned absorption of the protein-abundance association.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite readback separates translation-null, protein-null, mediated, and direct-or-confounded outcomes.  This measured panel has positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ and positive $S^{QP}$ in all four organisms, but the mediated fraction differs sharply by organism.  Yeast is the clear measured-mediated case in this surface.  $E.\ coli$ and human remain weak measured-mediated entries.  Danio is a positive-positive row without measured absorption, since the raw mediated fraction is negative and the bounded value is $0$.  This matters because direct protein-omics survival alone is not a mechanism statement: the larger direct protein-abundance experiment found positive $S^{QP}$ in fifteen organisms, with $R^2_{QP}$ ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in $Halobacterium\ salinarum$ to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, but those direct values do not by themselves identify a measured translation route.

\paragraph{Relation to other measured layers.}
The measured-TE survival surface supports the existence of a non-null translation readout in the four organisms used here: all nine residual coordinates survive against measured $TE$ in danio, $E.\ coli$, human, and yeast, with $R^2_{QT,meas}$ equal to $0.04034217614654355$, $0.009394266754822262$, $0.018837911344127926$, and $0.13681931343730436$.  A separate joint variance partition that also includes mRNA stability reports joint mediation fractions of $0.2404669586626661$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ in all three organisms.  That auxiliary row is consistent with the present record in one important way: measured translation is strongest in yeast, but protein abundance is also shaped by non-translation readouts such as mRNA stability.  Therefore this chapter records measured translation-conditioned absorption, not a complete biological pathway.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The claim is ready as a finite measured-readout statement.  The row data show a rank-powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ matrix with measured $Q \to T$, measured $Q \to P$, and a controlled $Q \to T \to P$ decomposition on joined CDS units.  The positive result is strongest in yeast, weak but positive in $E.\ coli$ and human, and null after bounding in danio.  The admissible conclusion is that measured ribosome-profiling $TE$ can absorb a finite part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association in a rank-powered panel, with organism-specific magnitude.

\paragraph{Local scope boundary.}
No broader biological conclusion follows from this finite surface without another measured contact at the relevant layer.  The record does not prove a causal translation mechanism, elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, evolutionary direction, perturbation rescue, or universal biological law.  The measured $TE$ contact licenses only a statistical translation-readout decomposition on the joined rows; folding, physical state, function, phenotype, and causality each require their own matched finite measurement and their own controlled statistic.
