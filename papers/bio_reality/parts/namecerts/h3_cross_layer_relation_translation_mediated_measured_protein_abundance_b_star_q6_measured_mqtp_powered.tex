\subsection{Measured translation conditioning for rank-powered $B^*_{Q6}$ protein abundance}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This BioReality record tests whether a finite panel of synonymous residual coordinates has a measured translation-conditioned route into protein abundance.  The tested surface has nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each coding sequence in the joined organism panel, the design residualizes codon-coordinate rows, measured translation efficiency, and measured protein abundance against the same control family: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline.  The readout $T$ is $\log_{10}$ measured TE, with TE computed as ribosome footprint divided by mRNA in the local ribosome profiling files.  The readout $P$ is $\log_{10}$ protein abundance in ppm from the matched proteomics abundance files.

\paragraph{Finite rows and rank.}
The powered measured-mediation run is $d71384d16e71$.  Four organisms enter the same-row codon--TE--abundance join: $13140$ danio rows from $14410$ reported CDS rows, $3415$ Escherichia coli rows from $3739$ reported CDS rows, $11554$ human rows from $19053$ reported CDS rows, and $5128$ yeast rows from $5815$ reported CDS rows.  The matching TE panels are independently recorded with ribosome-TE join rates $0.9118667591950035$ for danio, $0.9133458143888741$ for E. coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for human, and $0.8818572656921754$ for yeast.  The broader direct protein-abundance surface gives abundance-CDS join hit rates $1.0$ for danio, $0.9978649586335735$ for E. coli, $0.9779294769799313$ for human, and $0.9156038419146592$ for yeast.  In every measured-mediation row the residualized coordinate matrix has rank $9$, the residualized measured-TE column has rank $1$, the joint $Q,T$ matrix has rank $10$, the control matrix has rank $23$, and the control-column count is $24$.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$ for danio, E. coli, human, and yeast respectively, so the reported survival and mediation fractions are not the all-saturated three-organism boundary.

The finite survival list separates $Q \to T$ from $Q \to P$.  In the measured-TE run $58611670ec19$, the $S^{QT,meas}$ values are $0.04034217614654355$ for danio, $0.009394266754822262$ for E. coli, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast; all four rows have all nine coordinates surviving at the positive threshold.  On the measured-mediation join, the corresponding direct $S^{QP}$ values are $0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$.  The direct protein-omics run $104db736a756$ gives the same qualitative direction on the broader abundance join: $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529807085213$ for danio, $0.10767526677985896$ for E. coli, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, again with nine surviving coordinates in each of those four rows.

\paragraph{Mediation readback.}
The measured translation-conditioned decomposition asks how much of the direct $Q \to P$ association is lost after adding measured $T$ to the residualized design.  The row-level finite calculation is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{danio:}      &\quad S^{QT}=0.04034217614654355,\quad S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652,\quad M^{QTP,meas}_{raw}=-0.04801486048721136,\quad M^{QTP,meas}=0.0,\\

Danio is therefore a suppressive or null measured-mediation row despite positive measured $Q \to T$ and $Q \to P$ survival.  E. coli and human have positive but small measured translation-conditioned absorption.  Yeast is the strongest row: measured TE absorbs about $47.93069656345167\%$ of the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association under the shared controls.

For danio, $X_Q$ explains $144.76301201119642$ units of $T$ energy and $1092.0114819316218$ units of $Y$ energy; measured $T$ alone explains $16.418999670692678$ units of $Y$, and the remaining direct $X_Q$ improvement given $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$.  For E. coli the corresponding values are $8.637891149939946$, $393.78692134854015$, $35.56347808682994$, and $377.67647487021645$.  For human they are $38.66659710161269$, $565.3850203147235$, $2.0678517436514716$, and $563.803837143658$.  For yeast they are $103.10404057290246$, $675.3275481062249$, $593.5847863333478$, and $351.63835021403213$.  The yeast row is therefore not merely a positive direct abundance association: it is the row in which the measured TE column itself carries a large amount of abundance-side energy and materially reduces the residual coordinate contribution.

\paragraph{Four-case placement and neighboring reductions.}
A separate four-case discriminant on modeled tAI and measured abundance, run $f37bd98e0739$, classifies yeast as case $D$ for all nine coordinates and classifies E. coli as seven case-$D$ coordinates plus two positive-positive rows without a mediation drop.  That modeled-readout result is not substituted for measured ribosome TE here; it only shows that the same coordinate family had already separated direct abundance survival from translation-conditioned absorption.  A measured-TE and mRNA-stability variance-partition run, $81f728964641$, gives joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$ for E. coli, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, with right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in all three rows.  Its single-mediator fractions separate TE-only from stability-only contributions: E. coli has $0.01975521340963268$ TE-only and $0.23052056324898387$ stability-only, human has $0.00549490864190343$ TE-only and $0.15314679870183337$ stability-only, and yeast has $0.4697188319693239$ TE-only and $0.0626618595344251$ stability-only.  The compact readout-dictionary run $a4dc9b09533a$ uses $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $1800$ joined yeast rows from $4758$ available rows, and $120$ permutations; its selection-aware coverage is $0.24538211439730545$, above a selection-aware null $95\%$ level of $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  These neighboring rows support the local interpretation that measured translation is a real but incomplete absorber of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The rank-powered measured panel is sufficient for a bounded cross-layer readback: there are positive measured $Q \to T$ entries, positive $Q \to P$ entries, and a directly computed $Q \to T \to P$ attenuation fraction on the same CDS-level rows.  The admissible claim is narrow.  Yeast shows a strong measured translation-conditioned abundance attenuation; E. coli and human show weaker positive attenuation; danio shows positive $Q \to T$ and $Q \to P$ survival but no positive measured-mediation fraction after clipping the suppressive raw value to the unit interval.  Thus $B^*_{Q6}$ is not only a direct protein-omics predictor on this panel, but its strongest protein-abundance row is materially routed through measured ribosome-derived TE.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
The computation is cross-sectional and observational.  It does not establish causality, temporal order, synonymous-edit rescue, elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, turnover mechanism, localization mechanism, post-translational modification, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal biological rule.  Measured TE is a condition-specific ribosome-profiling ratio, and PAXdb abundance is a steady-state proteomics readout.  A stronger biological conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact: perturbation or rescue for mechanism, structure or biophysical assay for folding and admissibility, functional assay for function, and phenotype measurement for organismal consequence.
