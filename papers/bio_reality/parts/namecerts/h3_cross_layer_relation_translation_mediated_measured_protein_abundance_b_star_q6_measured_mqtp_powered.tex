\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein abundance for $B^{*}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests a finite cross-layer matrix whose rows are nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates and whose columns are measured translation-efficiency and protein-abundance readouts on matched coding-sequence rows.  The residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$ from organism-local ribosome-profiling files, and the protein readout is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance in ppm joined by protein identifier.  Before the $Q\to T\to P$ calculation, $Q$, $T$, and $P$ are residualized against the same finite control family: intercept, coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline.

\paragraph{Rank and join checks.}
The measured mediation run is powered rather than a rank-saturated three-organism artifact.  The four organisms have three-way joins of $13{,}140$ rows for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $3{,}415$ rows for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $11{,}554$ rows for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $5{,}128$ rows for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  In every organism the control rank is $rank(Z)=23$ with $24$ control columns, the residualized coordinate rank is $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, the translation rank is $rank(\widetilde T)=1$, and the joint coordinate--translation rank is $rank(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)=10$.  The residual degrees of freedom after the control projection are $13{,}117$, $3{,}392$, $11{,}531$, and $5{,}105$, respectively.  Thus the row surface is large enough to distinguish a positive measured-translation association from a degenerate all-one fit.

\paragraph{Measured mediation statistic.}
For each organism the direct protein-abundance survival $S^{QP}$, the measured translation survival $S^{QT}_{meas}$, and the clipped measured mediation fraction $M^{QTP}_{meas}$ are reported as finite scalars:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{danio\_rerio}:&\quad S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652,\quad S^{QT}_{meas}=0.04034217614654355,\quad M^{QTP}_{raw}=-0.04801486048721136,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0,\\
\texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}:&\quad S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074,\quad S^{QT}_{meas}=0.009394266754822262,\quad M^{QTP}_{raw}=0.04091158340950673,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.04091158340950673,\\
\texttt{homo\_sapiens}:&\quad S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745,\quad S^{QT}_{meas}=0.018837911344127926,\quad M^{QTP}_{raw}=0.0027966485036786744,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0027966485036786744,\\
\texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}:&\quad S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042,\quad S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436,\quad M^{QTP}_{raw}=0.4793069656345167,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest measured translation-conditioned reduction is the yeast entry, where nearly one half of the direct $Q\to P$ association is absorbed by the measured ribosome-efficiency readout.  The bacterial and human entries remain positive but small.  The zebrafish entry is suppressive on the raw scale and is therefore read back as zero measured mediation, not as hidden support.

\paragraph{Energy readback.}
The same conclusion is visible in the residual energy decomposition.  For \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, $X$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units of residual protein-abundance energy, measured $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$, and the remaining direct improvement of $X$ after $T$ is $351.63835021403213$.  The measured translation readout therefore carries a substantial part of the same protein-abundance component.  For \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, by contrast, $X$ explains $565.3850203147235$ units on $P$, while $T$ explains only $2.0678517436514716$ units on $P$ and the direct improvement of $X$ after $T$ remains $563.803837143658$.  The human row is a positive measured-readout surface, but it is not a strong translation-mediated absorption surface.  The \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} row has $X$ energy on $P$ equal to $393.78692134854015$ and $T$ energy on $P$ equal to $35.56347808682994$, while the \texttt{danio\_rerio} row has $X$ energy on $P$ equal to $1092.0114819316218$ and $T$ energy on $P$ equal to $16.418999670692678$.

\paragraph{Relation to direct and translation-only surfaces.}
The direct protein-omics experiment found positive $S^{QP}$ across a broader fifteen-organism panel, with all nine coordinates surviving in each organism and examples ranging from $R^2_{QP}=0.019256877907572044$ in \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum} to $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  The measured ribosome-efficiency experiment found positive measured $S^{QT}$ on the four measured-TE organisms: $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, $0.009394266754822262$ for \texttt{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast.  The present row is stricter than either marginal surface: it asks whether the same measured translation readout reduces the direct codon-residual association with protein abundance.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite outcome is not a uniform biological law.  It is a four-case readback over residualized rows.  A coordinate or organism can be translation-null and protein-null, translation-surviving only, protein-omics-surviving only, or translation-surviving with measured absorption of the protein association.  The four-organism joint row supports the last case most clearly in yeast, weakly in \texttt{E. coli} and human, and not in zebrafish after clipping the suppressive raw fraction.  A related joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition also shows that mediation can be split across readouts: in yeast the joint mediation fraction is $0.5195182225621592$, with measured TE contributing $0.4697188319693239$ and mRNA stability contributing $0.0626618595344251$; in \texttt{E. coli} the corresponding joint fraction is $0.2404669586626661$, dominated by stability at $0.23052056324898387$ rather than TE at $0.01975521340963268$; in human it is $0.16890712099923358$, again dominated by stability at $0.15314679870183337$.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is a finite statistical decomposition on matched rows.  It says that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface has positive direct protein-abundance association and positive measured translation association, and that in some organisms the measured translation readout absorbs a portion of the direct association.  It does not by itself establish translation realization in the causal sense, ribosome mechanism, synonymous-edit rescue, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality.  Those stronger readings require separate layer-matched contacts and a separate finite statistic at the corresponding layer.  In particular, bulk PAXdb abundance is not a folding assay, measured TE is condition-specific and cross-sectional, and conditioning on TE is a variance-partition operation rather than a temporal intervention.
