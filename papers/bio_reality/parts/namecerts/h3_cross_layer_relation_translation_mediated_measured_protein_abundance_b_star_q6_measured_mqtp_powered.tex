\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein abundance for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-translation-protein-mediation}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer surface whose rows are coding-sequence units and whose explanatory coordinates are the nine residual $B^*_{Q6}$ directions
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$.  The readout $T$ is measured ribosome-sequencing translation efficiency, stored as $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$, and the readout $Y$ is measured protein abundance, stored as $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from proteomics rows joined by protein identifier.  The tested panel contains four organisms with same-CDS codon, measured-TE, and abundance joins: Danio rerio has $13140$ three-way rows out of $14410$ reported CDS rows; Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3415$ three-way rows out of $3739$ CDS rows; Homo sapiens has $11554$ three-way rows out of $19053$ CDS rows; and Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5128$ three-way rows out of $5815$ CDS rows.  The corresponding valid abundance row counts are $14410$, $3747$, $19483$, and $6351$, while the valid measured-TE row counts are $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.

\paragraph{Residualization and rank.}
For each organism the design removes a local control space $Z$ before testing either translation survival or protein-abundance survival.  The control columns are the intercept, coding length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine residual coordinates.  The resulting control rank is $rank(Z)=23$ with $24$ control columns in every organism.  The residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ matrix keeps full coordinate rank $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, the measured-TE readout has $rank(\widetilde T)=1$, and the joint design has $rank(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)=10$.  Thus the reported mediation quantities are not produced by the all-saturated three-row boundary; the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $13117$ for Danio rerio, $3392$ for Escherichia coli, $11531$ for Homo sapiens, and $5105$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

\paragraph{Measured translation survival.}
The first required contact is the positive $Q \to T$ contact.  The measured ribosome-TE survival values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{Danio} &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
S^{QT}_{E.coli} &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
S^{QT}_{Human} &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
S^{QT}_{Yeast} &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
All four organisms retain all nine residual coordinates as surviving coordinates under the positive threshold $10^{-12}$.  The measured-TE joins are also nontrivial: the TE join hit rates are $0.9118667591950035$ for Danio rerio, $0.9133458143888741$ for Escherichia coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.8818572656921754$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  These values make the measured translation layer a real finite contact for this chapter, while still remaining condition-specific ribosome-sequencing measurements rather than a universal translation law.

\paragraph{Measured mediation scalar.}
The mediation statistic compares the direct $Q \to Y$ fit with the fit after conditioning on measured $T$.  The finite result is organism-specific rather than uniform.  The raw measured mediation fractions and clipped reported fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}_{Danio,raw} &= -0.04801486048721136, & M^{QTP,meas}_{Danio} &= 0,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{E.coli,raw} &= 0.04091158340950673, & M^{QTP,meas}_{E.coli} &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{Human,raw} &= 0.0027966485036786744, & M^{QTP,meas}_{Human} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{Yeast,raw} &= 0.4793069656345167, & M^{QTP,meas}_{Yeast} &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readback is therefore strongest in yeast, small but positive in Escherichia coli, barely positive in human, and suppressive or null in Danio rerio.  In energy terms, conditioning on measured $T$ leaves direct $X$ improvement energies $1144.4442608870015$, $377.67647487021645$, $563.803837143658$, and $351.63835021403213$ for Danio, Escherichia coli, human, and yeast respectively.  The measured-TE incremental improvement given $X$ is $68.85177862607247$, $19.45303160850625$, $0.486668572585927$, and $269.89558844115504$ in the same order.  Hence the record supports a measured translation-conditioned component, but it also shows that much of the direct protein-abundance association remains outside the single measured-TE scalar.

\paragraph{Four-case interpretation.}
The finite readback has four local cases.  A coordinate is translation-null when the $Q \to T$ entry does not survive; protein-omics-null when the $Q \to Y$ entry does not survive; direct-only when $Q \to Y$ survives without the measured translation readout absorbing it; and measured-translation-mediated when both entries survive and conditioning on $T$ reduces the direct signal.  A separate modeled-tAI four-case audit on Escherichia coli and yeast classifies all coordinates into the direct-prediction case for the reported modeled readout: Escherichia coli has case counts $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=7$, plus $2$ positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop; Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=9$, plus $0$ positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop.  This auxiliary classification is not substituted for the measured ribosome-TE mediation above; it only shows that modeled tAI and measured TE must be kept as distinct readout routes.

\paragraph{Joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability comparison.}
A second finite decomposition conditions the same abundance residual on measured TE, mRNA stability, and both together for Escherichia coli, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The single-mediator splits show different biological shapes: Escherichia coli has $TE$-only fraction $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only fraction $0.23052056324898387$; human has $0.00549490864190343$ and $0.15314679870183337$; yeast has $0.4697188319693239$ and $0.0626618595344251$.  The overlap diagnostic is approximately independent in all three organisms, with joint-minus-single-sum values $-0.009808817995950458$, $0.010265413655496769$, and $-0.012862468941589822$ under a descriptive tolerance of $0.05$.  This strengthens the local readback: the measured-TE part is especially large in yeast, while the human and bacterial panels place more of the abundance connection into non-TE measured covariates.

\paragraph{Residual readout dictionary and $f_3$ stress.}
The yeast residual is not exhausted by the measured dictionary.  In the partial mediation certificate the active join uses $n=1800$ rows from $4758$ available rows, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $\rho_{join}=0.05$.  The best selected readout family covers $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ of the source signal after selection correction, with raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, naive fixed-subset null $95\%$ value $0.00598374993486675$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  For the $f_3\_stress$ component in yeast, the dwell mediation run uses $n=1257$ joined rows, base $n=1800$, $5$ folds, and $120$ permutations.  The base held-out $R^2$ is $0.1855578757644517$, the full held-out $R^2$ is $0.20412261520502883$, and the dwell increment is $0.01856473944057712$ against a permutation null $95\%$ value $0.0013115362189692937$.  The same $f_3$ component has dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$, and the positional audit assigns the leading dwell segment to the $5'$ window rather than the $3'$ window.  A structure-adjacent positional audit labels the ramp signal as $ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized$: the primary $50$-codon ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, the body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value is $0.05859375$, which does not pass the localization gate.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter therefore has a ready finite readback.  The data support positive $B^*_{Q6}$ survival against measured translation efficiency and positive survival against protein abundance on rank-powered joined panels.  They also support measured translation-conditioned attenuation in three of four organisms, with the strongest measured mediation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and a null or suppressive measured fraction in Danio rerio.  The conclusion is deliberately narrow: the chapter records a controlled statistical decomposition of codon-residual, measured-TE, and measured protein-abundance rows.  It does not show translation causality, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbation rescue, organismal phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger claim would require an additional layer-matched measurement or intervention and a separate finite statistic at that layer.
