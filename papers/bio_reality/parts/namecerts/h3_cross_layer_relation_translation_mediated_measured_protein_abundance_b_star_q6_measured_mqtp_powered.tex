\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein abundance for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-translation-conditioned-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite matrix surface whose rows are coding-sequence units and whose explanatory coordinates are the nine residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ directions
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The measured translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ supplied as ribosome footprint divided by mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling files.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from local proteomics abundance rows joined by protein identifier.  The covariate block contains an intercept, CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline; in each measured-mediation organism the reported control block has $24$ columns, rank $23$, and the residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block has rank $9$.  Thus the finite object being read is not a symbolic codon pattern alone, but a joined row table with codon residuals, measured translation efficiency, protein abundance, and the declared controls.

\paragraph{Joined measured rows.}
The measured $Q \to T \to P$ computation is present for four organisms.  Danio rerio has $14410$ CDS rows in the protein-abundance join and $13140$ three-way codon--TE--abundance rows.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3739$ CDS rows and $3415$ three-way rows.  Homo sapiens has $19053$ CDS rows and $11554$ three-way rows.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5815$ CDS rows and $5128$ three-way rows.  The corresponding measured-TE join hit rates in the TE survival computation are $0.9118667591950035$ for Danio rerio, $0.9133458143888741$ for Escherichia coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.8818572656921754$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  These row counts are large enough to avoid the rank-degenerate all-one surface that would occur in a small organism panel, and every listed organism is marked rank-sufficient for the residualized nine-coordinate test.

\paragraph{Residualized equations.}
For each organism, let $X$ denote the residualized $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ matrix, let $T$ denote residualized measured $\log_{10}(TE)$, and let $Y$ denote residualized protein abundance.  The mediated fraction is computed from the drop in the direct $X \to Y$ association after adding $T$ as a measured translation readout, with negative raw drops clipped to the reported unit interval endpoint.  In the notation used by the finite table,
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{meas} &= R^2(T \sim X \mid Z),\\
S^{QP} &= R^2(Y \sim X \mid Z),\\
M^{QTP}_{meas} &= \frac{S^{QP} - S^{QP\mid T}}{S^{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The computation is explicitly described as a joint nine-dimensional $X_Q$ mediation scalar with a per-coordinate decomposition audit, so the reported scalar is a controlled matrix readout rather than a single hand-selected coordinate.

\paragraph{Measured translation survival.}
All four measured-TE organisms show positive $Q \to T$ survival after residualization.  The measured $S^{QT}_{meas}$ values are $0.04034217614654355$ for Danio rerio, $0.009394266754822262$ for Escherichia coli, $0.018837911344127926$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.13681931343730436$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  Each entry reports all $9$ coordinates as surviving under the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The residual TE energies after controls are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$, respectively.  These values establish that the measured translation layer is not empty in the finite panel, although they do not by themselves determine whether the protein-abundance association is transmitted through measured translation.

\paragraph{Measured protein-abundance survival.}
The same coordinates also survive against measured protein abundance.  In the measured mediation table, $S^{QP}$ is $0.04926578454614652$ for Danio rerio, $0.10941535951828074$ for Escherichia coli, $0.04288464996165745$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.3146818503437042$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The larger direct protein-omics survey gives the same qualitative boundary across fifteen organisms: the direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.061906245886660105$ for Bacillus subtilis, $0.10217906758013379$ for Caenorhabditis elegans, $0.04613529807085213$ for Danio rerio, $0.10767526677985896$ for Escherichia coli, $0.04454565118019448$ for Homo sapiens, $0.049827984062539195$ for Mus musculus, $0.29837624994140827$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and $0.06397514251295629$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus, with all nine coordinates surviving in each listed organism.  This wider protein-omics result is a direct association surface only; the measured-mediation table is the narrower test that asks whether measured $TE$ absorbs part of that association.

\paragraph{Measured mediation readback.}
The four measured mediated fractions separate sharply by organism.  Danio rerio has a raw fraction $-0.04801486048721136$, reported as the unit-interval value $0.0$; thus the measured $TE$ readout does not absorb the direct protein-abundance association in that joined panel.  Escherichia coli has $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.04091158340950673$, with $S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074$ and $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.009394266754822262$.  Homo sapiens has $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0027966485036786744$, with $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$ and $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.018837911344127926$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has the strongest measured absorption, $M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793069656345167$, with $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$ and $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436$.  In energy form, the residualized table reports $X$-explained energy on $Y$ of $1092.0114819316218$, $393.78692134854015$, $565.3850203147235$, and $675.3275481062249$ for Danio, Escherichia coli, human, and yeast, while the direct $X$ improvement after conditioning on $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$, $377.67647487021645$, $563.803837143658$, and $351.63835021403213$.  The yeast entry is therefore the clearest finite-row case in which the measured translation readout absorbs a substantial part of the direct $Q \to P$ surface.

\paragraph{Four-case interpretation.}
The older modeled-tAI four-case table is useful only as a bounded comparison.  In that table, Escherichia coli has $R^2_{QT}=0.5416736993691318$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$, with seven coordinates assigned to case $D$ and two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $R^2_{QT}=0.8213457220900503$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, with all nine coordinates assigned to case $D$.  The measured ribosome-TE table replaces modeled tAI with observed footprint/mRNA readouts and gives smaller but still positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ values, especially in yeast.  The classification lesson is therefore not that tAI is a mechanism, but that the measured-readout matrix preserves a real distinction between translation-surviving, protein-omics-surviving, and translation-absorbed entries.

\paragraph{Adjacent residual checks.}
A joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition gives a broader context for the protein-abundance residual.  Its right-tail permutation $p$-value is $0.000999000999000999$ in Escherichia coli, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, respectively; the TE-only fractions are $0.01975521340963268$, $0.00549490864190343$, and $0.4697188319693239$, and the stability-only fractions are $0.23052056324898387$, $0.15314679870183337$, and $0.0626618595344251$.  The reported overlap mode is approximately independent in all three organisms under the fixed descriptive tolerance $0.05$.  Other candidate residual carriers do not replace the translation-mediated readback: localization absorption is $0.009254913329269913$ in human and $0.011225105608516642$ in yeast, while human turnover absorption is only $3.5740764900116538 \times 10^{-6}$ and does not exceed its null.  These adjacent computations support a bounded statement: measured translation and transcript/stability variables explain part of the abundance residual, but large unassigned residual structure remains.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is positive for a bounded cross-layer relation and negative for stronger biological promotion.  At least one organism, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, has positive measured translation survival, positive protein-abundance survival, and a large measured translation-conditioned absorption fraction, $0.4793069656345167$.  Escherichia coli and Homo sapiens show smaller positive measured fractions, while Danio rerio shows a suppressive or null measured fraction after clipping.  Therefore the chapter may state that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates reach measured protein abundance partly through measured translation readouts in some joined organism panels, with yeast as the strongest finite-row witness.  It may not state that this is causal mediation, a universal translation law, a folding result, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype realization, or a mechanism.  Those claims would require separate layer-matched contacts, perturbation or temporal evidence where causality is asserted, and additional measurements beyond codon counts, measured $TE$, mRNA controls, and protein abundance.
