\subsection{Measured Translation-Conditioned Protein Abundance}
\label{sec:bior-h3-cross-layer-translation-mediated-measured-protein-abundance-b-star-q6}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests a finite cross-layer surface in which nine synonymous residual coordinates are matched to measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency and measured protein abundance on the same coding-sequence units.  The coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K_{\mathrm{AAA}},\ \mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}},\ \mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUR}},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUG}},\\
&\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{AGY}},\ \mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UCG}},\ \mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{ACY}},\ f_{3}\text{-stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The four measured rows are ordered as
$$
\begin{aligned}
O=(\textit{Danio rerio},\ \textit{Escherichia coli}\ \mathrm{K12}\ \mathrm{MG1655},\ \textit{Homo sapiens},\ \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}).
\end{aligned}
$$
For each row, $X$ is the residualized $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design, $T$ is $\log_{10}$ measured translation efficiency with $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$, and $Y$ is $\log_{10}$ protein abundance in ppm from the local proteomics abundance tables.  The control design has $24$ columns before rank reduction and residualizes against CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; in all four rows the computed control rank is $23$, the residualized coordinate rank is $9$, the residualized translation-readout rank is $1$, and the joint $X,T$ rank is $10$.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank sufficiency.}
The three-way joins are nondegenerate.  The measured TE, abundance, and CDS joins contain $13{,}140$ rows for \textit{D. rerio}, $3{,}415$ rows for \textit{E. coli}, $11{,}554$ rows for human, and $5{,}128$ rows for yeast.  The corresponding residual degrees of freedom after the control fit are $13{,}117$, $3{,}392$, $11{,}531$, and $5{,}105$.  The rank-sufficiency flag is true in every row, with degeneracy threshold $0.999999$ and rank tolerance $10^{-10}$, so the result is not an all-fit rank artifact.  The measured TE join rates in the supporting TE audit are $0.9118667592$, $0.9133458144$, $0.6064136881$, and $0.8818572657$ in the same organism order; the protein-abundance-only join rates for those organisms are $1.0$, $0.9978649586$, $0.9779294770$, and $0.9156038419$.

\paragraph{Two survival lists.}
The translation-readout and protein-abundance survival lists are both positive in every measured row:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}} &= (0.0403421761,\ 0.00939426675,\ 0.0188379113,\ 0.1368193134),\\
S^{QP} &= (0.0492657845,\ 0.1094153595,\ 0.0428846500,\ 0.3146818503).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite matrix is not merely a direct protein-omics association: each organism has a positive measured $Q\to T$ readout and a positive direct $Q\to P$ readout after the stated controls.  The sizes are uneven.  Yeast is the strongest row on both measured TE and protein abundance, human has a detectable but small measured-TE component, \textit{E. coli} has a small measured-TE component with a larger direct abundance component, and \textit{D. rerio} has a positive measured-TE association but does not show positive attenuation in the mediation scalar below.

\paragraph{Measured mediation scalar.}
The mediation computation compares the direct $X\to Y$ explained component with the remaining $X\to Y$ component after conditioning on the measured $T$ readout.  In the same row order, the raw and clipped measured fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,\mathrm{meas}}_{\mathrm{raw}} &= (-0.0480148605,\ 0.0409115834,\ 0.00279664850,\ 0.4793069656),\\
M^{QTP,\mathrm{meas}} &= (0,\ 0.0409115834,\ 0.00279664850,\ 0.4793069656).
\end{aligned}
$$
The unit-interval flag is true in all four rows.  The interpretation is therefore row-specific.  Yeast supplies the clearest finite measured-readout mediation signal: roughly $47.93\%$ of the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance association is absorbed by the measured TE readout.  \textit{E. coli} supplies a small positive measured mediation fraction of about $4.09\%$.  Human supplies only a trace measured fraction of about $0.28\%$.  \textit{D. rerio} is suppressive on the raw scale and is clipped to zero; it remains positive for both $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}$ and $S^{QP}$, but this row does not support measured TE absorption of the direct protein-abundance association.

\paragraph{Energy accounting.}
The residual energy diagnostics give the same finite pattern.  For \textit{D. rerio}, $X$ explains $1092.0114819$ units of $Y$ energy and the direct improvement after conditioning on $T$ is $1144.4442609$, producing the negative raw fraction.  For \textit{E. coli}, these two quantities are $393.7869213$ and $377.6764749$.  For human, they are $565.3850203$ and $563.8038371$.  For yeast, they are $675.3275481$ and $351.6383502$, which is the large attenuation case.  The measured TE readout itself explains $16.4189997$, $35.5634781$, $2.06785174$, and $593.5847863$ units of $Y$ energy, respectively.  The yeast row is therefore not just a larger $Q\to T$ survival value; it is also the row in which measured TE carries a large amount of protein-abundance residual energy.

\paragraph{Checks against nearby readouts.}
A separate TE-plus-mRNA-stability variance partition on \textit{E. coli}, human, and yeast reports joint mediation fractions $0.2404669587$, $0.1689071210$, and $0.5195182226$, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ in each row.  This does not make the present measured-TE scalar causal, but it shows that the abundance residual is reducible by measured readout families in the same finite regime.  A yeast readout-dictionary certificate with $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$ gives raw best coverage $0.2745002077$, selection-aware coverage $C^{\ast}=0.2453821144$, selection-aware null $95\%$ level $0.02914900724$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856$.  A structural-order positional audit classifies the related ramp signal as \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}, with weighted ramp held-out $\Delta R^{2}=0.00111656401$, weighted body held-out $\Delta R^{2}=-0.000261573139$, ramp sign-flip $p=0.0478515625$, and paired ramp-minus-body $p=0.05859375$.  These checks keep the present chapter in the measured-abundance and measured-TE layer rather than promoting it into structural localization or function.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite readback has four local outcomes.  A row is translation-null if $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}\leq 0$, protein-omics-null if $S^{QP}\leq 0$, direct-only if both are positive but conditioning on $T$ fails to reduce the direct signal, and measured-mediation-positive if both are positive and the measured mediation fraction is positive.  Under that rule, no row is translation-null and no row is protein-omics-null.  \textit{D. rerio} is direct-only on the measured mediation scalar.  \textit{E. coli}, human, and yeast are measured-mediation-positive, with yeast carrying the only large fraction in this finite panel.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The claim established here is a controlled, cross-sectional statistical decomposition over finite joined rows.  The separate measured TE contact licenses the bounded statement that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against a measured translation-efficiency readout in these rows.  It does not by itself establish a causal translation process, elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, synonymous-edit rescue, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universal biological law.  A stronger statement at any of those layers would require its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  Bulk protein abundance also remains a steady-state proteomics readout: it can participate in the $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition, but it cannot substitute for perturbation timing, folding measurement, localization, turnover, PTM state, pathway function, or organismal phenotype.
