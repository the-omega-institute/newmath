\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein abundance for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{subsec:bior reality-q6-measured-translation-protein-mediation}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine residual coordinates of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ retain a protein-abundance association after the translation layer has been measured directly and placed in the conditioning set.  The coordinate list is finite and fixed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{\ast}_{Q6}=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each coding sequence row, the matrix carries residual coordinates $Q$, a measured translation readout $T=\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$, and a protein readout $Y=\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from matched abundance rows.  The experiment is identified by run $d71384d16e71$ and uses the same CDS-level units for codon usage, measured ribosome-derived translation efficiency, matched mRNA abundance, and protein abundance whenever the join succeeds.

\paragraph{Matched rows and rank.}
The joined panels are not rank-degenerate.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5128$ three-way CDS rows, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3415$, Homo sapiens has $11554$, and Danio rerio has $13140$.  After the control projection, the residual degrees of freedom are respectively $5105$, $3392$, $11531$, and $13117$.  In all four organisms the residualized control matrix has rank $23$ with $24$ control columns, the residual coordinate block has rank $9$, the measured translation column has rank $1$, and the joined $(Q,T)$ block has rank $10$ at tolerance $10^{-10}$.  Thus the all-$1.0$ small-panel artifact is not being used here; the panels have enough residual rank to separate a nine-coordinate $Q$ block from a one-dimensional measured translation readout.

\paragraph{Controlled decomposition.}
The control set removes amino-acid composition, organism-specific coding context, $GC3$, CDS length, $M$-density, and matched transcript controls before the three measured surfaces are read.  The reported quantities are $S^{QT}$ for residual-coordinate survival on measured translation efficiency, $S^{QP}$ for residual-coordinate survival on protein abundance, and $M^{QTP,meas}$ for the fraction of the direct $Q \to P$ association absorbed by the measured translation readout.  The finite readout is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{S. cerevisiae:}\quad &S^{QT}=0.13681931343730436,\quad S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042,\quad M^{QTP,meas}=0.4793069656345167,\\
\text{E. coli K12:}\quad &S^{QT}=0.009394266754822262,\quad S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074,\quad M^{QTP,meas}=0.04091158340950673,\\
\text{H. sapiens:}\quad &S^{QT}=0.018837911344127926,\quad S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745,\quad M^{QTP,meas}=0.0027966485036786744,\\
\text{D. rerio:}\quad &S^{QT}=0.04034217614654355,\quad S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652,\quad M^{QTP,meas}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw Danio mediation fraction is $-0.04801486048721136$ and is therefore clipped to the admissible measured interval at $0$.  The other three organisms have positive but very different measured fractions, from a weak human value through a small bacterial value to a large yeast value.  This makes the result a finite cross-layer decomposition rather than a universal rule.

\paragraph{Energy audit.}
The same conclusion is visible in the residual-energy accounting.  In yeast, $Q$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units of protein-abundance residual energy, $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$, and the remaining direct $Q$ improvement after $T$ is $351.63835021403213$; the measured translation column accounts for a substantial but incomplete part of the protein-abundance surface.  In E. coli, $Q$ explains $393.78692134854015$ units on $Y$, $T$ explains $35.56347808682994$, and the direct $Q$ improvement after $T$ remains $377.67647487021645$.  In human, $Q$ explains $565.3850203147235$ units on $Y$, while $T$ explains only $2.0678517436514716$ and leaves $563.803837143658$ units of direct $Q$ improvement after $T$.  In Danio, $Q$ explains $1092.0114819316218$ units on $Y$, $T$ explains $16.418999670692678$, and the direct $Q$ improvement after $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$, consistent with the suppressive raw mediation sign.  The measured TE column is therefore strongest as a mediator in yeast and much weaker or non-absorbing in the other panels.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
Each organism-row surface is read by four cases after the shared residualization.  Case A is null when neither $S^{QT}$ nor $S^{QP}$ survives.  Case B is translation-readout survival without protein-abundance survival.  Case C is direct protein-abundance prediction without a measured translation-mediated absorption step.  Case D is measured translation-conditioned protein mediation: $S^{QT}>0$, $S^{QP}>0$, and the measured $T$ column removes or reduces a positive part of the direct $Q\to P$ association.  Under this finite run, yeast is the clearest case D row, with $47.93069656345167\%$ measured absorption.  E. coli is a weak case D row, human is a minimal case D row, and Danio is not case D despite positive $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ because its measured mediation fraction is clipped to zero from a negative raw value.  Danio is therefore reported as direct or suppressive protein-omics association under measured TE conditioning, not as mediated propagation.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The claim stops at a powered, cross-sectional statistical decomposition.  Measured ribosome-derived $TE$ supplies a translation-layer readout for these rows, and PAXdb-style abundance supplies a protein-abundance readout, but this does not by itself establish a causal mediation effect, temporal ordering, elongation or initiation mechanism, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, molecular function, physical admissibility, phenotype, perturbation rescue, or a universal biological law.  Any such assertion would need its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  The present record says only that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has positive measured translation survival and positive protein-abundance survival in all four rank-powered joined panels, while measured translation absorbs a substantial part of the direct protein-abundance association only in yeast, weakly in E. coli and human, and not in Danio.
