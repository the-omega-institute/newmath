\subsection{Measured translation conditioning of $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein abundance}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-translation-mediated-measured-protein-abundance-b-star-q6-measured-mqtp-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite measured surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional synonymous residual surface $B^{*}_{Q6}$ carries a protein-abundance association that is reduced after conditioning on a measured translation readout.  The finite rows are coding-sequence units for which codon counts, ribosome-derived translation efficiency, and PAXdb protein abundance are all available on the same protein or gene key.  The residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  In every measured organism the design has $9$ residual coordinates, $24$ control columns, control rank $23$, and residual coordinate rank $9$ after the controls.  The controls remove amino-acid composition, coding-sequence length, $GC3$, organism-matched composition effects where present, and the $M$-density baseline before the tested $Q$, $T$, and $P$ relations are read.

\paragraph{Readouts and joins.}
The translation readout is $T=\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ computed as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling files.  The abundance readout is $P=\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\ ppm})$ from the matched proteomics abundance file joined by protein identifier.  The measured three-way joins are large enough to avoid the rank-saturation boundary: danio rerio has $13140$ joined rows from $14410$ codon-abundance rows and $13140$ TE rows; Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3415$ joined rows from $3739$ codon-abundance rows and $3415$ TE rows; Homo sapiens has $11554$ joined rows from $19053$ codon-abundance rows and $11554$ TE rows; Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5128$ joined rows from $5815$ codon-abundance rows and $5128$ TE rows.  The measured TE hit rates in the sibling translation-efficiency contact are $0.9133458143888741$ for E. coli, $0.6064136881331024$ for human, and $0.8818572656921754$ for yeast, with danio at $0.9118667591950035$.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
Let $X_{Q}$ be the residualized $9$-column $B^{*}_{Q6}$ matrix, let $T$ be residualized measured translation efficiency, and let $Y$ be residualized protein abundance.  The reported scalar is the fraction of the direct $X_{Q}\rightarrow Y$ association that is removed after adding $T$ to the model, clipped to the unit interval for the final measured-fraction field while retaining the raw suppressive sign when it occurs:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QP} &= R^{2}(Y\sim X_{Q}\mid Z),\\
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}} &= R^{2}(T\sim X_{Q}\mid Z),\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}} &= \frac{R^{2}(Y\sim X_{Q}\mid Z)-R^{2}(Y\sim X_{Q}\mid Z,T)}{R^{2}(Y\sim X_{Q}\mid Z)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The calculation is therefore a measured-readout attenuation test, not a causal model.  It asks whether a ribosome-derived $T$ absorbs part of a $Q$ to protein-abundance association after the declared controls.

\paragraph{Per-organism outcomes.}
Danio has $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ and $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.04034217614654355$, but its raw mediation fraction is $-0.04801486048721136$ and the clipped measured fraction is $0.0$.  This is a suppressive or non-absorbing case: $Q$ survives against both readouts, but measured $T$ does not explain the direct abundance association.  E. coli has $S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$, and $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.04091158340950673$.  Human has $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$, and $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.0027966485036786744$.  Yeast is the strong measured-mediation row, with $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, and $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.4793069656345167$.  The direct and conditioned energy audit is consistent with this ranking: yeast has $X$ explained energy on $Y$ of $675.3275481062249$ and a remaining direct $X$ improvement after $T$ of $351.63835021403213$, while human has $565.3850203147235$ and $563.803837143658$, respectively.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The same finite geometry has a modeled-translation four-case audit that is useful as a diagnostic contrast but is not substituted for measured ribosome TE.  In the modeled-tAI discriminant, yeast has $R^{2}_{QT}=0.8213457220900503$ and $R^{2}_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, and all nine coordinates land in case $D$.  E. coli has $R^{2}_{QT}=0.5416736993691318$ and $R^{2}_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$, with seven coordinates in case $D$ and two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop.  This explains why the measured mediation chapter separates direct protein-omics survival from mediated readback: positive $Q\rightarrow P$ and positive $Q\rightarrow T$ are not enough unless conditioning on $T$ reduces the $Q\rightarrow P$ term.

\paragraph{Relation to adjacent measured rows.}
The direct protein-omics panel shows that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ surface is not a small artifact of one organism: across fifteen PAXdb joins, all nine coordinates survive the direct abundance readout, with $R^{2}_{QP}$ ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in Halobacterium salinarum to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast.  The largest direct row is yeast, whose join rate is $0.9156038419146592$ with $5815$ proteins used.  A joint TE and mRNA-stability decomposition on E. coli, human, and yeast gives joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, respectively, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ in all three organisms.  In yeast, the measured-TE-only fraction in that joint audit is $0.4697188319693239$ and the stability-only fraction is $0.0626618595344251$, close to the measured-mediation row in which $T$ removes $0.4793069656345167$ of the $Q$ abundance association.  A separate yeast partial-mediation dictionary gives coverage $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$ at $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ on $1800$ sampled joined rows.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell row adds a local ribosome-occupancy check: in yeast, $f3\_stress$ has dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$, approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$, and held-out $\Delta R^{2}=0.01856473944057712$ above a permutation null $95\%$ value of $0.0013115362189692937$.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The finite result is ready as a cross-sectional statistical mediation statement.  It supports the following bounded reading: in a rank-powered joined panel, at least one measured organism, especially yeast, shows a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-to-protein abundance association that is materially reduced by measured ribosome-derived translation efficiency.  It does not by itself establish translation as a causal process, does not isolate initiation or elongation, does not identify protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Those interpretations require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.  Direct $Q\rightarrow P$ survival without measured $Q\rightarrow T\rightarrow P$ attenuation remains a protein-omics prediction boundary rather than a mechanism claim.
