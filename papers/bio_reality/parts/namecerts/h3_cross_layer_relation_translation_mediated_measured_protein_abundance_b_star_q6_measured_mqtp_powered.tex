\subsection{Measured translation-conditioned protein abundance for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-mqtp-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record is a finite matched matrix over nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, one measured translation-efficiency column, one measured protein-abundance column, and a shared control design.  The residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE$ defined as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA in the local ribosome-efficiency files.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from local proteomics abundance rows joined by protein identifier.  All reported quantities are residualized against the same $Z$ family: intercept, coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine residual coordinates.  In every measured-mediation organism the residual coordinate rank is $9$, the residualized translation rank is $1$, the combined $Q,T$ rank is $10$, the control rank is $23$, and the control column count is $24$.

\paragraph{Observed joins and rank support.}
The measured mediation table is rank-powered rather than a small saturated surface.  The three-way joins contain $13140$ rows for Danio rerio, $3415$ rows for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $11554$ rows for Homo sapiens, and $5128$ rows for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The corresponding reported CDS joins are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, respectively.  These joins are paired with valid abundance counts $14410$, $3747$, $19483$, and $6351$, and valid measured-TE counts $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $13117$ for Danio, $3392$ for E. coli, $11531$ for human, and $5105$ for yeast.  Thus the four rows are not the all-$1.0$ rank-degenerate boundary; they support an actual residualized regression comparison.

\paragraph{Measured $Q \to T$ and direct $Q \to P$ survival.}
The measured translation-survival audit gives positive $S^{QT,meas}$ in all four organisms: $0.04034217614654355$ in Danio, $0.009394266754822262$ in E. coli, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast.  Direct protein-abundance survival on the same measured-mediation rows is also positive: $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ in Danio, $0.10941535951828074$ in E. coli, $0.04288464996165745$ in human, and $0.3146818503437042$ in yeast.  The older broad proteomics survival table, computed across fifteen organisms with the same nine coordinates, is consistent with a direct protein-omics association but not with mechanism by itself: for example yeast has $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, E. coli has $0.10767526677985896$, human has $0.04454565118019448$, mouse has $0.049827984062539195$, and Halobacterium salinarum has $0.019256877907572044$, each with all nine coordinates surviving under the stated positivity threshold.

\paragraph{Measured mediation calculation.}
The mediation statistic asks how much of the direct residual association between $Q$ and $P$ is absorbed when the measured translation readout $T$ is placed in the model.  In the finite row notation used here, the scalar summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas} &= \frac{S^{QP}-S^{QP\mid T}}{S^{QP}},\\
0 &\leq M^{QTP,meas} \leq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
after clipping suppressive raw values to the null side of the readback.  The measured fractions are organism-specific.  Danio has raw $M^{QTP,meas}=-0.04801486048721136$, so its reported mediated fraction is $0.0$: the measured translation readout survives, but it does not absorb the direct protein-abundance signal.  E. coli has $M^{QTP,meas}=0.04091158340950673$.  Human has a very small positive value, $0.0027966485036786744$.  Yeast is the strong row, with $M^{QTP,meas}=0.4793069656345167$.  The corresponding residualized energy diagnostics also separate the rows.  In yeast, $X$ explains $675.3275481062249$ units on $Y$, $T$ explains $593.5847863333478$ units on $Y$, and the direct $X$ improvement after $T$ is $351.63835021403213$.  In human, the comparable values are $565.3850203147235$, $2.0678517436514716$, and $563.803837143658$, showing that the measured TE column is present but barely mediating the protein-abundance association.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite readback is therefore not a single biological label.  Danio is a positive-translation, positive-protein row with suppressive or null measured mediation.  E. coli is positive on both surfaces but only weakly measured-mediated.  Human is positive on both surfaces with a near-zero measured mediation fraction.  Yeast is the row in which translation conditioning removes a substantial part of the direct $Q \to P$ signal.  This justifies the local four-case distinction: translation-surviving and protein-surviving does not by itself imply mediated propagation; the mediation statement is reserved for rows where conditioning on measured $T$ reduces the $Q \to P$ association.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The measured rows support a bounded statement: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured translation efficiency and measured protein abundance in the four joined organisms, and yeast gives a substantial measured translation-conditioned absorption of the protein-abundance association.  E. coli and human give only weak measured absorption, and Danio gives a suppressive raw mediation value clipped to the null side.  Consequently the claim is ready as a finite statistical mediation record, not as a causal biological mechanism.  No broader translation mechanism, protein folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, phenotype statement, or universal biological law follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies that layer and is evaluated on joined rows with its own controls.
