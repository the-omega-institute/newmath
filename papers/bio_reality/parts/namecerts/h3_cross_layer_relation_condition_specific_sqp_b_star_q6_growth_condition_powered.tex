\subsection{Condition-specific protein-abundance readback of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual packet}
\label{sec:bioreality-condition-specific-sqp-b-star-q6-growth-condition}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual packet gives the same protein-abundance readback in two growth conditions of \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The response variable is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from the GFP-tagged yeast PAXdb condition files labelled by the task as the Newman growth-condition contrast; the downloaded file headers report Newman, Nature, 2006 and \texttt{paxdb\_publication\_year=2006}.  The two conditions are YEPD rich medium and SD minimal medium.  The calculation is deliberately limited to a descriptive cross-layer association between codon-residual coordinates and a protein-abundance readout; it is not a perturbation, a translation-efficiency experiment, or a molecular mechanism assay.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control design.}
For each joined coding sequence, the design uses the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates
$\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}/\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The nuisance basis $Z$ has 24 columns in each condition and includes an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same real CDS counts, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the reported $S^{QP}$ entries are not raw codon-count correlations; they are residualized abundance associations after amino-acid composition, sequence length, GC3, and the simpler codon-support baseline have been accounted for.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The condition files yielded 2442 joined SD rows and 2517 joined YEPD rows, with 2209 proteins shared between the two condition-specific fits.  In both conditions the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ design has rank $9$, the control basis has rank $23$, and the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $2186$.  The fitted condition-level survival scores are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{SD}) &= 0.3050385538305022,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{YEPD}) &= 0.3280825603424665.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding explained and residual energies are
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{expl}}(\mathrm{SD}) &= 131.4599852310053, & E_{\mathrm{res}}(\mathrm{SD}) &= 430.9618688529857,\\
E_{\mathrm{expl}}(\mathrm{YEPD}) &= 149.3320327478236, & E_{\mathrm{res}}(\mathrm{YEPD}) &= 455.16601855320954.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both condition fits select $f_3$-stress as the dominant coordinate.  Comparing the two nine-entry $S^{QP}$ vectors over the shared 2209 proteins gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos(S^{QP}_{\mathrm{SD}},S^{QP}_{\mathrm{YEPD}}) &= 0.9993775935182967,\\
\rho(S^{QP}_{\mathrm{SD}},S^{QP}_{\mathrm{YEPD}}) &= 0.9983880256003688,
\end{aligned}
$$
with 1000 permutations used for the condition-comparison readback.  The same dominant coordinate and the near-unit cosine show that, within this one yeast study and this pair of media, the abundance-facing $B^*_{Q6}$ signature is highly condition-stable rather than condition-switched.

\paragraph{Relation to coordinate decomposition.}
A separate yeast coordinate-decomposition calculation gives a useful internal check on the interpretation of the dominant coordinate.  On the unified yeast abundance join it used $n=1258$ rows, a 5-fold held-out design, 120 deterministic target-label permutations, and the same nine-coordinate source design residualized against sibling controls.  The decomposition table contains all 9 coordinates, and the reported real-source set includes $\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f_3$-stress, with $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}/\mathrm{UCG}}$ not listed among the coordinates passing that held-out/permutation gate.  This does not re-estimate the two-condition matrix, but it supports the narrower statement that the yeast abundance association is not carried only by a single arbitrary coordinate artifact.

\paragraph{The $f_3$-stress substructure.}
Because the two growth-condition fits both identify $f_3$-stress as dominant, the family decomposition of that coordinate is relevant.  In the yeast family calculation, the full $f_3$ projection has norm-square $52.76911713166682$ and accounts for $0.12259712041843235$ of the target energy.  The target norm-square is $430.42705205115925$.  The decomposition spans 21 synonymous-family subcoordinates and, under a 5-fold held-out design with 120 permutations, reports 19 families as real abundance sources: $\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{UUU}\to\mathrm{UUC}}$, $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{UUA}\to\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{UCU}\to\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Y}_{\mathrm{UAU}\to\mathrm{UAC}}$, $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{UGU}\to\mathrm{UGC}}$, $\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{CCU}\to\mathrm{CCG}}$, $\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{CAU}\to\mathrm{CAC}}$, $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{CAA}\to\mathrm{CAG}}$, $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{CGU}\to\mathrm{CGG}}$, $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{AUU}\to\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{ACU}\to\mathrm{ACG}}$, $\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{AAU}\to\mathrm{AAC}}$, $\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{AAA}\to\mathrm{AAG}}$, $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{AGA}\to\mathrm{AGG}}$, $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{GUU}\to\mathrm{GUG}}$, $\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{GCU}\to\mathrm{GCG}}$, $\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{GAU}\to\mathrm{GAC}}$, $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{GAA}\to\mathrm{GAG}}$, and $\mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{GGU}\to\mathrm{GGG}}$.  The condition-specific result therefore fits a broader yeast pattern in which $f_3$-stress is not merely a label for one codon family, but a compound synonymous-family axis with many contributing family-level readbacks.

\paragraph{Mediation boundary.}
The condition-specific result should also be separated from mRNA-stability mediation.  In the yeast mRNA half-life mediation calculation, the three-way join contains 5085 rows, the baseline abundance association is $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, conditioning on residualized log half-life gives $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, and the absorbed share is $0.027596042074056304$.  The mediation fraction is $0.09994436477872035$, with a deterministic residual-mediator permutation null mean $0.00019674487584783138$, null 95th percentile $0.0014571003414947088$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  Those numbers show that an mRNA-stability readout can absorb a nonzero part of the yeast abundance association, but the two-condition GFP-abundance comparison here did not include matched mRNA rows and did not estimate a causal path.  The present chapter therefore reports condition stability of an abundance-facing residual projection, not a resolved transcriptional or translational mediation chain.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row evidence supports a bounded positive statement: in one \emph{S. cerevisiae} protein-abundance study, after amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and $M$-density controls, the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ packet has a substantial residual association with $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ in both SD and YEPD, with $R^2_{QP}=0.3050385538305022$ and $0.3280825603424665$, and the condition-specific survival vectors are nearly parallel.  The empirical readback is therefore condition-stable for this pair of media and this assay, with $f_3$-stress dominant in both fits.  Nothing in this finite row establishes ribosome-mediated translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Such claims would require separate layer-matched contacts, such as measured ribosome profiling, matched mRNA control, perturbation, structural, physical, functional, or phenotype assays, and their own controlled statistics.
