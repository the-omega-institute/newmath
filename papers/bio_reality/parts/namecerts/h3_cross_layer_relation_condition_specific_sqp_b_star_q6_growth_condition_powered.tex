\subsection{Condition-specific $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance projection in yeast}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-condition-specific-sqp}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row and measured object.}
This record isolates one organism, \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and asks whether the nine synonymous-residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates retain a protein-abundance projection in two growth conditions after the same sequence-derived controls are imposed.  The readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from GFP-tagged yeast abundance rows routed through the PAXdb condition files.  The two condition labels are rich medium YEPD and minimal medium SD.  The source note is kept explicit: the task label names Newman et al. 2009, whereas the downloaded file headers report Newman, Nature, 2006 and $\mathrm{paxdb\_publication\_year}=2006$.  The finite join contains $2517$ YEPD rows and $2442$ SD rows before the shared-condition intersection, with $2209$ shared proteins used for the condition comparison.

\paragraph{Residual design.}
For each condition the design uses the same nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K_{AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg}_{AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile}_{AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{CUN/UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{UUA/UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR/AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser}_{UCA/UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr}_{ACR/ACY},\ f_3\text{-stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control block has $24$ columns in both SD and YEPD: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$ computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same CDS rows, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The resulting control rank is $23$, the residualized $Q$ rank is $9$, and the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $2186$ in both conditions.

\paragraph{Condition-specific projection values.}
The finite-row statistic is the controlled protein-abundance projection score $S^{QP}$, reported here by its partial $R^2$ and dominant coordinate.  In SD the computation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{SD})&=0.3050385538305022,\\
\mathrm{explained\ energy}(\mathrm{SD})&=131.4599852310053,\\
\mathrm{residual\ readout\ energy}(\mathrm{SD})&=430.9618688529857,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $f_3$-stress as the dominant coordinate.  In YEPD the corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{YEPD})&=0.3280825603424665,\\
\mathrm{explained\ energy}(\mathrm{YEPD})&=149.3320327478236,\\
\mathrm{residual\ readout\ energy}(\mathrm{YEPD})&=455.16601855320954,
\end{aligned}
$$
again with $f_3$-stress as the dominant coordinate.  The dominant-coordinate identity therefore does not change across the two tested growth conditions.

\paragraph{Condition comparison.}
The two $S^{QP}$ vectors are almost collinear on the shared $2209$ proteins.  The finite comparison reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos\bigl(S^{QP}_{\mathrm{SD}},S^{QP}_{\mathrm{YEPD}}\bigr)&=0.9993775935182967,\\
\rho\bigl(S^{QP}_{\mathrm{SD}},S^{QP}_{\mathrm{YEPD}}\bigr)&=0.9983880256003688,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $1000$ permutations used for the condition-comparison check.  This supports a narrow statement: under this yeast GFP-abundance contact and this shared residual design, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance projection is condition-stable between SD and YEPD at the level of the nine-coordinate projection vector.  It is not a growth-law statement and it is not a perturbational causal estimate.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate context.}
The separate $f_3$-stress family decomposition supplies a useful diagnostic for why the condition row is not merely a nameless nine-dimensional score.  On a yeast abundance join with $n=1800$, the full residualized $f_3$-stress projection has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_{f_3}\|^2&=52.76911713166682,\\
\|P_{\mathrm{target}}\|^2&=430.42705205115925,\\
\|P_{f_3}\|^2/\|P_{\mathrm{target}}\|^2&=0.12259712041843235.
\end{aligned}
$$
That decomposition enumerates $21$ synonymous-family subcoordinates and marks $19$ families as real abundance sources under the held-out/permutation gate: $F_{UUU\to UUC}$, $L_{UUA\to UUG}$, $S_{UCU\to UCG}$, $Y_{UAU\to UAC}$, $C_{UGU\to UGC}$, $P_{CCU\to CCG}$, $H_{CAU\to CAC}$, $Q_{CAA\to CAG}$, $R_{CGU\to CGG}$, $I_{AUU\to AUA}$, $T_{ACU\to ACG}$, $N_{AAU\to AAC}$, $K_{AAA\to AAG}$, $R_{AGA\to AGG}$, $V_{GUU\to GUG}$, $A_{GCU\to GCG}$, $D_{GAU\to GAC}$, $E_{GAA\to GAG}$, and $G_{GGU\to GGG}$.  This auxiliary row does not prove the condition-specific comparison, but it makes the repeated $f_3$-stress dominance biologically inspectable at synonymous-family resolution.

The abundance association also has bounded neighboring rows that constrain interpretation.  In the yeast mRNA-stability mediation row, the same nine-coordinate abundance projection has $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, while conditioning on residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA half-life gives $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$.  The absorbed component is $0.027596042074056304$, the mediation fraction is $0.09994436477872035$, and the right-tail permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$ against a null mean of $0.00019674487584783138$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0014571003414947088$.  In the non-translation residual ranking row, the stated description-length regularizer is $\lambda=0.01$ with $DL=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$; for yeast the highest ranked candidate is mRNA with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_H C_H=0.0011462622274400686$, score $-0.008853737772559931$, $n=5128$, null $95$th percentile $0.0004745820831535835$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0013014749238591248$.  These neighboring numbers say that transcript-layer covariation is present, but they do not replace the condition-specific $S^{QP}$ calculation.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is deliberately small.  The row shows that, in one yeast study and two growth conditions, the nine residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates retain substantial controlled association with GFP-based protein abundance, with $f_3$-stress dominant in both conditions and with almost identical projection direction across SD and YEPD.  It does not establish ribosome-mediated translation, elongation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or any universal biological rule.  It also does not treat modeled tAI, tRNA gene-copy counts, mRNA abundance, or protein abundance as interchangeable measurements.  Each stronger layer would require its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
