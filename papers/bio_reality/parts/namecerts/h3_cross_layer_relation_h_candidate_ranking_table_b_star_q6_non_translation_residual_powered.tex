\subsection{Direction-aware ranking of non-translation residual readouts}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-ranking-b-star-q6-non-translation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record studies a finite cross-layer ranking problem after the modeled translation component has been separated from the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection.  For each organism $o$, the computation begins with the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  These coordinates are residualized against a control block $Z$ with $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every organism in the ranking table.  The control block contains the intercept, CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition coordinates, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The joined row counts for the base coordinate readback are $14410$ in zebrafish, $3739$ in $E. coli$ K-12 MG1655, $12418$ in chicken, $19053$ in human, $4129$ in $M. smegmatis$, and $5815$ in yeast.  In each of these six organisms the complement coordinate $a_\perp$ is nine-dimensional, so the finite carrier is a six-organism table of residual coordinate vectors together with organism-specific candidate readout panels.

\paragraph{Complement to modeled translation.}
The ranking is not applied to total protein abundance directly.  It is applied to the component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection that remains after removing the modeled-tAI direction.  If $P_Q(o)$ is the abundance component explained by residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates and $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}(o)$ is its modeled-tAI complement, the reported finite ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(E.\ coli) &= 0.6629960038578534,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{chicken}) &= 0.9400341806302775,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(M.\ smegmatis) &= 0.8972672018746484,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21173219572706714.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding squared norms are $P_Q=1149.4722114308006$ and $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}=1005.3614101653926$ in zebrafish, $432.8905002445726$ and $287.00467177017873$ in $E. coli$, $739.9012556307761$ and $695.5324705841902$ in chicken, $1370.6490492302532$ and $1262.2237678771598$ in human, $183.85037767342047$ and $164.9629139386273$ in $M. smegmatis$, and $882.6526208776509$ and $186.88597748267557$ in yeast.  The large values in zebrafish, chicken, human, and $M. smegmatis$ mean that most of the abundance-aligned $B^*_{Q6}$ energy is outside the modeled-tAI direction; the yeast value means that modeled tAI absorbs a much larger part of the same projection.

\paragraph{Ranking rule.}
For every available candidate readout $H$, the table reports an absorption magnitude $A_H$, a coordinate-support alignment $C_H$, their signed product $A_H C_H$, and a regularized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Score}(H) = A_H C_H - 0.01\log_2(1+n_H),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_H$ is the number of readout columns.  Thus one-column readouts have $DL_H=1.0$, whereas the localization panel has $DL_H=3.700439718141092$ because it carries $12$ columns.  This rule is descriptive: it ranks candidate explanations for the modeled-translation complement; it is not a causal model and it is not a biological pathway assertion.

In yeast, the codon-residual to mRNA-half-life statistic has $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, cosine $0.8040935468384264$, dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $n=5085$, and permutation right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$. These finite rows show that mRNA-related readouts are real candidate absorbers in some organisms, but the signs, dominant coordinates, and magnitudes differ across taxa.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
The table is a direction-aware ranking of candidate readouts for the modeled-tAI complement of a $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component.  Its module is cross-layer relation, and its pass status means that the complements and every computable $H$ ranking were produced with finite numerical values.  It does not say that any selected $H$ is a cause, a pathway, a ribosome-mediated mechanism, a folding constraint, a physical-admissibility condition, a molecular function, or a universal biological rule.  Measured translation efficiency in zebrafish, complex membership in chicken and human, and mRNA in yeast are organism-specific statistical readbacks under the stated controls.  Empty or negative cases, including $E. coli$ and $M. smegmatis$, remain part of the result: they say that the candidate table does not supply a stable shared non-translation absorber across the six-organism panel.
