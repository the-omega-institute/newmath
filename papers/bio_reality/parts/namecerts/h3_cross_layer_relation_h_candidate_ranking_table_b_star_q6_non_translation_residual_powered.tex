\subsection{Direction-aware ranking of non-translation residual readouts}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-ranking-b-star-q6-non-translation-residual}

\paragraph{Object of the finite computation.}
This chapter records a direction-aware ranking experiment for the residual part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that is not removed by the modeled translation surface.  The coordinate basis is the nine-dimensional residual family
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  For each organism, the computation first forms the abundance component $P_Q$ explained by the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, removes the component explained by modeled tAI, and reads the remaining vector $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ back against candidate readouts $H$.  The tested candidates include measured translation efficiency, modeled tRNA/tAI readout, mRNA abundance, turnover, protein complex membership, domain count, transmembrane count, PTM density, and localization where rows are available.

\paragraph{Controls and fitted space.}
All organism-level joins use the same covariate scheme: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, giving $24$ control columns with rank $23$ in each reported organism.  The joined row counts for the base residual space are $n=14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $n=19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $n=5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The modeled translation surface used as the subtraction target is the rank-powered modeled-tAI surface with $40$ organisms, $9$ residual coordinates, $9$ readout columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $17$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, and $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$.

The companion complement computation reports the same qualitative module: yeast has the largest modeled-translation-covered share, with $m=0.788267804272933$, whereas the other organisms retain larger non-modeled residual fractions, including $m=0.12537127895073397$ in \emph{D. rerio}, $m=0.3370039961421468$ in \emph{E. coli}, $m=0.05996581936972245$ in chicken, $m=0.07910506443205445$ in human, and $m=0.10273279812535108$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}.  These numbers classify the chapter as a cross-layer residual-ranking record rather than a direct translation-survival claim.

\paragraph{Ranking rule.}
For a candidate readout $H$, the computation reports its explained residual amount $A_H$, its direction agreement $C_H$ with the nine-coordinate residual support, and the product $A_HC_H$.  The displayed score applies the stated regularizer with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{col})$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Score}(H) = A_H C_H - 0.01\,DL_H.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus one-column candidates have $DL_H=1$, while localization, with $12$ columns, has $DL_H=3.700439718141092$.  The raw product is reported separately because a negative score can still arise from a positive but small direction-aligned readout after the regularization term is applied.

PTM density and modeled tRNA/tAI also exceed their Bonferroni gates in zebrafish, but their regularized scores are negative.

\paragraph{Relation to mRNA-stability mediation.}
The yeast mRNA selection is compatible with, but does not prove, a mediation interpretation.  In the separate yeast abundance--half-life decomposition, the direct abundance association is $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, the association after conditioning on mRNA half-life is $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, and the absorbed fraction is $0.09994436477872035$ against a permutation null mean $0.00019674487584783138$, null $95\%$ level $0.0014571003414947088$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  This supports the local reading that mRNA-linked variation accounts for a measurable part of the yeast residual abundance component, while leaving the H-ranking table descriptive and organism-specific.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite table is ready as a bounded cross-layer ranking result.  It says that the modeled-tAI complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component can be decomposed and compared with candidate non-translation readouts, and that the preferred candidate differs by organism: measured TE in zebrafish, complex membership in chicken and human, mRNA in yeast, no selected candidate in \emph{E. coli}, and no computable ranking in \emph{M. smegmatis}.  It does not say that $B^*_{Q6}$ causes translation, creates protein folding, establishes physical admissibility, realizes biological function, identifies a mechanism, or defines a universal biological law.  Those stronger conclusions would require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.
