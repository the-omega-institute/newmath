\subsection{Direction-aware ranking of non-translation residual readouts}
\label{sec:h-candidate-ranking-b-star-q6-non-translation-residual}

\paragraph{Object of the finite computation.}
This record asks which non-translation readout families best account for the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after removing the modeled-tAI direction.  The coordinate side is the same nine-dimensional synonymous residual system used throughout this packet:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each organism $o$, the computation first forms the abundance component $P_Q(o)$ explained by the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, removes the modeled-tAI component, and reads the leftover vector back in the nine-coordinate basis as $a_{\perp}(o)$.  The residual fraction is reported as
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_o &= \frac{\|P_{Q,\perp T_{mod}}(o)\|^2}{\|P_Q(o)\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The six organisms with this complement computation are \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc2 155, and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Each joined design uses 24 control columns with residualized control rank 23, and the organism-level joins contain 14410, 3739, 12418, 19053, 4129, and 5815 rows respectively.

\paragraph{Modeled-tAI complement size.}
The complement is not uniform across organisms.  Yeast is the strongest modeled-tAI absorption case: $\|P_Q\|^2=882.6526208776509$, $\|P_{Q,\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=186.88597748267557$, and $d=0.21173219572706714$.  In contrast, the vertebrate rows keep most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component outside the modeled-tAI direction: human has $\|P_Q\|^2=1370.6490492302532$, $\|P_{Q,\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=1262.2237678771598$, and $d=0.9208949355679455$; chicken has $\|P_Q\|^2=739.9012556307761$, $\|P_{Q,\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=695.5324705841902$, and $d=0.9400341806302775$; zebrafish has $\|P_Q\|^2=1149.4722114308006$, $\|P_{Q,\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=1005.3614101653926$, and $d=0.874628721049266$.  The bacterial rows separate: \emph{E. coli} has $d=0.6629960038578534$, while \emph{M. smegmatis} has $d=0.8972672018746484$.  These values agree with the independently reported complement function to zero displayed difference, and they also match the supplied roadmap references within 0.02 for all six organisms.

\paragraph{Ranking rule.}
For every computable readout family $H$, the table reports a magnitude term $A_H$, a signed coordinate-support term $C_H$, the product $A_H C_H$, a description-length penalty $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{col})$, and the score
$$
\begin{aligned}
Score(H) &= A_H C_H - 0.01\,DL_H.
\end{aligned}
$$
Single-column readouts have $DL_H=1.0$, so their penalty is $0.01$.  Localization has 12 columns, hence $DL_H=3.700439718141092$ and a penalty of approximately $0.03700439718141092$.  The table also reports a degree-of-freedom null and a Bonferroni gate for each readout family; a readout can pass the null gate while still receiving a negative score if the stated penalty exceeds $A_H C_H$.

\paragraph{Organism-level outcomes.}
In zebrafish, the selected readout is measured translation efficiency, with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, $Score=0.001566051846174291$, $n=13140$, null $p95=0.0003297458343745959$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$.  The mRNA row is also positive after penalty, with $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$ and $Score=0.000734588577977589$.  PTM density and measured tRNA-tAI pass their null gates but are penalized below zero.  In \emph{E. coli}, no $H_o^\star$ is selected: mRNA has $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$ and $Score=-0.008470202379021127$, and measured translation efficiency has $A_HC_H=0.0005536661857560242$ and $Score=-0.009446333814243976$; neither exceeds the Bonferroni gate.  In chicken, complex membership is the reported top family, with $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$, $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$, $Score=-0.00575848521855683$, $n=1906$, null $p95=0.0027087118702889454$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0055416578806643324$.  PTM density also exceeds the null gate in chicken, with $A_H=0.023360446890988432$, $C_H=0.10469593979758543$, and $A_HC_H=0.0024457439413436166$, but its penalized score is negative.

\paragraph{Human and yeast contrasts.}
Human also selects complex membership as $H_o^\star$, but the finite row is not a clean positive penalized-score call: $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, $Score=-0.0034339698846599055$, $n=18335$, null $p95=0.0002334419901244703$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0002646171322365322$.  Human mRNA and PTM density likewise exceed their gates, with $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$ and $0.0053651472982577525$, but remain below the penalized zero line.  The measured-TE human row is especially useful as a sign check: it exceeds the Bonferroni gate, but its support coefficient is negative, $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, giving $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$ and $Score=-0.01031302107580362$.  In yeast, mRNA is the selected family with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, $Score=-0.008853737772559931$, $n=5128$, null $p95=0.0004745820831535835$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0013014749238591248$.  Yeast localization exceeds its null gate but pays the 12-column penalty, yielding $A_HC_H=0.004768324079158734$ and $Score=-0.03223607310225219$.  \emph{M. smegmatis} has no computable $H$ ranking in this table, so the complement row is retained without assigning a candidate readout.

\paragraph{Relation to the modeled translation matrix.}
The ranking is downstream of a powered modeled-tAI survival computation, not a replacement for it.  The modeled translation readout table used 40 organisms, nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, nine modeled per-codon tAI readout columns, residualized 24 controls to rank 23, and left 17 residual degrees of freedom.  The reported modeled $S^{QT}$ surface has shape $9\times 9$, rank $9$ in the residualized coordinate block, and $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$.  That result licenses a bounded modeled-readout comparison only: modeled tAI from tRNA gene-copy and wobble weights is not ribosome profiling, measured elongation speed, protein output, folding, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Scientific interpretation.}
The finite rows show that the modeled-tAI complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component is large in several organisms and small in yeast, and that the best-scoring candidate readout is organism-specific.  Zebrafish is the only row in this table with a positive penalized score, selecting measured translation efficiency and also retaining mRNA as a positive-scored competitor.  Human and chicken point first to complex membership under the null gate, yeast points to mRNA, \emph{E. coli} has no selected readout, and \emph{M. smegmatis} has no computable ranking.  Therefore the record supports a direction-aware descriptive ranking of residual readout families, not a single conserved downstream biological channel.

\paragraph{Scope restriction.}
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, causal pathway, or universal biological law follows from this table alone.  The quantities $A_H$, $C_H$, $A_HC_H$, and $Score$ are statistical readbacks of coordinate support under a stated penalty, and $H$ absorbing part of a residual component is not evidence that $H$ is the biological process that generated it.  A measured translation, protein-output, localization, complex-membership, structure, function, or perturbation claim requires its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present chapter is therefore ready only as a finite, organism-indexed ranking of candidate readouts for the modeled-tAI complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component.
