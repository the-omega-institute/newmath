\subsection{Direction-Aware Ranking of the Modeled-Translation Complement}\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-ranking-b-star-q6-non-translation-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record considered here is the direction-aware $H$-candidate ranking attached to the modeled-tAI complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component.  The input coordinate panel is the same nine-dimensional synonymous residual basis
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  For each organism the computation first forms the residual protein-abundance component explained by these nine coordinates after the control family $Z$ is removed.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every reported organism, with rows $n=14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The statistic then removes the modeled-tAI direction and asks which additional readout classes $H$ align with the remaining coordinate support.

\paragraph{Complement sizes.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance projection carried by the nine residual coordinates and $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ its component orthogonal to the modeled-tAI readout.  The complement fraction is reported as
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{mod}} &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2} .
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row values are highly organism-dependent: $d_{\mathrm{mod}}=0.8746287210$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.6629960039$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.9400341806$ for chicken, $0.9208949356$ for human, $0.8972672019$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0.2117321957$ for yeast.  Equivalently, the modeled-tAI-aligned fraction $m=1-d$ is about $0.1254$, $0.3370$, $0.0600$, $0.0791$, $0.1027$, and $0.7883$, respectively.  This list is already a warning against a single global interpretation: the yeast abundance component is mostly absorbed by the modeled-tAI direction, while the vertebrate and mycobacterial rows leave most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection in the residual complement.

\paragraph{Ranking rule.}
For each candidate readout $H$ the computation records an absorption magnitude $A_H$, a signed support alignment $C_H$, the product $A_H C_H$, and a description-length penalty.  The score used for ordering is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda DL_H,\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
DL_H &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Single-column readouts therefore have $DL_H=1$, while the twelve-column localization readout has $DL_H=3.7004397181$.  The raw product $A_HC_H$ is kept visible because the penalized score can demote broad readout families even when their coordinate support is substantial.

\paragraph{Organism-level winners.}
In \emph{Danio rerio}, measured translation efficiency is the top-ranked candidate: $A_H=0.0175942900$, $C_H=0.6573753096$, $A_HC_H=0.0115660518$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.0015660518$ on $13140$ joined rows.  mRNA is second with $A_HC_H=0.0107345886$ and score $0.0007345886$ on the same row count.  Both exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, respectively $0.0007199950$ and $0.0005878304$, and both also exceed their degree-of-freedom null $95\%$ levels, $0.0003297458$ and $0.0002356009$.  PTM density and measured tRNA-tAI also exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, but their penalized scores are negative: $-0.0006084600$ and $-0.0030154432$.

For \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 no candidate survives as a positive ranked readout.  The two computable entries are mRNA with $A_H=0.0017706955$, $C_H=0.8639529831$, score $-0.0084702024$, and measured translation efficiency with $A_H=0.0010982956$, $C_H=0.5041139754$, score $-0.0094463338$, both on $3415$ joined rows.  Neither exceeds its Bonferroni threshold.  This is a negative finite-row outcome, not a failure of the construction: it records that the available $H$ panel does not explain the modeled-tAI complement strongly enough under the declared penalty.

For chicken, complex membership is selected as $H^*_o$ even though its penalized score is still negative: $A_H=0.0074424743$, $C_H=0.5699065405$, $A_HC_H=0.0042415148$, and score $-0.0057584852$ on $1906$ rows.  PTM density has the larger absorption magnitude, $A_H=0.0233604469$, but the support alignment is only $C_H=0.1046959398$, yielding $A_HC_H=0.0024457439$ and score $-0.0075542561$.  Both exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, $0.0055416579$ for complex membership and $0.0048217412$ for PTM density, but the ranking remains weak after the uniform $\lambda=0.01$ cost.

For human, complex membership is again selected as $H^*_o$: $A_H=0.0075272000$, $C_H=0.8723071163$, $A_HC_H=0.0065660301$, and score $-0.0034339699$ on $18335$ rows.  mRNA follows with $A_HC_H=0.0062247772$ and score $-0.0037752228$ on $11554$ rows; PTM density gives $A_HC_H=0.0053651473$ and score $-0.0046348527$.  Several human entries exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, including complex membership, measured translation efficiency, mRNA, PTM density, transmembrane count, and localization, but the signed alignment of measured tRNA-tAI and measured translation efficiency is negative in the score-bearing product for two entries: measured tRNA-tAI has $C_H=-0.3998537725$ and measured TE has $C_H=-0.1081353722$.  Thus the human table separates statistical detectability from direction-aware support.

For yeast, mRNA is selected as $H^*_o$ with $A_H=0.0018976055$, $C_H=0.6040571891$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622$, and score $-0.0088537378$ on $5128$ rows.  Measured tRNA-tAI is next with $A_HC_H=0.0008377718$ and score $-0.0091622282$ on $5815$ rows.  Localization has a much larger raw product, $0.0047683241$, but after the twelve-column cost its score is $-0.0322360731$.  The Bonferroni-surviving yeast entries are mRNA and localization, with thresholds $0.0013014749$ and $0.0062952175$, while measured TE, measured tRNA-tAI, turnover, complex membership, PTM density, transmembrane count, and domain count do not pass their respective Bonferroni gates.

\paragraph{Relation to abundance and mediation rows.}
The ranking table should be read together with the independent abundance and mediation rows.  In the yeast mediation computation, the direct nine-coordinate abundance association is $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140374$, while conditioning on mRNA half-life gives $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.2485179953$.  The absorbed fraction is therefore $0.0275960421$ in absolute $R^2$ and $0.0999443648$ as a mediation fraction, with a right-tail permutation value $0.0009990010$ against a null mean $0.0001967449$ and null $95\%$ level $0.0014571003$.  The three-way join contains $5085$ yeast rows, and the half-life join reports a CDS hit rate of $0.9156038419$.  These numbers support a bounded statistical decomposition, but they do not turn mRNA or translation-efficiency labels into causal intermediates.

The per-coordinate abundance decomposition gives another local check.  On the unified yeast context join, $1258$ rows pass the decomposition filter with $5$ folds and $120$ permutations; eight of the nine coordinates have a reported real-source flag, while $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$ is the one coordinate not listed as passing that gate.  This confirms that the ranking table is not merely a one-coordinate artifact, even though individual organisms select different $H$ labels.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
This chapter records a finite descriptive ranking of readout classes against the modeled-tAI complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection.  It does not establish translation as a biological mechanism, does not infer folding or structural order, does not certify physical admissibility, does not assign molecular function, and does not state a universal biological law.  A measured translation, structure, function, or mechanism statement would require its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present result is narrower: the complement exists with concrete organism-level sizes, the candidate $H$ rows have explicit signed support scores, and the best readout class is organism-specific rather than globally stable.
