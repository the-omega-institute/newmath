Thus the tested primitive is a real abundance-associated residual surface, not a rank-degenerate all-null surface.

\paragraph{Reduction families.}
The residual-primitive test asks whether the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance signal should be treated as irreducible with respect to the listed readout reductions.  The answer is negative for the registered finite problem.  The current certificate status says that the source signal is real and that at least one listed reduction family refutes the original irreducibility hypothesis.  A concrete reduction is already visible in the partial-mediation dictionary calculation on the same yeast context: with $n=1800$, $5$ folds, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $120$ permutations, the best selected readout dictionary covers
$$
\begin{aligned}
C^{*} &= 0.24538211439730545,\\
1-C^{*} &= 0.7546178856026946,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the reported selected family is bounded by measured TE, mRNA stability, and turnover readouts.  Its raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.2745002077400602$; the selection-aware null $95\%$ level is $0.029149007242956115$, while the naive fixed-subset null $95\%$ level is $0.00598374993486675$.  This is enough to reject residual primitiveness, but it is not enough to eliminate the residual.  The correct finite conclusion is partial reducibility: a nontrivial measured-readout dictionary accounts for about one quarter of the abundance-aligned component, and roughly three quarters remain outside that dictionary.

\paragraph{Case classification and non-primitive verdict.}
The modeled-tAI four-case classifier gives a useful local label for the direct-versus-mediated ambiguity. In \emph{E. coli}, $7$ coordinates are classified as case $D$ and $2$ are positive-positive without the mediation-drop condition.  This supports the finite statement that the yeast abundance signal is strongly translation-conditioned under the modeled readout, whereas the broader panel includes direct or unresolved components.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The certificate is therefore a powered rejection of residual primitiveness, not a proof that the remaining signal is exhausted.  It asserts the following finite implication: on the yeast powered join, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance-associated residual surface is real, and at least one measured-readout reduction family explains a nonzero, selection-aware amount of that surface.  It does not assert a causal pathway.  It does not assert translation in the absence of a measured or explicitly bounded translation contact.  It does not assert folding, localization mechanism, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality.  The localization audit illustrates the same caution: yeast localization absorbs $0.011225105608516642$ of the modeled complement on $4656$ joined rows, above its dof-matched null $95\%$ level $0.004958116973149795$, while human and danio have their own join-dependent values.  The human turnover audit is even more restrictive: on $8057$ joined rows, turnover absorption is only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, below its null $95\%$ level $0.0004342285066808464$.  These layer-specific readouts may carry pieces of the residual, but each must be read only at the layer it measures.

\paragraph{Finite conclusion.}
For the standalone BioReality record, the ready statement is: the rank-powered yeast $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual signal is not primitive relative to the tested measured-readout dictionary.  The finite evidence combines a real direct protein-abundance association, positive measured-TE survival, large yeast measured-TE mediation, significant joint TE--stability reduction, a selection-aware partial dictionary reduction, and residual dwell/positional structure that remains only partially explained.  The mathematically correct classification is \emph{partially reducible residual signal}, not irreducible biological mechanism.
