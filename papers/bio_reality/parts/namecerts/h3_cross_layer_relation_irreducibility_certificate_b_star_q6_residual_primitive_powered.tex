\subsection{Irreducibility Test for the Yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ Protein-Abundance Residual}\label{sec:bioq6-irreducibility-residual-primitive}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The record tested here is the Saccharomyces cerevisiae residual-primitive question for the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinate surface.  The source design is the matrix of nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress, residualized against the same sibling control basis used in the yeast translation and protein-abundance analyses.  The target vector is log$_{10}$ protein abundance after the corresponding $Z$ residualization on the unified yeast context join.  The primitive hypothesis would say that the abundance-facing $B^*_{Q6}$ signal cannot be materially reduced to a smaller measured readout family under the stated finite-row protocol.

\paragraph{Finite rows and ranks.}
The irreducibility run used the yeast organism key \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  The available unified context contained $4758$ joined rows, with a base context size reported as $4759$ and an active finite test slice of $1800$ rows.  The control basis had rank $24$.  Five held-out folds were used, with $120$ deterministic row-label permutations.  The reduction penalty and join threshold were fixed at
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{DL} &= 0.01,\\
\rho_{join} &= 0.05,\\
K_Q &= 9,\\
\operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 24,\\
N_{join} &= 1800.
\end{aligned}
$$
The deterministic seed was \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_irreducibility\_certificate\_powered:deterministic}.  These numbers make the test a finite matrix calculation rather than an asymptotic narrative: the certificate is about this controlled yeast join, this row budget, this penalty, this permutation family, and this target vector.

\paragraph{Observed verdict.}
The run passed in the registered sense that the source signal was real and that at least one listed reduction family refuted the original irreducibility hypothesis.  Thus the chapter does not certify that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual is primitive.  It records the opposite boundary: under the tested yeast context, the abundance-facing residual is not fully irreducible with respect to the measured reduction families made available to the protocol.  In logical terms, the result is a rejection of a strong primitive-residual reading, not a promotion to mechanism.

\paragraph{Reduction family.}
The companion partial-mediation certificate on the same yeast context identifies the active reduction family as a single-codon readout dictionary family drawn from measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and turnover.  The best selected family had size at most three and was reported as $\{\text{measured TE},\text{mRNA stability},\text{turnover}\}$.  Its selection-aware coverage was
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_* &= 0.24538211439730545,\\
1-C_* &= 0.7546178856026946.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw best coverage over the candidate family was $0.2745002077400602$, while the selection-aware null $95$th percentile was $0.029149007242956115$ and the naive fixed-subset null $95$th percentile was $0.00598374993486675$.  The same penalty and row geometry were used there: $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $N_{join}=1800$, five folds, $120$ permutations, and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=24$.  These values explain why the irreducibility claim fails in a precise way: a measured low-cardinality readout family absorbs a nontrivial part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual, but it leaves most of the residual unexplained.

\paragraph{Translation and abundance context.}
The yeast direct protein-abundance survival calculation gives a strong protein-omics association before mediation is imposed.  On $5815$ joined yeast CDS/protein rows, all nine coordinates survived the direct $Q\to P$ screen, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
\#\{\text{surviving coordinates}\} &= 9.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same yeast organism also has a measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readout: $5128$ genes with measured TE were joined, with a reported TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  The measured $Q\to T$ survival was
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas} &= 0.13681931343730436,\\
\#\{\text{surviving coordinates}\} &= 9.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition on the same yeast readout class, the three-way join contained $5128$ rows.  The measured mediation fraction was $0.4793069656345167$, with $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$ and $S_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$.  These values are compatible with partial reduction, not with full elimination of the abundance-facing residual.

\paragraph{Joint mediator split.}
The yeast joint TE/stability calculation gives a more detailed readout split.  In the four-way yeast join, $4759$ rows were used, with $5128$ genes carrying TE and $5085$ mRNA half-life records available through the primary mRNA key.  The single and joint residual values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.1439989545940314,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.25453612246767165,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13047582645538838.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding mediation fractions were $0.4697188319693239$ for measured TE alone, $0.0626618595344251$ for mRNA stability alone, and $0.5195182225621592$ for the joint TE-plus-stability conditioning.  The right-tail permutation probability was $0.000999000999000999$.  The overlap diagnostic classified the two mediator contributions as approximately independent within the declared tolerance: the single-mediator sum was $0.532380691503749$ and the joint-minus-single-sum value was $-0.012862468941589822$.  This is the main numerical reason the residual-primitive interpretation is too strong: the protein-abundance association is substantially reduced by measured readouts already present in the finite row table.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
A modeled-tAI four-case screen gives a separate bounded readout on $5815$ yeast protein-abundance rows.  In that screen, yeast had $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ and $R^2_{QT}=0.8213457220900503$, and all nine coordinates were classified into case D.  The case-count vector was
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 0,\\
B &= 0,\\
C &= 0,\\
D &= 9.
\end{aligned}
$$
This modeled readout is not the same as ribosome profiling, but it supports the same qualitative partition: the $Q$ surface has both translation-facing and protein-abundance-facing signal, and the direct abundance signal is not best described as an unconditioned primitive object.

\paragraph{Dwell and positional residue.}
The $f_3$-stress coordinate has an additional yeast dwell readout on a smaller active join.  The dwell mediation run used $1257$ joined rows inside a base size of $1800$, five folds, and $120$ permutations.  The held-out increment from dwell was $0.01856473944057712$, exceeding the fixed readout permutation null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$.  The $f_3$-dwell correlation was $0.1615441450901653$, with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  The positional run localized the leading dwell contribution to the $5'$ window: the weighted $f_3$ ramp increment was $0.0011165640103326878$, the body increment was $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the ramp-minus-body value was $0.001378137149572267$.  The positional verdict was ramp-positive but not fully localized, with sign-flip probabilities $0.0478515625$ for the ramp and $0.05859375$ for the paired ramp-minus-body contrast.  This adds a coordinate-specific reduction pressure without turning the residual into a structural or functional claim.

\paragraph{Nontranslation candidates.}
Two higher-layer candidate checks delimit what remains after the measured translation-facing reductions.  Yeast localization had $4656$ joined rows and $4831$ localization records over $12$ categories, with localization absorption $0.011225105608516642$ against a dof-matched null $95$th percentile of $0.004958116973149795$.  Human turnover, tested on $8057$ joined rows, had $R^2_{Q,Tturn}=0.004615408769189838$ but absorbed only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the modeled complement, below its null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  These two checks point in different directions: coarse localization can carry a small residual component in yeast, while the available human half-life readout does not explain the large human complement.  Neither check repairs the failed primitive-residual reading; they only show that remaining residual mass may require additional layer-specific readouts.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The finite-row conclusion is therefore narrow.  The yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual is real, and a low-cardinality readout family measurably reduces it; hence the strong irreducibility interpretation is not supported by the registered test.  At the same time, the unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ prevents a collapse of the whole residual into measured TE, mRNA stability, and turnover.  The chapter licenses only the following statement: in this finite yeast join, the residual is partially reducible and not primitive in the tested sense.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule.}
No translation mechanism, folding consequence, physical admissibility statement, molecular function, system phenotype, causal direction, or universal biological law follows from this calculation by itself.  Measured TE, modeled tAI, mRNA stability, turnover, localization, and protein abundance are separate readout classes with their own noise and join structure.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate matched contact at that layer and a statistic showing the relevant attenuation or survival under the same sort of controls.  The present result is a controlled finite-row reduction certificate for a yeast abundance residual, not a mechanism certificate.
