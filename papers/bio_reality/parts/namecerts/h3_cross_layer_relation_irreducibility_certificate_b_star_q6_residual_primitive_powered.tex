\paragraph{Finite surface.}

The control basis had rank $24$, the cross-validation split count was $5$, the deterministic permutation count was $120$, the description-length penalty was $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and the join threshold was $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$.  The run was recorded under experiment id $2040d8536a26$ with deterministic seed $\mathrm{sha256{:}b\_star\_q6\_irreducibility\_certificate\_powered{:}deterministic}$.

Its status semantics are the opposite: the current claim was tested, the source signal was real on the finite panel, and at least one listed reduction family refuted the original irreducibility hypothesis.  Thus the appropriate mathematical readback is not a certificate of primitive status but a finite obstruction to primitive status on the registered reduction menu.

\paragraph{Direct protein-omics contrast.}

The yeast value is large among the listed surfaces, but direct protein-omics survival is only a downstream association.  It is not by itself a translation mechanism, a folding statement, or a functional statement.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The finite result supports only the following constrained statement: on the registered yeast panel, the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual abundance signal is real and is partly reducible to measured readout families, including measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, turnover, and dwell-related ribosome-occupancy summaries.  It does not certify that $B^\ast_{Q6}$ is globally primitive.  It also does not establish causation, translation realization as a biological mechanism, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or a universal law.  Such claims would require separate layer-matched measurements and their own finite statistics.  The chapter's positive content is therefore a disciplined negative: the original primitive-residual hypothesis is refuted on the finite powered yeast surface, while a large unexplained fraction remains available for later, separately grounded readouts.
