\subsection{Residual Primitive Test for the Yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ Protein-Abundance Surface}
\label{sec:boreality-q6-residual-primitive-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface should be treated as primitive after the same rows are offered several lower-dimensional or biologically named reductions.  The active matrix is the Saccharomyces cerevisiae unified context join.  The source design is the $9$-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis, residualized against the sibling control basis $Z$ with rank $24$.  The target is log$_{10}$ protein abundance after the same control residualization.  The irreducibility run used $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$ rows from $n_{\mathrm{join,available}}=4758$ usable joined rows, with base panel size $4759$, $5$ folds, $120$ row-label permutations, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$.  Its deterministic seed was recorded as sha256:b\_star\_q6\_irreducibility\_certificate\_powered:deterministic.  The registered status is passed only in the limited sense that the current finite test was executed and that the source signal is real while at least one listed reduction family defeats the stronger primitive-residual reading.

\paragraph{Source signal and direct protein readout.}
The direct protein-omics surface is not empty.  In the measured PAXdb protein-abundance panel, all $9$ coordinates survived the direct $Q \to P$ residual fit in every loaded organism.  For yeast, the PAXdb join used $5815$ CDS rows with join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, residual degrees of freedom $5792$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$.  Other direct residual protein associations were smaller but still positive, including human $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ on $19053$ joined rows with hit rate $0.9779294769799313$, mouse $R^2_{QP}=0.049827984062539195$ on $19395$ rows with hit rate $0.9808334176190958$, Escherichia coli $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ on $3739$ rows with hit rate $0.9978649586335735$, and Bacillus subtilis $R^2_{QP}=0.061906245886660105$ on $4042$ rows with hit rate $0.9975320829220138$.  These numbers justify treating the residual protein surface as a real finite object to decompose, but they do not by themselves identify translation, folding, function, or causation.

\paragraph{Measured translation and mediation rows.}
The measured TE contact gives a separate translation-readout surface on organism-matched rows.  In yeast, $5128$ genes with TE were joined to $5815$ CDS rows, giving ribosome-TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$ and measured $R^2_{QT}=0.13681931343730436$ after the same $Z$ controls.  The measured protein mediation run then used the same yeast TE/protein/codon join and reported $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S^{\mathrm{meas}}_{QT}=0.13681931343730436$, raw measured mediation fraction $0.4793069656345167$, and clipped measured mediation fraction $0.4793069656345167$.  E. coli also had a positive but much smaller measured mediation fraction $0.04091158340950673$ on $3415$ three-way rows, while human was near zero at $0.0027966485036786744$ on $11554$ rows and danio clipped to $0.0$ despite $S^{\mathrm{meas}}_{QT}=0.04034217614654355$ on $13140$ rows.  Thus the yeast surface is not merely a direct protein association; it contains a measurable translation-conditioned component, but the component is organism- and readout-dependent.

\paragraph{Reduction families.}
The primitive reading fails because measured lower-dimensional readouts explain a nontrivial part of the yeast residual protein surface.  The partial mediation certificate tested a single-codon readout dictionary family on the same yeast context, again with $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$.  Its best registered reduction used at most three measured readouts, namely measured TE, mRNA stability, and turnover, and gave selection-aware coverage
$$
\begin{aligned}
C^* &= 0.24538211439730545,\\
1-C^* &= 0.7546178856026946.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw best coverage over the candidate family was $0.2745002077400602$, the naive fixed-subset null $95\%$ value was $0.00598374993486675$, and the selection-aware null $95\%$ value was $0.029149007242956115$.  This is the finite-row reason the chapter records reducibility rather than a primitive certificate: the signal is real, but the measured dictionary absorbs about one quarter of the target surface under a selection-aware null.

\paragraph{Joint mediator decomposition.}
A separate joint TE and mRNA-stability decomposition gives the same conclusion from a different finite matrix.  In yeast, the four-way join used $4759$ rows, with $5128$ genes carrying TE and $5379$ valid mRNA half-life records.  The single and joint residual fits reported $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$.  The resulting TE-only fraction was $0.4697188319693239$, the stability-only fraction was $0.0626618595344251$, and the joint mediation fraction was $0.5195182225621592$, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The overlap diagnostic classified the pair as approximately independent because the joint-minus-single-sum value $-0.012862468941589822$ lay within the fixed descriptive tolerance $0.05$.  E. coli and human also had significant joint decompositions at the same right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$, but with smaller joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$ and $0.16890712099923358$.

\paragraph{Dwell and positional modules.}
The yeast $f_3$ stress component has an additional local dwell readout.  On $1257$ joined rows, with base size $1800$, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, and target $P_{f3}$ equal to the projection of $Z$-residualized log$_{10}$ protein abundance onto $Z$-residualized $f_3$ stress, the base model had held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$ and the base+dwell model had held-out $R^2=0.20412261520502883$.  The held-out increment was $0.01856473944057712$, above the permutation null $95\%$ value $0.0013115362189692937$; the null range ran from $-0.013799622026666476$ to $0.005001350039772926$.  The dwell-alone $R^2$ was $0.034336580953121976$, and the $f_3$--dwell correlation was $0.1615441450901653$ with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  The positional companion classified the leading segment as $5'$ rather than $3'$, with the five-prime ramp passing and the three-prime body not passing.  This is a module-level localization of part of the residual protein signal, not a proof of a protein-folding or functional mechanism.

\paragraph{Four-case classification and structural boundary.}
The modeled-tAI four-case discriminant is consistent with the non-primitive reading.  In yeast, all $9$ coordinates were classified as case D under the modeled translation/protein readout; in E. coli, $7$ coordinates were case D and $2$ were positive-positive without a mediation drop.  The yeast modeled-tAI surface had $R^2_{QT}=0.8213457220900503$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, while E. coli had $R^2_{QT}=0.5416736993691318$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$.  A later structural-order ramp test did not promote this to structure: with primary ramp length $50$, the weighted $f_3$ ramp increment was $0.0011165640103326878$, the body increment was $-0.00026157313923957926$, the paired ramp-minus-body increment was $0.001378137149572267$, and the module conclusion was ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized.  The ramp sign-flip value was $0.0478515625$, while the paired ramp-body localization gate did not pass at $0.05859375$.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded statement: the yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ residual protein-abundance surface is real, rank-powered, and partly reducible to measured translation, mRNA-stability, turnover, and dwell-related readouts.  It is therefore not certified as a primitive residual object in the strong sense.  The strongest local statement is a partial-reduction certificate: direct $Q\to P$ signal survives, measured $Q\to T$ signal exists in yeast, and several measured readouts absorb identifiable fractions of the abundance-projected surface.  No claim of translation mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mediation, evolutionary direction, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from these rows.  Such claims would require their own layer-matched measurements and controls beyond the finite matrices summarized here.
