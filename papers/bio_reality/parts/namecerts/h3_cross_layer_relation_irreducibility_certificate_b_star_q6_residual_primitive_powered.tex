The result is a passed finite-row test of the registered claim, but the pass has a negative logical direction for irreducibility: the source signal is real, and at least one measured reduction family refutes the stronger primitive-residual hypothesis.

The modeled-tAI four-case discriminant classifies yeast as $D=9$ with no positive-positive entry lacking a mediation drop, while \textit{E. coli} has $D=7$ and $2$ positive-positive entries without a mediation drop.  Thus the measured and modeled translation summaries agree that the $Q\to P$ signal is not a single primitive block; it can be partially assigned to translation and RNA-stability readouts, with residual pieces still left over.

\paragraph{Reduction families.}
The explicit reduction certificate is stronger than a qualitative statement.  On the yeast unified join, the partial mediation dictionary uses the same $base\_n=4759$, $n_{join}=1800$, five folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $\rho_{join}=0.05$.  Its selected readout family is \{measured TE, mRNA stability, turnover\}.  The raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.2745002077$, the selection-aware null $95$th percentile is $0.0291490072$, the naive fixed-subset null $95$th percentile is $0.0059837499$, and the accepted coverage is
$$
\begin{aligned}
C^{*} &= 0.2453821144,\\
1-C^{*} &= 0.7546178856.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the numerical reason the irreducibility wording must be weakened: a nontrivial fraction of the source signal is reducible to measured readouts, and the remaining fraction is not licensed as a new mechanism.  The finite certificate says that the residual is only partly explained by the listed families, not that the unexplained part is biologically primitive.

\paragraph{Dwell, position, and structural-order checks.}
The $f_3$-stress component gives an additional finite-row route by which the residual can be localized in readout space.  In yeast, the dwell mediation run uses $n_{join}=1257$, $base\_n=1800$, five folds, $120$ permutations, and $rank(f_3)=1$.  The base $R^2$ is $0.1855578758$, the dwell-alone $R^2$ is $0.0343365810$, the full $R^2$ is $0.2041226152$, and the held-out dwell increment is $0.0185647394$ against a permutation null $95$th percentile of $0.0013115362$; the $f_3$--dwell correlation is $0.1615441451$ with approximate $p=7.8739160572\times 10^{-9}$.  The positional dwell run identifies the $5'$ segment as dominant: the $5'$ window passes, the $3'$ body window does not, and the same $n_{join}=1257$ and $base\_n=1800$ are used.  A separate structural-order positional test is more guarded: at primary ramp length $50$, the weighted $f_3$ ramp increment is $0.0011165640$, the weighted body increment is $-0.0002615731$, and the paired ramp-minus-body increment is $0.0013781371$, with sign-flip probabilities $0.0478515625$ for positive ramp and $0.05859375$ for ramp greater than body.  Its conclusion is ramp-positive but not localized.  Hence these contacts support a positional translation-adjacent readout, not folded structural order.

\paragraph{Higher-layer probes do not promote the claim.}
Localization and turnover were also tested as candidate readouts, and both enforce the same nonprimitive reading.  For localization, the joined rows are $4656$ in yeast, $16008$ in human, and $1984$ in zebrafish, with $12$ localization columns and control rank $23$ in each case.  The absorbed localization fractions are $0.0112251056$ for yeast, $0.0092549133$ for human, and $0.0095088645$ for zebrafish; the corresponding dof-matched null $95$th percentiles are $0.0049581170$, $0.0013758521$, and $0.0113213287$.  The zebrafish localization join is therefore below its own null boundary, while yeast and human show small excess absorption.  For human turnover, the joined abundance--CDS--turnover panel has $8057$ rows, the recomputed complement reference reports $d_{modeled}=0.9208949356$, and the turnover absorption is only $3.57407649\times 10^{-6}$ with null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285$, so it does not exceed the dof-matched null.  These measurements are useful probes of the residual, but neither produces a causal or physical interpretation.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is therefore asymmetric.  The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate family has a real protein-abundance association and a real measured-translation surface in the available matched panels.  However, the irreducibility certificate does not certify irreducibility.  It records that the primitive-residual hypothesis is refuted for the current yeast certificate because at least one measured reduction family explains a selection-aware positive fraction of the source signal.  The proper current statement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{direct signal} &> 0,\\
\text{measured reductions} &> \text{selection-aware null},\\
\text{certified irreducibility} &= \text{not established}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, phenotype, causal path, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter.  Such a claim would require a separate layer-matched empirical contact and a new controlled statistic at that layer.  The present chapter only licenses a finite, row-indexed conclusion about partial reducibility of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual under the listed controls and joins.
