\subsection{A powered residual certificate for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-residual-primitive-certificate}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The certificate concerns a finite yeast row surface in which the source design is the $9$-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate matrix, residualized against the same sibling control block used throughout the abundance and translation tests.  The response is $\log_{10}$ protein abundance after the sibling controls, and the active organism is \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The run used $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$ rows from $n_{\mathrm{join,available}}=4758$ available joined rows, inside a base yeast context with $n_{\mathrm{base}}=4759$.  The control basis has rank $24$.  The held-out protocol used $5$ folds, $120$ deterministic permutations, a description-length penalty $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and a join threshold $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$.  Its deterministic seed was $\mathtt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_irreducibility\_certificate\_powered:deterministic}$.

\paragraph{Certificate predicate.}
Let $Q$ denote the residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ source block, $P$ the residualized abundance target, and $\mathcal R$ the listed family of lower-dimensional measured readout reductions.  The finite certificate is not the assertion that $Q$ is irreducible.  It is the opposite operational test: the source signal is admitted as real on the powered yeast join, and then reducibility is tested by asking whether a member of $\mathcal R$ absorbs a nontrivial part of the source-to-target signal.  In the registered run the status is passed with the semantics that at least one listed reduction family refutes the original primitive-residual hypothesis.  Thus the valid conclusion is a reducibility certificate for the present row surface, not a proof that the residual coordinate block is primitive in biology.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{base}} &= 4759, \\
 n_{\mathrm{join,available}} &= 4758, \\
 n_{\mathrm{join}} &= 1800, \\
 \operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 24, \\
 k_Q &= 9, \\
 \lambda_{\mathrm{DL}} &= 0.01, \\
 \rho_{\mathrm{join}} &= 0.05, \\
 B &= 120.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $B$ is the number of deterministic permutation draws.  The target is a protein-abundance residual, so the certificate lives at the protein-omics association layer unless a separate measured translation contact is explicitly attached.

\paragraph{Residual source and direct abundance survival.}
The same coordinate list was used in the surrounding protein-abundance tests: $K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  On the broader direct protein-omics matrix the yeast entry has $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving against measured PAXdb abundance after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and M-density controls.  This number establishes a strong direct abundance association on yeast, but by itself it is only a direct $Q \to P$ statistic.  It does not identify translation, folding, localization, turnover, molecular function, phenotype, or causality.

It is not a causal mediation effect, and it does not isolate initiation, elongation, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, or pathway activity.

\paragraph{Reduction by a small readout dictionary.}
The strongest local reason to reject primitive irreducibility is the partial-mediation certificate.  On the same yeast context, using $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, the best selection-aware readout dictionary over at most three measured readouts $\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{turnover}\}$ covers
$$
\begin{aligned}
 C^{\ast} &= 0.24538211439730545, \\
 1-C^{\ast} &= 0.7546178856026946, \\
 C_{\mathrm{raw,best}} &= 0.2745002077400602, \\
 N_{95,\mathrm{selection}} &= 0.029149007242956115, \\
 N_{95,\mathrm{fixed}} &= 0.00598374993486675.
\end{aligned}
$$
The covered fraction is far above the selection-aware null boundary, while the unexplained fraction remains large.  Hence the correct finite statement is mixed: the yeast abundance residual is not a pure primitive residue, because a small measured-readout dictionary explains a substantial and selection-aware significant fraction; nevertheless the remaining $0.7546178856026946$ fraction is not eliminated by that dictionary.

\paragraph{Dwell and positional structure inside the residual.}
A separate yeast dwell test isolates the $f3\_stress$ abundance component.  On $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1257$ rows, with $5$ folds and $120$ permutations, the base model has $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, dwell alone has $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, the full model has $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, and the held-out dwell increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{heldout,dwell}} &= 0.01856473944057712, \\
 N_{95} &= 0.0013115362189692937, \\
 p_{\mathrm{approx}} &= 7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}, \\
 \operatorname{corr}(f3,\mathrm{dwell}) &= 0.1615441450901653.
\end{aligned}
$$
The target component has $\lVert P_{f3}\rVert^2=44.20095750389323$ and accounts for $0.1999063916718794$ of the target.  The positional audit reports a dominant $5'$ segment: the $5'$ ramp gate passed, the $3'$ body gate did not pass, and the leading segment by held-out increment was $5prime$.  These rows give a concrete reason why the residual is not featureless: at least one component is partially organized by measured ribosome-occupancy summaries, especially near the $5'$ window.

\paragraph{Joint TE and mRNA-stability decomposition.}
The yeast joint decomposition against measured TE and mRNA stability gives another finite reduction surface.  In the four-way join, $4759$ yeast rows were joined, with a reported codon-abundance join count of $5815$, $5128$ genes with TE, and $5379$ valid mRNA half-life rows.  The single and joint residual abundance values are

\begin{aligned}

 R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.25453612246767165, \\
 R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13047582645538838.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding variance-partition fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 F_T &= 0.4697188319693239, \\

 F_{T,M} &= 0.5195182225621592, \\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,right}} &= 0.000999000999000999.
\end{aligned}
$$
The joint fraction is close to the sum of the single-mediator fractions within the stated descriptive tolerance, with joint-minus-single-sum $-0.012862468941589822$.  This supports the same conclusion as the dictionary certificate: measured TE is a major finite reduction direction for the yeast $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance association, mRNA stability contributes a smaller direction, and a substantial remainder persists.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The powered yeast evidence therefore classifies the residual surface as partially reducible rather than irreducible.  The source signal is real on the finite matrix, but the primitive-residual interpretation fails because measured TE, mRNA stability, turnover-family dictionary entries, and dwell summaries absorb nontrivial portions of the abundance signal.  The chapter-level readback is a finite row certificate of partial reduction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{source\ signal} &> 0, \\
 \mathrm{dictionary\ coverage} &= 0.24538211439730545, \\
 \mathrm{unexplained\ remainder} &= 0.7546178856026946, \\
 \mathrm{measured\ TE\ mediation\ fraction\ in\ yeast} &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The certificate does not license claims about translation as a causal process, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or a universal biological law.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  The only supported statement here is that, on the registered yeast abundance surface, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block is a real direct protein-omics predictor and is partially reducible by measured readout families, with a large quantified remainder still open.
