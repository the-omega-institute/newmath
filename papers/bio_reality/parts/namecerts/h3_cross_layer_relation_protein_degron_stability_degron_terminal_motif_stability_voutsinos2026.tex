\subsection{Translation-Bounded RNA Decay Bridge for Terminal-Motif Stability Rows}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is the finite matched panel for the claim identifier \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_degron\_stability.degron\_terminal\_motif\_stability\_voutsinos2026}.  Its verified terminal-motif table contains $5303$ C-terminal rows and $5303$ proteins, with deterministic execution time recorded as $40.3029$ seconds.  These numbers are used only to delimit the protein-side terminal-motif surface.  They do not by themselves provide an RNA half-life experiment, a ribosome-profiling matrix, a measured decay-factor RBP occupancy table, or a perturbation-and-rescue panel.  The same audit bundle also records two neighboring finite-row surfaces: a human meltome charge-patterning table with $6961$ rows and a reported $70.0$ second runtime bucket, and a ubiquitylation acidic-microenvironment table with $10410$ sites on $2883$ proteins using $2000$ permutations.  Those neighboring facts are useful as scale controls for the BioReality packet: this chapter is not asserting that thermal stability, ubiquitylation occupancy, and C-terminal degron stability share one mechanism.  It is asserting that all three are finite biological readout tables whose row counts can be inspected without promoting an internal coordinate into an unmeasured biological cause.

\paragraph{Coordinate object.}
The object under test is a finite panel of synonymous RNA or CDS windows carrying $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels.  A row may contain a high-coordinate or low-coordinate window label, an amino-acid-preserving local sequence context, a same-row translation readout when such a readout is available, an RNA half-life value, a matched mRNA abundance control, and prespecified occupancy or control columns for decay-factor RNA-binding proteins when those columns are externally attached.  The intended readback is bounded: after amino-acid composition, local composition, transcript abundance, isoform state, and translation-readout controls are present, the table asks whether RNA half-life differences track a measured decay-factor RBP route.  The coordinate label is therefore a finite row descriptor, not an observation of translation, protein abundance, folding, cellular function, or adaptation.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
A positive bridge is permitted only for the following finite pattern.  First, the same row must carry the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ window label, the translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, and RNA half-life.  Second, the decay-factor RBP must be specified before looking at the coordinate contrast.  Third, measured occupancy must be decoupled from coordinate state, so that the bridge is not merely the statement that one coordinate class is enriched for an unmeasured sequence motif.  Fourth, the RNA half-life effect must collapse after target-RBP loss and return after wild-type rescue, while binding-defective rescue or non-target controls fail to restore the effect.  In symbolic form, the admitted row pattern is only the finite implication
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{coordinate contrast} + \text{same-row translation readout} + \text{mRNA control} \\
&\quad +\ \text{measured RBP occupancy} + \text{loss/rescue controls}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{bounded RBP-decay half-life readback}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If any required component is missing, the row remains a coordinate association or an RNA-stability boundary record, not a cross-layer mechanism.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null treats high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows as exchangeable for RNA half-life after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation boundary are controlled.  Under that null, a half-life contrast may be reported only as a finite association unless the RBP bridge passes the occupancy, decoupling, perturbation, rescue, and non-target control requirements.  This is stricter than a terminal-motif or protein-row screen: the $5303$ terminal rows are real finite rows for the degron-side claim, but they do not contain the RNA-decay bridge columns needed to turn the present conjecture into a positive RBP-mediated statement.

\paragraph{Relation to nearby readout surfaces.}
The $6961$-row meltome surface and the $10410$-site ubiquitylation surface show that the BioReality ledger can carry different protein-level readout tables beside the terminal-motif table.  They are not used as substitutes for the missing RNA-side quantities.  Thermal stability does not measure RNA half-life, ubiquitylation occupancy does not measure decay-factor RBP binding, and terminal degron rows do not measure same-row translation efficiency.  Their role in this chapter is comparative: each finite table has its own biological contact, and none authorizes a jump into another layer without the corresponding matched rows.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No claim of translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, causation, adaptation, mechanism, or universal sequence law follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RBP mechanism follows when the coordinate label remains predictive only in the absence of measured occupancy, when occupancy is unchanged, or when occupancy has not been experimentally separated from coordinate state.  No cross-layer bridge follows if target-RBP loss fails to remove the half-life effect, if wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or if binding-defective and non-target controls behave like the wild-type rescue.  The present chapter is therefore a bounded readiness record for a future joined RNA/CDS/RBP panel: the finite terminal-motif evidence is real, but the RNA-decay RBP bridge remains conditional on the missing same-window biological measurements.
