\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA decay and RBP occupancy for the Q6 residual coordinate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This chapter records the BioReality status of the claim
$\mathrm{q6.translation\mbox{-}bounded.rna\mbox{-}decay\mbox{-}rbp\mbox{-}bridge.cross\mbox{-}layer}$ for a finite panel of matched RNA/CDS windows.  The coordinate side is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label assigned to synonymous RNA windows after amino-acid composition and local sequence controls.  The biological side is not a free interpretation of the coordinate: it must be supplied by same-row translation readout, matched RNA half-life, matched mRNA abundance, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or control column.  The available finite run for the linked degron-stability record contains $5303$ C-terminal rows over $5303$ proteins, with run identifier $1a932ef5684e$ and runtime $40.3029$ seconds.  This row count is useful as a finite protein-terminal contact scale, but it is not a same-window RNA half-life and RBP perturbation panel.  Two linked finite cross-layer records provide scale checks only: the human meltome charge-patterning record has $6961$ rows, and the ubiquitination acidic-microenvironment record has $10410$ sites over $2883$ proteins with $2000$ actual permutations.  These numbers certify that the present record is being compared to finite tabular objects, not to an unlimited biological law.

\paragraph{Admissible carrier.}
A row of the required carrier has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_i = (&q_i, w_i, a_i, h_i, m_i, t_i, b_i, \ell_i, c_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $w_i$ is the local RNA/CDS window, $a_i$ records amino-acid and local sequence controls, $h_i$ is the RNA half-life readout, $m_i$ is the matched mRNA abundance control, $t_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $b_i$ is measured occupancy by the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $\ell_i$ records target-RBP loss or matched wild-type rescue state, and $c_i$ records binding-defective or non-target controls.  The intended finite comparison is not merely high-coordinate versus low-coordinate half-life.  It is the stronger question whether the half-life contrast follows measured RBP occupancy after coordinate and occupancy have been experimentally separated, disappears under target-RBP loss, and returns under wild-type rescue but not under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Local statistic.}
The permitted statistic is a bounded finite readback of an RBP-mediated RNA-decay contrast after translation has been represented on the same row.  One convenient schematic target is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6,RBP}
  &= \bigl(E[h_i \mid q_i = \mathrm{high}, b_i = 1, Z_i]
      - E[h_i \mid q_i = \mathrm{low}, b_i = 1, Z_i]\bigr) \\
  &\quad - \bigl(E[h_i \mid q_i = \mathrm{high}, b_i = 0, Z_i]
      - E[h_i \mid q_i = \mathrm{low}, b_i = 0, Z_i]\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
with $Z_i$ containing transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the same-row translation readout.  This expression is only a local finite-row target; the supplied data do not include the occupancy, perturbation, rescue, or binding-defective columns needed to evaluate it here.  In particular, no $R$ lists, $M$ lists, lambda values, $p$-values, module classification, or hit-rate scalars are present for this RNA-decay RBP bridge in the finite-row payload.  The only concrete scalar evidence available for this chapter is row scale and runtime provenance from the linked finite records.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The current record supports a boundary statement rather than a positive mechanism claim.  RNA half-life contacts can say that organism-scoped transcript or gene rows carry curated half-life estimates; matched mRNA abundance can serve as a transcription-layer control; ribosome profiling can supply a translation-efficiency surface when joined to the same CDS rows.  None of those contacts, by name or by existence, supplies measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective controls.  Therefore the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate remains a bounded descriptor, or at most a candidate RNA-stability association, until the missing same-window RBP panel is attached and the declared statistic is computed.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from RNA half-life alone.  A same-row translation readout is a gate for interpreting the RNA half-life association as translation-bounded, but it is not itself proof of RNA decay biology.  Likewise, a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate difference is not an RBP mechanism unless the difference tracks measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, survives the coordinate/occupancy separation test, collapses under target-RBP loss, and is restored by wild-type rescue while failing under binding-defective or non-target controls.  On the present finite-row evidence, the correct BioReality status is a disciplined null boundary: the proposed bridge is well specified, finite, and testable, but not yet empirically closed.
