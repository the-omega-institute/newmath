\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA decay and RBP occupancy near terminal degron windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-terminal-degron-stability}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record treats the terminal-degron stability row as a finite matched-panel problem rather than as an immediate protein-stability mechanism. The available finite computation for the degron-terminal-motif subject contains $5303$ C-terminal windows over $5303$ proteins, with experiment run id $1a932ef5684e$ and a recorded runtime of $40.3029$ seconds. These numbers certify the size of the row set being considered for the terminal-window side of the question; they do not, by themselves, certify RNA decay, RBP occupancy, translation output, or degradation kinetics. The biological carrier required for the present cross-layer claim is stricter: each row must contain a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label, the same local RNA/CDS window, a same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, an RNA half-life readout, and prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation columns when such measurements are attached. Without these joined columns, the finite terminal-window computation remains a bounded descriptor of the C-terminal panel.

\paragraph{Question being tested.}
The proposed readout asks whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA windows remain exchangeable for RNA half-life after the declared controls are imposed, or whether the half-life contrast follows a measured decay-factor RBP route. The finite null can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{0}:\quad &\Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}(B^{*}_{Q6}\ \mathrm{high},B^{*}_{Q6}\ \mathrm{low}\mid Z,T,A)=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, transcript abundance, batch, and simpler sequence baselines; $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout; and $A$ denotes the matched mRNA abundance control. A positive cross-layer interpretation would require more than rejection of this exchangeability statement. It would also require that the contrast track a prespecified measured decay-factor RBP occupancy surface under coordinate/occupancy decoupling, collapse after target-RBP loss, and return under wild-type rescue while failing under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Observed finite support.}
The finite facts currently available are sufficient to state the panel scale but not sufficient to promote the row to an RNA-decay mechanism. The terminal-degron row has $n_{\mathrm{cterm}}=5303$ and $n_{\mathrm{proteins}}=5303$. Two neighboring cross-layer records show the scale of other finite biological panels already handled in the same audit style: the charge-patterning thermal-stability row contains $6961$ rows with a recorded deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds, and the ubiquitylation-acidic-microenvironment row contains $10410$ sites over $2883$ proteins with $2000$ permutations. These neighboring row counts are not evidence for the present RNA-decay/RBP claim; they only show that the paper is using finite, row-indexed empirical panels rather than unconstrained biological generalization. For this subject, no finite $R$ list of measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, no perturbation/rescue matrix $M$, no fitted half-life coefficient $\lambda$, no $p$-value, no module class, and no hit-rate scalar are present in the supplied data.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The admissible positive route is narrow. A row may be read as a bounded translation-attached RNA-stability bridge only if the same organism and same window carry all of the following columns: residual coordinate label, local sequence controls, RNA half-life, matched mRNA abundance, translation readout, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. The statistical claim must then be computed on that joined table rather than borrowed from contact names. The half-life effect must move with measured RBP occupancy after coordinate/occupancy decoupling, disappear when the target RBP is lost, and reappear only when wild-type binding is restored. If those conditions are not met, the record has at most a finite RNA-stability association or a coordinate-labeled descriptor.

\paragraph{Local scope restriction.}
The present row does not allow a protein-output claim from RNA half-life alone. It also does not allow a folded-structure claim, physical-admissibility claim, functional claim, phenotype claim, adaptation claim, causal mechanism claim, or universal sequence law. A same-row translation readout is only a boundary condition for considering cross-layer attachment; it is not proof that an RNA decay effect realizes protein abundance or protein degradation. Similarly, matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not an RNA half-life measurement, and a ribosome-profiling route is a translation-efficiency route, not an RBP occupancy or perturbation-rescue assay. The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate is therefore kept as a finite coordinate label until the missing RBP and perturbation columns are supplied.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
For the terminal-degron stability subject, the current finite-row evidence supports a ready negative-scope statement: the $5303$-row C-terminal panel can host the proposed test, but the supplied data do not yet contain the joined RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, translation, mRNA-control, loss, rescue, and binding-control matrix needed for a positive cross-layer RNA-decay/RBP bridge. The scientifically valid current reading is conditional and bounded: if the missing columns are later attached and the prespecified finite statistic passes the occupancy, decoupling, loss, and rescue tests, the row may be promoted to a translation-bounded RNA-stability bridge; otherwise it remains a finite terminal-window coordinate record with no inferred mechanism beyond the observed columns.
