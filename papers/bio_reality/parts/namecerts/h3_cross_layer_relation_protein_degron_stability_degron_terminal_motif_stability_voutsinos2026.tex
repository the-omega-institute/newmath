\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA-decay RBP bridge for terminal degron-coordinate windows}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge-degron-terminal-motif}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.} This chapter records a finite cross-layer test for synonymous $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate windows attached to a terminal-degron stability claim.  The direct audited finite panel for the degron-terminal-motif row contains $5303$ C-terminal records over $5303$ proteins, with deterministic runtime $40.3029$ seconds under experiment run $1a932ef5684e$.  The row is therefore finite in the strict sense used here: each candidate transcript or coding-window comparison must be represented by an explicitly enumerated coordinate label, a same-row readout boundary, and a declared statistic before any biological interpretation is allowed.

\paragraph{Question tested.} The admissible question is not whether the residual coordinate is intrinsically causal.  It is whether a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous RNA window can be read as a bounded RNA-decay bridge after the row also contains same-row translation information, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, and a prespecified measured decay-factor RBP occupancy/control panel.  The intended finite row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_i = (q_i, T_i, A_i, H_i, O_i, P_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, $P_i$ records perturbation and rescue state, and $Z_i$ collects the declared local controls.  The bridge statistic is only meaningful on rows where all of these coordinates are present.

\paragraph{Admissible positive pattern.} A positive bridge requires more than a half-life difference between high-coordinate and low-coordinate windows.  The half-life contrast must track measured RBP occupancy after coordinate and occupancy are experimentally separated; target-RBP loss must collapse the contrast; wild-type rescue must restore it; and binding-defective or non-target controls must fail to restore it.  In finite notation, the evidence must distinguish the two comparisons
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H(q) &= \mathbb{E}[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high}, Z_i] - \mathbb{E}[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low}, Z_i],\\
  \Delta_H(q \mid O,P,T,A) &= \mathbb{E}[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high}, O_i,P_i,T_i,A_i,Z_i] - \mathbb{E}[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low}, O_i,P_i,T_i,A_i,Z_i].
\end{aligned}
$$
The first line is only an RNA-stability association.  The second line can support the proposed bridge only if the occupancy and perturbation fields are measured rather than inferred from the coordinate itself.

\paragraph{Local controls.} The null model treats high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels as exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation boundary are controlled.  The mRNA abundance field is a control denominator, not an RNA-decay mechanism.  The translation field is a gate on interpretation, not proof that a half-life association has reached protein output.  The RBP field is decisive: without measured occupancy and target-specific perturbation/rescue rows, the chapter stops at a bounded RNA half-life association.

\paragraph{Relation to adjacent finite audits.} Two other audited cross-layer panels are useful as scale checks but do not supply evidence for this RNA-decay mechanism.  The ubiquitination-occupancy acidic-microenvironment panel lists $10410$ sites across $2883$ proteins with $2000$ actual permutations under run $0b96a8e4da3c$.  The charge-patterning thermal-stability panel lists $6961$ rows under run $8873440304f9$ with a deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds.  These figures show that the BioReality ledger can carry finite protein-state and stability panels, but neither panel measures same-window RNA half-life, decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or rescue behavior for the present $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA-window question.

\paragraph{Failure modes.} The bridge fails locally if the half-life effect remains after measured RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not decoupled from coordinate state.  It also fails if target-RBP loss does not remove the half-life contrast, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, or if binding-defective and non-target controls behave like wild-type rescue.  In all of these cases the coordinate remains a finite descriptor or, at most, a bounded RNA-stability association.  The finite row cannot be promoted by terminology: review readiness, gate passage, codon-window geometry, or residual-coordinate notation are not biological observations.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.} This chapter does not infer translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality from RNA half-life alone.  Translation requires its own same-row readout and controls.  Protein output requires a protein-level contact.  Folding, localization, physical admissibility, function, and phenotype each require a separate layer-matched measurement.  The only claim allowed here is conditional: if the complete finite panel contains same-row translation, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, then a bounded RNA-decay RBP bridge may be evaluated by the declared finite statistic.  Otherwise the row remains below the cross-layer bridge threshold.
