\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA half-life bridge for degron-window coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge-degron-voutsinos}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose internal coordinate column is a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual label on synonymous coding windows.  The biological target is not the coordinate itself, but a possible bounded relation among four row-level surfaces: the residual coordinate, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation column.  The degron-window audit for this subject contains $5303$ C-terminal windows over $5303$ proteins, with experiment run identifier $1a932ef5684e$ and deterministic runtime $40.3029$ seconds.  These numbers define the available finite row support for the degron-side carrier; they do not, by themselves, supply RBP occupancy, RBP loss, rescue, or binding-defective controls.

\paragraph{Comparison scale.}
The same BioReality ledger also records two other finite cross-layer audits that are useful only as scale anchors.  The charge-patterning thermal-stability audit contains $6961$ rows with run identifier $8873440304f9$ and a deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds, while the ubiquitylation acidic-microenvironment audit contains $10410$ sites across $2883$ proteins with $2000$ permutations and run identifier $0b96a8e4da3c$.  These companion counts show that the degron-window carrier is of comparable finite size, but they are not imported as evidence for RNA decay, RBP binding, translation, folding, or function.  Their role here is restricted to documenting that the present record is a finite-row audit rather than a free-form biological generalization.

\paragraph{Admissible positive statement.}
A positive bridge is allowed only after the finite table contains, in the same organism and at the same local window resolution, all of the following columns: high-versus-low $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, the same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a coordinate/occupancy decoupling design, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  The intended finite readout is a bounded half-life statistic in which the coordinate-associated difference tracks the measured RBP occupancy, disappears when the target RBP is lost, and returns under wild-type rescue but not under binding-defective or non-target control arms.  In symbolic form the permitted claim has the following row-level shape:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q_{\mathrm{high}},q_{\mathrm{low}} \mid Z,T,A) \text{ follows measured } O_{\mathrm{RBP}}, \\
&\Delta H \to 0 \text{ under target-RBP loss}, \\
&\Delta H \text{ returns under wild-type rescue and not under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $H$ denotes RNA half-life, $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout, $A$ denotes matched mRNA abundance, $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ denotes measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, and $Z$ collects the declared controls: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline terms.

\paragraph{Null condition.}
The null is not merely absence of a visible RNA half-life difference.  The required null says that, after the listed controls and the translation-readout boundary are attached, high- and low-coordinate synonymous windows are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life unless the measured RBP bridge supplies the missing direction.  Thus a coordinate-associated half-life contrast without measured occupancy is only an RNA-stability association; a contrast with occupancy but without coordinate/occupancy decoupling is only a confounded occupancy association; and a contrast that fails target-RBP loss or wild-type rescue is not an RBP-mediated bridge.  The available degron audit has the finite carrier count, but the supplied memory does not contain the occupancy and perturbation rows needed to reject this null for the RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
This chapter does not infer translation, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence law from RNA half-life.  The same-row translation readout is a gate on the interpretation of the RNA half-life panel, not proof of translation realization or protein production.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control for transcript-level abundance, not RNA half-life evidence by itself.  Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency is a translation-layer readout, not RBP occupancy, RNA half-life, target-RBP loss, or rescue evidence.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate is kept as a finite coordinate label; it is not curated biological evidence and cannot select the RBP without a prespecified measured occupancy and perturbation panel.

\paragraph{Present verdict.}
The record is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because it gives a concrete finite degron-window carrier, declares the exact same-row contacts required for an RNA half-life bridge, and states the local failure modes that prevent promotion.  The current positive claim remains blocked at the mechanism step: $5303$ rows are enough to define the finite degron-side window universe, but not enough to establish an RBP-mediated RNA-decay bridge without measured decay-factor occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls on the same joined panel.
