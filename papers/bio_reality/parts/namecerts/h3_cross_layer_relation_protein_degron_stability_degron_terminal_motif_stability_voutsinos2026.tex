\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA half-life and decay-factor occupancy}
\label{sec:translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels, same-row translation readout status, RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and, only when available as a separately measured attachment, decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy and perturbation columns. The operative biological object is not a protein-degron assertion by itself. It is a bounded question about whether synonymous residual-coordinate windows can be connected to RNA half-life only after translation readout and transcript-abundance controls have been explicitly joined on the same row. The local finite audit for the associated C-terminal degron-stability surface contains $5303$ C-terminal rows over $5303$ proteins, with experiment run id $1a932ef5684e$ and runtime $40.3029$ seconds. That count is used here only as a row-scale anchor for the surrounding protein-degron record; it is not itself an RNA-decay, RBP-occupancy, or perturbation measurement.

\paragraph{Readout variables.}
For a row $i$, write $q_i$ for the residual coordinate label, $T_i$ for the attached translation readout when present, $H_i$ for RNA half-life, $A_i$ for matched mRNA abundance, and $O_i$ for measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP. A positive bridge is permitted only for a joined table in which the same row carries all required biological measurements and control fields. The intended finite statistic has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A)
  &= H(q=\mathrm{high},O=1 \mid Z,T,A)
   - H(q=\mathrm{low},O=0 \mid Z,T,A),\\
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{RBP\ loss})
  &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{WT\ rescue})
  &\text{ returns while binding-defective or non-target controls do not.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ denotes the declared adjustment set: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, translation readout, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline controls. The displayed formula is a scope definition for the finite table, not evidence that the required occupancy and rescue rows are already present.

\paragraph{Positive condition.}
The coordinate may be treated as a bounded cross-layer relation only under four simultaneous requirements. First, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows must be compared after amino-acid composition and local sequence controls. Second, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy must be present and experimentally separable from coordinate state, so that coordinate labels are not merely acting as a proxy for an unmeasured motif or binding surface. Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the half-life contrast, and wild-type rescue must restore it while binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it. Fourth, the same-row translation readout boundary must be attached before any downstream interpretation is attempted. Without these joins, the finite observation remains an RNA half-life association or a coordinate descriptor, not a mechanism and not a protein-output result.

\paragraph{Negative boundary.}
RNA half-life alone does not establish translation, protein abundance, folded structure, protein function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general sequence law. A ribosome-profiling contact can supply translation-efficiency information only when its rows are actually joined to the same CDS or transcript rows and paired with matched mRNA abundance. A matched mRNA-abundance column is a control denominator or covariate, not an RNA half-life mechanism. A half-life table is a stability readout, not measured RBP occupancy. The current memory for this subject explicitly lacks same-organism, same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and a computed finite RBP-decay half-life statistic. Therefore the chapter records a sharply bounded testable bridge, not a completed positive mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The safe reading is conditional. If a finite panel joins $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, then the declared statistic may test whether the half-life difference follows the RBP bridge rather than the coordinate label alone. If any of those fields are absent, unchanged, or not separable from coordinate state, the result must stay at bounded RNA-stability scope. In particular, no claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, RNA-decay mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from the available half-life boundary by itself.
