\subsection{Cross-dataset axis-transfer audit for synonymous-window perturbation}\label{sec:biorReality-cross-dataset-axis-transfer-synonymous-window}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record tested here is a finite audit surface whose rows are synonymous perturbation panels and whose columns are the controlled B$^{*}_{Q6}$ window axes available for transfer.  A row is admissible only when the perturbation table supplies a synonymous edit or synonymous-window contrast, the response variable is measured in the same experimental record, and the regression design keeps amino-acid identity, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the M-density baseline as controls rather than as promoted coordinates.  The meta experiment itself ran with $B=200$ resamples and took $974.407$ seconds.  Those two scalars certify that the registered meta job executed, but they do not by themselves report the cross-dataset transfer coefficients, the per-axis transfer ranks, or the null distribution needed for a positive axis-transfer call.

\paragraph{Rows entering the audit.}
The finite perturbation ledger contains heterogeneous rows rather than a single biological system.  In the yeast synonymous-mutant row, $20$ genes and $1783$ synonymous mutants were joined; the coding-sequence map hit $21$ records, the real codon-stability coefficient did not absorb the residual signal, and the real-versus-proxy coefficient correlation was $0.39717918221186976$.  In the Escherichia coli mRFP row, $1459$ variants were joined, the control model had $R^2=0.12648386762689323$, the full model had $R^2=0.1798829276988051$, and the observed increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta DL &= 19.089327473282296\ \text{bits},\\
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= p_{\mathrm{within}}=0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same mRFP audit records that real CAI did not absorb the residual effect, that the real CAI route was robust, and that the real-versus-proxy CAI correlation was $0.17884920449487354$.  The separate five-prime boundary audit on the same variant count did not support a downstream-window rescue: step one had $R^2=0.06748192482378434$, step two had $R^2=0.6060702157093043$, step three had $R^2=0.6049438075310105$, and the downstream B$^{*}$ increment was negative, with $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta LL=-2.082967365015851$, block $p=0.1691542288557214$, and sign stability false.

\paragraph{Additional perturbation rows.}
The GFP row contains $3556$ variants split as $1124$ in region 1 and $2432$ in region 2, with a protein-on-out-of-frame mRNA slope of $0.016523671594873352$.  The human prime-editor row contains $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous records in the primary table, and $24000$ synonymous records in the compact table; the held-out design used gene-block folds and capped the analysis at $3000$ records per cell line.  The endogenous Escherichia coli fitness row contains $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits.  Each of these rows is useful as a test surface for transfer, but the present meta record does not expose a row-by-row transfer matrix saying which B$^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate carried over, which direction was retained, or which null family was defeated.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance controls.}
The broader B$^{*}_{Q6}$ mediation record gives a concrete finite comparison class for what a successful mediated readback would look like.  Across $12$ organisms, the protein-abundance mediation experiment joined $4042$ Bacillus subtilis proteins, $11661$ Caenorhabditis elegans proteins, $14410$ Danio rerio proteins, $13295$ Drosophila melanogaster proteins, $3739$ Escherichia coli proteins, $12418$ Gallus gallus proteins, $19053$ human proteins, $19395$ mouse proteins, $4129$ Mycobacterium smegmatis proteins, $13322$ rat proteins, $5815$ yeast proteins, and $1656$ Sulfolobus solfataricus proteins.  The mediated fractions ranged from $0.01850668564589326$ in Sulfolobus solfataricus to $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, with representative values $0.612922252793667$ in Escherichia coli, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse.  These numbers are not a substitute for the synonymous-perturbation transfer matrix; they only show that a controlled $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition can be finite, ranked, and organism-indexed when the required joined rows exist.

\paragraph{Coordinate identity.}
The structural-order axis audit computed the nine coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  In that independent audit, exactly one coordinate passed the fold-level sign-flip gate: $f3\_stress$, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip right-tail probability $0.03125$.  Other coordinates had weaker or negative held-out increments, for example $Arg\_AGR$ at $0.0020139270616115154$ with $p=0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ at $0.0013067592718559024$ with $p=0.0693359375$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ at $-0.00045340330387383447$ with $p=0.529296875$.  This coordinate identity is relevant because a cross-dataset transfer claim must say whether the same axis, a signed subspace, or only a generic residual norm transfers across perturbation panels.

\paragraph{Boundary of the conclusion.}
The available finite rows support a bounded audit statement: several perturbation datasets contain enough rows to test whether a B$^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous-window axis improves held-out response prediction beyond simpler baselines, and at least one individual perturbation row shows a strong positive controlled increment.  The specific meta record, however, reports only $B=200$ and runtime $974.407$ seconds.  It does not disclose the cross-dataset transfer ranks, the per-axis loadings, the transfer loss, the lambda or shrinkage settings, the row-normalized hit rates, or the null quantiles that would let this chapter assert a positive cross-panel axis-transfer theorem.  Therefore the present chapter must stop at a data-audit verdict: the transfer surface is well motivated and has concrete component rows, but the registered meta output is too thin for a positive transfer conclusion.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No statement in this section turns a synonymous residual coordinate into observed translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not treated as protein realization unless the same row first supplies a surviving controlled $Q_i\to T_j$ readout and then a downstream matched abundance readout.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights remain bounded codon-adaptation annotations; ribosome-profiling, matched mRNA abundance, proteomics, localization, stability, and functional assays each require their own joined contact rows before claims at those layers become admissible.  The exact genetic-code falsification checks from the lower-level coordinate audit had $40$ successes in $40$ independent-replication trials and $40$ successes in $40$ boundary-probe trials, each with one-sided binomial upper-tail probability $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$, plus $0$ unexpected successes in $20$ negative-control rows and $0$ unexpected successes in $20$ ablation rows.  Those numbers keep the codon-coordinate basis grounded, but they do not fill the missing cross-dataset transfer matrix for this perturbation meta claim.
