\subsection{Cross-dataset transfer of a synonymous-window residual axis}\label{sec:bior-cross-dataset-axis-transfer-synonymous-window}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite question.}
The record tests whether a B$^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-window coordinate can be carried across perturbation panels as the same finite axis rather than as a dataset-specific regression accident.  The admissible rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ only after the codon-window residual $q_i$ is kept distinct from the translation or output readout.  Direct promotion from $q_i$ to a protein, fitness, or abundance interpretation is not allowed at this stage; the row is read as an axis-transfer audit over finite perturbation tables.  The powered audit for this meta-packet used $B=200$ resampling draws and completed in $974.407$ seconds.

\paragraph{Coordinate surface.}
The residual coordinates available to the transfer audit are the nine named B$^*_{Q6}$ axes
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_{AAA},\ Arg\_{AGR},\ Ile\_{AUA},\ Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f_3\text{-stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
They were also the axes used in the broader translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition, where the joined organism panels contained $4042$ Bacillus subtilis proteins, $11661$ Caenorhabditis elegans proteins, $14410$ Danio rerio proteins, $13295$ Drosophila melanogaster proteins, $3739$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 proteins, $12418$ Gallus gallus proteins, $19053$ Homo sapiens proteins, $19395$ Mus musculus proteins, $4129$ Mycobacterium smegmatis proteins, $13322$ Rattus norvegicus proteins, $5815$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins, and $1656$ Sulfolobus solfataricus proteins.  This wider finite support matters because the meta-claim is not a one-organism sign claim; it asks whether a window coordinate remains usable when panels, organisms, and readout types change.

\paragraph{Perturbation panels.}
The finite perturbation evidence is heterogeneous.  The yeast synonymous-mutation panel used $20$ genes and $1783$ synonymous mutants, with $21$ CDS-map hits; its codon-stability control comparison had real-versus-proxy correlation $0.39717918221186976$, and the real control did not absorb the B$^*$ signal.  The human prime-editor panel used $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous edits in the main table, and $24000$ synonymous edits in the compact table, with at most $3000$ variants per cell line and gene-block held-out folds.  The Chen GFP protein-escape table used $3556$ variants, split as $1124$ in region 1 and $2432$ in region 2, and the protein-on-out-of-frame-mRNA slope was $0.016523671594873352$.  The Nieuwkoop E. coli mRFP protein-output panel used $1459$ variants and gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2}&=0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{L3}&=0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2&=0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta DL&=19.089327473282296\ \text{bits},\\
p_{matched}&=p_{within}=0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated five-prime boundary audit on the same variant count found a downstream-window increment $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$ and $\Delta LL=-2.082967365015851$, while the start-window increment was only $1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$ in $R^2$.  The Yang E. coli endogenous-fitness panel contributed $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits.  The distributed E. coli recoding table contributed $47$ AmpR rows, $53$ GmR rows, and $19$ multi-strata $D$ classes; its AmpR slope was $0.0$ with independent $p=1.0$.

\paragraph{Axis-transfer statistic.}
Let $D_a$ denote one perturbation dataset, let $Y_a$ denote its measured response, and let $Z_a$ collect amino-acid sequence or composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, local window position, M-density or codon-baseline controls, and dataset-specific nuisance strata.  The transfer audit asks whether a common residual coordinate block $Q$ improves held-out description of $Y_a$ beyond $Z_a$ in more than one finite panel without relying on a single biological story.  In schematic form, the quantity under audit is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_a &= Score(Y_a\mid Z_a,Q)-Score(Y_a\mid Z_a),\\
\mathcal{T} &= \{a:\Delta_a>0\ \text{under the declared null resampling for }D_a\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The meta-packet is therefore an axis-transfer result only when the rows support a shared residual direction after each dataset has paid its own controls.  It is not a claim that all panels have the same sign, the same molecular mechanism, or the same biological endpoint.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance rows.}
The same B$^*_{Q6}$ coordinate family has a bounded translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition on matched CDS/protein rows.  In that decomposition the mediated fraction $M^{QTP}$ stayed in the unit interval for all twelve organisms.  Examples include $0.6084594553474505$ in Bacillus subtilis, $0.612922252793667$ in E. coli K12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast.  The largest yeast total residual-to-protein association in that table was $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, with direct conditioned value $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$ and indirect difference $0.28320534058057895$.  These numbers are useful as layer-adjacent context, but the present meta-record remains a perturbation-axis transfer audit.  Protein abundance, ribosome occupancy, and translation efficiency are not interchangeable readouts unless the row explicitly contains the relevant contact and the conditioning step is computed.

\paragraph{Consistency checks against nearby layers.}
The structural-order audit identified $f_3$-stress as the only fold-level significant axis: its weighted held-out increment was $0.0031744502294756994$ with sign-flip $p=0.03125$, while the full coordinate block was significant and the other named axes did not pass that exact fold-level gate.  The observable execution decomposition in yeast used $3289$ genes and ended with the finite verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  By contrast, the evolutionary selection-imprint audit used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species, and rejected the strong imprint gate because $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$ was below the null boundary $0.05427157066522958$ and leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$.  The mass-balance audit on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals had $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits; the turnover residual audit on $3290$ genes had $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{matchedbin}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{recoded}=1.0$.  These neighboring rows keep the transfer claim narrow: axis portability in synonymous perturbation panels does not imply selection imprint, mass-balance closure, turnover prediction, or a general protein-state rule.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite evidence supports reporting a ready, bounded cross-dataset axis-transfer record for B$^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-window perturbation analysis.  The strongest directly cited perturbation support is the Nieuwkoop E. coli mRFP result with $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched/within null probabilities $0.004975124378109453$, together with nonidentical finite panels in yeast, human prime editing, GFP escape, endogenous E. coli fitness, and distributed E. coli recoding.  The five-prime boundary audit prevents reading the result as a generic downstream-window effect, and the negative AmpR, mass-balance, turnover, and selection-imprint rows prevent promotion into a universal biological law.  No translation realization, folding result, physical admissibility result, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from this record unless a separate layer-matched biological contact supplies the corresponding rows and the declared controlled statistic is evaluated on that layer.
