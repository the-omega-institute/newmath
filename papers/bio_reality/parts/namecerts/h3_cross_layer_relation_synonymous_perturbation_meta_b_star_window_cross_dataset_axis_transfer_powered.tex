\subsection{Cross-dataset transfer of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-window axis}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-window-cross-dataset-axis-transfer}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record is a finite comparison surface whose rows are synonymous perturbation or protein-output experiments and whose columns are bounded $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates, translation-conditioned controls, and downstream readouts.  The carrier is not a direct map from codon geometry to protein realization.  It is the restricted matrix of tests in which a residual coordinate is allowed to move toward a protein or fitness readout only through an explicitly recorded translation or translation-adjacent measurement, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density baseline, and matched transcript abundance kept as controls.  The target cross-dataset run itself used $B=200$ null draws and completed in $974.407$ seconds.  This chapter therefore treats the cross-dataset object as a transfer audit over finite rows, not as a universal codon law.

\paragraph{Perturbation rows.}
The finite perturbation panel is heterogeneous by design.  The yeast synonymous-mutant row contains $20$ genes, $1783$ synonymous mutants, $21$ coding-sequence map hits, and a real codon-stability-correlation comparison with correlation $0.39717918221186976$ against the proxy; the real control did not absorb the signal and the robustness flag was true.  The human prime-editor row contains $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous observations, and a compact synonymous surface of $24000$ rows, with gene-block held-out folds and no direct gene dummy in the predictive design because held-out genes are unseen at test time.  The Chen GFP row contains $3556$ variants split as $1124$ in one region and $2432$ in the other, with protein-on-out-of-frame mRNA slope $0.016523671594873352$.  The Nieuwkoop mRFP row contains $1459$ variants and gives the clearest positive protein-output row in the panel: the control-only value is $R^2=0.12648386762689323$, the control-plus-$B^{\ast}$ value is $R^2=0.1798829276988051$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.1798829276988051-0.12648386762689323 \\
           &= 0.05339906007191191,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits and both matched and within-null $p=0.004975124378109453$.  The corresponding five-prime boundary audit on the same variant count does not license a start-window story: the downstream $B$ increment has $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta \ell=-2.082967365015851$, null-$95=0.5254770343527547$, block $p=0.1691542288557214$, and the sign-stability flag is false.  The Yang endogenous row contributes $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits.  The matched distributed recoding row separates $47$ AmpR and $53$ GmR observations over $19$ multi-$D$ strata; its AmpR slope is $0.0$ with independent $p=1.0$, so it is carried as a null or nontransfer row, not as support for a shared direction.

\paragraph{Translation-mediated background.}
The perturbation transfer audit is interpreted against the larger translation-mediated protein-abundance surface.  In that surface the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate set has nine residual axes,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress.
\end{aligned}
$$
Twelve organism rows were joined in the protein-abundance mediation matrix: $4042$ Bacillus proteins, $11661$ Caenorhabditis proteins, $14410$ Danio proteins, $13295$ Drosophila proteins, $3739$ Escherichia coli proteins, $12418$ Gallus proteins, $19053$ human proteins, $19395$ mouse proteins, $4129$ Mycobacterium proteins, $13322$ rat proteins, $5815$ yeast proteins, and $1656$ Sulfolobus proteins.  Every listed organism passed the rank-sufficiency diagnostic for the residualized $9$-coordinate design.  The mediated fraction $M^{QTP}$ ranges from $0.01850668564589326$ in Sulfolobus to $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast; selected intermediate values are $0.612922252793667$ in Escherichia coli, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse, and $0.6084594553474505$ in Bacillus.  These numbers justify including a cross-dataset protein-output transfer question, but they do not identify a causal translation mechanism: the translation readout in that matrix is modeled per-protein tAI from tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights, and the protein readout is log PAXdb abundance.

\paragraph{Axis identity.}
The structural-order axis audit gives a useful constraint on transfer interpretation.  The same nine axes were tested, and exactly one coordinate passed the fold-level sign-flip gate: $f3\_stress$.  Its weighted held-out increment was $0.0031744502294756994$, its weighted absolute unit loading was $0.5109586606724208$, and its sign-flip right-tail probability was $0.03125$.  The remaining axes had right-tail probabilities above the same threshold, for example $0.142578125$ for $Arg\_AGR$, $0.0693359375$ for $Ile\_AUA$, $0.2294921875$ for $K\_AAA$, $0.779296875$ for $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $0.24609375$ for $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $0.2158203125$ for $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $0.4873046875$ for $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, and $0.529296875$ for $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$.  The cross-dataset record therefore should be read as axis-transfer evidence only when a row shows a finite improvement under its own nulls, and not as an instruction to impose one biological sign on all organisms or all assay classes.

\paragraph{Rows that bound the interpretation.}
Several linked rows prevent overstatement.  The modeled-tAI survival surface used only $3$ organisms; it found $81$ entries above the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute coupling $3801961.031607896$, but it explicitly remains underpowered for a powered survival claim because $3$ organisms are fewer than the required $40$ matched species.  The yeast structure-mediation row joined $4759$ genes and estimated $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$; the structure-only fraction is only $0.003284564452662019$, with structure-incremental permutation $p=0.1258741258741259$.  The evolutionary imprint row uses $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; its positive imprint gate is false because $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$ falls below the null-$95$ value $0.05427157066522958$ and the leave-clade-out increment is $-0.0021477729853302424$.  The mass-balance row uses $120$ genes and $718$ intervals and is negative for the stronger closure, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=-668.8046070381928$ bits.  The turnover row uses $3290$ genes and is also negative beyond expression, translation efficiency, pLDDT, and N-end controls, with $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=-13.68819707902732$ bits, matched-bin $p=0.8855721393034826$, and recoded $p=1.0$.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite record supports a bounded cross-dataset transfer audit for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-window residuals.  The positive Nieuwkoop mRFP row, the robust yeast codon-stability control row, the large human and Yang perturbation rows, and the twelve-organism mediation matrix show that the axis family is worth carrying across assay surfaces.  The same finite record also gives its own refusal rules: the Chen distributed recoding AmpR surface is null, the five-prime boundary audit is not sign-stable, modeled tAI coupling is not measured ribosome occupancy, and several higher-layer rows are explicitly negative or underpowered.  Thus the chapter claims only controlled finite-row transfer of a residual axis family across perturbation and protein-output datasets.  It does not claim translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant observation and the corresponding controlled statistic survives.
