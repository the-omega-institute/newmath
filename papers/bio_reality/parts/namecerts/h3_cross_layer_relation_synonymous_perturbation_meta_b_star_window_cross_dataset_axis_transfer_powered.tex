\subsection{Cross-dataset transfer audit for synonymous perturbation windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-synonymous-perturbation-meta-axis-transfer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the cross-dataset axis-transfer test as a finite panel of synonymous-perturbation rows.  Each row carries a perturbation design, a measured readout, a window-level $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate surface, and a control bundle.  The intended readback is not a direct claim that a codon residual realizes protein biology.  It is a comparison of whether the same residual window geometry keeps predictive or attenuation value across assays with different organisms, reporters, measurement scales, and edit designs.  The primary meta run used $B=200$ resampling units and completed in $974.407$ seconds, so the chapter records a computed finite audit rather than a proposed route.

\paragraph{Observed perturbation panels.}
The audit is anchored by several finite-row contacts.  In the yeast synonymous-mutation panel, $20$ genes and $1783$ synonymous mutants were joined, with $21$ CDS map hits; the real codon-stability-control comparison was marked robust, and the real-versus-proxy correlation for $\Delta$CSC was $0.39717918221186976$.  In the human prime-editor panel, the joined design contained $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous observations in the main analysis, and $24000$ compact synonymous observations, with $B=200$ and gene-block held-out folds.  In the Chen GFP reporter panel, $3556$ variants were split across $1124$ region-one variants and $2432$ region-two variants, and the protein-on-out-of-frame mRNA slope was $0.016523671594873352$.  In the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP protein-output panel, $1459$ variants gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{controls}} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B_\perp} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= 19.089327473282296,\\
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= p_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same mRFP boundary audit on the $5'$ window contained $1459$ variants and separated the large step-two gain from the residual downstream term: step two had $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ and $\Delta\ell=628.6217154500001$ against step one, while the downstream $B$ term had $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta\ell=-2.082967365015851$, null-$95$ value $0.5254770343527547$, and block $p=0.1691542288557214$.  The Yang endogenous \emph{E. coli} fitness panel contributed $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits.  The distributed recoding panel contributed $47$ ampicillin rows and $53$ gentamicin rows across $19$ multi-$D$ strata, with an ampicillin independent $p$-value of $1.0$ and slope $0.0$.

\paragraph{Axis content.}
The shared coordinate vocabulary is the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate structural-order audit identified $f3\_stress$ as the only coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate: its weighted held-out increment was $0.0031744502294756994$, its unit loading was $0.5109586606724208$, and its sign-flip right-tail probability was $0.03125$.  Other coordinates did not pass that gate: for example $Arg\_AGR$ had increment $0.0020139270616115154$ with $p=0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ had increment $0.0013067592718559024$ with $p=0.0693359375$, and $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$ had increment $-0.0003205780368125179$ with $p=0.779296875$.  This makes the transfer audit axis-specific: it may discuss repeated finite predictive contact for a named coordinate family, but it may not average the whole nine-dimensional surface into a universal biological direction.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated abundance.}
The cross-dataset perturbation audit is also bounded by the larger translation-mediated abundance calculation.  In that calculation, $12$ organisms were joined, including $5815$ yeast proteins, $3739$ \emph{E. coli} proteins, $19053$ human proteins, and $19395$ mouse proteins.  The mediation scalar was positive and in the unit interval for every organism, but it varied strongly: yeast had $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, \emph{E. coli} K12 had $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$, human had $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$, mouse had $M^{QTP}=0.15488799907323988$, and \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} had $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$.  The yeast entry also had total $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, direct conditioned $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect difference $0.28320534058057895$.  Those numbers show that a protein-abundance contact can be represented as a controlled finite mediation surface, but they do not license importing the same magnitude or sign into every perturbation assay.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded meta statement: cross-dataset transfer is testable and has at least one positive protein-output panel, one sharp $5'$ boundary audit, and multiple large joined perturbation surfaces.  The strongest single perturbation contact in the listed rows is the \emph{E. coli} mRFP panel with $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ and matched/within $p=0.004975124378109453$.  The boundary audit prevents reading this as a generic start-window law, because the downstream $B$ term in the same variant count was negative and not block-significant.  The human and endogenous \emph{E. coli} panels provide large-row stress tests, while the yeast and GFP panels provide smaller or reporter-specific contacts.  The correct chapter-level readback is therefore an axis-transfer audit over finite synonymous perturbation surfaces, not a claim that synonymous geometry by itself realizes translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
All higher-layer interpretations remain blocked unless an appropriate same-row contact supplies them.  A residual-to-protein association is not accepted as protein realization unless the residual first survives a controlled translation-readout step.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control for separating transcriptional and translational components, not a translation measurement by itself.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights are bounded readouts of codon adaptation, not observed ribosome occupancy.  Protein abundance is not folding, function, stability, localization, phenotype, causation, or mechanism.  No CUN/UUR sign, organism-specific slope, reporter effect, or perturbation-window result is promoted to a population law without multi-organism controls, simpler-baseline ablation, and a layer-matched contact for the promoted layer.
