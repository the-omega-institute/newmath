\subsection{Cross-Dataset Transfer of the B-Star Window Axis}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-synonymous-perturbation-meta-b-star-window-cross-dataset-axis-transfer-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object of this record is a finite mediation surface whose rows are synonymous perturbation panels and whose columns are the shared $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates, translation-readout controls, and matched protein or fitness readouts available in each panel.  The registered meta-run completed with $B=200$ resampling arms and runtime $974.407$ seconds.  Its role is not to replace the individual assays by a single biological law.  It asks a narrower question: whether the same residual coordinate family can be kept as a transportable axis across datasets after each dataset is read with its own measurement geometry, controls, and null arms.

\paragraph{Panels entering the axis-transfer surface.}
The perturbation-side surface contains several finite panels with distinct biological readouts.  The yeast synonymous-mutation panel used $n=1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ genes, with $21$ CDS-map hits; its real codon-stability coefficient comparison had correlation $0.39717918221186976$, and the real coefficient did not absorb the $B^{\ast}$ signal.  The human prime-editor panel used $n=9000$ synonymous variants, a compact synonymous count of $24000$, $1822$ genes, gene-block held-out folds, and at most $3000$ analysis rows per cell line.  The GFP escape panel used $3556$ variants, split into $1124$ region-one and $2432$ region-two rows, with protein-on-out-of-frame-mRNA slope $0.016523671594873352$.  The E. coli mRFP protein-output panel used $1459$ variants and reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{controls}} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B^{\ast}_{\perp}} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296,
\end{aligned}
$$
with matched-null and within-null probabilities both $0.004975124378109453$.  The same E. coli source also supplied a five-prime boundary audit over $1459$ variants: the downstream window had $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta\ell=-2.082967365015851$, block probability $0.1691542288557214$, null-$95$ value $0.5254770343527547$, and unstable sign.  The Yang endogenous-fitness panel contained $46686$ synonymous edits over $30$ genes.  The distributed recoding panel separated $47$ ampicillin rows and $53$ gentamicin rows over $19$ multi-row $D$ strata; its ampicillin independent probability was $1.0$ and its ampicillin slope was $0.0$.

\paragraph{Axis identity used by the transfer readback.}
The shared coordinate family is the nine-dimensional residual list
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\; Arg\_AGR,\; Ile\_AUA,\; Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\; Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\; Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\; Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\; f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate structural-order axis audit identified $f3\_stress$ as the dominant coordinate: its weighted held-out increment was $0.0031744502294756994$, its weighted absolute unit loading was $0.5109586606724208$, and its sign-flip probability for positive increment was $0.03125$.  The remaining coordinates did not pass that same fold-level gate: for example $Arg\_AGR$ had increment $0.0020139270616115154$ with probability $0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ had increment $0.0013067592718559024$ with probability $0.0693359375$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ had increment $-0.00045340330387383447$ with probability $0.529296875$.  Thus the transfer surface has a concrete leading axis, but only one such axis is licensed by this particular structural-order audit.

\paragraph{Translation and protein-abundance calibration surface.}
The broader cross-layer matrix keeps the perturbation panels separate from translation-mediated protein abundance.  In the twelve-organism $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition, the per-protein readout was modeled tAI and the downstream readout was $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance.  Joined protein counts ranged from $1656$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $19395$ in mouse and $19053$ in human.  The mediation fractions were positive and bounded in every listed organism, including $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in E. coli K12 MG1655, $0.6084594553474505$ in \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.4872268357227724$ in \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  These numbers justify using a mediated statistical readback as a bounded comparison surface, but they do not make the synonymous-perturbation meta-panel a causal translation experiment.

\paragraph{Modeled translation contact.}
The modeled tAI survival contact used three organisms rather than a powered species panel.  It computed all nine residual coordinates, with codon-count space $61$, organism count $3$, coupling threshold $0.01$, and $81$ matrix entries above threshold.  The maximum absolute modeled coupling was $3801961.031607896$.  The local tRNA payload contained all standard amino-acid families in the three organisms: human had $432$ matched FASTA headers out of $432$, yeast had $275$ out of $275$, and E. coli K12 had $87$ matched out of $89$.  The declared restriction is essential: three organisms are not enough for a powered survival claim, and the modeled tAI surface is not ribosome profiling or proteomics validation.

\paragraph{Meta-level readback.}
The axis-transfer claim is therefore read as follows.  Let $D_a$ range over the finite perturbation panels, let $Q_i$ range over the nine residual coordinates, let $T_j$ range over the available translation annotations or controls, and let $Y_a$ be the dataset-specific output, such as protein fluorescence, endogenous fitness, edited-cell fitness, or antibiotic-condition readout.  The meta-run with $B=200$ supplies the finite audit envelope for comparing whether a $Q_i$-indexed window direction remains useful after each $D_a$ has been forced through its own controls and null design.  The strongest concrete perturbation entry in the supplied rows is the E. coli mRFP panel, where the controlled $B^{\ast}_{\perp}$ increment is positive and null-stable.  The five-prime boundary audit supplies the opposite check: a downstream window can fail, with negative $\Delta R^2$ and non-stable sign.  The distributed recoding ampicillin arm also supplies a null-side constraint with slope $0.0$ and probability $1.0$.  Thus the chapter records a finite, mixed-sign transfer surface rather than a universal synonymous-code rule.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No conclusion about translation realization follows merely from the cross-dataset axis transfer.  No conclusion about folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, causation, selection, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the appropriate same-row statistic is computed.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ or $Q_i\to Y_a$ association is not treated as protein realization when the intervening $Q_i\to T_j$ step is absent, null, rank-degenerate, or untested.  Similarly, modeled tAI or stAI weights remain bounded codon-adaptation readbacks, not observed ribosome dynamics.  Matched mRNA abundance is a transcription-layer control for translation-efficiency residualization, not a substitute for translation output.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite rows support a ready but bounded BioReality statement: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates can be audited as a transportable synonymous-perturbation axis across several finite panels, with one structurally identified leading coordinate, positive protein-output evidence in the E. coli mRFP panel, negative boundary evidence in the five-prime audit, and explicit null-side rows in the distributed recoding panel.  The result remains a controlled cross-dataset axis-transfer record.  It is not a claim that synonymous residual geometry by itself realizes translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, or a global biological law.
