\subsection{Cross-dataset axis transfer for synonymous perturbation windows}
\label{sec:biorality-cross-dataset-axis-transfer-synonymous-perturbation}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object considered here is a finite comparison surface whose rows are synonymous perturbation or matched coding-sequence observations and whose columns record whether the same $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window axes remain useful after the obvious local explanations have been kept in the conditioning set.  The coordinates are the nine residual directions
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A row is admitted to the transfer surface only when the measured endpoint belongs to one of the registered perturbation panels or to a matched abundance/readout panel, and the codon-window coordinate is evaluated as an internal explanatory coordinate rather than as a biological observation.  The meta-audit was run with $B=200$ resampling units and completed in $974.407$ seconds under run id $2cd06dd54208$.  This chapter therefore reads the result as a completed cross-panel audit of transfer eligibility, not as a claim that a single molecular mechanism has been measured.

\paragraph{Perturbation panels entering the comparison.}
The finite panels span multiple experimental designs and endpoints.  The yeast synonymous mutation panel contributed $20$ genes and $1783$ synonymous mutants; its real codon-stability coefficient control did not absorb the window signal, with real-versus-proxy correlation $0.39717918221186976$, $B=200$, and run time $289.093$ seconds.  The human prime-editor panel used gene-block folds, $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous observations in the main analysis, and $24000$ synonymous compact rows with a per-cell-line cap of $3000$ rows.  The Chen GFP panel contributed $3556$ variants, split as $1124$ rows in region $1$ and $2432$ rows in region $2$, with a protein-on-out-of-frame-mRNA slope of $0.016523671594873352$.  The Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output panel contributed $1459$ variants and is the clearest positive perturbation-output contact in the list: the controls-only fit had $R^{2}=0.12648386762689323$, the controls-plus-window-coordinate fit had $R^{2}=0.1798829276988051$, the improvement was $\Delta R^{2}=0.05339906007191191$, the description-length gain was $19.089327473282296$ bits, and both matched and within-panel tests gave $p=0.004975124378109453$.  The same Nieuwkoop source also supplied a boundary audit with $1459$ variants in which the downstream-window term did not survive as a stable explanation: $\Delta R^{2}=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta LL=-2.082967365015851$, block $p=0.1691542288557214$, downstream null $95\%$ cutoff $0.5254770343527547$, and sign stability false.  The Yang endogenous fitness panel contributed $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits.  The Chen distributed recoding panel contributed $47$ AmpR rows and $53$ GmR rows, with $19$ multi-row $D$ strata, AmpR slope $0.0$, and independent AmpR $p=1.0$.

\paragraph{Transfer criterion.}
Let $d$ index a dataset, $r$ a row within that dataset, $y_{dr}$ the measured perturbation endpoint, $x_{dr}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}$ the window coordinate vector, and $z_{dr}$ the local control vector containing amino-acid sequence constraints, organism identity where relevant, GC or GC3, coding length or window position, abundance or mRNA controls when available, and the simpler codon-adaptation or codon-count baseline declared by the dataset.  The audit asks whether a direction learned from one finite surface remains informative on another surface after those controls.  The local quantity is the held-out improvement
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{d}(u)
  = R^{2}_{\mathrm{heldout}}(y_{d}\mid z_{d}, x_{d}u)
    - R^{2}_{\mathrm{heldout}}(y_{d}\mid z_{d}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $u$ is a signed axis or a small axis subspace built from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates.  Cross-dataset transfer requires more than one isolated positive $\Delta_{d}(u)$: the same direction must remain meaningful when the training surface and the evaluation surface are separated.  A panel-specific success is therefore weaker than a cross-panel axis.  This distinction is forced by the finite data above: mRFP protein output shows a positive protein-output improvement, while the 5-prime boundary audit on the same variant count reports a negative downstream increment and false sign stability, and the Chen distributed recoding AmpR endpoint reports $p=1.0$ with zero slope.

\paragraph{Cross-layer anchoring.}
The perturbation panels are interpreted against the larger matched-readout context rather than in isolation.  In the twelve-organism modeled translation-to-protein abundance surface, the joint $9$-coordinate $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition was computed on matched CDS and PAXdb abundance rows.  The joined row counts were $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for zebrafish, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for chicken, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The mediated fractions $M^{QTP}$ were positive and inside the unit interval in every organism, ranging from $0.01850668564589326$ in \emph{Sulfolobus} to $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast.  Representative values include $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse, and $0.6084594553474505$ in \emph{B. subtilis}.  These values show that the window axes can be placed inside a controlled $Q\to T\to P$ statistical surface, but they do not turn the perturbation meta-axis into a causal translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Axis identity and negative controls.}
The strongest axis-identity audit did not distribute the structural-order signal uniformly across the nine coordinates.  In the structural-order run, the dominant increment and loading axis was $f3\_stress$, with weighted held-out $\Delta R^{2}=0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip $p=0.03125$.  The remaining axes had larger sign-flip probabilities or weaker increments: for example $Ile\_AUA$ had $p=0.0693359375$ and $\Delta R^{2}=0.0013067592718559024$, $Arg\_AGR$ had $p=0.142578125$ and $\Delta R^{2}=0.0020139270616115154$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ had negative held-out increment $-0.00045340330387383447$.  This matters for transfer: the audit supports an axis-sensitive finite pattern, not a blanket statement that every residual coordinate transfers.

The broader negative controls reinforce the same boundary.  The evolutionary selection-imprint panel used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; its positive selection-imprint gate was false, with $\Delta R^{2}=0.03250515015613468$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^{2}=-0.0021477729853302424$, null $95\%$ maximum $0.05427157066522958$, and $\Delta DL=-258.6010084301837$ bits.  The mass-balance run used $120$ genes and $718$ intervals; its downstream increment was negative, with $R^{2}_{L2}=0.9712443377743347$, $R^{2}_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$, $\Delta R^{2}=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{TEbin}=0.6831683168316832$, and time-shuffle $p=0.9306930693069307$.  The turnover audit used $3290$ genes and $B=200$ and likewise reported no added turnover prediction beyond the controls, with $R^{2}_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^{2}_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^{2}=-0.005461979433706483$, matched-bin $p=0.8855721393034826$, and recoded $p=1.0$.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The finite facts justify a bounded conclusion: cross-dataset axis transfer is supported only as a statistical comparison of synonymous perturbation windows and matched readout panels.  It is not a license to infer translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, selection, phenotype, or universality from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate itself.  Translation claims require a separate translation contact.  In the modeled tAI survival audit, only $3$ organisms were present, while powered survival required at least $40$ matched species; the run nevertheless computed $81$ bounded coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ and a maximum absolute modeled coupling of $3801961.031607896$.  That result is a modeled codon-adaptation contact, not ribosome profiling or proteomics validation.  The phase-one genetic-code contact is also deliberately lower-layer: it produced $40$ successes out of $40$ trials in both the boundary probe and independent replication, with exact one-sided binomial upper-tail probability $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$ in each case, and zero unexpected successes in $20$ negative controls and $20$ critical ablations.  Those numbers certify a finite code-table contact, not any higher biological layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The cross-dataset audit is ready as a chapter-level finite record because the target meta-run completed with $B=200$, and because the surrounding perturbation panels provide enough numerical heterogeneity to state the proper conclusion.  There is a reproducible positive protein-output signal in one mRFP panel, a yeast synonymous-mutation signal that is not absorbed by the real codon-stability coefficient, a large human prime-editor surface, and several explicit null or nonpromotion outcomes.  The correct readback is therefore not a universal law and not a mechanism claim.  It is the narrower assertion that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-window axes define a reusable audit surface across perturbation datasets, with transfer allowed only where held-out improvement, sign stability, and the local control set survive on the target panel.  Where those finite tests fail, the coordinate remains a residual codon-window descriptor rather than a protein-realization, translation-mechanism, structure, function, selection, or phenotype explanation.
