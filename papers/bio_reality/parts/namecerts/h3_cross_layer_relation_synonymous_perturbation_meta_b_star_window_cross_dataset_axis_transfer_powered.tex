\subsection{Cross-dataset transfer of the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ perturbation axis}
\label{subsec:biorality-cross-dataset-axis-transfer}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the perturbation meta-analysis as a finite surface whose rows are synonymous coding perturbations or matched gene rows, whose columns are $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, and whose readouts are dataset-local consequences such as protein output, fitness, expression-associated abundance, or a bounded translation/protein mediation scalar. The object under audit is not a direct map from a codon residual $q_i$ to a protein or phenotype. It is a cross-dataset transfer test: a residual axis is allowed to travel across datasets only when the same coordinate family remains useful after the dataset-specific controls have absorbed amino-acid composition, GC3, coding length, organism identity where applicable, M-density baseline, and the simpler codon or adaptation baselines available in that dataset.

The registered meta run completed with $B=200$ resampling units and runtime $974.407$ seconds. The row support is heterogeneous rather than a single assay. The yeast synonymous mutation panel contains $20$ genes, $1783$ synonymous mutants, and $21$ CDS map hits; its real codon-stability coefficient audit reported $\operatorname{corr}(\Delta\mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{real}},\Delta\mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{proxy}})=0.39717918221186976$, with the real CSC control not absorbing the residual signal. The human prime-editor screen contributes $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous variants, and a compact synonym set of $24000$ rows under gene-block held-out folds. The GFP synonymous protein-escape panel contributes $3556$ variants, split as $1124$ in region 1 and $2432$ in region 2, with out-of-frame mRNA slope $0.016523671594873352$. The \textit{E. coli} mRFP perturbation panel contributes $1459$ variants and gives a controlled protein-output increment $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, with matched and within-null $p$-values both $0.004975124378109453$. The five-prime boundary audit on the same mRFP family keeps the boundary local: the downstream term has $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta \mathrm{LL}=-2.082967365015851$, matched-block $p=0.1691542288557214$, and null95 $0.5254770343527547$, while the start-window term has only $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$.

\paragraph{Coordinate axes and transfer rule.}
The axis list used by the surrounding $B^\ast_{Q6}$ audits is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{Q}=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The transfer test is therefore rank-aware rather than sign-only. A coordinate that improves one dataset is not promoted to a general biological law; it is only counted as transferable when the held-out or null-controlled statistic remains positive after the dataset's own controls. The structural-order audit identifies $f3\_stress$ as the only axis passing the fold-level sign-flip gate, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip $p=0.03125$. The other listed axes have weaker or negative fold-level increments: for example $Arg\_AGR$ gives $0.0020139270616115154$ with $p=0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ gives $0.0013067592718559024$ with $p=0.0693359375$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ gives $-0.00045340330387383447$ with $p=0.529296875$. Thus the meta record may speak about a recurrent residual-axis candidate, especially the $f3\_stress$ direction, but it must not flatten the nine-coordinate surface into a universal synonymous-code effect.

\paragraph{Protein and translation-mediated background.}
The broader matched abundance audit supplies a useful scale for the meta interpretation. In the twelve-organism $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition, every organism has a rank-sufficient residualized design for the joint nine-dimensional $Q$ block and a one-dimensional modeled translation readout $T$. The mediated fractions $M^{QTP}$ range from $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Concrete entries include $0.6084594553474505$ for \textit{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.612922252793667$ for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.4872268357227724$ for \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $0.27206289160119157$ for human, $0.15488799907323988$ for mouse, and $0.17489681798543982$ for rat. These numbers justify using the translation-mediated protein-abundance surface as context for perturbation transfer, but they do not by themselves prove a causal translational mechanism. They are statistical decompositions on matched CDS and protein-abundance rows.

The same caution is forced by negative or bounded neighboring audits. The evolutionary selection-imprint run used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; its positive gate failed, with $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$, null95 $0.05427157066522958$, and description-length change $-258.6010084301837$ bits. The mass-balance run used $120$ genes and $718$ intervals; after adding the residual block, $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$. The turnover audit used $3290$ genes and reported $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-13.68819707902732$ bits, matched-bin $p=0.8855721393034826$, and recoded-null $p=1.0$. These failures are part of the finite evidence, not exceptions to be hidden: they bound the claim to transfer across some perturbation and protein-output surfaces, not across every downstream layer.

\paragraph{Perturbation readback.}
The meta claim is supported as a controlled cross-dataset axis-transfer record when read in the following restricted way. Across the yeast, human, GFP, mRFP, endogenous \textit{E. coli}, and distributed-recoding panels, the same nine-coordinate residual vocabulary is repeatedly tested against finite perturbation rows. The Yang endogenous \textit{E. coli} panel adds $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits. The distributed recoding run separates $47$ AmpR rows and $53$ GmR rows across $19$ multi-strata $D$ groups and reports an independent AmpR $p$-value of $1.0$ with slope $0.0$, so that panel contributes a bounded null rather than a positive transfer hit. The mRFP protein-output panel is the sharpest positive perturbation entry in the supplied facts; the five-prime boundary audit prevents that result from being reinterpreted as a generic start-window or downstream-position law. The GFP and human panels enlarge the finite row support but, in the supplied table, contribute row counts and runtime rather than a displayed transfer statistic. The correct meta readback is therefore a mixed finite ledger: some panels support residual-axis transfer after controls, some panels enforce null boundaries, and the registered meta run records that this ledger was evaluated with $B=200$.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No stronger biological statement follows from this chapter. A positive perturbation or abundance statistic does not establish observed translation unless a translation readout is present on matched rows and survives its controls. It does not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, selection, or universality. A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not read as protein realization unless an admissible $Q_i\to T_j$ step is present and the downstream readout remains interpretable after matched mRNA abundance and the declared baseline controls. Conversely, a null panel does not erase the coordinate system; it only blocks promotion at the tested layer. The chapter's content is the finite, controlled transfer status of the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ perturbation axis across the listed datasets, with explicit negative audits retained as part of the mathematical object.
