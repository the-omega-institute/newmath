\subsection{Cross-dataset axis transfer for synonymous perturbation windows}
\label{sec:biorality-cross-dataset-axis-transfer-synonymous-perturbation}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studied here is a finite cross-dataset transfer surface for the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates evaluated on synonymous perturbation windows.  Its rows are not free biological assertions.  A row is admissible only when it names a residual coordinate, a dataset-specific perturbation readout, the baseline controls used in that dataset, and the decision statistic that compares the residual window against a simpler coding or expression baseline.  The meta-run for this chapter completed with $B=200$ resampling units and runtime $974.407$ seconds.  The audit therefore concerns a finite comparison ledger rather than an unrestricted claim that one codon axis is a universal cause.

\paragraph{Transfer criterion.}
Let $d$ range over the perturbation datasets, let $q$ range over the nine residual axes, and let $Y_d$ be the dataset-specific output, such as fluorescence, protein output, compact fitness, or ampicillin or gentamicin response.  The admissible transfer score is the signed improvement of a residual-window model after the local control surface has already been fitted.  In the datasets that expose likelihood or variance units, this is read as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_d(q) &= \operatorname{score}(Y_d \mid Z_d, q)-\operatorname{score}(Y_d \mid Z_d),\\
\mathcal{T}(q) &= \{d : \Delta_d(q) \text{ is positive under the declared finite null for } d\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_d$ denotes the dataset's own amino-acid, codon-adaptation, gene, window-position, expression, or mRNA-derived controls, according to the finite table supplied by that experiment.  A positive entry is a transfer datum only inside that dataset's measurement layer; it is not promoted to translation, folding, function, mechanism, or organism-level phenotype unless a separate measured contact supplies that layer.

\paragraph{Positive and negative rows.}
The strongest protein-output row in the perturbation set is the \textit{E. coli} mRFP synonymous panel.  It contains $1459$ variants, gives a residual increment $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, improves description length by $19.089327473282296$ bits, and passes both matched and within-design nulls with $p=0.004975124378109453$.  The same row records that real CAI is not absorbing the residual signal and gives a real-CAI versus proxy correlation of $0.17884920449487354$.  By contrast, the five-prime boundary audit on the same variant count is not a positive transfer row: the downstream residual gives $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, the downstream null threshold is $0.5254770343527547$, and the block null gives $p=0.1691542288557214$.  Thus the meta-surface separates a protein-output perturbation row from a nearby boundary row that fails to support the same interpretation.

\paragraph{Other perturbation surfaces.}
The yeast synonymous mutation row has $1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ genes, with $21$ CDS-map hits.  Its codon-stability-control audit reports real CSC robustness, non-absorption by the CSC control, and a real-versus-proxy correlation $0.39717918221186976$.  The Chen GFP escape row contains $3556$ variants, split as $1124$ in region 1 and $2432$ in region 2, and the out-of-frame mRNA control has protein slope $0.016523671594873352$.  The human prime-editor compact surface contains $9000$ synonymous edits, a compact synonymous panel of $24000$ rows, and $1822$ genes, with gene-block folds used because held-out genes are unseen at test time.  The Yang endogenous \textit{E. coli} row contains $46686$ synonymous edits over $30$ genes.  The distributed recoding row has $47$ ampicillin-resistance rows and $53$ gentamicin-resistance rows, uses $19$ multi-strata $D$ classes, and gives an ampicillin slope of $0.0$ with $p=1.0$.  These rows do not collapse to a single shared effect.  They form a typed list of where a residual-window representation is informative, where it is silent, and where the observed endpoint is too far from the translation readout to carry a stronger conclusion.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance mediation.}
A separate protein-abundance mediation surface supplies the bounded interpretation that motivates the meta-axis test.  Across twelve organism rows, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual set has nine coordinates and the controlled mediation fraction remains in $[0,1]$ for every organism.  The mediated fractions include $0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.6165167748739756$ in \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, $0.4872268357227724$ in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  These values justify using translation-conditioned protein-output language only for the finite statistical decomposition in which modeled tAI is explicitly present.  They do not turn every perturbation-row transfer into a ribosome-dynamics or protein-realization claim.

\paragraph{Axis identity and downstream limits.}
The structural-order axis audit identifies $f_3$-stress as the sole coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate, with held-out weighted increment $0.0031744502294756994$, unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip tail probability $0.03125$.  Other coordinates are smaller or fail the same gate: for example $\operatorname{Arg}_{AGR}$ has increment $0.0020139270616115154$ with $p=0.142578125$, and $\operatorname{Ile}_{AUA}$ has increment $0.0013067592718559024$ with $p=0.0693359375$.  Two further audits enforce the upper boundary.  The turnover residual audit is negative after the expression, translation-efficiency, pLDDT, and N-end controls, with $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  The dynamic mass-balance audit likewise decreases fit after adding the tested residual layer, from $R^2=0.9712443377743347$ at $L2$ to $R^2=0.8953999568421264$ at $L3$, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits.  These negative rows are part of the same chapter claim: cross-dataset transfer is real only where the finite perturbation or mediation row supports it, and it stops at the layer where the row becomes null.

\paragraph{Local scope boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a controlled meta-pattern: some synonymous perturbation datasets preserve a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-window signal after their own baselines, while adjacent or higher-layer tests can be null.  The admissible conclusion is axis transfer across a finite list of perturbation and mediation surfaces.  No translation event, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, detailed mechanism, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter alone.  Such a stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled finite statistic.
