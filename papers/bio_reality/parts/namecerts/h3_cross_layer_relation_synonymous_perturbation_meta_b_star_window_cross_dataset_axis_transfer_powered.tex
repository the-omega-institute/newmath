\subsection{Cross-Dataset Axis Transfer for B-Star Synonymous Perturbation Windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-dataset-axis-transfer}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the cross-dataset transfer question as a finite mediation-and-perturbation surface rather than as a direct biological law.  The rows are indexed by synonymous-window residual coordinates $q_i$, translation or expression-side readouts $t_j$, and downstream protein or fitness readouts $p_k$.  The admissible readback is therefore not $q_i \to p_k$ alone, but a controlled comparison of whether the same residual axes that appear in one perturbation panel retain predictive orientation across other panels after ordinary sequence baselines have been held fixed.  The registered meta run used $B=200$ resampling units and completed in $974.407$ seconds.  It is interpreted together with the individual finite panels: yeast synonymous mutants have $20$ genes and $1783$ synonymous mutants; the human prime-editor panel has $1822$ genes, $9000$ synonymous variants, and a compact analysis count of $24000$; the Chen GFP panel has $3556$ variants split as $1124$ region-1 and $2432$ region-2 variants; the Nieuwkoop mRFP panel has $1459$ variants; the Yang endogenous E. coli panel has $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits; and the Chen distributed recoding panel has $47$ AmpR rows and $53$ GmR rows across $19$ multi-strata $D$ bins.

\paragraph{Controlled transfer statistic.}
Let $X_d$ denote the B-star window coordinates in dataset $d$, let $Z_d$ denote the local controls available in that dataset, and let $Y_d$ denote the measured protein-output, abundance, or fitness readout.  The transfer question is whether a residual direction selected on one finite panel improves held-out readout on another panel after the dataset-specific controls are already present.  In schematic form the admissible increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_d(q) &= R^2(Y_d \mid Z_d, q)-R^2(Y_d \mid Z_d),\\
\Delta_{a\to b}(q) &= R^2(Y_b \mid Z_b, q_a)-R^2(Y_b \mid Z_b),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_a$ is a direction selected without using the response rows of dataset $b$.  The meta record is therefore a transfer audit over finite rows, not a claim that a synonymous coordinate has become a molecular mechanism.  The perturbation panels have heterogeneous endpoints and designs, so the common object is the residual axis and its controlled predictive increment, not a pooled phenotype scale.

\paragraph{Positive and negative row evidence.}
The yeast perturbation run reports that real codon-stability coefficients do not absorb the B-star signal, with real-versus-proxy correlation $0.39717918221186976$ and $real\_csc\_robust=true$ over $1783$ mutants.  The Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output run gives a stronger finite protein-output contact: the control model has $R^2=0.12648386762689323$, the full model has $R^2=0.1798829276988051$, so $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ with $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits and matched and within-panel permutation values both $0.004975124378109453$.  By contrast, the five-prime boundary audit on the same $1459$ variants shows that the downstream B-star window is not stabilized as a broad boundary law: the downstream increment is $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta LL=-2.082967365015851$, with block value $p=0.1691542288557214$ and $sign\_stable=false$.  This pair of results is important for the meta claim: axis transfer is allowed only where the held-out finite statistic survives, and a neighboring positional story is not promoted when its own rows fail.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated abundance.}
The larger translation-mediated abundance panel supplies the background rank and mediation discipline.  Across $12$ organisms, the per-protein decomposition uses $9$ B-star Q6 coordinates, one modeled translation readout, and $24$ control columns, with rank diagnostics reporting $rank(X)=9$, $rank(T)=1$, and $rank(X,T)=10$ in every organism-specific residualized design.  The mediated fractions are finite scalars, not causal effects: for example Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect increment $0.28320534058057895$; Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$, $c=0.10767526677985896$, $c'=0.04167869969498872$, and indirect increment $0.06599656708487024$; Homo sapiens has $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$, $c=0.04454565118019448$, $c'=0.03242643251185274$, and indirect increment $0.012119218668341744$.  These rows justify using translation-conditioned language only as a bounded mediation decomposition.  They do not convert the perturbation meta-axis into folding, function, or organismal phenotype.

\paragraph{Modeled translation boundary.}
The modeled tAI survival contact is also finite and bounded.  It used $3$ organisms, computed $9$ synonymous residual coordinates, and found $81$ entries above the coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  The same run explicitly records that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that at least $40$ matched species would be needed for the powered survival request.  Thus the cross-dataset axis-transfer result can use modeled tAI only as an annotated translation-side coordinate.  It cannot be read as observed ribosome occupancy or as a general translation rule.

\paragraph{Verdict and boundary.}
The finite-row verdict is that the meta packet is ready as a controlled axis-transfer record: the registered meta run completed with $B=200$, and the attached perturbation panels provide concrete row counts and mixed positive and negative finite statistics.  The strongest local protein-output support is the Nieuwkoop mRFP increment $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ with $p=0.004975124378109453$, while the five-prime boundary audit prevents promotion to a broad positional rule.  The yeast, human, GFP, Yang, and distributed-recoding panels expand the finite surface but do not erase the heterogeneity of endpoints.  No translation realization, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, mechanism claim, population law, or universality statement follows from these rows by itself.  Such a statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new controlled statistic on the corresponding finite rows.
