\subsection{Direct protein-omics survival of $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-direct-protein-omics-survival}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite CDS-by-organism table in which each row joins a real coding sequence to a measured protein-abundance readout and to the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The response column is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance after joining to real CDS codon counts.  The control block has $24$ columns before rank reduction and contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates.  In every organism listed below the residualized coordinate block has $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ and the control block has $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, so the nine-coordinate survival calculation is not a rank-empty artifact.

\paragraph{Row support and identifier contact.}
The joined protein tables cover $15$ organisms with organism-specific row counts and hit rates: $4042$ rows in \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168 at hit rate $0.9975320829220138$; $11661$ rows in \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans} at $0.8913774652193854$; $14410$ rows in \emph{Danio rerio} at $1.0$; $6974$ rows in \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum} at $0.9469110658520027$; $13295$ rows in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster} at $0.99565640679997$; $3739$ rows in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 at $0.9978649586335735$; $12418$ rows in \emph{Gallus gallus} at $1.0$; $1747$ rows in \emph{Halobacterium salinarum} at $1.0$; $19053$ rows in \emph{Homo sapiens} at $0.9779294769799313$; $19395$ rows in \emph{Mus musculus} at $0.9808334176190958$; $4129$ rows in \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc2 155 at $1.0$; $5006$ rows in \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1 at $0.9944378228049265$; $13322$ rows in \emph{Rattus norvegicus} at $0.8231586752347998$; $5815$ rows in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} at $0.9156038419146592$; and $1656$ rows in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} at $1.0$.  These are protein-abundance contacts, not translation-rate observations.  The finite support is nevertheless large enough for the residual regression to be meaningful: residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ range from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in mouse, and every organism is marked as rank-sufficient for the nine-coordinate residual block.

\paragraph{Survival statistic.}
For each organism $o$, the recorded statistic is the partial protein-omics survival of the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ block against residualized protein abundance:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QP}(o) &:= R^2\bigl(P_o \sim \widetilde Q_o \mid Z_o\bigr),\\
\widetilde Q_o &:= (I-\Pi_{Z_o})Q_o,\\
\widetilde P_o &:= (I-\Pi_{Z_o})P_o.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold for a coordinate-level entry is $10^{-12}$.  The aggregate $R^2_{QP}$ values are all positive: $0.061906245886660105$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.10217906758013379$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $0.04613529807085213$ for zebrafish, $0.08141786718177903$ for \emph{D. discoideum}, $0.0770952887136557$ for fly, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.03980793596647631$ for chicken, $0.019256877907572044$ for \emph{H. salinarum}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.03209009170649934$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.0646471273126996$ for \emph{P. aeruginosa}, $0.02763998603083845$ for rat, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, and $0.06397514251295629$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}.  In each of the $15$ organism tables, all $9$ residual coordinates have positive surviving entries under the stated threshold.  The largest aggregate value is the yeast value $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ on $5815$ joined rows; the smallest is the \emph{H. salinarum} value $R^2_{QP}=0.019256877907572044$ on $1747$ joined rows.

\paragraph{Control-energy check.}
The fitted control surfaces are nontrivial but not degenerately exhaustive.  For example, yeast has explained control energy $882.6526210803958$, residual response energy $2958.1865890925337$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and residual degrees of freedom $5792$.  Human has explained control energy $1370.6490494847067$, residual response energy $30769.5367150478$, and residual degrees of freedom $19030$.  Mouse has explained control energy $2431.9284360407714$, residual response energy $48806.47856410273$, and residual degrees of freedom $19372$.  The degenerate-$R^2$ guard is $0.999999$, and no listed organism is reported as degenerate.  Thus the finite-row reading is a controlled association between synonymous residual coordinates and protein abundance, not a trivial consequence of having more columns than independent rows.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The direct protein-omics result must be read below the mechanism line.  The available protein contact measures cross-sectional PAXdb abundance, while the translation contact in the linked finite record is only a modeled tAI readout on $3$ organisms with modeled tRNA-copy and dos Reis wobble weights.  That modeled-tAI table reports $61$ sense codons per organism, all standard amino-acid families represented, $81$ bounded-coupling entries above threshold $0.01$, and maximum absolute coupling $3801961.031607896$; it also states that $3$ organisms are not enough for powered survival.  Consequently a positive $S^{QP}$ entry is not a measured $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ chain.  It says that the residual codon-topology coordinate retains protein-abundance predictive content after the listed sequence controls; it does not say that a measured translation mediator has absorbed, reduced, or carried that content.

\paragraph{Adjacent mRNA comparison.}
The same nine-coordinate control design also has mRNA half-life contacts in separate finite tables, which sharpen the boundary rather than replacing the missing mediator.  In yeast, the mRNA half-life table has $5085$ joined rows, $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, cosine $0.8040935468384264$ between $S^{QM}$ and $S^{QP}$, dominant mRNA coordinate $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, and permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ over $1000$ permutations.  In \emph{E. coli}, the table has $2720$ joined rows, $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, cosine $-0.7784923375569599$, dominant coordinate $Arg_{AGR}$, and the same permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In human, the table has $16165$ joined rows across $54$ consensus dataset columns, $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, cosine $0.6351183883924848$, dominant mRNA coordinate $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, and permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These comparisons show that the codon residual block can survive against transcript-layer and protein-layer readouts in related finite joins, but they do not supply a translation mediator for the direct protein-omics claim.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
This chapter records a ready finite-row protein-omics association: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block has positive direct $S^{QP}$ survival in all $15$ organism-specific PAXdb joins after length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls, with $9$ surviving coordinates in every organism.  The interpretation is deliberately narrow.  No translation mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, localization, stability, phenotype, causal effect, adaptation claim, or universal biological law follows from this result.  Any such promotion would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic that explicitly joins the same CDS rows to measured translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, and the downstream readout being interpreted.
