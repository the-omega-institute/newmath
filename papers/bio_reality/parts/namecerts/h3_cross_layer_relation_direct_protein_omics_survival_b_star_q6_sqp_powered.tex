\subsection{Direct protein-omics survival for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{subsec:bior_sqp_direct_protein_omics_survival}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record studied here is a finite CDS-by-organism table whose rows join real coding-sequence codon counts to measured PAXdb protein abundance.  Each row carries nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates after the amino-acid quotient,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured protein abundance, joined to real CDS codon counts.  The direct statistic is denoted $S^{QP}$ because it asks whether the residual codon coordinate block $Q$ still predicts a protein-omics readout $P$ after the declared CDS-derived baseline has been removed.  It is not a translation-rate statistic.

\paragraph{Residualization model.}
For each organism, the baseline matrix $Z$ contains 24 control columns: an intercept, CDS length, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism below, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, the residualized $Q$ block has $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, and the rank check is nondegenerate under the threshold $R^2<0.999999$.  The tested quantity is the partial energy recovered by the residualized $Q$ block from the residualized protein-abundance vector:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_{QP} &= \frac{\|\Pi_{\widetilde X}\widetilde y\|^2}{\|\widetilde y\|^2},\\
  \widetilde X &= (I-\Pi_Z)X_Q,\\
  \widetilde y &= (I-\Pi_Z)y_P.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold used by the run is $10^{-12}$, so the reported organism-level positives are far above the numerical floor.

\paragraph{Coordinate survival.}
All 15 organism-level fits report nine surviving coordinates.  Thus the finite-row result is not a single-coordinate anecdote: in each organism the full nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block retains positive protein-omics explanatory energy after the amino-acid, length, GC3, and M-density baseline is removed.  The row counts are large enough that the rank test is not the three-organism saturation failure seen in the earlier modeled-tAI screen.  That earlier screen had only three organisms and explicitly refused powered survival from $n=3$, even though modeled tAI bounded coupling was nonzero with 81 entries above the $0.01$ threshold and maximum absolute coupling $3801961.031607896$.  The present protein-omics table therefore supports a direct protein-abundance association, while the earlier modeled-tAI table remains only a bounded translation-readout contact.

\paragraph{Boundary against translation promotion.}
The direct $S^{QP}$ values do not show that $B^*_{Q6}$ controls translation.  They show that, in the joined PAXdb/CDS rows, residual synonymous codon topology predicts residualized protein abundance.  A translation interpretation would require same-row measured translation readouts $T_j$, matched mRNA controls, and an explicit mediation calculation showing a $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ route.  The present direct protein-omics run itself states that the measured PAXdb abundance is cross-sectional protein abundance, not a translation-rate measurement; that the statistic is not causal; that the run does not use perturbation, rescue, ribosome profiling, or matched transcript controls; and that positive entries are not $M^{QTP}$.  Therefore no claim about translation mechanism, protein folding, localization, post-translational modification, pathway function, physical admissibility, organismal phenotype, or universality follows from this chapter.

\paragraph{RNA half-life context.}
Separate mRNA-stability rows show that the same coordinate family also contacts transcript-layer readouts, but those contacts remain distinct from the protein-abundance result.  In yeast, the Neymotin half-life join has 5085 rows and $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, with $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ on the same join, cosine $\cos(S^{QM},S^{QP})=0.8040935468384264$, dominant $S^{QM}$ coordinate $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, and permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ over 1000 permutations.  In \textit{E. coli}, the Esquerr\'e half-life join has 2720 rows, $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, cosine $-0.7784923375569599$, dominant coordinate $Arg_{AGR}$, and the same 1000-permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In human, the Agarwal--Kelley consensus has 16165 joined rows across 54 dataset columns, $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, cosine $0.6351183883924848$, dominant $S^{QM}$ coordinate $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, and permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These transcript-layer comparisons support the claim that $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals are not exhausted by amino-acid composition and simple CDS covariates, but they still do not turn the direct protein-abundance statistic into a mechanistic translation, decay, folding, or functional claim.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row evidence is sufficient for the narrow statement that $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates survive as direct predictors of measured protein abundance in organism-matched CDS/proteomics joins under the stated baseline controls.  The correct readback is direct DNA-to-protein-omics predictive irreducibility.  The result remains below a translation-mediated protein mechanism until a separate joined matrix supplies measured translation readouts, matched mRNA abundance, and a mediation fit that tests whether conditioning on $T_j$ removes, reduces, or leaves the $q_i\to P_k$ signal.
