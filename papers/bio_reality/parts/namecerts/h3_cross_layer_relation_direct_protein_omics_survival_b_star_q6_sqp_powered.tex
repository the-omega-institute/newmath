\subsection{Direct protein-omics survival for the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals}\label{sec:biorality-q6-direct-protein-omics-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The tested object is a finite CDS-by-organism table in which each row carries real coding-sequence codon counts, a matched PAXdb protein-abundance value, amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, an $M$-density baseline, and nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The nine coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.
The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The central finite question is whether the residualized $Q$ block has positive direct association with a protein-omics column after the baseline covariates have already absorbed length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density support of the nine projected coordinates.

\paragraph{Residualization contract.}
For each organism, let $y$ be the protein abundance column, $Q$ be the nine-column residual-coordinate matrix, and $Z$ be the control matrix containing the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The computation first removes the $Z$ span from both $y$ and $Q$, then measures the remaining explanatory energy of the projected $Q$ block.  The statistic reported here is the direct $Q \to P$ survival score $S^{QP}$, summarized as a partial $R^2_{QP}$ value for each organism:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= \frac{\|\Pi_{\widetilde Q}\widetilde y\|^2}{\|\widetilde y\|^2}, \\
\widetilde y &= (I-\Pi_Z)y, \\
\widetilde Q &= (I-\Pi_Z)Q.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive-entry threshold is $10^{-12}$, so the reported nonzero values are not threshold-edge artifacts.  In every organism, the residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$, the control matrix has rank $23$ despite $24$ listed control columns, and all nine coordinates survive the positive-entry screen.

Thus the finite panel supports a direct, controlled, protein-omics association between $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates and measured protein abundance.

The positive calls are not based on a three-organism rank-saturated fit.  At the protein level, residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ range from $1{,}633$ in $Sulfolobus$ and $1{,}724$ in $Halobacterium$ to $19{,}372$ in mouse and $19{,}030$ in human. Each organism has $\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde Q)=9$, so the nine-coordinate block remains numerically available after baseline control removal.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The same finite memory contains a separate modelled translation-readout record based on GtRNAdb tRNA gene copy and dos Reis wobble weights for three organisms.  That record computed nine synonymous residuals and found $81$ modelled-tAI bounded-coupling entries above the threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}$ and maximum absolute diagonal value both equal to $3{,}801{,}961.031607896$.  It also records that only $3$ organisms were present, and that this is insufficient for powered survival.  Consequently the present protein-omics result is not a measured translation result.  It is a direct $Q \to P$ association after protein-level controls.  It does not by itself establish a $Q \to T \to P$ chain, because a matched measured translation readout $T$ and an explicit mediation fit are not part of this finite protein-abundance panel.

\paragraph{RNA stability comparison.}
Three linked mRNA-stability panels show why the direct protein-omics readout should be kept layer-specific.  In yeast, the same nine-coordinate style gives $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$ for log half-life, $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ on the same join, cosine $0.8040935468384264$ between the $S^{QM}$ and $S^{QP}$ vectors, dominant $S^{QM}$ coordinate $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ with $1000$ permutations.  In $E.\ coli$, the mRNA panel gives $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, cosine $-0.7784923375569599$, dominant coordinate $Arg\_AGR$, and the same permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  In human, the consensus relative half-life panel gives $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, cosine $0.6351183883924848$, dominant $S^{QM}$ coordinate $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $16{,}165$ joined genes, and $54$ dataset columns.  These comparisons support the conservative reading: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block can survive against more than one molecular readout, but the direction and dominant coordinate can change with the readout layer.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row result is ready as a controlled direct protein-omics survival statement.  The evidence says that the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates retain positive association with measured protein abundance in each of fifteen organism-specific CDS-protein panels after amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  The result should be read as DNA-sequence codon-residual predictive irreducibility for a protein-abundance readout.  It must not be read as translation-rate measurement, translation mediation, protein folding, localization, post-translational state, biochemical function, physical admissibility, phenotype, causation, adaptation, or a universal biological law.  Any such stronger statement requires a separate finite contact at the relevant layer, and a translation-mediated protein claim requires same-row measured $T$ columns showing that $Q_i \to T_j$ survives and that conditioning on $T_j$ removes or reduces the direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association.
