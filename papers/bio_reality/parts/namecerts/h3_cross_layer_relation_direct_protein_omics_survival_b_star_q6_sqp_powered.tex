\subsection{Direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual survival against measured protein abundance}\label{subsec:b-star-q6-direct-protein-omics-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The finite table used here has one row for each coding sequence that can be joined to a measured protein-abundance record in the same organism.  Its coordinate block consists of nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates after the amino-acid quotient:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress.
\end{aligned}
$$
These columns are not amino-acid composition columns.  They are residual codon-topology coordinates left after the amino-acid grouping has already been quotiented out.  The response column is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance, joined to real CDS codon counts.  The calculation therefore tests a direct sequence-to-protein-omics association, not a translation-rate measurement.

\paragraph{Observed rows and joins.}
Three organism tables were available.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $3739$ proteins were used after joining CDS rows to abundance rows, with join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$.  In \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $4129$ proteins were used, with join hit rate $1.0$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ proteins were used, with join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$.  Thus the computation is not an organism-level three-row survival test; each organism contributes thousands of per-protein rows, and the residual-coordinate rank is evaluated inside each organism table.

\paragraph{Residualization controls.}
For each organism, the protein-abundance response and the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates were residualized against the baseline covariate block $Z$ containing CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and M-density controls.  The implemented control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organism.  The residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design has rank $9$ in each organism, so the nine-coordinate block remains estimable after baseline projection.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $3716$ for \emph{E. coli}, $4106$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $5792$ for yeast.  The residual response energies are respectively $4020.333671802061$, $5729.194538214237$, and $2958.1865890925337$, while the abundance energy explained by the control block is $432.89050065533684$, $183.85037813566998$, and $882.6526210803958$.  The degeneracy cutoff used for rank sufficiency is $R^2=0.999999$, and all three organism tables pass the powered-rank check.

\paragraph{Direct survival statistic.}
Let $Q$ denote the nine residual codon-coordinate columns, $P$ the measured protein-abundance column, and $Z$ the baseline control block.  The reported statistic is the partial direct protein-omics survival score obtained after projecting both $Q$ and $P$ away from $Z$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QP} &= R^2(P_{\perp Z}; Q_{\perp Z}),\\
Q_{\perp Z} &= (I-\Pi_Z)Q,\\
P_{\perp Z} &= (I-\Pi_Z)P.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive entry is counted only above the declared partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  Under that threshold, all nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates survive in each organism table.  The direct block-level partial values are $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $R^2_{QP}=0.03209009170649934$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ for yeast.  These values support a finite direct association between synonymous residual codon coordinates and measured steady-state protein abundance after the stated sequence-composition controls.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The same data record also reports a separate modeled translation-contact computation using codon usage, GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy information, and dos Reis wobble weights.  That modeled contact computed nine residual coordinates over three organisms and found nonzero bounded coupling above threshold $0.01$, with $81$ entries above threshold and maximum absolute score $3801961.031607896$.  The three modeled organisms were \emph{E. coli} K-12, human, and yeast; their standard amino-acid family coverage was $20$ families each, and their matched FASTA header counts were $87$ of $89$, $432$ of $432$, and $275$ of $275$.  This establishes a modeled codon-adaptation readout route, but the record itself states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered translation survival and that powered survival requires at least $40$ matched species.  Consequently, the direct $S^{QP}$ result above must not be re-described as a measured translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite result is a protein-omics survival statement only: after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and M-density controls, the residual codon-coordinate block retains positive explanatory power for measured PAXdb abundance in three organism-specific per-protein tables.  The strongest reported block value is the yeast value $0.29837624994140827$; the weakest is the \emph{M. smegmatis} value $0.03209009170649934$, still positive above the declared threshold.  The row counts and ranks make the positivity non-vacuous for this direct abundance readout.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule.}
No claim about translation rate, ribosome occupancy, causal mediation, folded structure, physical admissibility, protein localization, post-translational modification, pathway function, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from these numbers alone.  In particular, a positive $S^{QP}$ is not an $M^{QTP}$ mediation matrix.  To promote the association to a translation-mediated protein-output statement, the same CDS rows would need matched measured translation readouts $T_j$, matched mRNA controls, and an explicit fit testing whether $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ survives and whether conditioning on $T_j$ removes or reduces the direct $q_i\to P_k$ signal.  Without that joint table, the present chapter records direct DNA-to-protein-omics predictive irreducibility under the stated finite controls, and nothing stronger.
