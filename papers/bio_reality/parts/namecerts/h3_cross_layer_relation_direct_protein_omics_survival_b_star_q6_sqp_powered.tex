\subsection{Direct B$^\ast_{Q6}$ protein-omics survival with a translation boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-direct-protein-omics-survival-b-star-q6}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite-row carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite CDS-by-organism table in which each coding sequence carries nine synonymous residual coordinates from the B$^\ast_{Q6}$ amino-acid quotient and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The measured response is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  In the finite computations reported here the control column count is $24$, the residualized control rank is $23$ in every organism, and the residualized B$^\ast_{Q6}$ coordinate rank is $9$ in every organism.

\paragraph{Protein-omics survival statistic.}
For each organism, the direct statistic $S^{QP}$ is read as the residual fraction of protein-abundance variation captured by the nine B$^\ast_{Q6}$ coordinates after the stated controls.  Equivalently, the reported scalar is the controlled partial $R^2_{QP}$ for the residualized coordinate matrix against residualized protein abundance, with positivity threshold $10^{-12}$ and degeneracy threshold $0.999999$.  The finite table is non-vacuous by rank: every organism has $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ and positive residual degrees of freedom after controls.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QP}(q,P) &:= R^2\bigl(P_{\perp Z},\,Q_{\perp Z}\bigr),\\
Z&=\{1,\log L,\mathrm{AA\ composition},\mathrm{GC3},\mathrm{M\mbox{-}density}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This notation is deliberately direct: it is a codon-residual to protein-abundance association after finite controls, not a claim that the residual coordinate has been seen acting through translation.

\paragraph{Joined rows and hit rates.}
The joined CDS-protein rows range from $1{,}656$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $19{,}395$ in mouse.  The join rates are high in most organisms: $1.000000$ in \emph{Danio rerio}, chicken, \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, and \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}; $0.9978649586$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655; $0.9975320829$ in \emph{Bacillus subtilis} 168; $0.9956564068$ in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}; $0.9944378228$ in \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1; $0.9808334176$ in mouse; $0.9779294770$ in human; $0.9469110659$ in \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}; $0.9156038419$ in yeast; $0.8913774652$ in \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}; and $0.8231586752$ in rat.  These hit rates describe identifier matching for the finite omics table; they do not themselves supply translation readouts or mechanistic mediation.

\paragraph{Observed direct survival.}
All fifteen organism-level finite rows have positive $R^2_{QP}$ under the stated controls, and all nine residual coordinates survive at the chosen positivity threshold in each organism.  The strongest direct protein-omics association in this table is yeast, with $R^2_{QP}=0.2983762499$ on $5{,}815$ joined proteins.  The next largest entries are \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 with $R^2_{QP}=0.1076752668$ on $3{,}739$ proteins and \emph{C. elegans} with $R^2_{QP}=0.1021790676$ on $11{,}661$ proteins.  The remaining direct entries are \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum} at $0.0814178672$ on $6{,}974$ proteins, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster} at $0.0770952887$ on $13{,}295$, \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} at $0.0646471273$ on $5{,}006$, \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} at $0.0639751425$ on $1{,}656$, \emph{Bacillus subtilis} at $0.0619062459$ on $4{,}042$, mouse at $0.0498279841$ on $19{,}395$, zebrafish at $0.0461352981$ on $14{,}410$, human at $0.0445456512$ on $19{,}053$, chicken at $0.0398079360$ on $12{,}418$, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} at $0.0320900917$ on $4{,}129$, rat at $0.0276399860$ on $13{,}322$, and \emph{Halobacterium salinarum} at $0.0192568779$ on $1{,}747$.

\paragraph{Rank and residual energy.}
The rank diagnostics distinguish this computation from the three-organism modeled-tAI surface.  Here each organism has many CDS-level rows and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ after a rank-$23$ control fit.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $4019$ in \emph{B. subtilis}, $11638$ in \emph{C. elegans}, $14387$ in zebrafish, $6951$ in \emph{Dictyostelium}, $13272$ in fly, $3716$ in \emph{E. coli}, $12395$ in chicken, $1724$ in \emph{Halobacterium}, $19030$ in human, $19372$ in mouse, $4106$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}, $4983$ in \emph{P. aeruginosa}, $13299$ in rat, $5792$ in yeast, and $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus}.  The corresponding residual protein-abundance energies range from $980.7615793$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} and $1047.6567892$ in \emph{Halobacterium} to $30769.5367150$ in human and $48806.4785641$ in mouse.  The table is therefore a powered CDS-level association table rather than an $n=3$ rank-saturated organism-level fit.

\paragraph{Relation to translation readouts.}
A separate modeled translation-readout computation used three organisms and dos Reis wobble weights derived from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy data.  It found $81$ modeled-tAI coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ and a maximum absolute $S_{ij}$ of $3{,}801{,}961.031607896$, but the organism count was only $3$.  That surface is useful as a bounded modeled readout, not as powered measured survival.  Its own break rule says that powered survival is not available without at least $40$ matched species or an equivalent high-row matched design.  The present protein-omics computation avoids that particular rank problem by using CDS-level rows, but it does not contain a measured translation mediator $T_j$ for the same rows.  Consequently, a positive $S^{QP}$ entry may be reported as direct protein-abundance predictivity after controls, but not as a translation pathway.

\paragraph{mRNA stability side checks.}
The same nine-coordinate residual system has also been tested against mRNA half-life surfaces in single-organism or consensus settings.  In yeast, $5{,}085$ joined rows give $R^2_{QM}=0.0517942904$, $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140374$ on the same join, cosine $\cos(S^{QM},S^{QP})=0.8040935468$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ with $1000$ permutations.  In \emph{E. coli}, $2{,}720$ joined rows give $R^2_{QM}=0.0229855664$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.1163231185$, cosine $-0.7784923376$, and the same permutation resolution.  In human, $16{,}165$ joined rows across $54$ consensus half-life columns give $R^2_{QM}=0.0105036704$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.0409521556$, cosine $0.6351183884$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  These side checks show that the coordinate system contacts RNA and protein abundance surfaces differently across organisms; they are not a replacement for a joint $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ mediation matrix.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite data support the following narrow statement: B$^\ast_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates retain positive direct association with measured PAXdb protein abundance after CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and M-density controls in fifteen organism-specific CDS tables.  The result is strongest in yeast and remains positive in every organism listed above.  The result does not establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, causal mediation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, localization, post-translational state, organismal phenotype, adaptation, or universality.  Those conclusions require separate layer-matched contacts.  In particular, a translation-mechanism reading would require same-CDS rows containing $Q_i$, a measured translation readout $T_j$, matched mRNA controls, and protein abundance $P_k$, followed by an explicit test of whether $Q_i\to T_j$ survives and whether conditioning on $T_j$ removes or reduces the direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association.
