\subsection{Transcript-surveillance position rules and the RNA half-life boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-transcript-surveillance-nmd-position-rule-teran2021}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite transcript-surveillance panel in which coding-RNA windows are read only through row-level molecular measurements.  A row is eligible for the present chapter only when the same window carries a synonymous coordinate label, a translation readout used as a boundary variable, an RNA half-life or decay readout, matched mRNA or transcription controls, isoform state, local sequence and structure covariates, and batch metadata.  The intended bridge is not the bare observation that some transcript-surveillance statistic changes with a coordinate.  It is the narrower claim that a coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference may be treated as a bounded cross-layer relation only when it is joined to a prespecified decay-factor RBP surface, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  The finite row type can be summarized as
$$\begin{aligned}
 r_i ={}& (q_i,t_i,h_i,o_i,a_i,s_i,d_i,g_i,c_i,u_i,b_i,p_i,w_i),
\end{aligned}$$
where $q_i$ is the synonymous coordinate label, $t_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $h_i$ is RNA half-life, $o_i$ is measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP, $a_i$ records transcription or mRNA abundance, $s_i$ records splice or isoform state, $d_i$ records local RNA structure, $g_i$ records motif density, $c_i$ records GC and dinucleotide composition, $u_i$ records codon-pair and amino-acid context, $b_i$ records batch, $p_i$ records perturbation state, and $w_i$ records rescue state.  This notation is only a finite bookkeeping device; it is not itself an assay.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}
The transcript-surveillance contact attached to the Teran-position-rule record retained $38{,}576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations, with a recorded runtime of $55.602$ seconds.  These numbers support a finite RNA-surveillance boundary statistic: they show that the joined position-rule surface can be evaluated on a nontrivial transcript set under a declared permutation regime.  They do not, by themselves, measure decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP depletion, rescue, protein production, folding, phenotype, or mechanism.  The correct reading is therefore conditional.  If the position label $q_i$ separates RNA half-life only after controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, translation readout, and batch, then the row set has crossed an RNA-stability boundary.  It has not yet crossed an RBP-mechanism boundary unless $o_i$, $p_i$, and $w_i$ move in the required finite pattern.

\paragraph{Cross-layer licensing rule.}
The chapter licenses the cross-layer wording only for the following finite implication.  Let $H(q)$ denote the controlled half-life contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels, and let $O(q)$ denote the corresponding local occupancy contrast for the nominated decay-factor RBP.  A positive bridge is available only when
$$\begin{aligned}
 &H(q) \neq 0,\quad O(q) \neq 0,\\
 &H(q) \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss},\\
 &H(q) \text{ returns under wild-type rescue},\\
 &H(q) \text{ is not restored by binding-defective or non-target controls}.
\end{aligned}$$
The translation readout $t_i$ is a boundary guard rather than a promotion device.  It prevents an RNA half-life result from being silently interpreted as translation realization.  Even when $t_i$ is present, the chapter remains about RNA stability and its possible RBP-mediated bridge; protein output requires its own matched protein contact, and structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, and universality require separate layer-appropriate contacts.

\paragraph{Nearby finite comparisons.}
The Teran-position-rule result is treated as one member of a broader RNA-layer reality surface, not as a global biological law.  A separate m6A half-life boundary used $5837$ genes and reported a static Spearman value of $-0.388929$, a causal Spearman value of $0.245804$, a length-partial Spearman value of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial-permutation value of $0.000999001$ under the stated coverage rule.  An ARE-deletion stability boundary used $226$ BEAS2B deletions and $2242$ Jurkat deletions, with $1000$ actual permutations and $2000$ grading permutations, and recorded sign concordance.  A miRNA target-repression boundary used $55{,}170$ rows, with median aggregate per-miRNA partial rho $-0.224966$ and $7$ replicated gradient cases.  These neighboring records show that finite RNA-layer claims can be numerically grounded in multiple ways, but they also mark the present limitation: an RNA half-life statistic remains an RNA-layer statistic unless the required same-row bridge measurements are attached.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim is refused if high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels become exchangeable for RNA half-life after the declared controls.  It is also refused if half-life differs while measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is unchanged, if the effect survives target-RBP loss, if wild-type rescue fails to restore the effect, or if binding-defective rescue or non-target controls reproduce the pattern.  A similar refusal applies when local RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, transcript abundance, codon-pair context, or batch explains the contrast.  In all such cases the result may still be a finite RNA-stability association, but it is not an RBP bridge and it is not evidence for translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or a universal sequence law.
