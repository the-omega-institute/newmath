\subsection{Transcript-surveillance boundary for an NMD position rule}
\label{sec:bioreality-nmd-position-rule-teran2021}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record treats a transcript-surveillance position rule as a finite matched-row question, not as a general law of coding sequence.  The subject is a panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows equipped with coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA or transcription controls, local sequence and structure covariates, splice or isoform state, batch, perturbation state, rescue state, and a declared stopping condition.  The observed Teran-style position-rule audit retained $38576$ rows, used $1000$ label permutations, and completed the registered computation in $55.602$ seconds.  These numbers are the finite support for the boundary statement: they show that the record has a nonempty transcript-surveillance surface with a permutation-calibrated readout, but they do not by themselves install an RNA-decay mechanism or a translation outcome.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout separation.}
The admissible comparison is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after amino-acid composition is already neutralized.  A row is interpretable only while its coordinate label remains paired with the RNA readout and with controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  In this chapter, the coordinate is therefore a bounded descriptor of a finite window panel.  It becomes a cross-layer relation only if the RNA half-life contrast survives the declared controls while remaining fenced off from the translation readout.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
A stronger decay-factor RBP bridge requires more than a half-life contrast.  The same finite rows must contain measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP, a decoupling between coordinate label and occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  In symbolic shorthand, the licensed bridge has the following finite-row form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{coordinate label} + \text{translation readout} + \text{RNA half-life} \\
&\quad + \text{measured RBP occupancy} + \text{loss/rescue/control state} \\
&\quad + \text{transcription, structure, motif, composition, isoform, abundance, batch covariates}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If the half-life pattern remains after ordinary covariate control but the measured RBP occupancy does not move with it, the result is only a finite RNA-stability association.  If target-RBP loss fails to collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or if a binding-defective or non-target control reproduces the pattern, the mechanism claim is rejected.

\paragraph{Numerical placement among RNA-stability contacts.}
The position-rule row is weaker than a fully quantified RBP-perturbation panel, because the available scalar payload for this subject reports $n=38576$ retained rows and $1000$ permutations but does not report a local RBP-occupancy statistic, rescue coefficient, or binding-defective contrast.  Nearby RNA-stability audits show how stronger finite scalars would look.  In the m6A half-life boundary row, $5837$ genes were joined, the static Spearman correlation between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman correlation with $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman statistic was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  In the endogenous ARE transfer row, $14933$ genes were evaluated with $2000$ permutations, the baseline $R^2$ was $0.0657771$, the test $R^2$ was $0.0827561$, the increment was $0.016979$, the ARE coefficient was negative with $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, and the permutation value was $0.00049975$.  These comparison rows do not prove the NMD position rule; they only clarify the finite style of evidence required before an RNA-stability boundary is promoted beyond a coordinate-associated readout.

\paragraph{Local refusal conditions.}
The chapter does not infer translation realization from RNA half-life.  It also does not infer protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality from codon-window geometry, from a coordinate label, from mRNA abundance, or from a same-row translation column that merely serves as a boundary control.  The translation readout is mandatory because it prevents accidental promotion of RNA stability into translation biology; it is not itself proof that a translation mechanism has been observed.  Likewise, the RNA half-life readout is mandatory because it anchors the transcript-surveillance surface; it is not itself proof of decay-factor occupancy.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded transcript-surveillance boundary record for the NMD position-rule subject.  The retained-row count and permutation count are sufficient for a self-contained BioReality chapter describing what was measured and what is refused.  The positive statement is therefore narrow: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life surface may be recorded for the finite panel, under the stated covariates and with the same-row translation boundary present.  A decay-factor RBP bridge remains conditional on row-matched occupancy, target loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, none of which are supplied by the reported scalar payload for this subject.
