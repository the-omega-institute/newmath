\subsection{A transcript-surveillance boundary for coordinate-labelled coding windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-transcript-surveillance-nmd-position-rule-teran2021}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA windows in which synonymous coordinate labels are kept on the same rows as the measured RNA readout.  The intended row is not merely a codon-position annotation: it must contain a coordinate label, a translation readout, an RNA half-life or decay readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, transcription and mRNA controls, local structure and motif covariates, isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, perturbation state, rescue state, and an explicit break condition.  The transcript-surveillance contact that is presently verified is narrower.  It supplies a finite position-rule boundary for the Teran-style NMD surveillance setting, with $38576$ kept rows, $1000$ actual permutations, and a recorded runtime of $55.602$ seconds under experiment run $\mathrm{c362df6daa98}$.  Thus the data support a finite transcript-surveillance surface, not a completed decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout separation.}
The coordinate part of a row records whether a synonymous coding-RNA window lies in a high-coordinate or low-coordinate class after amino-acid composition is controlled.  The readout part records RNA stability or half-life on the same finite row.  A positive cross-layer statement would require the following additional separation: the coordinate label must be decoupled from measured local RBP occupancy, and the RNA half-life effect must follow the measured occupancy rather than merely follow GC content, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, motif density, local structure, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, transcription rate, or batch.  In symbols, the admissible claim has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{coordinate label} + \text{measured RBP occupancy} + \text{perturbation/rescue controls} \\
&\qquad \longrightarrow \text{bounded RNA half-life relation},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the translation readout present only as a required boundary row, not as an interpretation of the RNA-stability effect.

\paragraph{Finite statistic already present.}
The Teran-linked surveillance record contributes a nonempty, permutation-checked transcript-surveillance boundary: $n_{\mathrm{kept}}=38576$ and $N_{\mathrm{perm}}=1000$.  These numbers certify that the record is not a free-form biological assertion; it is attached to a finite tested table.  However, the stored scalars do not include local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP depletion, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target rescue, or the coordinate/occupancy decoupling statistic.  Consequently the verified row count and permutation count license only the transcript-surveillance reality boundary.  They do not yet license the stronger RBP-mediated RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Comparison with a completed RNA-half-life boundary.}
The same finite-row audit bundle contains a separate RNA-modification half-life boundary with $5837$ genes, a static Spearman coefficient $-0.388929$ for m6A load against DMSO log half-life, a causal Spearman coefficient $0.245804$ for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life, a length-partial Spearman coefficient $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value $p=0.000999001$.  That comparison is useful only as a calibration of what a scalar half-life boundary can look like when the row-level statistic is present.  It is not imported as evidence for the transcript-surveillance RBP claim.  The Teran-linked record has the kept-row and permutation surface, while the RBP-occupancy and perturbation/rescue coordinates needed for the stronger claim are not included in the verified finite facts.

\paragraph{Admissible local conclusion.}
The local conclusion is therefore deliberately bounded.  A finite transcript-surveillance position-rule surface exists for the cited NMD-style record, with $38576$ retained rows and $1000$ permutations.  A coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference may be discussed only as a candidate RNA-stability boundary unless the same rows also show measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss of the effect after target-RBP perturbation, restoration by wild-type rescue, failure of binding-defective or non-target controls to restore it, and persistence after transcription-rate, mRNA-abundance, local-structure, motif-density, composition, codon-pair, isoform, transcript-abundance, and batch controls.  If high-coordinate and low-coordinate windows are exchangeable for RNA half-life after those controls, the coordinate label remains descriptive.  If RNA half-life changes without an occupancy change, the result remains an RNA-stability association.  If occupancy changes but target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not govern the effect, the result remains short of an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Negative boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, causation, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate geometry, the NMD position-rule surface, the half-life contact, the mRNA control, or the mere presence of a translation readout.  Each of those stronger layers requires its own same-row contact and its own declared finite statistic.  The translation readout in this record is a guard against misreading an RNA-stability result as translation biology; it is not itself a license to promote the RNA half-life boundary into protein-level or function-level evidence.
