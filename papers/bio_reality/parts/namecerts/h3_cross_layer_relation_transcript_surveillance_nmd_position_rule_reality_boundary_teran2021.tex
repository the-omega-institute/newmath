\subsection{Transcript-surveillance position rule as a bounded RNA half-life boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-transcript-surveillance-nmd-position-rule-teran2021}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA windows in which synonymous coordinate labels are compared only after amino-acid composition is held fixed.  Each admissible row has a coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, transcription or mRNA-abundance controls, local sequence and structure covariates, isoform state, batch, perturbation state when present, rescue state when present, and a declared break condition.  The intended biological object is therefore not a global codon law and not a protein-level conclusion.  It is a finite RNA-surveillance boundary: a coordinate-associated half-life contrast may be discussed only while the translation readout remains an attached boundary variable rather than a promoted interpretation.

For the Teran transcript-surveillance contact, the verified finite computation is recorded under \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.transcript\_surveillance.nmd\_position\_rule\_reality\_boundary\_teran2021}.  The run identifier is \texttt{c362df6daa98}.  It retained $38576$ rows, used $1000$ permutations, and ran for $55.602$ seconds.  In this payload there are no declared $R$-lists, $M$-lists, $\lambda$ values, module-class labels, or hit-rate scalars; the row count and permutation count are therefore the concrete numerical anchors for the chapter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{kept}} &= 38576,\\
B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 55.602\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Admissible readback.}
The finite rows support a boundary statement of the following form.  Let $q$ denote the synonymous coordinate label after amino-acid control, let $T$ denote the matched translation readout, let $H$ denote RNA half-life, and let $Z$ collect transcription rate or matched mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  The permitted statistic is a controlled finite-row comparison of $H$ across coordinate labels with $T$ and $Z$ still present in the same row.  The admissible target is not $q \to T$, not $q \to$ protein output, and not $q \to$ mechanism.  It is only the bounded question of whether the RNA half-life readout remains separated from transcriptional, abundance, structural, compositional, isoform, and batch explanations on the finite panel.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{row}_i
  &= (q_i,T_i,H_i,Z_i,\mathrm{batch}_i,\mathrm{break}_i),\\
\Delta_H(q;Z,T)
  &= \mathrm{controlled\ finite\ contrast\ of\ } H \mathrm{\ by\ } q.
\end{aligned}
$$

A positive half-life contrast, by itself, is not enough to license translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, adaptation, causation, phenotype, or universality.  Those conclusions require separate contacts at the layer they name.  In particular, a same-row translation readout is mandatory as a boundary variable because it prevents RNA half-life from being silently re-described as translation realization; it does not itself prove that translation caused the RNA stability pattern.

\paragraph{RBP bridge rule.}
The stronger decay-factor RBP bridge requires more than the verified Teran boundary numbers.  A coordinate-associated RNA half-life effect can be treated as an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation only if the same finite rows also contain measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a target-RBP loss condition, wild-type rescue, a binding-defective or non-target control, and coordinate--occupancy decoupling.  The bridge is refused when half-life changes without measured occupancy movement, when occupancy is not tied to the prespecified RBP, when target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, when wild-type rescue does not restore it, or when binding-defective rescue, non-target perturbation, motif density, local structure, transcription, isoform state, transcript abundance, or batch can reproduce the pattern.

Thus the chapter separates two assertions.  The verified Teran computation supplies a finite RNA-surveillance boundary with $38576$ retained rows and $1000$ permutations.  The RBP mechanism bridge remains conditional on additional same-window occupancy and perturbation/rescue rows.  The absence of those rows does not invalidate the half-life boundary; it only prevents promotion to a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Numerical cross-check from a neighboring RNA half-life contact.}
A second finite RNA-stability contact is useful as a calibration of scope, not as evidence for the Teran mechanism.  The record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500} reports $5837$ genes, a static Spearman association of $-0.388929$ between m6A load and DMSO log$_2$ half-life, a causal Spearman association of $0.245804$ between m6A-load change and half-life change, a length-partial Spearman value of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial-permutation value $p=0.000999001$.  Its own status string says that passing the boundary statistic is not a mechanism or function claim.  This is exactly the local discipline used here: RNA half-life statistics may cross a finite readout boundary without becoming protein, structure, function, or universal-law statements.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{m6A}} &= 5837,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{static}} &= -0.388929,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}} &= 0.245804,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}} &= 0.227683,\\
p_{\mathrm{partial\ perm}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$

The comparison also marks what is missing from the present RBP bridge.  The m6A contact names a site-load half-life boundary and supplies its own permutation statistic; the Teran transcript-surveillance contact supplies retained rows and permutation count for an RNA-surveillance boundary.  Neither record supplies measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective control for the present coordinate-labeled coding-window panel.

\paragraph{Local refusal conditions.}
The claim breaks if coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  It also breaks if RNA half-life differs while measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent or unchanged, if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect, or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, or batch reproduce the pattern.  Under any of those conditions the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor or a finite RNA-stability association, not an RBP bridge.

The chapter therefore records a ready boundary result with a strictly local interpretation.  The finite data support a transcript-surveillance RNA half-life boundary at $N=38576$ retained rows under $1000$ permutations.  They do not support translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, a decay mechanism, or a general biological law unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and control structure.
