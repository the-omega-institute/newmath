\subsection{Transcript-surveillance position rule as an RNA-half-life boundary}\label{subsec:h3-cross-layer-relation-transcript-surveillance-nmd-position-rule-reality-boundary-teran2021}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.} The record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA windows in which synonymous coordinate labels are kept on the same rows as RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance or transcription-rate control, isoform state, local sequence covariates, and a mandatory translation-readout boundary. The intended biological object is not a free-standing codon law. It is a row-wise test of whether a coordinate-associated transcript-surveillance rule can be read as a bounded RNA-stability relation after the amino-acid composition has been held fixed and after the local position, abundance, splice or isoform state, motif density, nucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and local-structure terms have been declared. The verified Teran-style transcript-surveillance run retained $38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations, with runtime $55.602$ seconds. These numbers are part of the row certificate: they say that the NMD-position boundary was tested on a nontrivial finite table, not that a full molecular mechanism has been identified.

\paragraph{Row variables.} A row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i = (&c_i, T_i, H_i, A_i, O_i, Z_i, I_i, B_i, P_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $c_i$ is the synonymous coordinate or position label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout when present, $H_i$ is RNA half-life or a decay-compatible stability readout, $A_i$ is matched transcript abundance or transcription-rate information, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy when an RBP bridge is claimed, $Z_i$ contains local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, amino-acid composition, and length controls, $I_i$ records splice or isoform state, $B_i$ records batch and identifier-mapping state, and $P_i$ records perturbation or rescue state. For the present transcript-surveillance certificate, the verified finite datum establishes the position-rule RNA-half-life boundary. It does not by itself establish measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue behavior.

\paragraph{Statistic.} The admissible statistic is a controlled finite-row contrast for RNA stability. Schematically, the positive direction requires that the coordinate term remain distinguishable in a model of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i &= \alpha + \beta_c c_i + \beta_T T_i + \beta_A A_i + \beta_Z Z_i + \beta_I I_i + \beta_B B_i + \varepsilon_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the declared permutation or matched-row null applied to the coordinate labels. The Teran-style run supplies the row count and permutation depth for this boundary: $n_{\mathrm{kept}}=38576$ and $N_{\mathrm{perm}}=1000$. A coordinate-associated half-life effect may therefore be discussed as a bounded transcript-surveillance relation only at the level of RNA stability. If the same finite rows do not also carry the translation readout, abundance or transcription controls, isoform controls, and break condition, the coordinate remains a descriptor rather than a cross-layer result.

\paragraph{Comparative calibration.} The interpretation is intentionally narrower than other RNA-stability contacts in the same finite-row family. In the m6A site-load half-life boundary, $5837$ genes crossed the registered boundary with static Spearman correlation $-0.388929$, causal Spearman correlation $0.245804$, length-partial Spearman correlation $0.227683$, and two-sided partial-permutation value $0.000999001$. In the ARE-deletion stability boundary, the finite panels contained $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, used $1000$ actual permutations, and recorded sign concordance under a corrected pooled-label permutation. Those contacts show that RNA half-life boundaries can be finite, quantitative, and condition-specific. They do not transfer their mechanism to the transcript-surveillance position-rule table; each claim keeps its own rows, controls, and null model.

\paragraph{RBP-bridge boundary.} A stronger decay-factor RBP bridge would require more than the Teran-style half-life boundary. The same rows would have to show measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, decoupling between coordinate and occupancy, collapse under target-RBP loss, restoration under wild-type rescue, and failure of restoration under binding-defective or non-target controls. If RNA half-life changes while measured occupancy does not change, the result remains an RNA-stability association. If target-RBP loss is not paired with wild-type rescue, the perturbation is not specific enough. If a binding-defective rescue, a non-target control, RNA-structure covariate, motif-density covariate, transcription-rate term, isoform state, transcript abundance, or batch variable reproduces the pattern, the bridge reading is rejected.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.} The finite evidence licenses the following narrow statement: in the retained transcript-surveillance panel, a position-associated RNA-half-life readout can be treated as a bounded cross-layer RNA-stability boundary when it is read with matched controls and the declared finite statistic. No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, causation, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate contrast. Those stronger claims require their own layer-matched contacts, with their own same-row measurements and their own finite statistics.
