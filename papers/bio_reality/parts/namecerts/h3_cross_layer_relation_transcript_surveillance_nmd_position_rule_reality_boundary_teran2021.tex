\subsection{Synonymous coding windows at an RNA-decay boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-nmd-position-rule-rbp-decay-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each retained row is interpreted only as a row of measured or controlled variables: a coordinate label for the coding window, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, transcription-rate or mRNA-abundance controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, sequence-composition covariates, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, perturbation state, rescue state, and an explicit break flag.  The coordinate distinction is therefore not a free biological cause.  It is a finite label whose admissible interpretation depends on whether the same rows also contain the RBP occupancy and perturbation information needed for an RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Audit row count.}
The available verified run is \texttt{c362df6daa98}.  It retained $38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations, with a recorded runtime of $55.602$ seconds.  In the notation of this subsection, the finite table has size
$$
\begin{aligned}
  N_{\mathrm{kept}} &= 38576,\\
  N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
  t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 55.602\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers certify the size and computational envelope of the screened panel.  They do not, by themselves, certify an RNA-decay mechanism, an RBP mechanism, or a translation effect.  The present payload does not report an $R$ list, an $M$ list, a $\lambda$ value, a p-value, a module class, or a hit rate for this chapter.  Consequently the chapter may state the finite boundary and the required acceptance conditions, but it may not report a positive scalar bridge statistic beyond the retained-row and permutation counts.

\paragraph{Admissible relation.}
The intended relation is a bounded cross-layer relation between a synonymous coordinate label and RNA half-life, but only under a measured RBP bridge.  Let $q$ denote the high-coordinate or low-coordinate synonymous label for a coding window, let $T$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H$ denote RNA half-life, let $O$ denote local occupancy of the chosen decay-factor RBP, and let $Z$ collect transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch.  The only admissible positive pattern is the following finite-row separation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  q &\not\equiv \text{protein output},\\
  q &\leadsto H \quad \text{only through rows carrying } T, O, Z,\\
  q &\leadsto H \quad \text{must collapse under target-RBP loss},\\
  q &\leadsto H \quad \text{must return under wild-type rescue},\\
  q &\leadsto H \quad \text{must not return under binding-defective or non-target controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation readout $T$ is a boundary variable, not the promoted conclusion.  Its role is to prevent an RNA half-life pattern from being misread as translation realization, protein abundance, or downstream protein biology.

\paragraph{Acceptance condition.}
A positive chapter-level statement is licensed only if high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels remain separated for RNA half-life after the declared controls, and if that separation tracks measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy.  The RBP condition is essential: an RNA half-life difference without measured occupancy change is only a finite RNA-stability association.  Similarly, an occupancy association without coordinate--occupancy decoupling is not sufficient, because the coordinate label could be tagging local structure, motif density, composition, isoform state, abundance, or batch.  The perturbation condition is also essential: target-RBP loss must remove the effect, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target controls must fail to restore it.  If any of those control rows reproduce the pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
The chapter does not infer translation realization from RNA half-life.  It does not infer ribosome dynamics from codon-window geometry.  It does not infer protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law from the retained rows.  None of these claims follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and its own finite statistic.  In particular, the matched RNA half-life contact is not measured RBP occupancy; the ribosome-profiling contact is not an RNA half-life or RBP-perturbation statistic; and the matched mRNA-abundance contact is a transcription-layer control rather than RNA-decay evidence.  The only statement made here is a conditional finite-row rule: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life pattern may be read as a bounded RBP-mediated RNA-decay bridge only when the same rows contain the translation boundary, measured local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared covariate controls.

\paragraph{Break conditions.}
The record breaks if the coordinate label is treated as a direct biological mechanism; if RNA half-life is promoted to translation, protein output, structure, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality; if the RBP is selected after seeing the coordinate effect rather than prespecified through measured occupancy; if high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for half-life after the controls; if half-life differs but RBP occupancy does not; if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect; or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, abundance, or batch reproduce the pattern.  Under those failures the retained $38576$ rows remain useful as a bounded RNA-stability panel, but the RBP-mediated cross-layer interpretation is not licensed.
