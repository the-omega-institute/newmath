\subsection{NMD position-rule contact as an RNA-stability boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-nmd-position-rule-rna-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA rows in which a transcript-surveillance position rule is compared with an RNA-stability readout.  Each retained row is treated as a bounded biological contact only after the row has a declared coordinate label, a transcript or coding-window identifier, a half-life or decay-compatible readout, matched abundance or transcription controls when available, and a stated break condition.  The verified run for the present record is \texttt{c362df6daa98}.  It retained $38576$ rows, used $1000$ realized permutations for the boundary audit, and completed in $55.602$ seconds.  These numbers define the finite surface used here; they do not by themselves identify an RNA-binding protein, a translation mechanism, or a protein-output layer.

\paragraph{Row semantics.}
Let $W$ be the retained finite set of transcript-surveillance rows.  For each $w \in W$, write $c(w)$ for the position-rule coordinate label, $h(w)$ for the RNA half-life or stability readout, $a(w)$ for matched mRNA abundance or transcription-rate control information when it is attached, $z(w)$ for local covariates such as transcript identity, isoform state, sequence composition, local structure, motif density, codon-pair context, and batch, and $b(w)$ for the declared break flag.  The boundary statistic is therefore not a naked comparison of labels.  It is a finite comparison of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{NMD}} &= \operatorname{contrast}\bigl(h(w) \mid c(w), a(w), z(w), b(w)=0\bigr),\\
  |W| &= 38576,\\
  N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The expression records only the kind of finite computation being licensed: a coordinate-associated RNA-stability contrast under declared controls.  It does not assert that the coordinate is the biological cause of decay, nor that the half-life contrast is a translation readout.

\paragraph{Boundary interpretation.}
The positive content of this chapter is deliberately narrow.  A transcript-surveillance coordinate may be reported as touching an RNA-stability boundary when the finite rows survive the prespecified controlled comparison and when the readout remains explicitly RNA-level.  The run identifier, retained-row count, and permutation count are sufficient to make the contact auditable as a finite external measurement surface.  They are not sufficient to promote the result to ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence law.

\paragraph{Comparison anchors.}
The same BioReality bookkeeping contains other RNA-stability contacts with more explicit scalar outputs, and those anchors help calibrate what is absent here.  In the m6A site-load half-life boundary run \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, the joined surface contained $5837$ genes, reported a static Spearman value $-0.388929$ for m6A load versus DMSO log half-life, a causal Spearman value $0.245804$ for m6A load versus $\Delta\log_2$ half-life, a length-partial Spearman value $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$.  In the ARE deletion stability run \texttt{a41440ab2792}, the finite deletion panels contained $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, used $1000$ actual permutations with $2000$ grading permutations, and recorded sign concordance as true.  These comparison anchors show the intended evidential scale: when scalar effects, permutation values, or sign-concordance flags exist, they are read as finite RNA-layer statistics only.  The present NMD position-rule run supplies $38576$ retained rows and $1000$ permutations, but it does not supply an RBP occupancy series, a rescue arm, or a binding-defective control.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
A stronger decay-factor RBP bridge would require the same finite rows to carry measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and a coordinate--occupancy decoupling design.  The available finite record does not contain those rows.  Consequently, even if the NMD-position contrast is stable at the RNA half-life boundary, it remains an RNA-stability contact rather than an RBP mechanism claim.  A half-life difference without measured occupancy and perturbation rescue cannot identify the bridge.  Likewise, measured translation in a neighboring or attached dataset would only mark a translation boundary; it would not turn the RNA half-life contrast into protein output or functional realization.

\paragraph{Local refusal rule.}
The admissible statement is therefore: in the retained Teran-style transcript-surveillance panel, the NMD position rule may be read as a finite RNA-stability boundary over $38576$ retained rows and $1000$ permutations.  The inadmissible statements are equally important.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality follows from the coordinate contrast.  No decay-factor RBP mechanism follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies same-row RBP occupancy, target loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription and abundance controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, isoform diagnostics, batch controls, and a declared finite bridge statistic.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The record breaks if the coordinate label is used as external biological evidence by itself; if the RNA half-life contrast is described as translation, protein abundance, structure, function, mechanism, or a global law; if batch, isoform state, transcript abundance, local structure, motif density, or composition reproduces the pattern; or if an RBP bridge is claimed without same-row occupancy and perturbation/rescue evidence.  Under those failures the finite panel remains useful only as a bounded RNA-stability association.
