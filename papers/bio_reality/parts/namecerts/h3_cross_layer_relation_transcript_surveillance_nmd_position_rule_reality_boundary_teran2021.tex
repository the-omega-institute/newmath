\subsection{Translation-Bounded Transcript Surveillance at an RNA Half-Life Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-nmd-position-rule-rna-half-life-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite matched panel of coding-RNA windows in which synonymous coordinate labels are read together with transcript-surveillance and RNA half-life measurements.  The intended row is not a protein-output row and not a phenotype row.  It is a bounded transcript-level row carrying, when available, a coordinate label, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, transcription or mRNA abundance control, local sequence and structure covariates, isoform state, batch, perturbation state, rescue state, and a declared break condition.  The Teran-position surveillance audit contributes the concrete finite scale for this record: $38576$ rows were kept, the null procedure used $1000$ permutations, and the runtime was $55.602$ seconds under run id $\mathrm{c362df6daa98}$.  These numbers are used only to identify the evaluated finite surface; they do not turn the coordinate geometry into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Row schema.}
For a kept window $w$, write $q(w)$ for the synonymous coordinate class, $T(w)$ for the matched translation readout when present, $H(w)$ for RNA half-life, $A(w)$ for mRNA abundance or transcription control, and $Z(w)$ for the collected covariates: local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and amino-acid composition.  A positive transcript-surveillance boundary would require a finite statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H(w) \mid q(w), T(w), A(w), Z(w)\bigr),\\
  \mathcal{B}
  &= \{w : w \text{ has the declared coordinate, translation, half-life, and control fields}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The Teran-position audit supplies the bounded set size $|\mathcal{B}|=38576$ and a $1000$-permutation finite null.  It does not, by itself, supply same-window measured occupancy of a decay-factor RNA-binding protein, a target-RBP loss arm, a wild-type rescue arm, or a binding-defective rescue arm.

\paragraph{Boundary interpretation.}
The finite result may be read as a transcript-surveillance contact only at the level actually measured: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life or position-rule surface after finite-row filtering and permutation.  A stronger RNA-decay RBP bridge would need the same rows, or a declared matched extension of those rows, to carry measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy.  The bridge would also need coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target controls to restore the same pattern.  Without those fields, the record remains an RNA half-life boundary rather than an RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Comparison controls.}
The surrounding finite transcript-layer evidence shows why the scope is kept narrow.  In the m6A half-life audit, $5837$ genes were evaluated, the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log$_2$ half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman association between m6A-load change and half-life change was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  In the ARE deletion audit, $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows were retained, with $1000$ actual permutations, $2000$ grading permutations, and sign concordance recorded as true.  These comparison surfaces demonstrate that finite transcript-stability contacts can carry concrete row counts and permutation statistics, but they also illustrate the same rule: a half-life association remains a transcript-layer readout unless the missing mechanistic rows are part of the finite carrier.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The local negative scope is part of the mathematical object.  If high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch, then the coordinate is only a bounded descriptor.  If RNA half-life differs but measured decay-factor RBP occupancy does not change, the result is only a finite RNA-stability association.  If target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, or batch reproduce the pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Allowed conclusion.}
The record is therefore licensed as a finite transcript-surveillance boundary with $38576$ kept rows and a $1000$-permutation null surface.  It can support the statement that a synonymously indexed coding-RNA coordinate has been tested against an RNA half-life readout under declared controls.  It cannot support translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality.  Any such stronger conclusion requires a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite rows and statistic.
