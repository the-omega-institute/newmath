\subsection{Transcript-surveillance position rule as a bounded RNA-stability contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-transcript-surveillance-nmd-position-rule-teran2021}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of coding-RNA windows in which synonymous coordinate labels are read together with transcript-surveillance position information and an RNA-stability readout. The panel is not treated as a sequence law. It is a row-indexed assay surface: each retained row carries a coding-window coordinate, the relevant position-rule label, the RNA half-life or decay proxy used by the experiment, and the covariates needed to prevent promotion of a coordinate association into a mechanism. In the Teran-position-rule run, the finite screen retained $38{,}576$ rows and evaluated the declared statistic against $1{,}000$ permutations. The registered computation completed in $55.602$ seconds under experiment run $\mathrm{c362df6daa98}$. These numbers fix the scale of the empirical contact: the admissible statement is about this finite row set and its declared randomization check, not about all transcripts, all cell states, or all coding sequences.

\paragraph{Readout map.}
For a retained row $i$, write $q_i$ for the synonymous coding-coordinate label, $x_i$ for the transcript-surveillance position label, $h_i$ for the RNA half-life or decay readout, $t_i$ for the same-row translation readout when present, and $z_i$ for the control tuple containing transcription or mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch. The contact is evaluated only through finite tuples of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_i=(q_i,x_i,t_i,h_i,z_i,b_i), \qquad i=1,\ldots,38576,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $b_i$ records whether the declared break condition has fired. The coordinate is allowed to remain in the model only as a bounded descriptor unless the half-life signal survives the control tuple and remains separated from the translation boundary. Thus a half-life contrast may be reported as an RNA-stability boundary statistic, but it does not by itself report translation, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
The admissible statistic compares high-position and low-position labels after the finite-row controls have been applied. In schematic notation, the permitted contrast is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
  = \operatorname{Stat}\bigl(h_i \mid q_i,x_i,z_i\bigr)
    - \operatorname{Stat}\bigl(h_i \mid q_i,z_i\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
with the permutation distribution generated inside the same retained row universe. The observed computation records $1{,}000$ actual permutations, so the paper claim is limited to the permutation-tested boundary induced by those rows. No scalar $p$-value or effect-size magnitude is present in the supplied Teran record; consequently the chapter does not invent one. The only numerical claims made here are the row count, permutation count, run identifier, runtime, and the fact that the statistic was evaluated as the registered transcript-surveillance contact.

\paragraph{Relation to other RNA-stability contacts.}
The same BioReality packet also stores independent RNA-stability boundary checks that calibrate the intended level of interpretation. In the m6A site-load half-life run, $5{,}837$ genes were joined after the stated coverage rule; the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman association between m6A load and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$. In the ARE deletion stability run, the finite panels contained $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2{,}242$ Jurkat rows, used $1{,}000$ actual permutations and $2{,}000$ grading permutations, and had a sign-concordant result under the corrected pooled-label permutation over deletions. These auxiliary facts are cited only to make the scope discipline explicit: an RNA half-life surface can cross a transcript-stability boundary while still remaining below translation, protein state, mechanism, phenotype, and law-level claims.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
The stronger decay-factor RBP bridge is not licensed by the finite Teran-position-rule row count alone. Such a bridge would require same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a prespecified target RBP, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target controls, and separation from local RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform state, abundance, batch, and codon-pair context. The supplied contacts explicitly lack measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and binding-defective control rows. Therefore the present chapter permits a transcript-surveillance RNA-stability boundary reading but refuses an RBP mechanism reading. If high- and low-coordinate labels become exchangeable for RNA half-life after the listed controls, the coordinate remains only a finite descriptor. If RNA half-life changes without matching RBP-occupancy change and perturbation/rescue behavior, the result is an RNA-stability association rather than a decay-factor bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim rule.}
The finite evidence does not promote codon-window geometry, position labels, RNA half-life differences, or same-row translation-readout availability into a protein or organismal conclusion. A translation claim would require an independent translation-efficiency or ribosome-occupancy contact on the same rows. A protein-output claim would require a matched protein-abundance contact. Folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, and universality each require their own layer-matched contact and statistic. The registered Teran run therefore supports only the bounded statement that a finite transcript-surveillance position-rule panel with $38{,}576$ retained rows and $1{,}000$ permutations can be used as an RNA-stability boundary surface under the declared controls; it does not establish a general sequence rule or an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.
