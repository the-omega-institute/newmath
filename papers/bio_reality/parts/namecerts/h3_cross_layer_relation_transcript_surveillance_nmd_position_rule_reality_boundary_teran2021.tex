\subsection{Transcript-surveillance position rule with an RNA half-life boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-transcript-surveillance-nmd-position-rule-teran2021}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA windows in which synonymous coordinate labels are compared against an RNA half-life readout while the same row is required to carry the controls needed to prevent an immediate promotion into translation, protein, structure, or function.  The biological object is not a free-standing sequence law.  It is a table of matched coding-window rows with coordinate labels, transcript or gene identifiers, RNA half-life values, transcript abundance or transcription controls when available, isoform state, sequence-composition covariates, local-context covariates, perturbation state, rescue state, and an explicit break condition.  The admissible readout is therefore a bounded statement about whether a coordinate-associated transcript-surveillance position signal is visible at the RNA half-life layer under the declared finite statistic.

\paragraph{Observed finite run.}
For the transcript-surveillance record indexed by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.transcript\_surveillance.nmd\_position\_rule\_reality\_boundary\_teran2021}, the verified run \texttt{c362df6daa98} retained $38576$ rows, used $1000$ permutations, and completed in $55.602$ seconds.  The row count is large enough to justify reporting a finite boundary statistic, but the reported payload gives no local decay-factor RBP occupancy matrix, no target-RBP loss rows, no rescue rows, and no binding-defective control rows.  Consequently the empirical content available here is an RNA half-life position-boundary contact, not an RBP mechanism result.

\paragraph{Local finite statistic.}
The row-level data may be summarized by a controlled comparison of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after the relevant transcript-level controls are attached.  In notation, the admissible statistic has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{NMD}} &= \Delta H_{\mathrm{RNA}}(\mathrm{high\ coordinate},\mathrm{low\ coordinate}\mid Z),\\
Z &= (\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motif},\mathrm{GC},\mathrm{dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{batch}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $H_{\mathrm{RNA}}$ denotes the finite RNA half-life readout, and $Z$ denotes the covariate block that prevents a coordinate label from being mistaken for a broader biological explanation.  The $1000$-permutation run records that the statistic was evaluated on $38576$ kept rows, but the present payload does not expose the realized signed effect size, the permutation $p$-value, or any list of row-wise residuals.  The chapter therefore treats the run as a verified finite contact with the RNA half-life layer and not as a complete mechanistic decomposition.

\paragraph{Translation-readout separation.}
A same-row translation readout is mandatory before any statement can mention translation-conditioned interpretation.  That readout is not supplied by an RNA half-life contact alone.  Ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows would have to be joined on the same organism and the same transcript or coding sequence, with matched mRNA abundance as the denominator or control surface.  Without that join, a coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference remains an RNA-layer association.  It does not say that ribosomes changed their occupancy, that translation efficiency changed, that protein output changed, or that the coding window has acquired a protein-level realization.

\paragraph{RBP bridge rule.}
A stronger decay-factor RBP bridge would require more than a half-life difference.  The same finite rows would need measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and a binding-defective or non-target control.  The admissible positive pattern would be: the coordinate-associated half-life effect tracks measured local RBP occupancy; the effect collapses when the target RBP is lost; wild-type rescue restores the effect; binding-defective or non-target rescue does not restore it; and the result remains after transcription-rate, local-structure, motif-density, sequence-composition, codon-pair, isoform, abundance, and batch controls.  Since the verified Teran-linked payload reports only $n_{\mathrm{kept}}=38576$, $1000$ permutations, and runtime, the bridge remains unlicensed at the RBP mechanism layer.

\paragraph{Boundary comparison.}
A neighboring RNA half-life BioReality record illustrates the difference between a bounded half-life statistic and a mechanism claim.  The m6A half-life record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500} reports $5837$ genes, a static Spearman value of $-0.388929$ for m6A load versus DMSO log half-life, a causal Spearman value of $0.245804$ for m6A-load change versus half-life change, a length-partial Spearman value of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value of $0.000999001$.  Those numbers support a finite mRNA half-life boundary in that separate record, while still explicitly avoiding a mechanism or function claim.  The transcript-surveillance Teran-linked record is handled with the same discipline: the table may carry a position-rule RNA half-life contact, but absence of same-row occupancy and perturbation data blocks promotion to an RBP-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The local exclusion rule is strict.  If high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after transcription, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch are controlled, then the coordinate is only a bounded descriptor.  If RNA half-life differs but measured decay-factor RBP occupancy does not differ, the record is only an RNA-stability association.  If target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, or batch can reproduce the pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.  If the translation readout is absent, the result cannot be read as translation realization.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim rule.}
From the verified Teran-linked finite run one may report only a bounded transcript-surveillance RNA half-life boundary over $38576$ retained rows and $1000$ permutations.  No translation effect, protein abundance effect, folding statement, physical admissibility statement, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this contact.  Each of those higher layers would require its own same-row contact and its own declared finite statistic.  In particular, codon-window geometry, coordinate labels, review status, or a half-life association cannot by themselves become evidence for ribosome behavior, protein output, RBP mechanism, folded structure, system phenotype, or general sequence law.
