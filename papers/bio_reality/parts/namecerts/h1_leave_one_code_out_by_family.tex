\subsection{Family-stratified leave-one-code-out readback}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite row.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table read through a six-coordinate codon cube.  The row does not claim that the cube is a law of biology.  It asks a narrower question: when one compares curated genetic-code variants against the standard-code label table, does the same local boundary region continue to catch the altered labels after the tables are grouped by biological family?  The biological contact is only a curated codon-to-label table.  It supplies amino-acid and stop labels for codons; it does not supply translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with codons displayed as six-bit positions and read back through the standard label table.  The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records the punctured-cube face data by the $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the contact region is not an arbitrary verbal collection: it is a finite codon subset with thirteen vertices, sixteen local edges, and four local tile faces.  The scalar spectrum attached to this row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with radial cubic residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the recorded spectral relation is numerically closed to the stated tolerance.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite null comparison for the $M$-shape used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  Among these samples there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling log reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  A second amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, records observed $M$-size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$.  Its reported values are $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}=0.0035$.  These numbers support the local shape as a finite readback phenomenon, not as an explanation of biochemical realization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out family row.}
The family-stratified test is the row identified by \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}.  It evaluated $5$ families.  The hit rates above the baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The unstratified leave-one-code-out row gives the surrounding scale: $23$ tables were evaluated, $63$ total targets were tested, the baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value $0.9841269841269841$ is reported as the hit rate above baseline.  Thus, after family grouping, four families remain at perfect above-baseline recovery and the alternative-yeast family remains high at $5/6$.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery and module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records no anchor codons, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, and high closure coverage $8$, with recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification covers every codon in $R$: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The complete module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has either predictable codons or boundary codons, so the family-stratified row is attached to a complete finite decomposition of the thirteen-codon boundary region.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The evidence supports a local code-read statement.  Window-six coordinates expose a stable boundary-tile pattern for the standard-code label table, and leave-one-code-out recovery remains strong after grouping the evaluated tables by family.  The row is therefore ready as a codon-label readback result: it says that the curated table, the finite cube coordinates, the boundary set $R$, the module partition, and the reported null comparisons agree on a local codon-tile phenomenon.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
Nothing in this row observes translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological rule.  Amino-acid label readback is still label readback.  A separate contact would be required before any statement about translation, folding, physical realization, function, or universality could be made.  In particular, cross-organism wording about codon families, CUN/UUR usage, or tRNA labels cannot turn this finite code-layer pattern into a structure or mechanism claim.
