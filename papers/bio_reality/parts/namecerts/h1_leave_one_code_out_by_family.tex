\subsection{Family-Resolved Leave-One-Code-Out Boundary Test}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
This record concerns a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_{6}$ whose vertices are read as codon displays, together with a curated standard-code label table.  The object being tested is not translation as a physical process, but a finite code-layer question: when code labels are partially removed by family, does the local window-six tile structure retain predictive contact with the held-out label pattern?  The finite distinguished residue set is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded size is $|R|=13$.  The same row identifies $R$ with the punctured-cube boundary pattern and gives the face-vector data $(13,16,4)$ for the finite tile complex.  These numbers are the coordinate evidence for treating the family test as a local boundary problem rather than as an unrestricted biological law.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The spectral row supplies three scalar quantities for the finite operator package:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the stored numerical check is at roundoff scale for the asserted cubic relation.  The shape test used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sample log also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ found no exact shape match, an observed module size $20$, and $7$ lower-tail samples no larger than the observed size, giving exact-shape value $0.0$ in the sampled table and lower-tail value $0.0035$.  The finite row does not provide the twenty labels of $M$ as a list, so this chapter uses only the reported cardinal and test statistics for $M$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out rows.}
The global leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the observed rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The corresponding rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with its boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-resolved row refines this into $5$ families.  The per-family hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus four of the five recorded families reach unit hit rate above the baseline criterion, and the remaining family still records five successes out of six in decimal form.  The natural finite conclusion is that the local tile boundary is stable under this family-level removal protocol, subject to the curated code table used for label readback.

\paragraph{Codon-module localization.}
The leave-one-codon-out module row classifies all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The recorded partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification therefore accounts for the whole thirteen-codon residue set and does not leave a hidden residual class inside this finite packet.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The curated standard-code table is the contact point: it assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels and thereby allows the code-layer split to be checked.  The experiment supports the narrower claim that window-six coordinates expose a stable local label-boundary pattern for the standard-code table and its recorded family removals.  It does not show that DNA or translation must obey this geometry, and it does not identify a folding rule, a physical admissibility criterion, a biochemical function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological principle.  Any step from label readback to translation, structure, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate observation of that higher layer.  In the present record the finite evidence remains aligned with code-level readback and with the boundary warning attached to the local codon tile.
