\subsection{Family-resolved leave-one-code-out stability}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The family-resolved leave-one-code-out row tests whether the window-six codon coordinate retains its local boundary signal when alternative code tables are grouped by biological code family rather than pooled into one aggregate.  The object under test is not translation itself, nor a biochemical realization of a protein.  It is a finite codon-label readback problem: a curated standard-code table supplies the codon-to-label contact, and the six-coordinate codon cube supplies a local geometric organization in which codons can be removed, compared, and classified.  The claim is therefore narrow.  Window-six coordinates can expose stable local boundary behavior across code families, while the amino-acid or stop labels remain curated observations attached to the code layer.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the recorded row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records the punctured-cube face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the test is not merely counting codons in isolation: it is reading a finite local tile with $13$ displayed vertices, $16$ displayed edges, and $4$ displayed two-faces.  No global biological law is inferred from this tile.  The tile is a code-layer witness for a finite split in the codon table, not an explanation of translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

These numbers support a reproducible finite-row geometry, but they do not identify a biochemical force or an organism-level principle.  They only say that the displayed codon split has a stable coordinate-and-spectrum profile under the chosen construction.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the sampled null for the $M$ shape row, the run used $N=2000$ samples, with $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact-shape count was $1$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, the observed $M$ size was $20$.  In $N=2000$ samples the exact-match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, and the recorded probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These rows make the boundary pattern statistically nontrivial for the finite experiment.  They do not turn the codon-coordinate construction into a necessary law of DNA, RNA, ribosomes, protein structure, or cellular function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out aggregate.}
The pooled leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The recorded above-baseline hit rate was also
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the leave-one-code-out signal is not a small marginal rise over baseline.  It is a strong code-layer recovery pattern for the finite family of evaluated tables.  The interpretation remains deliberately local: the data say that the coordinate tile is informative for codon-label recovery in the curated code rows, not that it realizes translation or predicts folded protein structure.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out support.}

\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\

Each displayed module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification.  This supports the family-level row by showing that the same $R$ codons are not left as an unstructured residue; they are partitioned into biologically legible codon-label modules at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, read as a codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop label table.  That contact can test code readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, a successful family-resolved leave-one-code-out row licenses only the following statement: within the finite window-six codon coordinate, the curated code labels exhibit a stable family-stratified boundary signal.  Any stronger biological assertion requires a separate contact of the appropriate kind.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded protein structures, CUN/UUR wording is not structural evidence by itself, and tRNA-Leu notation does not by itself supply translation, function, admissibility, or universality.
