\subsection{Leave-one-code-out behavior by reassignment family}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record considered here is the family-level form of the leave-one-code-out test for the standard genetic-code label table.  The working carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read only as a finite codon display with curated amino-acid or stop labels attached afterward.  The distinguished reassignment region is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row audit reports $|R|=13$ and identifies the same list as a punctured-cube region with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the claim is not about all codons uniformly; it is about a finite local face-and-boundary region inside the codon cube, with the standard-code table supplying the label readout.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The associated spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value listed for the radial polynomial residual, so the numerical certificate is consistent to the supplied tolerance.  The shape randomization row used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the rarity statement is tied to a concrete finite sampling process rather than an asymptotic model.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information.  In that row the observed $M$ size is $20$, the sample size is again $N=2000$, and the exact-shape count is $0$, so the reported exact-shape probability is $0.0$ at the displayed resolution.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving $p=0.0035$.  This does not by itself identify a molecular cause.  It says that, under the finite class-preserving shuffling procedure actually recorded, the observed local object remains unusually small or structured relative to that null.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out aggregate.}
The aggregate leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  These numbers justify reading the test as a strong finite recovery pattern at the code-label layer: when one code table is withheld, the local cube geometry and boundary information recover nearly all of the listed target behavior above the baseline frequency.

\paragraph{Family stratification.}
The family-level row partitions the leave-one-code-out result into $5$ families.  The reported above-baseline hit rates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The only family below perfect recovery in the displayed table is alternative yeast, and it remains high at five-sixths of the above-baseline target rate.  The other four family blocks are recorded at full above-baseline recovery.  The finite conclusion is therefore family-specific: the same local codon-window region is not merely effective in the pooled leave-one-code-out table, but remains stable across the named reassignment families supplied in the row data.

\paragraph{Module decomposition.}
The codon-level module row assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same audit states that every module has either predictable or boundary status.  The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This explains why the family-level leave-one-code-out result is compatible with the module split: the thirteen-codon region is exhausted by six named modules, and each module is accounted for by either prediction or boundary behavior.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, understood as a codon-to-label record.  That contact can test whether the six-bit codon display recovers local label splits and reassignment-family targets.  It cannot test whether translation is realized in a cellular system, whether a peptide folds, whether a molecule is physically admissible, whether any biological function is obtained, whether a mechanism is universal, or whether the local cube pattern is a law of biology.  Amino-acid label strings are still code-layer outputs.  They are not protein structures, not functional assays, and not organism-level mechanisms.  Any move beyond code readout would require an additional contact of the appropriate kind, such as a translation experiment, a structural observation, or a functional measurement.

\paragraph{Claim.}
For the finite rows supplied, the leave-one-code-out by-family claim is ready at the code-read layer.  The evidence consists of the thirteen-codon punctured-cube region, the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, the shape rarity values $0.0005$ and $0.0125$, the class-preserving lower-tail value $0.0035$, the aggregate $63$-target recovery rate $0.9841269841269841$ above baseline, and the five-family stratification with four perfect family rates and one rate $0.8333333333333334$.  The scientific content is a local finite recovery phenomenon for curated codon labels, not a claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
