\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Stability by Genetic-Code Family}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table through a six-bit codon display, where each codon is treated as a point of the codon cube $Q_6$ and the observed labels are supplied by a curated standard genetic-code table. The mathematical object is not a mechanism for translation. It is a finite coordinate-and-label experiment: codon positions, local closure behavior, spectral data, and label relations are compared against the standard-code readout. The finite residue set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row reports that $R$ is a punctured cube with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the family-level claim is anchored in a concrete set of thirteen codons, not in an unrestricted codon-table narrative.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape controls.}
The associated spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value listed for the radial polynomial check, so the numerical certificate is effectively at floating-point zero for this finite computation. The shape test used $N=2000$ randomized trials with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Only $1$ exact shape match occurred, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, while $25$ samples reached Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. A second amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reported observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape probability $0.0$, and lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These controls make the local shape nontrivial relative to the sampled nulls, while still leaving the biological interpretation at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out family rows.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ target instances. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$; the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$; and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary allowance was again $0.9841269841269841$. The family stratification contains $5$ families. Their above-baseline hit rates are: bacterial-plastid $1.0$, mitochondrial $1.0$, nuclear-ciliate $1.0$, candidate-other $1.0$, and alternative-yeast $0.8333333333333334$. The claim supported by these rows is therefore a family-resolved stability pattern: most families are perfectly covered by the local rule-plus-boundary readout, and the remaining listed family is still substantially above the baseline rate.

\paragraph{Codon modules and recovery boundary.}
The leave-one-codon-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high-closure-coverage count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification is complete for the thirteen-codon residue set: $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are $\{AGA,AGG\}$ for $agr$, $\{AAA\}$ for $ar\_lys$, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ for $cun$, $\{AUA\}$ for $ile\_met$, $\{UCA,UUA\}$ for $ssn$, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$ for $stop\_trp$. This is the finite combinatorial content of the row: every residue codon is assigned to a named local module, and every module contains a predictable member or a boundary member under the reported criterion.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The supported conclusion is narrow. The six-bit window exposes a local tile-and-boundary pattern in the codon-label table, and the family split shows that this pattern remains strong when whole code families are held out. The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop labels, so the row can test label recovery, local module coverage, and family-level robustness of that finite readout. It cannot, by itself, test whether ribosomes realize translation in a particular organism, whether an amino-acid string folds, whether a molecule is physically admissible, whether a protein has a function, whether a biochemical mechanism has been found, or whether the pattern is a universal biological law. Any such higher-layer assertion would require a separate contact with translation, structure, physical chemistry, function, or organism-specific mechanism data. In the present record, the finite evidence supports a code-read statement only: the local codon geometry and the curated label table agree strongly across the reported leave-one-code-out family rows, while all claims above codon-label readback remain outside the certified scope.
