\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery by Code Family}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This record studies a six-coordinate codon display whose readout is the curated standard genetic-code label table. The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, read as a bit-level presentation of the $64$ codons. The experiment does not treat the cube as a biological mechanism. It asks a narrower question: whether the finite coordinate geometry and its induced closure neighborhood identify the same local label-boundary region when one compares standard-code labels against related code tables. The observed exceptional region $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports the face data of this region as the punctured-cube vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the carrier-level observation is not merely a count: the recovered set has $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ square faces in the local cubical complex. These numbers are the direct finite witness used by the chapter.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The finite-row spectrum supplies three scalars for the local operator associated with the region: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The reported cubic relation is satisfied up to numerical residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the radial polynomial residual. In the sampled shape comparison, $N=2000$ null draws were attempted with seed $42$. The row reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the test is visibly finite and duplication-heavy rather than an asymptotic claim. Among those samples there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$. These values support the claim that the observed local tile is not a typical shape under that null experiment, while still leaving the result inside a finite sampling protocol.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class information. In this run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed size of the compared set was $20$. The exact-shape count was $0$, reported as $p=0.0$ at the available sampling resolution. The lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving lower-tail probability $0.0035$. This is a stronger control than a pure shape shuffle because it keeps label-class constraints in the background while still failing to reproduce the observed local pattern at ordinary frequency. The result should be read only as finite evidence that the code-label boundary has an unusually stable local form under the tested coordinate construction.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. Against that baseline, the recovery rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with its boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. These numbers say that, when one removes one code table at a time, the finite coordinate neighborhood still recovers almost all target changes relative to the low baseline. The statement is family-specific rather than universal: it records success on the supplied table family rows and does not claim that every possible genetic-code variant must obey the same geometry.

\paragraph{Family stratification.}
The family-stratified row reports $5$ families. Their hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{alternative yeast} &=0.8333333333333334,\\
\text{bacterial/plastid} &=1.0,\\
\text{candidate other} &=1.0,\\
\text{mitochondrial} &=1.0,\\
\text{nuclear ciliate} &=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The alternative-yeast family is therefore the only listed family below perfect recovery, while the other four families hit every tested target above baseline. This is the chapter's central finite-row fact. It gives a family-level refinement of the aggregate leave-one-code-out result and prevents the aggregate $0.9841269841269841$ rate from being misread as homogeneous behavior across all families.

\paragraph{Codon-module classification.}
The leave-one-codon-out module classification assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ to six named modules, with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{AGR} &= \{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\},\\
\text{AR/Lys} &= \{\texttt{AAA}\},\\
\text{CUN} &= \{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\},\\
\text{Ile/Met} &= \{\texttt{AUA}\},\\
\text{SSN} &= \{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\},\\
\text{Stop/Trp} &= \{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row states $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from modules, and no unassigned codons. Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status. The associated leave-one-codon-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. Thus the code-family result is not an isolated table-level statistic: it is compatible with a complete finite partition of the same $13$ exceptional codons.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is adequate for code-label readback and for checking local codon-tile splits. It is not a translation experiment, not a folding experiment, not a physical-admissibility assay, not a functional assay, and not evidence for a universal mechanism. Amino-acid label sequences are still label sequences; they are not folded protein structures. The observed code-family recovery therefore remains at the code-read layer. A stronger biological conclusion would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer being claimed.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
For the supplied finite rows, the claim is supported at the level of local code-label geometry. The region $R$ is a $13$-codon punctured-cube-shaped boundary with vector $(13,16,4)$; its spectrum records $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$; its shape is rare in the sampled nulls with exact-shape probability $0.0005$ and amino-acid-class-preserving lower-tail probability $0.0035$; the aggregate leave-one-code-out recovery above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$ over $23$ tables and $63$ targets; and the family split gives four perfect family rates and one alternative-yeast rate of $0.8333333333333334$. The finite evidence is therefore ready for the standalone BioReality paper as a code-read record, provided the boundary is kept explicit: no translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows without an additional reality contact.
