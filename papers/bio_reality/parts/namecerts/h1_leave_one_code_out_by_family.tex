\subsection{Family-resolved leave-one-code-out behavior}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether the window-six codon coordinate gives a stable local readback for alternative genetic-code tables after one code table is withheld. The object under test is the standard-code codon table as a finite label table on the codon cube $Q_6$. The coordinate carrier has six displayed bits, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. Its internal data are coordinate position, closure, spectrum, and relation; the biological readback is only the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The experiment therefore asks a code-layer question: when code-table rows are grouped by biological family, does the same local boundary pattern remain predictive above a finite baseline?

\paragraph{Finite boundary set.}
The finite relation $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that $R$ is a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is not a free-form biological narrative. It is a finite codon subset with a declared combinatorial shape: thirteen codon vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional tile faces inside the six-bit codon display.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The coordinate packet also records a spectral separation. The values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the finite computation is numerically consistent with the stated cubic constraint at the displayed tolerance. These values are used only as code-coordinate evidence. They do not by themselves say that translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality has been observed.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The finite shape was tested against two row-level null comparisons. In the first comparison, $N=2000$ distinct samples were requested with seed $42$; the run needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the amino-acid-class-preserving comparison, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed mutable set size was $20$. The exact-match count was $0$, and $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below the observed size. The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite code-layer anomaly relative to the sampled baselines. They do not supply a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
Across the leave-one-code-out experiment, $23$ code tables were evaluated and $63$ target events were counted. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The local rule achieved hit rate $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M\setminus T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary. The reported above-baseline hit rate was likewise $0.9841269841269841$. The family-resolved row then partitions the result into $5$ families:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The least saturated family is alternative yeast, still above the finite baseline. The other four listed families have full above-baseline recovery in this row. The chapter claim is therefore family-resolved and finite: the local boundary code readback survives the leave-one-code-out partition by family for all five reported families, with one family at $5/6$ and four families at $1$.

\paragraph{Codon-module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. Its module classification covers all thirteen codons in $R$ with no extras, no missing module members, and no unassigned codons:

The accounting row states $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$. This matters because the family-level prediction is not being read from an unclassified residue: every codon in the boundary set is assigned to one of the six named modules.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The result supports a narrow statement. The six-bit codon display and its local tile boundary expose a finite pattern that predicts code-label changes above the listed baselines, including a family-level leave-one-code-out check. The curated standard-code table is the reality contact that makes the labels readable, but it is only a codon-to-label bridge. A codon label is not a translated molecule, a folded structure, an admissible physical state, a biological function, or a universal law. Any claim at those higher layers would require a separate contact designed for that layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The family-resolved row is accepted as a code-read observation: $5$ families are evaluated, the family hit rates are $0.8333333333333334,1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0$, the global leave-one-code-out hit rate with boundary is $0.9841269841269841$, and the underlying boundary set is the explicitly listed thirteen-codon punctured-cube relation. The result remains local to codon-label readback. It does not claim translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or biological universality.
