\subsection{Family-resolved leave-one-code-out boundary record}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite codon coordinate frame.}
The record concerns a six-coordinate codon display whose observable readback is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The carrier is the full codon cube $Q_6$, while the finite boundary set isolated by the local tile calculation has cardinality $13$. The verified boundary row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the observed local object is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has a finite cubical incidence profile, and the profile is read only at the code-label layer. The chapter therefore treats $R$ as a local code-read boundary inside the codon cube, not as a biological law about translation, folding, fitness, or organismal mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model calibration.}
The spectral row associated with the same finite carrier reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. Its radial or cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at tolerance scale for the finite computation. The shape test against random samples used $N=2000$ draws, with $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null row also used $N=2000$ and reported observed $|M|=20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape probability $0.0$, and lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These values support the claim that the local geometry is not a routine consequence of the simplest label-preserving shuffles, while still leaving the biological interpretation constrained to the finite code table contact.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result by biological table family.}
The family-resolved experiment evaluated $5$ table families. The hit-rate above baseline was $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate-other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast tables. The aggregate leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the above-baseline hit rate was $0.9841269841269841$. The rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary contribution was $0.9841269841269841$. These numbers make the family statement precise: across the supplied table families, the local codon-tile boundary gives a strong code-layer recovery signal, with the alternative-yeast family as the only family below perfect above-baseline recovery in this row.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon and module decomposition.}
The leave-one-codon row supplies a second check on the same boundary interpretation. It reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$: $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, assigned count $13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The finite modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the supplied row. This is the concrete finite content of the family-resolved claim: the signal is not only a global hit-rate statistic but also a complete assignment of the thirteen boundary codons into named finite modules.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code-read behavior and local label splits in the codon tile. It cannot by itself test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, folded-protein structure, function realization, or a universal mechanism. Consequently, the chapter records a positive code-layer alignment and a negative higher-layer boundary at the same time. The finite rows justify saying that the six-coordinate codon display exposes a stable local boundary pattern for standard-code label readback and for leave-one-code-out recovery by family. They do not justify saying that DNA must obey this geometry, that proteins fold according to it, that a codon-label sequence realizes a physical structure, or that the pattern is a universal biological law. Any such stronger statement would require an additional contact measuring the relevant process directly.
