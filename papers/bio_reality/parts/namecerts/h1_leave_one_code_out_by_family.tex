\subsection{Family-stratified leave-one-code-out recovery}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns a window-six codon coordinate on the codon cube $Q_6$, with a curated standard-code table used only as a label contact.  The tested claim is not that the coordinate system determines biology.  It is the narrower finite-row assertion that, after one genetic-code table is withheld, the local codon-tile construction still places the withheld table's changed labels inside a small predicted region at a rate that remains above the baseline when the results are grouped by code family.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The finite exceptional set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the run recorded here it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the exceptional codons are not treated as an unconstrained list of labels; they sit in a finite cubical shape with $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ square faces.  The companion module classification assigns all $13$ codons and leaves no residue: the modules are $\mathrm{AGR}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{AR}_{\mathrm{Lys}}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{CUN}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{IleMet}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{SSN}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{StopTrp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The recorded completeness row has $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The global leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The predicted-region hit rate in $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate in $M-T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported hit rate above baseline is therefore also $0.9841269841269841$, far above the baseline row.  The family-stratified row then separates the same phenomenon into $5$ code families.  The hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\

Thus every recorded family remains above baseline, with four of the five families at $1.0$ and the alternative-yeast family at $5/6$.  This is the chapter-level reason to keep the family row as its own claim rather than merging it into a single pooled statistic.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out boundary.}
The codon-level deletion row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This agrees with the module classification: every module has either predictable or boundary support, while the construction does not create a codon outside the recorded module list.  The finite record therefore supports a local statement: the observed changed-code labels cluster in the closure-and-boundary region associated with the window-six tile.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for code-label readback and for checking local codon-tile label splits.  It is not an observation of translation dynamics, folded protein structure, energetic admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Consequently the chapter asserts only a code-read layer result: the family-stratified leave-one-code-out data preserve the local tile signal above baseline.  Any claim about translation, folding, physical realization, function, mechanism, or universality would require an additional contact of the appropriate kind and is not supplied by this row.
