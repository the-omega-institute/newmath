\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery by code family}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The carrier for this record is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, used only as a finite display for codon labels.  A codon is read as a six-bit window, and the local data separate a tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The readback contact is the curated standard-code label table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, but it does not by itself assert any mechanism of translation or any physical realization of a protein.

The distinguished local boundary set has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a punctured-cube shape inside the window-six display, the same set has face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has a local combinatorial boundary profile in the cube.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The associated spectral row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic relation is numerically satisfied at $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is also the recorded radial-polynomial residual.  This is a numerical certificate for the local finite geometry, not a biological law.

The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ null samples with seed $42$.  It observed one exact shape match, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
It also observed $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so the row should be read as a finite randomized comparison with substantial rejection from already-seen samples.  A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null row used $N=2000$ with the same seed, recorded observed $M$-size $20$, found no exact class-preserving shape match, and counted $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size.  Its reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
No explicit list of the $20$ codons in $M$ is present in this finite row, so only its size and derived hit rates are asserted here.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out family row.}
The code-family experiment evaluates recovery under a leave-one-code-out protocol.  Across the broader leave-one-code-out row, $23$ code tables supplied $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9365079365079365.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the recorded boundary gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is also the hit rate reported above baseline.  The by-family row groups the same phenomenon into $5$ code families.  The family-level hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the chapter-specific claim: the local window-six boundary is not only visible in the standard-code row, but remains highly recoverable when variant code families are withheld one at a time.  The weakest listed family is alternative yeast at $5/6$, while the other four listed families are complete at the recorded precision.

\paragraph{Codon-level module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, no anchors, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons of $R$ with assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has at least one codon classified as predictable or boundary-relevant under the finite row.  This makes the record internally complete at the level of codon-label readback: all boundary codons are assigned to named modules, and the module list neither omits nor adds codons relative to $R$.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, resolved at codon-label readback.  It supports tests of the code-read layer and of the local codon-tile label split.  It does not observe translation dynamics, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the finite cube pattern may be used to discuss local label recovery and code-family boundary behavior, but it must not be promoted into a claim about protein structure, organismal function, physical possibility, or biological necessity without an additional contact that directly measures the higher layer.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For claim $h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family$, the finite row is ready at the code-read layer.  The carrier is $Q_6$, the distinguished boundary has $13$ codons with punctured-cube vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral row supplies $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, the shape nulls give small empirical probabilities, and the family protocol reports $5$ families with four perfect family rates and one rate equal to $0.8333333333333334$.  The evidence is therefore a finite, table-contacted codon-label statement: strong for local recovery by family, silent about all higher biological layers.
