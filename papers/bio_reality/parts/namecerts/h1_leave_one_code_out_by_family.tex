\subsection{Family-stratified leave-one-code-out stability}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object in this packet is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  The finite display is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with codons read as the sixty-four vertices and with curated amino-acid or stop labels attached by a table contact.  The claim does not treat those labels as a dynamical mechanism.  It asks whether the window-six coordinate presentation exposes a stable local boundary pattern when whole alternative code tables are withheld by family.  The distinguished boundary set $R$ has size $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row records that this set has punctured-cube face data with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the chapter is not asserting a continuous geometric law; it is recording a finite codon-window shape that can be checked against curated label tables.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape coordinates.}
The shape record comes with a numerical spectrum.  The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial check.  The Monte Carlo shape comparison used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and twenty-five samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving comparison, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite-local non-randomness reading for the codon-coordinate packet, but they do not by themselves identify a biochemical process.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out family row.}
The family-stratified test evaluates whether the leave-one-code-out signal survives when held-out tables are grouped by biological code family rather than pooled.  The finite row has experiment id $7dda978c6c97$ and reports $5$ families.  The family hit rates above the baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The pooled leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The family row therefore says that the signal is not concentrated in a single named family: four of the five listed families are perfect under this statistic, and the remaining family is still far above the pooled baseline.

\paragraph{Codon-level module check.}
The codon-withholding companion row gives a recovery rate of $1.0$.  It reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, no anchors, and $8$ high-coverage closure cases.  The module classification assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite assertion is consequently sharper than a loose enrichment statement.  Every codon in the thirteen-point boundary set is accounted for by one of the named modules, and every module has a predictable-or-boundary explanation in the codon-level row.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for code-layer readback and local label-split tests.  It is not an observation of ribosomal translation, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, evolutionary necessity, or a universal biological mechanism.  Amino-acid label sequences are therefore not protein structures in this chapter.  Cross-table stability of the local tile can motivate further experiments, but a stronger biological claim would need a separate contact of the appropriate kind: translation data for translation, structural data for folding, physical measurement for admissibility, and functional assay data for function.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For claim $h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family$, the finite data support a ready code-read statement: the six-bit codon coordinate presentation isolates a thirteen-codon local boundary set whose leave-one-code-out behavior remains high across five held-out table families.  The strongest numerical row is the family table, where the hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334,1.0,1.0,1.0,1.0$ across the five families.  The supporting rows give the exact $R$ list, the punctured-cube $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, significant finite-sample shape comparisons, pooled leave-one-code-out rates, and a complete six-module assignment of the thirteen boundary codons.  The conclusion is local and finite: it concerns codon-label table structure in the code layer, not the higher biological layers that would require independent measurements.
