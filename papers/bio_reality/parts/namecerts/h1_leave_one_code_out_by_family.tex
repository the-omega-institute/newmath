\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Stability by Code Family}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim packet.}
The packet $\mathrm{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}$ records a finite code-read test on the standard codon table.  The object under test is the curated codon-to-label table, viewed as a six-coordinate codon display on the codon cube $Q_6$.  The construction distinguishes coordinate positions, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  Its readback is therefore only the amino-acid-or-stop label assigned to each codon; it is not a model of translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary region $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies $R$ as a punctured cube with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the tested object is not an arbitrary list of exceptional codons: it has a small cubical incidence profile inside the six-bit codon coordinates.  The spectral check gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, within numerical tolerance.  These numbers are used only as finite coordinate diagnostics of the code-read geometry.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The family-level test evaluated $5$ code families.  The verified hit rates above the baseline were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the claim-specific finite row: every named family except the alternative-yeast group reaches perfect above-baseline recovery, while the alternative-yeast group still reaches five sixths.  The aggregate leave-one-code-out computation evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the above-baseline hit rate was $0.9841269841269841$.  The corresponding rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary allowance was again $0.9841269841269841$.  In finite-count terms, the boundary-allowed rule recovers all but one of the $63$ targets.

\paragraph{Codon-level support.}
The leave-one-codon-out row supplies a smaller local diagnostic.  It reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module-classification row assigns all $13$ boundary codons with no extras, no missing module members, and no unassigned codons.  The verified modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification completeness row gives $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, and empty lists for extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons.  This supports the claim that the boundary behavior is organized by finite codon modules rather than by scattered single entries.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Two null rows constrain the strength of the observation.  In the shape-significance run, $N=2000$ distinct samples were attempted with seed $42$; because duplicates were rejected, $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries were recorded.  Exactly $1$ sample matched the observed shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, while $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, exact match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding reported values were $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These comparisons make the finite coordinate pattern nontrivial relative to the sampled controls, but they do not turn the pattern into a biological necessity.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code label table.  That contact is adequate for code-layer readback and for checking whether local codon-tile splits align with amino-acid-or-stop labels.  It contains no observation of translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label-sequence readback remains at the code layer.  Any later claim about structure, function, organism-level mechanism, or physical feasibility must be backed by a separate contact designed for that layer.
