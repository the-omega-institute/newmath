\subsection{Family-Stratified Leave-One-Code-Out Readback}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon window. The object under test is not translation itself, but a finite code-layer readback: codons are displayed as points of the codon cube $Q_6$, local tile faces are inspected, and curated amino-acid or stop labels are used only as label contacts. The distinguished boundary set is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit reports $|R|=13$, and the same list is returned by the punctured-cube check. Its face-count summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local object is a small punctured cubical tile rather than a genome-wide rule. The associated spectral row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with radial cubic residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$. This is numerical evidence for a stable finite coordinate pattern, not evidence for a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Family-level leave-one-code-out test.}
The family-stratified probe removes one genetic-code family at a time and asks whether the omitted targets still fall into the tile-controlled readback zone more often than a baseline selection would suggest. Five families are present in the finite row. The measured family hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast}&=0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus all five family rows exceed the baseline, and four of the five are saturated at $1.0$. The related aggregate leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The same experiment reports $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M-T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ inside $M-T$ together with the boundary contribution. These numbers support a narrow code-read statement: the finite tile is a strong predictor for the omitted code-label rows, especially once the boundary class is admitted.

\paragraph{Codon-level module check.}
The codon-level leave-one-out row gives a separate internal control. It reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification accounts for all thirteen members of $R$: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the extra, missing, and unassigned lists are all empty. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has either predictable or boundary status. This is the local reason the family-stratified omission test has content: each affected codon class has a finite position in the same small boundary-and-closure decomposition, rather than being an isolated label exception.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The shape-significance row uses $N=2000$ comparison samples with seed $42$. It reports one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite comparison was constrained by the combinatorics of the available class-preserving space. A second amino-acid-class-preserving null row also uses $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It reports observed $|M|=20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape $p$-value $0.0$, and lower-tail $p$-value $0.0035$. These rows do not turn the tile into a law of biology; they show that the observed finite shape is unusual under the stated code-label randomizations.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is enough to test code-label readback and local label splits. It is not an observation of ribosomal realization, folded structure, physical viability, biological function, evolutionary cause, or universality across organisms. The correct conclusion is therefore scoped: window-six codon coordinates expose a robust local boundary-tile pattern for code-layer labels, and the family-stratified omission test preserves this pattern across five code families. No claim about translation, folding, admissible molecular physics, function, mechanism, or universal biological necessity follows from this packet unless a separate empirical contact is supplied for that higher layer.
