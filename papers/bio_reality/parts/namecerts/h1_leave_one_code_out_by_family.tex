\subsection{Family-level leave-one-code-out stability for the codon boundary tile}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record concerns the standard genetic-code label table viewed through a finite six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, whose vertices encode codons in a six-bit presentation.  The biological contact used here is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label.  The contact therefore tests code-layer readback and local label splits, not translation as a biochemical process.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The finite boundary set detected in the display has cardinality $13$.  The verified row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\},\qquad |R|=13 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object under study is not an unconstrained list of codons.  It is a finite local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ square faces after the puncture pattern is imposed.

The associated finite operator data give the numerical spectrum

These numbers describe the internal geometry of the codon tile; by themselves they do not assert a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape tests against finite null rows.}
The first null comparison sampled $N=2000$ finite alternatives, with $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled row matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005 .
\end{aligned}
$$
For the weaker high-overlap test, $25$ sampled rows had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125 .
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null comparison preserving amino-acid classes used $N=2000$ rows.  It found $0$ exact matches to the observed shape and $7$ rows in the lower tail at or below the observed module size $20$.  The corresponding finite-row values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035 .
\end{aligned}
$$
These tests support the claim that the displayed tile is not a typical output of the sampled finite null procedures.  They do not convert the tile into a universal law of biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The family-level claim is a refinement of a larger leave-one-code-out test.  In the aggregate run, $23$ code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with boundary codons was $0.9841269841269841$.  In short, when one code row is withheld, the finite local geometry recovers nearly all target rows above the stated baseline criterion.

This is the central finite-row content of the chapter: the leave-one-code-out signal persists after grouping the code rows by biological family labels.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out support.}
The companion leave-one-codon-out run records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, and $8$ codons with high closure coverage.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, and the above-threshold recovery rate is also $1.0$.  This provides a codon-level check on the same local boundary pattern: the test does not rely only on whole-code withholding, because individual codon removals also remain recoverable in the reported finite regime.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module classification is complete on the $13$ codons of $R$.  It assigns all $13$ codons, reports no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\

Every listed module has either predictable status or boundary status in the finite record.  The module table explains how the $13$-codon boundary is distributed across familiar local recoding regions rather than appearing as one undifferentiated set.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The verified data support a code-layer statement: the six-bit codon geometry exposes a local boundary tile whose leave-one-code-out recovery remains strong across $5$ family groups.  The evidence is finite and row-level: it consists of the $13$-codon boundary set, the punctured-cube face-vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, $\mu=3.051487585298128$, null-row probabilities $0.0005$, $0.0125$, $0.0$, and $0.0035$, the $23$-table by $63$-target aggregate leave-one-code-out row, and the five family hit rates listed above.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code evolution, or a universal biological law.  The curated code table observes codon-to-label assignments only.  Amino-acid label readback is not folded structure, and a local codon tile is not a global rule for living systems.  Any claim about translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that directly observes that higher layer.
