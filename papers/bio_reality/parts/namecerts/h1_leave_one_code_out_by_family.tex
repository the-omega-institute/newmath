\paragraph{Claim.}
The finite record for claim $\mathrm{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}$ tests whether the window-six codon coordinate can recover local code-table changes after one genetic-code table is held out. The biological object is the standard-code codon table, read only as a codon-to-label table. The claim is therefore deliberately narrow: a six-bit codon display may reveal a local boundary-tile pattern in the curated label table, but the labels are supplied by a biological code table and are not derived from geometry alone. The layer under test is $\mathrm{code\_read}$, not translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and contact.}
The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$. The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The readback contact is a curated standard genetic-code table, used at codon-label resolution. That contact says that codons receive amino-acid or stop labels; it does not observe ribosomal realization, protein structure, biochemical admissibility, or organism-level function. Consequently the finite experiment can support only code-layer claims about label recovery and local split behavior.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The local result is aligned with the curated code-table contact, but it remains blocked at the code layer. The finite records justify the following scientific reading: window-six coordinates and the punctured codon support detect a strong local pattern in codon-label readback, and the leave-one-code-out family table shows above-baseline recovery across five families. They do not show that DNA translation is forced by the coordinate system, that any amino-acid label sequence folds, that a protein is physically admissible, that a biological function is realized, or that the tile is a universal biological law. Any such higher-layer conclusion would require a separate contact whose observation is about translation, structure, chemistry, or function rather than codon-label assignment.

For $\mathrm{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}$, the finite evidence is ready at the code-read layer. The chapter records a reality-constrained local codon-table pattern: $R$ has $13$ codons with $f(R)=(13,16,4)$, the spectral values are numerically stable, the random controls give small finite probabilities, the aggregate leave-one-code-out hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, and the family-resolved row reports five above-baseline family rates with four equal to $1.0$ and alternative yeast equal to $0.8333333333333334$. The result is a local codon-label boundary statement, not a promotion to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
