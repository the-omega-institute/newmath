\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery by Code Family}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object and contact.}
This record concerns a window-six codon coordinate system on the $64$ codons of the standard code.  The biological contact used here is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The contact therefore supports code-layer label readback and local split tests, but it does not observe translation in a cell, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code formation, or a universal law of biology.  Any move to those layers requires an additional contact of the appropriate kind.

$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.

\begin{aligned}

Thus the local object is not merely a count: it has a specific codon support together with a finite face profile.  The row also records the spectral quantities

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the reported random comparison, the shape calculation used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only $1$ exact shape match occurred, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null run also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It observed $0$ exact matches and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The observed value was $|M|=20$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the claim that the local codon partition is not behaving like a typical sampled shape under the recorded null models.  They do not, by themselves, identify a biochemical cause.

The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for every codon in $R$ with no extra codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. This is the finite check that the family-level recovery statement is being made over the same residual support, not over an unmatched or drifting codon inventory.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The verified rows support a code-read claim: the window-six local geometry, the residual set $R$, and the module partition give a finite explanation of why many leave-one-code-out targets land inside the observed local pattern or its boundary.  The evidence is strongest where the row stays with codon labels and finite tile membership.  The evidence stops at that boundary.  From these data one may say that curated codon labels display a reproducible local split with high leave-one-code-out recovery across the listed families.  One may not infer that the coordinate system is a biological necessity, that the labels realize translation dynamics, that an amino-acid string folds, that a physical molecule is admissible, that a function is present, or that a universal biological mechanism has been found.  Those claims would need separate empirical contacts rather than this code-table contact.
