\subsection{Leave-one-code-out behavior by code family}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Claim and finite setting.}
The record tests whether a window-six codon coordinate system, read only as a six-bit display over the codon cube $Q_6$, retains local predictive structure when whole genetic-code tables are held out by biological code family.  The biological object is the standard-code codon-label table, and the layer under test is only code readback: codons are compared with curated amino-acid or stop labels.  The internal distinctions used by the record are coordinate position, local closure, spectrum, relation, a singleton label corner, a local tile face, and a three-point label class.  These distinctions are not a model of translation, folding, energetics, organismal function, or biochemical necessity; they are a finite way to ask whether a local codon tile remains visible after code-family exclusions.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished relation row has cardinality $13$.  The listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the relevant signal is not an arbitrary list of codons: it is a thirteen-codon boundary pattern inside the six-coordinate codon display.  The codons in $R$ split into six named modules in the classification row: $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The module audit is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and there are no extra module codons, missing codons, or unassigned codons.

The finite row does not enumerate all twenty members of $M$; the available member list is the thirteen-codon $R$ display above.  The appropriate reading is therefore shape-level and module-level, not an invented full list of $M$ codons.

The family-level holdout row evaluates $5$ code families.  The hit rates above the recorded baseline are

This says that four of the five named families are perfect at the recorded family resolution, while the alternative-yeast family is still above the baseline but not perfect.  The companion leave-one-code-out row supplies the larger comparison scale: $23$ tables were evaluated, with $63$ total targets, baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate above baseline $0.9841269841269841$, hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$.  These values support the narrow statement that the local codon-tile signal persists through most code-table exclusions and remains organized by the same boundary modules.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery check.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module-classification row strengthens the bookkeeping: every named module has at least predictable or boundary status, and the six modules cover exactly the thirteen codons of $R$.  The role of this check is local stability.  It does not show that a held-out codon is synthesized, translated, folded, or biologically functional; it only shows that, within the finite coordinate-and-label table, the codon remains recoverable by the recorded local closure and boundary pattern.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code bridge, whose observed fact is that a curated standard-code table assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-label tile splits.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or functional realization.  Consequently the amino-acid label sequence produced by readback is still a label sequence, not a protein structure and not a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For the claim $\mathrm{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}$, the finite evidence is ready at the code-read layer.  The recorded facts show a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary, complete module coverage of that boundary, significant separation from the listed null models, stable codon-level recovery, and family-level leave-one-code-out rates that are at or above the recorded baseline for all five families.  The correct conclusion is deliberately limited: window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern in curated codon-label data.  No claim about actual translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or biological law follows from this chapter unless a different contact, designed for that higher layer, is supplied.
