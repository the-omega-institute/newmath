\subsection{Leave-one-code-out behavior by code family}\label{sec:bior-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a finite six-coordinate codon display.  The claim is not that the codon cube determines biology.  It is the narrower observation that, after the curated standard-code labels are used as the code-layer contact, the window-six coordinates expose a stable local boundary pattern when alternative code rows are removed one family at a time.  The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, equipped here with coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data.  The distinctions used by the record are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The biological readback is only the standard codon-to-label table: codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels, and no protein realization is observed by this table alone.

\paragraph{Finite relation.}
The distinguished relation $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its observed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same thirteen codons appear in the punctured-cube check, with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object being tested is finite and explicit: thirteen codons occupy the selected relation, sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional tile faces are counted, and the row-level tests ask how this shape behaves under code-family deletion rather than under a free biological interpretation.

\paragraph{Shape against null rows.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ sampled rows with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and there were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$.  The exact-shape count was $0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These statistics support the finite code-layer separation: the observed tile is uncommon under the sampled alternatives, including the class-preserving comparison.  They do not say that the geometry is a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The all-row leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level refinement has $5$ families.  Its recorded rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The family split is therefore almost saturated except for the alternative-yeast family, which still exceeds the baseline by a large margin.  The family data are the central finite-row content of this claim: deleting one code family at a time preserves the local recovery behavior across all five named families, with one family recorded at $5/6$ and the other four at full hit rate.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out modules.}
The companion codon-level probe records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The thirteen relation codons are partitioned by the module-classification record as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the extra, missing, and unassigned codon lists are all empty.  Each listed module has either predictable or boundary status.  This is the finite local reason that the family-level leave-out data can be read as a boundary-tile phenomenon rather than as a diffuse table-wide coincidence.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, at codon-label resolution.  It supplies the fact that a standard-code row assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  It does not supply translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, organismal universality, or a mechanism explaining why the row should occur.  Consequently, the result may be stated as a code-read claim: the window-six display, together with the curated labels, detects a local split involving the relation $R$, the observed $M$ size, the spectral signature, and the leave-out family rates.  It may not be promoted to a claim about protein structure, molecular realization, function, or universal biological necessity unless another contact independently supplies that higher-layer observation.

\paragraph{Scientific verdict.}
The finite data support the following self-contained conclusion.  In the six-coordinate codon display, the standard-code label table yields a thirteen-codon punctured local relation with stable spectrum, rare null-shape behavior, complete module assignment, perfect or near-perfect family-level leave-one-code-out recovery, and full codon-level recovery inside the recorded predictable-or-boundary modules.  The observation is strong at the code-read layer and deliberately silent above it.
