\subsection{Leave-one-code-out behavior by code family}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
This record studies the standard-code codon table through a finite six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$.  The carrier is not treated as a biochemical mechanism.  It is a finite coordinate object whose cells can be read against a curated standard-code label table.  The relevant internal distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The internal operations used here are coordinate selection, closure, spectral measurement, and relation extraction.  The reality contact is narrower: the curated table assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels and therefore tests only code-layer readback and local label-split checks.

\paragraph{The distinguished thirteen-codon relation.}
The finite relation $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the verified run $5d4a3459997d$, the relation is exactly
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The independent punctured-cube check in run $e19917012c60$ gives the same list and reports the face vector
$$\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the row is not merely a count.  It fixes a concrete subset of codons and a finite local shape inside the codon cube.  The same row also determines the six module classes used for later readback: $\{AGA,AGG\}$, $\{AAA\}$, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $\{AUA\}$, $\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact can test code readback and local codon-tile label splitting.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or biological function.  Therefore the chapter does not infer that the codon cube is a global law of biology, that window-six geometry by itself explains translation, or that an amino-acid label sequence determines folded protein structure.  Any claim about translation, folding, admissible physical realization, function, mechanism, or universality would require an additional independent contact at that higher layer.  With the present data, the supported conclusion is exactly a code-read conclusion: finite six-coordinate codon geometry exposes a local boundary-tile pattern whose family-level leave-one-code-out behavior is strong against the recorded baselines, while curated biological labels remain the external source of the readback.
