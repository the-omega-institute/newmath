\subsection{Family-Stratified Leave-One-Code-Out Readback}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Object and scope.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read through a curated codon-to-label table whose entries assign each codon an amino-acid label or a stop label.  The experiment is deliberately local: it studies whether a window-six coordinate description isolates a boundary-tile pattern that remains visible when alternative code tables are held out by family.  The observed signal is therefore a statement about codon-label readback, not about translation chemistry, folded protein geometry, admissible molecular dynamics, cellular function, a mechanism of origin, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Finite carrier rows.}
The distinguished residual row set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same set is verified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This gives the code-layer boundary a concrete finite shape: thirteen codon vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces inside the local codon display.  The row set is not being interpreted as a physical compartment or as an evolutionary necessity; it is a finite coordinate pattern whose labels are supplied by the curated standard table.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling witnesses.}
The finite shape has a recorded spectral profile.  The values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the associated cubic residual reported as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, within numerical tolerance for the recorded computation.  Under the shape randomization run with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-shape count is $1$, so $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ is $25$, so $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, which is itself useful bookkeeping: the comparison class is finite and collision-prone rather than a continuous asymptotic model.  A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$-size $20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$, and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These numbers support the claim that the row geometry is not a generic artifact of preserving only coarse amino-acid classes.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
Across the leave-one-code-out evaluation, $23$ code tables supplied $63$ target events.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, matching the hit rate after allowing the $M-T$ region plus the boundary.  The stricter $M-T$ count alone has hit rate $0.9365079365079365$.  Thus the boundary contribution is small in size but material for recovery: it raises the recorded rate from $0.9365079365079365$ to $0.9841269841269841$ on the same $63$ targets.  The related leave-one-codon-out run records $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor cases, closure-coverage-high $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These figures place the family-stratified result in the same local-tile regime as the codon-level holdout test.

\paragraph{Family stratification.}
The family-stratified leave-one-code-out row contains $5$ families.  Their hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The important feature is not merely the near-unity aggregate value.  Four families are recorded at $1.0$, while the alternative-yeast family is lower but still high at $0.8333333333333334$.  This prevents the result from being presented as a single pooled statistic with no internal check.  The family row says that the local boundary pattern persists after stratification, with one visible family-level weakening rather than a complete collapse.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The same finite residue set is completely assigned by the module classification.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Each module has a predictable-or-boundary interpretation in the recorded run.  This is the finite bookkeeping that makes the family result interpretable: the observed family hit rates are not floating over an unspecified residue set, but over a completely assigned thirteen-codon boundary pattern.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The claim supported here is narrow.  A window-six codon coordinate system exposes a local tile boundary whose finite row set, spectral profile, randomization behavior, holdout recovery, family-stratified recovery, and module accounting are all mutually consistent at the code-read layer.  The curated standard-code table is the contact that supplies the labels.  It does not supply ribosomal realization, folded structure, energetics, organism-level function, or a universal mechanism.  Any statement crossing from codon-label readback to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires an additional contact of the appropriate kind.  Without that added contact, the scientifically warranted conclusion is only that the finite codon-label table contains a recoverable local boundary-tile pattern whose family-stratified leave-one-code-out behavior remains high above the recorded baseline.
