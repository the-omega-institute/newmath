\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery by reassignment family}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}

\paragraph{Finite claim.}
The claim tested here is a code-layer claim about the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The object of study is not translation dynamics and not protein structure.  It is the finite label table in which each codon has an amino-acid or stop label, together with the question of whether the window-six coordinate presentation exposes a stable local tile boundary when codon reassignment tables are withheld by family.

The distinguished residual set has cardinality $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the finite cube computation this same set is reported as a punctured cubical tile with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a list of exceptional labels.  It is a finite subcomplex-shaped residue inside the codon coordinate display.  The measured spectrum separates the observed $M$-region and the residual $R$-region: the row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the recorded radial polynomial residual
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This numerical residual is effectively zero at the precision of the run, so the spectral row is treated as a checked finite computation rather than as an informal visual impression.

\paragraph{Observed local shape.}
The shape row records an observed $M$-size of $20$.  Against $N=2000$ sampled comparison tables, using seed $42$, only $1$ exact shape match was found.  The resulting exact-shape frequency is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the relaxed high-overlap test, $25$ samples reached Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The distinct-sample generator required $57377$ attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the quoted frequencies should be read as finite sampled frequencies under the stated row generator, not as asymptotic distributional facts.

A second null row preserves amino-acid classes.  In that test, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-shape count is $0$, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These two null rows support the narrower reading that the local codon-coordinate tile is unusually organized relative to the tested finite generators.  They do not license any statement about cellular mechanism, folding, biochemical feasibility, or organism-level function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The global leave-one-code-out computation evaluates $23$ reassignment tables and $63$ held-out targets.  The baseline hit rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within $M\setminus T$ the hit rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9365079365079365,
\end{aligned}
$$
and within $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary allowance it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is recorded as the hit rate above baseline.  In plain terms, the held-out code-table labels are usually recovered by the local region and its boundary, whereas the finite baseline is much lower.

The family-stratified row refines this result into five reassignment families.  The reported family count is $5$, and the hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The alternative-yeast family is the only listed family below perfect recovery, and it remains far above the baseline rate $0.19821739130434785$.  The row therefore records a family-stable code-read pattern: withholding a reassignment family does not erase the local tile signal.

\paragraph{Codon-level module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, and $8$ cases with high closure coverage.  Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is also recorded as the recovery rate above threshold.  The module classification exhausts the residual set $R$: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the missing, extra, and unassigned lists are all empty.

The finite modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable member or a boundary member.  This is the local finite reason that the by-family result is meaningful: the code-table changes are not distributed as isolated unrelated codons, but fall into six named residual modules that cover the entire $13$-codon set.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code label table.  That contact supplies codon-to-label readback: codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels, and the finite experiment can compare withheld labels against the coordinate-derived local region and its boundary.  The contact does not observe ribosomal translation, three-dimensional protein fold, thermodynamic admissibility, phenotype, biochemical function, or a universal law of genetic codes.

Consequently the supported conclusion is deliberately narrow.  Window-six coordinates expose a statistically sharp and family-stable local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table, with residual set $R$ of size $13$, punctured-cube face-vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, exact-shape frequency $0.0005$, amino-acid-class lower-tail frequency $0.0035$, global leave-one-code-out boundary hit rate $0.9841269841269841$, and five-family recovery rates ranging from $0.8333333333333334$ to $1.0$.  Nothing in this row upgrades that code-layer observation into translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate contact that measures the stronger biological layer directly.
