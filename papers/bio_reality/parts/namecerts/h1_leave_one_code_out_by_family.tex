\subsection{Family-resolved leave-one-code-out readback for a local codon boundary tile}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and claim.}
This record concerns the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ equipped with a standard-code label readback.  Each codon is treated as a finite coordinate object before any biological interpretation is attached to it.  The biological contact used here is only the curated table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The claim tested in this row is narrower than a theory of translation: after a genetic-code table is withheld from the comparison pool, the local boundary tile in $Q_6$ still recovers most of the affected label targets above a baseline rate, and this recovery remains visible when the held-out code tables are grouped by family.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The observed members are $AAA$, $AGA$, $AGG$, $AUA$, $CUA$, $CUC$, $CUG$, $CUU$, $UAA$, $UAG$, $UCA$, $UGA$, and $UUA$.  The finite cubical check identifies the same set as a punctured-cube shaped region with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R| &= 13,\\
(f_0,f_1,f_2) &= (13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the coordinate object used by the leave-one-code-out readback.  It is not itself a molecular mechanism; it is a finite label geometry extracted from the code table contact.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model calibration.}
The row also carries spectral calibration for the same local shape.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,\\
p_{\mathrm{radial}} &= -6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The numerical residual is effectively zero at the stated tolerance, so the spectral row is internally consistent with the cubic relation used by the probe.  Against the shape null with $N=2000$ trials and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which should be read as a finite enumeration pressure rather than as a mechanistic biological claim.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$-size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, and the corresponding values were $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out rates.}
The code-level readback experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The local rule reached a hit rate of $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M\setminus T$, and the rate became $0.9841269841269841$ when the boundary contribution was included.  The above-baseline hit rate was also $0.9841269841269841$.  Thus the finite evidence says that the six-coordinate tile is informative for code-label recovery after withholding a code table; it does not say that the tile performs translation or selects functional proteins.

\paragraph{Family-resolved row.}
The family-resolved check contains $5$ families.  Four families achieved perfect above-baseline recovery in the reported statistic: bacterial-plastid $1.0$, candidate-other $1.0$, mitochondrial $1.0$, and nuclear-ciliate $1.0$.  The alternative-yeast family achieved $0.8333333333333334$.  In display form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334.
\end{aligned}
$$
The family split is therefore not a single-table coincidence in the recorded data: the readback survives across the reported family grouping, with one family lower but still above the baseline level.

\paragraph{Codon-module classification.}
The leave-one-codon-out module row accounts for all $13$ boundary codons.  The classification has $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are $AGR=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $AR\_Lys=\{AAA\}$, $CUN=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $Ile\_Met=\{AUA\}$, $SSN=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $Stop\_Trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  Every listed module has at least one predictable or boundary-supported member in the recorded classification.  The companion leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite result supports a code-layer statement: the standard-code label table, when read through the $Q_6$ coordinate display, exposes a small boundary region whose shape, spectrum, and leave-one-out behavior are reproducible in the reported rows.  The contact is still only a curated codon-to-label table.  It observes label assignments such as amino acid and stop symbols, not ribosomal realization, folding trajectory, physical admissibility, cellular function, or a general law of biological organization.  Any assertion about translation, protein structure, physical viability, biological function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that directly observes that higher layer.
