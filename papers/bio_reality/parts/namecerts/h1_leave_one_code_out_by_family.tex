\subsection{Familywise leave-one-code-out behavior for the codon window}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out-by-family}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon window.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, whose rows are six-bit displays of codons.  The observable readout is not translation itself, but the curated codon-to-label table assigning each codon an amino-acid or stop label.  The tested claim is that leave-one-code-out perturbations, grouped by code family, still land above the baseline recovery rate for the local boundary-tile pattern.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The internal coordinates support closure, spectrum, and relation calculations, while the label table remains an outside contact with the biological code layer.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has exactly thirteen codons.  The two finite rows for cardinality and punctured-cube shape agree on the same list:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\qquad |R|=13 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube row reports the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local object is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it also carries a small cell-combinatorial signature.  The module classification partitions these thirteen codons without omission or surplus.  The modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The row gives $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The spectral row records three scalar values attached to the local construction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\

The same row reports a radial cubic residual of $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, also listed as the cubic-polynomial residual, so the numerical relation is satisfied to near machine precision.  The shape-significance row used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was one exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and there were $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null row, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$.  The exact shape count was $0$, hence the exact-shape estimate was $0.0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving lower-tail probability $0.0035$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out family result.}
The familywise leave-one-code-out row evaluates five families.  The hit rate above baseline is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every recorded family lies above the reported baseline, and four of the five families are complete hits at the tested resolution.  The companion leave-one-code-out row gives $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the overall hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  These numbers support a local code-read statement: the finite window and boundary coordinates recover most withheld code-label targets relative to the baseline procedure, with the effect visible family by family rather than only after pooling.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out compatibility.}
The codon-level companion row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Its recovery-rate-above-threshold value is also $1.0$.  This does not turn the code table into a mechanism; it only says that, for the recorded codon-level deletion experiment, every recovered codon falls into the predictable or boundary-controlled part of the finite classification.  Together with the complete module assignment above, this makes the familywise result internally coherent: the same thirteen-codon boundary set supports both the family grouped code-table perturbation and the codon grouped module accounting.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact observes codon-to-label assignment at code-read resolution.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, molecular folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Therefore the correct conclusion is limited: the six-coordinate codon window exposes a stable local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table, and leave-one-code-out tests by family remain strongly above the baseline in the recorded finite rows.  No claim about translation, folded protein structure, physical realizability, function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows unless an independent contact at that higher layer is supplied.
