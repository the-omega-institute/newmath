\subsection{B$^*_{Q6}$ abundance residuals through yeast mRNA half-life}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-mrna-stability-mediation-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Question.}
This BioReality row asks whether the residual codon coordinates in $B^*_{Q6}$ retain an association with measured yeast protein abundance after the amino-acid quotient and ordinary sequence covariates have been imposed, and whether part of that association is statistically accounted for by a measured mRNA-stability readout.  The tested protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\ ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real yeast CDS codon counts.  The mediator is $\log_{10}(t_{1/2}\ \mathrm{minutes})$ from the Neymotin yeast 4tU metabolic-labeling mRNA half-life table.  The row is therefore a finite statistical decomposition over one organism, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, not a claim that codon choice causally sets transcript lifetime or protein output.

\paragraph{Rows and controls.}
The joined table contains $5085$ usable CDS/protein/mRNA-half-life rows.  The CDS-to-half-life join uses the CDS protein identifier after removing the $4932.$ prefix and matching it to the Neymotin \texttt{Syst} field.  The input audit reports $5815$ CDS rows with abundance data, $5379$ valid half-life records, a CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$, and $730$ CDS records skipped because no half-life match was present.  No records were lost for empty sense-codon counts, invalid length, missing protein identifier, nonpositive abundance, nonpositive half-life, duplicate \texttt{Syst}, or unparseable yeast ORF.  The half-life payload hash is \texttt{cee1ee9a816f0ec1e351bc2d63d4838ae603ea20566fc3ff529514a6ba6cf7d2}.

The residual design uses the nine coordinates
$K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}/\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f3\_\mathrm{stress}$.  The control matrix has $24$ columns: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 from real CDS codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The reported rank of the control matrix is $23$, leaving $5062$ residual degrees of freedom after controls.

\paragraph{Regression decomposition.}
Let $X$ denote the $9$-column residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate matrix, $M$ the residualized mRNA half-life mediator, and $P$ the residualized protein abundance readout.  The row computes the joint $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance association and its value after conditioning on the mRNA-stability mediator:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2761140373962526,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.24851799532219632,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{absorbed\ by}\ M} &= 0.027596042074056304.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting mediated fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid M}}{R^2_{QP}} &= 0.09994436477872035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, in this finite yeast table, roughly ten percent of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual association with measured protein abundance is removed when residualized log half-life is added as a one-dimensional mediator.  The remaining direct abundance association is still substantial: $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$.

\paragraph{Rank and energy diagnostics.}
The rank audit is nondegenerate for the stated finite model.  The $X$ design has rank $9$, the mediator has rank $1$, and the combined $(X,M)$ design has rank $10$.  The residual protein-abundance energy is $2041.4436456219576$, while the residual mediator energy is $229.94736158731112$.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates explain $563.6712471096034$ units of protein-abundance residual energy before conditioning on $M$, and the direct $X$ improvement given $M$ is $507.335482373205$.  The mediator explains $66.5878075095855$ units of protein-abundance energy on its own, its incremental improvement given $X$ is $10.2520427731871$, and $X$ explains $11.909960434207074$ units of mediator energy.  The stated degeneracy guard is $0.999999$, and the observed fits are well below a saturated surface.

\paragraph{Permutation calibration.}
The mediation fraction is compared to a deterministic residual-mediator permutation null with seed \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_mrna\_stability\_mediation\_powered|residual\_mediator\_permutation|perm\_index|index|n}.  The null mean is $0.00019674487584783138$, the null $95$th percentile is $0.0014571003414947088$, and the right-tail permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$.  The observed fraction $0.09994436477872035$ is therefore far above the finite permutation envelope for this design.  This supports a real statistical overlap between the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component and the yeast mRNA half-life readout in this row.

\paragraph{Relation to the mRNA-stability surface.}
The companion mRNA-stability row on the same yeast join reports $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$ for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals predicting residualized log half-life, with $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ on the same joined rows.  Its dominant mRNA-stability coordinate is $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, whereas the abundance surface on the same join is dominated by $f3\_\mathrm{stress}$, and the reported cosine between the two nine-coordinate surfaces is $0.8040935468384264$.  This explains why the mediation fraction is positive but incomplete: the half-life readout is aligned with much of the abundance-associated coordinate geometry, while it does not replace the full abundance residual pattern.

\paragraph{Coordinate-level context.}
A separate yeast coordinate-decomposition audit reports $n=1258$ in its held-out unified yeast context join, a $5$-fold design, $120$ permutations, rank $24$ for the sibling control basis, and all nine coordinate rows present.  In that audit, the source-real criterion is a held-out $R^2$ of each single residualized coordinate against the residualized abundance target compared with a deterministic label-permutation null.  The coordinates with real-source evidence include $K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f3\_\mathrm{stress}$.  This does not change the mediation statistic, but it gives a finite-row check that the abundance association is not merely a single opaque aggregate produced by the joint nine-dimensional fit.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite row supports the following bounded statement.  In a yeast CDS/protein/half-life join with $5085$ rows and the declared amino-acid, length, GC3, and $M$-density controls, the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate block has a measured association with residualized protein abundance, and about $0.09994436477872035$ of that association is statistically removed by adding residualized log mRNA half-life.  The permutation-calibrated mediation fraction is positive relative to the reported null envelope.  The correct biological reading is a variance-partition result for one organism and one half-life dataset: it is evidence for an mRNA-stability overlap with the codon-residual abundance component, not a proof of a molecular pathway.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
Nothing in this row establishes translation realization, ribosome-mediated elongation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality.  The mediator is an observational steady-state 4tU half-life estimate, and reverse or shared-confound explanations are not ruled out.  A translation claim would require a separate matched translation readout such as ribosome profiling or measured translation efficiency under its own controls.  A folding, physical, functional, or universal claim would require a separate layer-matched measurement and a new finite statistic for that layer.
