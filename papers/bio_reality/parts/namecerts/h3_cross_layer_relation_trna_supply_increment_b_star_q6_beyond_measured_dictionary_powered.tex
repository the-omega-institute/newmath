\subsection{Yeast tRNA-supply increment beyond the measured translation dictionary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-trna-supply-increment}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The finite object in this record is a yeast matched-row regression panel in which the target is log10 protein abundance after residualization against the sibling sequence controls.  The active join contains $3290$ proteins.  The baseline dictionary is the standardized residualized triple consisting of log10 measured translation efficiency, log10 mRNA stability, and turnover.  The extended design appends one additional standardized residualized column: the per-gene tAI geometric mean computed from a $61$-codon weight table.  The tRNA-supply readout has $268$ usable tAI records joined to the gene panel, $3290$ per-gene tAI values, and no zero raw codon weight replaced by the geometric-mean fill rule.  The fold rule is deterministic: active protein identifiers are sorted and then assigned by index modulo $5$.  The ridge parameter is $10^{-8}$, and the null is generated by $120$ deterministic SHA256 permutations of the gene-to-target labels while leaving the readouts and folds fixed.

\paragraph{Measured increment.}
The baseline held-out coefficient of determination is $0.4579960005021211$, while the full held-out coefficient of determination is $0.5186044342149576$.  Thus the observed held-out increment is
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{heldout}}
  &= R^2_{\mathrm{full}}-R^2_{\mathrm{base}} \\
  &= 0.5186044342149576-0.4579960005021211 \\
  &= 0.06060843371283653.
\end{aligned}$$
The same finite fit records the context-only in-sample increment as $0.06016972939113374$ and the tAI-alone held-out value as $0.33369275999817194$.  Under the fixed-fold permutation null, the null mean is $-0.0004019510944510909$, the null minimum is $-0.00510268312590334$, the null maximum is $0.0010291561244795133$, and the $95$th percentile is $0.0007222485676979806$.  The measured held-out increment is therefore larger than the displayed null envelope by roughly two orders of magnitude relative to the $95$th-percentile cutoff:
$$\begin{aligned}
0.06060843371283653 &> 0.0007222485676979806.
\end{aligned}$$
This is the finite-row content of the result: on this yeast join, a tAI-derived tRNA-supply coordinate adds held-out prediction beyond the measured translation-efficiency, mRNA-stability, and turnover dictionary after the shared sequence controls have been removed.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured translation and protein dictionary.}
The measured translation-protein table that supplies the dictionary boundary reports, for yeast, $5128$ valid translation-efficiency rows and $5815$ CDS-protein abundance rows before the three-way measured join.  In that measured Q--T--P decomposition, yeast has $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, and a measured mediated fraction of $0.4793069656345167$.  E. coli gives $S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$, and a measured mediated fraction of $0.04091158340950673$; human gives $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$, and a measured mediated fraction of $0.0027966485036786744$; danio gives $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.04034217614654355$, and a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, clipped to $0$ for the reported measured fraction.  These numbers show why the present tRNA-supply test is not a rephrasing of the measured translation term: it asks whether a modeled codon-adaptation supply coordinate still predicts the residual protein-abundance target after the measured dictionary columns have already been included.

\paragraph{Stability of the dictionary boundary.}
A separate joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition gives additional context for the same family of controls.  On the four-way join, yeast uses $4759$ rows, E. coli uses $2517$ rows, and human uses $11322$ rows.  The joint mediation fractions are $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, $0.2404669586626661$ for E. coli, and $0.16890712099923358$ for human, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In yeast the single-and-joint residual protein-abundance values are $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$.  These finite-row values support the local interpretation that measured TE and mRNA stability are substantial but incomplete descriptors of the residual protein-abundance relation.  The tRNA-supply increment is therefore read as an additional held-out predictive coordinate beyond that measured dictionary, not as a replacement for it.

\paragraph{What the record does not assert.}
The positive held-out increment is a bounded protein-abundance readout on a specific yeast join.  It is not a proof of translation dynamics, because the added column is a modeled tAI-derived supply coordinate rather than an observed ribosome-footprint measurement.  It is not a folding, localization, post-translational-modification, protein-stability, function, phenotype, mechanism, causation, adaptation, or universal-law claim.  It also does not say that any individual synonymous coordinate is physically realized in isolation.  Those stronger interpretations would require their own layer-matched contacts: direct translation measurements for translation realization, structure or stability assays for physical protein-state claims, functional or perturbational measurements for mechanism and function, and a broader design for general biological law.  The finite conclusion here is only that, after the declared residualization and cross-validated permutation test, the yeast tAI-supply column contributes $\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{heldout}}=0.06060843371283653$ beyond the measured dictionary whose baseline held-out value is $0.4579960005021211$.
