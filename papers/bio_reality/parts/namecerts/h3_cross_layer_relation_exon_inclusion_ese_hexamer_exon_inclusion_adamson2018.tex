\subsection{ESE-hexamer exon inclusion as a control surface for translation-boundary residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The record considered here is the finite table entry
$\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.exon\_inclusion.ese\_hexamer\_exon\_inclusion\_adamson2018}$.
Its directly verified row count is $n=1037$, and the measured collinearity between the ESE-sequence score and the MaxEnt splice score is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\Delta\mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta\mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
The runtime recorded for the finite computation is $4.569444656$ seconds.  This number is close to zero and has the sign opposite to what a simple one-coordinate splice-strength story would normally invite.  Thus the Adamson-style ESE-hexamer row is usable here as a bounded splice-layer reality contact, but not as a hidden translation measurement and not as evidence that a coding-window residual has crossed into a ribosome-occupancy readout.

\paragraph{Exon-inclusion contact.}
The Adamson row is an exon-inclusion contact, not a position-resolved ribosome contact.  Its $n=1037$ finite matrix tests whether an ESE-hexamer score moves together with a splice-strength score.  The observed value $-0.008429245619$ says that, on this row set, the two splice-layer summaries are essentially unaligned.  A separate exon-inclusion contact in the same finite inventory, the mouse VASTDB/MaxEnt cross-organism row, contains $n=50633$ entries and records that all tissue checks are positive.  These two splice-layer facts can coexist: the larger cross-organism splice readout reports a positive tissue-wise direction, whereas the Adamson ESE-hexamer row does not supply a collinear MaxEnt surrogate.  Neither fact is a ribosome-footprint statistic.

\paragraph{Translation-boundary test.}
The translation-boundary conjecture asks for a row of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-stress}}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_{\mathrm{position}}$ is a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency residual and $Z$ contains the stated organism, amino-acid, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA, position, rank, and baseline controls.  The present finite entry does not contain that joined matrix.  It contains a splice-layer ESE/MaxEnt comparison and a finite codon-coordinate inventory.  Therefore the admissible conclusion is negative in a precise sense: the ESE-hexamer row can certify a splice-contact boundary, but it cannot promote $f_3$-stress, wobble asymmetry, or quotient codon geometry into a translation readout.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded scientific statement.  A finite splice-layer table exists, and its measured ESE/MaxEnt collinearity is nearly zero on $1037$ rows.  A finite codon-coordinate inventory also exists, with a twenty-codon $M$ set, an eighteen-codon saturated $f_3$ residual set, and a ten-row quotient surface.  What is absent is the same-organism, same-CDS, same-window table joining ordered codons, wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA abundance, and codon-indexed ribosome footprints.  Without that row, no translation realization follows.  The same absence also blocks claims about folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, system phenotype, and universality.  Those claims require their own layer-matched contacts and cannot be read out from this exon-inclusion row or from the codon-coordinate geometry alone.
