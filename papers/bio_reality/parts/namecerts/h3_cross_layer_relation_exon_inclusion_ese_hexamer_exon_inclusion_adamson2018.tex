\subsection{ESE-hexamer inclusion as a bounded cross-layer row}
\label{subsec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}

\paragraph{Finite certificate.}
This record fixes a finite exon-inclusion surface rather than a general rule about splicing or translation.  The observed table has $n=1037$ rows and compares two exon-local scores on the same row set: an ESE-hexamer change score and a MaxEnt splice-context change score.  The computed finite-row collinearity is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\Delta ESE_{\mathrm{seq}},\Delta MaxEnt)=-0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the certified datum is not a positive alignment between the two scoring systems.  It is a near-orthogonality certificate for this row set: the ESE-hexamer coordinate can be kept as a separate exon-inclusion readout coordinate, while the MaxEnt splice-context coordinate cannot be silently substituted for it.  The runtime attached to the run is $4.569444656$ seconds, so the row certificate is small enough to be treated as a directly auditable finite computation rather than a population-level biological law.

\paragraph{Coordinate boundary.}
The biological object of the surrounding packet is a residual $f_3$-stress coordinate in coding sequence windows, tested only after amino-acid projection and after controls for organism, $GC3$, length, $M$-density, mRNA level, and position or amino-acid bins.  The exon-inclusion table gives a useful contrast case: a same-row finite comparison may be meaningful even when the signed association is essentially zero.  The relevant BEDC-side codon-topology memory is finite.  The saturated $f_3$ residual set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas the larger $M=\mathrm{Med}_R\cup\{ACA,ACG\}$ list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two codons $ACA$ and $ACG$ therefore remain outside the displayed saturated $f_3$ residual list in this finite certificate.  The boundary component reported as $f_3$ is empty at the listed boundary, so no additional boundary codon is being smuggled into the translation interpretation.

\paragraph{Quotient geometry.}
The quotient geometry used by the residual coordinate is also finite.  The recorded $M$ vector is $(20,38,26,8,1)$, and the quotient vector is $(10,14,6,1)$.  The quotient image has size $10$ and is represented by the ten binary rows

These numbers justify treating the coordinate as a controlled finite residual coordinate.  They do not, by themselves, give an exon-inclusion mechanism, a translation mechanism, or a downstream protein-state claim.  They only state which finite combinatorial rows are present after the quotienting operation.

\paragraph{Readout interpretation.}
The Adamson-style exon-inclusion row check says that ESE-hexamer and MaxEnt coordinates are not collinear on the certified table.  The sign and magnitude of $-0.008429245619$ support separation rather than promotion.  In particular, a positive exon-inclusion claim would require a row-level response variable for inclusion, a declared control set, and a survival statistic for the ESE coordinate after simpler splice-context baselines.  The present certificate is narrower: it permits the ESE-hexamer coordinate to remain available as a distinct row coordinate, and it blocks any claim that the MaxEnt coordinate already explains the ESE direction on this table.

\paragraph{Comparison with the translation boundary.}
A separate structural-order run illustrates why this chapter keeps the layer boundary explicit.  In that run the primary ramp window has $n=50$, with weighted $f_3$ ramp held-out increment $0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, and paired ramp-minus-body increment $0.001378137149572267$.  Its sign-flip values are $0.0478515625$ for the ramp, $0.54296875$ for the body, and $0.05859375$ for the paired ramp-minus-body comparison.  The recorded conclusion is ramp-positive but not localized.  This is a useful local analogy: a coordinate may show a bounded finite signal while still failing the stronger localization or mediation gate.  The same discipline applies here.  The exon-inclusion row table does not license a translation-readout claim, and the $f_3$ residual coordinate does not become a splicing mechanism merely because both are finite BEDC-style row coordinates.

\paragraph{Null and no-promotion clause.}
The admissible statement is therefore local.  On $1037$ exon rows, the ESE-hexamer coordinate and the MaxEnt splice-context coordinate have computed collinearity $-0.008429245619$, so the two coordinates should be kept distinct in any exon-inclusion analysis.  Nothing in this certificate establishes position-resolved ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts: for translation, same-organism and same-CDS codon counts, wobble or adaptation weights, matched mRNA controls, and measured ribosome-footprint or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency rows; for protein or phenotype layers, their own matched readouts and controls.  If those rows are absent, the correct status is a bounded coordinate certificate with a null promotion beyond the row statistic reported here.
