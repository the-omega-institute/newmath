\subsection{ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion collinearity and the translation-readout boundary}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-exon-inclusion-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record isolates a cross-layer question whose observed row surface is splice-regulatory rather than translational. The admissible table is finite: it has $n=1037$ Adamson-style exon-inclusion rows, and the recorded scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{corr}(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
The near-zero value is the chapter-level fact. It says that, on this finite surface, the ESE-hexamer perturbation coordinate and the MaxEnt splice-site coordinate do not form a strong collinear axis. Consequently the ESE signal can be treated as a distinct finite splice-regulatory coordinate for this dataset, but only at the level of exon-inclusion readout. The runtime recorded for the finite computation is $4.569444656$ seconds, so the row object is not a theoretical proxy for an unavailable experiment; it is a completed finite table with a concrete cardinality, a concrete scalar relation, and a fixed computation trace.

\paragraph{Local coordinate separation.}
The biological coordinate in this chapter is not protein output and not a translation mechanism. It is an exon-inclusion coordinate indexed by an ESE-hexamer perturbation surface and compared with a splice-site-strength surface. The measured scalar $-0.008429245619$ gives the local relation between the two splice-layer summaries. Since the magnitude is small, the finite row data do not support a reduction of the ESE coordinate to the MaxEnt coordinate. The correct readback is therefore bounded:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq} &\not\equiv \Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt},\\
|\operatorname{corr}(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt})|&=0.008429245619,\\
 n&=1037.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a relation statement, not a mechanism statement. It permits a finite-row claim that the two splice-layer axes are empirically non-collinear in the recorded table. It does not license any assertion about translation efficiency, ribosome pausing, folding, protein abundance, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Comparison with other finite-row surfaces.}
The same audit table contains several other bounded row counts, and they sharpen the scope of this chapter. A splice MaxEnt cross-organism mouse VASTDB surface has $n=50633$ and records that every tissue direction in its finite summary is positive. An alternative-polyadenylation PAS-strength surface has $24740$ genes and $296950$ PAS rows. A thermal-stability surface has $8092$ protein rows and a UniProt sequence match rate of $0.963104$. These are separate readout layers. Their row counts show that the BioReality ledger is not restricted to the $1037$ Adamson exon-inclusion rows, but none of those other surfaces supplies the missing mediation needed to promote the ESE-hexamer relation into translation, folding, function, or organismal phenotype.

\paragraph{Position-resolved translation boundary.}
The linked $f_3$ translation-boundary conjecture requires a different finite table: same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, bounded wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint occupancy. That table is not supplied by the Adamson exon-inclusion fact. Therefore the only admissible translation statement here is negative-scope: an ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion relation can coexist with codon-side coordinates, but it is not itself a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy statistic. A future translation row would need a non-vacuous statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-stress}}(\mathrm{position\ resolved\ ribosome\ occupancy}\mid Z)>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes amino-acid composition or position bins, organism, $\mathrm{GC3}$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, mRNA abundance, rank sufficiency, coverage, mappability, and simpler-baseline ablations. In the present finite data, that statistic is absent. Hence the Adamson scalar is ready for a splice-layer chapter, while the translation-readout promotion remains unavailable.

\paragraph{Non-promotion boundary.}
The structural-order ramp surface illustrates the same discipline numerically. Its primary ramp size is $50$, with five folds and minimum joined count $300$. The weighted held-out $R^2$ increment for the $f_3$ ramp is $0.0011165640103326878$, while the body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$ and the ramp-minus-body increment is $0.001378137149572267$. The ramp sign-flip probability is $0.0478515625$, the body sign-flip probability is $0.54296875$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability is $0.05859375$. Its local classification is \emph{ramp positive but not localized}. This comparison is not imported as evidence for ESE biology; it is used to keep the logic honest. Even when a nearby finite surface has small positive translation-adjacent increments, the claim still cannot move into protein abundance, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal law without a layer-matched contact and the appropriate controlled statistic.

\paragraph{Chapter claim.}
The ready claim is therefore deliberately narrow. In the $1037$-row Adamson ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion surface, the ESEseq perturbation coordinate is almost uncorrelated with the MaxEnt splice-site coordinate, with observed collinearity $-0.008429245619$. This supports a finite splice-layer non-collinearity record: ESE-hexamer perturbation and splice-site-strength perturbation should not be collapsed into a single explanatory axis for this row table. The same chapter explicitly refuses stronger interpretations. No translation realization follows from the ESE scalar; no ribosome-occupancy, protein-abundance, folding, physical-admissibility, functional, mechanistic, phenotypic, or universal biological conclusion follows unless a separate finite contact supplies that layer and survives the relevant controls.
