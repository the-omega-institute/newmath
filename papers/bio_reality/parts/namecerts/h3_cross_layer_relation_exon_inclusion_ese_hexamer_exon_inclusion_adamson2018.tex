\subsection{ESE-Hexamer Perturbation and Exon-Inclusion Readback}
\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}

\paragraph{Finite row under test.}
This record treats the Adamson-style exon-inclusion surface as a finite cross-layer table rather than as a mechanism claim. The carrier is a row set of size $n=1037$ in which each row has a local exon or minigene perturbation coordinate, an ESE-hexamer score displacement, a splice-site model displacement, and an exon-inclusion readout. The finite statistic recorded for this chapter is the collinearity between the ESE-sequence displacement and the MaxEnt splice-model displacement:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{collinearity}(\Delta ESE_{seq},\Delta MaxEnt) &= -0.008429245619,\\
 n &= 1037,\\
 t_{runtime} &= 4.569444656\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The value is close to zero and slightly negative, so the finite row does not support a collapse of ESE-hexamer response into the MaxEnt splice-site coordinate. It is instead a separation certificate: the ESE perturbation axis and the splice-site-scoring axis are recorded as distinct finite observables over the same exon-inclusion table.

\paragraph{Local biological interpretation.}
The admissible biological reading is narrow. The row set supports an exon-inclusion comparison in which ESE-hexamer perturbation and splice-site model displacement can be placed side by side without becoming the same coordinate. A near-zero collinearity means that a signal in one coordinate should not be automatically read as a signal in the other. In particular, this chapter may say that the finite table contains a measured inclusion-layer surface with $1037$ rows and that the two recorded sequence-derived predictors are almost uncorrelated on that surface. It may not say that the ESE coordinate is a universal splicing mechanism, nor that MaxEnt score change captures all exon-recognition information.

\paragraph{Cross-checking against neighboring finite surfaces.}
The exon-inclusion row has a useful contrast with the larger splice-readout table in which the mouse VASTDB splice-MaxEnt cross-organism surface has $n=50633$ rows and reports \texttt{all\_tissue\_positive} as true. The two exon-inclusion records therefore occupy different finite regimes: the Adamson-style ESE table is small enough to expose coordinate non-collinearity directly, while the VASTDB-linked splice table records a much larger tissue-positive splice-score association. A separate alternative-polyadenylation surface has $24740$ genes and $296950$ PAS rows; it is relevant only as a scale comparison, not as evidence for exon inclusion.

\paragraph{Code-topology coordinates kept separate.}
The same audit bundle also contains codon-topology facts, but they do not enlarge the exon-inclusion claim. The saturated $f_3$ residue list is

&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},

$$
whereas the larger $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{ACA,ACG\}$ list has $20$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.

The quotient geometry record gives $|\pi_M|=10$, an $M$-vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, and a quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$. These are finite coding-sequence geometry facts. They are not exon-inclusion measurements and they are not translation measurements. In this chapter they function only as a warning against coordinate promotion: a codon quotient, an $f_3$ residual, and an exon-inclusion table are different finite surfaces unless a same-row data join explicitly connects them.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The chapter is therefore ready only for the following bounded assertion: in the $1037$-row exon-inclusion table, ESE-hexamer displacement and MaxEnt displacement are empirically separated by the recorded collinearity $-0.008429245619$, so ESE-associated inclusion response should be tracked as its own finite coordinate. No translation readout, protein abundance, folding state, physical admissibility, biochemical function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this table. Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite rows, controls, rank check, and null comparison.

\paragraph{Comparison with a translation-bound record.}
The structural-order ramp packet in the same audit bundle illustrates why this boundary matters. There the primary ramp size is $50$, the fold count is $5$, and the recorded conclusion is \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}; its weighted held-out increments are $0.0011165640103326878$ for the $f_3$ ramp and $-0.00026157313923957926$ for the $f_3$ body, with sign-flip probabilities $0.0478515625$ and $0.54296875$ respectively. Those numbers belong to a translation-adjacent structural-order test, not to this exon-inclusion chapter. They are cited here only to show the local discipline: each finite surface is interpreted at the layer where its rows actually live. The Adamson ESE chapter remains an exon-inclusion coordinate-separation record, not a downstream translation or protein-state result.
