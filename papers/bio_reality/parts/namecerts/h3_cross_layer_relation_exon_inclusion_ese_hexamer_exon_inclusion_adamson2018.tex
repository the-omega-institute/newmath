\subsection{ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion row as a cross-layer finite contact}\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The exon-inclusion record considered here is a finite table entry for a splicing-layer contact, not a translation-output claim.  Its row key is
$\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.exon\_inclusion.ese\_hexamer\_exon\_inclusion\_adamson2018}$, and the measured finite payload is the Adamson-style ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion comparison with $n=1037$ assayed rows.  The recorded scalar check is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{corr}(\Delta\mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta\mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619,
\qquad
n=1037,
\qquad
\mathrm{runtime}=4.569444656\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This value is effectively centered at zero for the particular pair of finite summaries named in the row.  The admissible reading is therefore narrow: the ESE-hexamer coordinate and the MaxEnt-style splice-score coordinate are present in the same finite audit surface, but this row does not show a positive collinearity between those two deltas.  It is still a useful cross-layer entry because it records a concrete negative or near-null alignment check rather than leaving the exon-inclusion interface at the level of vocabulary alone.

\paragraph{Coordinate discipline.}
The row is interpreted as a splicing contact whose carrier consists of finite exon or minigene entries, hexamer-derived enhancer scores, a splice-score comparator, and an inclusion readout.  The biological object is exon inclusion under a finite sequence-derived regulatory coordinate, not codon-mediated translation.  The local statement is that an ESE-hexamer change can be placed beside a splice-model change and an exon-inclusion readout in a finite matrix, after which the observed collinearity between the two named score changes is approximately zero.  Thus the row supports the existence of a bounded splicing assay surface, while refusing the stronger assertion that the two score systems are interchangeable.

\paragraph{Readout and null behavior.}
The primary readout for this row is the finite scalar $-0.008429245619$ on $1037$ entries.  Since the sign is negative and the magnitude is small, the row should be read as a boundary result: the named ESE and splice-score deltas can be co-indexed, but this finite check does not justify a positive monotone relation between them.  A defensible local statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{ESE}}(\mathrm{exon\ inclusion}\mid Z)
&=\mathrm{finite\ inclusion\ gain\ after\ sequence,\ splice\ score,\ and\ assay\ controls},\\
\mathrm{row\ status}
&=\mathrm{coindexed\ contact\ present,\ positive\ collinearity\ absent\ for\ }\Delta\mathrm{ESEseq}\mathrm{\ versus\ }\Delta\mathrm{MaxEnt}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The displayed expression is a local bookkeeping convention for the paper narrative; the actual finite scalar recorded here is the observed collinearity value above.  If a later inclusion matrix supplies stronger controls or a direct inclusion residual, it must be evaluated as a new finite row rather than inferred from this near-zero comparison.

\paragraph{Separation from translation and downstream layers.}
The splicing row does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  In particular, modeled tAI or stAI weights, codon-window geometry, $f_3$-stress residuals, and position-resolved ribosome-occupancy contact names are not exon-inclusion measurements.  Conversely, an ESE-hexamer inclusion surface is not a ribosome-occupancy surface.  Any claim about position-resolved translation must attach same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA abundance, and ribosome-footprint occupancy, then survive organism, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA, coverage, mappability, rank, and simpler-baseline checks.  Without that separate contact, the present chapter remains a splicing-layer record only.
