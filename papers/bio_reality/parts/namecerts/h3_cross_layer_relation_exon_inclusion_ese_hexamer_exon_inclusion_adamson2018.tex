\subsection{ESE hexamer exon inclusion and the translation-boundary audit}
\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row under audit.}
The Adamson-style exon-inclusion row is a finite splice-regulatory contact, not a translation realization result. Its observed table contains $n=1037$ exon-window records and the measured collinearity between the ESE-sequence score difference and the MaxEnt splice-score difference is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
This value is effectively centered at zero at the level of the finite row. The row therefore certifies a bounded splice-layer comparison: ESE hexamer perturbation and splice-site MaxEnt scoring can be placed on the same exon-inclusion audit surface, but the specific paired score direction does not itself supply a positive cross-layer mechanism. The runtime for the recorded computation was $4.569444656$ seconds, which is relevant only as an artifact-scale datum and not as biological support.

\paragraph{Local coordinate separation.}
The chapter keeps three objects apart. First, the exon-inclusion surface is a splice-context row indexed by exon-window measurements. Second, the codon-topology and $f_3$ residual coordinates belong to a coding-sequence quotient analysis, where the verified $M$ list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its $f_3$-saturation row over $R$ omits $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Third, a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy claim would require a same-organism, same-CDS, same-window translation readout. The present exon-inclusion row does not contain that joined translation matrix. The verified quotient geometry has $\pi_M$ size $10$, $M$-vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, and quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$; these numbers constrain the finite coordinate background, but they do not change the splice-row verdict.

\paragraph{Readback and null comparison.}
The finite Adamson row supports a narrow readback: compare exon-inclusion-associated ESE hexamer signal against a splice-site MaxEnt score channel in the same finite audit surface. With $n=1037$ and $\rho=-0.008429245619$, the admissible conclusion is not that ESE information is absent, but that this specific collinearity channel is not a positive mechanism certificate. A stronger splice claim would need a declared response variable, held-out directionality, covariates for exon length, GC composition, splice-site strength, motif density, transcript context, and a rank check for the design matrix. A stronger translation claim would need position-resolved ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency rows joined to ordered CDS coordinates, matched mRNA abundance, codon counts, bounded wobble weights, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.

\paragraph{Comparison with other finite surfaces.}
The surrounding audit contains other finite rows that are not substitutes for this claim. The cross-organism mouse VASTDB splice-MaxEnt surface has $n=50633$ and records all tissue directions as positive, but that is a separate splice-readout surface. The alternative-polyadenylation PAS table records $24740$ genes and $296950$ PAS rows, which belongs to a 3-prime processing layer rather than exon inclusion. The protein thermal-stability row has $8092$ proteins and a UniProt sequence match rate of $0.963104$, but it tests a protein-state readout, not exon inclusion. The structural-order ramp audit records a primary ramp length $50$, five folds, minimum joined count $300$, and a verdict of \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}; its ramp statistic has held-out increment $0.0011165640103326878$, body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, and paired ramp-minus-body increment $0.001378137149572267$, with sign-flip probabilities $0.0478515625$, $0.54296875$, and $0.05859375$ respectively. Those values illustrate how a positive finite signal can still fail a localization gate, but they do not repair the near-zero ESE--MaxEnt collinearity in the Adamson row.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a splice-layer finite contact with a sharply bounded interpretation. The Adamson row may be cited for the existence of a same-row ESE-hexamer and exon-inclusion audit surface, and for the measured near-zero collinearity with MaxEnt splice-score change. It may not be used to infer translation, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular mechanism, organismal phenotype, or universality. Any such assertion requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. In particular, $f_3$-stress residual geometry, wobble-weight asymmetry, codon-window counts, or the mere naming of a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy contact cannot promote this splice row into a translation-readout claim.
