\subsection{ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion row and its separation from translation claims}\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The Adamson-style exon-inclusion row is a same-record comparison between two splice-regulatory coordinates on $n=1037$ assayed exon records.  The measured scalar is the collinearity between the ESE-sequence perturbation coordinate and the MaxEnt splice-site coordinate,
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \rho(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619,
  \qquad n=1037.
\end{aligned}
$$
The computation completed in $4.569444656$ seconds under run identifier $8c0a6b9aeaf4$.  This number is close to zero, so the finite row should be read as a separation result rather than as evidence that the ESE-hexamer coordinate is a disguised splice-site-strength coordinate.  The admissible observation is only that, on this finite panel, the local ESE-hexamer perturbation score and the MaxEnt splice-site perturbation score do not move collinearly in any material way.

\paragraph{Local certificate.}
The biological carrier for this chapter is the finite exon table indexed by assayed exon, local hexamer perturbation, and splice-site scoring coordinate.  The row does not require a population-level law: it records a bounded comparison between two features inside one curated exon-inclusion experiment.  The object certified here is therefore not exon inclusion in general, but the finite statement that the ESE-sequence coordinate has a measured near-orthogonal relation to the MaxEnt coordinate on the available $1037$ rows.  Equivalently, the data allow the paper to keep an ESE-hexamer coordinate as a separate exon-regulatory coordinate, because the observed cross-coordinate collinearity is $-0.008429245619$ rather than a large positive or negative alignment.

\paragraph{Relation asserted.}
The chapter-level assertion is the following finite-row statement.  On the Adamson exon-inclusion table, the ESE-hexamer perturbation coordinate and the MaxEnt splice-site perturbation coordinate have measured collinearity $-0.008429245619$ across $1037$ rows.  This supports a local cross-layer bookkeeping decision: an ESE-hexamer coordinate may be retained as a distinct exon-inclusion surface, not collapsed into splice-site-strength scoring.  The assertion is bounded by the exact columns used in the row.  It does not say that ESE hexamers are the sole determinant of exon inclusion, that MaxEnt scores are irrelevant, or that the observed near-zero collinearity must recur in every organism, tissue, exon class, or experimental design.

\paragraph{Null and break conditions.}
The row would stop supporting the separation claim if the joined table were replaced by unmatched rows, if the assayed exon count no longer matched the recorded $n=1037$, if the two perturbation columns were not computed on the same exon records, or if a recomputation produced a materially large absolute collinearity after the declared controls and filtering.  A downstream claim would also fail if the finite row were used as evidence for translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  None of those quantities is measured by this exon-inclusion table.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
The certified content of this chapter is a finite comparison between ESE-hexamer perturbation and MaxEnt splice-site perturbation on $1037$ exon records.  No translation-readout statement follows from it.  No codon-window residual, ribosome-occupancy, tAI, stAI, protein-abundance, folding, physical-admissibility, biological-function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal-law conclusion follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and a declared statistic on those rows.  In particular, the $f_3$ codon lists, the empty $f_3$ boundary, the quotient face vectors, and the structural-order ramp numbers are not evidence for exon-inclusion mechanism; they only help keep the coordinate ledger separated across layers.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite Adamson exon-inclusion row is ready as a bounded BioReality record.  Its positive content is the near-zero measured collinearity $-0.008429245619$ between $\Delta\mathrm{ESEseq}$ and $\Delta\mathrm{MaxEnt}$ over $1037$ rows.  Its negative content is equally important: the row licenses separation of the ESE-hexamer coordinate from splice-site-strength scoring in this finite assay, but it does not license any promotion to translation, structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.
