\subsection{ESE hexamer scoring and exon-inclusion response in the Adamson finite row}
\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}

\paragraph{Finite claim surface.}
This record concerns a bounded cross-layer relation between an exon-local sequence score and an exon-inclusion readout.  The finite row is not a claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  It is a same-layer splicing test: an ESE-hexamer coordinate is compared against an exon-inclusion response surface in the Adamson finite panel.  The audited table contains $n=1037$ rows, and the computed scalar comparison between the ESE-sequence differential and the splice-model differential is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{collinearity}(\Delta ESE_{\mathrm{seq}},\Delta MaxEnt) = -0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
The runtime recorded for this finite computation is $4.569444656$ seconds.  The sign and magnitude matter: the row supports the existence of a checked finite comparison, but it does not support a positive collinearity statement.  In particular, this entry is best read as a calibrated boundary on an ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion relation rather than as evidence that the two score changes move in the same direction.

\paragraph{Admissible mathematical reading.}
Let $W$ denote the finite set of assayed exon windows in the Adamson row, with $|W|=1037$.  For each $w \in W$, write $e(w)$ for the ESE-hexamer sequence-score contrast and $m(w)$ for the matched splice-model contrast.  The audited scalar is the empirical collinearity summary of the paired vector $\{(e(w),m(w)) : w \in W\}$.  The finite statement is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
W &\text{ is finite},\\
|W| &= 1037,\\
C_{ESE,MaxEnt}(W) &= -0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a real finite-row certificate: the two coordinates are present on the same row set and the numerical relation has been computed.  It is not a structural theorem about all exons, all cell types, or all splicing contexts.  It also does not identify a causal splice factor, an RNA-binding mechanism, or a universal grammar of exon recognition.

\paragraph{Downstream boundary.}
A nearby structural-order audit reports a primary ramp length of $50$, five folds, a minimum joined count of $300$, and the verdict $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$. \paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row conclusion is therefore narrow and explicit.  The Adamson ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion table supplies a checked same-row comparison on $1037$ observations, with $C_{ESE,MaxEnt}=-0.008429245619$.  This certifies a computed splicing-coordinate relation and simultaneously blocks a stronger positive-alignment reading.  Any claim above this level would need a separate row-matched contact at the relevant layer: measured ribosome occupancy for translation, protein abundance for protein-output mediation, structural or stability measurements for folding and physical admissibility, perturbation or functional assays for mechanism and function, and population or system assays for phenotype or universality.
