\subsection{Finite exon-inclusion boundary for ESE hexamer and splice-strength coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The finite observation attached to this record is the Adamson-style exon-inclusion table indexed by assayed sequence rows with two local splice-regulatory coordinates: the ESEseq-derived exon-splicing-enhancer displacement and the MaxEnt splice-strength displacement.  The checked finite surface contains $n=1037$ rows.  On this table the measured collinearity between the two displacement coordinates is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{corr}(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt}) &= -0.008429245619,
\end{aligned}
$$
with a recorded runtime of $4.569444656$ seconds for the finite computation.  The number is effectively centered at zero at this resolution: the ESE-hexamer coordinate and the splice-strength coordinate are both exon-inclusion-facing descriptors, but this row check does not allow one to replace the other.  The chapter therefore treats the Adamson table as a finite separation witness between two same-layer splicing readbacks, not as a mechanism by which a codon-topological or translation coordinate becomes an exon-inclusion law.

\paragraph{Coordinate relation.}
The admissible relation is a bounded cross-layer bookkeeping statement: an exon-inclusion readout may be compared with local enhancer-hexamer and splice-strength coordinates only on the assayed rows, and a further promotion to a translation, protein, folding, physical, functional, or organismal layer would require a separate matched measurement at that layer.  In the present row set the ESE and MaxEnt coordinates are nearly uncorrelated, so the finite table supports a local non-collapse statement rather than a universal biological rule.  A positive exon-inclusion conclusion would have to be phrased as a row-level survival statistic after the relevant sequence, splice-site, position, and library controls; this finite entry records only the measured relation between the two supplied displacement coordinates.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
The nearby translation-boundary evidence remains explicitly limited.  In the structural-order ramp computation, the primary ramp window $50$ gave a weighted $f_3$ ramp held-out increment $0.0011165640103326878$, a body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, and a ramp-minus-body increment $0.001378137149572267$.  The corresponding sign-flip probabilities were $0.0478515625$ for the ramp, $0.54296875$ for the body, and $0.05859375$ for the paired ramp-minus-body comparison.  Its recorded conclusion was ramp-positive but not localized.  These numbers show why the present chapter keeps the layers separated: even a finite translation-adjacent signal with a positive ramp entry is not automatically a localized structural, splicing, or mechanistic result.  It also does not supply the missing same-organism, same-CDS, same-window ribosome-footprint matrix required for an $f_3$-stress position-resolved translation claim.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For the Adamson exon-inclusion finite row, the safe statement is therefore narrow.  The table supplies $1037$ assayed rows and a near-zero ESEseq--MaxEnt collinearity of $-0.008429245619$.  This supports a chapter-local distinction between enhancer-hexamer displacement and splice-strength displacement in an exon-inclusion assay.  It does not support translation realization, ribosome pausing, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such assertion would need an additional matched contact: for translation, a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency residual joined to codon counts and matched mRNA; for protein output, a matched proteomics readout; for structure or function, a layer-specific structure, perturbation, or assay table.  Until those finite rows are present, the exon-inclusion entry remains a bounded splicing-coordinate record with a measured same-table separation and no downstream promotion.
