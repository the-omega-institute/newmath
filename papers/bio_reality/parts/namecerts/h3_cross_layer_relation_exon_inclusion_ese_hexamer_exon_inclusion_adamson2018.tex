\subsection{ESE hexamer and splice-window separation}
\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-exon-inclusion-adamson2018}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The finite record considered here is the splice-layer table keyed by the claim identifier \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.exon\_inclusion.ese\_hexamer\_exon\_inclusion\_adamson2018}.  Its measured row count is $n=1037$, and the computed collinearity between the ESEseq exon-inclusion perturbation coordinate and the MaxEnt splice-score perturbation coordinate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{collinear}(\Delta ESEseq,\Delta MaxEnt)=-0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
The run certificate attached to this row is \texttt{8c0a6b9aeaf4}, with reported runtime $4.569444656$ seconds.  Thus the chapter is not using a symbolic splice story: it is using a finite matrix whose primary numerical outcome is a near-zero signed association between two splice-window coordinates on $1037$ assayed rows.

\paragraph{Coordinate being tested.}
The biological coordinate is an exon-inclusion surface built from ESE hexamer perturbation scores and a matched splice-model perturbation coordinate.  The first coordinate records how hexamer-level exon-splicing-enhancer information changes the observed or assayed inclusion tendency.  The second coordinate records the corresponding change in a splice-strength model.  The row is therefore a cross-layer relation only in a narrow sense: it compares an enhancer-sequence readout to a splice-window score readout.  It does not compare codon topology to translation, ribosome flow, protein abundance, folding, stability, localization, phenotype, or fitness.

\paragraph{Numerical verdict.}
The finite statistic is close to zero, with negative sign and magnitude below $10^{-2}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left| -0.008429245619 \right| &= 0.008429245619,\\
0.008429245619 &< 0.01.
\end{aligned}
$$
For this row, the practical reading is separation rather than reinforcement.  The ESEseq perturbation coordinate and the MaxEnt splice perturbation coordinate are not behaving as the same one-dimensional signal on the measured table.  A positive exon-inclusion statement would therefore need to be carried by the ESE hexamer readout itself, or by a separately specified inclusion model, rather than by treating the MaxEnt perturbation coordinate as an interchangeable proxy.

In this chapter they serve only as a finite coordinate background: the splice row remains a splice row.

\paragraph{Admissible reading.}
The admissible conclusion is that the Adamson-style ESE hexamer exon-inclusion finite row supports a bounded splice-layer separation claim.  On $1037$ rows, the ESEseq perturbation direction and the MaxEnt perturbation direction have collinearity $-0.008429245619$, so the measured table does not justify collapsing those two coordinates into a single splice signal.  The result is compatible with the view that enhancer-hexamer effects and splice-strength-model effects can occupy different local directions in a finite exon-inclusion assay.

\paragraph{Local null boundary.}
This chapter does not infer translation, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, or universality from the ESE hexamer row.  None of those conclusions follows from the splice-window statistic above.  A later claim at any of those layers would need its own matched biological table, its own row keys, and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, modeled codon weights, codon-window residuals, or quotient geometry cannot be promoted into a translation mechanism unless a separate position-resolved ribosome or translation-efficiency contact is joined and survives the declared controls.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The record would fail its stated interpretation if the near-zero collinearity were described as evidence that ESEseq and MaxEnt are the same signal, or if the splice-window comparison were used as support for translation or protein-layer claims.  It would also fail if the codon-topology lists were treated as observations of splicing or ribosome behavior.  The finite-row result is narrower: it records a measured splice-coordinate comparison with $n=1037$ and a near-zero signed association between two exon-inclusion-related perturbation coordinates.
