\subsection{ESE Hexamer Inclusion and the Cross-Layer Readout Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-ese-hexamer-adamson}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row.}  The finite record for this chapter is the Adamson-style exon-inclusion table keyed by an exon-level sequence feature and a matched splice-model readout.  Its measured row count is $n=1037$, and the recorded scalar relation between the ESE-sequence direction and the MaxEnt-splice direction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{collinearity}(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
The runtime attached to this finite computation is $4.569444656$ seconds.  Thus the observed contact is not a large positive alignment between the two coordinates; it is a near-zero, slightly negative finite-row relation on the available panel.  The record is therefore useful as a boundary example: an exon-inclusion table can be joined to a sequence-derived splice-readout coordinate, but the joined table does not by itself license a positive cross-layer claim.

\paragraph{Coordinate content.}  The admissible row has three pieces.  First, it contains an exon-local sequence coordinate whose biological interpretation is restricted to splice-regulatory sequence evidence.  Second, it contains a matched splice-readout coordinate, here represented by the MaxEnt direction used in the finite computation.  Third, it contains the measured inclusion-facing comparison through $\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq}$ versus $\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt}$.  The numerical result says that, on these $1037$ rows, the two directions do not move together in a way that would support a monotone ESE-to-splice-strength promotion.  The sign and magnitude matter: the scalar $-0.008429245619$ is compatible with a null or mismatch reading, not with a powered positive-survival statement.

Separately, the quotient-geometry record has $\lvert \pi_M\rvert=10$, with $M$-vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$.  These numbers are legitimate finite coordinates, but they are not exon-inclusion measurements.  They can organize a codon-side residual analysis only when the row carrier actually contains coding-window codon counts and the declared downstream readout.  They do not replace the Adamson exon table, the ESE hexamer coordinate, or the MaxEnt splice score.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}  The chapter-level conclusion is a bounded negative or null readback for the named exon-inclusion relation.  The finite evidence confirms that the panel exists and that the ESE-derived direction can be compared to a splice-model direction on $1037$ rows.  It does not confirm a positive cross-layer survival claim, because the recorded collinearity is $-0.008429245619$ rather than a positive non-vacuous survival statistic.  In the language of the table, the safe statement is that the ESE hexamer coordinate and the MaxEnt splice coordinate can be co-indexed and audited, while this run gives no positive alignment strong enough to promote the relation beyond a finite splice-readout boundary.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}  Nothing in this row establishes translation, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality.  The codon lists, quotient vector, and empty $f_3$ boundary are finite coordinate facts, not biological proof of a downstream layer.  The exon-inclusion computation is likewise confined to its own splice-readout surface.  Any future statement about translation, protein output, structure, function, or phenotype would require a separate matched contact at that layer, a joined finite panel, rank sufficiency, controls appropriate to that readout, and a positive statistic after simpler-baseline ablation.
