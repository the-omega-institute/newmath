\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite matched transcript or coding-sequence panel in which a synonymous residual coordinate label is read only after the amino-acid quotient has removed the direct protein-sequence difference.  The carrier rows are required to contain, on the same row or through an explicitly declared join, a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate window, a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP route.  The biological question is therefore not whether a codon-window coordinate exists, and not whether RNA half-life can be measured in isolation.  The question is whether a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label remains associated with RNA half-life after translation conditioning, and whether that association is carried by measured local occupancy of a declared decay-factor RBP rather than by composition, isoform, abundance, structure, or batch effects.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The poly(A)-tail-length / CPE-grammar finite record supplies $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with the cross-species concordance flag equal to false and a recorded run time of $31.5212$ seconds.  In symbols, the row audit for this subject contains
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{human}} &= 8854,\\
 n_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 3990,\\
 \mathrm{cross\_species\_concordant} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are sufficient for a bounded finite-row readout about species-scoped RNA-tail or RNA-stability related behavior, but they do not by themselves establish a translation-conditioned RNA-decay mechanism.  The larger audit context also records an RBP-binding contact with $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and a translation dataset with $6494$ rows from the mTOR translation readout run.  Those companion row counts show that translation and RBP-facing data surfaces exist in the finite ledger, but they are not yet the same-window perturbation and rescue panel required for the present RBP-bridge claim.

\paragraph{Statistic required for a positive bridge.}
A positive result would have to be computed on joined rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_i = (&q_i, T_i, H_i, A_i, O_i, L_i, R_i, C_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the high- or low-coordinate residual label after amino-acid quotienting, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $A_i$ is matched transcript abundance, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ is the target-RBP loss condition, $R_i$ is the rescue condition, $C_i$ records binding-defective or non-target controls, and $Z_i$ contains transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline controls.  The bridge statistic must compare high- and low-coordinate rows only after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$, and must then check that the residual half-life effect tracks $O_i$, collapses under $L_i$, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Null interpretation of the available evidence.}
The finite evidence presently supports a stricter reading.  The human and yeast row counts are real finite contacts, but the false cross-species concordance flag blocks any broad species-invariant statement from this record.  More importantly, the supplied rows do not contain the complete same-window sequence of measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The correct null is therefore that, after controlling transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the translation readout, high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life unless the RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern is observed on the same finite panel.  A half-life contrast without that pattern is at most a bounded RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Boundary against over-interpretation.}
This chapter licenses no claim about translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  The translation readout is a mandatory conditioning boundary, not proof of an RNA decay mechanism.  The matched mRNA contact is a control surface, not RNA half-life evidence by itself.  The ribosome-profiling contact is a translation surface, not an RBP-occupancy or rescue assay.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry is a finite label system for organizing rows; it is not curated biological evidence unless joined to the relevant measurements.  No protein output, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this record.  Each of those claims would require its own layer-matched contact and its own declared finite statistic.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The ready statement is negative and bounded: the row evidence can be used to record a finite RNA half-life / poly(A)-tail related boundary for $8854$ human and $3990$ yeast rows, together with the observed failure of cross-species concordance.  It cannot yet be promoted to a translation-conditioned RBP bridge.  Promotion would require a same-organism, same-window panel containing the residual coordinate label, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared controlled statistic.
