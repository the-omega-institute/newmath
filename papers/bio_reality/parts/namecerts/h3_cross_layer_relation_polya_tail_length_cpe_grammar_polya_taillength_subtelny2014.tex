\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life and the Missing Decay-Factor Occupancy Panel}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched transcript panel in which synonymous residual-coordinate labels are allowed to meet RNA stability only through row-level measurements.  The proposed carrier consists of transcript or coding-sequence rows with a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy by a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and perturbation controls for that same factor.  The positive bridge is deliberately narrow: the coordinate may be discussed as an RNA half-life bridge only when the half-life residual is still visible after the translation-readout boundary is attached, the relevant decay-factor occupancy changes at the editable RNA window, the effect collapses after loss of the target RBP, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The available poly(A)-tail-length contact supplies a bounded finite comparison, not the full decay-factor RBP panel.  The run identified $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, and its cross-species concordance flag is false.  In scalar form the observed payload is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{human}} &= 8854,\\
  n_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 3990,\\
  \mathrm{cross\_species\_concordant} &= \mathrm{false},\\
  \mathrm{runtime\_sec} &= 31.5212.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough to say that a poly(A)-tail or CPE-grammar surface was inspected on finite organism-scoped rows, but it is not enough to assert a conserved coordinate law, a translation-conditioned RNA half-life mechanism, or a decay-factor RBP bridge.  The false cross-species flag is especially important: it prevents the finite readout from being promoted into an organism-independent stability rule.

\paragraph{Translation and RBP comparison rows.}
Two neighboring finite contacts calibrate what is missing.  The translation readout contact contains $6494$ rows in the mTOR translation run, with a recorded runtime of $9.469$ seconds.  Separately, the RBP-binding contact contains $900$ total peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  These numbers show that finite translation and finite RBP-binding surfaces exist as separate row types, but they are not joined here into one same-transcript table with RNA half-life, local decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  A contact name or neighboring run is therefore not a substitute for the joined panel required by the conjecture.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls are attached.  The control set includes amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, transcript abundance, splice or isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and the mandatory same-row translation readout.  In schematic finite-row notation, the permitted test has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_{0}:\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}}(B^{*}_{Q6}\mathrm{\ high},B^{*}_{Q6}\mathrm{\ low}\mid Z,T)
  &= 0,\\
  Z &= \{\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{structure},
  \mathrm{motifs},\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{batch}\},\\
  T &= \mathrm{same\mbox{-}row\ translation\ readout}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Even rejection of this null would only create a bounded RNA-stability association unless the RBP conditions are also satisfied on the same rows.  The bridge statistic must therefore include both the conditioned half-life residual and the occupancy/perturbation pattern.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
A positive bridge requires a finite panel in which the same transcript window carries all relevant columns.  The needed columns are the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, an occupancy-decoupling manipulation or comparable separation, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The scientific predicate is not merely that half-life differs between coordinate classes.  It is that the half-life difference follows measured local decay-factor occupancy and obeys the loss/rescue logic on the same finite carrier:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{bridge}=1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  &\Delta_{\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}}(B^{*}_{Q6}\mid Z,T)\neq 0,\\
  &\Delta_{\mathrm{occupancy}}(\mathrm{target\ RBP})\neq 0,\\
  &\Delta_{\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}}\ \mathrm{collapses\ under\ target\ RBP\ loss},\\
  &\Delta_{\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}}\ \mathrm{returns\ under\ wild\mbox{-}type\ rescue},\\
  &\Delta_{\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}}\ \mathrm{does\ not\ return\ under\ defective\ or\ non\mbox{-}target\ rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The current finite facts do not instantiate these clauses.  They instead define a disciplined boundary: the available poly(A)-tail rows and adjacent translation/RBP contacts motivate the shape of the required joined experiment while leaving the bridge unclaimed.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a bounded finite-row statement about what the available measurements do and do not support.  The $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows do not establish cross-species concordance, because the stored concordance flag is false.  The $6494$ translation rows do not turn RNA half-life into translation realization.  The $900$ RBP peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases do not supply same-window decay-factor occupancy, target loss, or rescue for this conjecture.  Consequently no protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, RNA-decay mechanism, or universal sequence law follows here.  Any such claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
