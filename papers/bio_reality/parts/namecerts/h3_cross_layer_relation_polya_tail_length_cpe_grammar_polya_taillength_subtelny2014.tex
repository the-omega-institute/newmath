\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as an RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{subsec:biorality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA panel whose rows carry synonymous residual-coordinate labels after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance and transcriptional controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP route.  The intended carrier is not a sequence law and not a protein-output claim.  It is a bounded table-level question: whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels remain exchangeable for RNA half-life once the row is conditioned on translation readout and on the ordinary transcript-level covariates.  In the available finite check for the poly(A)-tail/CPE grammar surface associated with Subtelny-style tail-length biology, the row counts are $n_{\mathrm{human}} = 8854$ and $n_{\mathrm{yeast}} = 3990$, with cross-species concordance recorded as false.  This scalar result is already enough to prevent any cross-species universalization of the poly(A)-tail grammar signal.  It leaves open only a narrower same-panel boundary: an RNA half-life association may be studied as a finite readout, but it is not yet an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Statistic shape.}
Let each eligible row $r$ contain a residual coordinate label $q(r) \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$, a translation readout $T(r)$, RNA half-life $H(r)$, matched abundance and transcription controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, isoform state, amino-acid composition, and batch.  The exchangeability null is that, after these controls, replacing $q(r)$ by a matched opposite-coordinate label does not change the residual distribution of $H(r)$.  A positive table statistic would therefore have to be read only through a controlled residual contrast such as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H(r) \mid q(r)=\mathrm{high}, T(r), Z(r)\bigr)
     - \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H(r) \mid q(r)=\mathrm{low}, T(r), Z(r)\bigr),\\
Z(r)
  &= \{\text{transcription, abundance, structure, motif density, composition, codon-pair, isoform, amino-acid, batch controls}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This formula is deliberately a finite-row residual comparison rather than a claim about translation, folding, protein abundance, phenotype, or evolutionary adaptation.  The same-row translation readout is a conditioning boundary: without it, an RNA half-life difference cannot be promoted to a translation-conditioned result; with it, the result is still only an RNA-stability association unless further perturbation and occupancy rows are present.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
The RBP bridge requires more than a half-life contrast.  It requires a prespecified decay-factor RBP, measured local occupancy at the editable RNA window, experimental decoupling of coordinate and occupancy, collapse of the coordinate-associated half-life effect after target-RBP loss, return under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The separate RBP-binding reality check in the current ledger contains $900$ total binding peaks and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  Those numbers establish that RBP binding can be represented as a finite contact surface, but they do not supply the same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue rows required by this RNA half-life question.  Similarly, the translation-readout contact represented by the mTOR translation surface has $n=6494$ rows and a declared readout source, but it is not an RNA half-life assay and is not an RBP perturbation experiment.  These contacts are therefore boundary witnesses for what must be joined, not substitutes for the joined panel.

\paragraph{Readback rule.}
A row-level positive result would need the following finite pattern on the same organism and window: high-coordinate versus low-coordinate labels separate in translation-conditioned RNA half-life; the same rows show a local change in the prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy; target-RBP loss removes the half-life contrast; wild-type rescue restores it; and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it.  If any part of that pattern is absent, the coordinate label remains a descriptor of a bounded RNA-stability association.  In particular, a half-life contrast without measured local occupancy is not an RBP mechanism, an occupancy contrast without coordinate/occupancy decoupling is not a coordinate bridge, and a loss effect without rescue controls is not a target-specific decay-factor claim.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no claim about ribosome dynamics, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality.  Codon-window geometry and residual-coordinate labels are bookkeeping for the finite matched rows; they are not themselves biological measurements.  RNA half-life data alone can bound an RNA-stability readout, and translation data alone can bound a translation-readout condition, but neither surface supplies decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation, rescue, or target specificity.  A positive RBP-bridge claim begins only after the same-row joined panel contains the occupancy/loss/rescue evidence and the declared controlled statistic survives the simpler transcript, composition, structure, isoform, abundance, and batch baselines.

\paragraph{Current finite verdict.}
The current finite evidence supports a constrained status rather than a mechanism claim.  The poly(A)-tail/CPE grammar check has finite human and yeast row counts but no cross-species concordance.  The RBP-binding and translation-readout checks supply nearby finite surfaces, with $900$ RBP peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and $6494$ translation rows, but they are not yet joined to the half-life panel as same-window decay-factor occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows.  The scientific consequence is a bounded null: high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable for the RBP-mediated interpretation until a same-organism, same-window table supplies translation-conditioned RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls under the stated covariates.
