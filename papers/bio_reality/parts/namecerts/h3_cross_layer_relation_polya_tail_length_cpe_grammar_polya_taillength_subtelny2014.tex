\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and a decay-factor RBP bridge}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched transcript or CDS table whose rows carry a synonymous residual-coordinate label $q \in B^*_{Q6}$ after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein where such measurements exist, and controls for transcription rate, transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler sequence baselines. The biological question is deliberately narrow: whether a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual label can be read as a bounded translation-conditioned RNA half-life effect that is carried by an experimentally separated decay-factor RBP bridge. The coordinate is not itself biological evidence. It is only a row label used to stratify finite measurements after the amino-acid sequence has already been quotiented out.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The present finite evidence ledger contains a poly(A)-tail and transcript-level readout associated with Subtelny-style data under the identifier $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.polya\_tail\_length.cpe\_grammar\_polya\_taillength\_subtelny2014$. Its parsed run is $9581dc27a332$, with $8854$ human rows, $3990$ yeast rows, runtime $31.5212$ seconds, and cross-species concordance recorded as false. Thus the row set is large enough to discuss a bounded RNA-readout surface, but it does not by itself satisfy a cross-species invariant claim. A separate RBP-binding ledger, $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode$, records run $b225890251c2$ with $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds. This second ledger is relevant only as evidence that finite RBP occupancy-style surfaces can be represented in the BioReality table language; it is not yet a same-window decay-factor RBP perturbation panel for the present poly(A)/half-life claim. A translation-readout comparison point, $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012$, records run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ with $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds, showing that same-row translation surfaces are separately representable but not automatically joined to the poly(A) and RNA half-life rows here.

\paragraph{Statistic to be licensed.}
A positive bridge would require a finite statistic comparing high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows only after conditioning on the same-row translation readout. In schematic form, the allowed target has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= E[H \mid q=\mathrm{high}, T, Z]
     - E[H \mid q=\mathrm{low}, T, Z], \\
\Delta_O
  &= E[O_{\mathrm{RBP}} \mid q=\mathrm{high}, T, Z]
     - E[O_{\mathrm{RBP}} \mid q=\mathrm{low}, T, Z],
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ is RNA half-life, $T$ is the translation readout, $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and $Z$ denotes the declared controls. The bridge is not licensed by $\Delta_H \ne 0$ alone. It is licensed only if the same finite panel also shows that the local RBP occupancy relation is experimentally separable from the coordinate label, that loss of the target RBP collapses the half-life effect, that wild-type rescue restores it, and that binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it. Without those rows, the same numerical half-life contrast remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life once the controls and the same-row translation readout have been attached. The operative null is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
H \perp q \mid T, Z,
\end{aligned}
$$
with an added bridge null: even if $H$ and $q$ are not exchangeable, the result is not an RBP bridge unless the prespecified occupancy, target-loss, wild-type-rescue, and defective-control pattern is present on the same matched row system. The current poly(A)-tail ledger reports $8854$ human and $3990$ yeast rows, but its cross-species concordance flag is false and it does not contain the required same-window perturbation and rescue structure. The finite result therefore supports at most a bounded RNA readout surface, not a realized decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Readback allowed by the present data.}
The present chapter may say that a finite RNA-related readout has been parsed, that the human and yeast row counts are explicit, and that the observed cross-species flag blocks a simple conserved-law reading. It may also say that RBP-binding and translation-readout ledgers exist as separate finite surfaces with their own row counts and runtimes. It may not say that the poly(A)-tail rows already contain the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy required for this bridge, nor that the translation rows have been joined to RNA half-life rows in a way that creates a mediation or mechanism result. The observed rows therefore define a bounded search target: join $B^*_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and controls on one finite panel, then test the declared break condition.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA decay mechanism follows from a same-row translation readout unless measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and the loss/rescue tests are present. No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the present coordinate rows, poly(A)-tail rows, RBP-binding rows, or translation ledgers. A binding-defective rescue, non-target control, RNA-structure-only explanation, motif-density-only explanation, transcription-rate explanation, isoform-state explanation, transcript-abundance explanation, GC/dinucleotide explanation, codon-pair-context explanation, or batch explanation must remain a blocking alternative unless explicitly defeated inside the same finite matched panel.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The record is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because it has concrete finite-row anchors: $8854$ human and $3990$ yeast rows for the poly(A)-tail ledger, a false cross-species concordance flag, $900$ RBP-binding peaks with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in the separate binding ledger, and $6494$ translation-readout rows in a separate translation ledger. The scientific conclusion is negative-conditional rather than positive-mechanistic: the current evidence defines the exact finite row system needed for a translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge, while the available rows do not yet cross that boundary.
