\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This chapter fixes a finite matched-panel question: can a synonymous residual coordinate window in a coding transcript be read as a bounded cross-layer relation to RNA half-life through a decay-factor RNA-binding protein route?  The carrier is a table of transcript or CDS rows with four required row-level fields: a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency surrogate, an RNA half-life measurement, and local occupancy or perturbation information for a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The intended bridge is not the mere observation that a high-coordinate and a low-coordinate synonymous label have different RNA half-lives.  It is the stricter finite pattern in which the half-life residual tracks measured local RBP occupancy, disappears when the target RBP is lost, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.

\paragraph{Available finite measurements.}
The row evidence currently attached to the poly(A)-tail-length contact is finite but not sufficient for the RBP bridge.  The verified record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.polya\_tail\_length.cpe\_grammar\_polya\_taillength\_subtelny2014} reports $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false and runtime $31.5212$ seconds.  Thus the available table is large enough to define organism-scoped poly(A) or RNA-stability summaries, but it already warns against treating the effect as a conserved universal law.  A separate RBP-contact record, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}, contains $900$ total peak rows and only $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in its verified summary.  This supplies an independent reminder that RBP occupancy cannot be inferred from motif or coordinate geometry alone.  A translation-side finite readout also exists in a different layer: \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} reports $6494$ rows from a ribosome-profiling translation source.  These three finite records justify the boundary form of the chapter: there are row-bearing surfaces for RNA/poly(A), RBP binding, and translation, but the required same-row joined matrix has not been supplied by any one verified fact.

\paragraph{Null model.}
Let $q_i\in\{0,1\}$ denote the high-versus-low residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life or poly(A)-linked stability readout, let $O_i$ be local occupancy for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline coordinates.  The chapter's exchangeability null is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, Z_i
\end{aligned}
$$
for the bounded RNA-stability question.  The stronger RBP-bridge null additionally refuses the bridge whenever the occupancy and perturbation pattern is absent:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H(q) \neq 0\ \wedge\ \neg\bigl(\Delta O(q)\neq 0\ \wedge\ \Delta H_{\mathrm{loss}}\approx 0\ \wedge\ \Delta H_{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}\approx\Delta H(q)\ \wedge\ \Delta H_{\mathrm{defective/non\ target}}\approx 0\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
In that case the finite table may record an RNA-stability association, but it does not record an RBP-mediated mechanism.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
A positive finite decision requires a joined panel in which every row has the coordinate label, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance control, RNA half-life or poly(A)-linked stability readout, measured same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target rescue, and the control vector $Z_i$.  The bridge statistic must be evaluated only after conditioning on translation and abundance controls.  The acceptable support pattern is directional and conjunctive: high-coordinate labels must separate from low-coordinate labels in $H_i$ under $T_i$ and $Z_i$; the same rows must show the prespecified local RBP occupancy contrast; loss of the target RBP must collapse the half-life residual; wild-type rescue must restore it; and the binding-defective or non-target arm must fail to restore it.  Motif density, RNA-structure-only, transcript-abundance-only, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair, splice/isoform, batch, and coordinate-only explanations remain null arms unless this full perturbation-and-rescue pattern is observed.

\paragraph{Readback status.}
The present finite evidence is therefore ready only as a bounded boundary chapter, not as a positive mechanistic bridge.  The poly(A)-tail-length record contributes $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows with no cross-species concordance; the RBP-binding record contributes a distinct finite binding surface with $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases; and the translation record contributes a separate $6494$-row translation surface.  None of these facts is a same-organism, same-window, same-row RNA half-life plus translation plus measured decay-factor RBP perturbation/rescue matrix.  Consequently the chapter licenses the following finite conclusion: a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate-associated RNA half-life readout can be reported as a translation-conditioned RNA-stability association only when the joined translation and RNA rows exist, and it can be promoted to an RBP-bridge only when measured occupancy, target loss, wild-type rescue, and defective or non-target rescue controls all follow the declared pattern.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RBP mechanism follows from half-life association alone, from motif density alone, from coordinate geometry alone, or from an RBP contact name that is not joined to the same RNA window.  No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the present rows.  Each such statement requires its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  The row counts above support a disciplined boundary: they are enough to define the missing joined-panel test precisely, and not enough to claim that the test has already succeeded.
