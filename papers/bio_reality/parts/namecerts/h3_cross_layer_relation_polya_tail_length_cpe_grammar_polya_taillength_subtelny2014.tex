\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows carrying synonymous residual-coordinate labels after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA controls, and an intended decay-factor RBP bridge.  The available finite half-life contact is not a complete perturbation panel: the verified poly(A)-tail and half-life-adjacent run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.polya\_tail\_length.cpe\_grammar\_polya\_taillength\_subtelny2014} reports $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with runtime $31.5212$ seconds and \texttt{cross\_species\_concordant = false}.  Thus the row inventory is large enough to define an organism-scoped finite readout surface, but it does not by itself identify a conserved cross-species law or a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Statistical object.}
Let $q_i$ denote the high- or low-coordinate residual label on row $i$, let $T_i$ denote the attached translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $C_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and simpler codon baselines.  The bounded testable surface is the translation-conditioned half-life residual
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,C_i\bigr)
     - \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,C_i\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Under the matched null, high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for $H_i$ after conditioning on $T_i$ and $C_i$.  A nonzero $\Delta_H$ is only an RNA-stability association unless the same finite rows also carry the prespecified RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern described below.

\paragraph{RBP bridge requirement.}
The RBP interpretation requires a stronger finite-row witness than half-life alone.  The panel must include measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, a decoupling check separating coordinate label from occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type target-RBP rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  In row notation, a positive bridge would require the half-life residual to track occupancy, to collapse under target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and to fail to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  This is a conjunction, not a menu of alternatives.

\paragraph{Available RBP and translation contacts.}
The separate RBP-binding verified fact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} contains $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  Those rows establish that finite RBP-binding surfaces exist elsewhere in the audit memory, but they are not the same-organism, same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue rows required for this RNA half-life bridge.  Likewise, the separate translation-readout fact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} reports $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds from a translation readout source, but this does not automatically join to the half-life and RBP-perturbation panel.  Translation conditioning is therefore a required row attachment, not an inference from naming a translation contact.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The current finite evidence supports only a bounded descriptor: an organism-scoped RNA half-life or poly(A)-tail-adjacent surface with $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, explicitly nonconcordant across species in the verified run.  It does not yet support the claimed cross-layer RBP bridge, because the necessary local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows are absent from the supplied finite panel.  The appropriate conclusion is therefore a blocked-null readback: any coordinate-associated half-life difference remains a finite RNA-stability association unless the full RBP perturbation and rescue pattern is attached and the declared statistic survives the stated controls.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RBP-decay mechanism follows from half-life, mRNA abundance, or translation-readout contact names without measured local occupancy and perturbation/rescue behavior.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law is licensed by this chapter.  Each such higher-layer claim would require its own layer-matched measurement panel and finite statistic.
