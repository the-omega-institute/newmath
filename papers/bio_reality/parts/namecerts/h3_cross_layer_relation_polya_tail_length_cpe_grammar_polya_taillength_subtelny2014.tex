\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and RBP occupancy boundary for Q6 residual windows}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows and claimed object.}
The record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows carrying a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label, an amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, and matched negative and rescue controls. The intended biological object is not a codon-usage law and not a protein-output claim. It is a bounded cross-layer question: whether high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual labels remain associated with RNA half-life after translation readout and ordinary transcript-level controls are attached, and whether such an association is specifically routed through a prespecified decay-factor RBP.

The directly verified poly(A)-tail and CPE-grammar contact is finite but negative for the requested cross-species strengthening. Its run reports $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false. In symbols, the available row inventory may be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{human}} &= 8854,\\
N_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 3990,\\
C_{\mathrm{cross\ species}} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers provide a real finite surface for poly(A)-tail-associated transcript readout, but they do not by themselves instantiate a same-window decay-factor RBP perturbation and rescue design. They therefore support a bounded RNA-level association audit rather than an RBP-mediated half-life mechanism.

\paragraph{Coordinate, relation, and trigger.}
Each admissible row has a coordinate component $q_i$, a translation component $T_i$, an RNA half-life component $H_i$, an RBP occupancy component $O_i$, and a control vector $Z_i$. The coordinate $q_i$ is read only after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, so that synonymous residual variation is not confused with peptide-sequence variation. The relation under inspection is the residual contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= E[H_i \mid q_i \in Q_{\mathrm{high}}, T_i, Z_i]
     - E[H_i \mid q_i \in Q_{\mathrm{low}}, T_i, Z_i],\\
\Delta_O
  &= E[O_i \mid q_i \in Q_{\mathrm{high}}, Z_i]
     - E[O_i \mid q_i \in Q_{\mathrm{low}}, Z_i].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_i$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline features. The trigger for a positive bridge is stricter than a nonzero $
\Delta_H$: the same finite panel must show measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapse of the half-life effect after loss of that target RBP, return under wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The RBP-binding inventory is also finite but not sufficient for the present bridge. The RBNS/eCLIP binding run reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases. This says that there is a bounded binding-readout surface elsewhere in the research memory, but it is not the same as a same-organism, same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue panel for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA half-life conjecture. The relevant distinction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
N_{\mathrm{cross\ RBP\ concordant}} &= 3,\\
\text{same-window perturbation/rescue rows} &\neq \text{generic binding peaks}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A translation-readout surface is likewise finite but separate. The mTOR translation contact reports $6494$ rows. That row count is useful as evidence that translation measurements can be represented as a finite matched layer, but it does not join the poly(A)-tail rows to RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP perturbation rows. Thus the contact inventory has finite pieces, not yet the joined matrix required for the claimed bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after all named controls and the same-row translation readout are conditioned. Formally, the null is not merely $\Delta_H=0$ in an uncontrolled table; it is the stronger statement that the coordinate label adds no half-life information once the finite row has been residualized against the declared biological and technical controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i
\;\mid\;
T_i,\ Z_i,\ \mathrm{amino\ acid\ quotient}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If an RNA half-life contrast survives this null but lacks measured local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target controls, then the result remains an RNA-stability association. It is not promoted to a decay-factor RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Positive bridge criterion.}
A positive result requires a single finite matched panel carrying all of the following rows and columns: $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, amino-acid quotient labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control, and the full control vector. The bridge condition is satisfied only when the sign and magnitude of the RNA half-life residual track the experimentally decoupled occupancy state and obey the perturbation pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{target\ loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{baseline}},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{non\ target\ rescue}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite-row criterion, not an inference from motif density or codon geometry alone. A motif-only, RNA-structure-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, abundance, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair, or batch explanation defeats the RBP bridge unless the stated perturbation and rescue behavior remains after those alternatives are controlled.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The verified poly(A)-tail contact supplies $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows but records no cross-species concordance. The verified RBP-binding contact supplies $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases but not the same-window loss/rescue design. The verified mTOR translation contact supplies $6494$ translation rows but not the RNA half-life and RBP perturbation join. Taken together, these facts justify a ready bounded chapter about the missing joined boundary: finite evidence surfaces exist, but the claimed RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge remains conditional on assembling the same-row panel and passing the perturbation criterion.

No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA half-life contrast becomes an RNA-decay mechanism unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy and the target-loss/wild-type-rescue/control-rescue pattern are present. No protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from this chapter. Those claims require their own layer-specific measurements and cannot be inferred from $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry, poly(A)-tail rows, generic RBP-binding rows, or a translation-readout contact name.
