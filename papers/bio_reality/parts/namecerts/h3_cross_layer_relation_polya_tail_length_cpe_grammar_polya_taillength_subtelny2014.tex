\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and polyadenylation-window boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched transcript panel in which each row is meant to carry a synonymous residual-coordinate label, a coding-sequence or transcript identifier, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and local annotations for a candidate RNA-binding decay factor. The intended distinction is not an amino-acid distinction: the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are compared only after quotienting by amino-acid sequence and after adding controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and translation readout. In this form the claim is a bounded cross-layer relation, not a protein-output or phenotype statement.

\paragraph{Observed finite counts.}
The polyadenylation-tail-length contact available for this record contributes two organism-scoped finite lists: $n_{\mathrm{human}}=8854$ human rows and $n_{\mathrm{yeast}}=3990$ yeast rows. The recorded cross-species concordance flag is false, and the run time for the finite computation is $31.5212$ seconds. Thus the local evidence does not support an invariant grammar shared across the human and yeast panels. The row-count summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{human}} &= 8854,\\
 n_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 3990,\\
 \mathrm{crossSpeciesConcordant} &= \mathrm{false},\\
 t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 31.5212\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers license only a finite-panel readout about the tested polyadenylation or tail-length surface. They do not by themselves identify a decay-factor RBP mechanism, because no same-window occupancy, loss, rescue, and binding-defective control rows are present in the finite table.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The translation layer is treated as a conditioning boundary rather than as a conclusion. A separate translation-readout record has $n=6494$ rows from the mTOR translation dataset, with run time $9.469$ seconds. That finite count shows that translation readout can be represented as its own matched surface, but it does not convert an RNA half-life or polyadenylation association into translation realization. For this chapter the admissible statistic must first attach same-row translation information and then ask whether the residual-coordinate half-life contrast remains after that conditioning. In schematic finite-row notation, if $Q_i$ is the residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ is the matched translation readout, and $H_i$ is RNA half-life, the admissible question has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H &= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{high}, T_i, Z_i)
          - \widehat{E}(H_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{low}, T_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ contains the declared transcript, sequence-composition, isoform, abundance, batch, and amino-acid controls. Without $T_i$ in the same row, the row remains an RNA-stability association at most.

\paragraph{RBP bridge test.}
The proposed bridge to a decay-factor RBP has a stricter finite-row requirement. The local RBP-binding audit supplies $n_{\mathrm{peaks,total}}=900$ peak rows and a cross-RBP concordance count of $3$, with run time $223.648043$ seconds. Those scalar facts show that RBP binding can be represented as a separate finite contact, but they are not the required same-window perturbation panel for this RNA half-life claim. A positive bridge would require, in the same organism and at the editable RNA window, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, experimental decoupling between coordinate and occupancy, collapse of the half-life effect after target-RBP loss, restoration by wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue. The finite bridge condition can be written as the conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
 &\Delta_H \neq 0,\\
 &\Delta_O \neq 0,\\
 &\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} \approx 0,\\
 &\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} \approx \Delta_H,\\
 &\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} \approx 0,\\
 &\Delta_H^{\mathrm{non\ target}} \approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $O$ denotes measured local RBP occupancy. The available finite facts do not instantiate these quantities for the same rows. Therefore the chapter records a boundary result rather than a positive RBP-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The matched null says that, after the declared controls and same-row translation conditioning, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. If a half-life contrast is observed without measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, without target-RBP loss collapse, or without wild-type rescue paired with failed binding-defective or non-target rescue, then the contrast remains a bounded RNA-stability association. It may motivate a follow-up perturbation panel, but it is not evidence that the coordinate caused an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone. It does not infer ribosome dynamics from polyadenylation rows, does not infer protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality from the residual-coordinate geometry, and does not treat RBP contact names as measured same-window perturbation evidence. A positive cross-layer bridge would require a separate finite contact supplying the missing occupancy, loss, rescue, and negative-control rows under the same identifiers used for the translation-conditioned RNA half-life statistic.
