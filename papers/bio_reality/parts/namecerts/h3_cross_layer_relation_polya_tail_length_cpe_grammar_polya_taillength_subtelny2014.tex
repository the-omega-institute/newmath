\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and an RBP bridge boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite matched RNA panel in which coding-transcript rows may carry a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a separately measured decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation surface.  The intended biological object is not poly(A)-tail length by itself and not codon geometry by itself.  It is the bounded question whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual can be promoted to a cross-layer RBP bridge only after the same row also carries translation conditioning and the required RBP evidence.

The finite row audit for the poly(A)-tail-length surface is concrete: run $9581dc27a332$ reports $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false and runtime $31.5212$ seconds.  Thus the available poly(A)-tail evidence is a finite readout surface with an explicit negative portability flag, not a universal sequence rule.  The RBP-binding audit is a separate finite surface: run $b225890251c2$ reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  The translation-readout comparator is also separate: run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ reports $6494$ rows for an mTOR translation readout, with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  These three finite objects are useful precisely because they do not collapse into one another: tail or half-life rows, RBP occupancy rows, and translation rows must be joined at the declared row boundary before the stronger bridge statement can be tested.

\paragraph{Coordinate split and null model.}
Rows are partitioned by the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual label after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented out.  The null keeps those labels exchangeable for RNA half-life after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been conditioned.  In compact form, the admissible comparison is only the residual contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{HL}
  &= E[H \mid Q=\mathrm{high}, Z, T]
   - E[H \mid Q=\mathrm{low}, Z, T],\\
Z
  &= \{\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motif},\mathrm{GC},
      \mathrm{dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},
      \mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{amino\ acid},\mathrm{batch}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ denotes the RNA half-life readout, $Q$ denotes the residual-coordinate label, and $T$ denotes the attached same-row translation readout.  A nonzero $\Delta_{HL}$ alone is still only an RNA-stability association.  It becomes an RBP bridge candidate only if the measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP changes in the same editable window, the coordinate and occupancy evidence are experimentally decoupled, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
The bridge criterion is deliberately conjunctive.  Let $O$ denote measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP, $L$ the target-RBP loss condition, $R_{wt}$ wild-type rescue, and $R_{bd}$ a binding-defective or non-target rescue.  A positive bridge requires the finite panel to satisfy the ordered pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_{HL}(Q;Z,T) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_O(Q;Z,T) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_{HL}(Q;Z,T,L) \approx 0,\\
&\Delta_{HL}(Q;Z,T,L,R_{wt}) \approx \Delta_{HL}(Q;Z,T),\\
&\Delta_{HL}(Q;Z,T,L,R_{bd}) \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The symbols here name finite-row contrasts, not asymptotic laws.  The present row memory does not supply the full same-organism, same-window occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue matrix for this subject.  Therefore the current finite state supports a bounded boundary statement: the poly(A)-tail or RNA half-life surface may motivate the test, and the RBP and translation audits show the kinds of separate surfaces that must be attached, but they do not themselves certify the bridge.

\paragraph{Readback status.}
The verified poly(A)-tail fact contributes two row counts and one negative portability flag: $n_{human}=8854$, $n_{yeast}=3990$, and cross-species concordance is false.  This is enough to prevent the chapter from reporting a general conserved grammar.  The verified RBP-binding fact contributes $900$ finite peak rows and only $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, which is enough to emphasize that RBP behavior is measured on its own surface and cannot be inferred from the coordinate label.  The verified mTOR translation fact contributes $6494$ translation-readout rows from a distinct experiment, which is enough to mark translation as an attachable readout type, not as an automatic consequence of RNA half-life or poly(A)-tail measurements.  No finite $p$-value, $\lambda$ parameter, $R$ list, $M$ list, hit rate, or module class for the declared RNA half-life RBP-bridge statistic is present in the supplied record.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It licenses no RBP-decay mechanism from a half-life contrast unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are present on the same finite row scheme.  It licenses no protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law.  Codon-window geometry, residual-coordinate labels, poly(A)-tail length, transcript abundance, and workflow readiness remain descriptors until a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the declared finite statistic is computed.
