\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP boundary for the Q6 residual coordinate}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows in which a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual label is joined, row by row, to an RNA half-life readout, an mRNA-abundance control, and a same-row translation readout. The intended biological question is deliberately narrower than a general codon-usage claim: after amino-acid quotienting and after the ordinary transcriptional and compositional controls are attached, can a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label be interpreted as a bounded RNA-decay relation mediated by a prespecified decay-factor RBP? The answer licensed by the present finite data is negative for the positive mechanism and positive only for the boundary test. The available verified poly(A)-tail-length/CPE-grammar row audit for the Subtelny-style comparison reports $n_{\mathrm{human}}=8854$ human rows and $n_{\mathrm{yeast}}=3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false and a finite run time of $31.5212$ seconds. Those numbers are enough to say that a large finite transcript table was examined and that the cross-species tail-length pattern did not pass the stated concordance check. They are not enough to identify a same-window decay-factor RBP mechanism for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate.

\paragraph{Controlled null.}
The local null model treats the high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels as exchangeable for RNA half-life once the comparison has conditioned on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In symbols, the admissible finite test has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_{0}:\;& \Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}(Q6_{\mathrm{high}},Q6_{\mathrm{low}}\mid Z,T)=0,\\
  Z=\;&\{\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motif},\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{batch}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T$ denotes the attached translation readout. A finite half-life difference may survive this control set as an RNA-stability association, but it does not become an RBP bridge unless a second, experimentally separated pattern is present: measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must vary in the editable window, target-RBP loss must collapse the half-life effect, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail to restore it. The present finite-row payload does not contain those same-window occupancy, loss, and rescue rows.

\paragraph{Readout actually present.}
The verified Subtelny-style poly(A)-tail-length entry supplies a row-counted transcript-level comparison rather than a perturbation-complete decay-factor RBP panel. Its concrete audit values are $8854$ human rows, $3990$ yeast rows, and the Boolean result $\mathrm{cross\_species\_concordant}=\mathrm{false}$. Thus the record may be used as a finite RNA-regulatory readout boundary for poly(A)-tail or CPE-associated behavior, but not as evidence that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate controls RNA decay through an RBP. The failed cross-species concordance is important: it prevents the chapter from treating the observed table as a conserved sequence law or as a transferable grammar for RNA half-life across organisms.

\paragraph{RBP comparison ledger.}
A separate verified RBP-binding audit in the same finite reality ledger reports $n_{\mathrm{peaks\ total}}=900$, $\mathrm{cross\_rbp\_concordant}=3$, and run time $223.648043$ seconds for an RBNS-affinity/eCLIP-binding comparison. This is relevant only as a scale check for the kind of finite RBP evidence that would be needed. It shows that RBP occupancy-like surfaces can be represented as finite rows, but it does not supply the particular same-organism, same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy rows required here. In particular, $900$ total peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases do not identify the target RBP for the present Q6 half-life question, do not perform target-RBP loss, and do not perform wild-type or binding-defective rescue.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The translation side is also only a boundary unless joined to the same transcript rows. A separate verified mTOR-translation audit reports $n=6494$ rows from the referenced translation readout source and run time $9.469$ seconds. This confirms that translation-layer row sets exist in the broader finite audit environment, but it does not by itself create the mandatory same-row conditioning matrix for the RNA half-life panel. Therefore the present claim cannot be promoted from RNA half-life association to translation realization. The admissible use is more limited: if a future joined table contains the same transcript identifier, the Q6 residual label, RNA half-life, mRNA abundance, translation readout, local RBP occupancy, perturbation, and rescue fields, then the translation readout may enter the conditioning set before the RBP-decay statistic is computed.

\paragraph{Decision rule for a positive bridge.}
A positive finite bridge would require all of the following in the same matched panel: a nondegenerate high-versus-low Q6 residual contrast after amino-acid quotienting; a translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual; a measured local occupancy difference for the prespecified decay-factor RBP; collapse of the half-life residual after loss of that target RBP; return of the residual after wild-type rescue; and failure of restoration under binding-defective or non-target controls. The finite statistic is conjunctive, not additive. A half-life signal without occupancy is only an RNA-stability association. Occupancy without loss and rescue is only binding correlation. Loss without wild-type rescue is only a perturbation phenotype. Wild-type rescue without failure of the binding-defective or non-target controls is not a specific RBP-binding mechanism.

\paragraph{Present verdict.}
The present finite-row evidence supports a bounded null-facing chapter, not a positive mechanistic claim. The poly(A)-tail-length/CPE-grammar audit has a substantial row count but records no cross-species concordance; the RBP-binding audit has finite peak counts but not the target decay-factor perturbation and rescue rows; and the translation audit supplies a separate translation surface rather than the required same-row conditioning field for this subject. Consequently, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate remains a finite descriptor that may be tested against RNA half-life under controls. It is not, on these rows, a demonstrated translation effect, RNA-decay mechanism, RBP-mediated causal relation, protein-output claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, biological-function claim, phenotype claim, adaptation claim, or universal biological law. Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact carrying the corresponding measurement and a declared finite statistic computed on the joined rows.
