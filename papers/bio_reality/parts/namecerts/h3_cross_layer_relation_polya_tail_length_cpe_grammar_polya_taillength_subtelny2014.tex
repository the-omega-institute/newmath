\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns finite transcript or CDS rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$ is joined, row by row, to an RNA half-life measurement, a translation readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and an explicitly declared decay-factor RBP bridge. The biological target is not poly(A)-tail length as a free-standing phenotype, and it is not translation efficiency by itself. The object is narrower: a coordinate-associated RNA-stability signal may be treated as a cross-layer RBP bridge only when the same finite row also carries the translation boundary and the RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue structure needed to separate RNA decay from ordinary covariate drift.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The available poly(A)-tail-length row set for the Subtelny-style CPE grammar comparison has the verified run identifier $9581dc27a332$. Its finite counts are $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with the recorded cross-species concordance flag equal to false and runtime $31.5212$ seconds. The local evidence therefore supports a bounded statement about finite RNA-level readouts, not a conserved cross-species grammar. The same audit bundle also records an RBP-binding contact run $b225890251c2$ with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, and an mTOR translation readout run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ with $6494$ rows. Those two neighboring finite contacts show that RBP occupancy surfaces and translation surfaces can be represented as separate row families, but they do not by themselves join to the poly(A)-tail-length rows as a same-window perturbation and rescue panel.

\paragraph{Declared statistic.}
The admissible statistic compares high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels only after amino-acid quotienting and after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In schematic form, the claimed contrast has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}} &= E[H_{\mathrm{RNA}} \mid B^*_{Q6}=\mathrm{high}, Z, T] - E[H_{\mathrm{RNA}} \mid B^*_{Q6}=\mathrm{low}, Z, T],\\
Z &= \{\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motifs},\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{batch}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H_{\mathrm{RNA}}$ denotes the RNA half-life readout and $T$ denotes the mandatory translation readout attached to the same row. The bridge is not licensed by $\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}} \neq 0$ alone. A positive bridge additionally requires measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, an experimental decoupling between the coordinate and the occupancy surface, collapse of the effect after loss of the target RBP, restoration under wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The operative null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after the listed controls. The verified row counts establish a finite RNA-readout setting, but the recorded cross-species concordance flag is false. Thus the available finite result is compatible with species-specific RNA-level structure, platform effects, identifier mapping limits, or CPE grammar differences, and it does not by itself identify a decay-factor RBP mechanism. No p-value, $\lambda$ value, R-list, M-list, or module class is present in the supplied row for this claim, so none is asserted here.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
A row may enter the RBP-bridge side only if all bridge components are present on the same organism and same editable RNA window: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual label, RNA half-life, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. Under that rule, the observed $8854$ human and $3990$ yeast rows provide a finite RNA-readout boundary, while the $900$-peak RBP-binding contact remains a separate occupancy surface until it is joined to the same windows and perturbations. The $6494$-row translation contact likewise marks a possible translation-readout surface, but a contact name or neighboring audit count does not compute a translation-conditioned RNA-decay statistic.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback is therefore negative for a completed RBP-decay bridge and positive only for a bounded finite-panel target. The chapter records that the relevant finite biology can be asked: whether high-coordinate synonymous residual windows differ from low-coordinate windows in RNA half-life after translation conditioning, and whether any such difference follows measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue logic. With the supplied data, the bridge remains unclosed because the row set lacks same-window measured RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter licenses translation realization from RNA half-life alone. Nothing licenses protein output, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or a universal biological law. A half-life association remains an RNA-stability association unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant measurement. An RBP interpretation remains blocked unless local occupancy changes, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure are all observed in the same finite matched panel.
