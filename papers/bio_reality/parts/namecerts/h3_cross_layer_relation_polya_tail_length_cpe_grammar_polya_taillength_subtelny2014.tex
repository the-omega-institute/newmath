\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and a bounded RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record considered here is a finite matched transcript/CDS panel whose rows carry four kinds of labels: a synonymous residual coordinate window after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout or translation-efficiency boundary, an RNA half-life readout, and a declared route by which a decay-factor RNA-binding protein could be tested at the same editable RNA window. The coordinate label is written as $B^{*}_{Q6}$ only as an internal row coordinate; it is not itself a measurement of translation, RNA decay, protein output, structure, or function. The finite readout already computed for the poly(A)-tail-length contact reports $n_{\mathrm{human}}=8854$ human rows and $n_{\mathrm{yeast}}=3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false in run $9581dc27a332$. This is enough to certify that the poly(A)-tail table supplies a finite readout surface, but it is not enough to certify a conserved or mechanistic RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Coordinate contrast.}
Each admissible row belongs to a high-coordinate or low-coordinate synonymous residual class after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented out. The admissible comparison is therefore not a raw codon-frequency comparison. It is a residual-coordinate comparison under controls for amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In this chapter the translation readout is a conditioning boundary, not the endpoint being claimed. A linked translation surface exists as a finite table in the mTOR translation run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, with $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds, but that table by itself does not provide RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, RBP loss, or rescue rows for the present subject.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
The positive bridge would require more than an RNA half-life difference. It requires a prespecified decay-factor RBP and a same-window occupancy contrast, followed by perturbation and rescue structure: local occupancy must differ in the predicted direction, loss of the target RBP must collapse the coordinate-associated RNA half-life effect, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail. In symbols, the admissible positive pattern is a finite conjunction of row-level tests,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q_{\mathrm{high}},q_{\mathrm{low}}\mid Z,T) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(q_{\mathrm{high}},q_{\mathrm{low}}\mid Z,T) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta H_{\mathrm{loss}} \to 0,\\
&\Delta H_{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} \to \Delta H_{\mathrm{native}},\\
&\Delta H_{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} \not\to \Delta H_{\mathrm{native}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ denotes RNA half-life, $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ denotes measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout, and $Z$ denotes the full control block. Without all five clauses, the table remains a bounded RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor. A separate RBP-binding contact is present in run $b225890251c2$, where $900$ peaks were processed and $3$ cross-RBP concordant units were recorded after $223.648043$ seconds, but that contact is not a same-transcript, same-window perturbation/rescue panel for this RNA half-life claim.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after all declared controls are attached. The null is not refuted by a half-life difference alone. It is also not refuted by a contact name, a workflow gate, an RBP motif annotation, an abundance covariate, or a translation table. The null is refuted only by a joined finite panel in which the coordinate contrast, translation conditioning, half-life readout, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are all present on compatible rows and evaluated by the declared statistic.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The current finite evidence gives a negative or bounded readback, not a positive mechanism claim. The poly(A)-tail run establishes the concrete scale of the available table, $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, and records that the cross-species concordance flag is false. The linked RBP-binding and translation runs show that finite RBP and translation surfaces exist elsewhere in the evidence ledger, with $900$ RBP peaks and $6494$ translation rows respectively, but they do not close the required same-window RNA-decay bridge. Thus the scientifically licensed statement is narrow: the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate can be carried into a finite translation-conditioned RNA half-life test design, and the design specifies what would count as an RBP-mediated bridge, but the presently supplied finite rows do not establish such a bridge.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RBP-mediated decay mechanism follows from a half-life association unless same-window occupancy and perturbation/rescue rows are present. No protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological rule follows from the coordinate label, from codon-window geometry, from the poly(A)-tail table, from the RBP peak table, or from the translation table without a separate layer-matched contact and statistic for that layer. The chapter therefore records a precise finite boundary: positive bridge status is withheld, while the finite row counts and the required experimental clauses define the next admissible measurement.
