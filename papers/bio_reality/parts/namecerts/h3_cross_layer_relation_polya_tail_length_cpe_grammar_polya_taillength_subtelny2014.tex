\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP bridge for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate windows}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about synonymous residual coordinate windows in coding transcripts. Each row is required to carry a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout or an explicitly bounded translation-efficiency proxy, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP route. The intended biological question is not whether codon geometry by itself predicts RNA stability, but whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual can be interpreted as a bounded cross-layer RBP-mediated effect after the translation readout has been attached and conditioned.

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence.}
The directly relevant poly(A)-tail and cytoplasmic-polyadenylation grammar run is recorded under experiment id \texttt{9581dc27a332}. Its finite panel contains $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, and the reported cross-species concordance flag is false. Thus the available poly(A)-tail record is informative as a finite readout surface, but it does not by itself provide a conserved cross-species rule. The associated runtime was $31.5212$ seconds, which is only a provenance scalar for the computation and has no biological meaning.

A separate RBP-binding reality run, experiment id \texttt{b225890251c2}, supplies a bounded contact surface rather than the specific perturbation panel demanded here. It reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant hits. This is useful for stating that RBP binding can be represented as finite rows, but it is not the same as measured same-window occupancy for the named decay-factor RBP in the RNA half-life panel. A translation-readout contact is also present in the linked evidence graph: experiment id \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} reports $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation readout source. That row count supports the existence of a finite translation readout route, but it does not merge the translation, half-life, and RBP perturbation rows into the same matched panel.

\paragraph{Local null model.}
The null comparison treats high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels as exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life once the required controls have been attached. The conditioning set includes amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In finite-row notation, the intended residual comparison has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i &= \text{RNA half-life residual for row } i,\\
Q_i &= \text{high or low } B^{*}_{Q6} \text{ coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting},\\
T_i &= \text{same-row translation readout},\\
Z_i &= \text{transcription, abundance, sequence-composition, isoform, and batch controls},\\
H_i &\perp Q_i \mid (T_i,Z_i) \quad \text{under the matched null.}
\end{aligned}
$$
A departure from this exchangeability is still not an RBP mechanism. It is at most a bounded RNA-stability association unless the RBP evidence satisfies the stricter finite-row tests below.

\paragraph{RBP bridge acceptance rule.}
A positive bridge requires more than RNA half-life separation. The finite panel must show that the effect follows measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, that coordinate and occupancy can be experimentally decoupled, that target-RBP loss collapses the coordinate-associated half-life effect, that wild-type target-RBP rescue restores it, and that binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it. Equivalently, the admissible positive pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &\to H_i \mid (T_i,Z_i),\\
Q_i &\to O_i \mid (T_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_{QH}^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_{QH}^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_{QH}^{\mathrm{baseline}},\\
\Delta_{QH}^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_{QH}^{\mathrm{non\ target}} &\approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $O_i$ is the measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP and $\Delta_{QH}$ is the coordinate-associated half-life contrast after the stated controls. The present finite evidence does not contain this complete occupancy, loss, rescue, and negative-control matrix.

\paragraph{Readback from the supplied rows.}
The poly(A)-tail record gives a concrete finite-row surface: $8854$ human rows, $3990$ yeast rows, and no positive cross-species concordance. The RBP-binding record gives $900$ finite RBP peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP-concordant outcomes, but these rows are not same-window decay-factor occupancy rows joined to the half-life panel. The translation readout record gives $6494$ finite rows, but the provided data do not certify a joined tuple $(Q_i,T_i,H_i,O_i,Z_i)$ with perturbation and rescue labels. Consequently, the chapter can state a bounded negative or incomplete-bridge result: the available rows are sufficient to specify the required test and to block overinterpretation, but not sufficient to assert a measured RBP-mediated RNA half-life mechanism.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No measured half-life difference may be promoted to a decay-factor RBP mechanism unless local RBP occupancy changes in the specified window, target-RBP loss removes the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail. No RNA-structure-only model, motif-density-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, transcript-abundance model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model counts as positive support for the RBP route. The finite rows also do not license protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the tested RBP-decay relation, or universality. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite statistics.
