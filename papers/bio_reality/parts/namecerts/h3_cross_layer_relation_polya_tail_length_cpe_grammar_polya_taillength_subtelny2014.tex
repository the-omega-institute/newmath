\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about synonymous residual coordinate windows in coding transcripts.  Its carrier consists of transcript or CDS rows carrying $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a measured local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls.  The biological question is not whether a coordinate label is associated with RNA stability in isolation.  The question is whether a bounded cross-layer bridge can be read only when translation, RNA half-life, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and negative rescue controls are all present on the same finite panel.

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence presently attached.}
The finite verification ledger supplies a poly(A)-tail-length contact for the Subtelny-style CPE grammar surface with $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows.  The computed cross-species concordance flag is false, and the recorded runtime is $31.5212$ seconds for run $9581dc27a332$.  Thus the available poly(A)-tail evidence is real finite-row evidence, but it is not a positive cross-species bridge for this claim.  A separate RBP-binding contact records $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run $b225890251c2$, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  A translation-contact ledger entry for the Thoreen mTOR translation surface records $6494$ rows in run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  These three finite surfaces show that the paper has row-bounded contacts for poly(A)-tail measurements, RBP binding, and translation readout routes, but they are not yet a single same-organism, same-window perturbation/rescue matrix for the present RNA half-life mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout.  In symbols, for a finite row set $I$ with coordinate label $q_i$, translation readout $T_i$, half-life readout $H_i$, and controls $Z_i$, the null treats the coordinate contrast as removable once $T_i$ and $Z_i$ are fixed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i \perp q_i \mid (T_i,Z_i)
  \quad\text{within the matched finite panel.}
\end{aligned}
$$
If a half-life contrast remains but the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, not decoupled from the coordinate contrast, or not tested by loss and rescue, the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated bridge.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
A positive bridge requires all of the following finite-row events to co-occur.  First, the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual contrast must be defined after amino-acid quotienting.  Second, the same row must carry a translation readout so that the half-life comparison is translation-conditioned rather than merely transcript-level.  Third, the editable RNA window must carry measured occupancy for an externally chosen decay-factor RBP, and the occupancy contrast must be experimentally separable from simpler sequence, abundance, and structure explanations.  Fourth, the half-life effect must collapse when the target RBP is lost.  Fifth, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore the effect, while binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to restore it.  These requirements are conjunctive, not interchangeable; no single contact name, workflow status, or coordinate calculation supplies the missing rows.

\paragraph{Readback statistic.}
The intended statistic is a finite matched-panel contrast, not a general law over transcriptomes.  Let $G_i$ mark the high-coordinate residual class, let $O_i$ be local measured target-RBP occupancy, let $L_i$ mark target-RBP loss, let $W_i$ mark wild-type rescue, and let $D_i$ mark binding-defective or non-target rescue.  A bridge-supporting pattern has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  &\Delta_H(G \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
  &\Delta_O(G \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
  &\Delta_H(G \mid T,Z,L) \approx 0,\\
  &\Delta_H(G \mid T,Z,L,W) \text{ returns},\\
  &\Delta_H(G \mid T,Z,L,D) \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The displayed conditions only describe the required finite pattern.  They are not asserted as verified for the current data, because the attached ledger supplies separate poly(A)-tail, RBP-binding, and translation contacts rather than the joined target-RBP perturbation and rescue panel.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the present row counts.}
The $8854$ human and $3990$ yeast poly(A)-tail rows are sufficient to document a bounded poly(A)-tail readout surface, and the false cross-species concordance flag blocks a positive conserved grammar interpretation for that contact.  The $900$ RBP peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases document an RBP-binding readout surface, but not measured same-window occupancy for the prespecified decay factor in the same rows as the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate and RNA half-life readout.  The $6494$ translation rows document a translation-readout route, but not an RNA decay perturbation or rescue experiment.  The scientifically conservative reading is therefore a boundary statement: the finite contacts are present as separate evidence surfaces, while the claimed RBP-bridged RNA half-life mechanism remains unpromoted until the joined panel exists.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no claim that $B^*_{Q6}$ geometry by itself realizes translation, protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality.  It also licenses no claim that RNA half-life alone identifies an RBP mechanism.  A measured half-life difference cannot be promoted to a decay-factor RBP bridge unless local RBP occupancy changes in the specified window, target-RBP loss removes the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail.  Likewise, a translation readout can condition the RNA half-life statistic, but it does not by itself supply an RNA-decay mechanism.  The current verdict is that the record is suitable for inclusion as a bounded finite-row boundary chapter, with the positive bridge withheld until a same-organism, same-window occupancy, perturbation, and rescue panel is attached.
