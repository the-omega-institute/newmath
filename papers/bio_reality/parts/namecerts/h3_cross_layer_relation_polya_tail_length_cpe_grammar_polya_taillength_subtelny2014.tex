\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life And The Decay-Factor Occupancy Boundary}\label{subsec:biorality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The subject is a finite matched transcript panel in which a synonymous residual coordinate label is allowed to be compared with RNA half-life only after the same row carries the layer information needed to prevent promotion across biological surfaces.  A row has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_r = (&q_r, a_r, T_r, H_r, A_r, O_r, L_r, W_r, C_r),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_r$ is the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate class after the amino-acid quotient, $a_r$ is the amino-acid context, $T_r$ is a same-row translation readout or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency readout, $H_r$ is an RNA half-life value, $A_r$ is matched transcript abundance, $O_r$ is measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, $L_r$ records target-RBP loss, $W_r$ records wild-type rescue, and $C_r$ records binding-defective or non-target controls.  The finite contact currently attached to the polyadenylation-tail grammar route supplied $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false and runtime $31.5212$ seconds.  This is enough to describe a bounded RNA-tail or RNA-stability surface, but it is not enough to assert an RBP-mediated RNA half-life mechanism.

\paragraph{Coordinate contrast.}
The coordinate test separates high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels only after the amino-acid sequence has been quotiented away.  Let $Q_r \in \{0,1\}$ denote the high-coordinate indicator and let $Z_r$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, translation readout, batch, and simpler baseline predictors.  The null model is local exchangeability of the coordinate labels with respect to the half-life readout:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_r \perp Q_r \mid Z_r, T_r.
\end{aligned}
$$
A finite half-life association is therefore only the weaker event
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H = E(H_r \mid Q_r=1,Z_r,T_r)-E(H_r \mid Q_r=0,Z_r,T_r) \ne 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This expression by itself is not a translation result, not an RBP mechanism, and not a protein-output statement.  It is only a controlled RNA half-life contrast within the matched rows.

\paragraph{Required bridge pattern.}
The stronger cross-layer claim requires an experimentally separated decay-factor RBP bridge.  The bridge is present only if the coordinate-associated half-life contrast follows a local occupancy contrast for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, disappears under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and does not return under binding-defective or non-target controls.  In finite-row notation, a positive bridge would require the same panel to support the pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_O &\ne 0,\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{target\ loss}} &\approx 0,\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{native}},\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{non\ target}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The attached data do not currently contain this full occupancy-loss-rescue panel.  The separate RBP-binding contact records $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds, but those rows are not the same-organism, same-window decay-factor occupancy and perturbation rows required here.  They show that RBP binding can be represented as a bounded contact class; they do not supply the local bridge for this subject.

\paragraph{Translation conditioning.}
The translation layer is included only as a conditioning boundary.  A separate translation-readout contact reports $6494$ rows for a TOP-score mTOR translation dataset, with runtime $9.469$ seconds and an NCBI GEO source route.  That scalar confirms that finite translation-readout rows can exist in the broader BioReality table system, but it does not join the present polyadenylation-tail rows to the same transcript windows, the same half-life estimates, and the same RBP perturbation/rescue rows.  Thus $T_r$ may be part of the required control set, but the current subject cannot infer translation realization from RNA half-life alone.

\paragraph{Decision.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded negative classification for the proposed RNA half-life RBP bridge.  The polyadenylation-tail grammar run has concrete organism-scoped row counts, and the RBP-binding and translation contacts show that the relevant biological surfaces are separately representable.  The missing item is the decisive joined panel: the same row must contain the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate label, translation readout, matched mRNA control, RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Without that panel, the result remains a bounded RNA-stability or tail-grammar association.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the present rows.  A half-life difference without measured local RBP occupancy is not an RBP mechanism.  An occupancy association without loss and rescue is not a decay bridge.  A wild-type rescue without binding-defective and non-target controls is not specific enough to promote the coordinate contrast.  Codon-window geometry and residual-coordinate labels remain finite descriptors until a separate layer-matched contact supplies the claimed biological readout.
