\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-measured-trna-tai-b-star-q6-modeled-residual-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This chapter records a bounded cross-layer contact between nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates and an organism-level tRNA gene-copy tAI readout.  The tested coordinates are
$K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each organism, the residual coordinate surface is estimated against a control design $Z$ containing an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The design has $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in every reported organism.

The finite panel contains three organism-level joins for the tRNA-copy tAI contact: Danio rerio has $14410$ joined CDS rows, $8846$ parsed tRNA genes, and an anticodon GCN table of size $57$; Homo sapiens has $19053$ joined CDS rows, $431$ parsed tRNA genes, and an anticodon GCN table of size $47$; Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5815$ joined CDS rows, $273$ parsed tRNA genes, and an anticodon GCN table of size $41$.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are $8607$, $422$, and $268$, respectively, and no raw $W$ entries required geometric-mean replacement in any of the three organisms.  Header exclusions were finite and explicit: Danio rerio excluded $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon records; Homo sapiens excluded $1$ selenocysteine record; Saccharomyces cerevisiae excluded $2$ undetermined-anticodon records.

\paragraph{Statistic and absorption readback.}
The measured-tRNA contact asks whether the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component $\widehat P\_{Q\perp Tmod}$ is further absorbed by a tRNA gene-copy tAI vector after the same $Z$-residualization boundary.  The reported modeled-complement distances exactly match the complement reference on the full join in all three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d\_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d\_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d\_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute differences from the reference are all $0.0$, so the tRNA-copy tAI absorption is evaluated on the intended full-CDS complement surface rather than on a coverage-shifted subset.

The measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption fractions are small but nonzero:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A\_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.020817102845637092,\\
 A\_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.00005426229300865148,\\
 A\_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.001530747712165342.
\end{aligned}
$$
The combined residual distances after adding the measured tRNA-copy tAI readout are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d\_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 d\_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 d\_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite table supports a bounded descriptive statement: measured tRNA gene-copy tAI can be projected onto the modeled-complement residual surface, and the amount it absorbs is positive in the three reported organisms, largest in Danio rerio and much smaller in Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The same $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate family also has a measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact on four organisms with matched abundance controls.  That panel contains $13140$ Danio rerio, $3415$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $11554$ Homo sapiens, and $5128$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae joined TE rows, again with $24$ control columns and rank $23$ per organism.  Its measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  The tRNA-copy tAI and measured-TE contacts therefore point to the same kind of bounded projection question, but they are not interchangeable: the tRNA-copy tAI readout is an organism-level modeled adaptation proxy derived from tRNA gene copy number and wobble choices, whereas measured TE is a ribosome-profiling ratio with matched mRNA abundance.

The measured-TE join is also not uniformly identical to the full complement reference.  Danio rerio shifts from a complement reference join of $14410$ to a measured-TE join of $13140$, with modeled-distance difference $0.03219747984141397$; Homo sapiens shifts from $19053$ to $11554$, with difference $0.06427590857052146$; Escherichia coli and Saccharomyces cerevisiae remain within the $0.02$ self-check tolerance.  This matters for interpretation: the tRNA-copy tAI result is evaluated on full abundance-CDS joins for the three reported organisms, while measured TE is a separate finite contact whose availability can change the effective row set.

\paragraph{Cross-organism boundary.}
The broader organism-specific meta table gives no license to turn the tRNA-copy tAI absorption into a universal organismal law.  In the complete-feature organism subset, the $M\_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 test has $n=12$, Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$, and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; the $M\_{QTP}$ fraction versus log tRNA-gene count test has $n=12$, $r=0.06029405206037823$, and $p=0.8621378621378621$; the $M\_{QTP}$ fraction versus log proteome size test has $n=12$, $r=-0.033628115789504955$, and $p=0.913086913086913$.  The domain comparison has $n=12$, variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$, right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$, and group means $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes.  The dominant-coordinate contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$.  These finite summaries are compatible with heterogeneous organism-specific behavior, not with a resolved domain rule.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter's positive content is narrow.  A full-join, rank-$23$ residualized projection was computed for three organisms, and measured tRNA gene-copy tAI absorbs $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$ of the modeled-complement residual in Danio rerio, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  This licenses only a descriptive cross-layer relation between the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface and a tRNA-copy tAI readout.  It does not establish direct tRNA abundance, ribosome occupancy, translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, causation, or universality.  Any such stronger statement would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic under the appropriate controls.
