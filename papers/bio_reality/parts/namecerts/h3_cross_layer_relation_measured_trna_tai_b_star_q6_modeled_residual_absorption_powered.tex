\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI as a bounded absorber of the modeled translation complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-measured-trna-tai-q6-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a measured tRNA gene-copy implementation of tAI accounts for any part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remained after the modeled tAI projection was removed.  The rows are coding sequences joined to organism-specific codon counts and to a tRNA gene-copy table.  The coordinate side is the fixed nine-dimensional residual family
$K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$.  The control design contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each organism the control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, so the reported projection is not an under-rank row artifact for this design.

\paragraph{Joined organism rows.}
The tRNA-copy contact is complete on the full abundance-CDS join used by the modeled-complement reference.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, the surface contains $14{,}410$ joined CDS rows, $8{,}846$ parsed tRNA genes, and an anticodon gene-copy table of size $57$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, it contains $19{,}053$ joined CDS rows, $431$ parsed tRNA genes, and an anticodon table of size $47$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, it contains $5{,}815$ joined CDS rows, $273$ parsed tRNA genes, and an anticodon table of size $41$.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are respectively $8{,}607$, $422$, and $268$, with no zero raw $W$ values filled by a geometric mean in any of the three organisms.  The tRNA parser excluded nonstandard records rather than forcing them into the sense-codon surface: in \emph{D. rerio} it excluded $3$ selenocysteine records, $25$ suppressor records, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon records; in human it excluded $1$ selenocysteine record; in yeast it excluded $2$ undetermined-anticodon records.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement identity check.}
Before measuring tRNA-copy absorption, the projected target was checked against the earlier modeled-complement abundance calculation.  The equality check is exact at the reported precision for all three joined surfaces:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{model}}(\mathrm{D.\ rerio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{\mathrm{model}}(\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{\mathrm{model}}(\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each organism the current full-join value equals the complement-reference value, the absolute difference is $0.0$, and the match is inside the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  This matters because sparse feature contacts elsewhere can change the protein-id join and thereby change the residual target.  Here the tRNA-copy test is evaluated on the same full join as the reference modeled complement, so the absorption numbers are read as absorption of the intended target rather than as a side effect of row loss.

\paragraph{Absorption readout.}
The measured tRNA-copy tAI absorbs only a small fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component.  The reported organism values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{D.\ rerio}) &= 0.020817102845637092,\\
 \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}) &= 0.00005426229300865148,\\
 \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.001530747712165342.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding combined residual fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{D.\ rerio}) &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 d_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}) &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 d_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the largest descriptive absorption occurs in \emph{D. rerio}, at about $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement component.  Human is effectively unchanged at this scale, and yeast shows a small reduction while starting from a much smaller modeled-complement fraction.  The finite result is therefore not a positive claim that measured tRNA-copy tAI explains the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis.  It is a bounded observation that this tRNA-copy implementation leaves most of the modeled-complement component present on these three organism surfaces.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
A separate measured translation-efficiency contact gives a useful scale check.  On the measured-TE all-readout join, the modeled-complement fractions were $0.8424312412078521$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.6628211177352172$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.8566190269974241$ for human, and $0.22386566043976477$ for yeast.  The measured-TE absorption values were $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  Those numbers are also small, and their joins differ from the full abundance-CDS join in \emph{D. rerio} and human because measured TE availability reduces the row set.  The tRNA-copy test in this subsection is therefore narrower but cleaner with respect to the modeled-complement reference: it preserves the full join for the three organisms where the measured tRNA-copy surface was parsed.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific summary does not license a domain-level biological law for these coordinates.  In the cross-organism meta table, the dominant-coordinate contingency test used $12$ complete-feature organisms and obtained Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous $M_{\mathrm{QTP}}$ fraction checks were similarly exploratory: versus GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$.  The domain grouping test used $12$ organisms, a variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These values support heterogeneity as a finite descriptive fact, not a resolved phylogenetic or mechanistic classification.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite claim supported here is only this: after amino-acid composition, organism-specific sequence covariates, GC3, CDS length, and $M$-density are included, measured tRNA gene-copy tAI can be projected against the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component on three full organism joins, and the resulting absorption is small.  The contact uses tRNA gene copy number as a proxy for tRNA abundance and uses the dos Reis wobble $s$-values as a modeling convention.  It is not direct tRNA-seq, not ribosome occupancy, not a causal mediator test, and not a measured protein-output result.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation-realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, causal pathway, or universal biological rule follows from this projection alone.  A stronger statement would need a separate layer-matched contact: measured ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency for translation output, matched proteomics for protein abundance, a structure or physical assay for admissibility, a functional assay for molecular role, and a perturbation or phenotype contact for system-level interpretation.  Within the present finite surface, the correct conclusion is negative-to-bounded: measured tRNA-copy tAI does not absorb the modeled-complement $B^*_{Q6}$ residual strongly enough to close the residual basis, and the remaining component stays open for other contacts such as measured TE, turnover, localization, interaction, or condition-specific regulation.
