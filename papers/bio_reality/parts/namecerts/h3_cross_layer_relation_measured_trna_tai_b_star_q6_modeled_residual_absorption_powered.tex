\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI Absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ Modeled-Translation Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-measured-trna-tai-q6-residual-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether an organism-level tRNA gene-copy implementation of tAI absorbs the component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual left after the modeled-tAI projection. The tested carrier is a finite coding-sequence by organism table with nine residual coordinates,
$K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$, together with controls for an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The control matrix has 24 columns and rank 23 in every organism reported here. The row surface is full relative to the abundance-CDS complement reference: $n=14410$ for danio rerio, $n=19053$ for homo sapiens, and $n=5815$ for saccharomyces cerevisiae. Thus the tRNA-copy test does not change the complement join used to define the residual target.

\paragraph{Measured tRNA-copy construction.}
For danio rerio, the parsed tRNA table contains 8846 tRNA genes and 57 anticodon gene-copy entries after excluding 3 selenocysteine headers, 25 suppressor headers, and 5 undetermined-anticodon headers. For homo sapiens, the table contains 431 tRNA genes and 47 anticodon entries after excluding 1 selenocysteine header. For saccharomyces cerevisiae, the table contains 273 tRNA genes and 41 anticodon entries after excluding 2 undetermined-anticodon headers. The usable measured-tAI record counts are 8607, 422, and 268, respectively, and no organism required replacement of a zero raw $W$ value by a geometric mean. The source payloads are finite and checksum-bound: danio rerio has 1747886 bytes with SHA256 \texttt{e81e605548df44e88ca0c3778b315202bd826c377ed9173e1a8575e21eb4090a}, homo sapiens has 82720 bytes with SHA256 \texttt{50bd6fe75f60ce7461864dc1604a3c51481f1adfae7ad74198a1f389c211ac89}, and saccharomyces cerevisiae has 56478 bytes with SHA256 \texttt{273c3519053ef469cf4e43063ce634187a943a5d53ea4e87879b2224860d82c8}.

\paragraph{Complement target stability.}
The modeled-complement self-check is exact on the tRNA-copy join. In each organism, the current full-join modeled residual fraction equals the complement-reference value with absolute difference 0.0:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
This matters because the downstream absorption calculation is then read against the same residual target rather than against a coverage-distorted subset. The three complement-reference row counts are exactly the current row counts, namely $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$, so the test is not confounded by a tRNA-table availability restriction at the coding-sequence join stage.

\paragraph{Absorption readout.}
The measured tRNA-copy tAI surface absorbs only a small part of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component. The computed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.020817102845637092,\\
 A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.00005426229300865148,\\
 A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.001530747712165342.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding combined residual fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 d_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 d_{\mathrm{combined}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest absorption is in danio rerio, about $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement component. Human is essentially unchanged at about $5.43\times 10^{-5}$, and yeast is low at about $0.153\%$. The result therefore supports a bounded negative reading: replacing modeled translation adaptation by measured tRNA gene-copy tAI does not, in these finite rows, erase most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ complement residual.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency rows.}
A separate ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency computation gives a useful scale for the same residual family. With measured TE available, the all-readout joins are $n=13140$ for danio rerio, $n=3415$ for escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for homo sapiens, and $n=5128$ for saccharomyces cerevisiae, again with 24 control columns and rank 23. On those TE joins, the measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively. The comparison is not a promotion of tRNA-copy tAI to observed ribosome dynamics; it only shows that both bounded readouts leave large residual fractions on their own tested joins. The TE calculation also records join sensitivity: the modeled-complement difference from the abundance-CDS reference is 0.03219747984141397 in danio rerio and 0.06427590857052146 in homo sapiens, while escherichia coli and yeast remain within 0.02. By contrast, the tRNA-copy calculation above has zero join displacement for the three organisms tested.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The organism-specific meta table is heterogeneous rather than a basis for a universal rule. In the available twelve-organism feature-complete subset, the $M^{QTP}$ fraction versus domain test has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$ over 1000 trials. The continuous tests likewise do not give a sharp global law: $M^{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $p=0.35264735264735264$, versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$, and versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$. The dominant-coordinate by domain table has Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ but right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$, so the finite panel records heterogeneity without licensing a domain-level explanation.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite computation is ready as a descriptive cross-layer readout: tRNA gene-copy tAI can be evaluated on the same abundance-CDS complement rows for danio rerio, homo sapiens, and saccharomyces cerevisiae, and the resulting absorption is small in all three organisms. The strongest local statement is that the measured tRNA-copy tAI surface does not substantially absorb the modeled-tAI-complement $B^*_{Q6}$ residual on this finite panel. The record does not establish causation, direct tRNA abundance, ribosome occupancy, translation mechanism, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality. Any such higher-layer interpretation requires a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic; the present table only reports a bounded projection of a finite residual coordinate surface against a tRNA-copy-derived tAI readout.
