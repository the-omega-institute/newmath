\subsection{Measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-residual component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer question: after constructing the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates on real coding sequences, and after removing the declared sequence-composition controls, how much of the abundance component left outside the modeled-tAI direction is absorbed by a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI readout?  The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-stress}$.  The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each organism the control-column count is $24$ and the reported control rank is $23$, so the test is not an under-specified three-row or saturated surface.

\paragraph{Joined rows and tRNA tables.}
The finite panel contains three full-join organisms for this measured-tRNA contact.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, the joined CDS count is $14410$, the anticodon gene-copy table has $57$ anticodon entries, and the parsed tRNA-gene count is $8846$.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, the joined CDS count is $19053$, the anticodon table has $47$ entries, and the parsed tRNA-gene count is $431$.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined CDS count is $5815$, the anticodon table has $41$ entries, and the parsed tRNA-gene count is $273$.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are respectively $8607$, $422$, and $268$, with zero raw $W$ entries filled by a geometric mean in all three organisms.  The parser excluded nonstandard header classes rather than treating them as ordinary anticodon observations: zebrafish excluded $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon records; human excluded $1$ selenocysteine record; yeast excluded $2$ undetermined-anticodon records.

\paragraph{Reference residual target.}
For each organism the modeled-complement target is unchanged relative to the full abundance-CDS join used by the abundance complement computation.  The absolute difference between the current full-join $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ and the complement reference is $0.0$ in all three organisms.  The reference values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the measured-tRNA test is not being compared against a shifted sparse-feature join.  It is evaluated on the same CDS support as the full modeled-complement reference for each organism in the panel.

\paragraph{Absorption values.}
The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI projection absorbs a small but nonzero fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual in each organism:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.020817102845637092, & d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.835657382343872,\\
A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.00005426229300865148, & d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.92041342656924,\\
A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.001530747712165342, & d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest absorption occurs in zebrafish, where the absorbed fraction is about $2.08\%$.  The human value is nearly zero on this scale, about $5.43\times 10^{-5}$.  Yeast is intermediate but still small, about $0.153\%$.  These values support only the descriptive statement that a tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface removes a limited part of the abundance residual left after the modeled-tAI complement construction.  They do not show that the remaining component has been explained.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency rows.}
A distinct measured-translation-efficiency computation tests ribosome-profiling TE as a first candidate readout by projecting the same kind of modeled-tAI-complement residual onto residualized measured TE.  On that contact, the reported measured-TE absorptions are $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  The TE joins have $n=13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \textit{E. coli}, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, again with control rank $23$.  This neighboring computation is useful as a calibration of scale, but it is not interchangeable with the measured-tRNA test: gene-copy tAI is a codon-adaptation proxy derived from tRNA annotations and wobble weights, whereas ribosome-profiling TE is a condition-specific readout formed from footprint and matched mRNA measurements.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite result is a bounded absorption record, not a causal mechanism.  tRNA gene copy number is used as a proxy for tRNA availability, not as direct tRNA-seq abundance.  The tAI wobble parameters are inherited from a modeling convention, so the measured-tRNA surface is still partly model-mediated.  The small absorption values mean that, on this panel, tRNA-gene-copy tAI does not remove most of the modeled-complement residual.  Zebrafish shows the strongest absorption, but even there the combined residual remains $0.835657382343872$.  Human remains essentially unchanged at $0.92041342656924$, and yeast remains at $0.20648621019305421$.
