\subsection{Measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI as a bounded absorption test for the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ modeled residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface absorbs the modeled-tAI-complement component of the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ abundance residual. The finite surface contains nine residual coordinates, namely $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The control matrix has 24 columns with rank 23 in each organism. The controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from those codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^\ast_{Q6}$ vectors. Thus the chapter is not using tRNA gene copy number as a free biological explanation; it asks a narrower finite question: after the sequence-composition controls are fitted, how much of the modeled residual component remains after adding measured gene-copy tAI?

\paragraph{Organism rows and tRNA tables.}
Three organism joins are present in the measured-tRNA absorption run. For \textit{Danio rerio}, the full CDS join has $n=14410$ rows, the anticodon GCN table has 57 entries, and 8846 tRNA genes are parsed. For \textit{Homo sapiens}, the join has $n=19053$ rows, 47 anticodon entries, and 431 tRNA genes. For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the join has $n=5815$ rows, 41 anticodon entries, and 273 tRNA genes. The usable measured-tAI record counts are 8607, 422, and 268, respectively, with zero raw $W$ entries filled by geometric mean in all three organisms. The parsed tRNA headers exclude three selenocysteine, 25 suppressor, and five undetermined-anticodon records in \textit{D. rerio}; one selenocysteine record in human; and two undetermined-anticodon records in yeast. These exclusions matter because the readout is a codon-adaptation proxy derived from gene-copy anticodon counts, not a direct measurement of charged tRNA abundance or ribosome motion.

\paragraph{Matched modeled-complement check.}
The residual component being absorbed is stable on this join. In each organism the current full-join modeled-complement value exactly matches the reference abundance/CDS complement value to the recorded precision:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute difference from the complement reference is $0.0$ for all three organisms, with the same join sizes as the complement reference: 14410, 19053, and 5815. This is stronger than a merely descriptive availability check. It says that adding the measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface did not change the residual target through a different row set. The absorption quantities are therefore interpretable as projections against the same modeled residual surface, bounded by the stated controls.

\paragraph{Measured-tRNA absorption values.}
The actual absorption by measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI is small in all three organisms. The recorded values are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.020817102845637092, & d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.00005426229300865148, & d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.001530747712165342, & d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest absorption is in \textit{D. rerio}, about $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement residual surface under this projection. Human is nearly unchanged, and yeast shows a small but nonzero absorption. This pattern is not a universal translation rule. It is a finite projection statement: measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI removes a limited part of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual, while most of the residual remains outside this particular tRNA proxy.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency rows.}
A separate measured-translation-efficiency run gives the local comparison point. That run uses the same nine $B^\ast_{Q6}$ coordinates and the same 24-column control design, but its readout is ribosome-profiling translation efficiency with matched abundance rows. The TE joins are $n=13140$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $n=3415$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for human, and $n=5128$ for yeast, again with rank 23 controls. In that run the measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast. The modeled-complement agreement is not uniform under the TE availability join: \textit{E. coli} and yeast match the full-join complement within $0.02$, whereas \textit{D. rerio} differs by $0.03219747984141397$ and human by $0.06427590857052146$. This comparison prevents a stronger inference from the tRNA-gene-copy run. The tRNA run has stable full joins for its own three organisms, but the measured-TE run shows that readout availability can alter the residual target when the row set changes.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific summary is exploratory rather than decisive. In the finite meta table, 16 organisms are assembled, with 12 complete rows for the tested $M_{QTP}$ comparisons. The complete organisms include yeast, \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, \textit{E. coli}, human, \textit{D. rerio}, chicken, \textit{Bacillus subtilis}, mouse, \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, and rat. Four organisms still need data for that comparison: \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, \textit{Halobacterium salinarum}, \textit{Dictyostelium discoideum}, and \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}. The domain test for $M_{QTP}$ has permutation right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$, and the dominant-coordinate-by-domain contingency has permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$. Continuous tests are likewise not decisive: $M_{QTP}$ versus GC3 has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, versus log tRNA-gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$, and versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$. These values support heterogeneity and finite-row reporting, not a cross-organism law.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The result is ready as a bounded cross-layer relation: a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI proxy has been projected against the modeled-tAI-complement $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual surface, with stable full joins and explicit composition controls in three organisms. The positive absorption values do not establish causality, direct tRNA abundance, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each of those claims. In particular, tRNA gene copy number is only a proxy for tRNA availability, the tAI wobble values are a modeling choice, and the absorption fraction is descriptive. Where absorption is small, the correct reading is not that the residual disappears, but that this measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface explains little of it under the finite controlled design.
