\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the modeled Q6 residual}
\label{subsec:measured-trna-tai-q6-modeled-residual-absorption}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer readout for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  The finite surface consists of coding sequences joined by organism to codon-count residual coordinates, a modeled-tAI-complement abundance component, and a measured tRNA gene-copy tAI score computed from organism-specific anticodon copy tables.  The coordinate list is fixed at nine entries:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The controls are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each organism the control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, so the fitted residual comparison is not using an under-rank control table.

\paragraph{Joined organism rows.}
The tRNA-copy contact is complete on the same CDS join as the modeled-complement reference for the three organisms used here.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the joined table contains $n=14410$ CDS rows, $8846$ parsed tRNA genes, and $57$ anticodon gene-copy bins.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the joined table contains $n=19053$ CDS rows, $431$ parsed tRNA genes, and $47$ anticodon bins.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined table contains $n=5815$ CDS rows, $273$ parsed tRNA genes, and $41$ anticodon bins.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are respectively $8607$, $422$, and $268$; no raw $W$ entries required geometric-mean filling in any of the three organisms.  The parser excluded $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon headers in zebrafish; $1$ selenocysteine header in human; and $2$ undetermined-anticodon headers in yeast.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement identity check.}
The first finite check is that the current tRNA-copy join preserves the modeled-complement residual target.  It does: the current $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ equals the complement-reference value to reported precision in all three organisms.  The values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.874628721049266,\quad |\Delta|=0,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455,\quad |\Delta|=0,\\
\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.21173219572706703,\quad |\Delta|=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This matters because the absorption test is only interpretable when the candidate readout is evaluated against the same modeled-complement residual target rather than against a different availability-filtered row set.

\paragraph{Absorption readback.}
Projecting the modeled-tAI-complement residual onto residualized measured tRNA-copy tAI gives a small descriptive absorption in each organism.  The largest value is in zebrafish, where measured tRNA-copy tAI absorbs $0.020817102845637092$ and lowers the combined residual distance to $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.835657382343872$.  Human shows essentially no absorption at this boundary, $0.00005426229300865148$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.92041342656924$.  Yeast is also small, $0.001530747712165342$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  Thus the finite verdict is not that measured tRNA-copy tAI explains the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual, but that the readout was joined on a full-rank control surface and its descriptive absorption was bounded organism by organism.

\paragraph{Measured TE comparison.}
A separate measured-translation-efficiency contact gives a useful scale for the boundary.  On the measured TE joins, the descriptive absorption by measured TE is $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast.  The TE joins have $n=13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$ rows respectively, again with rank $23$ for the same $24$-column control design.  These TE values do not upgrade the tRNA-copy result to a mechanism; they only show that both a modeled tRNA-copy score and a measured TE score can be placed on controlled finite surfaces with small descriptive absorption values.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The wider organism-specific summary remains exploratory.  In the available meta table, the complete-feature row count is $12$ out of $16$ assembled organisms.  The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  Continuous meta tests are also non-resolving at this scale: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$; and versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$.  These rows support a descriptive organism-specific record, not a cross-organism law.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The contact used here is tRNA gene copy number converted into a tAI-style score; it is a proxy for tRNA availability, not direct tRNA abundance and not ribosome occupancy.  The wobble parameters are a modeling choice, and the projection is statistical rather than causal.  Consequently, this chapter licenses only the finite absorption entries stated above.  It does not establish translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger statement requires its own matched contact and its own controlled finite statistic.
