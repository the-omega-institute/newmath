\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-measured-trna-tai-q6-absorption}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This record tests a narrow cross-layer question: after the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates have been residualized against ordinary sequence-composition controls, how much of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component is absorbed by a measured tRNA gene-copy tAI readout?  The tested coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organism used for this tRNA-contact computation: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions measured from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same CDS counts, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The tRNA-copy tAI contact is evaluated on three full organism joins.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the join contains $14{,}410$ CDS rows, $8{,}846$ parsed tRNA genes, an anticodon-gene-copy table of size $57$, and $8{,}607$ usable measured-tAI records.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the join contains $19{,}053$ CDS rows, $431$ parsed tRNA genes, an anticodon table of size $47$, and $422$ usable measured-tAI records.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the join contains $5{,}815$ CDS rows, $273$ parsed tRNA genes, an anticodon table of size $41$, and $268$ usable measured-tAI records.  The parsed tRNA headers exclude nonstandard categories before tAI construction: \emph{Danio rerio} removes $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon entries; \emph{Homo sapiens} removes $1$ selenocysteine entry; \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} removes $2$ undetermined-anticodon entries.  No zero raw $W$ value is filled by a geometric mean in any of the three organisms.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability.}
The tRNA-copy contact is computed on the same full abundance--CDS joins used by the modeled-complement reference, so the modeled residual target is not perturbed by contact availability.  The equality check is exact in all three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.874628721049266, & |\Delta| &= 0,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9208949355679455, & |\Delta| &= 0,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21173219572706703, & |\Delta| &= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tRNA-copy absorption values below are not artifacts of a reduced protein identifier join.  They are descriptive projections of the same modeled-complement component onto a residualized tAI surface built from organism-specific tRNA gene-copy tables and fixed wobble parameters.

\paragraph{Observed absorption.}
The measured tRNA-copy tAI contact absorbs only a small fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component.  The largest value is in \emph{Danio rerio}, where the absorbed fraction is $0.020817102845637092$ and the combined residual fraction is $0.835657382343872$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the absorbed fraction is $0.00005426229300865148$ and the combined residual fraction is $0.92041342656924$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the absorbed fraction is $0.001530747712165342$ and the combined residual fraction is $0.20648621019305421$.  In compact form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 2.0817102845637092\times 10^{-2},\\
 A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5},\\
 A_{\mathrm{tRNA}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 1.530747712165342\times 10^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore not a broad absorption of the non-modeled component by measured tRNA gene-copy tAI.  It is a bounded, organism-specific readout in which the proxy tRNA surface removes a visible but still small part of the modeled-complement residual in zebrafish and almost none in the human and yeast joins.

\paragraph{Translation-efficiency comparison.}
The separate measured-translation-efficiency contact gives a useful scale for the same residual family.  In that computation, the controlled TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding joined row counts are $13{,}140$, $3{,}415$, $11{,}554$, and $5{,}128$, again with control rank $23$.  These TE values are also small, but they are not identical to the tRNA-copy tAI values: in zebrafish, tRNA-copy tAI absorption is slightly larger than the measured-TE absorption; in human, measured TE absorbs more than tRNA-copy tAI; in yeast, tRNA-copy tAI absorbs more than measured TE but both remain near zero on the modeled-complement scale.  This comparison supports a bounded reading: measured tRNA-copy tAI and measured TE are finite readouts of translation-adjacent surfaces, not interchangeable evidence for a single mechanism.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific survey reinforces this caution.  Across the complete meta rows, the $M\_{QTP}$ fraction has only weak permutation-tested association with the continuous organism proxies: for GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$ over $12$ organisms; for $\log$ tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$; and for $\log$ proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$.  The domain test is likewise exploratory rather than decisive: the between-domain statistic has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$, using one archaeal, three bacterial, and eight eukaryotic complete-feature organisms.  The dominant-coordinate contingency has Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, but its right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814185814$.  These finite rows do not support a universal organism-domain law for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The positive statement licensed here is deliberately modest.  On three full organism joins, a measured tRNA gene-copy tAI surface can be constructed without zero-$W$ imputation and can be projected against the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual after the declared sequence-composition controls.  The resulting absorption is finite and reproducible on the stated rows, but it is small: roughly $2.08\%$ in zebrafish, $0.0054\%$ in human, and $0.153\%$ in yeast.  The result therefore leaves most of the modeled-complement component outside this tRNA-copy tAI readout, especially in human and yeast.  It is best read as a measured-proxy boundary test: tRNA gene copy number can touch the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface, but it does not close the residual as a translation explanation.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter does not infer translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality from the tRNA-copy tAI projection.  tRNA gene copy number is only a proxy for tRNA abundance, not direct tRNA-seq.  The tAI wobble parameters are a modeling choice.  The absorbed fraction is a descriptive projection statistic, not a mediator analysis and not a perturbation result.  A measured translation-efficiency claim requires its own joined ribosome-profiling and matched mRNA rows; a protein claim requires a protein-layer contact; structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and universal-law claims each require separate layer-matched contacts and finite statistics.
