\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the modeled-Q6 complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface can absorb any part of the abundance residual component that remains after the modeled-tAI direction has already been removed from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  The finite carrier is the organism-by-CDS matrix obtained by joining coding-sequence codon counts, the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates, the standard control block, and organism-specific tRNA gene-copy tables.  The coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control block has $24$ columns in each organism and rank $23$.  It contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The tested response is not a protein, phenotype, structural, or mechanistic variable.  It is a descriptive projection of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto a measured tRNA-copy tAI coordinate after the same control residualization.

\paragraph{Joined organism rows.}
The test is powered by three complete organism joins.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the full CDS join contains $n=14410$ rows, the parsed tRNA table contains $8846$ tRNA genes, and the anticodon gene-copy table has $57$ anticodon entries.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the full join contains $n=19053$ rows, with $431$ tRNA genes and $47$ anticodon entries.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the full join contains $n=5815$ rows, with $273$ tRNA genes and $41$ anticodon entries.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are $8607$, $422$, and $268$, respectively.  No raw codon weight was filled by a geometric mean in any of the three organisms: the fill count is $0$ for each row block.  The tRNA parser removed headers outside the sense-anticodon target: zebrafish excluded $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon headers; human excluded $1$ selenocysteine header; yeast excluded $2$ undetermined-anticodon headers.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
The first check is that the residual target is the same modeled-complement object used by the abundance-complement experiment.  In all three organisms the current full join exactly matches the complement reference join.  The equality is numerical, not just qualitative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Drerio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Hsapiens}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Scerevisiae}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute difference against the complement reference is $0.0$ in each organism, with reference rank $23$ throughout.  Thus the tRNA-copy tAI calculation is not being evaluated on a shifted row universe.  It is evaluated on the same full abundance-CDS residual target for which the modeled-tAI complement was defined.

\paragraph{Measured tRNA-copy absorption.}
Let $D_o$ denote the modeled complement fraction for organism $o$, and let $A^{\mathrm{tRNA}}_o$ denote the fraction of that complement absorbed after adding the measured tRNA-copy tAI readout to the controlled projection.  The computed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{\mathrm{tRNA}}_{\mathrm{Drerio}} &= 0.020817102845637092, & D^{\mathrm{combined}}_{\mathrm{Drerio}} &= 0.835657382343872,\\
A^{\mathrm{tRNA}}_{\mathrm{Hsapiens}} &= 0.00005426229300865148, & D^{\mathrm{combined}}_{\mathrm{Hsapiens}} &= 0.92041342656924,\\
A^{\mathrm{tRNA}}_{\mathrm{Scerevisiae}} &= 0.001530747712165342, & D^{\mathrm{combined}}_{\mathrm{Scerevisiae}} &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest finite-row absorption is therefore the zebrafish value, approximately $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement residual.  Yeast is smaller, approximately $0.153\%$, and human is effectively near zero at approximately $0.0054\%$.  This is a bounded absorption statement: measured tRNA gene copy number supplies a small descriptive readback in some organisms, but it does not collapse the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual into a tRNA-copy explanation.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency rows.}
A nearby measured translation-efficiency check gives a useful boundary for interpretation.  On all-readout TE joins, the row counts are $13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, each with the same $24$-column control block and rank $23$.  The measured-TE projection absorbs
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.01759429001506974 &\quad \text{in zebrafish},\\
0.0010982956489726373 &\quad \text{in } \emph{E. coli},\\
0.0028947149257963897 &\quad \text{in human},\\
0.0002762783754375593 &\quad \text{in yeast}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These TE numbers are not interchangeable with the tRNA-copy tAI numbers: the TE join changes the row universe in zebrafish and human, with modeled-complement differences $0.03219747984141397$ and $0.06427590857052146$ against the full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The tRNA-copy experiment, by contrast, stays on the full join and matches the complement reference exactly.  The two contacts therefore answer different finite questions: measured tRNA-copy tAI asks how much of the modeled-complement residual aligns with a genome-level tRNA-copy adaptation surface, while measured TE asks how much aligns with an observed ribosome-profiling efficiency surface on the rows where that readout is available.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The organism-specific meta table also argues against promoting the tRNA-copy absorption result to a universal rule.  Across the complete-feature organism set, the $M_{QTP}$ fraction has only weak continuous associations: against GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; against log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$; against log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$.  The domain comparison has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  The dominant-coordinate contingency has Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ but permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$ on $n=12$ complete rows.  These finite statistics support heterogeneity, not a resolved domain law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter records a positive but small measured-tRNA-copy tAI absorption surface for the modeled-$Q6$ complement.  The strongest entry is zebrafish, followed by yeast, with human near zero.  Since the residual target is stable across the three full joins, the result is suitable as a finite cross-layer readout for the tRNA-copy contact.  It is not evidence for direct ribosome occupancy, not a causal mediation test, and not a statement about protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organism phenotype, mechanism, or universality.  Any such interpretation would require an additional layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  Here the only licensed conclusion is narrower: measured tRNA gene-copy tAI absorbs a small organism-dependent fraction of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, while most of the modeled-complement residual remains outside this tRNA-copy explanation.
