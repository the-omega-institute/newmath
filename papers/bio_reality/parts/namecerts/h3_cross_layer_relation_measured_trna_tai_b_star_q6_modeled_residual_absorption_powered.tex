\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-measured-trna-tai-b-star-q6-modeled-residual-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-layer test in which the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are evaluated against a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface.  The coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The controlled background $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the same CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, where $M$-density is the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The design has $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each organism.  The tested readout is not direct tRNA-seq abundance.  It is a tRNA gene-copy proxy passed through the tAI construction, using the dos Reis wobble $s$-values as a modeling convention.

\paragraph{Matched organism panels.}
The finite rows are complete for three organisms on the full abundance-CDS join used by the modeled-complement comparison.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the panel has $n=14410$ joined CDS rows, $8846$ tRNA genes, and an anticodon gene-copy table of size $57$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the panel has $n=19053$ joined CDS rows, $431$ tRNA genes, and an anticodon table of size $47$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the panel has $n=5815$ joined CDS rows, $273$ tRNA genes, and an anticodon table of size $41$.  The parsed tRNA headers exclude nonstandard entries: \emph{D. rerio} excludes $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon records; human excludes $1$ selenocysteine record; yeast excludes $2$ undetermined-anticodon records.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are $8607$, $422$, and $268$, respectively, with zero raw-$W$ geometric-mean fill count equal to $0$ in all three organisms.

\paragraph{Complement identity check.}
Before the tRNA-copy tAI absorption is read as a bounded cross-layer statistic, the modeled-complement residual target is checked against the complement experiment on the same full join.  The equality check is exact at the reported precision in all three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{model,Dr} &= 0.874628721049266, & |\Delta_{Dr}| &= 0.0,\\
 d_{model,Hs} &= 0.9208949355679455, & |\Delta_{Hs}| &= 0.0,\\
 d_{model,Sc} &= 0.21173219572706703, & |\Delta_{Sc}| &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tRNA-copy test is not being run on a shifted protein-id subset: the current join sizes match the complement references, namely $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$.  This matters because a shifted join can change the residual target before any biological comparison is made.

\paragraph{Absorption values.}
Let $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component after the declared controls.  The measured tRNA-copy tAI projection asks how much of this residual component is absorbed by the tRNA-gene-copy tAI readout.  The finite results are small and organism-specific:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{trna}(Dr) &= 0.020817102845637092, & d_{combined}(Dr) &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 A_{trna}(Hs) &= 0.00005426229300865148, & d_{combined}(Hs) &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 A_{trna}(Sc) &= 0.001530747712165342, & d_{combined}(Sc) &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest absorption is in zebrafish, about $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement residual target.  Yeast is lower, about $0.153\%$.  Human is nearly zero at about $0.0054\%$.  The reported values therefore do not support a broad statement that measured tRNA-copy tAI explains the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled residual.  They support only a bounded finite-row statement: the residual component can be projected against a measured tRNA-copy tAI proxy, and the resulting absorption is positive but weak, with strong organism dependence.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
A separate measured-TE computation tests ribosome-profiling translation efficiency on all-readout joins after the same codon-derived controls, with joined row counts $13140$ in zebrafish, $3415$ in \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ in human, and $5128$ in yeast.  Its measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  That comparison is useful only as a neighboring bounded readout.  The tRNA-copy tAI result in this chapter is not a ribosome-footprint measurement, and the measured-TE result is not a tRNA-gene-copy measurement.  The two surfaces may be placed side by side as finite cross-layer projections, but neither one promotes the other to a causal mechanism.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The chapter licenses a descriptive absorption statement and no more.  The tRNA data are gene-copy counts, not direct tRNA abundance; the tAI weights depend on a wobble-parameter convention; and the absorption statistic is a projection, not an intervention.  Consequently, no translation mechanism, ribosome-occupancy mechanism, protein-abundance statement, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, phenotype statement, universality statement, or causal claim follows from these rows alone.  Any such assertion would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  If the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual remains after this tRNA-copy tAI projection, the correct reading is that the modeled residual is not absorbed by this bounded translation-proxy surface, leaving other hypotheses such as measured TE, turnover, localization, protein-state, or condition-specific readouts to be tested separately.
