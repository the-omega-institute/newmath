\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy adaptation against the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This chapter records a bounded cross-layer test in which the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are compared with an organism-matched tRNA-gene-copy adaptation surface.  The tested coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The readout is not a protein, structure, phenotype, or mechanism measurement.  It asks only whether a tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface absorbs part of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ after the sequence controls have already removed the declared baseline geometry.

\paragraph{Controls and rank condition.}
For each organism the control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 from real CDS codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The resulting control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every reported organism.  Thus the chapter uses the same residual coordinate surface before the tRNA-copy tAI projection is read.  In symbols, the finite operation is the descriptive comparison
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{trna}(o) &= d_{modeled}(o)-d_{combined}(o),\\
S_{trna}(o) &= \mathrm{absorbed\_by\_trna}(o),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $o$ ranges over the organisms with joined coding-sequence and tRNA-copy rows.  The value $S_{trna}(o)$ is a descriptive absorption scalar only; it is not a causal mediator coefficient.

\paragraph{Joined organism rows.}
Three organisms have complete rows for this tRNA-copy tAI contact.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the joined coding-sequence panel has $n=14410$ rows, the tRNA parser reports $8846$ tRNA genes, and the anticodon gene-copy table has size $57$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the joined panel has $n=19053$ rows, $431$ tRNA genes, and anticodon table size $47$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined panel has $n=5815$ rows, $273$ tRNA genes, and anticodon table size $41$.  The usable measured-tAI record counts are $8607$, $422$, and $268$, respectively, and no zero raw $W$ value was filled by a geometric mean in any of the three organisms.  The parser excluded $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon headers in \emph{D. rerio}; $1$ selenocysteine header in human; and $2$ undetermined-anticodon headers in yeast.

\paragraph{Absorption values.}
The modeled-complement self-check is exact on this contact because the tRNA-copy panel uses the full abundance-CDS join for each organism.  The modeled-complement reference and current full-join values are identical: $0.874628721049266$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, and $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast, with absolute difference $0.0$ in all three cases.  After adding the tRNA-copy tAI surface, the combined residual values are $0.835657382343872$, $0.92041342656924$, and $0.20648621019305421$.  The corresponding absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{trna}(\mathrm{D.\ rerio}) &= 0.020817102845637092,\\
S_{trna}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.00005426229300865148,\\
S_{trna}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.001530747712165342.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest finite-row absorption is therefore the zebrafish value, about $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement component under this projection convention.  Human and yeast remain close to zero on this contact.  The tRNA-copy tAI surface therefore has a small but nonzero descriptive contact with the modeled-complement residual in one organism and much weaker contact in the other two reported organisms.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The same nine-coordinate residual system was also tested against measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency with matched mRNA information available on a separate panel.  That panel contains $n=13140$ rows for \emph{D. rerio}, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, again with $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in every organism.  Its measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  Those rows are relevant because they show that the tRNA-copy tAI contact is not being read as direct ribosome occupancy.  It is a modeled adaptation surface whose finite absorption can be compared with, but not substituted for, a measured TE surface.  In particular, the zebrafish tRNA-copy absorption $0.020817102845637092$ and measured-TE absorption $0.01759429001506974$ are of similar small magnitude, while the human tRNA-copy absorption $0.00005426229300865148$ is far below the human measured-TE absorption $0.0028947149257963897$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The wider organism-specific summary remains exploratory.  In the meta table, the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction has weak continuous associations in the same table: versus GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log protein count, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and versus log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The domain variance-ratio comparison gives $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These scalars prevent promotion from the three-organism tRNA-copy absorption table to a broad domain law.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The result is a finite descriptive contact: tRNA-gene-copy tAI absorbs a small part of $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ in zebrafish, almost none in human, and a small amount in yeast, under the declared $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate and sequence-control surface.  This is compatible with the claim that the residual basis can be compared with translation-adaptation readouts, but it does not establish translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  A stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched contact: direct tRNA abundance or charging for the tRNA layer, ribosome-profiling rows for measured translation, proteomics for protein output, structure or physical assays for admissibility, and perturbation or functional readouts for mechanism and phenotype.
