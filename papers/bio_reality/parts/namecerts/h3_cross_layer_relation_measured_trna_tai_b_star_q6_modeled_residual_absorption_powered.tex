\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the modeled residual component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI readout absorbs the modeled-tAI-complement component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface. The carrier is a finite CDS-by-organism table: each row is a coding sequence joined to codon-count coordinates, the baseline covariates, and an organism-specific tRNA-copy-derived tAI value. The residual basis has nine named coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.
The control design uses 24 columns with rank $23$ in each organism: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The organism row counts are $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; these are full joins against the complement reference, so the reported $d_{modeled}$ values are not changed by tRNA availability filtering.

\paragraph{tRNA contact construction.}
The measured tAI contact is built from tRNA gene-copy counts, not from direct tRNA abundance sequencing. The parsed tRNA gene counts are $8846$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $431$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $273$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Their anticodon GCN table sizes are respectively $57$, $47$, and $41$. The usable measured-tAI record counts are $8607$, $422$, and $268$, with zero raw $W$ entries filled by geometric mean in all three organisms. The excluded tRNA headers are also finite and organism-specific: \emph{Danio rerio} excludes $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon headers; \emph{Homo sapiens} excludes $1$ selenocysteine header; yeast excludes $2$ undetermined-anticodon headers. Thus the contact is a bounded gene-copy readback of codon adaptation, using dos Reis style wobble parameters as a modelling convention, rather than an observation of elongating ribosomes.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
Let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the abundance residual component left after the modeled-tAI direction has been removed under the stated controls. The measured-tRNA tAI candidate is assessed by projecting this component onto the residualized measured tAI column. The complement distance is unchanged relative to the reference complement experiment in all three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute difference from the reference complement value is $0.0$ for each organism, with reference rank $23$ throughout. The measured-tRNA absorption values are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 a_{tRNA}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.020817102845637092, & d_{combined} &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 a_{tRNA}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.00005426229300865148, & d_{combined} &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 a_{tRNA}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.001530747712165342, & d_{combined} &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest absorption is therefore in \emph{Danio rerio}, where the measured-tRNA tAI readout removes about $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement residual component. The human value is effectively negligible on this scale, and the yeast value is small. This pattern supports a descriptive statement only: the measured tRNA-copy tAI column can be inserted into the residual projection and its finite absorption can be reported, but the resulting values are not a causal mediation test.

\paragraph{Relation to measured TE rows.}
A separate measured-translation-efficiency run supplies a useful boundary check. In that run the joined TE rows are $13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, with the same 24-column control design and rank $23$. The measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast. These TE rows are not substituted for the measured-tRNA tAI rows, because the two contacts have different empirical meanings: TE is a ribosome-footprint-over-mRNA readout, while measured-tRNA tAI is a tRNA-copy-derived adaptation score. Their agreement in being small is informative, but it does not identify a mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For this finite panel, the measured-tRNA tAI contact is computable, rank-admissible, and joined without changing the modeled-complement reference surface in the three reported organisms. The result is a bounded cross-layer relation: the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement abundance component is only weakly absorbed by measured tRNA-copy tAI, with the largest reported absorption in \emph{Danio rerio}. If this contact is used as evidence, it should be stated as a descriptive residual-projection fact on the declared finite rows. It does not establish direct translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Any such claim requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
