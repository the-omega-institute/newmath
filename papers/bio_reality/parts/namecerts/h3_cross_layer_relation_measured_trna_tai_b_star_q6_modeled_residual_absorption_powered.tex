\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-residual component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-modeled-residual-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite contact.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer question: whether organism-specific tRNA gene-copy tAI, computed from measured tRNA gene-copy tables and a fixed wobble model, absorbs any part of the modeled-tAI-complement component carried by the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates. The coordinate surface is not treated as a biological observation by itself. It is first residualized against amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, and only then compared with a finite tRNA-copy readback. The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The control matrix has $24$ declared columns and rank $23$ in each organism considered here.

\paragraph{Joined rows and tRNA-copy readback.}
The finite panel contains three organisms with full codon-usage joins for this contact. In \emph{Danio rerio}, the joined CDS count is $14410$, the parsed tRNA table contains $8846$ tRNA genes, and the anticodon gene-copy table has $57$ entries. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the joined CDS count is $19053$, the parsed tRNA table contains $431$ tRNA genes, and the anticodon table has $47$ entries. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined CDS count is $5815$, the parsed tRNA table contains $273$ tRNA genes, and the anticodon table has $41$ entries. The usable measured-tAI record counts are $8607$, $422$, and $268$ respectively, with no zero raw $W$ values filled by geometric means in any of the three organisms.

\paragraph{Modeled residual target.}
The tested target is the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$, not total abundance and not a causal mediator. The complement self-check shows exact agreement with the reference full-join complement values in all three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{modeled}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{modeled}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{modeled}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each of these rows the absolute difference from the complement reference is $0.0$, so the tRNA-copy test is performed on the same residual target rather than on a coverage-shifted subset.

\paragraph{Absorption values.}
After residualization, measured tRNA-copy tAI accounts for only a small fraction of the modeled-complement component. The observed absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{tRNA}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.020817102845637092,\\
 A_{tRNA}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.00005426229300865148,\\
 A_{tRNA}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.001530747712165342.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding combined residual fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{combined}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.835657382343872,\\
 d_{combined}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.92041342656924,\\
 d_{combined}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the largest tRNA-copy absorption occurs in \emph{D. rerio}, but even there the absorbed fraction is approximately $2.08\%$. In human and yeast the measured tRNA-copy tAI contact leaves almost all of the modeled-complement residual unchanged. This supports a descriptive finite-row conclusion: the gene-copy tAI proxy does not materially close the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement component in this panel.

\paragraph{Measured translation-efficiency comparison.}
A separate ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact gives a useful boundary check. On the measured-TE all-readout joins, the matched row counts are $13140$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, again with rank-$23$ controls. The measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively. These values are also small, and two organisms have complement-target shifts above the $0.02$ self-check tolerance: $0.03219747984141397$ in \emph{D. rerio} and $0.06427590857052146$ in human. This comparison prevents over-reading the tRNA-copy result as a direct ribosome readout. The tRNA-copy table is a proxy surface; ribosome-profiling TE is a distinct measured surface with its own row availability and its own residual target checks.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific summary is heterogeneous rather than resolved into a domain-level rule. In the finite meta table, the complete-feature row count is $12$ among $16$ assembled organisms. The dominant-coordinate contingency test across domain has Pearson $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ permutations. The $M_{QTP}$ fraction tests likewise do not license a general biological law: the correlations are $r=-0.3062616500469447$ against GC3 with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ against log proteome size with $p=0.913086913086913$, and $r=0.06029405206037823$ against log tRNA gene count with $p=0.8621378621378621$. These numbers place the tRNA-copy absorption result inside a small, descriptive, non-phylogenetically-corrected organism panel.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row result is therefore a bounded negative-to-small absorption record. Measured tRNA gene-copy tAI can be computed cleanly on the three full joins, and it absorbs a nonzero but small part of the modeled-complement abundance residual. The result does not establish a translation mechanism, does not prove ribosome occupancy, and does not imply protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. Within the present contact, the defensible statement is only that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual remains largely present after this tRNA-copy tAI readback.
