\subsection{Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption of the modeled residual component}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-measured-trna-tai-residual-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns the claim identified by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.measured\_trna\_tai.b\_star\_q6\_modeled\_residual\_absorption\_powered}.  The finite panel joins coding-sequence rows to the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$, then asks whether a measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI surface absorbs the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$.  The control block has $24$ columns with rank $23$ in each organism.  Its explicit covariates are an intercept, $\log({\rm cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined organism rows.}
The tRNA-copy panel is full-join relative to the reported CDS join for each organism in this run.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=14410$ CDS rows are joined, the anticodon gene-copy table has $57$ entries, and the parsed tRNA set contains $8846$ genes.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=19053$ CDS rows are joined, the anticodon table has $47$ entries, and the parsed tRNA set contains $431$ genes.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=5815$ CDS rows are joined, the anticodon table has $41$ entries, and the parsed tRNA set contains $273$ genes.  The tRNA FASTA parsing removed special headers rather than forcing them into sense-anticodon weights: zebrafish excluded $3$ selenocysteine, $25$ suppressor, and $5$ undetermined-anticodon headers; human excluded $1$ selenocysteine header; yeast excluded $2$ undetermined-anticodon headers.  The usable measured-tAI record counts after this parsing were $8607$, $422$, and $268$, respectively, and no organism required geometric-mean filling for a zero raw $W$ value.

\paragraph{Residual absorption numbers.}
The descriptive absorption calculation gives a small but nonzero measured-tRNA-copy contribution in all three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
{\rm Danio\ rerio:}      &\quad {\rm absorbed\_by\_trna}=0.020817102845637092, & d_{\rm combined}=0.835657382343872, \\
{\rm Homo\ sapiens:}    &\quad {\rm absorbed\_by\_trna}=0.00005426229300865148, & d_{\rm combined}=0.92041342656924, \\
{\rm Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae:} &\quad {\rm absorbed\_by\_trna}=0.001530747712165342, & d_{\rm combined}=0.20648621019305421.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite rows reproduce the modeled-complement reference exactly within the stated tolerance: the absolute difference is $0.0$ in zebrafish, human, and yeast.  The corresponding modeled-complement values are $0.874628721049266$, $0.9208949355679455$, and $0.21173219572706703$, with reference join counts $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$.  This exact agreement matters because the absorption is not being estimated on a shifted availability surface: unlike sparse feature contacts, the tRNA-copy surface does not change the residual target used by the modeled-complement calculation.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency rows.}
The paired measured-TE fact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.h\_candidate\_measured\_te.b\_star\_q6\_nontranslation\_residual\_powered} supplies the neighboring ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency comparison.  On the measured-TE join, the same nine coordinates and the same control design are used, but the row set differs by organism: zebrafish has $n=13140$ TE-valid rows from $14410$ CDS-abundance rows, \emph{E. coli} has $n=3415$ from $3739$, human has $n=11554$ from $19053$, and yeast has $n=5128$ from $5815$.  The measured-TE descriptive absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  Thus the tRNA-copy tAI contact is not being used as a substitute for ribosome profiling; it is a separate codon-adaptation proxy surface whose strongest absorption in this run is zebrafish at approximately $2.08\%$ of the modeled-complement residual, while the measured-TE contact has its own availability and its own organism-specific descriptive projection.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The organism-specific meta fact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.organism\_specificity\_meta.b\_star\_q6\_cross\_organism\_powered} shows why this record should be read as a finite panel rather than a universal rule.  In that meta table, the complete-feature organism count is $12$ out of a larger assembled row count of $16$.  The $M\_QTP$ fraction versus GC3 test has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$ over $1000$ trials; the log tRNA-gene-count comparison has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$; and the domain comparison has permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These numbers do not establish a resolved phylogenetic or domain law.  They only place the tRNA-copy absorption result inside a heterogeneous cross-organism coordinate landscape.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The run is ready as a bounded descriptive absorption record.  It supports the statement that measured tRNA gene-copy tAI, computed from parsed organism-specific tRNA gene-copy tables and dos Reis-style wobble weights, can be projected against $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ on a full CDS join and that the resulting absorbed fractions are finite, reproducible, and small.  The largest finite effect in this record is the zebrafish value $0.020817102845637092$; the human value is nearly zero at $5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5}$; and the yeast value is $0.001530747712165342$.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
This chapter does not claim that tRNA gene copy number is direct tRNA abundance, that tAI is an observed ribosome-occupancy measurement, or that the absorbed fraction is causal.  The result also does not license a translation-realization claim, a protein-output claim, a folding or structural-order claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a function claim, a mechanistic claim, or a universal biological law.  Each of those assertions would require its own layer-matched contact and statistic.  Here the only positive assertion is the finite descriptive projection of a measured tRNA-copy tAI surface against the modeled-tAI-complement residual under the stated coding-sequence controls.
