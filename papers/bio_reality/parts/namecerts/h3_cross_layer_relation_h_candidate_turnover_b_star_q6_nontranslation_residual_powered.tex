\subsection{Turnover Contact for the Q6 Non-translation Residual}
\label{sec:biorq-q6-turnover-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether measured protein turnover can account for the part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after the modeled tAI contact has been removed.  The finite carrier is the joined yeast surface containing coding-sequence codon counts, protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, and SGD protein half-life rows.  The residual side has nine projected coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control block has 24 columns and rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The turnover readout is $\log_{10}$ protein half-life from the SGD backend protein-experiment-details surface associated with Christiano et al. 2014, mapped by taking the SGD systematic locus name to the STRING protein identifier via the prefix $4932.$.  The parsed turnover table contains $3640$ proteins, and the payload hash recorded for the retrieved turnover data is \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.

\paragraph{Join geometry and measured rows.}
The same yeast background reports $6351$ valid abundance proteins, $5815$ CDS rows joined to abundance, $5128$ valid translation-efficiency proteins, and $3640$ valid turnover proteins.  The turnover-abundance-CDS join contains $3640$ rows, and the joint matrix with measured translation efficiency, turnover, abundance, and CDS rows contains $3537$ rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, while the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The skipped-record accounting is also finite: there are no duplicate, missing, or nonpositive abundance records; no duplicate, missing, or nonpositive turnover records; no duplicate, missing, non-object, nonpositive footprint, nonpositive mRNA, or nonpositive TE records; and among CDS records the only substantive losses are $2175$ rows without a turnover match and $103$ rows without an optional measured-TE match.  Thus the readout is not a symbolic route or a contact name alone: it is an explicitly joined yeast table with a defined rank, a defined residual basis, and a measured half-life column.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability.}
The turnover experiment is anchored to the same modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual used in the preceding Q6 contact series.  On the turnover join the squared norm of the projected Q6 component is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the modeled-complement fraction on this join is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled,current}}=0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The full abundance-CDS complement reference for yeast is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ joined rows.  The turnover join therefore differs from the full-join reference by $0.011538255214205373$, which is inside the declared $0.02$ stability tolerance.  This matters because the turnover table changes the available protein universe from $5815$ abundance-CDS rows to $3640$ turnover rows.  Within that restricted universe, the target residual remains close enough to the reference yeast complement to support a bounded turnover-contact test rather than merely a coverage artifact.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The actual half-life projection is small.  Regressing the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual against residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The second number is nearly unchanged from $d_{\mathrm{modeled,current}}=0.2232704509412724$.  In this finite row set, measured steady-state protein half-life therefore removes less than one thousandth of the remaining modeled-tAI-complement Q6 abundance component.  The result is not a positive turnover explanation for the Q6 residual; it is a bounded negative contact showing that this yeast half-life surface does not materially absorb the residual left after the modeled translation-adaptation surface.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The turnover packet sits next to, but is not identical with, the measured-translation-efficiency packet.  In yeast the measured-TE all-readout join has $5128$ rows, rank $23$ controls, $P_Q$ norm squared $675.3275479192903$, $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ norm squared $151.18264752811882$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$.  The measured-TE absorption in that yeast surface is only $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover surface has fewer rows but a stable modeled-complement fraction relative to the abundance-CDS reference; both contacts say that the yeast non-translation residual is modest in absolute size and is not appreciably absorbed by either aggregate TE or steady-state half-life in these measurements.

\paragraph{Relation to modeled tRNA adaptation.}
The modeled-readout context is also finite.  In the measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact, yeast uses $273$ tRNA genes, an anticodon table of size $41$, and $5815$ joined CDS rows, again with control rank $23$.  The full-join modeled complement is exactly the yeast reference value $0.21173219572706703$, and the measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption is $0.001530747712165342$, giving $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  This provides a useful scale: the direct tRNA-copy adaptation proxy absorbs a small but larger fraction than turnover in yeast, while neither contact closes the residual as a mechanism.  The turnover entry is therefore best read as an H-candidate screen after modeled adaptation, not as an explanation of why the residual exists.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The broader organism-specific Q6 survey gives no license to turn the yeast turnover result into a universal law.  The complete-feature meta table has $12$ organisms out of a $16$-row assembled panel, and the available permutation tests are weakly resolved: for $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus domain the right-tail permutation value is $0.4155844155844156$; for dominant coordinate versus domain it is $0.5814185814185814$ with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ on $12$ organisms; for $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 the two-sided permutation value is $0.35264735264735264$ with $r=-0.3062616500469447$; for log proteome size it is $0.913086913086913$ with $r=-0.033628115789504955$; and for log tRNA gene count it is $0.8621378621378621$ with $r=0.06029405206037823$.  These numbers are useful as descriptive guardrails, but they do not make a phylogenetically corrected, cross-domain, or causal turnover statement.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite conclusion is therefore narrow.  In the yeast joined half-life surface, the Q6 modeled-tAI-complement residual is present at about $d\approx 0.223$, the join remains close to the full abundance-CDS complement reference, and measured protein turnover absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ of that component.  The contact supports the statement that Christiano-style steady-state half-life is not a strong absorber of this particular yeast Q6 non-translation residual.  It does not establish translation realization, ribosome mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Any such statement would require a separate measured contact at the appropriate layer, with its own joined rows, controls, and statistic.  Here the only licensed claim is a bounded descriptive projection: turnover was measured on a rank-supported yeast table, the residual target was stable under the turnover join, and the resulting absorption was negligible.
