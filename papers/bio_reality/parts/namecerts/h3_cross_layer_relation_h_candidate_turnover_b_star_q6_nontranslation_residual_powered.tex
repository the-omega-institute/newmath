\subsection{Protein Turnover as a B*$\_Q6$ Non-translation Residual Contact}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-nontranslation-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} The turnover contact tests whether a residual component that remains after a modeled translation-adaptation projection is measurably aligned with a protein half-life readout in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The finite carrier is the joined table of coding-sequence codon counts, whole-organism protein abundance, ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and SGD half-life records, restricted to rows whose identifiers can be placed on the same protein axis. The test uses nine B*$\_Q6$ residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The control design has 24 columns and rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same codon counts, and an M-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected B*$\_Q6$ vectors.

\paragraph{Row accounting.} The turnover readout is yeast-only. The source half-life table contains 3640 valid turnover proteins, and all 3640 enter the abundance--CDS--turnover join. The CDS join reports 5815 rows, the abundance table has 6351 valid protein identifiers, and the matched ribosome-profiling TE table has 5128 valid protein identifiers. The fully joined abundance--CDS--TE--turnover panel contains 3537 rows; the abundance--CDS--turnover panel contains 3640 rows. The reported CDS hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$. The identifier route is concrete: SGD locus systematic names are mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name. No abundance record is skipped for missing, duplicate, or nonpositive protein identifiers; no turnover record is skipped for missing, duplicate, or nonpositive half-life; the CDS side skips 2175 rows for absence of a turnover match and 103 rows for absence of an optional measured-TE match.

\paragraph{Turnover readout.} The measured half-life coordinate is the SGD protein-experiment-details \texttt{data\_value} in hours, attributed to the Christiano 2014 per-locus protein half-life source. The payload has 77817 bytes and SHA-256 digest \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}. The tested scalar is $\log\_{10}$ half-life after the same finite-row residualization discipline used for the B*$\_Q6$ coordinates. The paired translation-efficiency contact is available for this yeast panel: in the Ingolia et al. 2009 Science GSE13750 rich-medium data, TE is defined as mean footprint normalized read density across two replicates divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across two matched replicates. The companion measured-TE archive gives, for yeast, $n=5128$, $rank(Z)=23$, and measured-TE absorption $0.0002762783754375593$ with combined residual dimension $d\_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.} Let $\widehat P\_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the abundance residual component left after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has been removed under the declared controls. On the turnover join, the residual energy numbers are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\widehat P\_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|\widehat P\_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d\_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The turnover contact then projects $\widehat P\_{Q\perp Tmod}$ onto residualized $\log\_{10}$ half-life. The computed turnover absorption is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d\_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the half-life coordinate explains less than one tenth of one percent of the modeled-translation complement in this finite yeast table. The contact is therefore a nearly null turnover absorption result, not a positive turnover explanation of the B*$\_Q6$ complement.

\paragraph{Join-stability check.} The same turnover join is checked against the full abundance--CDS complement reference. The full reference has $5815$ joined rows and $d\_{modeled}=0.21173219572706703$. The current turnover join has $3640$ rows and $d\_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$. Their absolute difference is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|0.2232704509412724 - 0.21173219572706703| &= 0.011538255214205373,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is below the declared $0.02$ stability tolerance. This matters because the turnover panel is smaller than the full abundance--CDS panel; the finite result is still read on a residual target close enough to the reference yeast complement to be reported as a stable descriptive contact.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The result says that, in this yeast-only steady-state half-life panel, protein turnover does not materially absorb the B*$\_Q6$ abundance residual left after the modeled translation-adaptation contact. Since $d\_{modeled}\approx 0.2233$ is already modest for yeast, the absolute non-translation residual available for downstream explanation is small; nevertheless the tested half-life readout accounts for only $0.0009086031889966327$ of that component. The finite contact therefore leaves the residual mostly outside transcription, modeled translation adaptation, measured TE, and this single turnover readout. It points toward remaining bounded candidates such as localization, complex membership, condition specificity, or annotation coverage, but it does not establish any of them.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.} This chapter licenses only a finite descriptive projection statement about one yeast turnover dataset. It does not show a causal mediator, a translation mechanism, a ribosome-occupancy law, a protein-abundance law, folding behavior, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality. In particular, neither a small absorption value nor a stable join comparison turns B*$\_Q6$ residual geometry into biological mechanism. Any claim about translation realization, folded structure, physical feasibility, functional consequence, causal process, or cross-organism law requires its own layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.
