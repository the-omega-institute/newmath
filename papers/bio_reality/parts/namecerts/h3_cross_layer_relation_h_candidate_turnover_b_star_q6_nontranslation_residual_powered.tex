\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded non-translation residual contact}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-nontranslation-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component, after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has been removed, is materially accounted for by a measured protein-turnover readout.  The finite panel is restricted to \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The turnover contact uses SGD protein half-life values from the Christiano 2014 per-locus protein-experiment endpoint, mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing the systematic ORF name with $4932.$.  The joined surface contains $3640$ proteins with CDS, abundance, and half-life rows; the CDS join reported upstream contains $5815$ rows, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  A measured translation-efficiency join is available on the same organismal background, with $5128$ genes carrying valid TE values and $3537$ rows simultaneously joining measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS records.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates and controls.}
The coordinate block has nine residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=&\{K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$.  Its declared components are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The fitted target for the turnover contact is not raw abundance and not raw codon usage.  It is the modeled-tAI complement of the abundance residual, projected against residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life on the yeast turnover join.

\paragraph{Observed finite numbers.}
On the turnover join, the modeled complement has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled,turnover}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against the full yeast abundance--CDS complement reference, the corresponding reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows.  The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is within the stated $0.02$ stability tolerance.  Thus the turnover-restricted join does not visibly change the modeled-complement target enough to invalidate the descriptive comparison.  The actual half-life absorption is small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The measured-TE contact on the same yeast organism reports $n=5128$, rank $23$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$ on its all-readout join, with measured TE absorbing only $0.0002762783754375593$.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact on the full yeast join reports $n=5815$, $273$ tRNA genes, $41$ anticodon table entries, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.21173219572706703$, with tRNA-copy tAI absorbing $0.001530747712165342$.  These neighboring contacts put the half-life number in scale: turnover is computed on a stable yeast residual target, but it removes less than one tenth of one percent of the modeled-complement quantity.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite result is a negative or nearly null turnover absorption result, not a failed computation.  The row counts are adequate for a yeast-only descriptive projection, the half-life payload is identified by SHA-256 digest $05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690$, duplicate or missing turnover identifiers are reported as zero, and the modeled-complement check stays inside the declared tolerance.  What the numbers say is narrower: under the stated covariates, the yeast protein half-life readout barely explains the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a finite, descriptive cross-layer comparison between a residual codon-coordinate surface and a yeast protein half-life readout.  It does not establish a causal mediator, a turnover mechanism, a translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance realization, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger statement would require its own layer-matched measurement and a separately declared statistic.  In particular, a small turnover absorption leaves the non-translation residual open rather than assigning it to localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, or any other biological layer.
