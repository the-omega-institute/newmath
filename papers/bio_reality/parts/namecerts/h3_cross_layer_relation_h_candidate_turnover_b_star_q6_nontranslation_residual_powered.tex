\subsection{Yeast protein-turnover contact for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite row set.}
This record tests whether a measured protein half-life readout absorbs any appreciable part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation proxy has already been removed.  The subject is not all biology of synonymous choice, but the narrower yeast contact in which coding-sequence counts, whole-organism protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, and SGD protein half-life rows can be joined on a common protein identifier.  The coordinate block has nine residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control matrix has 24 columns and rank 23: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined biological table.}
The turnover run uses the yeast whole-organism PaxDB abundance surface together with SGD protein-experiment half-life rows.  The abundance table reports 6351 valid protein-abundance identifiers, the coding-sequence join reports 5815 CDS rows, and the measured translation-efficiency table reports 5128 valid protein identifiers.  The half-life contact contributes 3640 valid turnover proteins, and the final turnover--abundance--CDS join also has 3640 rows.  When measured translation efficiency is additionally required, the all-readout join has 3537 rows; 103 CDS records are therefore lost only at the optional measured-TE step, while 2175 CDS records lack a turnover match.  The reported CDS join hit rate is 0.9156038419146592 and the turnover join hit rate is 0.8818572656921754.  The identifier conversion is explicit: an SGD locus systematic name is mapped to the STRING protein identifier by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name.  The half-life column is the SGD protein-experiment-details value in hours, from the Christiano et al. 2014 per-locus protein-half-life contact, and the payload hash is \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.

\paragraph{Residual size before and after the turnover readout.}
On the turnover join, the modeled-translation-complement component has squared norm data
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P\_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P\_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d\_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same quantity is stable against the abundance-CDS complement reference for yeast: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$, the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, within the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  This matters because the turnover contact is not merely changing the target row set beyond recognition; the residual component being tested is still close to the yeast complement surface used by the upstream abundance calculation.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The turnover candidate is evaluated by projecting the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $P\_{Q\perp Tmod}$ onto residualized $\log\_{10}$ protein half-life after the same control boundary.  The computed absorption is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d\_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the yeast half-life contact is a negative or near-null H-candidate for this residual block.  The residual remaining after modeled translation is not materially explained by this single steady-state turnover readout.  Numerically, the residual fraction changes from $0.2232704509412724$ to $0.22306503721327192$, a decrease of only $0.00020541372700048$ on the reported $d$ scale.  The correct reading is therefore bounded and descriptive: the half-life table was joined successfully, the projection was computed, and the measured turnover coordinate did not absorb a substantial part of the non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring translation contacts.}
The yeast measured-translation-efficiency run gives a compatible scale for the same family of tests.  In the measured-TE contact, yeast has $n=5128$ valid joined rows, the same 24-column control design with rank 23, and $d\_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$ on the TE-available join.  Its measured-TE absorption is $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d\_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact on the full yeast CDS join has 5815 joined rows, 273 tRNA genes, 41 anticodon entries, 268 usable measured-tAI records, and absorption $0.001530747712165342$, with $d\_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  These adjacent contacts show that the yeast residual is already modest after the modeled translation proxy, and neither measured TE nor protein turnover provides a large additional absorption in the reported finite surfaces.

\paragraph{Scope of the biological claim.}
This chapter licenses only the finite projection statement just described.  It does not say that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates determine protein half-life, and it does not turn a statistical projection into a causal mediator test.  The Christiano 2014 half-life table is a single yeast steady-state turnover contact, not a perturbation, pulse-chase panel across conditions, or direct degradation-mechanism assay.  No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this result unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and statistic.  In particular, low turnover absorption leaves the remaining H-question open rather than closing it: the unexplained component may require localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, RNA-state contacts, or a different protein-state readout, and each such interpretation must be tested by its own finite joined surface.
