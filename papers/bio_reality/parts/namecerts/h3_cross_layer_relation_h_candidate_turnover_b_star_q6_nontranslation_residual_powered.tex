\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded readout for the residual complement}
\label{subsec:biorality-q6-turnover-residual-complement}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests whether a measured protein half-life surface explains any part of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual carried by the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The finite coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$.  Its covariates are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 computed from real CDS codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The turnover contact is yeast-only: SGD systematic locus names were mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$, and the half-life value was read from the SGD protein-experiment-details field reported in hours.  The joined turnover-abundance-CDS panel contains $3640$ rows, with $3640$ valid turnover proteins, $6351$ valid abundance proteins, and $5815$ CDS rows joined to abundance.  The reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$, while the CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$.

\paragraph{Readout construction.}
The measured translation-efficiency rows are available for the same yeast study context: $5128$ genes have valid TE, and $3537$ rows join measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS simultaneously.  The TE definition used in the joined source is mean footprint normalized read density across two rich-medium replicates divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across two matched rich-medium replicates.  Thus the turnover calculation is not a naked protein-state association: it is located after a codon-derived residual basis, after abundance matching, and beside a measured TE boundary.  The turnover experiment nevertheless asks a narrower question.  It projects the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual $\widehat{P}_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life from the Christiano 2014 yeast steady-state turnover surface.

\paragraph{Residual scale.}
On the turnover join, the modeled complement has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This value is stable against the full yeast abundance-CDS complement reference: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows, the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$ on $3640$ rows, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is within the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  The stability check matters because feature availability can otherwise change the target residual itself.  Here the turnover join preserves the upstream residual scale well enough for a bounded yeast turnover readback.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
After adding residualized half-life as the turnover candidate, the combined residual fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.0009086031889966327.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row interpretation is therefore negative for substantive turnover absorption.  Protein half-life, as represented by this single yeast Christiano-style steady-state surface, removes only about $9.086\times 10^{-4}$ of the modeled-tAI-complement residual fraction.  Since the upstream yeast residual is already modest, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}\approx 0.2233$, the absolute residual component under test is small before the turnover projection is even considered.  The result is not a failure of the coordinate construction; it says that this particular half-life contact does not materially account for the remaining yeast non-translation residual on the matched panel.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The admissible conclusion is descriptive and finite.  The row set supports the statement that, in yeast, a matched SGD half-life readout was joined to codon-derived $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and standard sequence controls, and that the measured half-life coordinate absorbed only $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-tAI-complement residual on the $3640$-row turnover panel.  It does not establish a causal mediator, a translation mechanism, a protein-output law, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  It also does not say that turnover can never matter for synonymous residuals; the contact is one steady-state yeast dataset, not a perturbation series, not a pulse-chase mechanism test, and not a multi-organism turnover panel.  Any positive statement about translation, folded structure, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, or broad biological law would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite statistic.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For this claim, the finite computation is ready as a bounded negative absorption result.  The turnover surface is compatible with the residual target because the join-specific $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ remains within $0.02$ of the full yeast complement reference, but its explanatory contribution is very small.  The residual therefore remains open beyond transcription, modeled translation adaptation, measured TE availability, and this yeast protein-half-life contact; plausible next contacts include localization, complex membership, condition-specific protein state, or perturbative turnover surfaces, each evaluated under its own joined-row design.
