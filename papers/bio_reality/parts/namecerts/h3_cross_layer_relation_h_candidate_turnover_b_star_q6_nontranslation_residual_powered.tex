\subsection{Yeast protein half-life as a bounded turnover contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-residual-contact}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein-turnover coordinate absorbs the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual that was not already accounted for by the modeled translation-adaptation projection.  The carrier is a finite yeast join whose rows are coding sequences with codon-count coordinates, abundance rows, measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency availability, and a protein half-life value.  The residual coordinate list is fixed at nine entries:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The control matrix contains 24 columns and has rank 23.  Its named covariates are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined turnover rows.}
The finite contact is yeast-only.  The CDS-abundance join reports 5815 CDS rows, while the turnover contact yields 3640 joined rows with positive half-life, abundance, and CDS data.  The reported CDS join hit rate is 0.9156038419146592, and the reported turnover join hit rate is 0.8818572656921754.  The same run also records 5128 genes with measured translation-efficiency values and 3537 rows in the optional joint table containing measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS data.  Systematic yeast locus names were mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name.  The turnover field is the SGD protein experiment details data value in hours, loaded from the SGD backend protein half-life endpoint for Christiano et al. 2014 per-locus records.  The turnover payload has byte size 77817 and SHA256 hash \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.  No abundance, TE, or turnover rows were skipped for duplicate protein identifiers, missing protein identifiers, or nonpositive values; among CDS records, 2175 lacked a turnover match and 103 lacked the optional measured-TE match.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The tested quantity is descriptive: project the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life.  On the turnover join, the modeled-complement norm values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The full abundance-CDS complement reference for yeast had $d_{modeled}=0.21173219572706703$ on 5815 rows.  The turnover join differs from that reference by 0.011538255214205373, which is inside the declared 0.02 tolerance.  Thus the turnover join is not treated as changing the residual target enough to invalidate the bounded comparison.

\paragraph{Observed turnover absorption.}
The half-life coordinate absorbs only a small part of the modeled-complement residual.  The recorded absorbed fraction is 0.0009086031889966327, and the combined residual summary after adding the turnover coordinate is $d_{combined}=0.22306503721327192$.  In local terms, the finite row set supports the following statement only: on this yeast half-life contact, protein turnover is a measured candidate covariate whose projection onto the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual is very small.  It does not close the residual, and it does not supply a causal mediator test.

\paragraph{Translation and downstream comparisons.}
The same evidence bundle contains nearby finite contacts that keep this interpretation bounded.  For measured ribosome-profiling TE, the yeast all-readout join has 5128 rows, rank 23 controls, and $d_{modeled}=0.22386566043976477$; measured TE absorbs 0.0002762783754375593, leaving $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.  For measured tRNA gene-copy tAI on the full yeast CDS join of 5815 rows, the anticodon table has 41 entries, 273 tRNA genes are parsed, and the tRNA coordinate absorbs 0.001530747712165342 with $d_{combined}=0.20648621019305421$.  These neighboring contacts show that the yeast non-translation residual is already modest and that neither measured TE nor turnover produces a large descriptive absorption.  The protein-feature contact is also sparse and bounded: for yeast, 5682 joined feature rows were available from 6156 feature records, and none of the four feature candidates exceeded its Bonferroni threshold in the recorded null comparison.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite result is therefore a refusal of promotion, not a positive mechanistic story.  The contact says that $B^*_{Q6}$ residual geometry can be compared against a yeast protein half-life table after the stated sequence and composition controls, and that the observed turnover absorption is approximately $9.09\times 10^{-4}$.  That value is too small to treat turnover as explaining the residual in this contact.  Conversely, the small value is not evidence that the residual is biologically irrelevant; it only says that this single steady-state yeast half-life table does not absorb it in the declared projection.  The remaining residual may require other contacts, such as localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, or additional post-transcriptional state measurements, before any stronger biological interpretation is licensed.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation-realization claim follows from this turnover row set alone.  No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the turnover absorption value, from the ribosome-profiling TE availability flag, from the codon residual coordinates, or from the modeled tAI complement.  Each of those claims would require its own layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.  The present chapter records only a finite yeast half-life projection with explicit controls, row counts, join diagnostics, and a negative bounded conclusion for turnover absorption.
