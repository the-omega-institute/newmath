\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded residual-contact test}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-turnover-residual-contact}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests one sharply delimited question: after the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates have been projected past amino-acid composition, CDS length, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, does measured protein turnover remove any appreciable part of the abundance residual that remains outside the modeled translation-adaptation component?  The carrier is the finite yeast join on coding-sequence rows, abundance rows, measured translation-efficiency rows when available, and Christiano-style protein half-life rows.  The nine residual coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control design has $24$ columns, including the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, with observed control rank $23$.

\paragraph{Joined rows.}
The turnover panel is yeast-only.  The reported CDS-abundance join contains $5815$ CDS rows, while the protein half-life contact supplies $3640$ usable turnover proteins.  The final turnover-abundance-CDS join has $n=3640$ rows, with the same value for the all-readout turnover join, and the stricter measured-TE-turnover-abundance-CDS join has $3537$ rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover identifier join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The identifier map is explicit: an SGD locus systematic name is converted to a STRING protein identifier by prefixing $4932.$.  The half-life value is read from the SGD protein experiment details field $\mathrm{data\_value}$ in hours.  The turnover payload has byte size $77817$ and SHA-256 digest $05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690$.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
On this finite join, the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same modeled-complement quantity is consistent with the full abundance-CDS complement reference at the declared tolerance: the full reference value is $0.21173219572706703$, the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$, the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, and the within-$0.02$ check passes.  This matters because the turnover test is not being evaluated on a radically different residual target merely because half-life rows are sparse.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
The half-life covariate is residualized as $\log_{10}$ Christiano 2014 half-life and then used as a descriptive projection target for $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$.  The computed turnover contribution is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the turnover contact does not close the residual channel.  The modeled complement before adding turnover is about $0.2232704509412724$, and after adding turnover it is $0.22306503721327192$; the observed change is only about $2.0541\times 10^{-4}$ in the reported complement scale.  The finite-row conclusion is therefore not that turnover explains the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual, but that this single steady-state half-life contact removes only a negligible part of the yeast residual left after modeled translation adaptation.

\paragraph{Translation readout comparison.}
The companion measured-translation-efficiency contact gives the local scale of the same family of tests.  In yeast, the measured TE panel has $5128$ valid TE proteins on the TE join, $5815$ CDS rows on the abundance-CDS join, and rank $23$ for the same control design.  Its modeled-complement value on the all-readout TE join is $0.22386566043976477$, matching the full abundance-CDS complement reference within $0.02$ with absolute difference $0.012133464712697745$.  The measured-TE projection absorbs only $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover contact is therefore slightly larger than measured TE on this descriptive absorption number, but both are far below a level that would justify calling the remaining component translated, causal, or mechanistically resolved.

\paragraph{Modeled tRNA comparison.}
The measured tRNA gene-copy tAI contact also keeps the yeast residual open.  For yeast it uses $273$ tRNA genes, an anticodon gene-copy table of size $41$, $5815$ joined CDS rows, and the same rank-$23$ control surface.  Its modeled-complement value exactly matches the complement reference on the full join, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.21173219572706703$.  The tRNA-copy tAI projection absorbs $0.001530747712165342$ and reports $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  This is larger than the turnover absorption, but it remains a bounded descriptive projection from gene-copy tAI, not a direct observation of ribosome speed, protein output, or decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader cross-organism summary is exploratory rather than decisive.  The organism-specific meta table has $16$ rows, with $12$ complete rows for the tested $M^{QTP}$ summaries.  The domain contingency test for dominant coordinate reports $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous tests are likewise non-confirmatory at this finite size: $M^{QTP}$ fraction versus $GC3$ has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log protein count has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$; versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The domain variance-ratio test reports $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These numbers support only heterogeneity under the available finite panel, not a universal organism-level law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a bounded negative-to-weak contact result.  The yeast half-life panel is real enough to test whether turnover absorbs the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual, and its join is sufficiently close to the abundance-CDS complement reference for the reported comparison.  The answer is that turnover absorption is positive but extremely small: $0.0009086031889966327$.  This does not license a translation claim, a folding claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a protein-function claim, a causal mechanism, or a universal biological rule.  It also does not prove that the residual is biologically irrelevant.  It says only that, on the yeast half-life contact used here, after the stated controls and joins, protein turnover barely reduces the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual.  Any stronger reading requires a separate layer-matched contact and a separately recorded statistic.
