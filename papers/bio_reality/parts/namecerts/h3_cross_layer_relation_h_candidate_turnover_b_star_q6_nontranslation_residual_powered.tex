\subsection{Yeast protein turnover as a bounded candidate for the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein half-life readout explains any appreciable part of the yeast abundance component left after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has been removed from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis.  The finite coordinate side contains nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control design uses 24 columns, with rank $23$, consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The tested organism is \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; the turnover readout is the SGD backend protein half-life value in hours, mapped from SGD systematic ORF names to STRING-style protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$.

\paragraph{Joined rows and identifier boundary.}
The turnover panel joins $3640$ protein rows.  The surrounding finite counts are important because the test is not a free-standing biological generalization: the reported abundance table has $6351$ valid proteins, the CDS join reports $5815$ proteins, the measured-TE table has $5128$ valid proteins, and the turnover source has $3640$ valid proteins.  The turnover-abundance-CDS join has $3640$ rows, while the joint turnover-abundance-CDS-measured-TE intersection has $3537$ rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The skipped-record audit is also finite: no abundance records were skipped for duplicate, missing, or nonpositive protein ids; no turnover records were skipped for duplicate, missing, or nonpositive half-life; the CDS side lost $2175$ rows to missing turnover and $103$ rows to missing optional measured TE.  Thus the result is a yeast-row statistic on the available half-life contact, not an organism-general turnover law.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component after the control projection, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the component remaining after the modeled translation-adaptation candidate.  On the turnover join, the computed squared norms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding modeled-complement fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This agrees with the full abundance-CDS complement reference within the declared tolerance: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$, the current turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is below $0.02$.  This matters because the turnover join changes the row set, but in this case the residual target remains close enough to the reference complement for a bounded yeast turnover check.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
The actual half-life candidate is evaluated by projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ Christiano 2014 half-life after the same control design.  The absorbed fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The numerical reading is therefore negative for turnover as an explanatory candidate in this panel: adding the yeast half-life readout changes the residual fraction only from $0.2232704509412724$ to $0.22306503721327192$.  The observed absorption is nonzero as a finite projection value, but it is too small to support the claim that turnover accounts for the remaining non-translation residual component.  In plain terms, the yeast half-life contact is measured and properly joined, but it does not materially compress the residual component that survived the modeled translation-adaptation step.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring readouts.}
The same yeast residual region is not explained by the nearby translation-side checks either.  On the measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact, yeast has $5128$ joined measured-TE rows with the same rank-$23$ control design, and measured TE absorbs only $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.  On the measured tRNA gene-copy tAI contact, the yeast table has $5815$ joined CDS rows, $273$ parsed tRNA genes, an anticodon GCN table size of $41$, and absorption $0.001530747712165342$, with $d_{combined}=0.20648621019305421$.  Protein-feature candidates are also bounded rather than decisive in yeast: reviewed feature joins cover $5682$ rows, and the per-candidate absorbed fractions are $0.00069212177545487$ for complex membership, $0.00007029698692511431$ for domain count, $0.0005759336988655581$ for PTM density, and $0.00031741485098156394$ for transmembrane count; none of the yeast feature candidates exceeds its Bonferroni-calibrated null threshold.  The turnover result therefore fits a coherent local picture: the yeast residual that remains after modeled translation adaptation is modest in size and is not substantially absorbed by measured TE, tRNA-copy tAI, reviewed protein features, or steady-state half-life.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
This subsection licenses only a descriptive finite-row statement: in the yeast Christiano half-life contact, residualized protein turnover absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual under the stated controls.  It does not establish causality, mediation, adaptation, translation realization, ribosome mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  It also does not imply that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ is biologically irrelevant; it says only that this particular half-life readout, in this yeast steady-state panel, does not materially explain the measured residual component.  Any stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own rows, controls, and statistic.
