\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded post-translation contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-contact}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the non-translation component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual is explained by a measured yeast protein-turnover contact.  The finite carrier is the joined Saccharomyces cerevisiae surface whose rows contain a coding-sequence codon-count profile, the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, the matched abundance row, the modeled translation-complement residual, and the SGD protein half-life row.  The coordinate list is
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Join and contact quality.}
The half-life contact is yeast-only and uses SGD per-locus protein-experiment details from Christiano et al. 2014, mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing the systematic ORF name with $4932.$.  The parsed turnover table contains $3640$ valid turnover proteins, and the same $3640$ rows enter the turnover-abundance-CDS panel.  The CDS bridge reports $5815$ abundance-matched coding sequences and a CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$; the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The matched translation-efficiency side contact is available for $5128$ yeast genes, with $3537$ rows in the joint measured-TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS surface.  The source payload is fixed by byte size $77817$ and SHA256 digest $05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the component left after the modeled-tAI translation candidate is removed.  On the turnover join, the computed squared norms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding full-abundance complement reference for yeast is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows.  The turnover-join value differs by $0.011538255214205373$, which is within the declared $0.02$ stability tolerance.  Thus the turnover panel is not being judged on a radically different residual target: the row restriction changes the modeled-complement fraction, but not enough to invalidate the comparison.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The tested readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life.  Projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ against that residualized half-life surface gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The change from $d_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$ to $d_{combined}=0.22306503721327192$ is numerically small.  In this finite contact, protein half-life does not materially account for the yeast non-translation residual left after the modeled translation candidate.  The result is therefore a bounded negative or near-null H-candidate readback for turnover, not a mechanism call.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The measured translation-efficiency contact supplies the adjacent translation-layer check.  In yeast, the measured-TE join has $5128$ rows, rank $23$, and the same $24$-column control design.  The measured-TE absorption is $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$ and $d_{modeled}=0.22386566043976477$ on that readout join.  The turnover panel therefore sits after a translation-efficiency contact that is itself weak on the yeast residual.  For comparison, the same measured-TE computation gives $0.01759429001506974$ absorption in Danio rerio, $0.0010982956489726373$ in Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, and $0.0028947149257963897$ in Homo sapiens.  These numbers support a row-level distinction: the yeast turnover test is not hiding a strong yeast translation-efficiency explanation upstream.

\paragraph{Relation to tRNA-copy tAI and organism specificity.}
The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact also gives a bounded comparison surface.  In yeast it uses $273$ tRNA genes, an anticodon gene-copy table of size $41$, and $5815$ joined CDS rows; its absorption of the modeled-complement residual is $0.001530747712165342$, with $d_{combined}=0.20648621019305421$.  The larger cross-organism summary remains descriptive: the $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus domain test has $n=12$, variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$, and right-tail permutation value $0.4155844155844156$; $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$; the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer value $0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$.  These meta-level values do not rescue the yeast turnover readout: they show heterogeneity without a powered domain-level law for the turnover explanation.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite result is a scoped cross-layer statement.  A yeast protein half-life contact can be joined to the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface with adequate row support and a stable modeled-complement target, but it absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ of the residual component being tested.  The most direct reading is that, in this yeast steady-state half-life dataset, turnover is not a substantial explanation for the residual that remains after modeled translation.  The open alternatives are not promoted here: localization, complex membership, condition specificity, RNA stability, or other post-transcriptional variables would require their own joined rows and their own controlled statistics.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
This subsection licenses only the finite projection quantities just stated.  It does not establish causation, does not identify a protein-degradation mechanism, and does not show that a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate controls translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or any universal biological rule.  The half-life contact is a single yeast steady-state turnover dataset; the measured translation-efficiency contact is condition-specific; the absorbed fractions are descriptive projection readbacks.  Any assertion about translation realization, protein structure, biochemical mechanism, functional effect, physical admissibility, or universality must be supplied by a separate layer-matched contact rather than inferred from this turnover statistic.
