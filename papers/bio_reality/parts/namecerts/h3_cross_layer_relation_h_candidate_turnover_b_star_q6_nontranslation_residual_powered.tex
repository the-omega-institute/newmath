\subsection{Yeast protein half-life as a bounded candidate for the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance complement}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Readout surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-layer test in which the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are not promoted to a translation, protein-folding, function, phenotype, or universal biological statement.  The tested object is narrower: in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, after controlling for amino-acid composition, coding-sequence length, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors, the remaining modeled-tAI-complement abundance component is projected against a protein half-life readout.  The turnover row is experiment \texttt{c48bd41e6202}; the measured-translation-efficiency row used only as a boundary comparator is experiment \texttt{bbd448cac2b4}.  The turnover readout is log-scaled Christiano 2014 protein half-life as retrieved through SGD per-locus protein-experiment records, mapped from SGD systematic locus names to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing \texttt{4932.}.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control basis.}
The residual coordinate family has nine entries:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control matrix has twenty-four declared columns and rank $23$.  Its columns are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$ computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same counts, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the test is not a raw codon-frequency association.  It asks whether the already controlled residual abundance complement has any descriptive overlap with a matched turnover coordinate.

\paragraph{Joined yeast rows.}
The finite yeast join contains $5815$ CDS rows with reported CDS-abundance matching and $3640$ rows with matched turnover, abundance, and CDS data.  The turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$, while the CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$.  The abundance table reports $6351$ valid protein-abundance records, the measured-TE table reports $5128$ valid translation-efficiency proteins, and the turnover table reports $3640$ valid protein half-life records.  The optional all-readout join with measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS rows contains $3537$ rows, with $103$ CDS records lacking the optional measured-TE match and $2175$ CDS records lacking a turnover match.  No skipped abundance, TE, or turnover records are attributed to duplicate identifiers, missing identifiers, or nonpositive numerical values in the relevant finite counters.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
On the turnover join, the modeled complement has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding full abundance-CDS complement reference for yeast is $d_{modeled}=0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows.  The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, below the declared $0.02$ matching tolerance.  This matters because the feature and turnover tests can otherwise change the joined protein set enough to move the residual target.  Here the turnover-restricted yeast surface remains close enough to the full yeast abundance-CDS complement to support a bounded descriptive comparison.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The turnover candidate is weak on this finite surface.  Projecting the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual onto residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The decrease from $d_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$ to $d_{combined}=0.22306503721327192$ is therefore only about $0.000205413727$ in absolute $d$ units.  In plain terms, the measured half-life coordinate accounts for only a tiny descriptive fraction of the yeast residual abundance complement left after the modeled translation-adaptation component.  The finite result is not that turnover is impossible as a biological influence; it is that this particular single-condition yeast half-life surface does not materially absorb the residual component.

\paragraph{Translation-efficiency boundary.}
The measured-TE comparator keeps the turnover result in its proper layer.  In the yeast measured-TE row, the same nine-coordinate family and rank-$23$ control surface appear on $5128$ valid TE proteins, with $d_{modeled}=0.22386566043976477$, $\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_measTE}=0.0002762783754375593$, and $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover calculation is therefore not being used as a hidden TE claim.  Translation efficiency is a separate readout, defined in the source row as mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across two rich-medium replicates from Ingolia et al. 2009.  The turnover chapter only asks whether a post-translational half-life coordinate describes the residual left after the modeled translation-adaptation complement; it does not assert ribosome occupancy, translation realization, or protein production.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For this finite yeast contact, the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement remains largely outside measured protein half-life.  The result is a bounded negative or near-null H-candidate readback: turnover was measurable, the identifier join was large enough to compute the projection, and the residual target was stable against the full yeast complement reference, but the observed absorption was only $0.0009086031889966327$.  The appropriate scientific reading is that this yeast residual remains open to other bounded contacts, such as localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, or data not present in this row.  No statement about causal mediation, direct translation mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or universality follows from this projection without a separate layer-specific contact and its own controlled statistic.
