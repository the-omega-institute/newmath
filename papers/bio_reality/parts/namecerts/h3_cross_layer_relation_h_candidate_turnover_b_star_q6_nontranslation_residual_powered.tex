\subsection{Yeast Turnover as a B*\_Q6 Non-translation Residual Candidate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-nontranslation-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}  This record tests whether a protein-turnover readout accounts for any part of the B*\_Q6 residual component that remained after the modeled translation-coupling projection.  The finite organism panel is deliberately narrow: the turnover contact is Saccharomyces cerevisiae only, with SGD protein half-life values from the Christiano 2014 per-locus protein-experiment backend.  The readout is log-scaled half-life, joined to coding-sequence codon counts, PaxDB abundance, and the Ingolia 2009 ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency table.  The coordinate side contains the nine projected B*\_Q6 residual coordinates
$$\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The control matrix has 24 declared columns and rank 23.  Its covariates are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected B*\_Q6 vectors.

\paragraph{Joined rows and identifier discipline.}  The joined turnover table contains 3640 proteins.  The same 3640 rows are available after joining turnover, abundance, and CDS records, while the stricter measured-TE--turnover--abundance--CDS join contains 3537 rows.  The source tables report 6351 valid abundance proteins, 5128 genes with valid translation-efficiency values, 5815 CDS rows joined to abundance, and 3640 valid turnover proteins.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  SGD systematic locus names are mapped to STRING identifiers by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF label.  The turnover payload has 77817 bytes and SHA-256 digest 05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690.  No duplicate, missing, or nonpositive records were reported for the abundance, translation-efficiency, or turnover sources after the declared filters; the CDS table lost 2175 rows for missing turnover matches and 103 rows for missing optional measured-TE matches.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}  The tested object is not a direct coordinate-to-half-life regression.  First, the abundance-side B*\_Q6 component is stripped of the modeled tAI component, giving the modeled-complement residual $\widehat P\_{Q\perp T\_{\mathrm{mod}}}$.  On the turnover join, the squared norm of the original projected Q component is $457.02479128996873$, and the squared norm after removing the modeled translation component is $102.04013124265222$.  Thus the finite modeled-complement fraction is
$$\begin{aligned}
d\_{\mathrm{modeled}}&=0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}$$
This value agrees with the full abundance-CDS complement reference at the declared tolerance: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$, the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, and the match flag is true for the $0.02$ tolerance.  The turnover candidate is then tested by projecting this modeled-complement residual onto residualized $\log\_{10}$ half-life.  The absorbed fraction is
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed}\_{\mathrm{turnover}}&=0.0009086031889966327,\\
d\_{\mathrm{combined}}&=0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}$$
Numerically, turnover removes only about nine ten-thousandths of the modeled-complement component on this joined yeast surface.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}  The same finite data family also contains a measured-translation-efficiency contact for yeast.  In that contact, the yeast measured-TE join has $n=5128$, rank $23$, and the same nine B*\_Q6 coordinates under the same 24-column control design.  Its measured-TE absorption is $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d\_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$, and its all-readout modeled-complement value is $0.22386566043976477$.  The turnover surface therefore sits in a compatible numerical neighborhood of the yeast measured-TE surface: both preserve a modest upstream non-translation residual near $0.22$, and neither contact absorbs a large fraction of it.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}  The finite result is a negative H-candidate outcome for protein turnover on this yeast panel.  The half-life surface is real enough to be joined and tested, and the modeled-complement reference is stable under the turnover join, but the actual absorption by turnover is very small.  The correct reading is therefore bounded and descriptive: yeast protein half-life, as represented by this single steady-state Christiano 2014 source, does not materially explain the residual B*\_Q6 abundance component left after the modeled translation readout.  Because the upstream residual itself is modest in yeast, this is not a strong positive discovery of an unexplained large effect; it is a finite exclusion of one plausible downstream candidate under the stated controls.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}  No translation mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mediation, or universal biological law follows from this turnover statistic.  The measured quantity is a statistical projection on a yeast half-life table, not a perturbation, rescue, mechanistic, or structural assay.  A separate contact would be required for ribosome dynamics beyond the bounded TE rows, for mature protein output beyond abundance tables, for protein folding or stability mechanisms, for physical admissibility, for functional realization, or for cross-organism universality.  If future localization, complex-membership, condition-specific turnover, or perturbation data are attached, they must be evaluated as new finite readout surfaces rather than inferred from the present half-life absorption value.
