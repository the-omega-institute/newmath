\subsection{Yeast protein turnover as a bounded candidate for the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-turnover-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests one narrow contact: whether measured protein turnover in budding yeast accounts for any part of the abundance-side residual component left after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has already been separated. The finite panel is not a general biological law and not a direct translation mechanism. It is a yeast-only joined table whose rows carry coding-sequence residual coordinates, abundance identifiers, ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency availability, and protein half-life values. The turnover readout is the SGD protein-experiment-details half-life value from the Christiano 2014 per-locus source, mapped from SGD systematic ORF names to STRING-style protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$. The joined turnover surface contains $3640$ proteins; the reported CDS join contains $5815$ rows, and the abundance table contains $6351$ valid proteins. Translation-efficiency rows are available for $5128$ genes, and the strict all-readout overlap of measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS contains $3537$ rows. The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, while the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate basis and controls.}
The residual coordinate basis has nine named coordinates:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$. Its columns are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$ computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons supported on the union of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. Thus the turnover contact is evaluated after basic amino-acid composition, coding length, synonymous GC3 structure, and the $M$-support baseline have already been accounted for.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component on the turnover join and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote its component after the modeled tAI direction has been removed. The finite computation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{modelled} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same statistic is checked against the full abundance-CDS complement reference for yeast. The reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ joined rows, while the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$ on $3640$ rows. The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is within the declared $0.02$ tolerance. This matters because the turnover table changes the row set; the check says that the row restriction has not materially changed the upstream modeled-complement quantity for this yeast contact.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The turnover candidate is then evaluated by projecting the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual onto residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life. The measured absorption is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
On this finite surface, protein half-life barely reduces the modeled-complement abundance residual. The result is therefore not a positive turnover explanation of the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ component. It is a bounded negative readback: after the declared controls and after modeled tAI complement separation, this yeast half-life contact absorbs less than one tenth of one percent of the residual target. Since the upstream residual fraction is already modest in yeast, with $d_{modelled}\approx 0.223$, the absolute remaining non-translation signal is also modest.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured translation-efficiency contact.}
The turnover result is interpreted next to, not in place of, the measured TE contact. In the measured-TE computation, yeast has $5128$ valid TE proteins, $5815$ reported CDS-joined rows, and rank $23$ for the same $24$-column control surface. On the measured-TE all-readout join, the modeled-complement value is $0.22386566043976477$, and measured TE absorbs only $0.0002762783754375593$, giving $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$. The turnover join keeps the same basic residual geometry stable, but turnover absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$, still a very small amount. Thus, within yeast, neither measured TE nor protein half-life supplies a strong descriptive account of the residual abundance component that remains after modeled translation adaptation.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured tRNA-copy tAI contact.}
A separate bounded readback uses measured tRNA gene-copy tAI in yeast. That surface has $273$ parsed tRNA genes, an anticodon table size of $41$, $268$ usable measured-tAI records, $5815$ joined CDS rows, and the same rank-$23$ control matrix. Its modeled-complement reference agrees exactly with the full yeast complement reference at $0.21173219572706703$, and the tRNA-copy tAI absorption is $0.001530747712165342$ with $d_{combined}=0.20648621019305421$. This comparison keeps the turnover claim in scale: the turnover absorption is lower than the tRNA-copy tAI absorption and only slightly above the measured-TE absorption, while all three quantities are descriptive projections rather than causal mechanisms.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite conclusion is a refusal to promote turnover into a satisfying explanation for the yeast residual component. The data support the following bounded statement: on $3640$ joined yeast proteins, using the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis and the declared rank-$23$ controls, the Christiano-style half-life readout absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual, while leaving $d_{combined}=0.22306503721327192$. The result is compatible with the residual being mostly outside this measured turnover contact. It does not say that turnover is irrelevant in general; it says that this particular steady-state half-life table does not carry much of the residual target after the specified controls and row matching.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
This chapter makes no claim that the residual coordinate itself is a measured translation process, a folding signal, a physical-admissibility result, a molecular-function result, a mechanism, or a universal biological rule. The half-life contact is single-organism and descriptive. The measured TE contact is also condition- and study-specific. The tRNA-copy tAI contact is a modeled adaptation readback, not observed ribosome occupancy. A stronger biological claim would need a separate layer-matched contact: direct ribosome or TE rows for translation realization, structural or stability measurements for folding or physical state, perturbation or binding evidence for mechanism, functional assays for function, and a powered cross-organism design for any universality claim. Without such contacts, the correct reading is simply that yeast protein turnover is tested and found to absorb only a very small fraction of the residual modeled-complement abundance component.
