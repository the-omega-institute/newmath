\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded candidate for the residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ complement}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein half-life coordinate accounts for any part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has already been removed.  The finite carrier is the yeast joined table on coding-sequence counts, whole-organism protein abundance, the Ingolia rich-medium ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readout, and the SGD protein half-life readout attributed to Christiano et al. 2014.  The turnover row is not a new universal statement about codons.  It is a single-organism, single steady-state half-life contact for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, evaluated after the same residual basis and control surface used by the $Q6$ candidate panel.

\paragraph{Controlled residual basis.}
The residual coordinate block has nine coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control matrix has $24$ named columns: an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  Its reported rank is $23$, so the fitted surface has one less independent control direction than the raw column count.  This rank statement is important because the half-life contact is judged only after the amino-acid, length, $GC3$, and $M$-support boundaries have already been imposed.

\paragraph{Joined rows.}
The yeast CDS-abundance join reports $5815$ CDS rows, with $6351$ valid protein-abundance identifiers and $5128$ valid translation-efficiency identifiers available in the companion measured-TE contact.  The turnover contact contributes $3640$ valid half-life proteins and yields $3640$ joined turnover-abundance-CDS rows.  The optional all-readout join containing measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS rows has $3537$ rows, because $103$ CDS rows lack the optional measured-TE match and $2175$ CDS rows lack a turnover match.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The half-life identifier map is explicit: the SGD locus systematic name is mapped to the STRING protein identifier by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF.  No turnover records were skipped for duplicate identifiers, missing identifiers, or nonpositive half-life values.

\paragraph{Reference complement check.}
Before asking whether turnover explains the residual component, the run checks that the modeled-translation-complement residual on the turnover join is close to the full yeast abundance-CDS complement reference.  The full reference has $5815$ rows and $d_{modeled}=0.21173219572706703$.  The turnover join has $3640$ rows and gives $d_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$.  The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is inside the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  Thus the turnover contact is not rejected merely because the joined row set moves the modeled-complement target too far away from the full yeast reference.

$$\begin{aligned}
 d_{full} &= 0.21173219572706703,\\
 d_{turnover} &= 0.2232704509412724,\\
 |d_{turnover}-d_{full}| &= 0.011538255214205373 < 0.02.
\end{aligned}$$

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
On the turnover join, the projected $Q6$ residual norm is $P\_Q\_norm2=457.02479128996873$, and the modeled-translation-complement norm is $P\_{Q\perp Tmod}\_norm2=102.04013124265222$.  The corresponding modeled complement fraction is $0.2232704509412724$.  The half-life candidate is then the residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life coordinate, tested as an additional one-dimensional readout against that modeled complement.  The absorbed fraction is small: $absorbed\_by\_turnover=0.0009086031889966327$, with $d_{combined}=0.22306503721327192$ after adding the turnover coordinate.

$$\begin{aligned}
 \|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
 \|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
 d_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724,\\
 A_{turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
 d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}$$

\paragraph{Readout.}
The finite result is therefore a bounded negative-to-weak readout for turnover as an explanatory candidate.  The join is admissible for this yeast contact, because the modeled-complement target remains within tolerance of the full yeast complement reference.  However, the actual half-life absorption is only about $9.09\times 10^{-4}$ of the modeled-translation-complement residual.  In practical terms, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ component that remains after modeled translation adaptation is not materially accounted for by this single yeast steady-state half-life coordinate.  The residual remains mostly open after transcription, modeled translation adaptation, measured translation-efficiency availability, and this turnover candidate.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured translation-efficiency contact.}
The companion measured-TE finite row gives the same nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ basis and the same $24$-column control recipe, with yeast $n=5128$ valid TE-by-protein rows and rank $23$.  In that contact, yeast has $d_{modeled}=0.22386566043976477$ on the all-readout join, measured-TE absorption $0.0002762783754375593$, and $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover test is therefore not replacing the translation-efficiency boundary; it is a downstream candidate asked whether protein half-life accounts for residual abundance structure left after the modeled translation-adaptation component has been separated.  The turnover absorption is larger than the measured-TE absorption on their respective yeast joins, but both are small in absolute terms, and neither supplies a mechanism by itself.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
This chapter licenses only the following finite statement: in the yeast joined panel, the turnover contact is computable, row coverage is recorded, the modeled-complement target remains stable within $0.02$, and residualized protein half-life absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  No independent translation realization, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, causal mechanism, phenotype statement, or universal biological law follows from this projection.  Any such stronger assertion would need its own layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, the half-life coordinate is a descriptive projection on one yeast steady-state dataset, not a causal mediator test and not a proof that the residual is biologically irrelevant.
