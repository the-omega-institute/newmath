\subsection{Yeast protein half-life as a bounded H-candidate for the B*$_{Q6}$ residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein-turnover coordinate accounts for any part of the yeast B*$_{Q6}$ non-translation residual that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation component has already been separated.  The finite surface is the joined \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} panel with CDS codon counts, integrated protein abundance, ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency availability, and SGD protein half-life values.  The turnover contact contributes $n=3640$ proteins with positive half-life values, mapped from SGD systematic locus names to STRING identifiers by prefixing $4932.$.  The same join reports $5815$ CDS-abundance rows, $6351$ valid abundance proteins, $5128$ valid measured-TE proteins, and $3537$ rows on the stricter measured-TE--turnover--abundance--CDS intersection.  The CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, while the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  Missingness is explicit: among CDS records, $2175$ have no turnover match and $103$ have no optional measured-TE match; missing protein identifiers, invalid lengths, nonpositive abundance records, nonpositive TE records, and nonpositive half-life records are all reported as zero in the joined data.

\paragraph{Coordinate basis and controls.}
The residual side uses nine B*$_{Q6}$ coordinates:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$.  Its covariates are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected B*$_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the half-life test is not a raw codon-to-turnover association: it asks only whether residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life absorbs part of the previously isolated modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual under the stated finite controls.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement consistency.}
On the turnover join, the B*$_{Q6}$ modeled-complement norm calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same quantity on the full abundance-CDS complement reference is $0.21173219572706703$, with reference join size $5815$.  The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, below the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  This matters because sparse or shifted joins can change the residual target; here the turnover join remains close enough to the full abundance-CDS complement reference to support a bounded yeast-only turnover readback.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
After residualizing the Christiano 2014 per-locus half-life values against the same control boundary, the turnover coordinate accounts for only a very small fraction of the modeled-complement residual:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore a computed negative-style readback for this H-candidate: protein half-life is present as an organism-matched finite contact and the projection is well defined, but the observed absorption is too small to explain the B*$_{Q6}$ residual left after transcription, abundance, and modeled translation-adaptation separation.  The finite conclusion is not that turnover is biologically irrelevant; it is that this single steady-state yeast half-life surface does not materially reduce the residual under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Relation to measured TE availability.}
The same yeast corpus has a measured translation-efficiency panel from Ingolia et al. 2009 rich-medium ribosome-footprint and mRNA files.  In the measured-TE H-candidate run, yeast contributes $5128$ valid TE rows, $5815$ CDS-abundance rows, control rank $23$, and $24$ control columns.  The measured-TE projection reports $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$, $\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_measTE}=0.0002762783754375593$, and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  These numbers place the turnover result in context: both measured TE and protein half-life are finite cross-layer contacts for yeast, and both leave nearly all of the small upstream yeast modeled-complement residual in place.  The turnover contact absorbs somewhat more than measured TE on this panel, but the magnitude remains below one tenth of one percent of the residual target.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The source for turnover is the SGD backend protein-experiment-details half-life field, identified as Christiano et al. 2014 per-locus protein half-life and recorded with payload hash $05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690$.  The protein-abundance surface is the integrated PaxDb whole-organism yeast panel, and the measured-TE definition is the mean footprint normalized read density across two rich-medium replicates divided by the mean mRNA normalized read density across two matched replicates.  These contacts are adequate for the bounded projection described above, but they do not establish a causal mediator.  The half-life value is a single steady-state yeast readout, the absorbed fraction is descriptive, and the upstream yeast residual is already modest with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}\approx 0.22$.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The allowed claim is narrow: on a finite yeast panel of $3640$ turnover-joined proteins, residualized protein half-life was tested as an H-candidate for the B*$_{Q6}$ modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual, and it absorbed $0.0009086031889966327$ of that residual while preserving a modeled-complement distance close to the full abundance-CDS reference.  This does not license a claim of translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universality.  Any such higher-layer statement would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present chapter only records that, for this yeast half-life surface, turnover is a measured but weak explanatory coordinate for the remaining B*$_{Q6}$ non-translation residual.
