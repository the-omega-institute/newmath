\subsection{Turnover Test for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Non-Translation Residual}
\label{sec:bioq6-turnover-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein half-life coordinate removes any appreciable part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation readout has already been separated.  The carrier is a finite yeast table whose rows are coding-sequence records joined to abundance, measured translation efficiency, codon-count controls, and protein turnover.  The tested residual coordinate family has nine columns,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control design has $24$ declared columns and rank $23$.  Its columns are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$ computed from the coding-sequence codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the turnover coordinate is not read against raw codon usage: it is read only after amino-acid composition, length, $GC3$, and the declared $M$-support baseline have been accounted for.

\paragraph{Joined yeast rows.}
The finite contact is restricted to \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The abundance source reports $6351$ valid abundance proteins, the coding-sequence join reports $5815$ CDS rows, the measured-TE source has $5128$ valid protein-level entries, and the turnover source has $3640$ valid protein-level entries.  The turnover test uses $3640$ joined abundance--CDS--turnover rows, with $3537$ rows also carrying measured TE.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  Turnover identifiers are mapped from SGD locus systematic names to STRING-style protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name.  The half-life column is the SGD protein-experiment-details value in hours, and the loaded payload is identified by SHA256 digest \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.  Rows excluded at this stage are also finite: $2175$ CDS records lack a turnover match, $103$ CDS records lack an optional measured-TE match, and no abundance, TE, or turnover records are skipped for duplicated protein identifiers, missing identifiers, or non-positive quantitative values.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $\widehat P_Q$ denote the abundance component carried by the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after the declared control space has been removed, and let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modeled translation-adaptation readout used in the preceding translation-complement step.  The turnover test begins from the modeled-complement residual
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
and projects it against residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life.  On the turnover join, the squared norm of the controlled $Q$ projection is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert \widehat P_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the squared norm of the modeled-translation complement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert \widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding modeled-complement fraction is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This agrees with the full abundance--CDS complement reference $0.21173219572706703$ to within $0.011538255214205373$, below the declared $0.02$ stability tolerance, despite the turnover join using $3640$ rows rather than the $5815$ rows in the full abundance--CDS reference.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
The half-life coordinate absorbs only a very small fraction of the modeled-translation complement.  The computed absorption is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Absorb}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.0009086031889966327,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the combined residual fraction after adding turnover is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The numerical change from $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ to $d_{\mathrm{combined}}$ is therefore about $2.0541\times 10^{-4}$ in the reported residual fraction, while the direct turnover absorption statistic is about $9.0860\times 10^{-4}$.  The finite row consequently supports a bounded negative interpretation: in this yeast steady-state table, Christiano-style protein half-life does not materially account for the $B^*_{Q6}$ component left after the modeled translation-adaptation readout.  The result is not a proof that turnover is irrelevant in general; it says only that this particular joined yeast half-life coordinate has very weak descriptive absorption for this particular residual target.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
Measured translation efficiency is available for this organism and dataset family.  In the measured-TE contact, the yeast all-readout join has $5128$ rows, control rank $23$, and a modeled-complement fraction $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$.  The measured-TE absorption there is $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover table has fewer joined rows, $3640$, but its modeled-complement fraction $0.2232704509412724$ remains close to the same yeast residual scale.  This comparison matters because the turnover coordinate is being tested as a candidate downstream explanation of an already small non-translation residual, not as a replacement for the ribosome-profiling TE contact.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The finite calculation is a projection audit, not a causal mediation experiment.  It uses a single yeast steady-state protein half-life source, a single rich-medium ribosome-profiling context for the matched TE definitions, and a bounded identifier join through SGD and STRING-style protein identifiers.  A nonzero projection of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized half-life is not a mechanism claim, and the small absorption value reported here does not establish a universal absence of turnover effects.  It only records that, under the declared controls and on the $3640$ joined yeast rows, turnover has negligible descriptive power for the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual.  No claim about translation realization, ribosome mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or biological universality follows from this row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant measurement and the corresponding controlled statistic.
