\subsection{Yeast protein turnover as a bounded candidate for the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question and admissible surface.}
This record tests one narrow contact: whether a measured protein half-life column in budding yeast accounts for any part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ component that remains after the modeled-tAI translation candidate has already been removed from the protein-abundance projection. The carrier is a finite CDS-by-protein surface with nine residual coordinates, the usual sequence controls, a yeast abundance join, a measured translation-efficiency availability check, and a protein-turnover readout. It is not a claim that synonymous residual geometry is a biological mechanism. It is only a controlled projection of one residual abundance component onto one experimentally reported turnover column.

The nine residual coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K_{\mathrm{AAA}},\ \mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}},\ \mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUR}},\ \\
&\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUG}},\ \mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{AGY}},\ \mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UCG}},\ \\
&\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{ACY}},\ f_{3,\mathrm{stress}}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The recorded control-column count is $24$, while the effective control rank is $23$, so the row is treated as rank-checked rather than as a saturated fit.

The all-readout intersection containing measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS rows has $3537$ proteins, while the turnover-abundance-CDS join used for the turnover projection has $3640$ proteins. The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.

The half-life column is the SGD protein-experiment-details value in hours, and the turnover source is recorded as the SGD backend protein half-life readout from Christiano et al. 2014, per locus. \paragraph{Modeled-complement target before turnover.}
The tested response is not total abundance. It is the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component, written here as $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$. On the yeast turnover join, the residual coordinate norm and the modeled-complement norm are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This value agrees with the full abundance-CDS complement reference within the declared tolerance: the full reference has join size $5815$ and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.21173219572706703$, while the turnover join has join size $3640$ and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2232704509412724$. The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, below $0.02$. This matters because the turnover readout is allowed to be interpreted as testing the same residual target only if the join has not moved the modeled-complement quantity too far.

\paragraph{Turnover projection result.}
The measured half-life column is used as a third candidate for the non-translation residual by projecting $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life after the same sequence controls. The computed turnover absorption is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the half-life contact is successfully attached and numerically evaluated, but it does not materially reduce the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual in this yeast panel. Since $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ changes from $0.2232704509412724$ to $0.22306503721327192$, the descriptive reduction is about $2.0541372930048\times 10^{-4}$ in the reported $d$ scale, while the recorded absorption fraction is $0.0009086031889966327$. The finite result is therefore a negative absorption result for this candidate, not a positive turnover explanation.

\paragraph{Relation to measured TE and tRNA-copy readouts.}
The same yeast study context contains a measured-TE contact from Ingolia et al. 2009, using rich-medium footprint and mRNA quantification files. TE is defined as mean footprint normalized read density across two rich-medium replicates divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across two rich-medium replicates. On the yeast measured-TE join, $5128$ rows are available, with $5815$ CDS rows reported and control rank $23$. The measured-TE absorption for the modeled complement is $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$ on that join. In contrast, the measured tRNA gene-copy tAI contact is computed on the full yeast CDS join of $5815$ rows, with $273$ tRNA genes and an anticodon-GCN table size of $41$; its absorption is $0.001530747712165342$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$. These neighboring readouts show that the yeast residual has already been tested against modeled translation, measured TE availability, tRNA-copy tAI, and turnover as separate finite contacts. The turnover row remains specifically a half-life projection, not a substitute for a translation-efficiency survival entry.

\paragraph{Protein-feature comparison.}

The tested single-column feature absorptions are $0.00069212177545487$ for complex membership, $0.00007029698692511431$ for domain count, $0.0005759336988655581$ for PTM density, and $0.00031741485098156394$ for transmembrane count. None of the yeast feature candidates exceeds its Bonferroni threshold under the recorded degree-of-freedom matched nulls: the thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$ for complex membership, $0.0011639234792592378$ for domain count, $0.001713645678402473$ for PTM density, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for transmembrane count. This places the turnover result in the same empirical regime as the yeast feature result: the relevant finite quantities are computed, but the available candidate columns do not close the residual as a stable downstream explanation.

They are included only to locate the yeast turnover row within the larger finite survey: organism-level heterogeneity is present, but the recorded permutation tests do not convert the yeast half-life projection into a general rule.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The turnover candidate is ready as a bounded negative-to-small absorption result. The contact is finite, joined, and rank-checked; the target residual is stable against the full yeast abundance-CDS reference within $0.02$; and the half-life projection was computed on $3640$ rows. The numerical conclusion is that yeast steady-state protein half-life absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual in this construction. The remaining residual should therefore be left open for other contacts such as localization, condition-specific regulation, complex-membership refinements, perturbation data, or other protein-state readouts.

Nothing in this chapter establishes a translation-realization claim beyond the separately bounded measured-TE and tRNA-copy contacts. Nothing here establishes folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Such claims require their own matched observations and their own controlled statistics. The half-life row is a descriptive projection against one yeast turnover readout, and its low absorption is evidence against treating this particular turnover column as the explanation of the non-translation residual.
