\subsection{Turnover Contact for the Residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Component}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-turnover-contact}

\paragraph{Finite contact surface.}
This record tests whether a yeast protein-turnover readout absorbs any visible part of the non-translation residual left after the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coding-coordinate surface has already been separated from the modeled translation-adaptation direction. The finite carrier is the joined Saccharomyces cerevisiae surface consisting of coding-sequence codon counts, protein abundance, measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and protein half-life rows. The turnover contact is not a protein-function or mechanism claim. It is a bounded regression contact whose readout is log-scaled Christiano 2014 half-life, obtained through the SGD backend per-locus protein-experiment field and mapped from SGD systematic ORF names to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$.

The operative coordinate list is the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual panel
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, namely an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the actual CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. On the turnover join, $n=3640$ rows remain. The reported CDS join contains $5815$ rows, protein abundance has $6351$ valid protein identifiers, measured TE has $5128$ valid protein identifiers, and the turnover source contributes $3640$ valid half-life rows. The CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P\_Q$ denote the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component after the declared controls, and let $T\_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modeled translation-adaptation direction. The turnover test starts from the modeled-complement residual $P\_{Q\perp T\_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ and projects it against residualized log half-life. On the turnover-specific join the finite norms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P\_Q\rVert^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\lVert P\_{Q\perp T\_{\mathrm{mod}}}\rVert^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d\_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same complement metric is close to the full-abundance CDS reference: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows, while the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$ on $3640$ rows, with absolute difference $0.011538255214205373$. This falls inside the declared $0.02$ join-stability tolerance. Thus the turnover join does not substantially change the modeled-complement target before the half-life contact is evaluated.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The half-life contact absorbs only a very small part of the modeled-complement residual. The computed absorption and combined residual are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d\_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a positive but extremely small descriptive absorption. It does not close the residual explanatory gap; rather, it says that the steady-state yeast half-life surface is almost orthogonal to the remaining $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement component under the specified controls. The result is therefore best read as a finite negative pressure on the hypothesis that this particular yeast turnover readout explains the non-translation residual. It is not a null about all turnover biology, because the contact is a single steady-state dataset and is organism-specific.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The turnover rows are not isolated from the translation-efficiency contact: measured TE is available for the same organism, and $3537$ rows join CDS, abundance, turnover, and measured TE simultaneously. The measured-TE study uses Ingolia et al. 2009 Science, GSE13750, BY4741 mid-exponential rich-medium footprint and mRNA quantification files, with TE defined as mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across the matched rich-medium replicates. In the broader measured-TE contact, Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5128$ valid TE rows, $5815$ CDS-joined rows, and rank $23$ for the same control family. Its measured-TE absorption is $0.0002762783754375593$ with $d\_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$. The turnover contact is therefore not replacing the TE boundary; it is a downstream candidate surface tested after the modeled translation-adaptation complement has been isolated.

\paragraph{Relation to measured tRNA adaptation.}
The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact provides a separate bounded translation-adaptation readback. For Saccharomyces cerevisiae it uses $273$ parsed tRNA genes, $41$ anticodon gene-copy table entries, $268$ usable measured-tAI records, and the full $5815$ CDS join. Its absorption of the modeled-complement component is $0.001530747712165342$ with $d\_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$. This comparison matters because the turnover absorption, $0.0009086031889966327$, is of the same small order as the tRNA-copy contact and remains far too small to promote the residual into a turnover mechanism. The three surfaces together say that the yeast residual is modest in magnitude upstream, since $d\_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ is about $0.21$ to $0.22$, and that neither measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, nor steady-state protein half-life absorbs it strongly on the tested joins.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The valid conclusion is finite and narrow: in the yeast half-life join, the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement abundance component has a computed turnover absorption of $0.0009086031889966327$ under the stated amino-acid composition, GC3, length, and $M$-density controls, with a stable complement target relative to the full abundance-CDS reference. No translation realization, folded-structure statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, causal mechanism, phenotype statement, or universal biological law follows from this entry. A claim at any one of those layers would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. The present row only licenses the bounded statement that this yeast turnover readout was tested and did not materially absorb the remaining non-translation residual.
