\subsection{Turnover Contact for the B$^*_{Q6}$ Non-Translation Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-contact}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite row.}
This record tests whether a yeast protein half-life surface accounts for any appreciable part of the B$^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has already been removed. The tested object is not a direct codon-to-turnover law. It is the descriptive projection of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto a residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life readout. The finite carrier contains nine residual coordinates, namely \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}. The control design has 24 columns before rank loss and rank $23$ after the amino-acid composition constraint. Its covariates are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions estimated from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected B$^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined yeast readout.}
The turnover row is restricted to \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Protein half-life values come from the SGD backend protein-experiment-details surface for the Christiano 2014 per-locus half-life measurements. The run mapped SGD locus systematic names to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing \texttt{4932.}. The finite join contains $3640$ turnover proteins and $3640$ CDS-abundance-turnover rows, against $5815$ CDS rows with abundance joins and $6351$ valid abundance proteins. The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$. A measured translation-efficiency join is available in the same yeast study: the Ingolia et al. 2009 rich-medium ribosome-profiling data give $5128$ genes with valid TE, where TE is the mean footprint normalized read density divided by the mean mRNA normalized read density across the paired rich-medium replicates. The triple join of measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS has $3537$ rows; $103$ CDS records are lost only because the optional measured-TE match is absent, and $2175$ are lost because no turnover match is present.

\paragraph{Numerical projection.}
On the turnover join, the modeled-complement norm is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This value agrees with the full yeast abundance-CDS complement reference within the declared tolerance: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$, the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373 < 0.02$. Thus the turnover surface is evaluated on a finite row set whose upstream non-translation residual is close enough to the full yeast abundance-CDS reference to be interpretable as the same descriptive target. The half-life projection itself is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Relative to the available non-translation component, the half-life coordinate removes less than one tenth of one percent in this run. The evidence therefore points to a negative or near-null turnover explanation for the measured yeast residual rather than to a turnover-resolved closure of the H-candidate.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring translation contacts.}
The measured-TE contact in the same collection gives yeast $n=5128$, rank $23$, and a measured-TE absorption of $0.0002762783754375593$ with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$. The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact on the full yeast CDS-abundance join uses $5815$ rows, $273$ tRNA genes, an anticodon GCN table of size $41$, and reports $\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ tRNA}=0.001530747712165342$ with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$. These adjacent rows are useful as scale checks: in yeast, the modeled non-translation residual is already modest, and neither measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, nor turnover absorbs a large part of it. The turnover row is therefore best read as a bounded exclusion of a simple half-life explanation on this joined yeast panel, not as a new positive layer assignment.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite result licenses the following narrow statement. In the yeast panel with $3640$ joined turnover rows, the B$^*_{Q6}$ modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual remains essentially intact after projection against residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life. The half-life surface is a tested H-candidate, but its observed absorption $0.0009086031889966327$ is too small to support a turnover-mediated explanation of the residual. The result is descriptive and conditional on the Christiano 2014 steady-state half-life surface, the SGD-to-STRING mapping, the PaxDB integrated whole-organism abundance surface from 2026, and the Ingolia et al. 2009 yeast TE availability used for the optional triple join.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No translation realization, protein-output mechanism, folding conclusion, physical admissibility claim, functional interpretation, phenotype claim, causal mediation result, or universal biological rule follows from this row. The calculation is a finite controlled projection. It says that this yeast turnover readout does not materially absorb the already-defined B$^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual under the listed controls. Any claim about direct ribosome occupancy, mature protein abundance, localization, complex membership, folding, physical behavior, molecular function, system phenotype, or mechanism would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.
