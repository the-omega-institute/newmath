\subsection{A turnover test for the remaining $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The finite surface used here is the yeast readout obtained by joining coding-sequence codon counts, whole-organism protein abundance, measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and protein half-life.  The tested object is not a protein-function claim and not a folded-structure claim.  It is the following bounded question: after the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates have been adjusted for ordinary coding-sequence covariates and after the modeled translation-adaptation component has been removed from the abundance projection, does measured protein turnover absorb any remaining component?

The residual coordinate list is finite and explicit:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_{AAA},\ Arg\_{AGR},\ Ile\_{AUA},\ Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f3\_{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $|Q|=9$.  The control design has $24$ columns before rank loss and rank $23$ after the amino-acid composition constraint is accounted for.  Its columns are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons lying in the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined rows and contact quality.}
The turnover contact is yeast-only.  The joined table contains $3640$ rows for turnover, abundance, and CDS records, and the same $3640$ rows are available for the all-readout turnover join.  The optional joint with measured translation efficiency contains $3537$ rows, because $103$ CDS records lack the measured-TE match on that stricter join.  The reported CDS join contains $5815$ coding sequences, the valid protein-abundance table contains $6351$ proteins, the valid measured-TE table contains $5128$ genes, and the valid turnover table contains $3640$ proteins.  The reported CDS hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, while the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The half-life source is the SGD backend per-locus protein half-life record, identified as the Christiano et al. 2014 steady-state turnover dataset; SGD systematic names are mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name.

The translation-efficiency values used for the measured-TE contact are defined from the Ingolia et al. 2009 rich-medium yeast ribosome-profiling data as mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across the two rich-medium replicates.  This matters because the turnover packet is not allowed to treat transcript abundance or ribosome occupancy as interchangeable with protein half-life.  The matched mRNA abundance has control status only: it separates a transcription-layer denominator from translation-efficiency readout, but it does not itself measure protein turnover.

\paragraph{Modeled complement before turnover.}
On the turnover join, the modeled-translation complement has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{mod} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same quantity is close to the full-abundance complement reference for yeast: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows, while the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$ on $3640$ rows.  The absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is below the $0.02$ comparison tolerance.  This is an important finite-row check: the turnover join changes the row set, but it does not strongly distort the residual target relative to the full yeast abundance-CDS join.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
The turnover statistic is computed by residualizing log-scaled half-life against the same control design and projecting the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual onto this turnover direction.  In the notation of this chapter, the descriptive absorption is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore not a turnover explanation of the remaining $B^{*}_{Q6}$ component.  The residual size changes from $0.2232704509412724$ to $0.22306503721327192$ after adding the half-life readout, a difference far too small to support a substantive turnover-mediated account.  The appropriate reading is negative but bounded: under this yeast steady-state half-life contact, protein turnover absorbs less than one tenth of one percent of the modeled-translation-complement component.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and tRNA readouts.}
The same family of finite computations prevents an over-reading of the turnover result.  In the measured-TE packet, yeast has $5128$ measured-TE rows, a rank-$23$ control design, $P_Q$ norm squared $675.3275479192903$, $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ norm squared $151.18264752811882$, and $d_{mod}=0.22386566043976477$.  Measured TE absorbs only $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast, giving $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.  In the measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI packet, the yeast full CDS join has $5815$ rows, $273$ tRNA genes, $41$ anticodon entries, and $268$ usable measured-tAI records; the tRNA readout absorbs $0.001530747712165342$ and gives $d_{combined}=0.20648621019305421$.  These companion numbers show that the yeast residual is already modest after modeled translation adaptation, and neither measured TE, tRNA-gene-copy tAI, nor turnover strongly removes the remaining component.

\paragraph{Protein-feature context.}
A separate yeast feature packet also gives only small per-feature absorptions on its $5682$-row feature join.  The four tested feature directions are complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane count.  Their absorptions are respectively $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  None of these yeast feature candidates exceeds its Bonferroni threshold under the degree-of-freedom matched null: the thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane count.  This agrees with the turnover result: in yeast, the remaining $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component is not explained by the tested half-life or coarse reviewed-feature contacts.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The broader organism table is heterogeneous and underpowered for universal biological language.  The domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$.  The continuous meta tests likewise do not license a broad rule: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$; versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These values support only a descriptive organism-specific table, not a phylogenetically corrected or causal conclusion.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is that the turnover contact is computable and rank-admissible for yeast, but it does not explain the modeled-translation-complement $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual.  The accepted statement is therefore narrow: on $3640$ joined yeast rows, with rank $23$ controls over the nine declared coordinates, log half-life absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  This is a descriptive projection statistic, not a causal mediator test.

No stronger biological promotion follows locally.  The chapter does not claim translation realization from turnover, does not infer folding or structural order, does not infer physical admissibility, does not infer molecular function, does not infer mechanism, and does not infer universality.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled finite-row statistic.
