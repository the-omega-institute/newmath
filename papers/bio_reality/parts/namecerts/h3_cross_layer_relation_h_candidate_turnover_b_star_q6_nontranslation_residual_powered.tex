\subsection{Turnover as a bounded candidate for the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual}\label{sec:bior-real-turnover-q6-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite readout considered here asks whether protein half-life can account for a residual part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-coordinate signal that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation component has already been removed from the abundance surface.  The tested object is not a protein mechanism and not a folding or function claim.  It is the finite panel consisting of yeast coding sequences with codon counts, abundance rows, measured translation-efficiency rows when available, and a matched protein half-life value.  The response used for the turnover candidate is $\log_{10}$ half-life from the Christiano steady-state protein-turnover source as exposed through the SGD backend API.  The residual side is the modeled-tAI complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ computed from the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate basis.}
The coordinate list is fixed before the turnover readout is consulted.  The nine coordinates are \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and numerical rank $23$.  Its named covariates are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 computed from the same CDS codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the turnover surface is evaluated only after amino-acid composition, length, GC3, and this codon-support baseline have been held fixed.

\paragraph{Rows and joins.}
The yeast abundance source reports $6351$ valid abundance proteins, and the CDS table reports $5815$ CDS rows joined to abundance.  The turnover contact contributes $3640$ valid positive half-life proteins, all $3640$ of which are retained in the turnover-abundance-CDS panel.  The optional all-readout panel that also requires measured translation efficiency contains $3537$ rows, because $103$ CDS rows have no measured-TE match under the optional joint readout.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, while the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The identifier rule is explicit: an SGD systematic ORF name is mapped to the STRING protein identifier by prefixing \texttt{4932.}.  No abundance records were skipped for missing identifiers, duplicate identifiers, or nonpositive abundance; no turnover records were skipped for missing identifiers, duplicate identifiers, or nonpositive half-life.  On the CDS side, $2175$ rows fail the turnover join, while empty codon counts, invalid length, missing protein identifier, missing abundance match, and non-object rows each contribute zero skipped rows.

\paragraph{Translation panel context.}
The matched translation-efficiency context is the Ingolia et al. rich-medium yeast ribosome-profiling panel.  Translation efficiency is defined here as mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across the two rich-medium replicates.  In the broader measured-TE panel, yeast has $5128$ valid measured-TE proteins from $5815$ abundance-matched CDS rows; the same $B^*_{Q6}$ basis and the same rank-$23$ control design give $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$ on the measured-TE join.  The measured-TE candidate itself absorbs only $0.0002762783754375593$ of the modeled-complement abundance residual in yeast, giving $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  This small measured-TE absorption is useful context for the turnover test: turnover is being evaluated as a later candidate for the residual, not as a substitute for the translation-efficiency contact.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability on the turnover join.}
On the turnover panel, the squared norm of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component is $457.02479128996873$, and the squared norm remaining after modeled translation adaptation is $102.04013124265222$.  The resulting complement fraction is
$$\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724 .
\end{aligned}$$
This value is close to the full yeast abundance-CDS complement reference $0.21173219572706703$.  The absolute discrepancy is $0.011538255214205373$, which is below the declared $0.02$ stability tolerance.  Therefore the turnover join does not materially change the upstream residual target: the half-life panel is sparse relative to the full CDS-abundance surface, but it remains close enough to the reference complement geometry to support a bounded descriptive turnover projection.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
The turnover candidate projects $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life after the same control family is applied.  The computed turnover absorption is
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192 .
\end{aligned}$$
The numerical effect is therefore very small.  Relative to the turnover-join complement value, the decrease is approximately
$$\begin{aligned}
0.2232704509412724 - 0.22306503721327192 &= 0.00020541372700048 .
\end{aligned}$$
Thus protein half-life does not explain the remaining yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement abundance residual at a scale comparable to the residual itself.  The result is still informative because it is not a rank or join artifact: the turnover panel has $3640$ joined rows, a rank-$23$ control design, and a modeled-complement value that agrees with the full yeast reference within the stated tolerance.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring candidates.}
Other finite contacts give the local scale of this negative turnover readback.  In the measured tRNA-copy tAI test, yeast has $273$ tRNA genes, $41$ anticodon-GCN table entries, $5815$ joined CDS rows, and an absorbed fraction $0.001530747712165342$ with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  In the protein-feature test, yeast retains $5682$ joined feature rows from $6156$ feature records; the feature-join modeled complement is $0.21235197749007645$, within $0.0006197817630094193$ of the full complement reference, but none of the four feature candidates gives a large absorption in yeast: complex membership absorbs $0.00069212177545487$, domain count absorbs $0.00007029698692511431$, PTM density absorbs $0.0005759336988655581$, and transmembrane-count absorbs $0.00031741485098156394$.  These neighboring finite tests agree in scale: for yeast, after the modeled translation-adaptation component is removed, the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual is modest upstream and is not substantially compressed by the tested turnover or coarse protein-feature contacts.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite conclusion is a bounded non-absorption result.  The turnover contact is real enough to be tested: it has a named half-life column, a deterministic identifier mapping, $3640$ joined yeast rows, and a stable upstream residual target.  But its absorption value, $0.0009086031889966327$, is too small to treat half-life as a resolved explanation of the modeled-complement $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual.  The correct readback is that the yeast residual remains largely outside transcription, modeled translation adaptation, measured translation efficiency, and this steady-state protein-turnover candidate.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
This subsection licenses only the descriptive projection stated above.  It does not show that $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates cause turnover, regulate degradation, alter ribosome dynamics, determine protein abundance, specify folding, establish physical admissibility, realize molecular function, determine phenotype, or define a universal biological law.  The Christiano half-life source is a single yeast steady-state turnover contact, not a perturbational mediator assay.  A positive mechanistic statement would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as perturbation, rescue, degradation-rate, localization, complex-membership, or condition-specific measurements with controls appropriate to that layer.  Until such a contact is attached and evaluated, the only supported statement is that this finite yeast turnover panel absorbs a very small fraction of the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement abundance residual.
