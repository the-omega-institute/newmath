\subsection{Yeast Turnover as a Bounded H-Candidate for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Residual}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-turnover-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests protein half-life as a narrow downstream candidate for the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual that is not already absorbed by the modeled translation-adaptation baseline.  The carrier is a finite yeast CDS-by-protein surface: real CDS codon counts are joined to PaxDB abundance, Ingolia-style measured translation-efficiency availability, and SGD protein half-life records.  The residual basis has nine coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_Q = \{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f3_{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 from the same CDS codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the turnover test is not a raw codon-count association; it is a controlled projection after composition, length, GC3, and the shared $M$-support baseline have already been removed.

\paragraph{Joined yeast rows.}
The turnover contact is restricted to \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The joined table contains $n=3640$ proteins with positive protein half-life, abundance, and CDS information, out of $5815$ CDS rows reported after abundance joining and $6351$ valid abundance proteins.  The turnover source contributes $3640$ valid half-life proteins, and the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$.  The half-life field is the SGD \texttt{protein\_experiment\_details} \texttt{data\_value} in hours, mapped from the SGD systematic locus name to a STRING protein identifier by prefixing \texttt{4932.}.  The loaded turnover payload has byte size $77817$ and SHA256 digest \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.  The optional all-readout intersection with measured translation efficiency has $3537$ rows, while the turnover-abundance-CDS join has $3640$ rows.  Skipped CDS records are concentrated in the layer being tested: $2175$ CDS rows have no turnover match and $103$ have no measured-TE match for the optional joint readout; missing protein identifiers, invalid lengths, empty sense-codon counts, and abundance mismatches are all reported as $0$ in this finite table.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection and let $T_{mod}$ denote the modeled translation-adaptation component.  The turnover candidate is evaluated only against the modeled-complement component $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.  On the turnover join, the squared norm of the controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ projection is $457.02479128996873$, while the squared norm of the modeled-complement component is $102.04013124265222$.  The resulting complement ratio is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{modeled} &= 0.2232704509412724,\\
d_{reference} &= 0.21173219572706703,\\
|d_{modeled}-d_{reference}| &= 0.011538255214205373.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite self-check therefore stays within the declared $0.02$ tolerance against the full abundance-CDS complement reference, even though the half-life join is smaller than the $5815$-row abundance-CDS reference.  This matters because a layer contact that changes the joined population can otherwise change the residual target being projected.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The half-life candidate is the residualized $\log_{10}$ Christiano-style protein half-life readout.  Projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto that one-dimensional turnover readout gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed}_{turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absorbed fraction is therefore positive but very small.  Its interpretation is descriptive: on this yeast-only steady-state half-life surface, measured turnover explains less than one thousandth of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  Because the upstream modeled-complement fraction in yeast is already modest, approximately $0.223$, the absolute room for a turnover explanation is limited in this contact.  The result supports a bounded negative reading rather than a mechanistic closure: turnover is a tested candidate, not an adequate explanation of the remaining residual on these rows.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The same yeast study context has a measured translation-efficiency route using mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across two rich-medium replicates.  In the measured-TE computation, yeast has $5128$ valid TE proteins, $5815$ CDS rows after abundance joining, control rank $23$, and the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates.  Its measured-TE absorption is $0.0002762783754375593$ with $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover table has $3640$ half-life rows and $3537$ rows when TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS are all required simultaneously.  These paired numbers show that the turnover contact is not replacing the translation-efficiency contact; it is a separate downstream readout tested after the modeled translation component has already been factored out.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite row data license the following statement and no stronger one: in the yeast half-life contact, the modeled-translation-complement component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual survives nearly unchanged after adding residualized protein half-life, with only $0.0009086031889966327$ absorbed by turnover.  The contact is useful because it places a quantitative bound on one plausible downstream explanation for the residual.  It does not establish a causal mediator, a ribosome-dynamics mechanism, a translation law, a protein-folding effect, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  Any such higher-layer assertion would require its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, half-life association alone cannot be promoted to translation realization, and the remaining residual should be read as still open to other layer-specific contacts such as localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, or other protein-state measurements.
