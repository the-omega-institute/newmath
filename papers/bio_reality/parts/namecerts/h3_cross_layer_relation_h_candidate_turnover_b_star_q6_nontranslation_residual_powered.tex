\subsection{Protein-turnover contact for the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ complement residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface and readout.}
This record tests one bounded downstream contact for the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual basis: whether the abundance component left after the modeled tAI projection is materially shortened by a yeast protein half-life readout.  The calculation is not a claim about translation itself.  It starts from nine residual coordinates, namely $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The nuisance matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, with an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The turnover readout is $\log_{10}$ protein half-life from the SGD backend protein-experiment records, using Christiano et al. 2014 per-locus half-life values and the systematic-name to STRING identifier rule $ORF \mapsto 4932.ORF$.

\paragraph{Joined yeast rows.}
The yeast abundance table contains $6351$ valid abundance proteins, the codon table reports $5815$ CDS rows after the abundance join, the measured translation-efficiency table has $5128$ valid proteins, and the half-life table supplies $3640$ valid turnover proteins.  The turnover calculation joins $3640$ rows with abundance and CDS coordinates, and the stricter all-readout intersection with measured translation efficiency contains $3537$ rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, while the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The skipped CDS rows are concentrated in the turnover boundary: $2175$ rows have no turnover match and $103$ rows lack the optional measured-TE match, with no rows lost for invalid length, empty sense-codon counts, missing protein identifier, or missing abundance.  The turnover payload has $77817$ bytes and SHA256 digest $05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
On the turnover join, the modeled-tAI-complement residual has squared norm $102.04013124265222$ inside a B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ projected norm of $457.02479128996873$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled,turnover}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This agrees with the full abundance-CDS complement reference within the declared tolerance: the full-join reference is $0.21173219572706703$, the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, and the comparison is marked as within $0.02$.  After adding residualized protein half-life as the candidate readout, the combined residual distance is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192,\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.0009086031889966327.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row set does not show a substantial turnover absorption of the non-translation abundance complement.  In this contact, protein half-life explains less than one thousandth of the modeled-complement residual fraction, so the observed effect is descriptive and weak rather than a stabilizing downstream account of the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured-TE and tRNA contacts.}
The same family of coordinates has already been evaluated against measured translation efficiency on organism-specific joins with matched mRNA abundance in the denominator.  Those measured-TE joins contain $13140$ zebrafish rows, $3415$ E. coli rows, $11554$ human rows, and $5128$ yeast rows, each with the same $24$ control columns and rank $23$ control matrix.  The absorbed fractions by measured TE are $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for E. coli, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  The yeast turnover result is therefore not an isolated refusal: in the yeast measured-TE contact, the measured-TE absorption is also very small, and in the yeast tRNA gene-copy tAI contact the absorbed fraction is $0.001530747712165342$ with $273$ tRNA genes and $41$ anticodon table entries.  These finite numbers keep the turnover contact in the same low-absorption regime as the yeast translation-readout controls.

\paragraph{Cross-organism scope check.}
The organism-specific summary is exploratory rather than a population law.  Its complete feature panel has $12$ organisms, while four organisms still need data for the M$^{QTP}$ summary.  The reported domain test for M$^{QTP}$ fraction has permutation right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$ with $1000$ trials, and the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ on $12$ rows.  The continuous checks are also non-decisive: M$^{QTP}$ versus GC3 has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, versus log protein count has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$, and versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These values prevent the yeast turnover contact from being promoted into a cross-organism rule.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite turnover surface is ready as a bounded negative-to-small result.  It shows that, on $3640$ yeast proteins with matched CDS, abundance, and half-life rows, the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-tAI-complement residual is stable to the turnover readout: $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ moves from $0.2232704509412724$ to $0.22306503721327192$, with turnover absorption $0.0009086031889966327$.  The correct reading is therefore conservative.  The B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ complement residual is not materially explained by this single steady-state yeast half-life contact, and the remaining signal stays open to other readout layers such as localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, or additional protein-state measurements.  No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this turnover statistic.  Any such statement would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.
