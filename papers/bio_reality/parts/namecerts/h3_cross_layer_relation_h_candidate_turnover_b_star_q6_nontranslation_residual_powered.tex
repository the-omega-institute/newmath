\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded candidate for the residual abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein-turnover coordinate absorbs any appreciable part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has been removed.  The finite surface is not a protein-function assay and not a translation-realization assay.  It is a yeast-only same-identifier join in which coding-sequence residual coordinates, abundance rows, optional translation-efficiency rows, and a protein half-life readout are brought onto the same STRING-shaped protein identifier scale.  The tested residual basis has nine coordinates, namely $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The control design has $24$ columns with rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined yeast panel.}
The turnover contact is a measured half-life table for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; the source field is the SGD backend protein-experiment detail value in hours, attributed in the row metadata to Christiano et al. 2014.  The identifier map uses the SGD locus systematic name converted to the STRING form by prefixing $4932.$.  The finite join contains $3640$ turnover proteins, $6351$ valid abundance proteins, $5815$ CDS rows joined to abundance, and $5128$ genes with measured translation efficiency available from the Ingolia et al. 2009 rich-medium ribosome-profiling and mRNA files.  The turnover-abundance-CDS join has $n=3640$ rows, and the stricter joint readout with measured translation efficiency has $n=3537$ rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The skipped-row accounting is also finite: no abundance row was skipped for missing, duplicate, or nonpositive protein identifiers; no turnover row was skipped for missing, duplicate, or nonpositive half-life values; $2175$ CDS rows lacked a turnover match; and $103$ CDS rows lacked the optional measured-TE match.

\paragraph{Projection quantity.}
The tested object is the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$, not a causal mediator.  On the yeast turnover join, the squared norm of the controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection is $457.02479128996873$, and the squared norm after removing the modeled-translation component is $102.04013124265222$.  Thus the residual fraction reported for this finite surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{modelled,turnover} &= 0.2232704509412724,\\
 P_Q^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
 P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}^2 &= 102.04013124265222.
\end{aligned}
$$
This finite value is consistent with the full-join complement reference for yeast within the declared tolerance: the reference abundance-CDS join has $5815$ rows and $d_{modelled}=0.21173219572706703$, whereas the turnover join has $3640$ rows and $d_{modelled}=0.2232704509412724$, giving absolute difference $0.011538255214205373<0.02$.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
After residualizing $\log_{10}$ half-life against the same control design, the turnover readout accounts for only a very small fraction of the modeled-translation-complement component.  The recorded absorption is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
 d_{combined} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite conclusion is therefore negative in the intended sense: on this yeast panel, steady-state protein half-life does not materially compress the residual component that remains after modeled translation adaptation has been removed.  Since the upstream yeast residual is already modest, with $d_{modelled}$ near $0.22$, the absolute amount available for a turnover explanation is small; nevertheless, the turnover contact was well populated, same-identifier joined, and close to the abundance-CDS complement reference.

\paragraph{Comparison with adjacent readouts.}
The measured-translation-efficiency record gives the relevant local contrast.  On the yeast measured-TE join, $n=5128$ rows are available, with $5815$ CDS-abundance rows and rank $23$ controls.  The measured-TE absorption is $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{combined}=0.2237928123367028$, and its modeled-complement value $0.22386566043976477$ differs from the full abundance-CDS reference by $0.012133464712697745$.  Thus, in yeast, both the measured-TE contact and the turnover contact leave nearly the same residual scale in place.  A second comparison comes from the measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI record, where the yeast full join has $5815$ rows, $273$ tRNA genes, $41$ anticodon table entries, and $d_{modelled}=0.21173219572706703$ exactly matching the complement reference on the full join.  Its absorbed fraction is $0.001530747712165342$, again small.  These neighboring finite rows support a bounded readout statement: the tested yeast residual is not appreciably removed by measured TE, tRNA-copy tAI, or protein half-life under their respective designs.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a descriptive cross-layer projection statement for one yeast turnover dataset.  It does not show that $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates regulate protein degradation, does not identify a molecular mechanism, and does not convert a half-life association into translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  The half-life table is a single steady-state turnover contact, and the absorption statistic is not a perturbation, rescue, pulse-chase mechanism test, ribosome-occupancy measurement, mature protein abundance claim, structural assay, or functional assay.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present finite result is narrower: after the declared CDS, amino-acid-composition, $GC3$, length, and $M$-density controls, yeast protein half-life absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual, so turnover is not a strong explanation of the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ component on this surface.
