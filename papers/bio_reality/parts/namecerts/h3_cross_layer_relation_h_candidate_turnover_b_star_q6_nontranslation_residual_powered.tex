\subsection{Turnover as a bounded candidate for the remaining $B^{*}_{Q6}$ signal}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The turnover packet asks a narrow question: after the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates have already been tested against codon-composition controls and a modeled translation-adaptation component, does measured protein half-life absorb any appreciable part of the remaining abundance-side component?  The row surface is yeast-only.  The joined table contains $3640$ proteins with coding-sequence, abundance, and turnover rows, with $3537$ rows also carrying the matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readout.  The reported coding-sequence join has $5815$ rows, the abundance table has $6351$ valid protein rows, the measured-TE table has $5128$ valid protein rows, and the turnover table has $3640$ valid protein rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  Turnover is read as the SGD protein-experiment half-life value, mapped from the SGD systematic locus name to a STRING protein identifier by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name.  The turnover payload has $77817$ bytes and SHA256 digest \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control design.}
The tested residual basis has nine coordinates: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The covariate matrix has $24$ declared columns and numerical rank $23$.  Its declared columns are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$ computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the finite statement is not a raw half-life association.  It is a controlled projection after amino-acid composition, length, third-position GC content, and the support-density baseline have already been placed in the background model.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the controlled $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance-side projection on the turnover join, and let $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the part left after the modeled translation-adaptation component is removed.  In the turnover table the computed norms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same statistic is close to the full abundance-CDS complement reference: the reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows, the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$ on $3640$ rows, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$, which is inside the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  This makes the yeast turnover join acceptable as a stable target for a bounded descriptive test, rather than as a new biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The half-life candidate is tested by residualizing $\log_{10}$ Christiano 2014 protein half-life against the same control surface and projecting $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ onto that one-dimensional readout.  The computed absorption is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Numerically, the turnover contact leaves the modeled-complement fraction essentially unchanged: $0.2232704509412724$ before the half-life readout and $0.22306503721327192$ after the readout is added.  The finite conclusion is therefore negative for this candidate in the measured yeast condition.  Protein half-life is not the observed route by which this residual component is absorbed in the available steady-state yeast table.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring bounded contacts.}
The result is also consistent with the nearby finite contacts.  In the measured-TE packet, yeast has $5128$ translation-efficiency rows and shows only $0.0002762783754375593$ absorption by measured TE, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  In the measured tRNA-copy tAI packet, yeast has $273$ tRNA genes, $41$ anticodon-GCN table entries, and $0.001530747712165342$ absorption by the tRNA-derived tAI readout, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  In the protein-feature packet, the yeast feature join has $5682$ rows and reports only small per-candidate absorptions: $0.00069212177545487$ for complex membership, $0.00007029698692511431$ for domain count, $0.0005759336988655581$ for PTM density, and $0.00031741485098156394$ for transmembrane count.  These neighboring tests do not make the turnover result stronger; they show that several convenient downstream readouts fail to explain much of the same remaining component in the yeast and cross-organism surfaces.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The organism-specific meta packet is exploratory rather than decisive.  Its complete-feature table has $12$ organisms out of $16$ assembled rows.  The dominant-coordinate-versus-domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M_{QTP}$-fraction tests likewise do not establish a resolved organism-level driver: against $GC3$, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; against log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and against log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  This context matters because the turnover experiment is yeast-only.  It can bound one candidate readout, but it cannot supply a population-level or phylogenetically corrected law.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The ready statement is therefore a refusal-strengthened bounded result.  On the available yeast panel, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement abundance residual has a modest upstream size, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}\approx0.2233$, and measured protein half-life absorbs only $0.0009086$ of it.  The finite row supports the statement that the yeast steady-state turnover readout does not explain the remaining component in this design.  It does not support a claim that translation is absent, that turnover is biologically irrelevant, or that the residual has no downstream route.  It only says that this particular half-life contact, under these controls and this join, contributes almost no descriptive absorption.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
No translation-realization, protein-folding, physical-admissibility, molecular-function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality claim follows from this row.  The ribosome-profiling, tRNA-copy, protein-feature, turnover, and organism-meta contacts are bounded statistical readouts, not causal perturbation systems.  A positive claim at any higher layer would require its own matched contact and its own statistic on the appropriate rows.  In particular, the half-life result is not a causal mediator test, the Christiano 2014 readout is a single steady-state yeast dataset, and the low absorption leaves the residual open to other layer-specific explanations such as localization, complex membership under better coverage, condition-specific regulation, or unmeasured post-translational state.
