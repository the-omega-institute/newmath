\subsection{Yeast turnover as a bounded test of the residual abundance complement}\label{sec:bior-hcandidate-turnover-q6-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the residual abundance component left after the modeled translation-adaptation projection is further accounted for by a matched protein-turnover readout.  The surface is deliberately narrow: it contains yeast coding-sequence rows with the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, the standard covariate block $Z$, the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual, and a log-scale half-life column from the SGD protein-experiment backend.  The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, built from an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the turnover test is not a raw codon-to-half-life association; it is a projection of an already controlled residual component against a matched half-life coordinate.

\paragraph{Join and measurement discipline.}
The turnover contact is yeast-only.  SGD systematic locus names were mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$, and the identified half-life column is the SGD protein-experiment $\mathrm{data\_value}$ in hours.  The run reports $5815$ coding sequences joined to the abundance/CDS surface, $6351$ valid abundance proteins, $5128$ valid translation-efficiency proteins, and $3640$ valid turnover proteins.  The turnover-abundance-CDS join has $n=3640$ rows, with the same $n=3640$ for the all-readout turnover join and $3537$ rows when measured translation efficiency is also required.  The reported coding-sequence join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The explicit skip accounting is also finite: $2175$ CDS records fail only because no turnover match is available, while $103$ fail the optional measured-TE joint requirement; missing identifiers, invalid lengths, duplicate abundance identifiers, nonpositive abundance, duplicate TE identifiers, nonpositive footprint, nonpositive mRNA, nonpositive TE, duplicate turnover identifiers, and nonpositive half-life all have count $0$ in the recorded filters.

\paragraph{Residual complement stability.}
The turnover join preserves the upstream modeled-complement quantity within the declared tolerance.  On the full yeast abundance/CDS reference surface, the modeled complement had value $0.21173219572706703$ at $5815$ rows.  On the turnover join it is recomputed as $0.2232704509412724$ at $3640$ rows, with absolute difference $0.011538255214205373$, below the $0.02$ stability boundary used by the run.  The recorded norm decomposition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This matters because the turnover projection is being applied to a stable version of the yeast modeled-tAI-complement residual, not to a join-distorted target.  The result is therefore eligible as a bounded descriptive readout for this yeast surface.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
Projecting the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual onto residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life gives only a small additional absorption:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite conclusion is negative in strength but informative in location.  Protein turnover, as measured by this Christiano-2014-style steady-state yeast half-life contact exposed through SGD, does not materially explain the non-translation residual left by the modeled-tAI complement.  The amount absorbed is below one tenth of one percent of the relevant scale, while the upstream yeast residual itself is already modest, with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}\approx 0.223$.  The correct reading is therefore not that turnover is impossible as a biological influence, but that this particular single-condition yeast half-life panel does not close the residual abundance component.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The same yeast study context supplies measured translation efficiency from Ingolia et al. 2009, using rich-medium footprint and mRNA quantification files with $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$.  On the measured-TE surface, yeast has $5815$ CDS rows in the abundance/CDS reference, $5128$ valid TE proteins, and a rank-$23$ control matrix with $24$ columns.  The measured-TE projection absorbs $0.0002762783754375593$, with $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  The turnover run then adds a compatible yeast half-life readout, but the turnover absorption is still only $0.0009086031889966327$.  Together these two finite checks say that, for yeast under these joins, neither aggregate measured TE nor protein half-life appreciably removes the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual.

\paragraph{Population and specificity boundary.}
The broader organism-specificity table prevents promotion from this yeast-only turnover contact to a cross-organism rule.  In the available cross-organism summary, $12$ organisms have complete feature rows, while $16$ organisms appear in the assembled meta table and four organisms still need data for the $M^{QTP}$ comparison.  The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction tests are likewise exploratory: versus GC3 the correlation is $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus $\log$ proteome size it is $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; versus $\log$ tRNA gene count it is $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$; and the domain variance-ratio test has statistic $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These values are not evidence for a universal turnover law.  They support only the narrower statement that organism-specific residual geometry varies, and that the present yeast turnover surface should be reported as a bounded, single-organism probe.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter therefore records a controlled non-closure result for the turnover candidate.  The measured half-life coordinate is attached to a real joined row set, the upstream residual target is stable under the turnover join, and the finite projection has been computed.  Its value, however, is too small to explain the yeast residual abundance complement in any strong sense.  The residual remains open to other bounded contacts such as localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, RNA stability with matched factors, or other post-translational state measurements, but those contacts must be supplied and estimated separately.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation-realization claim follows from the turnover entry by itself.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from it either.  The half-life measurement is a descriptive projection target on a yeast matched panel, not a causal mediator test and not a protein-structure or function assay.  A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own rows, controls, and statistic; the present record licenses only the finite statement that yeast protein half-life absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual on the $n=3640$ turnover join.
