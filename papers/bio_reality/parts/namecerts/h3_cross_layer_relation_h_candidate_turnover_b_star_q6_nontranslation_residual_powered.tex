\subsection{Turnover Contact for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Non-translation Residual}
\label{subsec:b-star-q6-turnover-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a measured protein half-life surface explains any appreciable part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has already been removed.  The carrier is a finite yeast row table: coding-sequence codon counts are joined to protein abundance, measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and SGD protein half-life rows.  The coordinate side has nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control design has 24 columns and rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined rows and contact identity.}
The half-life contact is yeast-only.  The input table reports $6351$ valid abundance proteins, $5815$ CDS rows joined to abundance, $5128$ genes with measured translation-efficiency values, and $3640$ valid turnover proteins.  The turnover test itself uses $n=3640$ joined turnover--abundance--CDS rows, with the same $3640$ rows available for the declared all-readout turnover table.  A stricter optional join including measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS rows has $3537$ rows, so $103$ CDS records are lost by requiring the optional measured-TE match.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  Protein half-life values are read from the SGD protein experiment details field $data\_value$ in hours, with SGD systematic ORF names mapped to STRING protein identifiers by prefixing $4932.$.  The turnover payload has byte size $77817$ and SHA-256 digest \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
The quantity tested here is not raw codon usage and not raw abundance.  The modeled-adaptation complement first forms the residual abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T\mathrm{mod}}$, then asks whether residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life accounts for that component.  On the turnover join the norm accounting is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T\mathrm{mod}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same modeled-complement quantity agrees with the full abundance--CDS complement reference within the declared tolerance: the full-reference value is $0.21173219572706703$, the turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373 < 0.02$.  Thus the turnover join changes the row set, but it does not produce a large displacement of the residual target relative to the yeast complement baseline.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption.}
After residualizing the half-life readout against the same control surface, the observed turnover absorption is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The interpretation is therefore negative for this candidate contact.  Protein half-life is a valid finite readout for a post-translational turnover layer, and the join is sufficiently close to the abundance-complement reference to make the comparison meaningful; nevertheless, the yeast Christiano-style steady-state half-life column absorbs less than one tenth of one percent of the modeled-translation-complement residual.  In this finite table, turnover does not materially close the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring readouts.}
The same yeast abundance complement is small upstream compared with the other organisms in the measured-TE contact: yeast has $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477$ on the measured-TE all-readout join, whereas zebrafish, \textit{E. coli}, and human have $0.8424312412078521$, $0.6628211177352172$, and $0.8566190269974241$, respectively.  Direct measured TE absorption in yeast is also small, $0.0002762783754375593$, and measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption in yeast is $0.001530747712165342$.  Protein-feature candidates are likewise bounded rather than decisive in yeast: complex membership absorbs $0.00069212177545487$, domain count absorbs $0.00007029698692511431$, PTM density absorbs $0.0005759336988655581$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.00031741485098156394$.  These finite numbers put the turnover result in context: yeast carries a modest non-translation residual after modeled translation adaptation, and the tested turnover, measured-TE, tRNA-copy, and coarse feature readouts each leave most of that residual unexplained.

\paragraph{Local decision.}
For this chapter the finite-row verdict is a bounded negative contact, not a biological promotion.  The turnover table is real enough to test the candidate, the row count is $3640$, the control rank is $23$, and the modeled-complement stability check passes the $0.02$ tolerance.  But the turnover absorption value $0.0009086031889966327$ is too small to support a positive turnover explanation for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual.  The remaining residual should be treated as still open to other layer-matched contacts such as localization, complex membership under richer coverage, condition-specific proteostasis, or perturbation readouts.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
This computation is a descriptive projection on a single yeast steady-state half-life dataset.  It is not a causal mediator test.  It does not establish realized translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, a mechanism, a phenotype, or a universal biological law.  It also does not license any claim that a $Q_i$ coordinate determines turnover.  Such claims require separate contacts at the corresponding biological layer, with their own matched rows, controls, and finite statistics.
