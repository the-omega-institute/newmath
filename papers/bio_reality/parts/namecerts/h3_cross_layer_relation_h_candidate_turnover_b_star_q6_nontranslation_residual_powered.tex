\subsection{Turnover Contact for the Yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ Non-translation Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
The turnover contact tests whether the yeast protein half-life readout accounts for any visible part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component left after modeled translation adaptation has been removed.  The finite carrier is the joined Saccharomyces cerevisiae surface on which coding-sequence codon counts, abundance rows, measured translation-efficiency rows, and protein half-life rows can be aligned by protein identifier.  The tested residual basis has nine coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$, consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 from the same CDS codon counts, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The turnover source contributes $3640$ positive half-life protein rows after parsing SGD locus systematic names into STRING identifiers by prefixing $4932.$, with no missing, duplicate, or nonpositive half-life records reported.

\paragraph{Join and control adequacy.}
The yeast abundance table contains $6351$ valid abundance proteins, the CDS join reports $5815$ coding-sequence rows, and the measured translation-efficiency table contains $5128$ valid TE proteins.  The turnover-specific analysis joins $3640$ turnover-abundance-CDS rows, and the same $3640$ rows are available for the all-readout turnover surface; adding measured TE reduces the joint surface to $3537$ rows.  The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the reported turnover join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The principal skipped CDS category is absence of a turnover match, with $2175$ CDS records excluded for that reason; $103$ CDS rows lack the optional measured-TE match, while invalid length, empty sense-codon counts, missing protein identifiers, and missing abundance matches are all reported as $0$.  Thus the turnover contact is not a whole-proteome claim: it is a finite aligned yeast panel whose rank and row counts are explicitly visible.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement target.}
The quantity projected onto turnover is the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual.  On the turnover join, the squared norm of the $Q$ projection is $457.02479128996873$, while the squared norm remaining perpendicular to modeled tAI is $102.04013124265222$.  The resulting modeled-complement fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This value is consistent with the full abundance-CDS complement reference for yeast: the full-reference value is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows, so the turnover-join value differs by $0.011538255214205373$, inside the declared $0.02$ stability tolerance.  The self-check therefore says that the turnover join changes the sample but does not visibly distort the modeled-complement target beyond the recorded tolerance.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
Protein half-life is then used as a one-readout H-candidate by projecting the modeled-tAI complement residual onto residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life from the Christiano 2014 steady-state turnover data.  The measured absorption is very small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
The interpretation is therefore negative at the scale of this contact.  Turnover is present as a matched yeast readout and passes the finite join and stability checks, but it explains less than one tenth of one percent of the modeled-complement residual on this surface.  Since $d_{\mathrm{combined}}$ remains essentially the same as $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$, the finite row does not support a claim that steady-state half-life accounts for the yeast $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured-TE and tRNA contacts.}
The neighboring measured-TE row gives the same yeast coordinate list and rank-$23$ control design on $5128$ TE-valid rows, with measured-TE absorption $0.0002762783754375593$ and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028$.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI row, computed on the full yeast CDS join of $5815$ rows with $273$ tRNA genes and an anticodon table size of $41$, gives $\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_trna}=0.001530747712165342$ and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.20648621019305421$.  These adjacent finite rows are useful because they show that the turnover outcome is not an isolated missing-file artifact: several bounded readouts are available, each absorbs only a small fraction of the same yeast residual component.  The turnover contact is therefore best read as a finite negative H-candidate rather than as evidence for a hidden degradation mechanism.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
This chapter licenses only the descriptive statement that the parsed yeast half-life readout has been joined to the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface and has negligible absorption of the modeled-tAI complement component under the stated controls.  It does not establish causation, mediation, adaptation, translation realization, ribosome occupancy, mature protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  The half-life data come from a single steady-state yeast turnover source, and the upstream yeast residual is already modest with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ near $0.21$.  Any stronger conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic; the present finite row only says that this particular turnover readout does not materially close the remaining yeast residual.
