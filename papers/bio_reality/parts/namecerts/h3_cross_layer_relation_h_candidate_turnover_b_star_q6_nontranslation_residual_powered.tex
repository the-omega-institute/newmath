\subsection{Turnover Contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Non-translation Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-turnover-contact}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests a bounded contact between the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual basis and a protein-turnover readout in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The coordinate side is the same nine-dimensional residual surface used for the $Q6$ non-translation panel:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control design has $24$ columns and numerical rank $23$.  Its columns are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 measured from codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The tested readout is not a new codon statistic.  It is the logged protein half-life table loaded from the SGD backend protein-experiment endpoint for the Christiano 2014 yeast steady-state turnover measurement.

\paragraph{Joined rows and identifier route.}
The finite join contains $3640$ proteins with turnover, abundance, and CDS rows.  The turnover table also contains $3640$ valid protein half-life records, and the reported abundance table contains $6351$ valid proteins.  The CDS side reports $5815$ joined coding sequences, so the turnover-side CDS hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$ on the reported CDS join and the protein-level join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$.  The mapping is explicit: the SGD locus systematic name is converted to the STRING protein identifier by prefixing $4932.$ to the ORF name.  The turnover column is the SGD \texttt{protein\_experiment\_details} \texttt{data\_value} in hours.  No abundance records were skipped for missing identifiers, duplicate identifiers, or nonpositive abundance.  On the CDS side, $2175$ records lacked a turnover match, and $103$ records lacked the optional measured-TE match.  The turnover payload has byte size $77817$ and SHA-256 hash \texttt{05077d4d73f9dc3f16f331a4ae0c4a4d482e65f17a94de3522b3c0c485335690}.

\paragraph{Residual target before turnover.}
The upstream target is the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual.  On the turnover join,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 457.02479128996873,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 102.04013124265222,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.2232704509412724.
\end{aligned}
$$
This $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ agrees with the full abundance-CDS complement reference $0.21173219572706703$ to within $0.011538255214205373$, below the stated $0.02$ tolerance.  The agreement matters because turnover availability changes the rows under test: the turnover join has $3640$ rows, whereas the full abundance-CDS reference has $5815$ rows.  The row change is therefore present, but it does not move the modeled-complement fraction enough to invalidate the yeast turnover comparison.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption estimate.}
The turnover candidate is evaluated by projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life under the same $Z$ controls.  The resulting absorption is small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 0.0009086031889966327,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.22306503721327192.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, in this finite contact, adding steady-state yeast half-life changes the modeled-complement residual by less than one tenth of one percent of the tested residual scale.  The result is a bounded negative or near-null turnover contact, not a turnover mechanism.  It says that this single yeast half-life table does not materially absorb the $B^*_{Q6}$ component that remains after modeled tAI.  It does not say that degradation is irrelevant in other organisms, conditions, assays, or protein-state definitions.

\paragraph{Measured-translation context.}
The same yeast corpus has a measured translation-efficiency join from the Ingolia et al. 2009 rich-medium ribosome-profiling and mRNA files.  In the measured-TE run, yeast has $5128$ valid TE proteins, $5815$ CDS rows reported, and $5128$ all-readout rows after matching.  Its measured-TE absorption is also small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_measTE} &= 0.0002762783754375593,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined,TE}} &= 0.2237928123367028,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled,TE\ join}} &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The optional joint table containing measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS rows has $3537$ rows.  This supports the local interpretation that the yeast non-translation residual is small upstream and remains mostly unabsorbed by the two measured readouts used here.  The turnover result is therefore a descriptive contact on the yeast row set, not a general law about translation, degradation, or abundance control.

\paragraph{Cross-organism scope check.}
The broader $B^*_{Q6}$ organism-specific summary remains heterogeneous.  The cross-organism meta table has $16$ assembled rows and $12$ complete-feature rows.  For the complete-feature subset, the $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 correlation has $n=12$, Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$, and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus log protein count has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$, and the comparison versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The domain comparison gives a variance ratio of $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These values do not license a cross-organism turnover interpretation for the yeast half-life contact.  They only keep the broader $Q6$ panel open as an organism-specific collection of bounded readouts.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The yeast turnover contact is numerically well-formed: the control matrix is full enough for the declared design, the turnover row count is finite and reported, the identifier route is explicit, and the modeled-complement reference remains stable under the turnover join.  The biological outcome is narrow.  Protein half-life absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled-tAI-complement residual and leaves $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.22306503721327192$.  This is evidence for a bounded non-absorption result in the Christiano 2014 yeast half-life contact, not evidence for translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such claim would require its own matched readout and its own controlled statistic at that layer.
