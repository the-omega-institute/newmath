\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery at the codon-label layer}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube.  The object is not treated as a mechanism for translation.  It is used as a finite table of codon labels, where each codon has a curated amino-acid or stop assignment.  The local question is whether the window-six coordinate presentation exposes a stable boundary tile that helps recover code-label changes when one code table is held out.

The finite row isolates a distinguished codon set $R$ of size $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records that this set has the punctured-cube face count
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R) &= (13,16,4),\\
|R| &= 13.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the relevant signal is not a diffuse property of all $64$ codons.  It is concentrated on a finite boundary-shaped subset, and the biological readout is only the curated code label attached to each codon.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape controls.}
The coordinate computation assigns a companion set $M$ of size $20$ and reports the spectral scalars
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check is numerically closed at
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same tolerance residual reported for the cubic polynomial.  These numbers are used only as finite coordinate diagnostics.  They do not say that cells, ribosomes, tRNAs, or proteins are governed by this geometry.

The shape-control run sampled $N=2000$ distinct null objects after $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserving amino-acid class also used $N=2000$.  It had observed $|M|=20$, no exact shape match, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These controls support the claim that the finite tile is not a typical output of the tested reshufflings.  They still remain code-table controls, not experiments on translation kinetics, folding, physical admissibility, organismal fitness, or function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ target events.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against this baseline, the finite coordinate tile recovered held-out code-label targets at the following rates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ above\ baseline} &= 0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in}\ M-T &= 0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in}\ M-T\ \mathrm{plus\ boundary} &= 0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The evidence therefore supports a narrow statement: when one code table is left out, the finite local codon geometry almost always places the held-out label change either inside the $M-T$ region or in its boundary completion.  The claim is about table-label recovery.  It is not a claim that the local tile causes the reassignment, implements translation, predicts protein structure, or describes a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Family-level resolution.}
The family split contains $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The only non-unit family rate in this finite row is the alternative-yeast family.  The result is still strong at the code-label layer, but this family split is also a warning against overstatement: a high local recovery rate is not a proof of a shared molecular pathway across families.

\paragraph{Codon-level module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, high closure coverage of $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module accounting covers all $13$ codons of $R$ with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This complete assignment is important because it prevents the recovery result from resting on an unclassified residue of the finite set.  Every codon in $R$ is placed into a named module, and every module contains either predictable or boundary codons.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The contact used here is a curated standard-code table.  It supplies codon-to-label observations and permits finite checks of local label splits.  It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Amino-acid label readback is therefore kept separate from protein structure.  A later structural or mechanistic claim would require a different contact, such as an organism-matched translation measurement, a structure experiment, a physical admissibility test, or a functional assay.  Without such a contact, the present chapter stops at code-layer recovery: the window-six tile is a compact finite predictor for held-out codon-label changes, not a global law of biology.
