\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery in the window-six codon cube}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim identified as $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$ tests whether the finite window-six codon representation contains enough local information to recover variant-code targets after one code table is withheld.  The object under study is the standard-code codon table, read only as a codon-to-label assignment.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with distinctions given by codon display, local tile face, singleton label corner, and three-point label class.  The internal data used here are coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data; the readback is the ordinary codon label table.  The point of the test is narrow: the geometry may expose a local boundary tile, while the labels themselves remain empirical contacts supplied by a curated code table.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and exceptional set.}
The distinguished relation set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row identifies $R$ with a punctured-cube pattern whose face-vector is $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the local object is not merely a list of biologically familiar codons; it has a four-face tile profile inside the six-bit cube.  The module decomposition accounts for all $13$ codons with no extra codons, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}, &
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}, &
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}, &
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each listed module has either predictable support or boundary support in the finite classification.  This is the local combinatorial content of the claim.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The spectrum row supplies three real scalars for the same codon geometry: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The cubic residual and the radial polynomial residual are both recorded as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical relation is satisfied to the reported tolerance.  A separate shape test used $N=2000$ randomized samples with seed $42$.  Among $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ were duplicate retries.  Only $1$ exact shape match occurred, giving $p=0.0005$, while $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, exact shape matches were $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0, &
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These rows support the interpretation that the observed tile is not a typical outcome of the tested finite null procedures.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$; the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$; and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Equivalently, the finite row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathrm{base}}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
h_{\mathrm{above\ base}}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
h_{M-T}&=0.9365079365079365,\\
h_{M-T+\partial}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The family split has five families.  The hit rate above baseline is $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and it is $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  This makes the recovery claim strongest as a finite code-read phenomenon: the withheld-code target is almost always recovered by the local tile plus its boundary, relative to the baseline recorded in the same row.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out companion check.}
The linked leave-one-codon-out row reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Its threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  This companion check is useful because it keeps the same local interpretation at the codon level: recovery can pass through predictable or boundary status without asserting that the codon geometry is a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Reality contact and interpretation.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact tests code-layer readback and local label splitting.  It does not test translation realization, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the finite-row evidence supports the following restricted conclusion: within the six-bit codon cube, the $13$-codon relation set has a punctured-cube tile shape, a stable module classification, non-null shape statistics under the recorded finite randomizations, and strong leave-one-code-out recovery against the recorded baseline.  It does not by itself promote the tile to a law of biology.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The chapter therefore treats $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$ as ready at the code-read layer only.  Amino-acid label readback is not folded protein structure.  A local codon tile is not translation, structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality unless a separate contact supplies observations at that higher layer.  Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, and codon-label reassignment patterns remain on their own organism-matched routes; none of them converts the present standard-code table contact into structural or functional evidence.
