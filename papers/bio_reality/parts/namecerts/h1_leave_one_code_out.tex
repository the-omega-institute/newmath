\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback on the Window-Six Codon Cube}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record studied here is a code-layer packet on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  Its carrier is the finite codon display obtained by writing each RNA codon as a six-bit window, with local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes treated as internal distinctions.  The only biological contact used in this section is the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  Thus the computation may test codon-label readback and local tile boundaries, but it does not observe translation chemistry, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or biological universality.

\paragraph{Residual codon set.}
The primary residual set $R$ has thirteen codons.  In lexicographic RNA notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The computed cardinality is $|R|=13$.  The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube shape with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This means that the residual tile is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it has a finite adjacency-and-face profile inside $Q_6$.  The module partition is complete: all thirteen codons are assigned, with no extra module codons, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has either a predictable member or a boundary member, so the finite row supports a local codon-tile interpretation rather than a global biological rule.

The radial cubic check gives $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial residual.  At this numerical tolerance the polynomial row is effectively zero, while the two displayed radii remain distinct.  The role of this row is diagnostic: it says that the finite tile has a stable numerical signature in the codon-window geometry.  It does not say that biology is forced to realize that signature as a mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ target events.  Its baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against that baseline, the finite tile achieved
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{hit rate above baseline}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\text{hit rate in }M-T&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\text{hit rate in }M-T\text{ plus boundary}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, when one code table is held out, the local codon-window packet nearly always places the target inside the predicted region or its boundary class.  This is the chapter's main finite claim: the window-six coordinates expose a strong local readback pattern for curated code labels.

Since the contact is still only a curated codon-label table, these family rows should be read as code-layer agreement, not as organism-level physiology or tRNA-copy evidence.

\paragraph{Comparison with shape nulls.}
Two null rows give scale for the tile.  In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match, yielding $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, yielding $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling process recorded $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact\ shape}}&=0.0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These null rows make the local shape look non-random relative to the tested finite ensembles.  They still do not promote the result beyond label readback.

\paragraph{Single-codon recovery row.}
The related leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closures, and $8$ predictable codons.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  This supports the same interpretation from a different deletion granularity: the codon-window packet is good at recovering local label positions inside the finite code table, especially through closure and boundary behavior.

\paragraph{Scope of the result.}
The result is ready at the code-read layer.  It supports the statement that window-six codon coordinates reveal a local boundary-tile structure in curated genetic-code labels.  It does not support any claim that the geometry is translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a law of molecular biology.  Amino-acid label readback is not a structure observation.  Cross-organism wording, CUN/UUR terminology, or tRNA-Leu notation would require separate organism-matched contacts before being used as evidence for a higher-layer claim.  Within the finite data actually supplied here, the proper conclusion is narrower and stronger: the standard-code label table aligns with a highly predictive local codon-window tile, and the boundary of that tile is where the claim must stop.
