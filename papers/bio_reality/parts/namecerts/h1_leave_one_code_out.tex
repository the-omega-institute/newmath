\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Stability for the Window-Six Codon Tile}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
The claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$ concerns a window-six presentation of the standard-code codon table.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, used only as a finite coordinate display for codon labels.  The tested layer is code readback: codons are compared with amino-acid or stop labels from the curated standard code table, and the resulting local tile is inspected for boundary behavior.  This chapter does not treat translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law as consequences of the coordinate geometry.  Those stronger readings would require separate observations of the corresponding kind.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The finite unstable region $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the verified run, the row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the empirical object is not an arbitrary codon subset: it is a thirteen-codon local boundary shape inside the six-bit display.  The row includes singleton-corner behavior, three-codon or multi-codon label classes, and local face participation, but these are read as code-table phenomena rather than as physical constraints on organisms.

This is a finite numerical consistency check on the tile; it is not evidence that the genetic code is generated by the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  Only $1$ exact shape match was found, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ matches at Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampling log also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the row was compared against a constrained finite sampling space rather than against a purely symbolic null.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$.  The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These comparisons support the statement that the local row is statistically non-generic relative to the tested finite nulls.  They do not identify a translation process, a selective history, or a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against that baseline, the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The finite conclusion is that when a code table is held out, the local tile and its boundary recover nearly all tested targets at the code-label layer.  This is the central positive observation of the chapter: the six-bit tile has strong leave-one-code-out stability for label readback.

\paragraph{Codon-level module classification.}
The leave-one-codon-out module classification assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each module, the row records that it has either predictable or boundary status.  The independent leave-one-codon-out summary gives $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, $8$ high-coverage closures, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Thus the codon-level and code-level tests agree on the local nature of the signal: it lives in predictable pieces plus boundary pieces, not in a claimed global mechanism.

\paragraph{Empirical contact and boundary.}
The empirical contact is the curated standard-code table, whose observed content is a codon-to-label assignment.  It resolves code labels and local codon-tile splits.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, folded protein structure, admissible molecular physics, phenotype, or function.  Consequently, amino-acid label sequences obtained from this table remain label sequences.  A claim about folds, structure, function, translation, or universality would need an additional contact that directly measures or validates that layer.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite row supports a ready code-read claim.  The evidence consists of the thirteen-codon punctured-cube row, the spectral consistency values, the low exact-shape and class-preserving null rates, the $23$-table leave-one-code-out test over $63$ targets, the five-family stratification, and the complete six-module classification of $R$.  The chapter therefore records a local boundary-tile regularity in the standard-code label table.  Its positive content stops at codon-label readback and local boundary recovery.
