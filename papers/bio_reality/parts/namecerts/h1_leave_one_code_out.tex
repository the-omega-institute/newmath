\subsection{Leave-one-code-out stability of the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The object recorded here is the standard-code codon label table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube. The claim is deliberately local: the window-six display detects a boundary-sensitive tile in the code-read layer, and the curated codon-to-label table supplies the label contact against which this tile is tested. It does not say that the tile is a biological mechanism, a rule of translation, a protein-folding principle, a physical-admissibility criterion, a function detector, or a universal law of genomes. Those stronger readings require independent observations at the corresponding layer.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and distinguished row set.}
The carrier is the $64$-point codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon represented by a six-bit display. The distinguished row set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent punctured-cube check returns the same list and gives the face-count vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the local object is not a verbal grouping of codons after the fact; it is a finite subcomplex in the six-bit codon display with a fixed vertex list and a fixed face profile.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The row set also carries a finite spectral signature. The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic check gives $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, numerically at zero at the stated tolerance, and the recorded spectral-radius witness is again $0.6752479308830213$. These numbers are used only as finite diagnostics of the tile geometry. They are not promoted into molecular energetics, translational dynamics, or structural biology.

\paragraph{Shape significance against finite nulls.}
The first null experiment used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. Because the sampling space contains many duplicates, it required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and logged $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the accepted samples, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. A second null, preserving amino-acid class information, again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It observed no exact shape match, recorded $p=0.0$ at the displayed Monte Carlo resolution for exact shape recovery, and found $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size $20$, giving lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These finite rows support the statement that the observed tile is uncommon under the tested reshufflings; they do not establish a generative biological law.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The primary row for this chapter evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. By contrast, the observed hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The stricter interior rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, while the rate after adjoining the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. In finite terms, the code-read contact is therefore stable under leave-one-code-out testing: almost every withheld target recovered by the tested procedure lies either in the interior tile or on its recorded boundary. This is the strongest positive statement licensed by the row.

\paragraph{Family-level split.}
The family summary contains $5$ families. The above-baseline hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes. This family decomposition matters because it prevents the headline rate from hiding a single-family artifact. The alternative-yeast family is the weakest listed family and is still far above the baseline rate $0.19821739130434785$.

\paragraph{Codon-module accounting.}
The module classification assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing and no extra codons. The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\}, & \mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}, & \mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\}, & \mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit row reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, an empty extra-module list, an empty missing-from-modules list, and an empty unassigned-codon list. Each module has a predictable or boundary status, so the finite recovery statement is not merely aggregate; it respects the displayed codon-module partition.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out consistency.}
The codon-level leave-one-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The thresholded recovery entry is also $1.0$. This is compatible with the code-level result: the local tile behaves as a boundary-and-closure object rather than as a list of individually anchored codons. The absence of anchors is part of the interpretation, not a defect to be filled by mechanism language.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The sole observation contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. Its resolution is codon label readback. It can test whether the finite six-coordinate tile aligns with local label splits in the code table. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Amino-acid labels read from a table are labels only; a label sequence is not a folded protein and is not evidence of admissible physical form.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The verified finite rows jointly support the following bounded conclusion. In the six-bit codon cube, the $13$-codon set $R$ forms a punctured-cube tile with face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral scale $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, significant rarity under the listed finite nulls, complete six-module accounting, and strong leave-one-code-out recovery across $23$ tables and $63$ targets. The chapter therefore records a local code-read phenomenon attached to the curated standard-code label table. No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows without a separate contact designed for that higher layer.
