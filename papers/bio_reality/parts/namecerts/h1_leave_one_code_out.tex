\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite object and scope.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-layer contact and asks whether a window-six coordinate model detects a stable local boundary pattern when code tables are held out. The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read through codon labels rather than through translation dynamics. The finite distinctions used here are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The internal organization is coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation. The biological object under contact is only the standard-code label table: codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels, and no claim about ribosomal realization, folding, admissibility in chemistry, organismal function, mechanism, or universality is part of the inference.

\paragraph{Exceptional relation row.}
The finite relation row $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codon set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is reported as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed packet is not merely a count anomaly: it includes a concrete thirteen-codon subset, an edge-and-face profile, and a specific codon inventory. The local codon list is concentrated around stop/tryptophan, arginine, isoleucine/methionine, leucine CUN, leucine UUR, serine-family, and lysine-adjacent positions. This is the level at which the chapter speaks: a finite pattern in codon-label coordinates.

They do not turn the codon table into a dynamical model of translation and do not certify any physical or functional law.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the reported finite null sample, the shape row used $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries. Exact shape agreement occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null row used $N=2000$, seed $42$, observed marked-size $20$, and found zero exact matches. Its lower-tail count at or below the observed marked size was $7$, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The marked region $M$ is therefore present in the finite packet as a twenty-codon region with significant shape behavior, but this row does not expose the full member list of $M$. The usable numerical facts here are its size, its relation to held-out recovery, and its null-model behavior.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The central held-out-code experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ held-out targets. The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas the reported hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9365079365079365,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was again
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the finite row is consistent with $59$ of $63$ targets landing inside $M\setminus T$ and $62$ of $63$ landing inside $M\setminus T$ together with its boundary. The experiment therefore supports a strong local boundary recovery statement for code-label tables. It does not support a higher-layer biological statement unless another contact supplies that layer.

This stratification is important because it prevents the global score from hiding all variation. The alternative-yeast family is weaker but still high, while the other four listed families are perfect in the supplied finite row. The inference remains a code-read inference: these are codon-label table families, not organism-level mechanism classes.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The thirteen codons of $R$ are completely assigned to six modules. The reported module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row states $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. In addition, every module has predictable or boundary status in the row. This makes the result legible at module scale: the local tile does not just recover isolated codons, but covers all listed relation modules under the finite classification supplied by the packet.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out check.}
The related codon-level row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. Its threshold comparison is also $1.0$. This row is not the main held-out-code statistic, but it is compatible with the same reading: the local structure is organized by predictable and boundary locations, not by a claim that codons intrinsically enforce biological outcomes.

\paragraph{Reality contact and blocked promotions.}
The contact used in this chapter is a curated standard genetic-code table at codon-label resolution. It can test code readback and local label splits. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, or function. Consequently, an amino-acid label sequence produced from the codon table is still only a label sequence. It is not evidence of a folded protein, not evidence of structural order, not evidence of biochemical admissibility, and not evidence of organismal function. The finite geometry may point to a boundary tile in $Q_6$, but it does not make that tile into a global biological law.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite rows support the following narrow statement: in the supplied window-six codon-coordinate packet, the thirteen-codon relation row, the punctured-cube profile $(13,16,4)$, the spectral diagnostics, the significant shape rows, the complete module classification, and the $23$-table leave-one-code-out experiment jointly support strong local code-layer recovery. The best numerical summary is that $62$ of $63$ held-out targets are recovered by $M\setminus T$ together with its boundary, compared with a baseline hit rate of $0.19821739130434785$. The statement stops there. Any move from local codon-label recovery to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires a distinct observational contact not present in this finite row.
