\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback for the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This packet treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label readout on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The relevant display is not a claim about molecular execution.  It is a code-layer arrangement whose internal coordinates distinguish codon positions, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The observed contact is the curated standard-code table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label, and the packet tests whether a window-six local tile organizes those label changes in a way that survives leave-one-code-out evaluation.

The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the supplied row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the signal is not merely the number of codons in $R$; it is also the local cubical incidence profile carried by those codons.  The module partition is complete on this $13$-codon set: $\mathrm{AGR}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{CUN}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{SSN}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The central leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  In other words, once the local tile and its boundary are allowed as the code-layer predictor, nearly all held-out targets in this finite evaluation are recovered above the baseline rate.

The family split keeps the same interpretation.  Five families are reported.  Their above-baseline hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes.  This family row says that the leave-one-code-out behavior is not confined to a single named family in the supplied finite sample, although it still remains a code-table readback claim rather than a mechanistic claim.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out cross-check.}
The leave-one-codon-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure coverage high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The recovery-rate threshold row is also $1.0$.  These values are consistent with the module classification: every module has either predictable or boundary support, and all $13$ codons in $R$ are assigned to the listed modules.  The code-layer picture is therefore local and finite: the tile sees codon labels through closure and boundary behavior, not through a separate observation of translation dynamics.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently the conclusion of this packet is deliberately narrow: window-six codon coordinates expose a strong local tile pattern for standard-code label readback, with $R$ equal to the listed $13$-codon punctured cube and with high leave-one-code-out recovery on the supplied rows.  No claim about translation realization, folded protein structure, physical possibility, functional effect, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate contact is introduced that directly tests that higher layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$, the finite evidence is ready at the code-read layer.  The supported statement is that the standard-code label table, when displayed in the six-bit codon cube, has a local boundary-tile organization that predicts held-out code-label targets far above the listed baseline.  The unsupported statements are equally important: amino-acid label readback is not folded structure, CUN or UUR wording is not structure evidence, and a local codon tile is not a global biological law.
