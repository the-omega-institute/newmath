\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback for the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out-readback}

\paragraph{Claim site.}
The claim tested here is a code-layer claim about the standard-code codon table.  The finite object is the six-coordinate codon cube, read as the $4^3=64$ codon display with each base position represented by two binary distinctions.  The relevant finite pattern is not a law of translation and not a protein-structure claim.  It is a local boundary pattern: after the standard-code labels are supplied by the curated codon table, the window-six coordinates isolate a small region whose label behavior is unusually recoverable under leave-one-code-out tests.

\paragraph{Finite tile data.}
The distinguished region $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the observed set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row also verifies that this region has punctured-cube face data with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite geometry is not merely a hand-picked list of recoding codons: it is recorded as a thirteen-vertex local tile with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces inside the codon cube.  The supplied row does not include the full membership list for the companion region $M$, but it does give the observed size $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null experiment and supplies the recovery rates measured against $M\setminus T$ and its boundary extension.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The coordinate summary includes a numerical spectral profile.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the cubic-polynomial residual.  This is a numerical near-zero at the scale of the recorded computation, so the spectrum row supports treating the tile as a stable finite coordinate pattern rather than as an arbitrary codon subset.  The interpretation remains strictly finite and descriptive: the spectrum names a coordinate regularity of the labeled codon display, not a biochemical force or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the recorded null sample with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact shape appeared once.  The row reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $J\geq 0.9$.  A second null that preserves amino-acid classes reports $N=2000$, observed $|M|=20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers justify recording the local tile as a statistically sharp code-read pattern.  They do not justify promoting the pattern beyond the code layer.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The main finite-row result evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $(M\setminus T)$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  In compact form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{baseline}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{>\mathrm{baseline}}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{M\setminus T}&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{(M\setminus T)+\partial}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The code-family breakdown has five families.  The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate.  This makes the claim a leave-one-code-out robustness statement about label readback near the local tile boundary.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out support.}
The companion leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  The absence of anchors is important: the row is not saying that a single codon alone carries the whole explanation.  Instead, the recoverable signal is distributed through closure and boundary behavior in the finite tile.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The thirteen codons of $R$ are completely assigned to six modules.  The recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{IleMet}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{StopTrp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completion row records $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Each module has either predictable or boundary status: $AGR$ contains $AGA,AGG$; $AR\_Lys$ contains $AAA$; $CUN$ contains $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$; $IleMet$ contains $AUA$; $SSN$ contains $UCA,UUA$; and $StopTrp$ contains $UAA,UAG,UGA$.  The module row therefore supplies a finite partition of the tile, not merely an aggregate hit rate.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only biological contact used by this chapter is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code-layer readback and local label splitting inside the codon tile.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal rule.  The measured pattern therefore has the following local meaning: once the codon labels are imported from the curated table, the six-coordinate display exposes a boundary-tile organization whose leave-one-code-out recovery is far above the recorded baseline.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows.  Amino-acid label sequences are still label sequences; they are not folded proteins.  CUN, UUR, stop, tryptophan, isoleucine, methionine, lysine, serine, or arginine labels in this table do not by themselves supply a structure experiment.  A later structural or functional claim would need its own contact, such as an organism-matched translation observation, a folding measurement, a physical admissibility assay, or a function assay.  Without such a separate contact, the chapter closes at the code-read layer: a finite codon-coordinate tile, a thirteen-codon punctured-cube region, a complete six-module classification, significant null comparisons, and high leave-one-code-out recovery against the curated standard-code labels.
