\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery at the Codon-Label Boundary}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The claim tested here is a code-layer recovery claim for the standard-code codon table.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary-window presentation of codon positions together with a curated amino-acid-or-stop label table.  The claim is not that the geometry of $Q_6$ is a biological mechanism.  It is the narrower assertion that window-six coordinates can expose a local boundary tile which remains visible when one code-table row is held out and then tested against the remaining label structure.  The biological contact is therefore the curated standard genetic-code assignment itself: codons are observed here only as code labels, not as translated molecules, folded structures, physical realizations, or functions.

\paragraph{Finite residual set.}
The residual codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the supplied finite row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the residual object is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it has a finite face profile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the local tile.  This is the combinatorial object used by the leave-one-code-out test.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinate.}
The associated finite spectrum supplies three scalar coordinates.  The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check is numerically zero to tolerance, with value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the reported spectral-radius entry is again $0.6752479308830213$.  The chapter uses these numbers only as finite diagnostics of the code-table tile.  They do not promote the codon display into a chemical or evolutionary law.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The observed $M$-shape test was run with $N=2000$ sampled controls and seed $42$.  Among $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ were duplicate retries.  The exact-shape count was $1$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and the high-Jaccard count at threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  These finite values support the statement that the observed local shape is uncommon under the sampled control process.  The amino-acid-class-preserving null gives a separate check: with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, the exact-shape value is $0.0$, and the lower-tail value is $0.0035$.  The available row does not give an itemized list of the $20$ members of $M$, so this chapter uses the reported $M$ size and the $M$-relative hit rates rather than inventing a codon list.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out outcome.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  Against that baseline, the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  In finite-count terms, the boundary-augmented criterion accounts for nearly all of the $63$ targets, whereas the baseline accounts for about one fifth.  The proper reading is therefore a code-table recovery signal: the local tile and its boundary retain enough finite structure to recover most held-out code-label targets in the evaluated family.

\paragraph{Codon-level companion test.}
The leave-one-codon-out companion row gives a stricter local view.  It reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The same value $1.0$ is reported for recovery above threshold.  This companion result is compatible with the code-level test: the recovery is carried by predictable or boundary status rather than by an external assertion about translation, structure, or function.

\paragraph{Family breakdown.}
The family-level split has $5$ families.  The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate-other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate.  Thus the weakest listed family still clears the baseline by five sixths, and the other four families are complete at the reported resolution.  This supports robustness across the finite family partition, while still staying inside the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete for the residual set.  It reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing members from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  The assigned modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has predictable or boundary status.  This is the local combinatorial explanation for why the leave-one-code-out test can score strongly without needing a biological-mechanism premise.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The contact used here is a curated codon-to-label table.  It can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded-protein order, physical admissibility, functional effect, or universality.  Amino-acid label readback is therefore not protein structure.  Likewise, a local tile in $Q_6$ is not a global law of biology.  Any future statement about translation, folding, chemistry, function, organism-specific usage, or universal mechanism must be attached to a separate observation of that layer.  Without such a contact, the supported conclusion is confined to this finite result: for the supplied standard-code label data, the residual tile $R$, its module partition, and the $M$-relative boundary criterion recover held-out code-label targets far above the stated baseline.
