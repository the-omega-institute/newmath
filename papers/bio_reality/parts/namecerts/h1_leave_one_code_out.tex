\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Stability for the Window-Six Codon Tile}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The object under inspection is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon read as a coordinate point and each curated amino-acid or stop assignment treated as a label contact. The construction is deliberately local: it records a tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes inside the codon display, but it does not identify those finite combinatorial features with translation chemistry, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.

The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified finite row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set with a punctured-cube shape having face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the record is not only a list of exceptional codons: it is a finite local shape in the six-coordinate codon display.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The associated spectral row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported residual for the radial cubic relation is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the finite numerical check is at floating-point tolerance rather than an exact symbolic identity. The shape significance row used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$. The same sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the row should be read as a finite randomized comparison with explicit collision pressure, not as an asymptotic theorem.

A second null comparison preserves amino-acid class information. In that row the observed set size is $|M|=20$. Among $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving alternatives, the exact-shape count is $0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$. The corresponding finite probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the claim that the observed local codon tile is not a routine outcome of the tested class-preserving shuffle. They do not, by themselves, supply a biochemical causal explanation.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The central finite row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The window-six local rule has hit rate $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M\setminus T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ after adjoining the boundary layer. The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is reported as the hit rate above baseline. Since $0.9841269841269841=62/63$, the row says that all but one of the $63$ targets are covered once the boundary contribution is allowed, while the stricter $M\setminus T$ part accounts for $59/63$ targets.

The family-level split is also finite. Five families are reported. Their above-baseline hit rates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The only non-unit family rate is therefore the alternative-yeast row, and even there the value is $5/6$. This supports a code-read stability statement: across the evaluated tables, the local window-six construction almost always recovers the relevant label target, and the one family with a visible miss remains well above the baseline row.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out companion row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification partitions the $13$ codons of $R$ without omissions or extras. The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row has $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. Therefore the local codon-tile boundary is not a loose narrative grouping: every codon in the residual set is placed in an explicitly named finite module.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table: a finite table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact tests label readback and local split behavior. It does not observe ribosomal translation, molecular dynamics, folding, admissible physical realization, organismal function, or universality across all biological systems. The correct conclusion is therefore a bounded code-layer one: the window-six coordinates expose a strong local boundary pattern for the curated codon labels, and the leave-one-code-out row shows near-complete target recovery against the stated baseline.

The claim must stop at that level. Amino-acid label sequences are not folded protein structures. A CUN/UUR or tRNA-wording pattern is not structural evidence unless a separate organism-matched contact supplies that evidence. A local codon tile is not a global law of biology. Any later promotion from code readback to translation, structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires an additional contact whose observed data live at that higher layer.
