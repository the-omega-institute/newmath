\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:bioreality-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The claim concerns a finite code-layer experiment on the standard genetic-code table. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary window display of codon positions. The observed biological contact is only the curated codon-to-label table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label. The BEDC-side distinctions used here are the six-bit display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class. The finite question is whether the window-six coordinate geometry identifies a local boundary pattern that helps recover altered code labels under leave-one-code-out testing. It is not a claim about biochemical translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality. Those layers require separate observations and are not supplied by the codon-label table.

&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.

Thus the finite object is not an undifferentiated list of codons: it carries a small local cubical shape, with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-faces in the relevant window-six subconfiguration.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape comparison used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. Exactly one sampled object matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserving amino-acid class reported observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$ among $2000$ trials. Its finite-row probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the narrower statement that the displayed tile is atypical under the recorded null procedures. They do not, by themselves, identify a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The primary leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The local method reached hit rate $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M-T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ inside $M-T$ plus the boundary. The recorded rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$. Equivalently, at the level of finite rows,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{baseline}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M-T)&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M-T+\partial)&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{tables}&=23,\qquad \mathrm{targets}=63.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore a code-read recovery statement: when one code table is withheld, the window-six local tile plus its boundary recovers almost all recorded targets relative to the baseline procedure.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out comparison.}
The companion leave-one-codon-out row gives recovery rate $1.0$, predictable count $8$, boundary count $5$, anchor count $0$, and high closure coverage $8$. These numbers are consistent with the code-level reading above: the thirteen-codon residue set separates into predictable and boundary-supported codons, not into a biochemical account of translation. The absence of anchors in this row is important. The recovery is mediated by the local tile and boundary accounting, not by a separately observed molecular anchor.

\paragraph{Boundary statement.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments and supports finite code-read tests. It does not observe ribosomal realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal law of genetic codes. Therefore the present result should be read as follows: the window-six codon coordinates expose a statistically atypical local residue tile, and that tile has strong finite recovery performance in leave-one-code-out testing across $23$ tables and $63$ targets. No higher-layer biological conclusion follows from this chapter unless an additional contact directly measures that higher layer.
