\paragraph{Finite carrier and contact.}

The contact is used only as a codon-to-label table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label, and no observation of translation dynamics, folded structure, energetic feasibility, phenotype, or biological function is included in this row.  The local object under test is therefore a code-layer tile in $Q_6$, not a global law of molecular biology.

Thus the finite display contains thirteen selected codon vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the recovered tile.  The number $13$ is not interpreted as a biochemical necessity; it is the size of this code-layer subset under the chosen coordinate and closure construction.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The standard-code table supports codon-label readback and local split checks.  It does not supply a ribosome-level contact, a structural assay, a thermodynamic admissibility test, a phenotype measurement, or a mechanism showing universality across biological systems.  Therefore the admissible conclusion is only that window-six codon coordinates expose a finite local boundary-tile pattern whose leave-one-code-out behavior is strong relative to the recorded baselines and null samples.  Amino-acid label readback remains label readback.  A sequence of labels is not a folded protein, a fold is not a function, and none of these higher-layer claims follows from the present contact without a separate observation at that layer.

The packet is aligned at the code-read layer and blocked above it.  Any future promotion beyond codon labels would require new contacts specific to translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, function, or mechanism.  Until such contacts are attached, the claim remains a finite, local, code-table result in the six-bit codon display.
