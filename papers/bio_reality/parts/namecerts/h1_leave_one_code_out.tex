\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.code.out} concerns a code-layer readback experiment on the standard codon table. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary-window display of codons with local face, corner, and three-point label-class distinctions. The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. Thus the claim is deliberately narrow: a window-six coordinate system may expose a local boundary-tile pattern in the codon-label table, but the labels themselves remain contact data supplied by the curated code table rather than by geometry alone.

\paragraph{Finite residue set.}
The distinguished residue set $R$ has cardinality $13$. The observed list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite shape calculation identifies this same set as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. In the local coding analysis, these thirteen codons form the boundary-sensitive rows where the coordinate display and the curated label table are tested against each other. The module classification is complete: all $13$ codons are assigned, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The classified modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},&
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},&
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},&
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has a predictable-or-boundary classification, so the finite row does not leave an unexplained residue inside $R$. This is a local combinatorial statement about the table rows. It is not a claim that these modules are realized as a universal biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The accompanying spectrum row reports

\begin{aligned}

The radial cubic check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, and the same value is recorded for the cubic-polynomial residual. These numbers are used here only as finite diagnostics of the coordinate construction. They do not turn the codon table into a physical model of translation, folding, admissibility, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the reported randomization row with seed $42$, $N=2000$ samples were evaluated after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9} &= 0.0125,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $25$ high-Jaccard matches. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null row, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-match count is $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, and the reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite rows support the presence of a non-generic local pattern in the curated table comparison. The support is empirical and coordinate-specific; it does not assert that the coordinate geometry is a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The observed recovery rates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ above\ baseline} &= 0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in}\ M\setminus T &= 0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in}\ M\setminus T\ \mathrm{plus\ boundary} &= 0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, after one code is held out, the local window-six structure recovers nearly all targets above the baseline row, and the boundary extension accounts for the remaining gain. The family-level row contains $5$ families: bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline, while alternative-yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out comparison.}
The companion leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The value recorded for recovery above threshold is also $1.0$. This comparison is relevant because it separates row recovery from an anchor explanation: the observed behavior is carried by predictable and boundary classifications rather than by fixed anchor codons.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used in this chapter is the curated standard-code label table. That contact can test codon-label readback and a local codon-tile label split. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Consequently, amino-acid label readback remains a code-level observation. A sequence of labels is not a protein fold, and a local split in $Q_6$ is not evidence for function unless a separate experiment supplies that higher-layer contact.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following restricted conclusion. In the standard-code table, the six-window codon coordinates isolate a thirteen-codon boundary-sensitive set with punctured-cube shape, complete module assignment, strong leave-one-code-out recovery across $23$ tables and $63$ targets, and low randomization frequencies in the reported null rows. The claim is therefore ready as a code-read statement. It must not be promoted to translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, global mechanism, or universality without additional contact data designed for those layers.
