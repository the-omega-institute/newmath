\subsection{Leave-one-code-out readback on the window-six codon cube}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Question.}
The claim tested here is that a six-coordinate codon display can expose a stable local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table without turning that pattern into a general biological principle. The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read at the code-label layer: each codon is a point of the cube, and the observed table assigns that point an amino-acid or stop label. The operative distinction is between a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The object under test is therefore not translation itself, not protein formation, and not organismal function; it is the finite codon-to-label table, used as a curated contact for code readback.

\paragraph{Distinguished codon set.}
The run isolates a thirteen-codon set $R$ in the standard-code table. In lexical codon notation this set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|R|=13$. The same run records that $R$ has the punctured-cube face profile
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local object is not merely a named codon list; it carries a finite face-incidence shape in the six-bit display. This is the geometric part of the evidence. The biological labels are supplied separately by the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The associated finite operator data give three numerical scales:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the radial polynomial value, and the spectral radius check is within tolerance at $0.6752479308830213$. These numbers support the use of the finite window-six geometry as a reproducible coordinate calculation. They do not, by themselves, identify a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape against shuffled code labels.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null model again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. In that restricted null, the exact shape count was $0$, the observed $M$-size was $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$. Thus the exact-shape probability was recorded as $0.0$, while the lower-tail probability was $0.0035$. The evidence is consequently finite and table-specific: the local tile is uncommon under the tested label-preserving reshufflings, but the result is still an observation about code labels.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. Against that baseline, the hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level breakdown used five families. The above-baseline family hit rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. This is the strongest positive part of the row: omitted code-label targets are usually recovered by the finite local pattern plus its boundary allowance.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out and module structure.}
The codon-level omission run found no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and $8$ predictable codons. The recovery rate was $1.0$, and the thresholded recovery-rate check was also $1.0$. The thirteen codons of $R$ were partitioned into six named modules with no missing or extra codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. Every module contains a predictable or boundary component. This supplies a concrete finite explanation of why the leave-one tests have high recovery: the omitted targets are not scattered arbitrary points; they sit inside a small module decomposition of the same local codon region.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table. It observes that codons have amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code. It does not observe ribosomal translation as a process, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Therefore the supported statement is deliberately narrow: window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern that is highly recoverable in finite leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out tests. The data do not license any claim that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological necessity, nor that an amino-acid label sequence is a folded protein. Any step from code-label readback to translation, fold, admissible structure, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.
