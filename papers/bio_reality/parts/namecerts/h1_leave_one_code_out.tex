\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery in the window-six codon cube}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record studies a code-layer phenomenon inside the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is read as a six-bit window after fixing the usual two-bit nucleotide display, and the only external table used here is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The object under test is therefore not translation as a cellular process.  It is the finite readback problem: after one code table is withheld from the comparison class, how often does the local window-six geometry still place the withheld label-change target inside the expected codon region or on its boundary?

\paragraph{Observed punctured region.}
The finite region $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports $R$ as a punctured-cube shape with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the empirical code-layer region is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it has a finite cubical incidence profile.  The module decomposition is complete: the $13$ codons are all assigned, with no missing or extra module codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every displayed module contains a predictable member or a boundary member, so the leave-one-code-out rows do not depend on an unassigned residue of the codon set.

The associated finite operator row gives

These numbers are used only as finite diagnostics of the codon-window construction; they are not promoted into a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The direct held-out-code experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ withheld targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  Against that baseline, the geometry recovered targets at rate
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit\_rate\_above\_baseline}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit\_rate\_in\_M\setminus T}&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit\_rate\_in\_M\setminus T\ plus\ boundary}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
In words, the local tile and its boundary nearly exhaust the observed held-out targets in this finite table ensemble.  The claim is deliberately narrow: it says that the code-label readback has a strong local boundary signature in the window-six coordinate system.

\paragraph{Family stratification.}
The family-level row contains $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast}&=0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower alternative-yeast value is still far above the global baseline row, while the other four families are perfect on the finite targets supplied to this packet.  This stratification matters because the table-level rate is not hiding a collapse in the named biological-code families represented in the finite sample.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out companion row.}
The companion codon-level recovery row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, $8$ high-coverage closure entries, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This row is not identical to the leave-one-code-out claim, but it supports the same local reading: the informative events occur inside the closure-and-boundary organization of the finite codon tile rather than through a free global law over all possible biological claims.

\paragraph{Null rows.}
Two finite null comparisons calibrate the strength of the shape.  In the shape-significance run, $N=2000$ samples were drawn with seed $42$.  There were $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly $1$ exact shape match was observed, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null row, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, and the reported rates were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These rows say that the finite shape is uncommon under the sampled alternatives used here.  They do not say that nature must realize the geometry in every organism or under every genetic-code convention.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary.}
The only external contact used in this subsection is the curated standard-code label table.  That contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and allows code-read comparisons.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, folding, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, evolutionary mechanism, or universality.  Consequently, the result may be stated as follows: window-six codon coordinates expose a strong local boundary-tile signature for held-out code-label targets in the supplied finite rows.  No claim about protein realization, folded structure, physical feasibility, biological function, general mechanism, or universal law follows unless a separate contact records that higher layer.
