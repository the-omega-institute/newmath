\paragraph{Question and finite coordinate setting.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object, represented on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, the local tile face, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard genetic-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and the computation asks whether the window-six coordinate presentation exposes a stable local boundary pattern in those labels.  The claim is not that the coordinate geometry is a biological law.  It is a finite readback test over code labels, with the table treated as the contact point and the geometry treated as the organizing coordinate system.

The leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The evidence is consequently appropriate for a local code-read claim: within the supplied finite data, the six-bit window and the curated labels expose a sharply structured boundary region.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The scientific scope is restricted.  The contact table observes codon-to-label assignments only.  From these rows one may say that the standard-code label table, when read through the window-six coordinate system, exhibits a finite local tile boundary with strong leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out recovery.  One may not infer that DNA or RNA translation is generated by the coordinate geometry, that an amino-acid label sequence has folded structure, that a protein is physically admissible, that any biological function is realized, that the construction supplies a mechanism, or that the pattern is universal.  Those stronger statements require separate contacts of the appropriate kind.  In particular, folded-structure evidence would have to come from an independent structural observation or structure-prediction contact, not from the codon-label table.  Cross-organism wording, CUN/UUR notation, and tRNA-copy discussion also remain separate contact routes unless they are tied to organism-matched observations.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite record supports the following bounded conclusion.  In the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, the curated standard-code label table determines a thirteen-codon residual boundary $R$ with face vector $(13,16,4)$, complete module assignment, nontrivial spectral values, and empirical null-model separation.  The leave-one-code-out test over $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ targets reaches hit rate $0.9841269841269841$ above a $0.19821739130434785$ baseline, while the leave-one-codon-out row gives full recovery in the supplied finite test.  The record is ready as a code-layer local-boundary claim and remains blocked for translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and universality until separate external contacts are supplied.
