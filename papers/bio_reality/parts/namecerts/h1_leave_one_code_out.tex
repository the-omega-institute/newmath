\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback over the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed boundary row.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through the finite window-six codon display.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes used only as finite coordinate distinctions.  The reality contact is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  In this chapter that contact is allowed to test code-layer readback and local label splitting; it is not treated as a mechanism for translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or universality.

The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is also certified as the punctured-cube row with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite object on which the leave-one-code-out probe is evaluated.  It is a codon-label boundary object, not a claim that the geometry of $Q_6$ governs biological realization.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape measurements.}
The associated spectral values separate the boundary row from an arbitrary label listing.  The verified run reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic or radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, so the recorded spectral relation is numerically closed at the stated tolerance.  The finite-shape test used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It observed one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ matches at Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling process required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which should be read as evidence that the tested finite shape is scarce under the chosen null generator, not as evidence for a biochemical law.

The amino-acid-class-preserving null model gives a second control.  In $N=2000$ samples with the same seed, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  Thus the recorded exact-shape probability is $0.0$, while the lower-tail probability is $0.0035$.  This control preserves label-class information more strongly than an unconstrained shuffle, so the persistence of a small lower-tail probability supports the claim that the local coordinate split is not a trivial consequence of class sizes alone.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas the hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary allowance was again $0.9841269841269841$.  These values are the central finite-row support for the chapter: when a code table is held out, the local codon-tile coordinates recover nearly all targets above the naive baseline, with the remaining distinction handled by the boundary part of the row.

The family breakdown localizes this behavior rather than hiding it in a pooled statistic.  Five families are recorded.  The hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  The alternative-yeast value is the visible weak point in the family table, but it still lies far above the global baseline $0.19821739130434785$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out and module classification.}
The codon-level companion probe records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, no anchor codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The high-coverage closure count is $8$, matching the predictable count.  The module table accounts for all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing or extra codons: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, the unassigned set is empty, the missing-from-modules set is empty, and the extra-module-codons set is empty.

The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has either predictable or boundary status in the codon-level probe.  This is why the row is useful for the leave-one-code-out question: the recovery does not depend on an unclassified residue of codons outside the packet.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and boundary of interpretation.}
The finite evidence supports a code-read claim: window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile structure in the curated standard-code table, and this structure predicts held-out code-label targets at a rate far above baseline.  The supported statement is deliberately narrow.  It says that codon labels, local tiles, the row $R$, the observed spectral values, the shape tests, and the leave-one-out recovery rates cohere at the code-read layer.

Nothing in these rows supplies a translation experiment, a folded-structure observation, a physical-admissibility assay, a functional phenotype, a mechanism of molecular implementation, or a universal biological law.  Amino-acid label readback remains label readback.  The codon table can witness how labels sit on the finite coordinate carrier, but it does not observe protein structure or biological function.  Any future claim crossing that layer boundary must introduce a separate contact that actually measures the higher-layer object.
