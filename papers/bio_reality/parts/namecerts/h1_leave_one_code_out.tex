\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim packet.}
The claim identified by \texttt{h1.leave.one.code.out} concerns a code-layer readback experiment on the six-bit codon display $Q_6$.  The carrier is the finite codon cube with standard-code labels attached by a curated codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop table.  The internal distinctions used in the packet are coordinate position, closure membership, spectral separation, and relation structure.  The biological object is therefore not translation itself, and not a protein object, but the standard-code codon table viewed as a finite labelled readback surface.  The tested question is whether a local window-six boundary tile remains predictive when one code table is withheld from the comparison set.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and exceptional set.}
The recovered exceptional row set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row is verified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the local tile against which code-label perturbations are measured.  The row is not asserted to be a law of biological sequence formation; it is a finite coordinate pattern in the codon display whose labels are supplied by the curated standard-code contact.

\paragraph{Spectral profile.}
The finite row also carries a numerical spectral readout.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, recorded in the same run as the cubic polynomial residual.  Thus the finite computation treats the observed local row as lying within numerical tolerance of its stated spectral relation.  The number $\lambda_M$ is attached to the larger selected shape $M$, while $\lambda_R$ is attached to the $13$-codon exceptional row $R$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the sampled coordinate null, the run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only $1$ exact shape match was observed, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
For high-overlap comparison, $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null preserving amino-acid class evaluated the same observed selected-size datum $|M|=20$.  In $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$, it produced no exact shape matches and only $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite numbers support the narrower statement that the coordinate-and-label pattern is not typical under the two sampled null comparisons used here.  They do not identify a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total target rows.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary allowance was $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value $0.9841269841269841$ is recorded for the hit rate above baseline.  Thus the withheld-code experiment is sharply above the baseline finite-row rate while remaining a code-read experiment: it predicts membership in the codon-label tile, not translation dynamics.

The alternative-yeast family is therefore the only listed family below perfect recovery in this finite packet, while the other four listed families are fully recovered by the recorded criterion.

Each listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the finite leave-one-codon-out accounting.  In that companion row, the anchor count is $0$, the boundary count is $5$, the predictable count is $8$, the high-coverage closure count is $8$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact has the resolution of codon-label readback.  It can test the code-read layer and the local split of a codon tile.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or biological function.  Consequently, the chapter records a finite code-layer alignment: the standard-code labels interact strongly with a window-six local tile under the stated leave-one-code-out probes.  No claim follows about protein folding, amino-acid-chain physical admissibility, organism-level function, a universal biological mechanism, or a necessary genetic-code law.  Any movement into those layers would require a separate observation with the corresponding biological resolution.
