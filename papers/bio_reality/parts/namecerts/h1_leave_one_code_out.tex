\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery in the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This BioReality record concerns the standard-code codon table only at the label-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the four nucleotide choices across three codon positions. The experiment isolates a local tile whose boundary set is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cardinality check gives $|R|=13$. The same finite row identifies this set as a punctured-cube boundary with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object studied here is not a whole-genome or whole-translation mechanism. It is a small coordinate tile inside the codon-label table, with a singleton-corner pattern and a three-point label-class pattern visible in the code readback.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The finite spectral row separates the measured tile from an arbitrary list of codons by recording the values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is numerically zero at the precision of the run. The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled comparison objects with seed $42$. It found exactly one exact shape match, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and found $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampling log also records $57377$ distinct sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. These values matter because the claim is finite and combinatorial: the tile is being compared against constrained finite alternatives, not against an informal visual impression.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null comparison.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class data and again runs $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$. In that comparison the observed size of $M$ is $20$. The exact-shape count is $0$, so the recorded exact-shape rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This row does not make the label table biological law. It says that, under a class-preserving finite comparison, the observed local shape remains unusually small and structured.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The main recovery row evaluates $23$ nonstandard or alternative code tables and $63$ held-out targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. When the local tile information is used, the hit rate above baseline is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$ and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. In plain terms, almost all held-out codon-label changes in the evaluated code tables land inside the finite local neighborhood or its recorded boundary, rather than being spread as baseline guessing would suggest.

\paragraph{Family-level row.}
The by-family row splits the same leave-one-code-out result into five code families. The family hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The weakest listed family is still well above the baseline rate of $0.19821739130434785$. This supports a narrow statement: the local codon tile is a strong predictor for the particular leave-one-code-out label task represented by these finite rows.

\paragraph{Codon-level module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, no anchors, and recovery rate $1.0$. Its module table accounts for all thirteen members of $R$ without extras or omissions. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row records $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing members, and no unassigned codons. This is the strongest finite bookkeeping point in the packet: every codon in the local boundary has a module placement, and every module has either a predictable member or a boundary member.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table: a codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label. That contact is sufficient for code-layer label readback and for testing local codon-tile label splits. It is not a measurement of translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universal biological necessity. A sequence of amino-acid labels is still only a label sequence at this layer. Any claim about folding, structure, chemistry, function, organismal mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that directly observes that higher layer.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For claim $h1.leave.one.code.out$, the finite evidence supports the following bounded conclusion. In the window-six codon cube, the thirteen-codon punctured boundary $R$ and its associated local neighborhood explain almost all held-out code-label targets in the evaluated leave-one-code-out task: $63$ targets across $23$ tables give hit rate $0.9841269841269841$ above baseline, with family rates ranging from $0.8333333333333334$ to $1.0$. The spectral, shape, null-model, and module-accounting rows make the observation sharper, because they show that the tile is small, structured, numerically stable, and completely assigned within the reported module partition. The result remains deliberately local: it is evidence about codon-label readback in a finite table, not about protein realization or biological law.
