\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback for a Local Codon Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite display.}
The packet studies the standard-code codon table through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The coordinates are not treated as a biochemical mechanism.  They are a finite display in which codons can be compared by local faces, label corners, and small label classes.  The biological contact is the curated standard genetic-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and it therefore supports only label readback at the code layer.  In this row the tested claim is that window-six codon coordinates expose a stable local boundary pattern when one code table is left out, while the labels themselves remain supplied by the curated table.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The reported boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records this set as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object is not merely a list of exceptional codons.  It has a local cubical footprint: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces remain visible in the selected boundary region.  This is the finite geometric content of the readback claim.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The coordinate calculation also reports the spectral values

The recorded radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same number appearing as the cubic-polynomial residual.  At the precision of the finite run, this places the spectral summary inside numerical tolerance.  The spectral row should be read as a coordinate certificate for the local pattern, not as evidence that molecular translation or physical selection has been derived from the cube.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite comparison run for the $M$-shape used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only $1$ exact shape match occurred, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These figures say that the observed local shape is rare under the stated finite comparison procedure.  They do not say that codon biology is governed by the coordinate system.  They support the narrower claim that the window-six presentation has nontrivial finite-row structure aligned with the curated label table.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class while testing the local pattern.  In that run $N=2000$, seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The recorded probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the code-layer reading: even after preserving amino-acid class information, the finite tile statistic is not behaving like an ordinary random relabeling outcome in this run.  The result remains a statement about finite codon-label readback, not about folded structure or functional realization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out test.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Equivalently, the reported finite rows satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{baseline} &= 0.19821739130434785,\\
\mathrm{above\ baseline} &= 0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{inside\ }M\setminus T &= 0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{inside\ }(M\setminus T)\cup\partial &= 0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The main readback point is therefore sharp: when one code table is withheld, the local coordinate-and-boundary description recovers nearly all of the target behavior measured by this row, far above the baseline rate.

It still does not justify promotion to a universal biological law, because the contact being used is a curated code table rather than an independent mechanism, translation assay, structure observation, or fitness measurement.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  In module form, all $13$ codons of $R$ are assigned with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The complete module allocation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

$$
This module table is the most concrete form of the local claim.  The boundary set is not an amorphous exception class: every codon in $R$ is placed into one of six named modules, and the finite accounting closes exactly at $13$ assigned codons.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The data support a code-layer statement: in the six-coordinate codon display, the curated standard-code label table aligns with a rare local boundary-tile pattern, and the leave-one-code-out probe recovers target labels at rates near $0.98$ against a baseline near $0.20$.  The $R$ set, its punctured-cube $f$-vector, the spectral residual, the null-model probabilities, the family hit rates, and the module allocation are mutually consistent finite-row evidence for that statement.

\paragraph{Local stopping rule.}
No stronger biological conclusion is licensed by this contact.  The curated code table does not observe ribosomal translation, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility, biological function, evolutionary mechanism, or universality.  Amino-acid label sequences are still label sequences.  A later claim about translation, folding, function, physical constraints, or global biological law would require a separate contact that directly measures that layer.  Until such a contact is supplied, the present chapter stops at local codon-label readback in $Q_6$.
