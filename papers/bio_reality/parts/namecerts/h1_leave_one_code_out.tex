\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Codon Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Codon-window carrier.}
This record concerns a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$ used only as a finite coordinate carrier for code-layer readback.  Each codon is treated as a point in the window-six cube, and the observed contact is the curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The construction separates four local distinctions: the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  These distinctions are enough to test whether a local boundary tile helps recover changed code labels after one code table is withheld, but they are not a biological mechanism.  The row is therefore a code-read statement: it reads codon labels against the curated table and does not assert translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or universality.

\paragraph{Relation support.}
The finite relation singled out by the standard-code window has cardinality $13$.  In the verified row, the relation is exactly
$R=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
The same thirteen codons are reported as the punctured-cube relation, with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the local carrier is not being adjusted after seeing a prediction target: it has a fixed finite footprint before the leave-one-code-out readback is evaluated.  The module annotation is also complete on this relation.  The six named modules cover all thirteen relation codons with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons: $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  This is the local boundary object used by the prediction row.

\begin{aligned}

The shape comparison against $2000$ sampled alternatives found one exact shape match after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts with $55377$ duplicate retries, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap threshold found $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model with $2000$ samples, the observed set size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These numbers support that the displayed local shape is not a typical sample artifact under the tested null families.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.}
The primary finite test withholds one code table at a time and asks whether the remaining local codon-tile information recovers the held-out target at the code-label layer.  The row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The local rule reached hit rate $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M\setminus T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ after adjoining the boundary contribution.  The same $0.9841269841269841$ value is recorded as the hit rate above baseline:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{tables}&=23,\\
\mathrm{targets}&=63,\\
\mathrm{baseline}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T)&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T+\mathrm{boundary})&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The family-level breakdown is consistent with the same interpretation.  Across five families, the above-baseline hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes.  This is strong evidence for a local code-label regularity in the curated table family, not evidence for any higher biological layer.

\paragraph{Codon-withholding cross-check.}
A separate leave-one-codon-out row gives a useful sanity check on the same boundary.  It reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The recovery rate above threshold is also $1.0$.  This means the local boundary contribution is not merely a table-level artifact: when individual codons are withheld, the row still recovers every predictable target available to this finite test.  The result remains a codon-label recovery statement, since the only external contact is the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The allowed conclusion is narrow.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a finite local tile whose boundary improves code-label readback under leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out tests.  The data do not show that DNA follows this geometry as a law of biology.  They do not show translation machinery, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, evolutionary mechanism, or universal necessity.  Amino-acid labels are not protein structures, and a codon-to-label table is not an observation of folding or function.  Any future structural, mechanistic, or functional claim must bring its own contact at that layer; it cannot be imported from this code-read table.
