\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Stability of the Codon Boundary Tile}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim site.}
This record tests the claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$ at the code-read layer of the standard genetic-code table. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is read as a six-bit display over the four nucleotide letters. The distinctions used in the test are a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The contact therefore tests codon-label readback and local label splitting, not translation in a cell, not folded protein structure, not physical admissibility, not biological function, not a mechanism, and not a universal law.

\paragraph{Finite boundary set.}
The boundary set isolated in the finite run has cardinality $13$. The codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this set as a punctured-cube boundary pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the object being tested is not an arbitrary verbal grouping of codons: it is a finite subcomplex of the six-coordinate codon cube with $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ two-faces. This is the row-level geometric content behind the local tile claim.

\paragraph{Spectral summary.}
The numerical spectrum of the associated finite structure is also fixed in the row data. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial check gives residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is numerically zero at the scale of the computation. The spectrum is therefore used only as a finite consistency signature for the codon-tile record. It is not interpreted as a biochemical energy, folding score, kinetic parameter, or evolutionary mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape significance against randomized label tables.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$. Among those samples, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite count is not being reported as an asymptotic formula. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, the observed module size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$ among $2000$ samples, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These two null checks support the statement that the observed local shape is uncommon under the tested finite randomizations, while leaving all higher biological interpretation outside the present contact.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary layer was $0.9841269841269841$. Thus the finite row says that, when one code table is withheld, almost all tested targets are recovered by the local code-read structure plus its boundary allowance. The result is a stability statement about label-table prediction, not a statement that the geometry causes codon assignment in living systems.

\paragraph{Family breakdown.}
The family-level row groups the $23$ evaluated tables into $5$ families. The above-baseline hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate-other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables. This breakdown is important because it prevents the aggregate rate from hiding a single-family effect. The weakest listed family, alternative yeast, is still above the baseline rate, while four of the five family classes record complete above-baseline recovery in the finite run.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out and module coverage.}
The companion codon-level run has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high-coverage closure count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. Its module classification assigns all $13$ boundary codons with no extras, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons. The six modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. Every module has predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification. This supplies a finite internal partition of the $13$-codon boundary set and explains why the leave-one-code-out statistic is read as a local boundary-tile phenomenon rather than as an undifferentiated global match.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The claim is ready at the code-read layer. The evidence consists of a concrete $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary, a stable spectral signature, two finite null comparisons, a $23$-table leave-one-code-out test over $63$ targets, family-level recovery rates, and a complete module assignment of the boundary codons. The result may be stated as follows: window-six codon coordinates expose a robust local boundary-tile pattern in curated codon-label data. The statement stops there. No translation process, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, or universal biological necessity follows from this chapter unless another independent contact is supplied for that higher layer.
