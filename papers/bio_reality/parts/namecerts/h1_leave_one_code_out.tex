\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Boundary Readback}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim tested here is a code-layer statement about the standard-code codon table in a six-bit codon cube $Q_6$. The row data say that the window-six coordinate system exposes a finite local boundary tile, but the amino-acid and stop labels used to read that tile are curated contact data. The biological object is therefore not translation, protein structure, physical realizability, or function. It is the standard-code label table restricted to codon-label readback.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row identifies $R$ as a punctured cubical pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object under test is not an arbitrary codon subset: it is a finite $13$-vertex local tile with $16$ edge incidences and $4$ square faces inside the six-bit display. The module classification is complete on this set: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, there are no extra module codons, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Module rows.}
The codons in $R$ are partitioned into six named local modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has at least one predictable or boundary member. This matters because the leave-one-code-out readback is not only a global hit-rate number; it is supported by a finite module accounting with no residual codons outside the declared classes.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectral data separate the larger local shape $M$ from the boundary set $R$. The verified scalars are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is numerically at zero at the recorded tolerance. The finite-row role of these values is descriptive: they record that the local codon tile carries a reproducible spectral signature. They do not by themselves turn the tile into a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the recorded null sample, the exact local shape appeared $1$ time in $2000$ draws, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold produced $25$ matches in $2000$ draws, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row also records $57377$ distinct sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries with seed $42$. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, the exact-shape count was $0$ in $2000$ draws, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed $M$ size in that row is $20$. These finite counts support the claim that the local tile is not a routine consequence of the tested randomizations.

The direct test for this claim evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ target events. Its baseline hit rate was

\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The observed hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
When boundary members are included, the rate returns to
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the code-table perturbation row says that nearly all held-out code events are recovered by the local $M\setminus T$ plus boundary account, far above the recorded baseline. Numerically, the claim is a finite leave-one-code-out recovery result, not a statement about the biochemical implementation of translation.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out companion row.}

\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The external contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. Its resolution is codon label readback. It can test the code layer and the local codon-tile label split. It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or functional realization. Consequently, an amino-acid label sequence read from the table is still only a label sequence at this stage.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result is aligned with the code-read claim and blocked against larger biological interpretations. The curated table supports codon-to-label readback and the finite split checks above. It supplies no observation of folded structure, no physical admissibility test, no protein-function assay, no translation dynamics, and no universal biological law. Any future claim crossing one of those boundaries must attach a separate contact of the appropriate kind. Without such a contact, the present evidence remains a finite code-layer recovery pattern on $Q_6$ with a distinguished $13$-codon boundary set, a $20$-member local shape row, reproducible spectral scalars, significant null comparisons, and high leave-one-code-out recovery.
