\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim recorded at the code-read layer.}
The finite object under test is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the distinctions used here are the six-bit codon display, the local tile face, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class.  The readback contact is a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  This chapter records a local recovery phenomenon for leave-one-code-out tests: window-six coordinates expose a boundary-tile pattern with strong finite support, while the amino-acid labels themselves remain supplied by the curated table.

\paragraph{Distinguished recovery region.}
The recovery set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same thirteen codons form the reported punctured-cube region, with finite face data $f=(13,16,4)$.  Thus the observed object is not just a label list: it is a finite region of the codon cube with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional tile faces.  The module assignment covers every codon in $R$ and adds no outside codons.  The modules are $agr=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  The module audit reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no missing codons, no unassigned codons, and no extra module codons.

The resulting finite frequencies are $p_{exact}=0.0005$ and $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$, after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact match count is $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-shape frequency is $0.0$, and the lower-tail frequency is $0.0035$.

The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  Against that baseline, the reported hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  Inside $M-T$ the hit rate was $0.9365079365079365$, and inside $M-T$ together with the boundary allowance it returned to $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level split is also finite: five families were recorded. These rows say that the local coordinate-and-boundary test nearly always recovers the relevant table-side target better than the baseline procedure, with the alternative-yeast family carrying the only listed subunit-rate below $1$.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery companion.}
The companion leave-one-codon-out run supplies the local boundary interpretation.  It reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The same run records the recovery rate above threshold as $1.0$.  This separates two finite roles inside the thirteen-codon region: some codons are recovered by the local closure pattern, and the remaining listed support is accounted for by boundary membership rather than by a hidden global biological rule.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope limit.}
The contact used in this chapter is only the curated standard-code table.  It can test codon-label readback and the local split of labels across the codon tile.  It cannot test translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Therefore the finite evidence supports a code-layer statement: the window-six codon cube, together with the curated label table, detects a strong leave-one-code-out recovery pattern concentrated around the thirteen-codon punctured region and its modules.  No claim follows here that DNA is forced by this geometry, that a label sequence is a folded structure, that a codon tile implements translation, or that the pattern is a global biological law.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact measuring that higher layer directly.
