\subsection{Leave-one-code-out stability for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object and scope.} The packet tests a code-layer question about the standard-code codon table. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as the $64$ codon positions obtained from the four RNA letters in three slots. The distinctions used here are a six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The biological contact is only the curated standard-code label table: it assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label. Thus the experiment may evaluate codon-label readback and local boundary behavior, but it does not observe translation chemistry, folding, physical realizability, biological function, mechanism, or universal law.

\paragraph{Exceptional codon row.} The finite row singled out by the coordinate-and-label computation is the $13$-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality check reports $|R|=13$. The same row is also recovered as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite combinatorial substrate on which the leave-one-code-out test is performed. It is not treated as a law of genetic coding; it is treated as a local object whose interaction with curated labels can be measured.

These values support the claim that the local row is not a common artifact of the tested label-preserving randomizations.

\begin{aligned}

The statement is deliberately limited: the computation says that a finite codon-label tile has strong leave-one-code-out localization for the curated tables tested. It does not say that biology must implement this geometry.

\paragraph{Module accounting.} The $13$ codons in $R$ are completely assigned to six modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}

The module audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. Every module has a predictable-or-boundary status. This matters because the leave-one-code-out result is not merely a scalar hit rate; the finite row also partitions cleanly into biologically interpretable recoding modules at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery check.} The companion leave-one-codon-out computation reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The absence of anchors is important: the recovery is not being credited to fixed singleton anchors already placed outside the boundary calculation. The recorded perfect recovery is therefore a local closure-and-boundary fact about the codon row, not a claim about downstream protein realization.

\paragraph{Local verdict.} The finite evidence supports the following bounded conclusion. In the window-six codon coordinate system, the row $R$ forms a $13$-codon punctured-cube tile with a stable spectral coordinate, significant rarity under the sampled null comparisons, complete module accounting, and high leave-one-code-out localization across $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ targets. The curated standard-code table supplies the label contact needed to read the row as codon labels. No result in this packet licenses promotion to translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, general mechanism, or universality. Any such stronger statement requires a separate empirical contact at the corresponding biological layer.
