\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery inside the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim tested here is that the standard-code codon table, when read only as a finite codon-to-label table, has a local recovery pattern in the six-coordinate codon cube. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with each RNA codon represented by a six-bit display. The relevant distinctions are the local tile face, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class. The tested layer is only code readback: the observations concern codon labels in a curated standard-code table, not translation chemistry, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed boundary row.}
The finite row singled out by the coordinate and closure analysis is a set $R$ of thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|R|=13$. The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube object with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not being introduced as a biological law. It is a finite combinatorial footprint in the six-coordinate codon display, later compared against the externally curated code-label table.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The associated spectral values separate the larger observed module $M$ from the boundary row $R$. The verified values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check is numerically closed at
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is within the reported tolerance for the spectral-radius computation. A shape-randomization run with $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts observed one exact shape match and twenty-five matches at Jaccard score at least $0.9$. The corresponding finite-row rates were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, recorded observed $|M|=20$, zero exact shape matches, and seven samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size. Its reported rates were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the narrower statement that the finite codon-coordinate object has nontrivial shape behavior under the stated nulls. They do not identify a molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out test.}
The main recovery row evaluates twenty-three code tables and sixty-three target changes. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. Against that baseline, the local window-six predictor has hit rate
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841,
\end{aligned}
$$
above baseline. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ alone is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9365079365079365,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite conclusion is therefore specific: when one code table is held out, the coordinate-local row recovers most observed code-label changes in this finite evaluation. The recovery is a code-label comparison against curated tables; it is not evidence by itself for translation realization, protein folding, physical viability, organismal function, or a universal constraint on biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out support.}
The related leave-one-codon-out check gives a second finite sanity test. It reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. In other words, the codon-level removal test does not require an anchor row in this packet; its observed support comes from boundary and closure behavior. This is consistent with the local-tile reading: the tested object is a finite code-coordinate neighborhood, not a global rule about all biochemical realizations.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The thirteen codons in $R$ are completely assigned to six modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification audit records $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. Each listed module has at least one predictable or boundary codon. This gives the packet a closed finite bookkeeping form: every codon in the boundary row is accounted for by the module partition used by the probe.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table that assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code-layer readback and local codon-tile label splits. It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, folded structure, biological function, or mechanism. The standard-code table is therefore a label bridge, not a biochemical completion of the claim.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The admissible reading is that window-six codon coordinates expose a finite local boundary-tile pattern with strong leave-one-code-out recovery against curated code labels. The inadmissible reading is that this geometry alone explains translation, predicts protein structure, proves physical viability, supplies biological function, or establishes a universal biological law. Any such stronger claim would need a separate external contact at the corresponding layer, such as an organism-matched translation experiment, a structure assay, a physical admissibility test, or a function measurement. Without that added contact, the result remains a code-read packet with a finite coordinate carrier, a thirteen-codon boundary row, spectral and null-model checks, complete module assignment, and high leave-one-code-out recovery on the reported finite table set.
