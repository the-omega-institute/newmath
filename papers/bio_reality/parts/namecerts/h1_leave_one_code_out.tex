\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Codon Tile Readback}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite question.}
The record tests a code-layer question about the standard-code codon table. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of codons with local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The contact therefore supports codon-label readback and local split checks, but it does not by itself observe translation in a cell, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal law. Those stronger layers would require separate observations with their own experimental contact.

\paragraph{Observed residue set.}
The finite residue set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube readout gives the same codon list and has face vector $(13,16,4)$. This is the local combinatorial object used by the leave-one-code-out test. It is not treated as a global biological necessity; it is a finite codon-table pattern whose interpretation is restricted to the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinate.}
The spectral row supplies a numerical coordinate for the same local object. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic check is numerically closed at the recorded tolerance, with radial polynomial value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$. Thus the finite geometry is not only a codon list: it also has a reproducible spectral signature in the six-bit coordinate system. This signature remains a code-table statistic, not a statement about molecular folding or organismal function.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the recorded shape null model, the run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape appeared once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. A high-overlap threshold at Jaccard at least $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed module size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$. The corresponding probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support a non-random local code-table split under the tested finite randomizations. They do not identify a translation mechanism, a physical selection principle, or a folded-structure constraint.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ alternative code tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The observed rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. Restricting to $M-T$ gave $0.9365079365079365$, while adding the boundary region, $M-T$ plus boundary, returned $0.9841269841269841$. In finite-count terms, this means that the local codon-tile coordinate almost always recovers the withheld code-level targets once the boundary part of the tile is admitted. The result is a strong code-read phenomenon: the withheld labels are recovered from local codon-table geometry at a rate far beyond the baseline comparison.

\paragraph{Family split.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out audit contains $5$ families. The hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate tables. The weakest recorded family is therefore alternative yeast, but even there the rate remains well above the baseline rate of $0.19821739130434785$. This family decomposition is important because it prevents the aggregate rate from hiding a single dominant source of success.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out check.}
The codon-level companion row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and $8$ predictable codons. The recovery rate is $1.0$, with the same value for the thresholded recovery entry. This companion row says that the same finite tile is not merely successful when whole code tables are withheld; it also gives complete recovery in the recorded one-codon withholding test. The absence of anchors and the presence of boundary cases keep the interpretation local: the evidence is concentrated in the tile boundary and closure behavior rather than in an asserted biological cause.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$. It reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has a predictable-or-boundary status. This closes the finite accounting for the local code-read object: the codons in $R$ are neither partially classified nor enlarged by stray module entries.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The appropriate conclusion is that window-six codon coordinates expose a stable local boundary-tile structure for curated codon labels. The standard-code table is the reality contact for those labels, and the finite tests show significant shape, spectral, family, module, and recovery behavior at the code layer. The conclusion stops there. No claim is made that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological law, that codon-label readback realizes translation, that an amino-acid label sequence determines a folded protein, that the split proves physical admissibility, or that it entails biological function. Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate contact whose observed fact is at that higher layer.
