\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery in a Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table as a finite code-read object on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The display is not treated as a mechanism for biology; it is a finite coordinate system in which codon labels can be read against a curated standard-code table.  The distinguished codons in the boundary set $R$ are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|R|=13$.  The same row also identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object used here has a concrete local tile shape: thirteen codon vertices, sixteen local edges, and four square faces.  This is the whole geometric carrier used in the present claim; it is not enlarged into a global biological rule.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The spectral row gives a numerical signature for the observed code-read tile.  The measured values are

\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\

$$
The radial cubic check is numerically zero at the recorded tolerance, with polynomial residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$.  In the finite sample comparison using $N=2000$ distinct randomized tables, the exact tile shape occurred once, so the exact-shape probability estimate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold produced $25$ matches at Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so these probabilities should be read as finite empirical counts under the stated sampling procedure rather than as asymptotic laws.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The principal recovery experiment removed whole code tables from the comparison family and asked whether the omitted targets were recovered by the local tile plus its boundary.  The row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  By contrast, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is recorded as the hit rate above baseline.  Equivalently, the finite-row summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{tables}&=23,\\
\mathrm{targets}&=63,\\
\mathrm{baseline}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T)&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T\cup \partial)&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the chapter's central positive claim: for the recorded table family, the window-six local tile and its boundary recover almost all held-out code-read targets, with a much higher rate than the finite baseline.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module row assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ and leaves no missing or extra codons.  The complete module decomposition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded summary is $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Each module has either a predictable element or a boundary role.  A related leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This supports the interpretation that the recovery behavior is organized by the finite module partition rather than by isolated codon coincidences.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null comparison.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null row gives an additional finite control.  In $N=2000$ samples, the exact observed shape appeared $0$ times, so the exact-shape count under this null is $0$ and the reported exact-shape probability is $0.0$.  The observed size of $M$ is $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below that size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This control does not prove a biological mechanism.  It says that, under the stated label-class preserving randomization, the observed local code-read shape and the small observed $M$ size are rare in the finite sample.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of interpretation.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test codon-label readback and local label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function, or universal mechanism.  Consequently the local tile may be used to describe a code-layer recovery phenomenon, but it may not be promoted to a claim that DNA must obey this geometry, that a protein fold follows from the amino-acid label sequence, or that a biological function has been observed.  Any statement crossing those layers would require a separate contact with the relevant phenomenon.  Within the present data, the verified content is finite and narrower: the standard-code label table, displayed on $Q_6$, contains a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary whose module decomposition and leave-one-code-out behavior recover the recorded code-read targets at $0.9841269841269841$ with boundary included, against a baseline of $0.19821739130434785$.
