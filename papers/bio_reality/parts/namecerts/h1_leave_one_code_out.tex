\subsection{Leave-one-code-out readback in the window-six codon cube}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object and contact layer.}
The claim records a code-read phenomenon on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is treated as a six-bit window display carrying a curated standard-code label.  The biological contact used here is therefore narrow: it is a codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop table, not an observation of translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, or a universal biological mechanism.  The internal distinctions are coordinate position, closure in the cube, a spectral summary, and a relation between a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The readback question is whether the finite cube geometry helps locate code-table differences when one code is held out.

$$\begin{aligned}

Thus the observed boundary is not a vague enrichment claim: it is a concrete thirteen-codon subconfiguration with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces in the selected cube representation.

$$\begin{aligned}

The sampler attempted $57377$ distinct draws and retried $55377$ duplicates.  An amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, found no exact match, so its exact-shape frequency is $0.0$. These finite rows support the claim that the local code-read pattern is not a typical draw under the tested null procedures.  They still remain label-table tests, because the contact contains labels and stops but no fold, no energy landscape, no ribosomal realization, and no phenotype.

It does not say that the cube explains why translation exists, why a protein folds, or why a biological system functions.
