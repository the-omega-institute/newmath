\subsection{Leave-one-code-out recovery on the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Object and finite display.}
This record treats the standard genetic-code table only at the codon-label level. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, viewed as a binary display of the $64$ codons. A curated standard-code table supplies the observed labels: each codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label. The mathematical object tested here is therefore not translation, folding, chemistry, cellular implementation, or function. It is the finite question of whether the window-six coordinate geometry isolates a local boundary tile that remains informative when one genetic-code table, or one codon label, is withheld.

\paragraph{Distinguished boundary set.}
The row identifies a $13$-codon boundary set $R$. Its members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit gives $|R|=13$, and the same row reports that this set has the punctured-cube face count
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is not merely a list of codons; it carries a small cubical signature inside $Q_6$. The codons in $R$ are further partitioned into six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit is exact: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons are all empty. Each module has either a predictable member or a boundary member in the codon-withholding test.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The same finite packet records a spectral comparison between the main tile $M$ and the boundary set $R$. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic constraint is numerically saturated to residual
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is also reported as the radial-polynomial value. This places the observed tile in a sharply constrained numerical position rather than in an arbitrary corner of the codon cube.

\paragraph{Null comparison for the tile shape.}
The finite shape comparison used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. Because duplicates were rejected, the sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the accepted alternatives, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null preserved amino-acid class information. In that test the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$ out of $2000$, and only $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below the observed size. The corresponding reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row does not enumerate all $20$ members of $M$; it does, however, give its size, spectral radius, null-tail behavior, and role in the recovery statistics below.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The primary claim is the leave-one-code-out test. Across $23$ evaluated genetic-code tables and $63$ withheld targets, the baseline hit rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the window-six local tile raises the hit rate above baseline to
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
Inside $M\setminus T$ the reported hit rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9365079365079365,
\end{aligned}
$$
and after adding the boundary contribution the rate again reaches
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The family-level split has five families. The hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate. These figures support the finite claim that the local codon tile is useful for label recovery under the table-withholding protocol.

\paragraph{Codon-withholding consistency.}
The companion leave-one-codon-out row reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases. Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is also the reported value above threshold. This is not a separate biological mechanism claim. It is a consistency check that the same finite tile structure remains informative when individual codon labels are withheld.

\paragraph{Local reading of the result.}
The evidence supports a bounded code-layer statement: the window-six coordinate representation exposes a small punctured-cube boundary whose modules organize standard-code label variation with high leave-one-out recovery. The curated standard-code table is the observational contact for codon-to-label assignments; the coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data organize those assignments after the fact into a finite geometric packet.

\paragraph{Scope restriction.}
No conclusion in this record asserts that DNA realizes this geometry as a biological necessity. No conclusion asserts translation dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, evolutionary mechanism, or universality. Amino-acid label readback is still only label readback. Any claim about translation, folding, molecular structure, fitness, or organism-level function would require a separate observation source designed for that layer. In the present row, the code table supplies labels and nothing higher.
