\subsection{Leave-one-code-out readback on the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ equipped with codon display coordinates, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The biological object read from this carrier is only the standard-code codon label table: each codon is read as an amino-acid label or a stop label.  The claim tested here is deliberately narrow.  Window-six coordinates may expose a local boundary-tile pattern in the standard code, and that pattern may transfer to related code tables in a leave-one-code-out task, but the contact remains a label-level comparison.  It is not a claim about translation being realized, a folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Finite codon set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the supplied finite row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records the local cell count by the face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed object is not merely a list of exceptional labels: it is a small two-dimensional punctured-cube tile inside $Q_6$.  The finite module partition is complete on these $13$ codons.  It assigns $\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}$ to the $\mathrm{agr}$ module, $\mathrm{AAA}$ to $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$, $\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}$ to $\mathrm{cun}$, $\mathrm{AUA}$ to $\mathrm{ile\_met}$, $\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}$ to $\mathrm{ssn}$, and $\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}$ to $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$.  The assignment count is $13$, with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

This is used only as a finite coordinate summary for the observed tile.  It does not turn the tile into a law of biology; it says that the particular code-label subset has a stable numerical descriptor in the chosen six-bit geometry.

\paragraph{Shape significance rows.}
Against the first null sample, the observed shape has $1$ exact match among $2000$ trials, after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $25$ samples at Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, the row records $N=2000$, observed $|M|=20$, $0$ exact matches, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed $M$ size.  The corresponding finite estimates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the local statement that the standard-code label pattern is unusually organized relative to the sampled comparison classes.  They do not identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ held-out targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The tile-based readback gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ above\ baseline}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in\ }M\setminus T&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in\ }(M\setminus T)\cup \mathrm{boundary}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
At the family level, five code families are represented.  The recorded rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate-other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate codes.  The result is therefore a strong code-read predictor for the supplied finite panel: almost every held-out table-level target is recovered by the local window-six tile plus its boundary allowance.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out companion row.}
The companion codon-level row has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high-closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This sharpens the interpretation of the code-level row.  The support is not a claim that every codon has an independent anchor.  Rather, the finite evidence says that the local boundary and predictable classes cover the tested leave-one-codon-out obligations in this packet.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact observes codon-to-label assignments and supports code-layer readback.  It does not observe ribosomal translation dynamics, protein folding, structural order, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal function, or a universal generative mechanism.  Consequently, the mathematical conclusion is confined to the finite label table and its local six-coordinate tile.  Any statement about translation, folded structure, physical realizability, function, or universality would require a separate contact designed for that higher layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$, the finite rows support a ready code-read statement.  The standard-code boundary set has $13$ named codons, a punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, a complete six-module classification, nontrivial spectral coordinates, significant sampled-shape behavior, and a leave-one-code-out hit rate $0.9841269841269841$ above a baseline of $0.19821739130434785$ across $63$ targets from $23$ evaluated tables.  The result is a finite, table-level discovery record: the codon cube exposes a local boundary-tile organization useful for label prediction, while all higher biological readings remain outside the evidenced layer.
