\paragraph{Carrier and observable table.}
The carrier for this record is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of codon positions together with a curated standard-code label table.  The observable layer is deliberately narrow: a codon is allowed to carry only its amino-acid or stop label as supplied by the table contact.  The local geometry may organize a tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class, but the labels themselves are not produced by the cube.  They are imported as finite table data and then tested against the coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation structure visible in $Q_6$.

The distinguished relation set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube calculation returns the same $13$ codons and has $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite relation is not treated as a vague enrichment of the codon table; it is a specific $13$-vertex boundary object with $16$ one-dimensional incidences and $4$ two-dimensional faces.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table.  It supplies codon-to-label assignments and supports code-layer readback, local split checks, and boundary accounting.  It does not observe translation as a biochemical event, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently the finite results above justify only a code-read statement: window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern whose recovery statistics are strong for the recorded tables and modules.  No claim about translation, folded structure, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter unless a separate contact supplies that higher-layer observation.
