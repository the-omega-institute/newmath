\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns the claim $\texttt{h1.leave.one.code.out}$ for the standard-code codon table viewed through the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The object under test is not translation itself, nor a protein-structure mechanism, but the code-layer assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The finite display uses six-bit codon coordinates, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  Its readback is the curated standard-code label table, so the only biological contact used here is the codon-to-label table.  The local claim is that the window-six geometry isolates a boundary-tile pattern which remains visible when one code table is withheld and then queried against the remaining code-layer data.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a punctured-cube row, the same set has face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the evidence is not merely a list of codons: it also carries a small cubical incidence signature.  The associated spectral row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with radial residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at the stated tolerance scale.  The finite table therefore gives both a coordinate boundary and a spectral check for the same codon region.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out test.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  In count terms this is $62$ hits out of $63$ targets.  Restricting to $M-T$ gives hit rate $0.9365079365079365$, namely $59$ out of $63$ targets, and adding the boundary returns the rate to $0.9841269841269841$.  Thus the finite row says that the boundary correction accounts for three additional recoveries beyond the $M-T$ part of the display.  The result is code-layer predictive readback: it says that withheld code-label behavior is concentrated near the window-six tile boundary, not that the coordinate system performs translation or explains biological realization.

\paragraph{Family split.}
The same experiment reports $5$ family classes.  The family hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{alternative yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\text{bacterial plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\text{candidate other} &= 1.0,\\
\text{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\text{nuclear ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This family profile matters because it blocks an over-reading of the aggregate $62/63$ count.  Four families are perfect on this finite test, while alternative yeast is lower at $5/6$.  The chapter therefore records a strong but still row-specific code-label regularity, not a universal statement about all possible biological coding systems.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module partition assigns all $13$ boundary codons, with no missing or extra codons: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and both the extra-module and missing-module lists are empty.  The six named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{AGR} &= \{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\},\\
\text{AR-Lys} &= \{\texttt{AAA}\},\\
\text{CUN} &= \{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\},\\
\text{Ile-Met} &= \{\texttt{AUA}\},\\
\text{SSN} &= \{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\},\\
\text{Stop-Trp} &= \{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has a predictable or boundary member in the reported classification.  The companion leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This supports the local reading that the finite tile has a boundary-sensitive organization rather than a purely label-random distribution.

\paragraph{Null checks.}
Against the shape null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-shape count is $1$, giving $p=0.0005$, and the high-Jaccard count at threshold $0.9$ is $25$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The sampling log reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite comparison is operating in a crowded discrete space rather than a continuous approximation.  Against the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-match count is $0$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the lower-tail count is $7$, and the reported lower-tail value is $0.0035$.  These two null rows make the observed boundary shape difficult to dismiss as a generic consequence of preserving label classes alone.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The reality contact is the curated standard genetic-code table, and its resolution is codon-label readback.  It can test whether local codon tiles split labels in the code table; it cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality.  Amino-acid labels are not protein folds, and a local codon tile is not a global biological law.  Any future statement crossing from code labels to translation, structure, physical realization, or function must bring a separate empirical contact at that higher layer.  With the present finite rows, the warranted conclusion is narrower: in the standard-code readback and its leave-one-code-out comparison set, the window-six codon cube exposes a sharply supported local boundary pattern concentrated on the $13$ codons listed above.
