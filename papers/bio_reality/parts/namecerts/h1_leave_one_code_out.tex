\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery on the Window-Six Codon Tile}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This BioReality record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as a codon display with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The claim is not that codon biology is determined by this geometry. The claim is narrower: when curated codon labels are supplied as a reality contact, the window-six coordinates expose a stable local boundary pattern whose leave-one-code-out behavior is sharply above the observed baseline.

\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the local object is not an unspecified codon subset. It is a named thirteen-codon region with sixteen edges and four two-faces in the recorded tile complex.

\end{aligned}

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, understood as a codon-to-label table. It supplies assignments of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. It does not supply translation dynamics, folded protein structures, physical feasibility measurements, organismal function, or a universal mechanism. The data therefore support code-layer readback and local label-split checks only.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The finite row licenses the following statement: in the six-bit codon cube, the thirteen-codon region $R$ has a punctured-cube tile shape, a reproducible spectral row, statistically uncommon shape behavior under the recorded null comparisons, complete module accounting, and leave-one-code-out recovery far above the displayed baseline. It does not license stronger biological promotion. No claim of translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate contact measuring that higher layer is added. Amino-acid label readback remains a code-level observation, not a protein-structure observation.
