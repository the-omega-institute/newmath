\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Stability for the Codon Window}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite claim.}
This record studies the finite claim identified by \texttt{h1.leave.one.code.out}.  The object under inspection is the standard-code codon table, read at the code-label layer only.  The coordinate carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is treated as a finite coordinate word and each coordinate word receives its amino-acid or stop label from a curated standard-code table.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  These distinctions are enough to ask whether a local codon-window geometry exposes a stable boundary pattern, but they are not enough to turn that pattern into a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The single contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  It supplies the finite assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Its resolution is codon label readback.  It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or universality.  Consequently, every numerical statement below is a statement about code-layer label recovery and local boundary behavior, not a statement about proteins as physical objects.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The finite row isolates a distinguished codon set $R$ of cardinality $13$.  The codons in $R$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The companion punctured-cube audit gives the same list and reports the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local set is not merely a cardinality count; it is a concrete finite subcomplex of the codon cube, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the reported window geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum row supplies the numerical shape scale used by this finite audit.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same residual is reported for the cubic relation.  The finite tolerance row therefore treats $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ as the spectral radius scale for the larger module carrier and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$ as the corresponding scale for the boundary set $R$.

\paragraph{Shape rarity.}
The unrestricted shape comparison used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only $1$ sampled alternative matched the exact observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ sampled alternatives with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reported observed module size $|M|=20$, exact match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$.  Its reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\text{-}class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\text{-}class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite comparisons support the claim that the observed local shape is not a common sample under the audited null procedures.  They do not by themselves identify a causal biological principle.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The direct leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against that baseline, the reported hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary allowance was $0.9841269841269841$.  In finite terms, the row says that when one code table is withheld from the comparison, the remaining code-window geometry still recovers almost all targets relative to the baseline criterion, provided the local module-and-boundary description is the permitted readback object.

\paragraph{Family stratification.}
The family-level row separates the same leave-one-code-out behavior into $5$ code families.  The above-baseline hit rates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The alternative-yeast family is the only listed family below perfect recovery, and it remains above the baseline scale.  This is a finite robustness statement across the reported family partition, not a claim that all organisms must implement the same mechanism.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison also reports $1.0$.  This means that, within the audited finite window, individual omitted codons are recovered either by predictable closure behavior or by the declared boundary class.  Since the anchor count is zero, the recovery statement is not being presented as a fixed-anchor interpolation theorem; it is a local closure-and-boundary classification over the finite codon display.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module audit partitions all $13$ codons of $R$ into six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Hence every codon in the boundary set belongs to exactly the audited module inventory, and every module has at least one predictable or boundary-supported representative.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite evidence supports the following narrow reading.  The six-bit codon window exposes a stable local tile-boundary pattern for the standard-code label table, and the leave-one-code-out experiment shows high recovery relative to its baseline across $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ targets.  The spectral, punctured-cube, null-model, family, codon-recovery, and module rows are mutually consistent with that code-layer reading.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
No result in this section asserts that DNA follows this geometry as a biological necessity.  No result here establishes translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organism-level mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Amino-acid label sequences are labels in the curated code table; they are not folded structures.  Any claim about translation realization, protein structure, physical admissibility, function, or universality must be supported by a separate contact that observes that higher layer directly.
