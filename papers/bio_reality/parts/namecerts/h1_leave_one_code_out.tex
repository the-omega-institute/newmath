\subsection{Window-six leave-one-code readback}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The packet treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label table placed on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is represented by a six-bit display, and the only biological contact used here is the curated assignment of each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The construction therefore has a deliberately narrow target: it tests whether a local tile in $Q_6$ organizes code-label changes under leave-one-code comparison.  It does not test translation, molecular realization, folded structure, physical feasibility, organismal function, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Punctured residual row.}
The residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the observed row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is certified as a punctured cube with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the code-read object is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has a finite local face profile inside the codon cube.  This is the geometric object whose label behavior is tested below.

\paragraph{Spectral signature.}
The associated finite operator has spectral values

\begin{aligned}

\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.

$$
The radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same residual is reported for the cubic-polynomial check.  At the numerical tolerance used by the run, this places the row on the intended cubic spectral branch, with spectral radius $0.6752479308830213$ for $M$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite null run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled object matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A looser Jaccard threshold of at least $0.9$ produced $25$ matches, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null run also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It found $0$ exact matches, while $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size of $20$.  The reported values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite code-layer anomaly claim: the row is uncommon under both the broad shape null and the amino-acid-class-preserving null.  They do not identify a causal biological process.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code test.}
The leave-one-code experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against this baseline, the observed hit rates were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T) &= 0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T\;\mathrm{plus\ boundary}) &= 0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit\ above\ baseline} &= 0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The conclusion is that the local codon-tile row has strong leave-one-code readback at the label level.  The improvement from the baseline is a finite-table result: it says that the row predicts nearly all held-out code-label targets once the row and its boundary are used.  It does not say that the six-coordinate geometry generates the genetic code as a biological necessity.

\paragraph{Family decomposition.}
The family-stratified run reports $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline by family were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This family split is important because the total hit rate is not carried by a single undifferentiated aggregate.  The weakest listed family still exceeds the baseline strongly, while four families report complete above-baseline recovery in the finite run.

\paragraph{Codon-level leave-one test.}
The leave-one-codon experiment reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable codons.  Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the threshold comparison also reported as $1.0$.  This row-level statement is compatible with the code-table result: prediction is being read from local closure and boundary placement, not from a claimed biochemical generator.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The $13$ codons of $R$ are completely assigned to six named modules.  The module table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Every module has either predictable or boundary support.  This rules out a bookkeeping explanation in which the finite hit rate is produced by ignoring part of $R$.

\paragraph{Readback and scope.}
The scientific content of this chapter is therefore a finite code-read statement.  The six-coordinate codon display, the punctured residual cube, the spectral row, the null comparisons, the leave-one-code recovery, the family split, and the module classification all point to a stable local tile at the label-table layer.  The only reality contact is the curated standard-code assignment table.  Consequently, amino-acid labels may be read back, and local label splits may be tested, but no conclusion about translation dynamics, folded-protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows.  Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate observation or experiment aimed at that higher layer.
