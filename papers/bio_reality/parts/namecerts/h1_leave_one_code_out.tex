\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery in a Local Codon Tile}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}\label{par:h1-leave-one-code-out-question}
This record tests whether a six-coordinate codon window can recover held-out code-table label changes from a local tile boundary, while keeping the biological reading strictly at the codon-label layer. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, represented by a six-bit display of the three nucleotide positions. The finite distinctions used here are coordinate position, closed local neighborhood, spectral shape, and the relation between singleton label corners and three-point label classes. The biological contact is the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, but it does not observe translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite codon locus.}\label{par:h1-leave-one-code-out-locus}
The exceptional local region $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its observed codons are
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row reports that this region is a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the object under test is not the whole code table and not a claim about cellular translation; it is a finite boundary pattern inside the codon-coordinate cube, read against the curated label table.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}\label{par:h1-leave-one-code-out-spectrum}
The spectral row gives
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also recorded as the radial-polynomial value, so the numerical witness is within the stated tolerance of the computed relation. A random-shape comparison with $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts produced one exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$. The corresponding values are
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null model with the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$ records observed $M$-size $20$, no exact shape matches, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed $M$-size, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
These values support the claim that the local tile is not a typical random row under the tested controls, but they still describe finite codon-label geometry rather than an organism-level law.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}\label{par:h1-leave-one-code-out-result}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables with $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. Against that baseline, the reported hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The stricter local count inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the count inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. In finite-row form,
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{tables}&=23,\\
\mathrm{targets}&=63,\\
h_{\mathrm{baseline}}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
h_{M\setminus T}&=0.9365079365079365,\\
h_{M\setminus T+\partial}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}$$
The chapter-level interpretation is therefore narrow: when a code-table row is held out, the local codon tile plus its boundary recovers almost all of the observed label targets relative to the baseline. The result is a code-read signal, not a demonstration that the genetic code is generated by the coordinate geometry.

The by-family row separates the $23$ evaluated tables into $5$ families. The hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes. The leave-one-codon-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The finite codon modules are complete over the $13$-codon region: assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are

Every listed module has a predictable or boundary role in the recorded classification. This is the main finite reason to keep the result: the local tile is not merely a scalar hit-rate event, since its $13$ codons also partition into named modules with complete coverage.

\paragraph{Reality contact and blocked extensions.}\label{par:h1-leave-one-code-out-boundary}
The only external contact used in this record is the curated standard-code table. That contact supports codon-to-label readback and local split checks. It does not contain a translation experiment, a folding assay, a structural model, a physical-admissibility test, a functional assay, or evidence for a universal biological mechanism. Consequently, an amino-acid label sequence read from the table must remain a label sequence. It is not a folded protein structure, not a protein-function observation, and not a proof that biological systems must obey the six-coordinate tile.

\paragraph{Verdict.}\label{par:h1-leave-one-code-out-verdict}
The finite rows support the claim that the six-coordinate codon display exposes a strong local boundary-tile pattern for leave-one-code-out readback. The decisive numbers are the $13$-codon punctured region with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, the random and class-preserving null values $0.0005$, $0.0125$, $0.0$, and $0.0035$, and the leave-one-code-out hit rate $0.9841269841269841$ over $23$ tables and $63$ targets. The result is ready as a local code-read record with an explicit scope limit: without another empirical contact, it cannot be promoted to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
