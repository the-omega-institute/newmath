\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-code-out}

\paragraph{Finite codon frame.}
The record is a code-layer statement about the standard-code codon table, represented on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  Its carrier is the finite set of codon coordinates together with local face, corner, class, closure, spectral, and relation data.  The observed biological contact is only the curated codon-to-label table: each codon receives an amino-acid label or a stop label.  The experiment asks whether local window-six geometry is informative for leave-one-code-out recovery of reassignment targets, while keeping the label table as the point where the finite construction touches observed code data.

The distinguished relation set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same set is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row is not merely counting reassigned codons; it records a specific finite shape: thirteen codon vertices, sixteen local edges, and four two-dimensional faces after the relevant puncture.  The amino-acid labels remain curated code labels, not inferred protein objects.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The finite spectrum attached to the same construction gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic relation is numerically satisfied to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same value is recorded as the radial-polynomial residual.  Within the supplied tolerance, the spectral radius is $0.6752479308830213$.  This supports the use of the codon-window geometry as a finite diagnostic object: it has a stable numeric signature, and that signature is attached to the same local codon set used by the leave-one-code-out test.

The shape comparison against random alternatives used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It produced one exact shape match, so the exact-shape probability was $p=0.0005$.  It produced $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The sampling also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite search space was tight enough that repeated proposals were substantial.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-shape count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, the observed $M$-size was $20$, and the lower-tail probability was $0.0035$.  These finite numbers support a non-generic local organization, but they do not identify a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The primary leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ alternative code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against that baseline, the reported hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The row therefore says that almost all tested code-table targets fall into the finite geometry after the boundary contribution is allowed.  The stronger reading is not that biology is governed by the cube, but that this cube-based local object is a high-coverage coordinate system for the tested code-label changes.

The family split keeps the same interpretation at a coarser biological grouping.  Five families were counted.  The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate-other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables.  The alternative-yeast family is the only reported family below perfect recovery, and even there the value remains well above the scalar baseline $0.19821739130434785$.

Every module has either a predictable member or a boundary member.  This is the finite reason the leave-one-code-out result is naturally read by modules rather than only by isolated codons.  The related leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Thus the geometry does not depend on fixed anchor codons; its recovery behavior is carried by predictable positions and boundary positions.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The claim supported by this row is narrow and code-level.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile organization around the standard-code label table.  The curated table supplies the codon labels; the finite construction supplies a coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation pattern in which reassignment targets are largely recovered.  The evidence is strongest for code-label readback and local split checking: $23$ tables, $63$ targets, a baseline rate near $0.1982$, a boundary-augmented recovery rate near $0.9841$, a thirteen-codon relation set with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, and module coverage with no unassigned codons.

No conclusion about translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows alone.  A codon-to-label table does not observe ribosomal realization; an amino-acid label sequence does not observe a folded protein; and a local cube pattern does not establish a general biological law.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact at the appropriate layer.  Until such a contact is supplied, the validated content of this record is the finite code-read pattern and its boundary-limited recovery behavior.
