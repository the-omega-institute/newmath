\subsection{Leave-One-Code-Out Readback for the Standard-Code Boundary Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-code-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim packet.}
The claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.code.out}$ records a code-layer test on the standard-code codon table. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a codon display with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The biological contact is only the curated standard-code label table: it assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels, and it does not by itself observe translation chemistry, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. The tested question is therefore deliberately narrow: when one code table is withheld from the finite label comparison, do the remaining code-level coordinates still recover the local boundary pattern better than a baseline rate?

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the packet is not using an unspecified large genome object: the support is a concrete $13$-codon tile inside the $64$-codon display. Its module partition is complete on this support: $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The assignment count is $13$, with $R$-size $13$, no missing module codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

No explicit list of the $20$ members of $M$ is present in this row, so only its size and its tested relationship to the null model are asserted here.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out result.}
The primary leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above that baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, while the rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$ and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. In finite-count terms, the packet is saying that the boundary-aware code-layer readback is almost always above the stated baseline across the tested targets, not that a biological mechanism has been derived from cube geometry.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery and family split.}
The linked leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The family-level split has $5$ families. Their above-baseline hit rates are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, $1.0$ for candidate-other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast. These figures make the local boundary claim sharper: the signal is not a single undifferentiated score, since the codon modules and family classes retain named finite rows with their own rates.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The result supports a code-read statement: the curated standard-code table can be used to check whether a window-six coordinate pattern recovers a local codon-label split. The support condition is that the contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and the finite computation measures recovery against the specified null rows. The observation remains at the level of label readback. It does not license a claim that DNA obeys this geometry as a biological necessity, that codon labels determine protein folding, that the observed tile is a universal law of translation, or that the packet establishes physical or functional realization. Any such stronger conclusion would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind, such as an observed translation experiment, a structure measurement, a physical-admissibility assay, or a function-level biological test.
