\subsection{Cross-organism m6A load and RNA half-life boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel in which coding or transcript-associated RNA rows carry an m6A-site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout.  The positive object is deliberately narrow: it is not a statement that methylation realizes translation, protein output, folding, phenotype, adaptation, or a general biological law.  It is a row-level RNA-stability contact in which the same ledger keeps the coordinate label, organism or dataset scope, half-life readout, abundance or transcription controls when available, and the declared condition under which the interpretation must stop.

For the cross-organism human m6Aconquer run, the finite computation retained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites.  The audit used $1000$ permutations and reported $3$ sign-concordant organism-level or dataset-level components, with runtime $338.238$ seconds.  The numerical content of the row is therefore the bounded assertion
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 15845,\\
N_{\mathrm{sites}} &= 135300,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
C_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers say that the finite m6A-load surface and the half-life surface are not empty and that the predeclared sign check recurs across three retained components.  They do not identify a decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and they do not by themselves supply a perturbation or rescue experiment.

\paragraph{Row semantics.}
Each retained row is read as a finite tuple rather than as an open-ended mechanistic claim.  A typical row contains a transcript or gene key, an m6A-load or m6A-site-count measurement, an RNA half-life measurement, dataset provenance, normalization state, and whatever covariates the source can support.  The admissible interpretation is a bounded relation between an RNA modification coordinate and an RNA half-life coordinate after the row has passed the finite join and permutation checks.  If a row lacks measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control behavior, local-structure control, motif-density control, transcription-rate control, isoform-state control, or same-row translation readout, then the row remains an RNA-stability row and cannot be promoted to an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.

The companion m6A site-load boundary row gives a useful calibration for what a stronger same-study half-life contact can look like.  In that finite audit, $5837$ genes were retained, the static Spearman correlation between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the length-partial Spearman correlation for m6A load versus $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.227683$, the causal Spearman correlation was $0.245804$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  That row was marked as crossing its pre-registered boundary statistic, but its own status statement also confines the result to the external m6A/half-life statistic rather than to function or mechanism.  In formulas, the calibration row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 5837,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{static}} &= -0.388929,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}} &= 0.227683,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}} &= 0.245804,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second row is not imported as proof of the present cross-organism claim; it fixes the local standard of evidence for an RNA half-life boundary with an explicit finite statistic.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
The chapter therefore licenses only the following finite statement.  There is a nonempty, externally joined human m6Aconquer m6A-load and RNA half-life panel with $15845$ genes, $135300$ sites, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant retained components.  This supports a bounded RNA-modification-to-RNA-stability relation at the level of the declared finite rows.  The relation is cross-layer only in the weak sense that an RNA modification coordinate is compared with an RNA half-life readout; it is not yet an RBP bridge, not a translation claim, and not a protein-level claim.

\paragraph{Stopping rule.}
The interpretation must stop if high- and low-load labels are exchangeable after the relevant controls, if the half-life effect disappears under batch or abundance correction, or if the available payload cannot distinguish transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, and dataset normalization.  It must also stop before mechanism whenever measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent, whenever target-RBP loss is unavailable, whenever wild-type rescue is unavailable, or whenever binding-defective and non-target controls are missing.  In particular, an observed RNA half-life association is not evidence for translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality unless a separate layer-matched contact measures that layer on the same finite rows and supplies its own statistic.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
A later RBP-mediated version would require a stricter finite table.  The same row would need a synonymous or m6A-load coordinate, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target control, transcription-rate control, mRNA abundance control, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, isoform state, batch, and a declared break condition.  The positive pattern would have to track occupancy, collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.  Since the current cross-organism m6Aconquer row supplies the m6A-load and half-life boundary but not that perturbation-rescue table, the present chapter records an RNA half-life boundary and refuses the mechanistic upgrade.
