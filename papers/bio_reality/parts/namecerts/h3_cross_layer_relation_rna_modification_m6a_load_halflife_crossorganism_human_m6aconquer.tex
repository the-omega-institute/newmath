\subsection{Human m6A Load and RNA Half-Life as a Bounded Cross-Layer Readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
This record concerns a finite human coding-RNA panel in which m6A site load is joined to an RNA half-life readout before any stronger biological interpretation is allowed. The operative rows are gene- or transcript-keyed records carrying an m6A burden, a half-life estimate, identifier provenance, and the covariates needed to prevent a coordinate label from being silently promoted into a mechanism. In the m6Aconquer-backed run, the finite table contained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites. The declared permutation budget was $1000$ permutations, and the recorded runtime was $338.238$ seconds. The reported cross-organism sign-concordance count was $3$, so the table supports a finite concordance statement about the direction of the m6A-load versus half-life relation, not a universal law about RNA decay.

\paragraph{Measured statistic.}
Let $G$ denote the finite set of retained genes, let $A_g$ be the site-load summary attached to gene $g$, and let $H_g$ be the matched RNA half-life readout. The BioReality statistic used here is only a finite association surface:
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{\mathrm{human}} &= 15845,\\
S_{\mathrm{m6A}} &= 135300,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
C_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The value $C_{\mathrm{sign}}=3$ is read as a bounded directional concordance count across the declared comparison surfaces. It does not say that every organism, cell type, transcript class, or experimental condition obeys the same relation. It also does not identify the molecular executor of any decay difference.

\paragraph{Boundary supplied by an adjacent finite contact.}
A separate m6A half-life boundary run gives a useful calibration for what stronger row-level evidence looks like. In that run, $5837$ genes passed the stated coverage rule, namely $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{STM2457})$ at or above the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes. The static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$. The causal contrast between m6A load and $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$. These numbers show how a finite m6A--half-life boundary can be supported by matched rows and a declared permutation statistic. They do not by themselves supply the decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation, or rescue rows required for an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Licensed interpretation.}
The positive content of this chapter is narrow. A finite human m6A load table, when joined to RNA half-life, licenses a bounded cross-layer readout from an RNA modification coordinate to an RNA-stability measurement. The readout is acceptable only as a table-level statement: higher m6A load and RNA half-life are compared on retained rows under the stated finite sampling and permutation design. The m6Aconquer-backed table contributes the row counts, site counts, and concordance count; the adjacent half-life boundary run contributes a concrete example of a controlled m6A-load statistic with $p=0.000999001$ on $5837$ genes. Together they justify treating m6A load as a finite RNA-stability coordinate worth tracking in the standalone BioReality ledger.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The same evidence does not establish translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or a global biological rule. RNA half-life is an RNA-layer readout. It remains separate from ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, protein state, phenotype, and fitness unless a separate matched contact supplies those measurements. In particular, the present rows do not contain measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target controls. Therefore an RBP-mediated decay mechanism is not claimed here.

\paragraph{Cross-layer gate.}
The gate for a stronger RBP-decay claim would require the same finite windows to carry synonymous coordinate labels, matched translation readout, matched mRNA or transcription controls, RNA half-life, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, isoform state, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, abundance, batch, and a declared break condition. If high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable for half-life after these controls, the coordinate is only a descriptor. If half-life changes without RBP occupancy changing, or if perturbation and rescue do not track the effect, the result remains an RNA-stability association. If structure, motif density, transcription, isoform state, abundance, batch, or a control arm reproduces the pattern, the bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The m6Aconquer-backed finite table is ready for the standalone BioReality paper as a bounded RNA-modification to RNA-half-life record. Its concrete support is the retained scale of $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant comparison surfaces, with the related controlled m6A half-life run providing $5837$ joined genes and a partial permutation value of $0.000999001$. The chapter should be read as a disciplined finite-row RNA-stability statement, not as a mechanism, translation, protein, structure, function, phenotype, or universality claim.
