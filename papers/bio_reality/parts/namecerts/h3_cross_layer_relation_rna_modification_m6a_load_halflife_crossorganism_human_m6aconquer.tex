\subsection{Cross-organism m6A load and RNA half-life as a bounded BioReality record}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined table in which coding or transcript-associated RNA rows carry an m6A-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout. The claim is deliberately narrower than an RNA-decay mechanism. The usable empirical surface is the row-level comparison between m6A site burden and RNA half-life, after the join has been restricted to rows with interpretable gene identifiers and an organism-scoped half-life measurement. In the human m6AConquer-derived run, the retained surface contained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, and the audit used $1000$ label permutations. The observed cross-organism sign check was concordant in $3$ organism-scoped comparisons. The run identifier was \texttt{d7949df4a751}, and the recorded runtime was $338.238$ seconds.

\paragraph{Measured statistic.}
The row object is not a free biological narrative; it is a finite tuple. A row must expose at least a gene or transcript key, an m6A site-load value or derived load coordinate, an RNA half-life readout, and enough provenance to keep organism, identifier mapping, and assay origin distinct. The statistic accepted here is a bounded association or sign-concordance readback between m6A load and half-life over the retained finite rows. In compact notation, the admissible readout has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{m6A/HL}}
  &= \{(g_i, a_i, h_i, o_i, b_i) : 1 \leq i \leq 15845\},\\
S_{\mathrm{site}}
  &= 135300,\\
P_{\mathrm{perm}}
  &= 1000,\\
C_{\mathrm{sign}}
  &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $g_i$ is the retained gene key, $a_i$ is the m6A-load value or its finite-row summary, $h_i$ is the RNA half-life readout, $o_i$ records the organism or dataset stratum, and $b_i$ records the finite batch or provenance stratum available to the joined table. The notation is only a bookkeeping device for the observed rows; it does not turn the coordinate into a molecular cause.

\paragraph{Companion half-life check.}
A separate m6A and half-life boundary run in the same evidence family gives scale and sign orientation for the kind of statistic being admitted. In the GSE163500-derived human m6A/half-life run, $5837$ genes passed the coverage rule $\min(\text{Mean T coverage DMSO},\text{STM2457})$ at or above the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes. The static Spearman coefficient between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$. The causal contrast between m6A-load change and log half-life change had Spearman coefficient $0.245804$, length-partial Spearman coefficient $0.227683$, and two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$. That companion run was explicitly marked as a boundary statistic rather than a mechanism or function claim. It therefore supports the scope discipline used here: m6A-load and half-life rows may cross a finite RNA-stability readout threshold without licensing a claim about how the transcript is destroyed, translated, folded, or used by the cell.

\paragraph{Coordinate distinctions.}
The biological object is a synonymously editable coding-RNA window panel, enriched with coordinate labels, same-row translation readout slots, RNA half-life measurements, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy slots, perturbation slots, rescue slots, non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, mRNA abundance controls, isoform state, motif density, and local-structure covariates. The cross-layer relation can only be considered if high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels remain separated after amino-acid composition control, and if any proposed RNA half-life difference survives controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC content, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch. The present verified m6AConquer row count establishes a finite RNA-stability contact, but it does not by itself fill every perturbation and rescue slot required for an RBP-mediated bridge.

\paragraph{RBP bridge requirement.}
A stronger decay-factor RBP interpretation would require the same finite rows to carry measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a coordinate--occupancy decoupling test, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, a binding-defective rescue or non-target control, and a declared finite statistic tying the occupancy change to the RNA half-life change. The finite m6A-load and half-life table does not satisfy that stronger design merely because it contains many genes and sites. If the half-life association remains but local decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not controlled by perturbation and rescue, the result remains an RNA-stability association. If a binding-defective rescue, non-target control, RNA-structure covariate, motif-density covariate, transcription-rate covariate, isoform effect, abundance effect, or batch term reproduces the pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The same-row translation readout is mandatory as a boundary guard, not as an automatic promotion. RNA half-life is not translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal rule. A row may contain a translation-readout slot so that downstream interpretations can be blocked or conditioned, but the RNA half-life statistic alone cannot be renamed as translation realization. The mRNA abundance control has the same status: it separates transcription-layer signal from translation-layer or stability-layer signal, but it is not itself evidence of translation output or RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Accepted local conclusion.}
The accepted conclusion is therefore finite and bounded. The m6AConquer-derived audit records a large human m6A-load and RNA half-life surface with $15845$ retained genes, $135300$ m6A sites, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant organism-scoped comparisons. Together with the companion GSE163500 half-life boundary statistic, it supports the statement that m6A-load rows can be evaluated against RNA half-life rows as an empirical RNA-stability readout. It does not establish a decay-factor RBP mechanism, a translation mechanism, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, causation, adaptation, or universality. Those claims would require separate layer-matched measurements and controls on the same finite rows.
