\subsection{Human m6A Load and RNA Half-Life as a Bounded Cross-Layer Record}\label{sec:bioreality-human-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of coding-RNA rows in which an m6A site-load coordinate is compared with an RNA half-life readout.  The operative module class is $\mathrm{cross\_layer\_relation}$, but the name is deliberately narrower than a mechanism claim.  Each admissible row has to be read as a tuple containing at least a coordinate label, an RNA half-life value, the relevant transcript or gene key, organism scope, batch or dataset provenance, and enough covariates to separate RNA abundance and transcript identity from the stability readout.  For the human m6Aconquer run, the computed finite surface used $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, with $1000$ actual permutations.  The run identifier was $\mathrm{d7949df4a751}$ and the recorded runtime was $338.238$ seconds.  Its scalar sign summary was
$$\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 15845,\\
  n_{\mathrm{sites}} &= 135300,\\
  n_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
  n_{\mathrm{sign\ concordant}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}$$
These numbers license a finite empirical boundary: the panel contains enough rows to report a reproducible m6A-load/half-life surface, but the row schema does not by itself contain local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue.

\paragraph{Matched comparison.}
The same scientific boundary is sharpened by an independent m6A half-life run on a smaller gene panel.  In that companion finite calculation, $5837$ genes passed the declared coverage rule, the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman association between m6A-load change and $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, and the length-partial Spearman statistic was $0.227683$.  The two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  The coverage predicate was the rule that the minimum of mean T coverage in DMSO and STM2457 had to be at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  In symbols, the comparison supplies a finite statistic of the form
$$\begin{aligned}
  \rho_{\mathrm{static}} &= -0.388929,\\
  \rho_{\mathrm{causal}} &= 0.245804,\\
  \rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}} &= 0.227683,\\
  p_{\mathrm{perm, two\ sided}} &= 0.000999001,\\
  n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 5837.
\end{aligned}$$
The direction change between the static and perturbative summaries is not treated as a contradiction.  It is treated as evidence that the finite readout is sensitive to the exact row construction, perturbation context, and control set.  The relevant mathematical object is therefore not a universal sign law, but a bounded family of row-indexed statistics whose interpretation is pinned to the declared panel.

\paragraph{Licensed statement.}
The licensed claim is that m6A site load can be placed in a finite cross-layer relation with RNA half-life when the row construction explicitly keeps the RNA half-life readout separate from translation, protein output, and mechanism.  The human m6Aconquer surface contributes a large site-level human panel, while the independent m6A half-life calculation contributes a gene-level perturbative comparison with a nonzero partial permutation signal.  Together they support the following restrained assertion: within finite matched rows, an m6A-load coordinate can be tested against RNA half-life after identifier matching and stated controls, and the resulting statistic may be reported as an RNA-stability boundary.  The record is not promoted to a claim about translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general biological law.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
A stronger bridge through a decay-factor RNA-binding protein would require additional columns in the same finite panel.  The missing columns are not cosmetic.  They include measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, a coordinate/occupancy decoupling arm, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or an equivalent non-target control, transcription-rate control, local RNA-structure control, motif-density control, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  Without those columns, the statistic can say that an m6A-load surface and an RNA half-life surface are jointly measurable on finite rows.  It cannot say that a named RBP causes the decay behavior.  Formally, the admissible implication is only
$$\begin{aligned}
  (\mathrm{m6A\ load},\ \mathrm{half\ life},\ \mathrm{controls})
  &\longmapsto \mathrm{bounded\ RNA\ stability\ statistic},\\
  (\mathrm{bounded\ RNA\ stability\ statistic})
  &\not\longmapsto \mathrm{RBP\ mechanism}.
\end{aligned}$$
The second arrow remains blocked until the finite rows contain the occupancy and perturbation evidence themselves.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The same-row translation readout is a boundary condition, not an automatic interpretation.  A row may carry ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency information as a guard against confusing RNA-stability behavior with translation behavior, but an RNA half-life difference alone is not a translation readout.  Matched mRNA abundance is likewise a control surface, not evidence for RNA half-life by itself.  A valid positive bridge would have to show that the RNA half-life effect remains separated from transcription rate, transcript abundance, isoform assignment, local structure, motif density, composition, codon-pair context, and batch, while also being disciplined by the translation-readout boundary.  If high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels become exchangeable for RNA half-life after those controls, the coordinate remains only a finite descriptor.  If RNA half-life differs but measured decay-factor RBP occupancy does not differ, or if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not govern the effect, the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than a mechanistic bridge.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter therefore records a ready finite-row RNA-stability boundary.  The human m6Aconquer calculation supplies $15845$ genes, $135300$ sites, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant organism-level outcomes for the named claim.  The separate m6A half-life calculation supplies $5837$ genes and a partial permutation value of $0.000999001$ for an m6A-load/$\Delta \log_2$ half-life relation under its coverage rule.  These figures are sufficient for a self-contained bounded BioReality statement about m6A load and RNA half-life.  They are not sufficient for translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the required measurements and the declared finite statistic is recomputed on those same rows.
