\subsection{Human m6A Site Load and RNA Half-Life as a Bounded Cross-Organism Readout}\label{sec:bioreality-h3-m6a-load-halflife-human-m6aconquer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite human RNA-modification panel in which coding or transcript-associated RNA rows are read through an m6A site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life outcome.  The verified finite run for the human m6Aconquer-linked packet retained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, and it evaluated the predeclared sign-concordance check with $1000$ permutations.  The run finished in $338.238$ seconds and returned $3$ sign-concordant organism or condition-level components.  Thus the object recorded here is not an unrestricted law about methylation and decay; it is a finite table-level statement about whether m6A load and RNA half-life carry a reproducible directional relation under the available joins.

\paragraph{Observed statistic.}
Let $G$ be the retained gene set, with $|G|=15845$, and let $S$ be the retained m6A-site multiset, with $|S|=135300$.  Each row is interpreted only after the gene or transcript identifier, m6A load, RNA half-life readout, and available organism or condition label have survived the join.  The finite statistic can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
|G| &= 15845,\\
|S| &= 135300,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
N_{\mathrm{concordant}} &= 3,\\
T_{\mathrm{run}} &= 338.238\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The scalar $N_{\mathrm{concordant}}=3$ is the positive finite-row contact for this packet: within the audited rows, the direction of the m6A-load and half-life relation is not merely a single-table fluctuation.  It is still only a bounded RNA-stability readout, because the rows do not by themselves include measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, a target-RBP loss arm, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue controls.

\paragraph{Same-layer comparison.}
The same audit bundle also contains a separate m6A perturbation boundary run, kept here only as a neighboring RNA-stability calibration rather than as evidence for this packet.  That run retained $5837$ genes, reported a static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life of $-0.388929$, a causal Spearman association between m6A-load change and half-life change of $0.245804$, a length-partial Spearman value of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value of $0.000999001$.  Its declared coverage rule was $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{STM2457})$ at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  These numbers help fix the scale of RNA-modification-to-half-life contact in the surrounding finite evidence, but they do not replace the human m6Aconquer row set, and they do not supply an RBP perturbation mechanism for it.

\paragraph{Licensed reading.}
The chapter licenses the following narrow statement.  In the retained human m6Aconquer-linked panel, m6A site load and RNA half-life have a finite cross-row relation with $15845$ joined genes, $135300$ joined sites, and $3$ sign-concordant components under a $1000$-permutation audit.  The biological object is therefore a bounded RNA-stability surface: an RNA-modification coordinate is associated with an RNA half-life readout across the finite retained rows.  The statistic may be used to motivate a more resolved same-window experiment, but it is not already such an experiment.

\paragraph{Refusal boundary.}
No translation claim follows from the half-life relation alone.  A same-row translation readout would have to be present before any statement about translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, or protein output could be made.  No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal sequence law follows from the observed m6A-load and half-life relation.  In particular, the finite concordance count does not identify a decay-factor RBP, does not show that an RBP binds more strongly at high-coordinate windows, and does not prove that loss of the RBP collapses the half-life effect.

\paragraph{Required bridge rows.}
To promote this RNA-stability surface to a decay-factor RBP bridge, the same finite rows would need measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, mRNA abundance controls, isoform-state controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch labels, and a same-row translation boundary.  If high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable for RNA half-life after those controls, the coordinate is only a bounded descriptor.  If RNA half-life differs but measured RBP occupancy is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect, the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP mechanism.  If binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, or batch reproduce the pattern, the bridge claim is refused.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The finite evidence is strong enough for a chapter-level BioReality record because the row counts, site counts, permutation count, runtime, and concordance count are concrete and auditable.  Its correct scope is nevertheless deliberately small: m6A load is connected to RNA half-life in the retained human panel, while translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, and universality remain outside the licensed claim until separate layer-matched contacts supply those readouts.
