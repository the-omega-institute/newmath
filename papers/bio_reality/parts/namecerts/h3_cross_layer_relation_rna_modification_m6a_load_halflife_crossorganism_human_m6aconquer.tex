\subsection{Cross-organism m6A load and RNA half-life boundary}
\label{subsec:h3-cross-layer-relation-rna-modification-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite panel in which coding-RNA windows are compared through synonymous coordinate labels, m6A site load, and RNA half-life readout.  The primary finite run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_load\_halflife\_crossorganism\_human\_m6aconquer} is experiment \texttt{d7949df4a751}.  It retained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, evaluated $1000$ permutations, and produced $3$ sign-concordant organism-level directions in $338.238$ seconds.  The linked same-layer half-life boundary record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500} supplies a stronger human perturbation comparator: experiment \texttt{7cd189f15c4b} retained $5837$ genes, used the coverage rule \emph{min(Mean T coverage DMSO, STM2457) at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes}, and reported static Spearman association $-0.388929$, causal Spearman association $0.245804$, length-partial Spearman association $0.227683$, and two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$.

\paragraph{Statistic actually licensed.}
The row-level claim is deliberately narrow.  Let $G$ denote the retained gene set, let $S$ denote the retained m6A-site table, and let $H$ denote the matched RNA half-life readout.  The finite payload supports only the following kind of statement: within the joined rows, m6A load has a reproducible signed association with RNA half-life after the declared joins and controls have been applied.  In compact notation the run records the scale of the table as
$$
\begin{aligned}
|G| &= 15845,\\
|S| &= 135300,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
N_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The GSE163500-linked comparator gives an independent finite check on the same RNA-stability boundary, not a free promotion to mechanism.  Its reported quantities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
|G_{\mathrm{coverage}}| &= 5837,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{static}} &= -0.388929,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}} &= 0.245804,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}} &= 0.227683,\\
p_{\mathrm{partial\ perm}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are used as finite row facts, not as universal constants.  The positive interpretation is an RNA half-life boundary: m6A load and half-life are not exchangeable in the retained finite tables under the reported analyses.

\paragraph{Coordinate, relation, and trigger.}
The coordinate part is the finite synonymously comparable coding-RNA window label, together with local composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and RNA-structure or motif-density covariates where those rows exist.  The relation part is the signed association between m6A load and RNA half-life.  The trigger part is the finite decision that the association crosses the predeclared RNA-stability boundary in the available rows: $3$ sign-concordant directions in the cross-organism m6Aconquer run, and a perturbation-linked human half-life statistic with $p=0.000999001$ in the GSE163500 comparator.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The chapter does not interpret RNA half-life as translation.  A same-row translation readout is a boundary variable that prevents over-reading the RNA-stability result.  If ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows are absent for the same finite windows, then no translation realization follows from the half-life result.  If such rows are present, they still have to be analyzed as a separate readout with matched mRNA abundance controls before any translation-layer sentence can be written.  RNA half-life can be compatible with translation differences, but compatibility is not measurement.

\paragraph{RBP mechanism boundary.}
The present finite facts do not by themselves measure local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue controls.  Therefore the chapter does not claim an RBP-mediated decay mechanism from the m6Aconquer table alone.  A decay-factor bridge would require same-organism, same-window rows carrying measured RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP perturbation, restoration by wild-type rescue, and refusal of restoration by binding-defective or non-target controls.  Without those rows, the licensed result remains an m6A-load/RNA-half-life association in finite joined data.

\paragraph{Controls and refusal cases.}
The association is not promoted when a simpler explanation remains row-compatible.  In particular, the claim is refused if transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC composition, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, or batch can reproduce the same pattern.  It is also refused if high- and low-coordinate synonymous labels become exchangeable for half-life after these controls are applied.  Likewise, if half-life differs while measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect, the result stays at RNA-stability association scope.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law is inferred from these rows.  The $15845$-gene, $135300$-site run and the $5837$-gene perturbation comparator are sufficient for a bounded RNA half-life relation, but they are not sufficient for protein-level or organism-level conclusions.  Any such conclusion would need its own layer-matched contact, its own finite rows, and its own declared statistic.
