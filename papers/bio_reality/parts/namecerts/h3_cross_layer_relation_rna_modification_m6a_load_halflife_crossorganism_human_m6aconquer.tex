\subsection{Cross-organism m6A load and RNA half-life as a bounded readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record considered here is a finite panel of coding or transcript-linked RNA rows in which an m6A site-load coordinate is joined to an RNA half-life readout.  The specific audited payload for the cross-organism m6AConquer route contains $135300$ m6A sites and $15845$ genes.  The declared computation used $1000$ permutations, ran for $338.238$ seconds, and returned sign concordance in $3$ organism-scoped comparisons.  This is enough to register a finite RNA-stability contact for m6A load, because the rows contain a molecular modification count or site-load coordinate and an independently curated decay-time response.  It is not enough, by itself, to identify a decay-factor RBP or to read the half-life difference as a translation, protein, function, phenotype, or universal-law statement.

\paragraph{Audited statistic.}
Let $G$ be the finite set of joined genes and let $S$ be the finite multiset of m6A sites assigned to those genes.  In this run the cardinalities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
  |G| &= 15845,\\
  |S| &= 135300,\\
  N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
  C_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $C_{\mathrm{sign}}=3$ means that the prespecified cross-organism direction check found three sign-concordant organism-level components.  The statistic is therefore a finite agreement test over joined modification-load and half-life rows, not a kinetic model of mRNA decay.  The point of the statistic is narrow: it asks whether the m6A-load coordinate survives as a reproducible RNA half-life boundary across the available matched organisms after the finite joining and permutation procedure.

\paragraph{Human perturbation comparison.}
A second audited m6A half-life contact supplies a sharper human perturbation boundary.  In the GSE163500 route, $5837$ joined genes passed the stated coverage rule, namely $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})$ at least the $20$th percentile among valid joined genes.  The static m6A-load to DMSO half-life Spearman statistic was $-0.388929$.  The perturbation-side Spearman statistic for m6A load against $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, and the length-partial Spearman statistic was $0.227683$.  The two-sided partial permutation value was
$$
\begin{aligned}
  p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
That comparison shows that the broader cross-organism m6AConquer record is not an isolated numerical artifact: an independent m6A half-life construction also crosses its declared RNA-stability boundary under a finite coverage filter and a permutation test.  The comparison still does not promote the claim into an RBP mechanism, because the required same-window occupancy, loss, rescue, and binding-defective controls are absent from both finite summaries.

\paragraph{Neighboring RNA-stability calibration.}
The endogenous ARE transfer route provides a useful calibration for what a finite RNA-stability contact can and cannot do.  That route used $14933$ genes and $2000$ permutations, with $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$.  Its negative ARE coefficient and nonzero permutation support are another bounded RNA half-life readout, not a protein-output or phenotype claim.  Placing the m6AConquer statistic beside this ARE statistic fixes the intended interpretation: these are finite transcript-stability surfaces, and each higher biological layer requires its own rows and its own statistic.

\paragraph{NameCert form.}
The carrier for this chapter is the finite row tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
  (&\mathrm{gene},\ \mathrm{transcript\ or\ isoform},\ \mathrm{m6A\ load},\ \mathrm{m6A\ site\ count},\
  \mathrm{RNA\ half\mbox{-}life},\\
  &\mathrm{organism},\ \mathrm{batch},\ \mathrm{abundance\ control},\ \mathrm{translation\ boundary\ flag},\
  \mathrm{declared\ null\ condition}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The relation is the finite comparison between higher and lower m6A-load labels after the available joins and controls have been applied.  The trigger is the sign-concordant, permutation-audited survival of the RNA half-life direction across the joined rows.  The break condition is equally explicit: if the load labels become exchangeable for half-life after the declared controls, or if the row set lacks the advertised half-life join, then the coordinate remains only a descriptor of modification load.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The chapter deliberately keeps translation outside the positive conclusion.  A same-row translation readout is part of the admissibility boundary for later cross-layer work, because RNA half-life can influence downstream protein production and can also be confounded by abundance, isoform state, and translation-linked surveillance.  In the present finite summaries, however, the positive data are m6A-load and RNA half-life data.  Ribosome profiling, translation efficiency, and matched mRNA abundance are named as necessary controls or future joins; they are not present here as a completed same-row matrix proving translation realization.  Thus the result may be phrased as an RNA-stability boundary for m6A load, but not as evidence that a coding-window coordinate has produced a translation-layer effect.

\paragraph{RBP mechanism boundary.}
The stronger conjecture asks for a prespecified decay-factor RBP bridge.  That bridge would require measured local RBP occupancy on the same windows, a coordinate--occupancy decoupling test, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  The audited m6AConquer payload does not contain those rows.  Therefore the accepted conclusion is only that the finite m6A-load coordinate has a supported RNA half-life contact.  It does not identify the RBP that carries the decay effect, does not show that RBP occupancy changes with the coordinate, and does not show that loss and rescue of the RBP control the half-life statistic.

\paragraph{Local refusal rule.}
No translation, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, RNA-decay mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the m6A-load half-life statistic alone.  Such a claim would need a separate layer-matched contact: ribosome or translation-efficiency rows for translation, proteomics rows for protein abundance, structural or physical assays for folding and admissibility, perturbation or rescue rows for mechanism, and phenotype or function assays for biological action.  The present chapter stops at the finite RNA half-life boundary licensed by $135300$ m6A sites, $15845$ genes, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant organism-level components, with the human perturbation and endogenous ARE records used only to calibrate the same bounded style of RNA-stability evidence.
