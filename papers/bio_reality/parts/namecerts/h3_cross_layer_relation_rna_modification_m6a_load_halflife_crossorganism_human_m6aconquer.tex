\subsection{Human m6A Load and RNA Half-Life as a Bounded Cross-Layer Readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined table in which coding or transcript-associated RNA rows carry an m6A site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout.  The registered human m6AConquer run contains $135300$ m6A sites joined to $15845$ genes.  The finite audit used $1000$ permutations and completed in $338.238$ seconds.  Its reported scalar outcome is $3$ sign-concordant organism- or panel-level directions for the m6A-load versus half-life relation.  Thus the row object is not a symbolic claim about all methylated RNA; it is a finite measured panel with a declared site count, gene count, permutation count, runtime, and concordance count.

\paragraph{Readout statement.}
For each retained row, the intended record is a tuple containing an m6A-load or coordinate label, an RNA half-life readout, transcript abundance or mRNA-control information where available, and the covariates needed to keep methylation load separate from ordinary expression and transcript-composition effects.  The minimal statistical reading is that a coordinate-associated RNA-stability signal is visible only as a bounded half-life relation.  In notation, the admissible readout has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_i &= (q_i, h_i, z_i, b_i),\\
  q_i &={\rm m6A\ load\ or\ site\ coordinate},\\
  h_i &={\rm RNA\ half\ life\ readout},\\
  z_i &={\rm declared\ controls},\\
  b_i &={\rm batch\ or\ panel\ label}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The m6AConquer finite-row result records that this table has enough measured rows to test directionality across the joined half-life surface, but it does not by itself identify a decay-factor RNA-binding protein, a translation-mediated pathway, or a protein-level consequence.

\paragraph{Numerical boundary.}
The primary verified row set for this subject is

\begin{aligned}
  n_{\rm sites} &= 135300,\\

  n_{\rm perm} &= 1000,\\

These numbers are the finite support of the chapter.  They license the statement that the m6A-load half-life boundary was evaluated on a large human joined table and that the reported direction count was sign-concordant in $3$ evaluated components.  They do not license a quantitative effect-size claim beyond the stored scalars, because this particular finite record does not provide an $R$ list, an $M$ list, a $\lambda$ value, a module class, a hit rate, or a target-RBP perturbation coefficient.

\paragraph{Relation to the m6A half-life calibration run.}
A separate m6A half-life boundary run supplies a useful calibration for the kind of finite statistic meant here: $5837$ genes passed the stated coverage rule, the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log$_2$ half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman association between m6A load and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman association was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  That calibration shows what a declared half-life boundary statistic can look like when effect size and permutation significance are present.  The human m6AConquer record in this subsection is narrower: it contributes the larger joined site/gene panel and the sign-concordance count, while leaving the effect-size and perturbation fields absent for this exact subject.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The allowed conclusion is a bounded cross-layer RNA-stability readout: m6A-load coordinates can be compared against RNA half-life rows in a finite human panel, and the stored audit reports $3$ sign-concordant directions under $1000$ permutations.  The statement remains at the RNA half-life layer unless the same finite rows also carry the additional surfaces required for a stronger bridge.  In particular, a positive RNA half-life association is not a translation readout, not protein abundance, not a folded-structure readout, not physical admissibility, not biological function, not a phenotype, and not a universal sequence law.

\paragraph{Required bridge for a stronger claim.}
A mechanism-level RNA-decay bridge would require same-row measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a target-RBP loss condition, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and a declared coordinate--occupancy decoupling test.  The finite panel would also need transcription-rate controls, mRNA abundance controls, isoform or splice-state tracking, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch labels, and a same-row translation readout used only as a boundary control.  Without those rows, the m6AConquer result remains an RNA half-life association surface rather than an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim is refused as a mechanism claim if the half-life pattern is reproduced by transcription rate, transcript abundance, local structure, motif density, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, or batch.  It is also refused if measured RBP occupancy is absent, if target-RBP loss fails to collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or if binding-defective and non-target controls reproduce the same pattern.  These refusals are part of the mathematical content of the finite record: they keep the observed half-life surface from being promoted into translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality without a separate layer-matched measurement.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite-row evidence is sufficient for a self-contained BioReality entry at bounded RNA-stability scope.  The chapter records a concrete human m6AConquer half-life panel with $135300$ sites, $15845$ genes, $1000$ permutations, $3$ sign-concordant directions, and a $338.238$ second runtime.  It deliberately stops before any claim about translation realization, protein output, RBP mechanism, structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or global biological law.
