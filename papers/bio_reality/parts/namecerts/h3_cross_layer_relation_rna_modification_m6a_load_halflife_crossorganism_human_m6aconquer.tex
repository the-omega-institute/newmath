\subsection{Cross-organism m6A load and RNA half-life as a bounded finite-row relation}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite cross-layer RNA-stability panel whose rows are genes or transcript-associated coding-RNA windows with an m6A site-load readout and an RNA half-life readout.  The verified run for the human m6Aconquer-linked cross-organism packet is \texttt{d7949df4a751}.  It retained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, used $1000$ realized permutations, and finished in $338.238$ seconds.  The finite statistic recorded $3$ sign-concordant organism-level or dataset-level directions.  This is enough to state a bounded RNA-stability relation: m6A load and RNA half-life are not treated as two unrelated annotations, because the checked rows carry a finite joined surface on which their direction can be counted after the declared matching operation.  It is not enough to state that the coding coordinate realizes translation, protein output, folded structure, organismal function, adaptation, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Row semantics.}
Each admissible row is read as a finite tuple containing an m6A burden or site-load coordinate, an RNA half-life quantity, gene or transcript identity, organism or dataset label, and the matching keys needed to compare rows without replacing measured half-life by a symbolic coordinate.  The statistic is therefore a row-counted relation between an RNA modification load and a stability readout.  In this chapter the notation $q$ denotes the m6A-load coordinate, $h$ denotes the RNA half-life readout, and $z$ denotes the finite collection of row controls or matching labels available in the run.  The observed packet is summarized by
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 15845,\\
N_{\mathrm{sites}} &= 135300,\\
B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
C_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are interpreted only inside the finite table that produced them.  They do not license a claim about all transcripts, all organisms, all m6A readers, or all RNA-decay mechanisms.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
The finite assertion is that the joined table supports a nonempty directional m6A-load versus RNA-half-life boundary.  The sign-concordance count $C_{\mathrm{sign}}=3$ records that the direction was not a one-row artifact of a single local join.  The permutation count $B_{\mathrm{perm}}=1000$ records that the boundary was tested by a finite randomization procedure rather than by visual inspection of an ordered list.  The scalar values do not specify a local reader protein, a decay-factor occupancy state, or a perturbation response.  Thus the chapter records a bounded relation between modification load and half-life, not a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Neighboring calibration.}
A separate finite m6A half-life packet, \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, gives a useful scale check without being merged into the present claim.  In that run $5837$ genes passed the coverage rule $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})$ at or above the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  The static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman association between m6A-load change and $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  That neighboring packet is not used as a proof of the cross-organism m6Aconquer result.  It is cited here only to make the local scope precise: m6A load can be attached to finite RNA half-life statistics in more than one curated panel, but each panel keeps its own row set, coverage rule, and null model.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The human m6Aconquer-linked finite result does not by itself contain a matched same-row translation-efficiency matrix.  A translation readout would require ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency rows joined to the same genes or windows, with matched mRNA abundance controls used to separate translational signal from transcript abundance.  Without that additional surface, no RNA half-life difference may be renamed as translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  The present chapter therefore keeps the half-life readout on the RNA-stability side of the boundary.

\paragraph{RBP mechanism boundary.}
The stronger biological object described by the conjecture would require the same finite rows to carry measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  Those rows are not present in the verified cross-organism m6Aconquer fact.  Therefore the observed $15845$-gene and $135300$-site panel may be used as a bounded m6A-load/RNA-half-life relation, while any claim of an RBP-mediated decay bridge remains refused until the occupancy and perturbation rows are supplied in the same finite panel.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter records a finite RNA-stability contact with explicit row counts and permutation depth.  Its positive content is narrow: m6A site load and RNA half-life form a checked finite relation in the verified human m6Aconquer-linked run, with $3$ sign-concordant directions across the declared comparison surface.  Its negative content is equally important: neither codon-coordinate geometry nor m6A load nor RNA half-life alone supplies translation, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.  Those claims require separate layer-matched rows and their own finite statistics.
