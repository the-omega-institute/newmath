\subsection{Cross-organism m6A load and RNA half-life as a bounded RNA-stability relation}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined panel in which coding-RNA or transcript-level rows carry an m6A site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout.  The intended biological object is not a protein, phenotype, or organism-wide law, but a matched RNA-stability surface: each admissible row is read as an RNA-level tuple containing an m6A coordinate load, a half-life measurement, organism or dataset provenance, identifier mapping, and the covariates required to prevent transcript abundance, isoform state, local sequence composition, batch, or length from being mistaken for an m6A-to-stability relation.  The row set used for the cross-organism human m6AConquer contact contains $135300$ m6A sites and $15845$ genes.  Its declared permutation budget is $1000$ permutations, and the run identifier is \texttt{d7949df4a751}.  The finite scalar recorded for cross-organism sign agreement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{sites}} &= 135300,\\
  n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 15845,\\
  B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
  C_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The value $C_{\mathrm{sign}}=3$ is used only as a bounded sign-concordance statistic over the declared finite comparison surfaces.  It is not a claim that m6A load by itself realizes translation, protein abundance, folding, cellular function, adaptation, or a universal biological regularity.

\paragraph{Comparison statistic.}
For a finite matched row set $W$, write $A_i$ for the m6A load or site-count coordinate attached to row $i$, and $H_i$ for the RNA half-life readout.  Let $Z_i$ denote the available control tuple, including gene or transcript identifier, batch or dataset source, local sequence composition when present, transcript abundance control when present, and any stated isoform or mapping diagnostics.  The only statistic licensed here is a controlled RNA-stability comparison of the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{A,H\mid Z}
    &= \operatorname{Stat}\{(A_i,H_i,Z_i): i\in W\},\\
  \operatorname{Perm}_{1000}(\Delta_{A,H\mid Z})
    &= \text{the declared finite permutation calibration},\\
  C_{\mathrm{sign}}
    &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This notation is intentionally modest.  It names the finite relation between an RNA modification coordinate and an RNA half-life readout after the declared row matching; it does not name an RBP mechanism, a translation mechanism, or a downstream protein-level event.  The same data may license the statement that the m6A-load coordinate crosses an RNA-stability boundary in the finite panel, because RNA half-life is itself an RNA-layer measurement.  The data do not license a stronger assertion that the coordinate acts through a particular decay-factor RBP unless the same rows also contain measured local RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Human perturbation anchor.}
A separate human m6A half-life boundary run supplies a more explicit local calibration for the same type of RNA-stability contact.  In that run, $5837$ genes passed the coverage rule \texttt{min(Mean T coverage DMSO, STM2457) >= 20th percentile among valid joined genes}.  The static association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was negative, with Spearman value $-0.388929$.  The perturbation readout under the methyltransferase-inhibition comparison was positive, with causal Spearman value $0.245804$, length-partial Spearman value $0.227683$, and two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$.  In compact form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{human}} &= 5837,\\
  \rho_{\mathrm{static}}(A,H_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
  \rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(A,\Delta H) &= 0.245804,\\
  \rho_{\mathrm{partial\ length}}(A,\Delta H) &= 0.227683,\\
  p_{\mathrm{perm,2s}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the chapter-level judgment that m6A-load rows can carry an RNA half-life boundary statistic when the finite joins and controls are declared.  They also show why the stronger bridge must remain separate: a half-life response, even with a perturbation-compatible statistic, still does not measure local decay-factor RBP occupancy or prove rescue specificity.

\paragraph{Allowed reading.}
The allowed reading is therefore a constrained cross-layer RNA relation: an RNA modification coordinate is compared with an RNA decay readout on finite matched rows, and the comparison is recorded only at the RNA-stability layer.  The cross-organism m6AConquer result contributes $135300$ sites, $15845$ genes, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant comparison surfaces.  The human perturbation boundary contributes $5837$ genes, a static half-life association of $-0.388929$, a perturbation-associated half-life statistic of $0.245804$, a length-partial statistic of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value of $0.000999001$.  Together they justify writing the m6A load--half-life relation as finite and empirically contacted, but only at the declared RNA layer.

\paragraph{Local refusal rule.}
No translation realization follows from these rows.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, cellular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, decay-factor RBP mechanism, or universal law follows from the m6A load--half-life statistic.  A mechanism statement would require same-row measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a predeclared target RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target controls, and controls for transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  If the half-life pattern persists without RBP-occupancy change, if target-RBP loss and rescue fail to control it, or if binding-defective, non-target, structure, motif-density, transcription, isoform, abundance, or batch explanations reproduce it, the result remains an RNA-stability association and is not promoted to an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite evidence is adequate for a bounded BioReality entry on m6A load and RNA half-life.  It is not adequate for a positive decay-factor RBP mechanism claim.  The scientifically stable statement is: within the declared finite row panels, m6A load has an RNA half-life readout with recorded cross-organism sign concordance and a separately calibrated human perturbation boundary; every interpretation beyond RNA-stability comparison must wait for its own layer-matched contact and finite statistic.
