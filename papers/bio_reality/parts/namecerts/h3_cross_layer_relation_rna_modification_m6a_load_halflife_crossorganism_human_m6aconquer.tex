\subsection{m6A Load, RNA Half-Life, and the Translation Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-crossorganism-human-m6aconquer}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite panel in which coding-RNA rows are read only through matched coordinate labels, RNA half-life measurements, and the covariates needed to prevent a stability signal from being promoted into a stronger biological conclusion.  The intended object is not an unbounded rule about m6A or codon geometry.  It is a row-wise question: whether an m6A-associated load measurement can cross the RNA-stability boundary while remaining separated from translation, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, and mechanism.  The cross-organism human m6AConquer run supplies a finite human-centered table with $135300$ m6A sites mapped across $15845$ genes.  The declared permutation count is $1000$, and the run completed in $338.238$ seconds.  The retained scalar summary reports $3$ sign-concordant organism or comparison units for the m6A-load versus half-life direction.

\paragraph{Observed statistic.}
The positive content of the record is deliberately narrow.  Let $W$ denote the finite joined window or gene panel, let $A(w)$ denote the m6A load attached to row $w$, and let $H(w)$ denote the RNA half-life readout.  The reported computation does not claim that $A$ causes $H$; it says that the predeclared finite table contains enough rows to evaluate an m6A-load/half-life boundary statistic and that the observed sign pattern is concordant in $3$ reported units under $1000$ permutations.  In symbols the admissible readout has the following shape:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  w &\in W,\\
  A(w) &= \text{measured m6A load or site-load summary},\\
  H(w) &= \text{RNA half-life readout},\\
  Z(w) &= \text{identifier, length, composition, batch, and transcript-level controls},\\
  S &= \text{finite statistic comparing } A(w) \text{ with } H(w) \text{ after the declared controls.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count and permutation count are part of the claim: the chapter is about a finite computed contact, not a qualitative assertion that methylation generally determines RNA lifetime.

\paragraph{Supporting half-life boundary run.}
A second m6A half-life computation gives a sharper within-study calibration for the same boundary type.  In the GSE163500 m6A/half-life run, $5837$ genes passed the declared coverage rule, namely that the smaller of mean thymidine coverage in DMSO and STM2457 is at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  The static association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was negative, with Spearman coefficient $-0.388929$.  The causal comparison between m6A-load change and log half-life change was positive, with Spearman coefficient $0.245804$, and the length-partial Spearman coefficient was $0.227683$.  The two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  This companion run is not substituted for the m6AConquer panel; it is used here only to state that the BioReality boundary is not a verbal expectation but a repeatedly computable finite-row relation between m6A load and RNA half-life.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The permitted conclusion is a bounded cross-layer RNA-stability statement.  A row can carry an m6A coordinate, a half-life readout, and a finite statistic relating the two after controls.  The m6AConquer computation contributes $135300$ site rows over $15845$ genes with $3$ sign-concordant units under $1000$ permutations.  The GSE163500 computation contributes a controlled same-study half-life boundary with $5837$ genes, a static coefficient of $-0.388929$, a perturbation coefficient of $0.245804$, a length-partial coefficient of $0.227683$, and permutation value $0.000999001$.  Together these facts license the following restricted form: m6A load may be treated as an RNA-half-life-associated coordinate in the finite panels where the statistic was actually computed.

\paragraph{Mechanism refusal.}
The same numbers do not establish an RNA-decay mechanism.  In particular, they do not provide same-window measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control behavior, or a same-row translation readout before downstream interpretation.  Therefore the record cannot be read as a decay-factor RBP bridge.  A genuine RBP bridge would require rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  (&\text{coordinate label},\ \text{translation readout},\ \text{RNA half-life},\ \text{RBP occupancy},\\
   &\text{target-RBP state},\ \text{wild-type rescue},\ \text{binding-defective control},\\
   &\text{transcription rate},\ \text{RNA structure},\ \text{motif density},\ \text{isoform state},\ \text{batch}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Those rows are not present in the verified finite facts.  The chapter therefore accepts the m6A-load/RNA-half-life boundary while refusing the stronger RBP-decay mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Local scope.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the m6A-load statistic.  The matched mRNA and ribosome-profiling contacts named by the research program are controls or future routes unless their rows are actually joined to the same finite panel.  If high- and low-coordinate labels become exchangeable for RNA half-life after transcription-rate, local-structure, motif-density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair, isoform, transcript-abundance, and batch controls, then the coordinate remains only a descriptor.  If half-life changes without measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, or if perturbation and rescue controls fail, the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than a mechanistic bridge.
