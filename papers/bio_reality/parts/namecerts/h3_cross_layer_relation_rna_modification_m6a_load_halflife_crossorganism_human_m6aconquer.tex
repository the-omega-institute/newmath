\subsection{Human m6A Load and RNA Half-Life as a Bounded Cross-Layer Record}\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-load-halflife-human-m6aconquer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite panel in which human coding or transcript-associated rows carry an m6A-site burden and an RNA half-life readout on compatible gene identifiers. The verified finite run for the human m6AConquer-side surface retained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, used $1000$ permutations, and completed in $338.238$ seconds. Its reported concordance count is $3$, meaning that the finite check records repeated sign agreement across the declared joined surfaces rather than a universal statement about RNA decay. The row object used here is therefore not an unrestricted transcriptome law; it is a counted table of gene-level or transcript-level entries after identifier joining, m6A-site aggregation, half-life attachment, and finite permutation evaluation.

\paragraph{Statistic readout.}
Let $G$ be the retained gene set, $S_g$ the number or load of m6A sites assigned to gene $g$, and $H_g$ the externally attached RNA half-life readout for the same joined row. The finite run records only the existence of a sign-concordant cross-organism or cross-surface pattern under its declared evaluation protocol:
$$
\begin{aligned}
|G| &= 15845,\\
\sum_{g \in G} S_g &= 135300,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
N_{\mathrm{sign\ concordant}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The interpretation is deliberately narrow. A sign-concordant m6A-load relation to RNA half-life can be used as a bounded RNA-stability readout only after the rows have been kept distinct from translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, organismal phenotype, and adaptive explanation. The statistic says that the curated finite data support a reproducible m6A-load/half-life boundary in the declared table; it does not by itself identify an m6A reader, writer, eraser, decay-factor RBP, or causal RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Same-layer control from a site-load half-life boundary.}
A second finite RNA-modification fact supplies a useful local calibration for how this chapter treats RNA half-life evidence. In the site-load half-life run, $5837$ genes passed the joined coverage rule, the static Spearman correlation between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman relation between m6A-load change and $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$. Its declared verdict was that the boundary is crossed, with the explicit semantics that this is support for a pre-registered external m6A/half-life statistic, not a mechanism or function claim. This nearby finite result is cited here only to fix the discipline of interpretation: even a stronger row count, an explicit partial statistic, and a small permutation value remain RNA-half-life evidence until a separate mechanism-bearing contact is attached.

\paragraph{Coordinate, relation, and trigger.}
The coordinate component is the finite m6A burden or synonymous coding-window label available on the same joined row as RNA half-life. The relation component is the observed dependence between that coordinate and the half-life readout after the declared joins and controls. The trigger component is the condition under which a mere RNA-stability association could be promoted to a decay-factor RBP bridge: the same finite rows would need measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a coordinate--occupancy decoupling design, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target controls, matched transcription or mRNA controls, isoform controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, composition covariates, codon-pair context, batch labels, and the same-row translation readout used only as a boundary variable.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The present finite evidence licenses the following bounded statement. In the verified human m6AConquer-side table, m6A site load and RNA half-life can be placed in a finite cross-layer relation from RNA modification to RNA stability, with $15845$ joined genes, $135300$ sites, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant evaluated surfaces. The statement is about a finite relation between a transcript modification burden and an RNA half-life readout. It may be used to motivate a more resolved same-window design, but it is not already such a design.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The record remains negative for all stronger readings. If high- and low-coordinate rows are exchangeable for RNA half-life after transcription rate, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch are controlled, the coordinate is only a bounded descriptor. If RNA half-life differs but measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not prespecified, the result is only an RNA-stability association. If target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target controls are missing, the result cannot be called an RBP-mediated bridge. If local structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, abundance, batch, or a simpler compositional baseline reproduces the pattern, the bridge interpretation is refused.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from the m6A-load statistic or from sign concordance. A translation readout, when present, only marks a boundary that prevents promotion from RNA stability into protein-level interpretation. A mechanism claim would require a distinct layer-matched contact carrying measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation/rescue behavior on the same finite rows. Until those rows exist, this chapter records a finite RNA-modification/RNA-half-life relation and nothing stronger.
