\paragraph{Finite surface.}

It is a table of organism-indexed summary rows whose columns include domain, GC3, tRNA-gene-count and proteome-size proxies, the fraction $M^{QTP}$ attributed to the modeled translation-conditioned protein route, and the dominant residual coordinate selected by the prior coordinate summaries. Thus the powered numerical claims below use $n=12$, while the surrounding descriptive registry still records $16$ organism rows.

\paragraph{Domain and dominant-coordinate readback.}
The domain comparison uses the same $n=12$ complete-feature surface. The group means of $M^{QTP}$ are $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$. The maximum-minus-minimum group-mean contrast is therefore $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria above archaea by group mean. The permutation test does not resolve this contrast as a domain law:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{domain}} &= 0.8889980452277374,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm},\mathrm{right}} &= 0.4155844155844156,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dominant-coordinate contingency table is likewise finite and heterogeneous. The finite table therefore supports organism-specific diversity of dominant residual coordinates, not a domain-classification theorem.

The organism-specific meta row is separate from the measured translation-efficiency survival computation. These numbers are useful for locating the meta table relative to translation-efficiency contacts, but they do not turn the cross-organism meta comparison into a direct ribosome mechanism or a protein-output claim.

\paragraph{Local verdict and refusal boundary.}
The finite verdict is that the $B^*_{Q6}$ organism-specific meta surface is computed and bounded. It reports a $16$-row registry, a $12$-row complete-feature testing surface, continuous-proxy correlations, a domain variance ratio, and a dominant-coordinate contingency test. The observed values show heterogeneity across organisms, but all reported permutation tests remain non-resolving at the stated finite-row resolution. Consequently the record is ready only as a descriptive cross-organism comparison. It does not establish translation realization, ribosome causality, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phylogenetically corrected inference, or universality. Any one of those stronger claims would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic rather than promotion from this organism-level meta table.
