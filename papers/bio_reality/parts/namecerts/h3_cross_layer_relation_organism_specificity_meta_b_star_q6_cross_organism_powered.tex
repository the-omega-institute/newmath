\subsection{Organism-Specific Cross-Layer Meta-Surface for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Residual Basis}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This record treats the organism-specific $B^{*}_{Q6}$ summaries as a finite cross-layer meta-surface, not as a universal biological law.  The rows are coding-sequence readout summaries indexed by organism, with $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates already formed before any interpretation in protein structure, protein function, phenotype, or mechanism language.  The assembled table contains $16$ organism rows.  The complete-feature subset used for the domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, proteome-size, and dominant-coordinate tests contains $12$ rows: \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, \emph{Mus musculus}, \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}.  Four organisms remain outside the complete-feature meta-test for the $M^{QTP}$ summaries: \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}, and \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana}.  The finite nature of the surface is therefore part of the claim: the result is an exploratory organism panel with explicit missing rows, not an inference over all organisms.

\paragraph{Domain-level $M^{QTP}$ readback.}
The domain comparison uses the $12$ complete rows and compares $M^{QTP}$ fraction against the organism-domain partition.  The fitted group summaries are small enough to state directly.  The archaeal group has $n=1$ and mean $0.01850668564589326$.  The bacterial group has $n=3$, mean $0.4351192457799102$, minimum $0.08397602919861291$, and maximum $0.612922252793667$.  The eukaryotic group has $n=8$, mean $0.3960177769015294$, minimum $0.15488799907323988$, and maximum $0.9491551041216973$.  The largest minus smallest group mean is $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria higher than archaea by this group-mean contrast, but the deterministic permutation null does not make this a resolved domain effect:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{domain}} &= 0.8889980452277374,\\
p_{\mathrm{right}} &= 0.4155844155844156,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite table contains visible between-domain heterogeneity, but the observed contrast is kept descriptive because the permutation probability is large and the domain groups are unbalanced.

\paragraph{Continuous organism covariates.}
The same $12$ complete-feature rows were tested against GC3, proteome size, and tRNA-gene-count proxies.  The GC3 comparison gives a negative Pearson correlation, $r=-0.3062616500469447$, with two-sided permutation probability $p=0.35264735264735264$ over $1000$ trials.  The log proteome-size comparison gives $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$.  The log tRNA-gene-count comparison gives $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  In compact form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},\mathrm{GC3}) &= -0.3062616500469447,\\
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{proteins}}) &= -0.033628115789504955,\\
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{tRNA\ genes}}) &= 0.06029405206037823.
\end{aligned}
$$
The signs are reported because they are part of the finite row surface, but none of these three tests supplies a powered cross-organism mechanism claim.  The corresponding permutation probabilities are all far from a small-tail regime, so the correct reading is weak organism-level covariation under the present panel rather than a codon-usage or translation law.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency table was also computed on $12$ complete rows.  The chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V$ is $0.6258327785172862$, and the right-tail permutation probability is $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The finite contingency surface has one archaeal row with dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$; three bacterial rows split across $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $K\_AAA$, and $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$; and eight eukaryotic rows with counts $3$ for $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $1$ for $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $1$ for $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, and $3$ for $f3\_stress$.  The top-by-domain summary is therefore $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ for archaea with count $1$ of $1$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$ for bacteria with count $1$ of $3$, and $f3\_stress$ for eukaryotes with count $3$ of $8$.  The moderate Cramer's $V$ records a structured finite table, while the permutation probability prevents promotion to a domain-enrichment conclusion.

The organism-specific meta-surface is interpreted alongside, but not replaced by, the measured translation-efficiency and tRNA-copy readouts. These side contacts support the bounded reading of the meta-surface: the cross-organism summaries compare already computed projection quantities against organism descriptors, while direct translation-efficiency survival remains a separate row-matched readout problem.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter-level conclusion is that the finite $B^{*}_{Q6}$ organism panel contains computable heterogeneity but no resolved cross-organism mechanism.  The table reports descriptive correlations, permutation probabilities, domain summaries, GC3 values, and dominant-coordinate counts.  It does not license a claim that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual geometry causes translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  A translation-efficiency claim would require same-organism and same-CDS ribosome-profiling or TE rows with matched mRNA abundance and the declared controls; a folding, physical, functional, phenotypic, or mechanistic claim would require an additional layer-matched contact and its own statistic.  Without those additional contacts, the correct verdict is a bounded organism-specific meta-readback: structured enough to guide further testing, but not sufficient to promote the residual basis into a biological law.
