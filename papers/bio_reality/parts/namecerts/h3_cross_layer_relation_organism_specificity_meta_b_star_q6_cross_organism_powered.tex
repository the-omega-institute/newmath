\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
The organism-specificity packet treats the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis as a finite cross-organism table rather than as a universal biological rule. The table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set needed for the reported $M^{QTP}$ comparisons. The complete rows are \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Four organisms remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ panel: \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. The GC3 readback is available for $15$ organisms, with \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana} still absent from that scalar surface.

\paragraph{Domain and coordinate tests.}
The domain test compares the $M^{QTP}$ fraction against the three-domain annotation on the $12$ complete rows. The group means are $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$. The largest minus smallest group mean is therefore $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest in this finite panel. The permutation statistic is a between-domain to within-domain mean-square ratio of $0.8889980452277374$, with right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials. This records heterogeneity, not a resolved domain law.

The dominant-coordinate contingency table has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The top coordinate in the single archaeal row is \texttt{Arg\_AGR}; the bacterial rows split one count each across \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, and \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}; the eukaryotic rows have \texttt{f3\_stress} as the top coordinate with count $3$ among $8$ rows. Thus the coordinate labels differ across the finite table, but the permutation result does not justify a domain-specific enrichment claim.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
The organism-specificity summary is downstream of bounded readout calculations, but it is not itself a ribosome-profiling result. In the measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI comparison, the full-join row counts were $14410$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $19053$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $5815$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}. These are modeled codon-adaptation readbacks based on tRNA gene copy and a wobble-weight convention, not direct ribosome occupancy.

The measured translation-efficiency comparison used organism-specific joins of $13140$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $3415$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $11554$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $5128$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}. Those small values are useful as bounded contacts with translation-efficiency data, but they do not turn the organism-level meta table into a mechanism or a population-level biological law.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter supports the following narrow statement. Across the computed finite panel, organism-specific summaries of the $B^*_{Q6}$ cross-layer surface vary by organism and by dominant residual coordinate, but the reported permutation tests do not resolve a domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, or proteome-size explanation. The strongest-looking descriptive contrast is the finite group-mean spread in $M^{QTP}$, where bacteria exceed archaea by $0.41661256013401693$ in the available rows, but the test value $p=0.4155844155844156$ keeps that contrast exploratory.

Nothing stronger is licensed here. No translation mechanism, ribosome-dynamics law, protein abundance claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, biological-function claim, phenotype claim, causal claim, phylogenetically corrected inference, or universality claim follows from this table. Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. In particular, the cross-organism rows are not phylogenetically independent samples, the panel is small, the modeled tAI mediation quantities are not ribosome-seq measurements, and $M^{QTP}$, $R^2_{QP}$, and dominant-coordinate labels remain statistical projections rather than mechanisms.
