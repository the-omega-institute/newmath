\subsection{Organism-specific meta-readout for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record treats the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates as a finite cross-organism readout surface rather than as a claim about a universal biological mechanism.  The assembled meta table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set needed for the $M^{QTP}$ comparisons.  The complete rows are \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, \emph{Mus musculus}, \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}.  Four rows, \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}, and \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana}, remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ panel.  The finite carrier is therefore a descriptive table of organism-specific residual summaries, GC3 values, tRNA-pool proxies, proteome-size proxies, and dominant-coordinate calls.

\paragraph{Domain and continuous covariate tests.}
The domain comparison for $M^{QTP}$ used $12$ complete rows.  The group means were $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$.  The largest minus smallest group mean was $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest, but the deterministic permutation test did not resolve this as a domain effect: the variance-ratio statistic was $0.8889980452277374$ and the right-tail permutation value was $p=0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous tests likewise remain descriptive.  For $M^{QTP}$ versus GC3, the Pearson statistic was $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $p=0.35264735264735264$.  For $M^{QTP}$ versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$.  For $M^{QTP}$ versus log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These values say that the finite table has visible heterogeneity, not that a phylogenetically corrected organism-level law has been obtained.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate contingency used $n=12$ organisms and the coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The Pearson chi-square statistic was $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V$ was $0.6258327785172862$, and the right-tail permutation value was $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The tabulation places the single archaeal row at $Arg\_AGR$; the bacterial rows distribute one count each across $Arg\_AGR$, $K\_AAA$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$; and the eukaryotic rows place three counts at $Arg\_AGR$, one at $Ile\_AUA$, one at $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and three at $f3\_stress$.  The top labels are therefore $Arg\_AGR$ for the one archaeal row, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ for bacteria under the reported tie-selected top entry, and $f3\_stress$ for eukaryotes.  Because the permutation value is large, the table is read as unresolved organism-specific heterogeneity rather than as a domain classifier.

\paragraph{Translation-efficiency contact and residual controls.}
The measured translation-efficiency contact is available as a separate controlled projection, not as a mechanism claim.  The four organism-specific TE joins used $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Each used $24$ control columns with rank $23$, including the intercept, log CDS length, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The measured-TE absorption fractions were $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{Escherichia coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding modeled-complement distances on the TE joins were $0.8424312412078521$, $0.6628211177352172$, $0.8566190269974241$, and $0.22386566043976477$.  Two of the TE joins changed the complement reference by more than $0.02$: \emph{Danio rerio} differed by $0.03219747984141397$ and \emph{Homo sapiens} by $0.06427590857052146$.  Thus the TE data are useful bounded measurements, but the join differences prevent promoting the meta-table into a single stable cross-organism translation claim.

\paragraph{Modeled tRNA and downstream candidates.}
The tRNA gene-copy tAI readout gives another finite bounded contact.  The full joins were $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; the parsed tRNA gene counts were $8846$, $431$, and $273$, respectively.  The absorbed fractions were $0.020817102845637092$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.00005426229300865148$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.001530747712165342$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  A yeast-only turnover contact joined $3640$ proteins, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; its turnover absorption was $0.0009086031889966327$.  Protein-feature contacts were sparse and join-sensitive: \emph{Danio rerio} joined $2242$ feature rows from $3180$ records, \emph{Homo sapiens} joined $18335$ from $18842$, and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} joined $5682$ from $6156$.  Some individual feature projections exceeded their finite null thresholds, for example human complex membership at $0.0075271999877804495$ against a Bonferroni threshold $0.0006605662314432479$ and human PTM density at $0.014884825920540636$ against $0.00042094892238120357$, but the feature panel is annotation-limited and does not close a causal path.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The supported conclusion is narrow: organism-specific $B^{*}_{Q6}$ summaries can be assembled and compared against domain, GC3, tRNA-pool size, proteome-size, measured TE, modeled tRNA adaptation, turnover, and sparse protein-feature contacts.  The meta tests are computed, and their reported permutation values are not small enough to justify a resolved domain preference or a phylogenetically corrected organism-level trend.  A positive or nonzero projection in this table is not, by itself, a statement about translation realization, ribosome mechanism, mature protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or universality.  Any such higher-layer statement would require its own layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.  In the present finite record, the safest reading is that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates expose organism-specific residual heterogeneity whose cross-layer contacts are measurable but not promoted beyond the measured contact that supplied each number.
