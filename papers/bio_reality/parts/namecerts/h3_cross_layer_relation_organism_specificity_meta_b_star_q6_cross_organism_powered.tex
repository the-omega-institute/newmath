\subsection{Organism-Specific Meta Readout for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Cross-Layer Summaries}
\label{sec:bioq6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the organism as the row unit and asks whether previously computed $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ cross-layer summaries show a stable dependence on broad organism-level descriptors. The finite panel contains $16$ organism rows at assembly time, of which $12$ have the complete feature set needed for the meta tests. The complete rows are \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Four rows remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ meta surface for this computation: \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. The row count is therefore large enough for a deterministic descriptive permutation screen, but not large enough for a phylogenetically corrected biological law.

\paragraph{Readout coordinates.}
The response variable is the organism-level $M^{QTP}$ fraction, a summary derived from the prior $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate-to-translation-to-protein projection bookkeeping. The tested predictors are domain membership, GC3, log tRNA gene count, and log proteome size. The dominant-coordinate table is also tested against domain membership. For the complete $12$-row meta panel, the dominant-coordinate contingency has six coordinate columns: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-stress}$. The observed table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{archaea} &: (0,1,0,0,0,0),\\
\text{bacteria} &: (1,1,0,0,1,0),\\
\text{eukaryote} &: (0,3,1,1,0,3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the single archaeal complete row is dominated by $Arg_{AGR}$; the bacterial rows have one count each for $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, and $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$; and the eukaryotic rows are concentrated most visibly in $Arg_{AGR}$ and $f_3\text{-stress}$, with three counts each.

\paragraph{Domain screen.}
The domain-level $M^{QTP}$ screen compares archaea, bacteria, and eukaryotes on the complete $12$-row subset. The group means are $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$. The maximum-minus-minimum group-mean contrast is $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest by group mean. The permutation statistic is the ratio of between-domain mean square to within-domain mean square, equal to $0.8889980452277374$, with right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ deterministic trials. This is a computed heterogeneity readout, not a resolved domain effect.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate screen.}
For dominant coordinate versus domain, the Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$ on $n=12$ rows. The associated Cramer's $V$ is $0.6258327785172862$, but the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The large nominal association size is therefore not promoted to a domain-specific enrichment claim. The finite table says only that the observed dominant coordinate labels are heterogeneous across this small organism panel.

\paragraph{Continuous predictor screens.}
The GC3 screen gives a negative Pearson correlation between $M^{QTP}$ fraction and GC3:

|r_{GC3}| &= 0.3062616500469447,\\
p_{GC3} &= 0.35264735264735264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The log proteome-size screen is nearly flat:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\log n_{proteins}} &= -0.033628115789504955,\\
|r_{\log n_{proteins}}| &= 0.033628115789504955,\\
p_{\log n_{proteins}} &= 0.913086913086913.
\end{aligned}
$$
The log tRNA-gene-count screen is also small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\log n_{tRNA}} &= 0.06029405206037823,\\
|r_{\log n_{tRNA}}| &= 0.06029405206037823,\\
p_{\log n_{tRNA}} &= 0.8621378621378621.

$$
All three continuous tests use the same $12$ complete organisms and $1000$ deterministic permutation trials. None supplies a powered organism-level rule.

\paragraph{GC3 row values.}
The GC3 surface was computed for $15$ named organisms and remained missing for \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. Several values show why the panel is useful as a heterogeneity check: \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum} has GC3 $0.14471498077638234$, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus} has $0.3061845898275272$, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} has $0.3809678913179245$, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} has $0.5642064879164637$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} has $0.5807133823494395$, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster} has $0.6313215697461184$, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155} has $0.8720662993842282$, \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1} has $0.8806155914396909$, and \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum} has $0.8944468418285529$. The wide GC3 span makes the negative $r_{GC3}$ direction interpretable as a descriptive trend candidate, but the permutation value prevents treating it as a resolved explanatory axis.

\paragraph{Relation to tRNA and measured translation readouts.}
The tRNA-gene-copy readout used in the linked translation-adaptation computation provides organism-specific scale information rather than direct ribosome occupancy. In that linked computation, \texttt{danio\_rerio} has $8846$ parsed tRNA genes and an anticodon GCN table size of $57$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} has $431$ tRNA genes and table size $47$, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} has $273$ tRNA genes and table size $41$. The same linked calculation reports $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement absorption by tRNA-copy tAI of $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast. These finite values justify including log tRNA gene count as a meta covariate, but they do not turn the organism-level meta screen into a translation mechanism. The measured translation-efficiency computation is also bounded: the organism-specific TE joins had $n=13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \texttt{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, each with $24$ control columns and rank $23$ after the declared controls. The measured-TE absorption values were small: $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in the same organism order. These numbers support the local interpretation that the meta surface is mostly a descriptive organism-specific summary, not a discovery of a universal translation-efficiency rule.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The positive content of the chapter is narrow. A finite organism table was assembled; $12$ rows had the complete covariates needed for the $M^{QTP}$ meta tests; domain, GC3, tRNA-pool size, proteome size, and dominant-coordinate labels were screened with deterministic permutation nulls; and every resulting $p$-value remained non-small in this panel. The record therefore keeps two statements separate. First, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ summaries are organism-specific enough to warrant finite-row stratification. Second, this stratification does not yet identify domain, GC3, tRNA pool size, proteome size, or dominant coordinate as a stable explanatory driver.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from this meta chapter alone. Modeled tAI mediation is not ribosome profiling. $M^{QTP}$ fractions, $R^2_{QP}$-style summaries, and dominant-coordinate labels are statistical summaries over finite joined rows, not mechanisms. A positive or negative organism-level correlation would still require a separate layer-matched empirical contact before it could be read as ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, folded structure, localization, function, phenotype, causation, or a global biological rule. In the present finite table, even the descriptive correlations remain exploratory because the complete panel has only $12$ rows and no phylogenetic comparative correction.
