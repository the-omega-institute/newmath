\subsection{Organism-Specific Cross-Layer Readback for $B^*_{Q6}$ Coordinates}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record considered here is a finite cross-organism summary surface for $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The biological object is not a protein, structure, molecular function, or phenotype.  It is a table in which coordinate-level summaries derived from coding-sequence residuals are compared with organism descriptors and bounded translation-adjacent summaries.  The assembled meta table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set used for the reported $M^{QTP}$ comparisons.  The complete rows are Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, Homo sapiens, Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Bacillus subtilis subsp. subtilis str. 168, Mus musculus, Caenorhabditis elegans, Drosophila melanogaster, Sulfolobus solfataricus, and Rattus norvegicus.  Four rows remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ surface: Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, Halobacterium salinarum, Dictyostelium discoideum, and Arabidopsis thaliana.  GC3 values were nevertheless computed for all except Arabidopsis thaliana; examples include $0.3809678913179245$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.5642064879164637$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.5807133823494395$ for Homo sapiens, $0.5450598668769285$ for Danio rerio, $0.8944468418285529$ for Halobacterium salinarum, and $0.14471498077638234$ for Dictyostelium discoideum.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The domain-by-dominant-coordinate table has $n=12$ complete rows and uses six dominant-coordinate labels: $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$.  The contingency counts are finite and sparse: archaea contributes one $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ row; bacteria contributes one $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ row, one $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$ row, and one $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$ row; eukaryotes contribute three $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ rows, one $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$ row, one $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$ row, and three $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$ rows.  The Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$.  Under $1000$ deterministic permutation trials, the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814185814$.  Thus the observed coordinate heterogeneity is recorded, but the finite table does not resolve a domain-specific dominant-coordinate enrichment.  The largest within-domain counts are $1/1$ for archaeal $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $1/3$ for bacterial $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$ under the recorded tie-breaking summary, and $3/8$ for eukaryotic $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$.

\paragraph{$M^{QTP}$ against organism descriptors.}
The $M^{QTP}$ fraction was compared with domain, GC3, log proteome size, and log tRNA gene count on the $12$ complete rows.  The domain comparison reports group means $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$.  The maximum-minus-minimum group mean is $0.41661256013401693$, and the variance-ratio statistic is $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous tests are likewise descriptive rather than decisive: $M^{QTP}$ versus GC3 has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; $M^{QTP}$ versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and $M^{QTP}$ versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These values are finite readbacks of the panel, not evidence for a universal domain law.

\paragraph{Translation-adjacent contacts used to keep the boundary sharp.}
The larger data packet records measured translation-efficiency joins for four organisms with the same nine residual coordinates $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$.  The measured-TE joins have $n=13140$ for Danio rerio, $n=3415$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for Homo sapiens, and $n=5128$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each organism.  Measured TE absorbs only $0.01759429001506974$ of the modeled complement in Danio rerio, $0.0010982956489726373$ in Escherichia coli, $0.0028947149257963897$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact is also bounded: absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ for Danio rerio, $0.00005426229300865148$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.001530747712165342$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  Yeast protein half-life absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled complement on $3640$ joined rows.  These small projection quantities help describe where the residual basis does and does not meet available readouts, but they do not turn the meta table into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Protein-feature and localization side checks.}
The protein-feature contact is explicitly sparse and organism-dependent.  It joins $2242$ Danio rerio proteins, $18335$ Homo sapiens proteins, and $5682$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins.  Some single-feature absorptions exceed degree-of-freedom null thresholds, such as Danio rerio PTM density with absorbed value $0.014211240113472575$ exceeding its Bonferroni threshold $0.003775603188484381$, Homo sapiens complex membership with absorbed value $0.0075271999877804495$ exceeding $0.0006605662314432479$, Homo sapiens PTM density with absorbed value $0.014884825920540636$ exceeding $0.00042094892238120357$, and Homo sapiens transmembrane count with absorbed value $0.0015569064882873212$ exceeding $0.0002697448894057371$.  The same packet reports that feature availability can change the residual target, for example Danio rerio has a full-join complement reference $0.874628721049266$ but a feature-join value $0.7361678489976374$.  A separate localization boundary check joins $8696$ rows, with $657$ mitochondrial rows and $2000$ permutations.  These side checks may motivate follow-up contacts, but the present organism-specific meta claim remains a finite descriptive comparison.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter licenses only the reported finite comparisons: row counts, coordinate contingencies, $M^{QTP}$ associations with organism descriptors, and bounded side-readouts.  It does not assert that a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate causes translation, determines ribosome occupancy, controls folding, establishes physical admissibility, realizes molecular function, explains organismal phenotype, or defines a universal biological law.  Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own joined rows, controls, and statistic.  In particular, mRNA abundance is a control for translation-efficiency residualization, not a translation mechanism by itself; modeled tAI is a codon-adaptation readout, not an observed ribosome-footprint measurement; and cross-organism correlation without phylogenetic correction is exploratory rather than a phylogenetic or causal inference.
