\subsection{Organism-specific meta-readback for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ cross-layer surface}\label{subsec:biorality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the organism-to-organism behavior of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface has any stable explanatory alignment with coarse biological descriptors: domain, GC3, tRNA-pool size, proteome size, and dominant residual coordinate. The unit of analysis is not an individual codon or protein. It is a finite organism row carrying a previously computed cross-layer summary, together with organism-level covariates. The resulting object is therefore a descriptive meta-surface: it can compare organism-specific summaries, but it cannot by itself create a translation, protein, structure, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal-law claim.

\paragraph{Rows and admissible columns.}
The assembled table contains $16$ organism rows. The complete-feature subtable used for the $M^{QTP}$ meta-tests contains $12$ rows: \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Four rows remain outside the complete-feature $M^{QTP}$ table: \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. The last of these also lacks the GC3 value in the available table. The recorded GC3 values include $0.3809678913$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, $0.5642064879$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $0.5807133823$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, $0.5450598669$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $0.8720662994$ for \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, $0.3061845898$ for \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and $0.8944468418$ for \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}.

\paragraph{Domain comparison of the $M^{QTP}$ fraction.}
The domain test compares the $M^{QTP}$ fraction across archaea, bacteria, and eukaryotes by a between-domain to within-domain variance ratio. On the $12$ complete-feature rows, the group means are $0.0185066856$ for archaea, $0.4351192458$ for bacteria, and $0.3960177769$ for eukaryotes. The largest minus smallest group mean is $0.4166125601$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest in this finite table. The permutation test does not make this a resolved domain effect: the variance ratio is $0.8889980452$ and the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.4155844156$ over $1000$ deterministic trials. The admissible reading is heterogeneity of the organism rows, not a domain law.

\paragraph{Continuous covariate checks.}
The same $12$ complete-feature rows were tested against three continuous organism-level predictors. The $M^{QTP}$ fraction has Pearson correlation $r=-0.3062616500$ with GC3, with two-sided permutation value $p=0.3526473526$ over $1000$ trials. Against log proteome size, the correlation is nearly absent, $r=-0.0336281158$, with $p=0.9130869131$. Against log tRNA-gene count, the correlation is also weak, $r=0.0602940521$, with $p=0.8621378621$. Thus the finite-row table records no powered monotone explanation of the $M^{QTP}$ fraction by GC3, proteome size, or tRNA-pool size.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate table uses $12$ rows and the coordinates \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}. Archaea contribute one \texttt{Arg\_AGR} row. Bacteria contribute one \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{K\_AAA}, and one \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} row. Eukaryotes contribute three \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, one \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and three \texttt{f3\_stress} rows. The Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.4$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785$, but the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The table is visibly nonuniform, yet the finite permutation test does not identify a domain-specific dominant-coordinate enrichment. The top entries are \texttt{Arg\_AGR} for the single archaeal row, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} among bacteria by the recorded tie-breaking output, and \texttt{f3\_stress} among eukaryotes with count $3$ of $8$.

These numbers support the same conclusion as the meta-table: the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ summaries are organism-specific and finite, while tRNA-copy, GC3, and domain descriptors do not resolve the residual surface into a single promoted biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a conservative organism-specificity statement: $B^{*}_{Q6}$ cross-layer summaries can be assembled and compared across organisms, and the resulting rows are heterogeneous, but the available permutation tests do not identify a powered domain, GC3, tRNA-pool-size, or proteome-size explanation. The domain comparison has $p=0.4155844156$, the dominant-coordinate contingency has $p=0.5814185814$, and the three continuous checks have $p=0.3526473526$, $p=0.9130869131$, and $p=0.8621378621$. Therefore the chapter records a bounded meta-readback rather than a mechanistic conclusion. No translation realization, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, causal mechanism, phylogenetically corrected inference, or universal biological law follows from these rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and statistic.
