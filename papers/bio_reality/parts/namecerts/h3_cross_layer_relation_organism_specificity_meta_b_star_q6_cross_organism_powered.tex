\subsection{Organism-specific cross-layer profile of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite organism-indexed table built from $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and cross-layer summary statistics.  The table has $16$ organism rows at the assembly stage, of which $12$ have the complete feature set used for the meta-analysis.  The complete rows are Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC$^{2}$ 155, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, Homo sapiens, Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Bacillus subtilis subsp. subtilis str. 168, Mus musculus, Caenorhabditis elegans, Drosophila melanogaster, Sulfolobus solfataricus, and Rattus norvegicus.  Four rows remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ comparison because the required data are absent for Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, Halobacterium salinarum, Dictyostelium discoideum, and Arabidopsis thaliana.

The observed object is not a protein, phenotype, or mechanism.  It is a finite set of per-organism descriptors attached to residual codon-coordinate summaries: the fraction $M^{QTP}$, the dominant $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate, GC3, tRNA-pool proxy size, proteome-size proxy, and broad domain label.  The purpose of the row is to ask whether the residual cross-layer summaries have detectable organism-specific structure after they have already been reduced to finite descriptive statistics.

\paragraph{Domain comparison.}
The domain comparison of $M^{QTP}$ uses $n=12$ complete rows.  The group means are $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with one row, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with three rows, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with eight rows.  The largest-minus-smallest group-mean contrast is therefore $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria above archaea by the raw group mean.  The permutation test does not resolve this contrast as a stable domain effect: the variance-ratio statistic is $0.8889980452277374$ and the right-tail permutation value is $0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ deterministic trials.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{archaea}} &= 0.01850668564589326,\\
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{bacteria}} &= 0.4351192457799102,\\
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{eukaryote}} &= 0.3960177769015294,\\
\Delta_{\max-\min} &= 0.41661256013401693,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.4155844155844156.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the finite row surface permits the statement that the observed bacterial mean is higher than the observed archaeal mean in this panel, but it does not license a domain law.  The archaeal group has only one complete row, and the permutation value remains large.

\paragraph{Continuous covariates.}
The same $12$ complete organisms were compared against GC3, log proteome size, and log tRNA gene count.  The $M^{QTP}$ fraction has a negative Pearson correlation with GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$, with a two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$ over $1000$ trials.  The comparison with log proteome size is nearly flat, $r=-0.033628115789504955$, with permutation value $0.913086913086913$.  The comparison with log tRNA gene count is also weak, $r=0.06029405206037823$, with permutation value $0.8621378621378621$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},\mathrm{GC3}) &= -0.3062616500469447, & p &= 0.35264735264735264,\\
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{proteins}}) &= -0.033628115789504955, & p &= 0.913086913086913,\\
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{tRNA\ genes}}) &= 0.06029405206037823, & p &= 0.8621378621378621.
\end{aligned}
$$

The finite GC3 values show substantial range across the larger assembled table: Dictyostelium discoideum has GC3 $0.14471498077638234$, Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $0.3809678913179245$, Homo sapiens has $0.5807133823494395$, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $0.5642064879164637$, Drosophila melanogaster has $0.6313215697461184$, Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC$^{2}$ 155 has $0.8720662993842282$, Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1 has $0.8806155914396909$, and Halobacterium salinarum has $0.8944468418285529$.  Arabidopsis thaliana lacks the needed GC3 entry in this finite surface.  These values support an organism-specific descriptive readback, but the tested correlations do not establish a GC-driven residual mechanism.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate contingency table is also computed on $n=12$ complete rows.  Its Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$.  The right-tail permutation value is $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials, so the apparent concentration is treated as heterogeneous coordinate usage rather than a resolved domain preference.  In the row summaries, the archaeal group has one dominant coordinate, Arg\_AGR.  The bacterial rows have one Arg\_AGR, one K\_AAA, and one Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY.  The eukaryotic rows have three Arg\_AGR, one Ile\_AUA, one Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, and three f3\_stress dominant-coordinate calls.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{domain}\times\mathrm{coord}} &= 9.399999999999999,\\
V_{\mathrm{Cramer}} &= 0.6258327785172862,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,right}} &= 0.5814185814185814.
\end{aligned}
$$

The strongest plain-language reading is therefore not that one biological domain owns a coordinate, but that the residual surface changes its dominant coordinate from organism to organism.  In this panel, f3\_stress is the most frequent eukaryotic dominant coordinate with $3$ of $8$ eukaryotic complete rows, while the three bacterial complete rows split across three different dominant coordinates.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-efficiency and modeled translation contacts.}
The organism-specific meta row is downstream of bounded cross-layer calculations.  In the measured translation-efficiency contact, the coordinate set has $9$ residual coordinates: K\_AAA, Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and f3\_stress.  The controlled measured-TE joins have $n=13140$ for Danio rerio, $n=3415$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for Homo sapiens, and $n=5128$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with rank $23$ control matrices in each case.  The measured-TE absorption values are small: $0.01759429001506974$ in Danio rerio, $0.0010982956489726373$ in Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact gives a separate bounded readout, not a ribosome observation.  It uses $14410$ joined Danio rerio CDS rows with $8846$ tRNA genes, $19053$ joined Homo sapiens CDS rows with $431$ tRNA genes, and $5815$ joined Saccharomyces cerevisiae CDS rows with $273$ tRNA genes.  The computed absorption by this tRNA proxy is $0.020817102845637092$ for Danio rerio, $0.00005426229300865148$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.001530747712165342$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  These numbers are useful for comparing organism-level residual summaries, but they remain descriptive projections.

\paragraph{Turnover contact as a yeast-only boundary check.}
The turnover contact is available only for the yeast half-life boundary in this finite record.  It joins $3640$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae protein rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and a turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  The modeled-complement comparison remains within the declared tolerance: the full-join complement reference is $0.21173219572706703$, the current turnover-join value is $0.2232704509412724$, and the absolute difference is $0.011538255214205373$.  The turnover absorption is $0.0009086031889966327$, with combined value $0.22306503721327192$.  This supplies a yeast-specific downstream descriptive contact, but it does not promote the organism-specific meta table to a causal degradation model.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite organism-specific record is ready as a descriptive cross-layer summary.  It supports the existence of an assembled organism-indexed surface with $16$ rows, a complete $12$-row meta-analysis subset, computed GC3 values for $15$ organisms, three continuous permutation tests, one domain permutation test, and one dominant-coordinate contingency test.  It also records that the strongest numerical effects in this panel are exploratory: the domain contrast has $p=0.4155844155844156$, the dominant-coordinate contingency has $p=0.5814185814185814$, and the continuous comparisons have permutation values $0.35264735264735264$, $0.913086913086913$, and $0.8621378621378621$.

No translation mechanism, ribosome-occupancy mechanism, protein abundance law, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, phenotype statement, or universal biological rule follows from these rows.  A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each such claim, and the contact would have to carry its own joined rows and controlled statistic.  The present chapter only says that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual summaries can be arranged into a finite organism-specific meta surface, and that the tested domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, proteome-size, and dominant-coordinate patterns remain descriptive rather than promoted to mechanism.
