\subsection{Organism-specific meta-readback for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record treats the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates as a finite cross-organism readout table, not as a biological law. The assembled meta table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set used for the permutation checks. The complete rows are $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, $\mathrm{Mycobacterium\ smegmatis}$ str. MC2 155, $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli}$ K12 MG1655, $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $\mathrm{Gallus\ gallus}$, $\mathrm{Bacillus\ subtilis}$ str. 168, $\mathrm{Mus\ musculus}$, $\mathrm{Caenorhabditis\ elegans}$, $\mathrm{Drosophila\ melanogaster}$, $\mathrm{Sulfolobus\ solfataricus}$, and $\mathrm{Rattus\ norvegicus}$. Four named rows remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ panel: $\mathrm{Pseudomonas\ aeruginosa}$ PAO1, $\mathrm{Halobacterium\ salinarum}$, $\mathrm{Dictyostelium\ discoideum}$, and $\mathrm{Arabidopsis\ thaliana}$. The resulting object is therefore a bounded organism-level comparison of residual summaries against domain, GC3, tRNA-gene-count, and proteome-size covariates.

\paragraph{Domain and dominant-coordinate tests.}
The dominant-coordinate contingency table is computed on $n=12$ complete organisms with domains archaea, bacteria, and eukaryote, and with observed dominant coordinates $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_{3}\mathrm{\_stress}$. The Pearson statistic for the domain-by-coordinate table is $\chi^{2}=9.4$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ and right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ deterministic permutations. The finite counts are sparse: the archaeal row has one organism and is dominated by $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$; the bacterial row has three organisms and a one-count tie across $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, and $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, reported with $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$ as the selected top coordinate; the eukaryotic row has eight organisms and top count $3$ for $f_{3}\mathrm{\_stress}$. Thus the large nominal association size is not a resolved domain preference, because the permutation value is large and the row counts are too small for a phylogenetically independent domain claim.

\paragraph{Continuous covariates.}
The $M^{QTP}$ fraction was compared with GC3, log proteome size, and log tRNA-gene count on the same $12$ complete rows. The GC3 test gives Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation value $p=0.35264735264735264$. The log-proteome-size test gives $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$. The log-tRNA-gene-count test gives $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$. The domain-level $M^{QTP}$ fraction comparison gives a variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$, right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$, and group means
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{archaea}}&=0.01850668564589326,\\
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{bacteria}}&=0.4351192457799102,\\
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{eukaryote}}&=0.3960177769015294.
\end{aligned}
$$
The maximum-minus-minimum group-mean spread is $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest, but the permutation value keeps the result descriptive. Representative GC3 values also show why the finite table must be read organism by organism rather than as a single monotone rule: $\mathrm{Dictyostelium\ discoideum}$ has GC3 $0.14471498077638234$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$ has $0.3809678913179245$, $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$ has $0.5807133823494395$, $\mathrm{Drosophila\ melanogaster}$ has $0.6313215697461184$, and $\mathrm{Halobacterium\ salinarum}$ has $0.8944468418285529$.

\paragraph{Translation-efficiency and tRNA context.}
The organism-specific meta table is interpreted next to, rather than in place of, the direct translation-efficiency and modeled-adaptation checks. In the measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency panel, all four organisms used a rank-$23$ control matrix with $24$ control columns and $9$ residual coordinates. The joined TE row counts were $13140$ for $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $3415$ for $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli}$ K12 MG1655, $11554$ for $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, and $5128$ for $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$. The measured-TE absorption fractions were small: $0.01759429001506974$ for $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $0.0010982956489726373$ for $\mathrm{E.\ coli}$, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast. In the measured tRNA gene-copy tAI check, the full-join rows were $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ for zebrafish, human, and yeast, with tRNA gene counts $8846$, $431$, and $273$, respectively. The tRNA absorption fractions were $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$. These finite numbers support only bounded readbacks: they show how much of a previously computed residual component is described by the attached readout, not why the component exists.

\paragraph{Local verdict and limits.}
The finite-row verdict is that organism-specific heterogeneity is visible but unresolved. The table supports reporting the computed contingency, correlation, and permutation quantities as descriptive cross-layer summaries. It does not support a population-level survival law, a domain law, a GC3 mechanism, or a universal codon-usage principle. The small $n=12$ complete panel, the $16$-row assembled table with four incomplete organisms, and the absence of phylogenetic correction are decisive limits. No general translation claim, protein-folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, function claim, mechanism claim, or universality claim follows from this surface unless a separate layer-matched empirical contact supplies the relevant measurement and an explicit controlled statistic. In particular, a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate surviving or failing in one bounded translation-efficiency contact must not be promoted to protein abundance, structure, phenotype, or causal mechanism.
