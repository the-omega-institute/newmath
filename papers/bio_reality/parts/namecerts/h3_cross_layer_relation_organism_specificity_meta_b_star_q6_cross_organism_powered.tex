\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record treats the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates as a finite cross-organism measurement surface, not as a universal biological rule.  The carrier consists of organism-indexed rows whose coordinates are inherited from the nine residual directions

The verified cross-organism table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set used for the powered meta tests.  The complete rows are $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, $mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155$, $escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655$, $homo\_sapiens$, $danio\_rerio$, $gallus\_gallus$, $bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168$, $mus\_musculus$, $caenorhabditis\_elegans$, $drosophila\_melanogaster$, $sulfolobus\_solfataricus$, and $rattus\_norvegicus$. Thus the finite object read in this subsection is the $12$-row complete meta-surface, with the $16$-row table retained only as the larger assembly boundary.

\paragraph{Domain and dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The categorical test compares organism domain against the dominant residual coordinate.  The contingency table has $n=12$ rows and three domain classes.  The archaeal class has one row, with $Arg\_AGR=1$ and all other listed dominant-coordinate counts equal to $0$.  The bacterial class has three rows, with $Arg\_AGR=1$, $K\_AAA=1$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY=1$, and zero counts for $Ile\_AUA$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The eukaryote class has eight rows, with $Arg\_AGR=3$, $Ile\_AUA=1$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY=1$, $f3\_stress=3$, and zero counts for $K\_AAA$ and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$.  The resulting Pearson contingency statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2 &= 9.399999999999999,\\
V_{\mathrm{Cramer}} &= 0.6258327785172862,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,right}} &= 0.5814185814185814
\end{aligned}
$$
under $1000$ deterministic permutation trials.  The largest within-domain coordinate counts are $Arg\_AGR$ for archaea with $1/1$ row, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ for bacteria with $1/3$ rows, and $f3\_stress$ for eukaryotes with $3/8$ rows.  The finite table is therefore heterogeneous but does not support a resolved domain-specific dominant-coordinate enrichment at this permutation scale.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row conclusion is a descriptive organism-specificity statement.  The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual summaries vary across the sampled organisms, and the dominant-coordinate table has nontrivial heterogeneity, but the powered tests do not resolve a domain, GC3, tRNA-pool-size, or proteome-size explanation.  The strongest categorical association reported here has $V_{\mathrm{Cramer}}=0.6258327785172862$ but a right-tail permutation value of $0.5814185814185814$; the continuous tests have two-sided permutation values $0.35264735264735264$, $0.913086913086913$, and $0.8621378621378621$.  The correct reading is therefore: the cross-organism surface is assembled and testable, with finite residual-coordinate heterogeneity, but it remains exploratory and not phylogenetically corrected.

No translation realization, folding conclusion, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, mechanistic explanation, phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from this subsection.  A positive result at any of those layers would require a separate layer-matched contact and a separately declared statistic.  In particular, modeled tAI mediation is not ribosome profiling, $M^{QTP}$ and dominant-coordinate summaries are statistical projections rather than mechanisms, and shared ancestry can explain apparent domain or GC patterns unless a phylogenetically corrected design is added.
