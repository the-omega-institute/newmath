\subsection{Organism-Specific Meta-Readout for $B^*_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats organism specificity as a finite descriptive surface, not as a law of coding biology. The carrier is the table of organism-level summaries obtained from $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and their downstream projection summaries. The assembled meta-table contains $16$ organism rows. Of these, $12$ rows have the complete feature set required for the reported $M^{QTP}$ comparisons: \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Four rows remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ comparison because the required feature information is not present in this finite surface: \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. GC3 was computed for $15$ organisms, with \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana} the missing GC3 row in this snapshot.

\paragraph{Domain comparison.}
The domain comparison asks whether the organism-level $M^{QTP}$ fraction separates by broad biological domain. The finite groups are unbalanced: archaea has $n=1$, bacteria has $n=3$, and eukaryotes have $n=8$ among the complete rows. The group means are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline M_{\mathrm{archaea}} &= 0.01850668564589326,\\
\overline M_{\mathrm{bacteria}} &= 0.4351192457799102,\\
\overline M_{\mathrm{eukaryote}} &= 0.3960177769015294.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed ordering places bacteria above archaea by group mean, with max-minus-min group-mean difference $0.41661256013401693$. The permutation test used $1000$ deterministic trials and returned right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$ for the between-domain to within-domain variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$. Thus the table reports a visible descriptive spread but not a resolved domain enrichment claim.

\paragraph{Continuous covariates.}
The same $12$ complete rows were compared against GC3, log tRNA gene count, and log proteome size. For GC3, the reported Pearson statistic was
$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},\mathrm{GC3}) &= -0.3062616500469447,\\
|r| &= 0.3062616500469447,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,2s}} &= 0.35264735264735264.
\end{aligned}
$$
For log tRNA gene count, the corresponding values were $r=0.06029405206037823$, $|r|=0.06029405206037823$, and two-sided permutation $p=0.8621378621378621$. For log proteome size, the values were $r=-0.033628115789504955$, $|r|=0.033628115789504955$, and two-sided permutation $p=0.913086913086913$. All three tests used $1000$ permutation trials. The signs are retained as finite-row readout, but none of these rows licenses a monotone organism-level rule.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate contingency table uses $12$ complete rows and records the domain by leading residual coordinate. The contingency entries are: archaea has one \texttt{Arg\_AGR} row; bacteria has one \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{K\_AAA}, and one \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} row; eukaryotes have three \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, one \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and three \texttt{f3\_stress} rows. The Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The top coordinate by domain is \texttt{Arg\_AGR} for the single archaeal row, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} as one of the tied bacterial leaders over $3$ bacterial rows, and \texttt{f3\_stress} over $3$ of $8$ eukaryotic rows. This supports only a heterogeneous coordinate profile, not a domain-specific rule.

\paragraph{Measured-translation boundary.}
The organism-meta surface is kept separate from the direct translation-efficiency panel. In the measured TE computation, four organisms had matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency rows after joining to CDS data: \texttt{danio\_rerio} with $n=13140$, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} with $n=3415$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} with $n=11554$, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} with $n=5128$. Each had $24$ control columns and rank $23$ for the control design. The measured-TE absorption values were small: $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in budding yeast. These figures show that measured TE was computed on finite matched panels, but they do not convert the organism-meta correlations into a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{tRNA-proxy and localization boundary.}
The measured tRNA gene-copy tAI comparison supplies a separate bounded readout, again not a direct ribosome observation. The full-join organism counts were $14410$ for zebrafish, $19053$ for human, and $5815$ for budding yeast, with anticodon GCN table sizes $57$, $47$, and $41$, respectively. The absorbed fractions were $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for budding yeast. A separate localization-boundary computation joined $8696$ rows and found $657$ mitochondrial rows with $2000$ permutations run in $11.034382$ seconds. These layer-specific facts are useful as neighboring checks, but they remain distinct from the organism-specific meta claim.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite readout is therefore: $B^*_{Q6}$ organism-level summaries vary across the sampled organisms, and the observed table contains domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, proteome-size, dominant-coordinate, measured-TE, tRNA-proxy, and localization-boundary numbers. The computed permutation results do not support a resolved domain, GC3, tRNA-count, or proteome-size explanation. The result is descriptive and exploratory because $N=16$ is small, only $12$ rows are complete for the $M^{QTP}$ comparisons, and the sampled organisms are not phylogenetically independent. Shared ancestry, lineage-specific annotation depth, and dataset availability can all shape the apparent pattern.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter licenses no claim that a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate causes translation efficiency, implements a ribosome mechanism, predicts mature protein abundance, determines folding, establishes physical admissibility, realizes biological function, or states a universal biological law. Such statements would require their own layer-matched finite contacts and controls. Here the only positive content is the bounded organism-specific meta-readout: the table was assembled, the stated null comparisons were computed, and the resulting scalar values remain below the threshold for a mechanistic or universal interpretation.
