\subsection{Organism-Specific Structure of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Cross-Layer Readout}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates as a finite cross-organism measurement surface, not as a biological law.  The assembled table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete metadata needed for the permutation tests on $M^{QTP}$, domain, GC3, tRNA-pool size, proteome size, and dominant residual coordinate.  The complete rows are \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc$^{2}$ 155, \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, \emph{Mus musculus}, \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}.  Four rows remain outside the $M^{QTP}$ test surface because at least one required feature is missing: \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}, and \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana}.  The readout is therefore finite and explicit: a $12$-row powered meta-surface embedded in a larger $16$-row organism ledger.

\paragraph{Coordinates and response.}
The residual coordinates are the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates used by the paired cross-layer experiments: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The controls used in the linked translation and protein readout experiments include an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the measured-translation-efficiency panel, each organism has $24$ control columns and rank $23$ after control construction.  The joined measured-TE row counts are $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  These values fix the measured-translation contact as a finite regression surface rather than a free biological interpretation.

\paragraph{Domain comparison.}
The domain-level $M^{QTP}$ comparison is weak as an inferential claim, even though the finite rows show visible heterogeneity.  The group means are $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$.  The maximum-minus-minimum group-mean spread is $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest.  However, the deterministic permutation test gives right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials, and the variance ratio is only $0.8889980452277374$.  Thus the table licenses the statement that organism rows are heterogeneous, but it does not license a resolved domain rule.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{archaea}} &= 0.01850668564589326,\\
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{bacteria}} &= 0.4351192457799102,\\
\overline{M}^{QTP}_{\mathrm{eukaryote}} &= 0.3960177769015294,\\
p_{\mathrm{domain}} &= 0.4155844155844156.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Continuous metadata tests.}
The continuous metadata tests likewise remain descriptive.  Against GC3, the $M^{QTP}$ fraction has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$ on $n=12$ rows.  Against log proteome size, the correlation is almost absent: $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$.  Against log tRNA gene count, the direction is positive but small: $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The finite table therefore rejects a strong reading in which GC content, proteome size, or tRNA-pool size alone explains the organism-specific cross-layer summaries.  The actual GC3 values span a broad range, including $0.14471498077638234$ for \emph{D. discoideum}, $0.3061845898275272$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}, $0.3809678913179245$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $0.5642064879164637$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.5807133823494395$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, $0.8720662993842282$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.8806155914396909$ for \emph{P. aeruginosa}, and $0.8944468418285529$ for \emph{H. salinarum}; yet this range does not produce a powered GC3 explanation for $M^{QTP}$ on the complete $12$-row surface.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},\mathrm{GC3}) &= -0.3062616500469447, & p &= 0.35264735264735264,\\
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{proteins}}) &= -0.033628115789504955, & p &= 0.913086913086913,\\
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{tRNA}}) &= 0.06029405206037823, & p &= 0.8621378621378621.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate contingency table gives a second finite view of heterogeneity.  On $n=12$ complete rows, the Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V$ is $0.6258327785172862$, and the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The observed top coordinates are $Arg\_AGR$ for the single archaeal row, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ as one of the tied bacterial leading coordinates over $3$ bacterial rows, and $f3\_stress$ with count $3$ over $8$ eukaryotic rows.  The contingency table is therefore useful as a finite classification readback, but its permutation value prevents promotion to domain-specific enrichment.

\paragraph{Translation and candidate-layer context.}
The linked measured-translation-efficiency experiment supplies a boundary check rather than a promotion.  The modeled-complement fraction $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ on the measured-TE join is $0.8424312412078521$ in \emph{D. rerio}, $0.6628211177352172$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.8566190269974241$ in \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.22386566043976477$ in \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  The amount absorbed by measured TE is small in all four rows: $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  Measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI is also bounded: the absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5}$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.001530747712165342$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  Protein-feature and turnover contacts give further finite checks but no mechanism: for example, yeast turnover joins $3640$ proteins and absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled complement, while the localization boundary has $8696$ joined human rows with $657$ mitochondrial entries and $2000$ permutations.  These side contacts show that the organism-specific meta-surface is connected to measured layers, but they do not turn the meta-analysis into a causal path.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The organism-specific $B^{*}_{Q6}$ meta-readout is ready as a descriptive finite-row chapter.  Its positive content is the existence of a computed $16$-row organism ledger with a $12$-row complete test surface, explicit GC3 and domain metadata, permutation-tested $M^{QTP}$ comparisons, and a dominant-coordinate contingency table.  Its negative content is equally important: no tested scalar reaches a small permutation value, the archaeal group has only one complete row, the organisms are not phylogenetically independent, and the translation, tRNA, protein-feature, turnover, and localization side contacts are descriptive projections rather than interventions.  Consequently the chapter supports organism-specific heterogeneity of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ cross-layer summaries, but it does not establish translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, universality, or phylogenetically corrected inference.  Any such statement would require a separate layer-matched measurement surface with its own finite statistic.
