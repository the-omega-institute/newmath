\subsection{Organism-specific cross-layer profile of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The finite surface considered here is not a universal codon law. It is a row-bounded comparison of organism-specific $B^{*}_{Q6}$ summaries against coarse organism descriptors. The assembled meta table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set needed for the $M^{QTP}$ comparisons. The complete rows are \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. The remaining rows with incomplete $M^{QTP}$ data are \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. Thus the powered numerical claims below are restricted to the $12$ complete rows, while the broader row count records the intended organism panel.

\paragraph{Controlled translation-efficiency boundary.}
The translation-efficiency contact used upstream is a matched ribosome-profiling readout with mRNA abundance retained as a control. For the measured-TE experiment, the organism joins were $n=13140$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $n=3415$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $n=11554$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}. Each used $24$ control columns with rank $23$, including intercept, $\log$ CDS length, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline. The nine residual coordinates were \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}. The modeled complement values on the measured-TE joins were $d=0.8424312412078521$ for zebrafish, $d=0.6628211177352172$ for \textit{E. coli}, $d=0.8566190269974241$ for human, and $d=0.22386566043976477$ for yeast. The measured-TE absorption values were small: $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively. These numbers support a bounded descriptive statement only: measured translation efficiency was computed under mRNA control, but the row does not by itself establish a translation mechanism or any downstream protein-level consequence.

\paragraph{Domain and dominant-coordinate summaries.}
The domain comparison of $M^{QTP}$ reports group means $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$. The largest minus smallest group mean is $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest and archaea lowest, but the between-domain statistic has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials. Dominant-coordinate enrichment is similarly descriptive. The domain by coordinate contingency has Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The top dominant coordinate is \texttt{Arg\_AGR} for the single archaeal row, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} among the three bacterial rows with count $1$, and \texttt{f3\_stress} among the eight eukaryotic rows with count $3$. These finite counts are compatible with heterogeneity, not with a resolved domain-specific mechanism.

\paragraph{Measured tRNA readback.}
A separate measured tRNA gene-copy tAI comparison gives a useful calibration boundary. The complete full-join organism counts were $14410$ for zebrafish, $19053$ for human, and $5815$ for yeast, again with $24$ control columns and rank $23$. Parsed tRNA gene counts were $8846$, $431$, and $273$, respectively; anticodon table sizes were $57$, $47$, and $41$. The tRNA absorption of the modeled complement was $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast. These values are not ribosome-footprint observations; they only show that a bounded tRNA-copy-derived adaptation surface was compared against the residual complement.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter therefore licenses one narrow conclusion. Organism-specific $B^{*}_{Q6}$ cross-layer summaries can be assembled and compared with domain, GC3, tRNA-pool size, and proteome-size proxies on a finite panel. The observed statistics are exploratory: $N=16$ for the assembled meta surface, $N=12$ for the complete $M^{QTP}$ comparisons, no phylogenetic correction is applied, and the permutation tests do not isolate a stable domain, GC3, tRNA, or proteome-size explanation. No translation-realization claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, causal mechanism, phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from these rows. Any such stronger statement would require an additional layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
