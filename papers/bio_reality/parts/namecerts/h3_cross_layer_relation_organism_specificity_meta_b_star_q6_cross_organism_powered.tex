\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The cross-organism surface is a finite descriptive panel for organism-specific summaries of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  The assembled table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set needed for the reported $M^{QTP}$ comparisons. This chapter therefore treats $n=12$ as the powered comparison size for the permutation tests and $n=16$ as the broader assembled organism inventory.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate table.}
The domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency table uses six observed dominant coordinates: \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}. The bacterial row contains three organisms, one each in \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, and \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}.  The eukaryotic row contains eight organisms, with counts $3$ for \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, $1$ for \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, $1$ for \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and $3$ for \texttt{f3\_stress}.  The Pearson chi-square statistic for the domain by dominant-coordinate table is $9.4$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$.  Under the deterministic permutation null with $1000$ trials, the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814185814$.  Thus the finite table is visibly heterogeneous, but the permutation test does not license a resolved domain-specific dominant-coordinate enrichment.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter's conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite rows show that organism-specific $B^*_{Q6}$ summaries can be compared with domain, GC3, tRNA-pool size, proteome size, and dominant-coordinate labels under deterministic permutation nulls.  The resulting $p$-values are not small in the reported tests, and the sample is small, non-independent across phylogeny, and incomplete for four organisms.  Consequently, no claim of translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from this table.  Any such higher-layer assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite rows, controls, and statistic.
