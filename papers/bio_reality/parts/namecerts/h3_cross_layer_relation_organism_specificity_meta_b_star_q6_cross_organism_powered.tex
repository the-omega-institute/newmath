\subsection{Organism-specific residual power for the $B^*_{Q6}$ panel}
\label{sec:bior-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record concerns an organism-level summary of the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates after the amino-acid quotient has already removed the ordinary amino-acid-composition explanation.  The finite table has $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set needed for the reported meta-tests.  The complete rows are \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}.  Four organism rows, \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}, remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ comparison because at least one required meta-feature is absent.  The target variable is the organism-specific $M^{QTP}$ fraction, read as a descriptive projection fraction rather than as a molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Domain comparison.}
The domain test uses the $12$ complete-feature rows and compares $M^{QTP}$ fraction across archaea, bacteria, and eukaryotes by the ratio of between-domain mean square to within-domain mean square.  The observed variance ratio is $0.8889980452277374$, with a right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$ from $1000$ deterministic permutation trials.  The group means are heterogeneous but not resolved as a powered domain law: archaea has $n=1$ with mean $0.01850668564589326$; bacteria has $n=3$ with mean $0.4351192457799102$, minimum $0.08397602919861291$, and maximum $0.612922252793667$; eukaryotes have $n=8$ with mean $0.3960177769015294$, minimum $0.15488799907323988$, and maximum $0.9491551041216973$.  Thus the largest observed group mean is bacterial and the smallest is archaeal, giving max-minus-min group mean $0.41661256013401693$, but the permutation value does not license a domain-specific rule.

\paragraph{Continuous organism covariates.}
The same $12$ complete rows were compared against GC3, log proteome size, and log tRNA gene count using Pearson correlation with deterministic permutation nulls.  The GC3 comparison gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP}\!\operatorname{-fraction},\mathrm{GC3}) &= -0.3062616500469447,\\
|r| &= 0.3062616500469447,\\
p_{\mathrm{two\text{-}sided}} &= 0.35264735264735264,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $1000$ permutation trials.  The log-proteome-size comparison is essentially flat, with $r=-0.033628115789504955$, $|r|=0.033628115789504955$, and $p=0.913086913086913$.  The log-tRNA-gene-count comparison is also weak, with $r=0.06029405206037823$, $|r|=0.06029405206037823$, and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These finite-row values say that the observed $M^{QTP}$ fraction varies by organism, but the tested continuous covariates do not explain that variation at this sample size.

\paragraph{GC3 values and missing row.}
The GC3 surface itself is concrete.  Among the reported rows, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum} has GC3 $0.8944468418285529$, \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1} has $0.8806155914396909$, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155} has $0.8720662993842282$, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster} has $0.6313215697461184$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} has $0.5807133823494395$, \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus} has $0.5817750962555773$, \texttt{mus\_musculus} has $0.577302922262734$, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} has $0.5642064879164637$, \texttt{gallus\_gallus} has $0.5467395284798344$, \texttt{danio\_rerio} has $0.5450598668769285$, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168} has $0.4467139786396889$, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans} has $0.3973579615210404$, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} has $0.3809678913179245$, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus} has $0.3061845898275272$, and \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum} has $0.14471498077638234$.  The missing GC3 entry is \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}.  This missing row is one reason the complete-feature meta-test is smaller than the assembled organism table.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
A second finite surface records the dominant $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate by domain.  The contingency table has $n=12$ complete rows and six observed dominant-coordinate columns: $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\mathrm{\_stress}$.  The Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V$ is $0.6258327785172862$, and the right-tail permutation value is $p=0.5814185814185814$ from $1000$ trials.  The archaeal row contains one $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ call.  The bacterial row has one $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, one $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, and one $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$ call.  The eukaryote row has three $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ calls, one $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$ call, one $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$ call, and three $f_3\mathrm{\_stress}$ calls.  The top coordinate is therefore $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ for the single archaeal row, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$ by tie-selected top count among the three bacterial rows, and $f_3\mathrm{\_stress}$ among the eight eukaryote rows.  The effect is descriptive heterogeneity, not a resolved phylogenetic or domain mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation contacts.}
The organism-specific meta surface is intentionally downstream of more local translation-readout computations.  In the measured translation-efficiency contact, the same nine-coordinate residual basis was evaluated with controls consisting of an intercept, $\log$ CDS length, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline.  After adding matched translation-efficiency availability, the joined row counts were $13140$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $3415$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $11554$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $5128$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, with rank $23$ for the control matrix in all four cases.  The measured-TE absorption fractions were $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  In the measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact, the matched full joins were $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}; the corresponding tRNA absorption fractions were $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$.  These small descriptive absorption values help explain why the organism-level summary is treated as a meta-pattern over residual projections rather than as direct evidence of ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite result is therefore a bounded organism-specificity record.  It supports the statement that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual summaries can be assembled across organisms and compared to domain, GC3, tRNA-pool-size, and proteome-size proxies with explicit permutation nulls.  It does not support a universal codon-usage law, a translation mechanism, or a phylogenetically corrected inference.  The rows are not independent evolutionary replicates, and no phylogenetic comparative correction has been applied.  The quantities $M^{QTP}$, $R^2_{QP}$ where used by neighboring computations, and dominant-coordinate labels are statistical projections on finite readout tables.  No translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from this chapter unless another layer-matched contact supplies that measurement and its own controlled statistic.
