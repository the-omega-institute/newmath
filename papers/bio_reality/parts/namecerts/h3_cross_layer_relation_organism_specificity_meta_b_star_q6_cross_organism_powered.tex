\subsection{Organism-Specific Meta-Surface for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}\label{subsec:bioq6-organism-specific-meta}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-organism meta-surface for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The object under test is not a protein, a structure, a pathway, or a phenotype.  It is a row-indexed comparison surface whose rows are organisms and whose columns are derived summaries of the residual-coordinate experiments: the fraction assigned to the modeled translation-conditioned protein component $M^{QTP}$, the dominant residual coordinate in each organism, GC3, organismal domain, tRNA-gene-count scale, and proteome-size scale.  The assembled table contains $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ have the complete feature set used for the powered meta-tests.  The four rows withheld from the $M^{QTP}$ meta-test because of missing feature completion are \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}.  Thus the powered meta-statistics below are deliberately computed on the complete $n=12$ panel, while the broader bookkeeping surface still records the $16$-row scope.

\paragraph{Controlled readout context.}
The organism-specific surface is downstream of finite residual-coordinate readouts, but it is not itself a claim of translation mechanism.  In the measured translation-efficiency contact, four organisms had organism-matched CDS, abundance, and translation-efficiency rows: \texttt{danio\_rerio} with $n=13140$, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} with $n=3415$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} with $n=11554$, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} with $n=5128$.  Each used a $24$-column control design whose effective rank was $23$, with controls including CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The measured-TE absorption fractions were small on that finite panel: $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for budding yeast.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact supplied a separate bounded comparison, with full-join sizes $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ for zebrafish, human, and budding yeast, respectively, and absorption fractions $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$.  These rows justify treating organismal summaries as finite statistical summaries; they do not turn the meta-surface into a mechanistic translation assay.

\paragraph{Domain test for $M^{QTP}$.}
For the complete $n=12$ panel, the domain comparison used the variance ratio
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{domain}} &= 0.8889980452277374,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,right}} &= 0.4155844155844156.
\end{aligned}
$$
The group means were highly uneven in sample size: archaea had $n=1$ and mean $0.01850668564589326$, bacteria had $n=3$ and mean $0.4351192457799102$, and eukaryotes had $n=8$ and mean $0.3960177769015294$.  The largest-minus-smallest group-mean contrast was therefore $0.41661256013401693$, with bacteria highest by mean and archaea lowest.  Because the permutation probability is not small and the archaeal group has only one complete row, the correct reading is descriptive heterogeneity rather than domain-resolved biological law.

\paragraph{Continuous covariates.}
Three continuous meta-tests were computed on the same $12$ complete organisms.  Against GC3, $M^{QTP}$ had Pearson correlation
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{GC3} &= -0.3062616500469447,\\
|r_{GC3}| &= 0.3062616500469447,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,two}} &= 0.35264735264735264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against log proteome size, the corresponding values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\log N_{\mathrm{prot}}} &= -0.033628115789504955,\\
|r_{\log N_{\mathrm{prot}}}| &= 0.033628115789504955,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,two}} &= 0.913086913086913.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against log tRNA-gene count, they were
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\log N_{\mathrm{tRNA}}} &= 0.06029405206037823,\\
|r_{\log N_{\mathrm{tRNA}}}| &= 0.06029405206037823,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,two}} &= 0.8621378621378621.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite surface does not resolve a powered continuous predictor among GC3, proteome size, or tRNA-pool scale.  Several GC3 values are nevertheless retained as row-level coordinates of the surface: \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum} has GC3 $0.14471498077638234$, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} has $0.3809678913179245$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} has $0.5807133823494395$, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster} has $0.6313215697461184$, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155} has $0.8720662993842282$, and \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum} has $0.8944468418285529$.  These values show that the panel spans a broad GC3 range, while the permutation test says that this range did not become a resolved $M^{QTP}$ predictor in the complete-row analysis.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency was also computed on $n=12$ rows.  Its Pearson chi-square statistic was $9.399999999999999$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ and right-tail permutation probability $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ deterministic permutation trials.  The domain table was sparse: archaea contributed one \texttt{Arg\_AGR} dominant coordinate; bacteria contributed one \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{K\_AAA}, and one \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}; eukaryotes contributed three \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, one \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and three \texttt{f3\_stress} rows.  The top coordinates by group were therefore \texttt{Arg\_AGR} for archaea with count $1$ of $1$, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} for bacteria with count $1$ of $3$, and \texttt{f3\_stress} for eukaryotes with count $3$ of $8$.  The large Cramer's $V$ records a finite sparse-table effect size, but the permutation probability and small row counts prevent a resolved domain-preference conclusion.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The only licensed conclusion is that the organism-specific $B^{*}_{Q6}$ summaries form a finite, auditable comparison surface with computed null tests.  The surface reports row counts, complete-row restrictions, permutation probabilities, correlations, group means, and dominant-coordinate contingencies.  It does not establish translation realization, ribosome mechanism, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, phylogenetically corrected inference, or universality.  Any such claim would require a separate layer-matched measurement contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, the $12$ complete organisms are not phylogenetically independent replicates, no phylogenetic comparative correction is applied here, and the modeled translation-conditioned summaries remain statistical projections rather than mechanisms.
