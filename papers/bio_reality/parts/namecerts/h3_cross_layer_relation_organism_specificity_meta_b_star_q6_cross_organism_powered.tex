\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats organism specificity as a finite comparative surface over B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual summaries, not as a biological law. The assembled meta table has $16$ organism rows, of which $12$ rows have the complete feature set used for the reported $M^{QTP}$ tests. The complete rows are \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Four named organisms, \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}, remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ comparison set. The carrier is therefore a finite organism-by-summary table with explicit incompleteness, rather than a universal panel.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate heterogeneity.}
The dominant-coordinate contingency table has $n=12$ complete rows and compares domain labels against the dominant B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate. The computed Pearson chi-square statistic is $9.4$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ from $1000$ deterministic permutations. The observed domain counts are sparse: archaea contributes one row with \texttt{Arg\_AGR}; bacteria contributes one \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{K\_AAA}, and one \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}; eukaryotes contribute three \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, one \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and three \texttt{f3\_stress} rows. The top coordinate by domain is \texttt{Arg\_AGR} for archaea with $1/1$, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} for bacteria with $1/3$, and \texttt{f3\_stress} for eukaryotes with $3/8$. These numbers support heterogeneity of the finite table as a descriptive pattern, but the permutation value does not justify a resolved domain-enrichment claim.

\paragraph{Continuous meta readbacks.}
The $M^{QTP}$ fraction was compared with GC3, log proteome size, and log tRNA gene count on the same $12$ complete organisms. Against GC3, the Pearson correlation is negative with $r=-0.3062616500469447$, $|r|=0.3062616500469447$, and two-sided permutation value $p=0.35264735264735264$ using $1000$ permutations. Against log protein count, the correlation is nearly zero, $r=-0.033628115789504955$, $|r|=0.033628115789504955$, with $p=0.913086913086913$. Against log tRNA gene count, the correlation is also weak, $r=0.06029405206037823$, $|r|=0.06029405206037823$, with $p=0.8621378621378621$. The domain comparison for $M^{QTP}$ gives a variance ratio of $0.8889980452277374$ and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$. Its group means are $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea with $n=1$, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria with $n=3$, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes with $n=8$; the largest minus smallest group mean is $0.41661256013401693$. The finite numbers show that bacteria has the highest group mean in this table, but the tested contrast remains exploratory under the permutation null.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The conclusion of this chapter is narrow. The finite organism table reports descriptive heterogeneity, weak continuous associations, and explicit missing rows. It does not establish translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, phylogenetically corrected evolution, or universality. Shared ancestry is not corrected here, the complete comparison has only $12$ organisms, and the full assembly has only $16$ rows. Any stronger claim requires a separate layer-matched contact and a fresh statistic at that layer. In particular, a B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, a dominant-coordinate label, a GC3 correlation, an $M^{QTP}$ fraction, or a tAI-style modeled readout remains a finite statistical descriptor unless an independent translation, protein, structure, function, perturbation, or phenotype contact supplies the corresponding evidence.
