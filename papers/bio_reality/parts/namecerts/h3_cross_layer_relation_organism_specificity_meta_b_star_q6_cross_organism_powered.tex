\subsection{Organism-Specific Meta-Readback for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Cross-Layer Coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-organism-specificity-meta}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This record evaluates an organism-specific meta-readback for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates. The carrier is a finite table whose rows are organisms and whose columns summarize previously computed coordinate contacts: the fraction $M^{QTP}$ attributed to the translation-conditioned protein-abundance route, the dominant residual coordinate, organism domain, GC3, tRNA-pool proxy, and proteome-size proxy. The purpose is not to infer a biological law from codon geometry. The purpose is to ask whether the observed cross-organism summaries display detectable dependence on domain labels, GC3, tRNA gene-copy scale, or proteome size once the computation is restricted to rows with the necessary finite metadata.

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The assembled metadata surface contains $16$ organism rows. Of these, $12$ rows have the complete feature set needed for the $M^{QTP}$ comparisons: \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Four organisms remain outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ comparison because the needed meta fields are absent for this surface: \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana}. The row restriction is therefore explicit: the reported null tests are $n=12$ tests, while the broader assembled registry has $16$ rows.

\paragraph{GC3 coordinate.}
The GC3 column was computed for $15$ organisms, with \texttt{arabidopsis\_thaliana} still requiring data for this field. The observed GC3 values include $0.3809678913179245$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, $0.8720662993842282$ for \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, $0.5642064879164637$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $0.5807133823494395$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, $0.5450598668769285$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $0.5467395284798344$ for \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, $0.4467139786396889$ for \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, $0.577302922262734$ for \texttt{mus\_musculus}, $0.3973579615210404$ for \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, $0.6313215697461184$ for \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, $0.3061845898275272$ for \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, and $0.5817750962555773$ for \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}. Additional computed values outside the complete $M^{QTP}$ row set are $0.8806155914396909$ for \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, $0.8944468418285529$ for \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, and $0.14471498077638234$ for \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}. These values are treated as descriptive covariates, not as mechanisms.

\paragraph{Domain contrast for $M^{QTP}$.}
The domain-level comparison used a deterministic permutation null with $1000$ trials. The statistic was the ratio of between-domain mean square to within-domain mean square. The observed variance ratio was $0.8889980452277374$, with right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$ over $n=12$ complete rows. The group means were not equal in raw magnitude: archaea had $n=1$ and mean $0.01850668564589326$; bacteria had $n=3$ and mean $0.4351192457799102$, with minimum $0.08397602919861291$ and maximum $0.612922252793667$; eukaryotes had $n=8$ and mean $0.3960177769015294$, with minimum $0.15488799907323988$ and maximum $0.9491551041216973$. The largest observed group-mean gap was bacteria minus archaea, equal to $0.41661256013401693$. Because the permutation value is not small and the archaeal group has only one row, this surface supports a bounded description of heterogeneity, not a domain law.

\paragraph{Continuous covariates.}
Three Pearson-correlation tests were computed against $M^{QTP}$ on the same $n=12$ complete rows, each with $1000$ permutation trials. The GC3 comparison gave a negative correlation,
$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},GC3) &= -0.3062616500469447,\\
|r| &= 0.3062616500469447,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm},2\text{-sided}} &= 0.35264735264735264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The proteome-size comparison used a log transform for the protein-count predictor and gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{proteins}}) &= -0.033628115789504955,\\
|r| &= 0.033628115789504955,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm},2\text{-sided}} &= 0.913086913086913.
\end{aligned}
$$
The tRNA-pool comparison used a log transform for tRNA gene count and gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
r(M^{QTP},\log n_{\mathrm{tRNA\ genes}}) &= 0.06029405206037823,\\
|r| &= 0.06029405206037823,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm},2\text{-sided}} &= 0.8621378621378621.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite surface does not resolve a stable monotone association between $M^{QTP}$ and GC3, proteome size, or tRNA-copy scale. The correlations are reported because they are the actual finite readbacks, but their permutation values keep them in exploratory descriptive status.

\paragraph{Dominant-coordinate contingency.}
The dominant-coordinate table compares organism domain with the coordinate attaining the row-level dominant status. The contingency surface has $n=12$ rows and six observed coordinate labels: \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}. The archaeal row contributes one \texttt{Arg\_AGR}. The bacterial rows contribute one \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{K\_AAA}, and one \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}. The eukaryotic rows contribute three \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, one \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, one \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and three \texttt{f3\_stress}. Pearson's chi-square statistic on this table is $9.399999999999999$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ and right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ from $1000$ trials. The top coordinate by domain is \texttt{Arg\_AGR} for archaea with count $1$ of $1$, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} for bacteria with count $1$ of $3$, and \texttt{f3\_stress} for eukaryotes with count $3$ of $8$. The effect-size statistic is visibly nonzero, but the permutation test does not support a resolved domain-specific enrichment on this small and phylogenetically dependent panel.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency contacts.}
The meta-readback is deliberately separated from direct translation-efficiency survival. In the measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency computation, four organisms were joined to codon-count and matched mRNA data: \texttt{danio\_rerio} with $n=13140$, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} with $n=3415$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} with $n=11554$, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} with $n=5128$. Each used a control surface of $24$ columns with rank $23$, including length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline. The measured-TE absorption fractions were $0.01759429001506974$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}. These are descriptive projection quantities against measured TE, not causal mediation estimates. They also show why the organism-specific meta layer must not be read as a direct ribosome-occupancy result.

\paragraph{Relation to tRNA-copy tAI readbacks.}
A separate bounded translation-adaptation contact tested measured tRNA gene-copy tAI against the modeled-complement abundance residual on three full joins. The row counts were $14410$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $19053$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $5815$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, again with $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each organism. The tRNA gene counts were $8846$, $431$, and $273$, with anticodon-table sizes $57$, $47$, and $41$, respectively. The absorbed fractions were $0.020817102845637092$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $0.00005426229300865148$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $0.001530747712165342$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}. This contact supplies organism-level adaptation-weight readback, but it is not an observed ribosome-footprint matrix. Therefore the meta correlations with tRNA-pool scale remain covariate summaries and do not become translation mechanisms.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite organism-specific surface is ready as a bounded descriptive chapter. It establishes that the assembled $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ cross-layer summaries can be compared against organism domain, GC3, tRNA gene-copy scale, proteome-size scale, and dominant-coordinate labels on a $12$-row complete panel, while retaining a $16$-row registry for broader context. The actual tests do not yield a powered domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, or proteome-size rule: the domain test has $p=0.4155844155844156$, the GC3 correlation has $p=0.35264735264735264$, the log-proteome correlation has $p=0.913086913086913$, the log-tRNA correlation has $p=0.8621378621378621$, and the dominant-coordinate contingency has $p=0.5814185814185814$. The proper conclusion is heterogeneous organism-specific readback under a small, non-independent panel.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, phylogenetically corrected inference, or universal biological law follows from this meta table alone. A direct translation claim requires a same-organism or same-CDS translation readout with matched mRNA control and an explicit controlled survival statistic. A structure, function, physical, localization, stability, perturbation, or phenotype claim requires a separate layer-matched contact at that level. The present record is a finite statistical summary of organism-specific cross-layer coordinates and their covariates; it is not a promotion of residual-coordinate geometry into biology beyond the readouts actually computed.
