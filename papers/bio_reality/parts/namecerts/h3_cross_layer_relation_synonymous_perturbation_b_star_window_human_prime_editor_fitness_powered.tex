\subsection{Human Prime-Editor Synonymous-Window Fitness Audit}\label{sec:h3-human-prime-editor-synonymous-window-fitness}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite perturbation table whose rows are synonymous prime-editor edits grouped by gene-block folds.  The registered subject is the coordinate family named $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, but the biological readback in this chapter is deliberately narrower than a protein-realization claim.  A row may contribute to the audit only as a synonymous edit with a measured fitness readout and an attached coding-sequence context; amino-acid identity, gene identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline remain controls rather than promoted explanatory coordinates.  The finite computation for this packet used $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=1822$ genes, $n_{\mathrm{syn}}=9000$ synonymous edits in the main analysis surface, and $n_{\mathrm{syn,compact}}=24000$ synonymous rows in the compact extraction.  The held-out design was capped at $3000$ rows per cell line and used $B=200$ resampling draws.

\paragraph{Registered computation.}
Let $q_i$ denote one of the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and let $y$ denote the observed prime-editor fitness readout after the synonymous edit.  The row table is read as a controlled finite matrix rather than as a mechanistic path.  The fold design is gene-blocked: held-out rows come from genes not used for training, so direct gene dummy effects are intentionally absent from the predictive model.  In finite-row notation the registered scale is
$$\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 1822,\\
  n_{\mathrm{syn}} &= 9000,\\
  n_{\mathrm{syn,compact}} &= 24000,\\
  B &= 200,\\
  n_{\max\,\mathrm{per\ cell\ line}} &= 3000.
\end{aligned}$$
The runtime of the registered execution was $295.329$ seconds.  These numbers identify the actual finite audit surface for the human prime-editor record; they do not by themselves establish translation, abundance, folding, or phenotype realization.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
The same coordinate family has a separate multi-organism protein-abundance mediation computation in which the translation coordinate $T$ is modeled as per-protein mean tAI and the abundance coordinate $Y$ is $\log_{10}$ protein abundance.  That computation is useful here only as a boundary comparison.  It reports nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates and twelve organism rows with joined CDS and protein-abundance counts: for example $19053$ human proteins, $19395$ mouse proteins, $5815$ yeast proteins, $3739$ \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655 proteins, and $1656$ \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} proteins.  In the human row of that separate mediation surface, the total residual-to-abundance coefficient was $c=0.04454565118019448$, the direct coefficient after modeled tAI was $c'=0.03242643251185274$, and the mediated difference was $c-c'=0.012119218668341744$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$.  Those values show that a translation-conditioned protein-abundance matrix can be computed when the row surface contains $Q$, $T$, and $P$ together, but they are not imported as evidence that the human prime-editor fitness edits in this chapter realize protein abundance or molecular function.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
A further bounded translation-readout contact used modeled tAI weights from GtRNAdb tRNA copy numbers and dos Reis wobble parameters.  It computed nine synonymous residual coordinates, including $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The modeled matrix used three organisms, not a powered multi-species survival panel: $n_{\mathrm{org}}=3$.  It found $81$ entries above the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$, but the same record explicitly marks the powered-survival condition as unmet because three organisms are insufficient for the requested powered survival design.  Thus the human prime-editor chapter may mention the coordinate system and the perturbation rows, but it may not infer measured ribosome occupancy or translation survival from the modeled tAI contact.

\paragraph{Chapter-specific readback.}
The admissible conclusion is a finite perturbation audit: the human prime-editor data provide a gene-blocked synonymous-edit surface on which $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates can be tested against held-out fitness variation.  The row counts make the audit nontrivial, but the biological layer remains the assayed edit-fitness layer.  A direct association between a residual coordinate and the fitness readout is not a proof of translation mediation.  To promote such an association to a chain $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$, the same rows would need a measured or explicitly bounded translation readout $T_j$, matched protein abundance $P_k$, matched mRNA abundance, and the declared controls in the same finite design.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter therefore closes at a controlled synonymous-perturbation readback.  No statement here asserts ribosome dynamics, translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, selection, or universality.  Each of those layers would require its own matched contact and a new finite statistic.  In particular, the human prime-editor rows do not license a shortcut from $Q_i$ to protein realization; any future protein-layer claim must first show a surviving $Q_i\to T_j$ translation step and then a downstream $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ matrix under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and mRNA controls.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence is ready for inclusion as a bounded BioReality chapter because the table size, resampling count, gene-block fold rule, and execution time are all recorded.  The scientific claim is intentionally modest: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-window coordinates are being audited against human prime-editor fitness rows, not promoted to translation, protein, structure, function, or global biological law.
