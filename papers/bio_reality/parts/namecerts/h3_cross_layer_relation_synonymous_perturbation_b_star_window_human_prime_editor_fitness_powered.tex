\subsection{Human prime-editor synonymous-window fitness record}
\label{sec:biorality-human-prime-editor-synonymous-window-fitness}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The human prime-editor packet is recorded as a finite synonymous-perturbation surface rather than as a general statement about protein realization. Its rows are synonymous edited windows whose coding-sequence changes preserve amino-acid identity while exposing the edited segment to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates. The verified run uses $9000$ synonymous rows in the main analysis and a compact surface of $24000$ synonymous rows, grouped over $1822$ genes. The per-cell-line analysis cap is $3000$ rows, and the finite null schedule uses $B=200$ resamples. The run identifier is \texttt{b1a1a8c7baae}, with wall time $295.329$ seconds. The held-out design uses gene-block folds: test genes are unseen during training, so direct gene dummy fixed effects are not included in the predictive held-out design. This is a stronger separation than a row-random split because it asks whether the synonymous-window coordinates carry signal across gene boundaries rather than memorizing gene identity.

\paragraph{Coordinate meaning.}
The coordinate side is the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual family used throughout the packet: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The human prime-editor rows do not by themselves assert a translation step. They provide a finite perturbational readout in which synonymous edits are scored against a fitness-like output after the edited window is projected onto these residual axes. The correct mathematical object is therefore a controlled matrix of edited windows, genes, cell-line-limited row blocks, and held-out fold scores, not a direct assertion that a residual coordinate has become a protein-level mechanism.

\paragraph{Local model statement.}
Let $q_i$ denote one of the residual coordinates, let $e$ index a synonymous prime-editor window, and let $y_e$ denote the observed fitness readout attached to that edited row. The finite audit supports only a held-out predictive question of the following form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(e,i) &= \operatorname{score}(y_e \mid Z_e,q_i)-\operatorname{score}(y_e \mid Z_e),\\
Z_e &= \{\text{gene-block split},\text{cell-line cap},\text{synonymous edit eligibility},\text{baseline sequence controls}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The key design point is that the fold split is by gene. A positive held-out score, if present in the underlying run table, is therefore a perturbational association on unseen genes rather than a fit to gene labels. The available finite summary records the sample geometry and resampling discipline, not a row-level list of coefficients; consequently this chapter treats the human prime-editor contact as a bounded perturbational fitness surface.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
The broader translation-mediated protein-abundance packet gives the cross-layer comparison scale. In that run, $12$ organisms had joined CDS and protein-abundance rows, including $19053$ human proteins, $19395$ mouse proteins, $5815$ yeast proteins, and $3739$ \emph{E. coli} proteins. The joint $Q \to T \to P$ decomposition used the same nine residual coordinates and a modeled per-protein tAI readout. Human rows had $c=0.04454565118019448$, $c'=0.03242643251185274$, indirect difference $0.012119218668341744$, and mediated fraction $0.27206289160119157$. Yeast had a much larger mediated fraction, $0.9491551041216973$, while mouse had $0.15488799907323988$ and \emph{E. coli} had $0.612922252793667$. These values are useful as neighboring finite-row context, but they do not convert the human prime-editor fitness packet into a protein-abundance mediation claim. The prime-editor experiment lacks, in this summary, a same-row $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ matrix with translation readout $T_j$ and matched protein abundance $P_k$.

\paragraph{Perturbation neighbors.}
Three neighboring synonymous-perturbation records show why the human prime-editor packet should be kept in a narrow lane. The yeast synonymous-mutation record used $1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ genes, with $21$ CDS map hits, $B=200$, and a real-versus-proxy CSC correlation of $0.39717918221186976$; its real CSC control did not absorb the residual signal. The Chen 2017 GFP record used $3556$ variants, split as $1124$ in region 1 and $2432$ in region 2, with $B=200$ and an out-of-frame mRNA-to-protein slope of $0.016523671594873352$. The Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP record used $1459$ variants and reported $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits, matched and within null probabilities both $0.004975124378109453$, and a real CAI versus proxy correlation of $0.17884920449487354$. These neighboring records establish that synonymous perturbation can be audited at finite-row resolution, but each readout remains tied to its own assay and controls.

\paragraph{Negative and boundary context.}
Several linked audits prevent over-reading the human prime-editor result. The turnover audit over $3290$ genes gave $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$, so the current data give no evidence that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts measured turnover beyond expression, translation-efficiency, structure, and N-end controls. The mass-balance audit over $120$ genes and $718$ intervals similarly had $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$. The cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; it found $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$ but leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$ against a null $95$ threshold of $0.05427157066522958$, so the strong selection-imprint gate was not passed.

\paragraph{What the record can claim.}
The admissible conclusion is that a finite human prime-editor synonymous-window surface exists with enough rows to support a gene-block held-out audit of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates against a fitness readout. The certified row geometry is large for perturbation work: $9000$ main synonymous rows, $24000$ compact synonymous rows, $1822$ genes, $3000$ maximum rows per cell line, and $200$ resamples. This supports a bounded statement about synonymous-window perturbational association under the recorded split design. It also gives a concrete human perturbation contact to compare with yeast, GFP, and \emph{E. coli} synonymous perturbation records.

\paragraph{What the record cannot claim.}
No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance mediation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection law, or universality follows from this finite prime-editor summary alone. Such a claim would require a separate layer-matched contact: for translation, same-row translation readouts such as ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency with matched mRNA controls; for protein abundance, matched proteomics; for structure, a structure-level measurement or validated prediction surface; for function or phenotype, a functional or organismal assay. A direct $Q_i \to y_e$ association on synonymous prime-editor rows is not a substitute for a surviving $Q_i \to T_j$ step, and a fitness-like readout is not by itself a molecular mechanism. The chapter therefore records the human prime-editor packet as a controlled perturbational fitness surface whose higher-layer interpretation remains conditional on additional contacts.

\paragraph{Audit status.}
The finite facts are sufficient for a self-contained BioReality name certificate: the carrier, row counts, fold discipline, resampling count, and neighboring finite-row boundaries are all explicit. The certificate is not a causal claim. It is a bounded record that the human prime-editor synonymous-window dataset can host a controlled $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ perturbation audit while preserving the separation between synonymous-coordinate association and downstream biological realization.
