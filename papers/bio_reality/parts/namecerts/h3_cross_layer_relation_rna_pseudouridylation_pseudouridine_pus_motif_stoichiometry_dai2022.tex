\subsection{Pseudouridine stoichiometry and the Q6 RNA-decay boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-pseudouridine-pus-q6-rna-decay-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record treats the Dai-style pseudouridine table as a finite RNA-modification contact, not as a completed decay-mechanism experiment.  The available row set for the pseudouridine claim contains $2040$ measured rows, with repository run identifier \texttt{cf233b5f0446} and recorded runtime $4.859092$ seconds.  These rows are sufficient to keep a pseudouridine stoichiometry surface in view, but they do not by themselves contain the same transcript window, the same-row translation readout, the matched RNA half-life value, and the perturbation/rescue fields required for a decay-factor RBP bridge.  A separate RBP-binding contact exists in the surrounding finite ledger with $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases under run \texttt{b225890251c2}; that contact is useful as evidence that binding contacts can be made finite, but it is not joined here to the pseudouridine windows as a measured decay-factor occupancy panel.  A translation contact also exists as an independent finite translation surface with $6494$ rows under run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}; it does not supply RNA half-life or RBP perturbation rows for this pseudouridine packet.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.}
Each admissible row is interpreted as a transcript or coding-region window carrying a residual synonymous coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, together with whatever pseudouridine stoichiometry measurement is present in the finite table.  The intended positive bridge would require the row to be enlarged to a tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i,\psi_i,T_i,H_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i,N_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the residual coordinate class, $\psi_i$ is the local pseudouridine or PUS-related stoichiometry readout, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ is the target-RBP loss arm, $W_i$ is the wild-type rescue arm, $B_i$ is a binding-defective rescue arm, and $N_i$ is a non-target control arm.  The present finite data instantiate only the pseudouridine side of this shape.  The missing fields are not cosmetic; without them the coordinate can mark an RNA-modification association, but it cannot be promoted to a translation-conditioned RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The local null is row exchangeability after the declared controls.  In words, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are treated as exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life once amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout have been controlled.  A positive half-life difference would still be insufficient unless it tracks the externally prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and then obeys the perturbation logic.  Formally, the bridge condition is not the single association
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longmapsto H_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
but the finite pattern in which occupancy separates the high-coordinate and low-coordinate windows, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it.  Any result lacking this pattern remains a bounded RNA-stability or pseudouridine-stoichiometry association.

\paragraph{What the present rows support.}
The $2040$ pseudouridine rows support a finite modification-level surface for asking whether local pseudouridine or PUS-related measurements co-vary with a residual coordinate description.  They can also motivate a future joined panel, because the contact is already finite and row-addressable.  The row count is large enough to make a matched-panel design meaningful in principle, but the present scalar summary does not report effect sizes, $p$-values, rank sufficiency, module classes, hit rates, lists of responsive regions, or decay-bridge coefficients.  Therefore the chapter records a bounded readiness claim: the pseudouridine surface can be carried into a cross-layer test only after the missing translation, half-life, occupancy, loss, rescue, and control arms are joined on the same rows.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation realization follows from pseudouridine stoichiometry alone.  No RNA half-life mechanism follows from RNA half-life alone.  No decay-factor RBP mechanism follows from a half-life association unless measured local occupancy changes in the required window, the target-RBP loss arm removes the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails.  The attached RBP-binding and translation ledgers show that neighboring contact types can be represented by finite rows, but they do not license a shortcut across the missing join.  In particular, this record does not claim protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those claims would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.

\paragraph{Operational test.}
A positive follow-up must build one matched panel in which each transcript window carries the residual coordinate label, pseudouridine or PUS stoichiometry, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control, and the declared covariates.  The decisive statistic is a translation-conditioned half-life residual that survives the ordinary transcriptional and compositional controls only when the RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern is present.  If the half-life signal persists without occupancy change, survives target-RBP loss, fails wild-type rescue, or is restored by a binding-defective or non-target construct, the bridge is rejected and the coordinate remains only a finite descriptor of an RNA-modification or RNA-stability surface.
