\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP-bridge test}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-pseudouridylation-rbp-half-life}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record considered here is the claim
$\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_pseudouridylation.pseudouridine\_pus\_motif\_stoichiometry\_dai2022}$, read as an ai-discovered finite-row BioReality target rather than as a general molecular law.  The available verified finite computation for the pseudouridylation contact has $n=2040$ rows and completed in $4.859092$ seconds under run identifier $\mathrm{cf233b5f0446}$.  These rows certify that the pseudouridylation-side packet has a finite measured surface, but the recorded scalars do not by themselves include same-row translation efficiency, RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective controls.  Therefore the chapter treats the $2040$ rows as a finite coordinate-bearing contact that can be joined into a stronger test only after the missing readout and perturbation columns are supplied.

\paragraph{Coordinate and quotient.}
Each candidate row is a matched transcript or CDS row carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $q_i$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The high-versus-low contrast is not a protein claim; it is a residual label contrast on coding sequence windows.  The row may carry a pseudouridylation or PUS-motif stoichiometry surface, but that surface is not promoted to RNA decay mechanism without an attached half-life and RBP perturbation panel.  The intended joined row has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_i = (&q_i,\; a_i,\; T_i,\; H_i,\; O_i,\; L_i,\; W_i,\; B_i,\; C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $a_i$ is the amino-acid quotient class, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $W_i$ records wild-type rescue, $B_i$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue controls, and $C_i$ contains transcription, abundance, isoform, sequence-composition, batch, and local-structure controls.  The finite carrier is the subset of rows for which these coordinates are identifier-matched in the same organism and editable RNA window.

The pseudouridylation computation supplies the finite count $n=2040$ for the Dai-style pseudouridine/PUS-motif stoichiometry surface.  A separate RBP-binding contact in the same BioReality ledger reports $900$ total peak rows, $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds under run $\mathrm{b225890251c2}$.  That second contact is useful only as evidence that finite RBP-binding surfaces can be represented in the ledger; it is not the required same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy for the present claim.  A translation-side contact reports $n=6494$ rows for the Thoreen mTOR translation readout and runtime $9.469$ seconds under run $\mathrm{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$.  That translation contact likewise shows that finite translation readouts can be represented, but it is not a joined same-row $T_i$ column for the $2040$ pseudouridylation rows.  The current record is therefore ready as a bounded chapter because it has concrete finite row counts for the relevant contact families, while its positive cross-layer verdict remains conditional on the missing matched panel.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels after the required controls and after conditioning on translation.  In finite-row notation, for a prespecified statistic $S$ comparing high-coordinate rows to low-coordinate rows,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 H_0:\quad
 S(q_i,H_i\mid T_i,C_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i)
 &= S(\pi(q_i),H_i\mid T_i,C_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\pi$ ranges over label permutations that preserve amino-acid class, transcript or CDS matching, batch strata, and the declared control strata.  The null is not defeated by an RNA half-life difference alone.  It is defeated only by a finite pattern in which the coordinate-associated half-life residual follows measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
The positive criterion has four finite checks.  First, the residual coordinate contrast must survive amino-acid quotienting and the controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout.  Second, the effect must be coupled to measured local occupancy of a decay-factor RBP chosen before inspecting the coordinate contrast.  Third, target-RBP loss must reduce or erase the half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect while binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  Written as a bounded decision rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Bridge}=1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 &\Delta_H(q;T,C)\neq 0,\\
 &\Delta_O(q;T,C)\neq 0,\\
 &\Delta_H(q;T,C,L=\mathrm{loss})\approx 0,\\
 &\Delta_H(q;T,C,W=\mathrm{wildtype})\approx \Delta_H(q;T,C),\\
 &\Delta_H(q;T,C,B=\mathrm{binding\mbox{-}defective\ or\ non\mbox{-}target})\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The displayed rule is a finite acceptance boundary, not a claim that the present payload already contains all five terms.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback sought by this chapter is a matched-panel statement: high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows are compared only after amino-acid identity is held fixed and after the same row carries translation readout, RNA half-life, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and negative rescue controls.  If the half-life residual appears but the occupancy column is unchanged, the result remains an RNA-stability association.  If occupancy differs but target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life residual, the result remains an occupancy association.  If loss collapses the signal but wild-type rescue fails, the perturbation may be nonspecific.  If binding-defective or non-target rescue restores the signal, the proposed RBP bridge is rejected because the effect is not tied to the intended binding event.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA decay mechanism follows from a half-life association unless the same-window RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern is present.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the $2040$ pseudouridylation rows, from the $900$ RBP-binding peak rows, from the $6494$ translation rows, or from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry.  Each of those stronger conclusions would require its own layer-matched contact and a declared finite statistic on rows that actually contain the relevant readout.  The present chapter licenses only a bounded formulation of the test and records why the positive RBP-bridge interpretation is unavailable until the same-organism, same-window joined panel is supplied.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The BioReality entry is ready as a self-contained finite-row chapter because the relevant ledger contains concrete row counts and runtimes for the pseudouridylation, RBP-binding, and translation contact families: $2040$ pseudouridylation rows, $900$ RBP-binding peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, and $6494$ translation rows.  The substantive biological verdict is deliberately narrower: the observed payload is sufficient to state the finite test, the null, the controls, and the rejection conditions, but it is not sufficient to assert an RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge.  A positive bridge requires the missing same-row half-life, translation, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue columns on the same finite panel.
