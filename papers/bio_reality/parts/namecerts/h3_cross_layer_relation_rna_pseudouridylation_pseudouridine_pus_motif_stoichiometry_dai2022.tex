\subsection{Pseudouridine Stoichiometry and the Translation-Conditioned RNA-Half-Life Boundary}
\label{subsec:bioreality-pseudouridine-pus-motif-stoichiometry-dai2022}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is the finite pseudouridylation panel indexed by the claim
\texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_pseudouridylation.pseudouridine\_pus\_motif\_stoichiometry\_dai2022}.  Its verified run is \texttt{cf233b5f0446}, with $2040$ parsed rows and runtime $4.859092$ seconds.  The biological surface of the row object is a collection of RNA sites with pseudouridine-related readout, PUS-motif context, and stoichiometry-bearing site information.  In the present chapter this finite panel is not treated as a protein-output, RNA-decay, or translation-mechanism assay.  It is treated as a bounded RNA-modification table that can be asked whether a stronger cross-layer interpretation is licensed only after the necessary matched readouts have been supplied.

The intended cross-layer question is stricter than the existence of pseudouridine sites.  The row carrier would have to join, on the same transcript or coding sequence rows, four classes of information: a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and measured occupancy or perturbation evidence for an externally prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the relevant editable RNA window.  The finite panel available in the verified run supplies the pseudouridylation-side row count, but it does not by itself supply the joined decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue rows needed for a positive RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Coordinate distinction.}
The mathematical distinction is between two finite classes of synonymous residual coordinates after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  Write $Q_{\mathrm{hi}}$ for rows carrying a high-coordinate residual label and $Q_{\mathrm{lo}}$ for rows carrying a low-coordinate residual label.  A permissible RNA-half-life statistic would compare these two classes only after conditioning on the declared controls.  In schematic finite-row notation the admissible comparison has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= H(Q_{\mathrm{hi}} \mid Z,T,O)
     - H(Q_{\mathrm{lo}} \mid Z,T,O),\\
Z
  &= \{\hbox{transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC/dinucleotide composition,}\\
  &\qquad \hbox{codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch,}\\
  &\qquad \hbox{amino-acid composition}\},\\
T
  &= \hbox{same-row translation readout},\\
O
  &= \hbox{measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $H$ denotes the RNA half-life readout after the stated residualization.  The symbol $O$ is not a motif-density proxy and not an inferred binding label from sequence geometry; it is a measured local occupancy row for the declared RBP.  Without $O$, the statistic can at most report a finite RNA-stability association, not an RBP-mediated decay bridge.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
A positive bridge requires a finite perturbation pattern, not just an association.  The required pattern is: the coordinate-associated half-life effect follows experimentally decoupled measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP; the effect collapses after loss of that target RBP; the effect returns under wild-type target-RBP rescue; and the effect is not restored by binding-defective rescue or by a non-target control.  In finite-row terms, if $E$ is the translation-conditioned half-life effect, the bridge condition requires the directional readback
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{occupied}} &\neq E_{\mathrm{reduced\ occupancy}},\\
E_{\mathrm{target\ loss}} &\approx 0,\\
E_{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx E_{\mathrm{occupied}},\\
E_{\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}} &\not\approx E_{\mathrm{occupied}},\\
E_{\mathrm{non\ target\ rescue}} &\not\approx E_{\mathrm{occupied}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are row-level requirements on the experimental panel.  A pseudouridine stoichiometry table with $2040$ rows can motivate where such a panel should be assembled, but it does not satisfy the perturbation pattern unless those occupancy and rescue columns are present on the same finite matched rows.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and after same-row translation conditioning.  Formally, the null says that the relabeling
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{hi}} \leftrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{lo}}
\end{aligned}
$$
leaves the controlled RNA half-life distribution invariant once transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and translation readout have been fixed.  If a half-life difference remains but the measured RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern is absent, the result is still not a decay-factor RBP bridge.  It remains a bounded finite association between a coordinate label and an RNA half-life readout.

\paragraph{Comparison within the RNA-modification audit batch.}
The same audit ledger contains a larger RNA-modification row object for the m6Am flanking-identity stoichiometry claim, with $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, $3230$ low-tail entries below $0.8$, $20942$ rows in the variable subset, and saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$.  That neighboring fact is useful only as a scale reference for row availability in RNA-modification surfaces: a large stoichiometry table may carry strong finite site information and still not license a translation, decay-factor RBP, or protein-output interpretation without the layer-matched contacts.  The present pseudouridine panel is smaller, at $2040$ rows, and the verified scalar data reported for it do not include the RBP-occupancy, loss, rescue, p-value, lambda, module, hit-rate, or matched half-life quantities required for a positive bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded RNA-modification surface, not a completed cross-layer mechanism.  The $2040$ verified pseudouridine rows do not by themselves establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, RNA decay mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  A stronger conclusion would require a separate finite matched panel carrying the same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance control, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared statistic under the full control set.  Until that panel exists, the mathematically correct status is a finite pseudouridine stoichiometry record with a sharply delimited route toward a possible RNA-half-life RBP bridge, not evidence that such a bridge has crossed the experimental boundary.
