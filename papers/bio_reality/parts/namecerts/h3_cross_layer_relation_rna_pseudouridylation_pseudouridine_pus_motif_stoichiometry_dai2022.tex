\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP-bridge target}\label{sec:biorality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel whose rows are indexed by coding-transcript windows.  Each admissible row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured or externally prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy at the editable RNA window, and matched controls.  The biological subject is not pseudouridine chemistry by itself, and it is not a general codon-usage law.  It is the narrower question whether a $B^*_{{Q6}}$ residual-coordinate window can be joined to a translation-conditioned RNA half-life readout through a decay-factor RBP bridge on one finite row table.

The available verified finite-row fact for the named pseudouridylation surface is the run
$$
\begin{aligned}
  N_{\psi} &= 2040,\\
  t_{\psi} &= 4.859092\,\mathrm{s},\\
  \mathrm{run}_{\psi} &= \texttt{cf233b5f0446}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers certify that the present paper has a concrete finite RNA-modification surface for the claim identifier, with $2040$ rows processed in the cited run.  They do not, by themselves, certify the RBP-decay mechanism.  They only fix the size and provenance of the finite surface on which the stricter bridge test would have to be evaluated.

\paragraph{Coordinate and null comparison.}
For each row $r$, write $q(r)$ for the residual-coordinate label, $T(r)$ for the attached translation readout, $H(r)$ for RNA half-life, and $O(r)$ for local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The coordinate comparison is made after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, so the primary split is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels rather than between different encoded proteins.  The null model is row-exchangeability of the coordinate label after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout.

In finite-row form the positive bridge would require a nonzero half-life contrast only in the part of the panel where the RBP bridge also changes:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
  &= \mathbb{E}\{H(r)\mid q(r)=\mathrm{high},T(r),Z(r)\}
   - \mathbb{E}\{H(r)\mid q(r)=\mathrm{low},T(r),Z(r)\},\\
  \Delta_O
  &= \mathbb{E}\{O(r)\mid q(r)=\mathrm{high},T(r),Z(r)\}
   - \mathbb{E}\{O(r)\mid q(r)=\mathrm{low},T(r),Z(r)\},\\
  Z(r) &= \text{the declared transcription, composition, structure, isoform, abundance, batch, and sequence controls.}
\end{aligned}
$$
A half-life contrast with $\Delta_O=0$ remains an RNA-stability association, not an RBP bridge.  Likewise, a local occupancy contrast without target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure remains an occupancy association rather than a mechanism assignment.

\paragraph{Readback requirements.}
The finite readback has five required signs.  First, the translation boundary must be present on the same row, so $H(r)$ is interpreted only after conditioning on $T(r)$.  Second, the local decay-factor RBP occupancy must change at the editable RNA window in a way that is experimentally decoupled from the coordinate label alone.  Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore it.  Fifth, binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to restore it.  The panel is therefore not satisfied by motif density, RNA structure, transcript abundance, GC content, codon-pair context, or batch separation alone.

The verified surrounding RNA-modification runs show why this stricter reading is necessary.  A nearby mRNA $m^6A$ stoichiometry surface contains $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites, a cross-condition-concordant classification, and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$ in run \texttt{2e8583415e99}.  A separate $m^6Am$ stoichiometry surface contains $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, a variable subset of $20942$, a low-tail count of $3230$ below $0.8$, and a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at the $0.95$ threshold in run \texttt{eb689b7dc73c}.  Its recorded partial correlations include $0.268094$ on the full set, $0.168335$ on the variable subset, and $0.098666$ on the low-tail subset, with a GC-matched null probability $0.000833$.  These finite RNA-modification facts demonstrate that modification-level row tables can carry strong scalar structure, but they also illustrate the boundary: stoichiometry, concordance, and positional identity do not automatically supply RBP perturbation, rescue, or RNA-decay mechanism rows.

\paragraph{Bounded conclusion.}
The present claim is therefore ready only as a bounded BioReality chapter, not as a positive RBP-mechanism result.  The finite pseudouridylation run supplies $N_{\psi}=2040$ rows for the named surface, and the linked RNA-modification records supply additional finite scalar comparators for stoichiometric row behavior.  What is licensed is a precise test form: a $B^*_{{Q6}}$ residual coordinate may be evaluated against translation-conditioned RNA half-life only when the same row also carries the translation readout and the full RBP occupancy, loss, rescue, and control pattern.  If the half-life effect persists without measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, or if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not behave as required, the row table remains a bounded RNA-stability association.

No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate geometry, the pseudouridylation row count, the half-life contact name, the mRNA-control route, or the translation-readout route.  Each of those stronger assertions would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic on matched rows.
