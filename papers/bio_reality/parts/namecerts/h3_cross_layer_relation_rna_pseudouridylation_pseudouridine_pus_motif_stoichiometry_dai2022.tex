\subsection{Pseudouridine stoichiometry as a bounded RNA-surface record}
\label{sec:bioreality-pseudouridine-pus-motif-stoichiometry-dai2022}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This chapter treats the Dai-style pseudouridine table as a finite RNA-modification surface, not as a general mechanism claim.  The operative rows are transcript-local RNA windows carrying a pseudouridine or candidate pseudouridine readout, an associated PUS-motif or PUS-context label where available, and the finite identifiers needed to keep transcript, coordinate, and experimental provenance attached to the same row.  The verified finite run for the surface
$h3.cross_layer_relation.rna_pseudouridylation.pseudouridine_pus_motif_stoichiometry_dai2022$ contains $2040$ rows and completed in $4.859092$ seconds.  Those two numbers are the entire hard scalar payload available for this chapter: the evidence is row-bounded and coordinate-bounded, with no attached same-row translation-efficiency matrix, no RNA half-life matrix, and no perturbation-rescue panel.

\paragraph{Claim being bounded.}
The biological question is whether a synonymous residual coordinate window, after the amino-acid quotient, can be promoted from an RNA-modification descriptor to a cross-layer statement involving translation-conditioned RNA half-life and an experimentally specified decay-factor RBP.  In finite-row terms, such a promotion would require a joined panel in which the same transcript or CDS row carries all of the following: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, the local pseudouridine or PUS-context readout, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and a binding-defective or non-target rescue control.  The available pseudouridine surface supplies only the RNA-modification side of that proposed panel.  It therefore licenses a finite statement about pseudouridine/PUS-context stoichiometry, but it does not by itself license an RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Local statistic form.}
Let $i$ range over the $2040$ finite pseudouridine rows.  A minimal row may be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_i=(\tau_i,c_i,u_i,p_i,s_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\tau_i$ is the transcript or mapped RNA identifier, $c_i$ is the local coordinate, $u_i$ is the pseudouridine or candidate-pseudouridine readout, $p_i$ is the PUS-motif or PUS-context label when present, and $s_i$ is the study/provenance state used to keep the row inside the finite panel.  The admissible within-surface comparison has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\Psi,PUS}
    &= \widehat{\mathbb{E}}(u_i\mid p_i=1,Z_i)
       -\widehat{\mathbb{E}}(u_i\mid p_i=0,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ denotes transcript-local controls that are actually present or reconstructible for the finite table.  This expression is only a pseudouridine/PUS-context contrast.  It is not a translation contrast, not a half-life contrast, and not an RBP-loss/rescue contrast unless the missing columns are supplied by a separate matched data source.

\paragraph{Cross-layer gate.}
The proposed cross-layer relation would need a stronger statistic, schematically
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{Q6\to H\mid T,RBP}
    &= \widehat{\mathbb{E}}(H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i,Z_i)
       -\widehat{\mathbb{E}}(H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $W_i$ records wild-type rescue, and $B_i$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The Dai-style pseudouridine row count of $2040$ supplies neither $H_i$ nor $T_i$ nor $O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i$.  Consequently the chapter keeps the positive object at the RNA-modification surface and assigns the RBP-mediated RNA half-life reading to an unmet join condition.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the relevant controls are attached.  In symbols, the intended null can only be tested on a larger joined panel:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i \perp q_i
  \mid T_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,B_i,Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the present pseudouridine table, the correct finite conclusion is weaker: the $2040$ rows can be used to describe RNA-modification occupancy or stoichiometry against PUS-context labels, but they cannot decide whether the same coordinate changes RNA half-life through a decay-factor RBP.  If a half-life difference were later observed without measured local RBP occupancy, loss collapse, wild-type rescue restoration, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure, that difference would still remain an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Controls required for promotion.}
A positive cross-layer reading would have to survive transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the mandatory same-row translation readout.  It would also need an experimentally decoupled RBP step: the occupancy change cannot merely follow the coordinate definition, the target-RBP loss must collapse the half-life effect, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail to restore it.  These requirements are not decorative exclusions; they are the difference between a finite RNA-coordinate association and a mechanism-bearing RNA-decay claim.

\paragraph{Comparison to neighboring finite RNA surfaces.}
The scalar payload here is deliberately smaller than other RNA-modification surfaces already represented as finite rows.  The m6A exon-architecture surface $h3.cross_layer_relation.rna_m6a_stoichiometry.m6a_stoichiometry_exon_architecture_glori$ reports $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance marked true, a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$, and runtime $78.316$ seconds.  The m6Am flanking-identity surface $h3.cross_layer_relation.rna_m6am_methylation.m6am_flanking_identity_stoichiometry_crownseq$ reports $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, cross-cell concordance marked true, a saturated-at-least-$0.95$ fraction of $0.343223$, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, $20942$ variable-subset rows, a GC-matched null $p$-value of $0.000833$, and runtime $324.086$ seconds.  Those examples show what a finite RNA-row surface can record when scalar tests are present.  The pseudouridine chapter has a real row count, but it does not yet have comparable scalar evidence for translation conditioning, half-life mediation, or RBP perturbation.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from the pseudouridine/PUS table alone.  No ribosome occupancy, protein output, folding state, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the $2040$ finite pseudouridine rows.  No RNA half-life association may be interpreted as a decay-factor RBP mechanism unless the same-row translation boundary and the measured occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls are present.  The chapter therefore records a bounded RNA-modification surface and an explicit unmet bridge condition: the available rows are suitable for pseudouridine/PUS-context readback, while the translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP claim remains blocked until a separate matched panel supplies the missing contacts.
