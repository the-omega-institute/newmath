\subsection{Pseudouridine Stoichiometry and the Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life RBP Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-pseudouridine-pus-motif-stoichiometry-dai2022}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is the finite pseudouridine stoichiometry panel indexed by the claim identifier $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_pseudouridylation.pseudouridine\_pus\_motif\_stoichiometry\_dai2022$.  The verified run $cf233b5f0446$ supplies $2040$ finite rows and completed in $4.859092$ seconds.  These rows are suitable for a bounded pseudouridine-site readback: they can hold site-level modification calls, local sequence or motif context, and row identifiers for transcript- or CDS-level joins.  They do not, by themselves, contain the full cross-layer panel needed for a translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge.  In particular, the available row count certifies a finite surface for pseudouridine-associated site analysis, not a measured decay-factor RBP perturbation chain.

\paragraph{Coordinate question.}
The intended biological question is whether a synonymous residual coordinate class, denoted here by $B^*_{Q6}$, can be related to RNA half-life only after the amino-acid quotient has removed ordinary protein-sequence explanation and after the same row also carries a translation readout.  The finite row type therefore has four logically separate coordinates: a high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label, a same-row translation measurement, an RNA half-life measurement, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation trace.  The relation is not the assertion that pseudouridine sites cause RNA decay.  It is the stricter statement that a coordinate-associated half-life effect may be called an RBP bridge only when the same editable or readable RNA window shows measured local occupancy by the named decay factor, loss of the target RBP collapses the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.

\paragraph{Finite statistic.}
For a finite matched panel $P$, let each row $r \in P$ carry a residual coordinate $q(r)$, translation readout $T(r)$, RNA half-life $H(r)$, control vector $Z(r)$, and, when present, RBP evidence $O(r)$ for local occupancy and $L(r)$ for perturbation or rescue state.  The only admissible positive bridge statistic has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q) &= H(r) \;\text{residualized against}\; Z(r), T(r), \text{and abundance controls},\\
\Delta_O(q) &= O(r) \;\text{at the same RNA window},\\
B(q) &= \bigl(\Delta_H(q),\Delta_O(q),L_{\mathrm{loss}}(q),L_{\mathrm{wt\ rescue}}(q),L_{\mathrm{defective\ rescue}}(q)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
The bridge is bounded and finite: it is a row-level decision rule over a matched panel, not a global law.  A half-life association remains below the bridge threshold if the same-row translation field is absent, if RBP occupancy is not measured at the relevant window, if the RBP perturbation does not remove the effect, if wild-type rescue is missing, or if a binding-defective or non-target rescue also restores the signal.

\paragraph{Controls.}
The null model keeps high- and low-coordinate labels exchangeable after controlling amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the mandatory translation readout.  This is a strong null because it blocks the usual shortcuts: a difference in RNA half-life cannot be promoted to translation realization, and a pseudouridine motif or stoichiometry pattern cannot be promoted to a decay-factor RBP mechanism without the measured occupancy and perturbation rows.  The pseudouridine panel contributes $2040$ rows to the site-level finite surface, but the scalar payload for this record contains no $R$ list, no $M$ list, no $\lambda$ value, no $p$-value, no module classification, and no hit-rate field for the proposed RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
The boundary is calibrated against other verified finite surfaces in the same evidence ledger.  The RBP binding check $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode$ reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases in run $b225890251c2$, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  That fact shows that finite RBP-binding rows can be represented as a separate contact, but it is not the same as same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue for the pseudouridine claim.  The RNA $m^6A$ stoichiometry check $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6a\_stoichiometry.m6a\_stoichiometry\_exon\_architecture\_glori$ reports $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance equal to true, and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$ in run $2e8583415e99$.  That comparison demonstrates that larger modification-stoichiometry panels can carry finite concordance and confound fields, but it does not fill the missing RBP perturbation fields for the pseudouridine case.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded readback rather than a positive mechanism.  A compliant future row set would have to join, on the same organism and the same RNA window or transcript/CDS row, the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, the pseudouridine or editable-site measurement, translation efficiency or ribosome occupancy, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Only after that join exists may the statistic ask whether the half-life residual follows the RBP occupancy trace and disappears or returns under the declared perturbation arms.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, decay mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the $2040$ pseudouridine rows.  No such conclusion follows from motif density, codon-window geometry, transcript abundance, RNA half-life alone, or workflow readiness.  The present finite result is narrower: the Dai-associated pseudouridine panel is a concrete row surface for modification-site analysis, and the proposed translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge remains blocked until same-row translation, half-life, RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue data are simultaneously present and evaluated under the declared controls.
