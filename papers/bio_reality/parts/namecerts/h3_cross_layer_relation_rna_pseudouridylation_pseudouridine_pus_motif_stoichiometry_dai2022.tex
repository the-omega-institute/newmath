\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel whose rows carry a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls for transcriptional and sequence composition effects. The biological object is not a free claim about pseudouridylation or codon usage in general. It is the narrower question whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ windows can separate an RNA half-life residual only when a measured decay-factor RBP occupancy route is present and experimentally decoupled from the coordinate itself.

The available pseudouridylation finite table for the Dai-style surface contains $2040$ parsed rows and was computed in $4.859092$ seconds under run $\mathrm{cf233b5f0446}$. This supplies a concrete row scale for the modification-side surface, but it does not by itself contain same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue rows. As a neighboring RBP contact scale, the RBNS/eCLIP binding surface reports $900$ total peak rows, with cross-RBP concordance equal to $3$, computed in $223.648043$ seconds. As a neighboring RNA-modification stoichiometry scale, the m6A stoichiometry surface reports $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites, a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$, and cross-condition concordance marked true. These numbers show that finite RNA modification and RBP binding surfaces are available in the research memory, but they are not yet the same joined panel required for the present RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
Let a row be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
r=(q,T,H,O,L,W,C),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q$ is the high-or-low residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $H$ is the RNA half-life readout, $O$ is local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L$ is the target-RBP loss condition, $W$ is the wild-type rescue condition, and $C$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline controls. The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after conditioning on $T$ and $C$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H \perp q \mid (T,C).
\end{aligned}
$$
A finite half-life separation may be recorded as an RNA-stability association only if this exchangeability fails in the declared statistic. It becomes an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation only under the stricter condition that the same finite rows also show a local occupancy change for the specified decay-factor RBP, collapse of the coordinate-associated half-life effect under target-RBP loss, return of the effect under wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Positive readback criterion.}
The intended finite statistic is not a marginal contrast of RNA half-life against codon geometry. It is a conditioned bridge test. A positive readback must have the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= E[H\mid q=\mathrm{high},T,C]-E[H\mid q=\mathrm{low},T,C],\\
\Delta_O &= E[O\mid q=\mathrm{high},T,C]-E[O\mid q=\mathrm{low},T,C],
\end{aligned}
$$
with the additional finite-row interventions showing that the half-life contrast is lost when the target RBP is removed and restored only by the wild-type RBP. A binding-defective rescue or non-target control must fail to restore the effect. The coordinate is therefore allowed to index the row comparison, but it is not allowed to choose the mechanism after the fact. The RBP must be specified before the bridge is asserted, and the occupancy, loss, and rescue rows must be part of the same joined panel or a declared matched panel with stable identifiers.

\paragraph{What the current rows support.}
The current verified pseudouridylation surface supports only a finite RNA-modification-side row count of $2040$ and a reproducible computation time of $4.859092$ seconds for that parsed surface. The current RBP-binding surface supports a separate finite binding-contact scale of $900$ total peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases. The current m6A stoichiometry surface supports a separate high-cardinality RNA-modification comparison scale of $60000$ rows and $49668$ distinct sites. None of these finite surfaces contains the full tuple $(q,T,H,O,L,W,C)$ for this conjecture. Consequently, the chapter records a bounded target with a live null: a half-life difference without measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure remains an RNA half-life association, not an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Local limitation.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RBP mechanism follows from a half-life difference unless the measured occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern are present. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate labels, pseudouridylation rows, RNA half-life rows, RBP-binding rows, or translation-conditioning language. Those claims require their own layer-matched contacts and finite statistics. The present record is therefore ready as a self-contained bounded statement of the RNA half-life RBP-bridge criterion, while the positive bridge remains unclaimed until the joined finite panel is supplied.
