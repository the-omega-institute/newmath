\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-fourcase-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record is a two-organism finite matrix test for the $9$ residual coordinates of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ against two matched readouts: a translation-side score $T$ and a protein-abundance score $P$.  The coordinates used in the finite surface are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-stress}$.  The translation readout in this run is not ribosome profiling; it is the per-protein modeled tAI average
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g) &= \frac{\sum_{c} n_g(c)\,w(c)}{\sum_c n_g(c)},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_g(c)$ is the codon count for the coding sequence of protein $g$ and $w(c)$ is the organism-scoped tAI weight derived from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with the dos Reis wobble rule.  The protein readout is $P(g)=\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\ ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to the same CDS rows.  The run uses real CDS codon counts and residualizes $Q$, $T$, and $P$ against the same finite control family: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in both organisms.

\paragraph{Rows and rank.}
Two organisms pass the finite-row gate.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the run uses $3739$ joined CDS/protein rows, with $3739$ proteins used and the tRNA organism key \texttt{escherichia\_coli}.  After residualization, the residual degrees of freedom are $3716$, the rank of the residualized $Q$ matrix is $9$ for both the $P$ and $T$ fits, and the design is marked powered-rank sufficient.  The residual energy of $P$ is $4020.333671802061$, while the residual energy of $T$ is $0.4728468725814029$.  The $Q$-explained energy on $P$ is $432.89050065533684$, and the $Q$-explained energy on modeled tAI is $0.256128714706293$.

For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the run uses $5815$ joined CDS/protein rows, with $5815$ proteins used and the tRNA organism key \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  The residual degrees of freedom are $5792$, the rank of the residualized $Q$ matrix is again $9$ for both the $P$ and $T$ fits, and the powered-rank flag is true.  The residual energy of $P$ is $2958.1865890925337$, while the residual energy of $T$ is $6.90537888244409$.  The $Q$-explained energy on $P$ is $882.6526210803958$, and the $Q$-explained energy on modeled tAI is $5.671703404506426$.  Thus the classification is not an $n=3$ saturated artifact and is not an all-$1.0$ rank-degenerate surface; both organisms have thousands of rows and full residualized $Q$ rank.

\paragraph{Discriminant rule.}
The four cases are assigned by the signs of the controlled translation and protein associations and by whether the protein association is reduced after conditioning on the translation readout.  The numerical positivity threshold is $10^{-12}$.  In words, case A is null on both sides, case B is translation-side survival without protein-side survival, case C is protein-side survival without a translation-conditioned mediation drop, and case D is the translation-conditioned pattern in which $Q$ survives against $T$, survives against $P$, and the direct $Q\to P$ component is reduced after including $T$.  The run reports the aggregate surfaces
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:\quad R^2_{QP} &= 0.10767526677985896, & R^2_{QT} &= 0.5416736993691318,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:\quad R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827, & R^2_{QT} &= 0.8213457220900503.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both organisms therefore have positive modeled-translation and measured-protein association surfaces for the $9$-coordinate block.  The finite classification is complete: there are no unclassified coordinates in either organism.

\paragraph{Four-case outcome.}
The \emph{E. coli} finite surface has case counts
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 0, & B &= 0, & C &= 0, & D &= 7,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $2$ additional positive-positive coordinates recorded as positive on both $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ but without the required mediation drop.  Those two entries are deliberately not promoted to case D, because the translation-conditioned reduction test is the distinguishing condition.  The \emph{S. cerevisiae} finite surface has
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 0, & B &= 0, & C &= 0, & D &= 9,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $0$ positive-positive-without-drop entries.  This makes the yeast matrix a complete modeled-tAI-conditioned case-D surface over all nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates, while the \emph{E. coli} matrix is a mixed positive surface with seven case-D coordinates and two positive-positive entries that fail the mediation-drop condition.

\paragraph{Relation to measured ribosome-efficiency rows.}
The same coordinate family has separate measured-TE survival records, but those records are not identical to the modeled-tAI four-case surface above.  In the measured-TE run, $S^{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$ is computed from ribosome footprint divided by mRNA.  The measured-TE associations are $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, with $9$ surviving coordinates in each of the four measured-TE organisms.  The measured-TE/protein mediation run reports measured mediated fractions $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.0$ for zebrafish after clipping a negative raw value.  These measured rows support the existence of a separate ribosome-TE contact, but the four-case classification in this chapter remains the modeled-tAI discriminant unless explicitly recomputed with measured TE in the same four-case rule.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance boundary.}
The direct protein-omics association is real as a finite correlation surface, but it is not itself a translation mechanism.  The broader direct $S^{QP}$ run over measured PAXdb abundance gives positive block-level protein associations across $15$ organisms, including $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.04613529807085213$ for zebrafish, and $0.03980793596647631$ for chicken, with $9$ surviving coordinates in each listed organism.  Those direct $Q\to P$ entries are downstream prediction surfaces.  They become translation-conditioned entries only when the same row system includes a translation readout and the conditioning calculation shows that $T$ absorbs or reduces the direct $Q\to P$ component.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
This finite record is ready as a bounded four-case discriminant over modeled tAI and measured protein abundance.  It supports the following chapter-local statement: after the stated residualization, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate block is not protein-null and not modeled-translation-null in the two powered organisms tested; yeast assigns all nine coordinates to case D, while \emph{E. coli} assigns seven coordinates to case D and leaves two positive-positive entries outside the mediation-drop condition.  The classification is therefore a finite readback of controlled association and attenuation, not a biological mechanism by itself.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter licenses a claim of observed ribosome dynamics from modeled tAI, folded structural order from protein abundance, physical admissibility from codon residuals, molecular function from a case label, organismal phenotype, perturbation rescue, evolutionary direction, or universal biological law.  Modeled tAI is a bounded translation-side score, not direct ribosome occupancy.  PAXdb abundance is a steady-state protein-omics readout, not isolated translation rate, folding, turnover, localization, post-translational state, or pathway function.  A higher-layer conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic on the same rows.
