\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-translation-conditioned-four-case-discriminant-b-star-q6-fourcase-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record fixes a finite regression surface whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns are the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each row also carries a protein-abundance readout $P$ and a translation-side readout $T$.  In this particular discriminant, $P$ is $\log_{10}$ abundance in ppm from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts, while $T$ is a bounded per-protein modeled tAI summary computed as the codon-count-weighted mean of GtRNAdb tRNA-copy and dos Reis wobble weights.  The tAI readout is therefore a modeled adaptation contact, not a ribosome-profiling measurement.

\paragraph{Controls and rank.}
The discriminant was computed for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The E. coli join contains $3739$ CDS/protein rows, with $3739$ proteins used and the tRNA organism read as Escherichia coli.  The yeast join contains $5815$ CDS/protein rows, with $5815$ proteins used and the tRNA organism read as Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  Both fits residualize the $Q$, $T$, and $P$ quantities against a $24$-column control design whose numerical rank is $23$.  After controls, the E. coli surface has residual degree count $3716$, $T$ residual energy $0.4728468725814029$, $P$ residual energy $4020.333671802061$, $X$-energy on $T$ equal to $0.256128714706293$, and $X$-energy on $P$ equal to $432.89050065533684$.  The yeast surface has residual degree count $5792$, $T$ residual energy $6.90537888244409$, $P$ residual energy $2958.1865890925337$, $X$-energy on $T$ equal to $5.671703404506426$, and $X$-energy on $P$ equal to $882.6526210803958$.  In both organisms the residualized $Q$ design has rank $9$ against both $T$ and $P$, and the powered-rank flag is true.

\paragraph{Four-case rule.}
The finite classifier uses the threshold $10^{-12}$ and records the sign of the controlled $Q \to T$ survival, the sign of the controlled $Q \to P$ survival, and whether conditioning on $T$ reduces the direct $Q \to P$ association.  At the organism level the two headline surfaces are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT}(E.\ coli) &= 0.5416736993691318, & R^2_{QP}(E.\ coli) &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QT}(S.\ cerevisiae) &= 0.8213457220900503, & R^2_{QP}(S.\ cerevisiae) &= 0.29837624994140827.
\end{aligned}
$$
The four-case readback is complete: there are no unclassified coordinates in either organism.  In E. coli the case counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=7$, with $2$ additional positive-positive coordinates that do not pass the mediation-drop condition.  In yeast the case counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=9$, with no positive-positive coordinate left outside the mediation-drop call.  Thus the finite table supports a strong modeled-tAI-conditioned case-$D$ pattern in yeast and a mostly case-$D$ pattern in E. coli, while preserving two E. coli coordinates as positive for both readouts without a recorded attenuation by $T$.

\paragraph{Relation to direct protein survival.}
The direct protein-omics survival surface is not empty: in the larger PAXdb scan, all $15$ listed organisms have $9$ surviving $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates after the same coordinate construction and sibling controls.  The direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.10767526677985896$ for E. coli, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.04613529807085213$ for zebrafish, $0.0770952887136557$ for Drosophila, $0.10217906758013379$ for C. elegans, and $0.061906245886660105$ for Bacillus subtilis.  These direct protein associations justify running the four-case discriminator, but they do not by themselves identify a translation path.  The point of the four-case table is precisely to separate protein-abundance prediction from a translation-conditioned attenuation test.

\paragraph{Measured-TE comparison.}
A separate measured-TE record keeps the modeled tAI result from being overread.  Using ribosome-profiling TE, the measured $S^{QT}$ values are $0.009394266754822262$ for E. coli, $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, each with $9$ surviving coordinates.  The corresponding joins contain $3415$ E. coli genes, $5128$ yeast genes, $11554$ human genes, and $13140$ zebrafish genes with measured TE.  The measured mediation record reports $M^{QTP}$ fractions $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0$ for zebrafish after clipping a raw suppressive value of $-0.04801486048721136$.  This confirms that the four-case modeled-tAI table is useful as a discriminant, while measured ribosome-efficiency contacts must be read separately when the claim is about observed translation efficiency.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite readback is therefore ready as a rank-powered four-case discriminant over two organism-specific modeled-tAI/protein-abundance panels.  The strongest local statement is that yeast places all nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates in case $D$ under the modeled-tAI rule, while E. coli places seven coordinates in case $D$ and leaves two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation-drop call.  The computation is not a perturbation experiment and it is not a causal mediation proof.  No direct ribosome-dynamic translation claim, no folding claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no molecular-function claim, no mechanism claim, and no universal biological law follows from this table unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies the relevant contact and the corresponding controlled statistic is computed.  Protein abundance remains steady-state omics output, modeled tAI remains a bounded translation-side proxy, and the case labels remain finite statistical readbacks rather than biological mechanisms.
