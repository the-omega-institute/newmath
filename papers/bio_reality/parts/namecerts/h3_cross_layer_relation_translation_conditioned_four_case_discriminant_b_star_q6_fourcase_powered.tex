\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discrimination for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-four-case-discriminant}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record evaluates a two-readout finite matrix whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns are the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The experiment identifier is $f37bd98e0739$, verified on 2026-06-27.  The translation-side readout is a per-protein modeled tAI average,
$$\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac{\sum_{c} n_c\,w_c}{\sum_c n_c},
\end{aligned}$$
where $n_c$ is the CDS codon count and $w_c$ is the GtRNAdb all-tRNA, dos-Reis-wobble tAI weight.  The protein-side readout is $P=\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The two organism panels are $E.\ coli$ K12 MG1655 with $3739$ joined CDS/protein rows and yeast with $5815$ joined rows.  In $E.\ coli$, the CDS organism key is $escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655$ while the tRNA organism key is $escherichia\_coli$; in yeast both the CDS and tRNA organism keys are $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$.

\paragraph{Residualization and rank.}
Both $T$ and $P$ are residualized against the same finite control surface: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The control column count is $24$ in both organisms and the residualized control rank is $23$.  The residualized $Q$ design has full rank for both target readouts: in $E.\ coli$, $rank(\widetilde X_Q\,\mathrm{for}\,P)=9$ and $rank(\widetilde X_Q\,\mathrm{for}\,T)=9$ with residual degrees of freedom $3716$; in yeast, the corresponding ranks are also $9$ and $9$ with residual degrees of freedom $5792$.  The degenerate-fit cutoff is $0.999999$, and both organisms are marked rank-sufficient rather than rank-saturated.  The finite residual energies are materially different across readouts: in $E.\ coli$, $Q$ explains $432.89050065533684$ units of $P$ energy but only $0.256128714706293$ units of $T$ energy, with residual energies $4020.333671802061$ for $P$ and $0.4728468725814029$ for $T$.  In yeast, $Q$ explains $882.6526210803958$ units of $P$ energy and $5.671703404506426$ units of $T$ energy, with residual energies $2958.1865890925337$ for $P$ and $6.90537888244409$ for $T$.

\paragraph{Four-case rule.}
The classification threshold is $10^{-12}$.  Each coordinate is assigned by the signs of the translation-side survival $S^{QT}$, the protein-abundance survival $S^{QP}$, and the attenuation test obtained after conditioning the direct $q_i \to P$ fit on the matched translation readout.  The aggregate readout strengths are
$$\begin{aligned}
E.\ coli: \quad R^2_{QT} &= 0.5416736993691318, & R^2_{QP} &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
S.\ cerevisiae: \quad R^2_{QT} &= 0.8213457220900503, & R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827.
\end{aligned}$$
The case table is fully classified with no unassigned coordinate.  For $E.\ coli$, the counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=7$, with $2$ additional coordinates positive for both $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ but not showing the mediation drop required for the $D$ call.  For yeast, the counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=9$, and there are no positive-positive coordinates lacking the mediation drop.  Thus the finite modeled-tAI discriminant reports a strong translation-conditioned pattern in yeast and a mostly translation-conditioned pattern in $E.\ coli$, while explicitly separating two $E.\ coli$ positive-positive direct associations from the stricter mediated class.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation readouts.}
The four-case run uses modeled tAI, not ribosome profiling.  A separate measured-TE survival run over ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA reports $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, each with all nine coordinates surviving by the same positive threshold.  A further measured-TE protein-abundance decomposition reports measured mediated fractions $0.04091158340950673$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0$ for zebrafish.  These companion finite rows make the interpretation sharper: the modeled-tAI four-case table is a discriminant over an explicitly bounded translation proxy, while measured-TE rows are the stricter contact for ribosome-based mediation.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The claim is supported at the level of a finite, controlled, rank-sufficient readout table: the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are not merely direct protein-abundance predictors in this panel; for yeast all nine coordinates and for $E.\ coli$ seven coordinates pass the modeled-translation-conditioned $D$ condition.  The two remaining $E.\ coli$ positive-positive entries are retained as direct or insufficiently attenuated associations rather than being promoted to mediation.  This record therefore certifies a four-case readback over matched CDS, modeled tAI, and PAXdb abundance rows, under the stated controls and threshold.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome-occupancy mechanism, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, phenotype claim, causal pathway, or universal biological law follows from this table alone.  Modeled tAI is a bounded translation-layer annotation, not a direct ribosome measurement.  PAXdb abundance is a steady-state protein-omics readout and is not isolated from mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, or pathway context.  Any stronger claim requires a separate layer-matched contact and a finite statistic on the same rows or on an explicitly justified matched panel.
