\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourcase-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite matrix surface.}
The record tests a finite cross-layer table in which each row is a coding sequence with real codon counts, a matched modeled translation-readout coordinate, and a matched measured protein-abundance value.  The residual coordinate block is the same nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ surface used across the surrounding protein-abundance analyses: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Each coordinate is residualized against the same finite control family: an intercept, coding-sequence length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The translation-side readout in this discriminant is not ribosome profiling.  It is the per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g) &= \frac{\sum_c n_g(c)\,w(c)}{\sum_c n_g(c)},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_g(c)$ is the codon count in gene $g$ and $w(c)$ is the organism-scoped tAI weight from tRNA gene-copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb abundance joined to the same coding-sequence rows.

\paragraph{Powered rows and ranks.}
Two organisms enter this particular four-case table.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the joined matrix has $3739$ coding-sequence/protein rows, with the tRNA organism set to \emph{Escherichia coli}.  After residualization, the control rank is $23$ with $24$ control columns, the residual degrees of freedom are $3716$, and the nine-coordinate block has rank $9$ both against the protein readout and against modeled tAI.  The residual energy of the protein readout is $4020.333671802061$, the residual energy of the modeled-tAI readout is $0.4728468725814029$, the explained energy of $Q$ on $P$ is $432.89050065533684$, and the explained energy of $Q$ on $T$ is $0.256128714706293$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined matrix has $5815$ rows.  The control rank is again $23$ with $24$ control columns, the residual degrees of freedom are $5792$, and the nine-coordinate block has rank $9$ for both readouts.  The corresponding residual energies are $2958.1865890925337$ for protein abundance and $6.90537888244409$ for modeled tAI, with $Q$ explaining $882.6526210803958$ units on $P$ and $5.671703404506426$ units on $T$.  Both organisms are therefore nondegenerate for the declared finite-row question: the coordinate rank is full, the residual degrees of freedom are large, and the classification is not an all-fitted small-rank artifact.

\paragraph{Four-case rule.}
The discriminant assigns each coordinate by the signs of $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ together with the behavior of the direct $q_i \to P$ signal after conditioning on the translation readout.  The threshold for a positive entry is $10^{-12}$.  The intended interpretation is local to this matrix:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &: S^{QT} \le 0,\; S^{QP} \le 0,\\
B &: S^{QT} > 0,\; S^{QP} \le 0,\\
C &: S^{QT} \le 0,\; S^{QP} > 0,\\
D &: S^{QT} > 0,\; S^{QP} > 0\text{ with translation-conditioned attenuation.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The separate counter $positive\_positive\_without\_mediation\_drop$ records the stricter warning case where both signs are positive but the modeled translation readout does not remove or reduce the direct protein-abundance signal in the required way.  In \emph{E. coli}, the computed global partial-$R^2$ values are $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ and $R^2_{QT}=0.5416736993691318$ across the nine coordinates.  The case table contains $0$ entries in $A$, $0$ in $B$, $0$ in $C$, $7$ in $D$, and $2$ positive-positive entries without the mediation drop.  No coordinate is left unclassified.  In yeast, $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ and $R^2_{QT}=0.8213457220900503$; all nine coordinates fall in $D$, with no unclassified coordinate and no positive-positive entry lacking the mediation drop.  Thus the finite readback is not merely that modeled tAI and protein abundance are each associated with the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block.  The sharper statement is that yeast gives a full nine-coordinate translation-conditioned attenuation pattern under the modeled-tAI readout, while \emph{E. coli} gives seven coordinates in the same class and two coordinates that remain direct protein-omics predictions rather than modeled-tAI mediated entries.

\paragraph{Relation to measured ribosome-efficiency rows.}
The modeled-tAI table must be kept distinct from measured ribosome-profiling TE.  A separate measured-TE computation gives $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, each with all nine coordinates surviving under the same positive threshold.  The measured TE/protein mediation table is also separate: the measured mediated fraction is $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0$ for zebrafish after clipping a negative raw value.  Those numbers show that measured TE can carry part of the abundance relation, especially in yeast, but they do not retroactively turn the modeled-tAI four-case labels into observed ribosome dynamics.  The present chapter records the modeled-tAI discriminant and uses the measured-TE results only as a comparison boundary.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance context.}
The direct protein-omics surface is broad.  Across the larger PAXdb panel, the direct $Q \to P$ residual association has positive $R^2_{QP}$ in every listed organism, including $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, and $0.02763998603083845$ for rat, with nine surviving coordinates in each of those entries.  This matters for the four-case readback because a direct protein-abundance association alone is not enough to assign a translation-conditioned class.  It can be a protein-omics prediction, a readout of mRNA abundance, a turnover effect, a localization effect, a condition-specific confounder, or a mixture of unmeasured processes.  The four-case table is therefore useful precisely because it separates direct $Q \to P$ survival from the stricter pattern in which a declared translation readout participates in the attenuation of that direct association.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row result is ready at the level stated here.  For the modeled-tAI readout, both organisms are rank-powered and fully classified: yeast has $9/9$ coordinates in case $D$, and \emph{E. coli} has $7/9$ in case $D$ with two positive-positive entries retained outside the mediation-drop condition.  The evidence supports a translation-conditioned four-case discriminant over modeled tAI and measured protein abundance, not a general biological law.  It does not by itself establish realized translation in ribosomes, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universality.  Any one of those stronger readings requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, modeled tAI is a bounded codon-adaptation readout; measured ribosome TE, perturbation, rescue, structure, localization, turnover, and function assays are separate data routes and cannot be inferred from this matrix alone.
