\subsection{Translation-Conditioned Four-Case Discriminant for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourcase-discriminant}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer discriminant on the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_{stress}$.
The readout $P$ is measured protein abundance, encoded as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb after joining protein rows to real CDS codon counts.
The readout $T$ is a bounded per-protein modeled tAI surface,
$\sum_c n_c w_c / \sum_c n_c$, using GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.
The experiment therefore classifies a modeled codon-adaptation readout against measured abundance; it is not a ribosome-profiling experiment and is not a perturbation or rescue design.

\paragraph{Controlled regressions.}
For each organism, the $Q$, $T$, and $P$ columns are residualized against a fixed covariate block containing CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, with $24$ control columns and effective $Z$-rank $23$.
Both computed organisms are rank-powered for the nine-coordinate $Q$ design: $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ for the $T$ surface and also $9$ for the $P$ surface.
The finite rows are not saturated: Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $3739$ joined CDS/protein rows and residual degrees of freedom $3716$, while Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5815$ joined rows and residual degrees of freedom $5792$.
The degeneracy guard is $0.999999$, and the four-case threshold is $10^{-12}$.
The residual energies and explained energies are concrete:
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{P,Q}^{E.\ coli} &= 432.89050065533684, & E_{T,Q}^{E.\ coli} &= 0.256128714706293,\\
\|P^{E.\ coli}_{\perp Z}\|^2 &= 4020.333671802061, & \|T^{E.\ coli}_{\perp Z}\|^2 &= 0.4728468725814029,\\
E_{P,Q}^{S.\ cerevisiae} &= 882.6526210803958, & E_{T,Q}^{S.\ cerevisiae} &= 5.671703404506426,\\
\|P^{S.\ cerevisiae}_{\perp Z}\|^2 &= 2958.1865890925337, & \|T^{S.\ cerevisiae}_{\perp Z}\|^2 &= 6.90537888244409.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding aggregate residual association strengths are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT}(E.\ coli) &= 0.5416736993691318, & R^2_{QP}(E.\ coli) &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QT}(S.\ cerevisiae) &= 0.8213457220900503, & R^2_{QP}(S.\ cerevisiae) &= 0.29837624994140827.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values say that the modeled tAI surface is strongly aligned with the residual coordinate block in both organisms, and that protein abundance also retains a direct residual association after the same baseline controls.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The discriminant assigns each coordinate to one of four finite cases using the sign pattern of $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ and the mediation-condition test for whether the direct $Q\to P$ component is reduced after conditioning on $T$.
No coordinate is left unclassified in either organism.
The Escherichia coli surface has case counts $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=7$, with $2$ additional positive-positive coordinates that do not satisfy the mediation-drop condition.
The Saccharomyces cerevisiae surface has $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=9$, with no positive-positive coordinate failing the mediation-drop condition.
Thus the computed panel supports a bounded four-case classification over modeled tAI and measured abundance rows, with all nine yeast coordinates and seven of nine E. coli coordinates passing the stricter positive-positive attenuation gate.
The finite observation is a classification result, not a claim that codon residuals cause protein abundance.

\paragraph{Comparison with measured TE and downstream mediation records.}
Separate measured-TE runs sharpen the boundary.  On ribosome-derived TE surfaces, the same nine-coordinate block has $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ in E. coli, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving the positive numerical threshold in each of those four organisms.
Measured TE to abundance mediation is much weaker outside yeast: the measured mediated fractions are $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, $0.0$ in zebrafish after clipping a negative raw fraction, and $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast.
A joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability variance partition gives joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$ for E. coli, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ under the registered pair-permutation test.
The partial readout dictionary record on the yeast context join reports coverage $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ at $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$ and $\rho_{join}=0.05$.
Together these auxiliary rows say that the four-case modeled-tAI discriminant is compatible with later measured-readout evidence, but measured TE and mRNA-stability do not exhaust the abundance residual.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The supported statement is finite and conditional: for the two modeled-tAI organisms with sufficient row rank, $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be sorted by the four-case $S^{QT}/S^{QP}$ discriminant after the stated baseline controls, and the dominant observed output is the $D$ class.
The result is strongest for yeast, where all nine coordinates are positive-positive with attenuation by the modeled $T$ readout, and more mixed for E. coli, where seven coordinates meet that same stricter label and two remain positive-positive without the required attenuation.
The direct protein-omics survival surface is real in the same controlled sense, with $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ in E. coli and $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, but direct $Q\to P$ association alone is not a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation process, ribosome occupancy, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mechanism, evolutionary direction, or universal biological law follows from this chapter by itself.
The $T$ surface here is modeled tAI rather than observed ribosome motion; protein abundance is steady-state bulk proteomics and remains entangled with mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, degradation, post-translational state, sample handling, and condition.
A stronger biological claim would require a separate matched contact at the relevant layer, joined to the same CDS units, and a controlled statistic showing that the $Q\to T$ step survives and that conditioning on the measured $T$ readout removes or reduces the direct $Q\to P$ signal.
Until that layer-specific contact is present, the admissible conclusion is a finite four-case readback on residual coordinates, modeled tAI, and measured abundance rows only.
