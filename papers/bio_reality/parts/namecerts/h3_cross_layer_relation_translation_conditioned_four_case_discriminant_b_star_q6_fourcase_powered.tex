\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case readback for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourcase-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record studies a finite matrix surface whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns are the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation-side readout $T$ is the per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g) &= \frac{\sum_{c} n_{g,c} w_c}{\sum_{c} n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{g,c}$ is the codon count in coding sequence $g$ and $w_c$ is the organism-scoped tAI weight from tRNA gene-copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein-side readout $P$ is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance in ppm, joined to real coding-sequence codon counts.  The tested organisms are \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The E. coli run used $3739$ joined CDS/protein rows, with the CDS organism key $escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655$ and the tRNA organism key $escherichia\_coli$.  The yeast run used $5815$ joined CDS/protein rows, with matched yeast CDS and tRNA organism keys.  In the broader direct protein-omics pass, the same protein-abundance bridge reported join hit rates $0.9978649586335735$ for E. coli and $0.9156038419146592$ for yeast, with $3739$ and $5815$ joined proteins respectively.

\paragraph{Residualization and rank checks.}
Both organisms were residualized against a $Z$ design containing an intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The control column count was $24$, the effective rank of $Z$ was $23$, and the degeneracy threshold was $0.999999$.  After projecting out $Z$, the residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ design retained rank $9$ for both the protein model and the translation model in both organisms.  E. coli had residual degrees of freedom $3716$, $T$ residual energy $0.4728468725814029$, and $P$ residual energy $4020.333671802061$.  Yeast had residual degrees of freedom $5792$, $T$ residual energy $6.90537888244409$, and $P$ residual energy $2958.1865890925337$.  The explained energies of the $Q$ design were
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{Q \to P}^{E.coli} &= 432.89050065533684, &
E_{Q \to T}^{E.coli} &= 0.256128714706293, \\
E_{Q \to P}^{yeast} &= 882.6526210803958, &
E_{Q \to T}^{yeast} &= 5.671703404506426.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the four-case readback is not a rank artifact in these two panels: the residual coordinate rank is full for the nine-coordinate packet, and the classifier reported $four\_case\_classified=true$ with no unclassified coordinates in either organism.

\paragraph{Four-case rule.}
The classifier uses threshold $10^{-12}$ and assigns each coordinate by the signs of $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ together with the behavior of the direct $q_i \to P$ association after conditioning on $T$.  The intended interpretation is finite and operational.  Case A means no translation-side survival and no protein-side survival.  Case B means translation-side survival without protein-side survival.  Case C means protein-side survival without a qualifying translation-conditioned drop.  Case D means both $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ are positive and the protein association is reduced by the translation readout in the fitted surface.  In aggregate, E. coli had
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{E.coli} &= 0.5416736993691318, &
S^{QP}_{E.coli} &= 0.10767526677985896,
\end{aligned}
$$
and yeast had
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{yeast} &= 0.8213457220900503, &
S^{QP}_{yeast} &= 0.29837624994140827.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite classification counts were sharply asymmetric across the two panels.  E. coli had $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=7$, plus $2$ positive-positive coordinates without the required mediation drop.  Yeast had $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=9$, and no positive-positive coordinate left outside the mediation-drop class.  The readback is therefore not merely that the $Q$ surface predicts protein abundance; it separates coordinates whose modeled translation readout absorbs part of the direct protein-side signal from coordinates whose positive-positive status remains outside that stricter condition.

\paragraph{Measured-readout comparison.}
The modeled-tAI classifier is bounded by construction, so it is compared locally with measured ribosome-profiling TE records where such records were available.  In the measured TE survival pass, E. coli used $3415$ genes with TE, a ribosome-TE join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$, and $S^{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ with $9$ surviving coordinates.  Yeast used $5128$ genes with TE, a join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, and $S^{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ with $9$ surviving coordinates.  The measured mediation decomposition against PAXdb abundance reported, for E. coli, $S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074$, $S^{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$, raw $M^{QTP}=0.04091158340950673$, and clipped measured mediated fraction $0.04091158340950673$ on a $3415$-row three-way join.  For yeast it reported $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S^{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$, raw $M^{QTP}=0.4793069656345167$, and clipped measured mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$ on a $5128$-row three-way join.  These measured-TE numbers do not replace the modeled-tAI four-case labels; they show that the same residual coordinate family also survives against independently measured TE in the organisms most directly comparable to the two-organism classifier.

\paragraph{Relation to abundance-side mediation.}
A separate joint TE and mRNA-stability decomposition gives additional finite context for the protein-abundance side.  In E. coli, $R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036$, with joint mediation fraction $0.2404669586626661$, TE-only fraction $0.01975521340963268$, stability-only fraction $0.23052056324898387$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In yeast, $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, with joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$, TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$, stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$, and the same right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  A compact yeast readout dictionary, using $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$ and $\rho_{join}=0.05$, covered $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$ of the abundance residual against a selection-aware null $95$th percentile of $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained mass $0.7546178856026946$.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell analysis on yeast had $n=1257$, base held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full held-out $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, a permutation null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $f3$--dwell correlation value $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  These companion rows support the narrow statement that some abundance-side signal is reducible to measured readouts, while a large residue remains outside the recorded mediators.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The finite result is a two-organism four-case classification over modeled tAI and measured PAXdb abundance, with measured-TE and abundance-side decompositions used only as nearby checks.  It does not establish a universal codon law, a causal translation mechanism, a folding claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a molecular-function claim, or a phenotype claim.  Modeled tAI is not ribosome occupancy.  PAXdb abundance is not a direct translation-rate assay and is not isolated from mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding state, or pathway context.  A stronger biological conclusion would require an additional layer-matched contact and a same-row statistic showing that the relevant $q_i \to T_j$ step survives and that conditioning on the measured $T_j$ removes or reduces the direct $q_i \to P_k$ association under the same residual controls.
