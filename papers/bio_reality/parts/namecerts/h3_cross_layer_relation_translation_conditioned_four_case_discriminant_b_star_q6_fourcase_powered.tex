\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discrimination for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{subsec:bioq6-translation-conditioned-four-case-discriminant}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite matched carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel in which each coding sequence contributes residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates $q_i$, a translation-layer readout $T$, and a protein-omics readout $P$.  The translation readout is a per-protein mean modeled tAI score,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac{\sum_{c} \operatorname{count}(c)\,w(c)}{\sum_{c} \operatorname{count}(c)},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $w(c)$ is the codon adaptation weight derived from organism-scoped tRNA gene-copy and wobble-weight inputs.  The protein readout is $P=\log_{10}(\operatorname{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to the same coding-sequence rows.  The two finite organism panels are not symbolic examples: the joined row counts are $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The coordinate source is the imported $q$-vector table from the powered $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation-survival computation, and each organism contributes $9$ tested residual coordinates.

\paragraph{Controlled residualization and rank gate.}
For each organism, the readout tests are performed only after residualizing against the declared controls: amino-acid composition, organism scope, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density, mRNA-related control information when present, and simpler codon baselines.  In the finite rows reported here, the control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in both organisms.  The powered residual coordinate matrix retains full residual coordinate rank: $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X_T)=9$ and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X_P)=9$ for both the translation and protein-abundance targets.  The remaining residual degrees of freedom are $3716$ for \emph{E. coli} and $5792$ for yeast, so the four-case call is not made from a rank-saturated three-row surface.  The recorded degenerate-$R^2$ boundary is $0.999999$, and both organism blocks are marked rank-sufficient.

\paragraph{Finite scalar readback.}
The controlled residual energies and explained energies separate the two layers.  For \emph{E. coli}, the translation residual energy is $0.4728468725814029$, the protein residual energy is $4020.333671802061$, the $Q$-explained energy on $T$ is $0.256128714706293$, and the $Q$-explained energy on $P$ is $432.89050065533684$.  For yeast, the corresponding values are $6.90537888244409$, $2958.1865890925337$, $5.671703404506426$, and $882.6526210803958$.  The aggregate controlled coefficients recorded for the two readout directions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{E. coli}:\quad &R^2_{QT}=0.5416736993691318,\qquad R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896,\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:\quad &R^2_{QT}=0.8213457220900503,\qquad R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values are used only as finite controlled readouts for this joined panel.  They do not by themselves assert causality, universality, structural order, molecular function, or a perturbation mechanism.

\paragraph{Four cases.}
Each coordinate is assigned by the signs of $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$, together with the translation-conditioning check on the direct $q_i\to P$ association.  Case A is the null-compression case $S^{QT}\leq 0$ and $S^{QP}\leq 0$.  Case B is translation-only persistence, with $S^{QT}>0$ but $S^{QP}\leq 0$.  Case C is protein-omics prediction without mediation by the measured translation readout, with $S^{QT}\leq 0$ and $S^{QP}>0$.  Case D is the mediated-propagation candidate: $S^{QT}>0$ and $S^{QP}>0$, with the direct $q_i\to P$ association reduced after conditioning on $T$.  Written as the local decision rule,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &: S^{QT}\leq 0,\; S^{QP}\leq 0,\\
B &: S^{QT}>0,\; S^{QP}\leq 0,\\
C &: S^{QT}\leq 0,\; S^{QP}>0,\\
D &: S^{QT}>0,\; S^{QP}>0,\; q_i\to P \text{ decreases after conditioning on } T.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification threshold is $10^{-12}$.  In the \emph{E. coli} finite block, the case counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=7$, with $2$ positive-positive coordinates that do not pass the mediation-drop clause.  In the yeast finite block, the case counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=9$, with no positive-positive coordinate left outside the mediation-drop clause.  No coordinate is reported as unclassified in either organism.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The positive case-D counts are translation-conditioned protein-abundance classifications over modeled tAI and measured abundance rows; they are not proof that a codon residual coordinate causes a protein phenotype.  The translation readout used here is modeled codon-adaptation from tRNA copy and wobble information, not ribosome-profiling occupancy or measured translation efficiency.  The protein readout is steady-state abundance, not folded structure, physical admissibility, localization, post-translational state, protein stability, molecular function, or organismal phenotype.  A direct $q_i\to P$ association is therefore not promoted unless the $q_i\to T$ step survives and the conditioning calculation reduces the direct protein association.  Case C, when it occurs, must be read as a protein-omics residual not mediated by the measured $T$ readout; it may reflect unmeasured translation variables, transcript effects, selection, confounding, turnover, pathway context, or non-translation routes.  No translation mechanism, folding conclusion, physical feasibility conclusion, functional realization, perturbation rescue, system phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this finite matrix unless a separate layer-matched empirical contact supplies that specific measurement and the corresponding controlled statistic is recomputed.
