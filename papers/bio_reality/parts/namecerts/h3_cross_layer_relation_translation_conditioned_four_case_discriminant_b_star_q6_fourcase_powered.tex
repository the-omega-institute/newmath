\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discrimination of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bq6-translation-conditioned-four-case-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The discriminant is evaluated on a finite matrix whose rows are coding-sequence units and whose columns are nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The four-case run uses two rank-powered organism panels with modeled translation and measured protein abundance readouts.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $3739$ CDS/protein rows are joined, using the \emph{E. coli} tRNA table for the modeled translation readout.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ CDS/protein rows are joined with the yeast tRNA table.  In both organisms the control design has $24$ columns, rank $23$, and a nondegenerate residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design of rank $9$ against both the protein and translation readouts.  The residual degrees of freedom are $3716$ for \emph{E. coli} and $5792$ for yeast, so the all-positive readbacks below are not the rank-degenerate $n=3$ boundary artifact.

\paragraph{Readouts and controls.}
The protein coordinate $P$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The translation coordinate $T$ in this discriminant is a bounded modeled-tAI readout, not ribosome profiling: for each protein it is the CDS mean of codon counts weighted by GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  All $Q$, $T$, and $P$ fits are read after residualization against CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, the $M$-density baseline, and the organism-matched control columns.  The classification threshold is $10^{-12}$.  The finite scalar surface for the two organisms is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.10767526677985896, & R^2_{QT}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.5416736993691318,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.29837624994140827, & R^2_{QT}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.8213457220900503.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding explained energies show that the same residual coordinate set is visible to both surfaces: in \emph{E. coli}, $X$ explains $432.89050065533684$ units of residual protein energy and $0.256128714706293$ units of residual modeled-tAI energy, with residual energies $4020.333671802061$ and $0.4728468725814029$ respectively.  In yeast, $X$ explains $882.6526210803958$ units of residual protein energy and $5.671703404506426$ units of residual modeled-tAI energy, with residual energies $2958.1865890925337$ and $6.90537888244409$.

\paragraph{Four cases.}
Each coordinate is assigned by the signs of $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ and by whether the direct $q_i \to P$ contribution is reduced after conditioning on the translation readout.  Case A is null against both readouts.  Case B is translation-only survival.  Case C is direct protein-omics survival without a detected translation-conditioned drop.  Case D is the translation-conditioned class in which the coordinate survives to translation and protein abundance and the direct abundance signal is reduced by the translation readout.  The observed counts are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad A=0,\ B=0,\ C=0,\ D=7,\quad 2\ \text{positive-positive coordinates without mediation drop},\\
\mathrm{yeast}:&\quad A=0,\ B=0,\ C=0,\ D=9,\quad 0\ \text{positive-positive coordinates without mediation drop}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No coordinates are left unclassified in either organism.  Thus the finite readback is not merely that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts protein abundance.  It separates a modeled-translation-conditioned majority class from the exceptional \emph{E. coli} coordinates whose signs are positive in both $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ but whose direct protein-abundance component is not removed by the modeled translation covariate.

\paragraph{Relation to direct protein-omics survival.}
The direct protein-abundance experiment supplies the wider $S^{QP}$ background and prevents the four-case call from being interpreted from a single organism.  Across fifteen organism-level PAXdb joins, all nine coordinates survive the direct $Q \to P$ fit at the positive threshold.  Representative direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.061906245886660105$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.06397514251295629$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast.  The row counts are also finite and nontrivial: $4042$ joined rows for \emph{B. subtilis}, $3739$ for \emph{E. coli}, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $1656$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}, and $5815$ for yeast.  Join hit rates range from $0.8231586752347998$ in rat to $1.0$ in several fully joined panels, with yeast at $0.9156038419146592$ and \emph{E. coli} at $0.9978649586335735$.  This establishes the protein-abundance side as a broad finite signal, but by itself it remains only direct omics association.

\paragraph{Measured-TE comparison.}
A separate measured-ribosome TE run replaces modeled tAI by $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$, where $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{footprint}/\mathrm{mRNA}$ in local ribosome-profiling files.  The measured-TE join uses $3415$ genes in \emph{E. coli} with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$, $5128$ genes in yeast with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, $11554$ genes in human with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$, and $13140$ genes in zebrafish with hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  The measured $S^{QT}$ values are $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish, again with all nine coordinates surviving at the finite threshold.  When measured TE is inserted into a $Q \to T \to P$ variance decomposition, the measured mediated fractions are $0.04091158340950673$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.0$ in zebrafish, with corresponding $S_{QP}$ values $0.10941535951828074$, $0.3146818503437042$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.04926578454614652$.  These measured-TE numbers support the local reading of the four-case table: yeast has a large translation-conditioned component, \emph{E. coli} has a small but positive one, human is weak under this measured TE contact, and zebrafish is suppressive or null in the mediated fraction despite positive $Q \to T$ and $Q \to P$ readbacks.

\paragraph{Other finite reductions.}
The same abundance residual admits further finite decompositions, none of which should be collapsed into the four-case label.  In the joint TE and mRNA-stability run, the joint mediation fraction is $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, and $0.16890712099923358$ for human, with right-tail permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ in all three panels.  The single-mediator components are organism-specific: \emph{E. coli} has TE-only fraction $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only fraction $0.23052056324898387$; yeast has TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$ and stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$; human has TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$ and stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$.  A readout-dictionary certificate in yeast, with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, and $120$ permutations, covers $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ of the residual using at most three readouts, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ above a selection-aware null $95\%$ value of $0.029149007242956115$.  For the $f3\_stress$ coordinate, a dwell-mediated yeast run gives held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ above null $95\%=0.0013115362189692937$, with $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  Its positional version identifies the $5'$ segment as dominant, while the structural-order ramp test reports a positive ramp signal but not a passed ramp-localization gate: weighted ramp $\Delta R^2=0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body $\Delta R^2=-0.00026157313923957926$, and paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip $p=0.05859375$.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is ready at the level of a translation-conditioned four-case discriminant.  The modeled-tAI table assigns all nine yeast coordinates to case D and assigns seven of nine \emph{E. coli} coordinates to case D, with two \emph{E. coli} positive-positive entries held outside the mediation-drop class.  Direct protein-omics survival is broad, measured TE survival is positive in four organism panels, and measured mediation is strongest in yeast, smaller in \emph{E. coli}, weak in human, and absent after clipping in zebrafish.  The scientific reading is therefore a finite classification of residual codon-coordinate behavior across translation and abundance readouts, not a completed biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universal law follows from the four-case labels.  Modeled tAI is a bounded translation proxy rather than observed ribosome occupancy; PAXdb abundance is a steady-state protein-omics readout rather than an isolated translation-rate or function assay; and the rows are cross-sectional rather than perturbational.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and statistic: measured translation rows for ribosome claims, structure or physical assay rows for folding or admissibility claims, functional or phenotype assays for function claims, and perturbation or rescue designs for mechanism claims.
