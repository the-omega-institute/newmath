\subsection{A translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance readback}
\label{sec:bior-bstar-q6-fourcase-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite question.}
The finite object in this record is a row-matched matrix whose columns are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, a translation readout $T$, a protein-abundance readout $P$, and the control block $Z$.  The coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The discriminant asks a deliberately narrow question: after residualizing $Q$, $T$, and $P$ against amino-acid composition, CDS length, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, which coordinates survive against translation, which survive against protein abundance, and which apparent $Q \to P$ signals are reduced after the translation readout is included?

\paragraph{Rows and readouts.}
The computed four-case run used two organism panels with complete modeled-translation and abundance rows.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $3739$ CDS/protein rows were joined, with CDS rows from \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} and tRNA weights from \texttt{escherichia\_coli}.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ rows were joined, with both CDS rows and tRNA weights organism-matched to \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  The protein readout was $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The translation readout was per-protein modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g) &= \frac{\sum_c n_{g,c}w_c}{\sum_c n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{g,c}$ is the codon count in CDS $g$ and $w_c$ is the GtRNAdb/dos-Reis tAI weight for codon $c$.  This $T$ is a bounded modeled translation score, not a ribosome-profiling measurement.

\paragraph{Rank and residualization checks.}
Both organism matrices were powered for the declared nine-coordinate question.  In \emph{E. coli}, the control block had $24$ columns and rank $23$, leaving residual degrees of freedom $3716$ after $Z$; the residualized $Q$ design had rank $9$ against both $P$ and $T$.  The residual protein energy was $4020.333671802061$, the residual translation energy was $0.4728468725814029$, the $Q$-explained energy on $P$ was $432.89050065533684$, and the $Q$-explained energy on $T$ was $0.256128714706293$.  In yeast, the same $24$-column control block had rank $23$, residual degrees of freedom $5792$, and rank $9$ for the residualized $Q$ design against both readouts.  The yeast residual protein energy was $2958.1865890925337$, the residual translation energy was $6.90537888244409$, the $Q$-explained energy on $P$ was $882.6526210803958$, and the $Q$-explained energy on $T$ was $5.671703404506426$.  The classification threshold was $10^{-12}$ and both panels were marked powered-rank sufficient rather than rank-degenerate.

\paragraph{Four-case rule.}
For each coordinate, the classifier first records whether the residual coordinate has positive translation survival $S^{QT}$ and positive protein-omics survival $S^{QP}$.  It then asks whether conditioning on $T$ reduces the direct $Q \to P$ signal.  The cases are read as follows: a coordinate can be null for both readouts, translation-only, protein-only or direct/confounded, or translation-attached with a mediation-condition drop.  The last label is the only one that can be used as evidence for a measured $Q \to T \to P$ statistical path, and even there the word ``measured'' is restricted to the rows and readouts in the finite panel.  A positive $Q \to P$ entry alone is not a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Observed classification.}
The modeled-tAI four-case matrix gave strong positive $Q$ readback on both readouts in the two computed organisms.  In \emph{E. coli}, the joint residual scores were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QT} &= 0.5416736993691318,
\end{aligned}
$$
with all $9$ coordinates classified and no unclassified coordinates.  The case count was $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=7$, with $2$ additional positive-positive coordinates lacking the required mediation drop.  Thus the finite readout says that seven coordinates pass the modeled-translation-conditioned abundance criterion, while two remain positive on both margins without the attenuation needed for a mediation label.  In yeast, the corresponding scores were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QT} &= 0.8213457220900503,
\end{aligned}
$$
and all $9$ coordinates were classified as case $D$, with no positive-positive-without-drop remainder and no unclassified coordinate.  The yeast result is therefore the cleaner finite instance: every one of the nine residual coordinates survives against the modeled translation score, survives against measured protein abundance, and satisfies the attenuation condition used by this classifier.

\paragraph{Direct protein-abundance context.}
The same nine-coordinate residual system also has direct protein-omics survival across a broader abundance panel, but that broader fact is not identical to the four-case result.  Direct $S^{QP}$ was computed after the same kind of length, amino-acid-composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls for $15$ organisms, with $9$ surviving coordinates in every organism.  Representative direct $R^2_{QP}$ values were $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.04613529807085213$ for zebrafish, and $0.019256877907572044$ for \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}.  The largest direct value in that panel was yeast at $0.29837624994140827$.  These direct associations justify asking the conditioning question, but they do not themselves identify translation as the route.

\paragraph{Measured-TE comparison.}
A separate ribosome-TE survival computation used log measured TE, where TE was footprint divided by mRNA in local ribosome-profiling sources.  That readout was available for zebrafish, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast.  It gave positive $S^{QT,meas}$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving in each organism, but at smaller organism-specific magnitudes than the modeled-tAI two-organism discriminant: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, $0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast.  The row joins were $13140$ genes for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, with reported TE join hit rates $0.9118667591950035$, $0.9133458143888741$, $0.6064136881331024$, and $0.8818572656921754$, respectively.  This comparison matters because the four-case chapter uses modeled tAI as $T$, whereas a stronger translation claim would have to repeat the same discriminant with ribosome-derived TE as $T$ on matched rows.

\paragraph{Measured mediation comparison.}
The measured-TE protein-abundance mediation run supplies the finite caution.  It joined measured TE, PAXdb abundance, and CDS rows for $13140$ zebrafish genes, $3415$ \emph{E. coli} genes, $11554$ human genes, and $5128$ yeast genes.  The measured mediated fractions were $0.0$ for zebrafish after clipping a raw value of $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  Thus yeast again shows a substantial translation-conditioned abundance component, while \emph{E. coli} and human show much weaker measured-TE mediation.  The four-case modeled-tAI result should therefore be read as a finite modeled-readout classification, not as a universal statement about ribosome occupancy or causal translation control.

\paragraph{Joint mediator context.}
A related three-organism variance partition included measured TE and mRNA stability together.  The joined row counts were $2517$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11322$ for human, and $4759$ for yeast.  The joint mediation fractions were $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, respectively, with right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in all three organisms.  The single-mediator components differed by organism: in \emph{E. coli}, TE-only was $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only was $0.23052056324898387$; in human, TE-only was $0.00549490864190343$ and stability-only was $0.15314679870183337$; in yeast, TE-only was $0.4697188319693239$ and stability-only was $0.0626618595344251$.  This makes the yeast four-case signal biologically plausible as a translation-heavy finite panel, while showing that other organisms can route much of the abundance association through non-TE readouts.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite claim is ready at the level of a modeled-translation four-case discriminant.  The two organism panels are rank-powered, the nine residual coordinates are present, the control block is explicit, and the case calls are non-vacuous: \emph{E. coli} has seven case-$D$ coordinates and two positive-positive coordinates without the mediation drop, while yeast has nine case-$D$ coordinates.  The result supports this exact statement: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be sorted by translation-survival, protein-abundance survival, and translation-conditioned attenuation on finite organism-matched rows.  It does not say that codon geometry by itself observes translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Such claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
