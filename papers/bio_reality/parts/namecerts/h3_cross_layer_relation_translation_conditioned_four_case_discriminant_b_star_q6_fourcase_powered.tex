\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-fourcase-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer readback for the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$.
The rows are real coding sequences joined to two readouts: a translation-side score $T$, defined here as per-protein mean modeled tAI from codon counts and GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights, and a protein-side score $P$, defined as $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance in ppm.  The four-case discriminant is applied only after residualizing $Q$, $T$, and $P$ against the same control family: intercept, coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The residualization rank was nondegenerate in both powered organisms: for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 the control matrix had $24$ columns, $rank(Z)=23$, $rank(\widetilde X)$ was $9$ for both $P$ and $T$, and the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $3716$; for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} the corresponding values were $24$, $23$, $9$, $9$, and $5792$.

\paragraph{Readout magnitudes.}
The powered matrix contained $3739$ joined \emph{E. coli} coding-sequence/protein rows and $5815$ joined yeast rows.  In the four-case run, the residual $Q$ surface explained $432.89050065533684$ units of protein-abundance energy and $0.256128714706293$ units of modeled-tAI energy in \emph{E. coli}; the residual energies of $P$ and $T$ were $4020.333671802061$ and $0.4728468725814029$, respectively.  In yeast, $Q$ explained $882.6526210803958$ units of protein-abundance energy and $5.671703404506426$ units of modeled-tAI energy, with residual energies $2958.1865890925337$ for $P$ and $6.90537888244409$ for $T$.  The resulting joint summaries were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.5416736993691318, & R^2_{QP}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QT}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.8213457220900503, & R^2_{QP}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.29837624994140827.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification threshold was $10^{-12}$, and no residual coordinate was left unclassified in either organism.

\paragraph{Four-case result.}
The discriminant separates four possible finite outcomes for each residual coordinate: translation-null/protein-null, translation-surviving/protein-null, translation-null/protein-surviving, and translation-surviving/protein-surviving with attenuation after conditioning on the translation readout.  In this run, the powered modeled-tAI surface placed all classified entries at the positive-positive end of the readout table, but the attenuation check split the organisms.  In \emph{E. coli}, the case counts were $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=7$, with $2$ additional positive-positive coordinates lacking a mediation drop.  In yeast, the case counts were $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=9$, with no positive-positive coordinate outside the mediation-drop class.  Thus the finite readback says that the modeled translation score and measured protein abundance are both visible to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface in these two organisms, and that the yeast panel gives the sharper modeled-tAI-conditioned absorption pattern.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation rows.}
The modeled-tAI result should be read beside the measured ribosome-efficiency panel, not substituted for it.  The measured TE survival run used ribosome-footprint-over-mRNA readouts and found $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving above the positive threshold in each organism.  Its joined measured-TE row counts were $3415$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11554$ for human, $13140$ for zebrafish, and $5128$ for yeast.  The measured TE-to-protein mediation run then reported measured mediated fractions of $0.04091158340950673$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, $0.0$ in zebrafish after clipping a negative raw fraction, and $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast.  These numbers support the local interpretation that yeast is the strongest finite row for translation-conditioned protein-abundance attenuation, while human and zebrafish remain much weaker on the measured-TE mediation axis.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance context.}
Direct protein-omics survival is broad but is not itself a mechanism.  The larger PAXdb scan joined $15$ organisms and kept all nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates above threshold in each, with direct $R^2_{QP}$ ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in \emph{Halobacterium salinarum} to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast.  Representative joined-row and hit-rate values were $3739$ rows at hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ for \emph{E. coli}, $5815$ rows at $0.9156038419146592$ for yeast, $19053$ rows at $0.9779294769799313$ for human, and $14410$ rows at $1.0$ for zebrafish.  The four-case result therefore does not discover protein-abundance association from scratch; it refines the already-positive direct protein surface by asking whether a translation-side readout absorbs the same residual signal.

Additional finite rows keep the interpretation bounded.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition, yeast had $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, giving a joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$ with right-tail permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$.  The corresponding fractions were $0.2404669586626661$ in \emph{E. coli} and $0.16890712099923358$ in human, again with right-tail probability $0.000999000999000999$.  A separate yeast readout-dictionary compression used $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, and $120$ permutations; its selection-aware coverage was $0.24538211439730545$ against a selection-aware null $95$ percent level of $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  For $f_3\_stress$ dwell mediation, the yeast held-out $\Delta R^2$ was $0.01856473944057712$ against null $95$ percent $0.0013115362189692937$, with $f_3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  The structural-order ramp check was weaker: the primary ramp window had weighted held-out increment $0.0011165640103326878$, body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, ramp sign-flip probability $0.0478515625$, and paired ramp-minus-body probability $0.05859375$, so it was positive but not localized by the stricter gate.

\paragraph{Local verdict and boundary.}
The finite-row verdict for the four-case modeled-tAI discriminant is ready: both tested organisms were rank-powered, all nine coordinates were classified, and the modeled translation readout was strong enough to place yeast entirely in the translation-conditioned positive-positive class, with \emph{E. coli} showing seven such coordinates and two positive-positive entries without the required drop.  The admissible statement is therefore a finite classification statement about residual codon coordinates, modeled translation readout, and measured protein abundance under stated controls.  It does not establish ribosome dynamics by itself, because modeled tAI is not a ribosome-profiling measurement.  It does not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mediation, synonymous-edit rescue, or a universal biological rule.  Those claims require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled finite statistics before they can be asserted.
