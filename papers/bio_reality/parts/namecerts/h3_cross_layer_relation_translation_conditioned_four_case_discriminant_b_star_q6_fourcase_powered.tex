\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourcase-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests whether the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates can be sorted, on matched coding-sequence rows, by their joint behavior against a translation readout $T$ and a protein-abundance readout $P$.  The active finite carrier is a row matrix of real CDS codon counts, residual coordinates $q_i$, a per-protein modeled translation score, and measured PAXdb abundance.  The coordinate list used in the computation has cardinality $9$:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The residualization controls are the intercept, CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition coordinates, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline induced by the union support of the nine projected residual coordinates.  The fitted control design has $24$ columns and residual rank $23$ in both reported organisms.

\paragraph{Readouts and decision rule.}
The translation readout in this finite run is not ribosome profiling.  It is a modeled per-protein tAI quantity computed as the codon-count weighted mean of organism-matched tAI weights:
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g) &= \frac{\sum_{c} n_{g,c} w_c}{\sum_c n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{g,c}$ is the count of codon $c$ in CDS $g$ and $w_c$ is the tAI weight derived from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with the dos Reis wobble weighting scheme.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to the same CDS rows.  The classification threshold is $10^{-12}$.  A coordinate is classified by the signs of its controlled $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ entries together with the attenuation test obtained after conditioning the direct $q_i \to P$ fit on the translation readout.  In this chapter the four letters have only this operational meaning: $A$ is null on both readouts, $B$ is translation-only, $C$ is direct protein-omics survival without the required translation-conditioned drop, and $D$ is positive on the translation and protein-abundance surfaces with the fitted direct signal reduced by the modeled translation readout.  These letters are finite-row labels, not biological mechanisms.

\paragraph{Row joins and rank checks.}
Two organism panels pass the rank check for the modeled-tAI four-case run.  The Escherichia coli panel uses $3739$ joined CDS/protein rows, with CDS organism key $escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655$ and tRNA organism key $escherichia\_coli$.  After residualizing against the control design, the rank of the residualized $Q$ matrix is $9$ for both $P$ and $T$, the residual degrees of freedom are $3716$, the residual energy of $T$ is $0.4728468725814029$, and the residual energy of $P$ is $4020.333671802061$.  The corresponding $Q$-explained energies are $0.256128714706293$ on $T$ and $432.89050065533684$ on $P$.

For Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the panel uses $5815$ joined CDS/protein rows and $5815$ proteins.  The residualized $Q$ ranks are again $9$ for both readouts, with residual degrees of freedom $5792$, residual $T$ energy $6.90537888244409$, and residual $P$ energy $2958.1865890925337$.  The $Q$-explained energies are $5.671703404506426$ on modeled tAI and $882.6526210803958$ on measured abundance.  The repeated full-rank result is important: the four-case labels are not produced by the low-organism, all-saturated boundary that can make degenerate matrices look uniformly positive.

\paragraph{Survival magnitudes.}
The organism-level controlled survival summaries are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QT}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.5416736993691318,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QT}(\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.8213457220900503.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values show that, in the modeled-tAI run, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface is strongly visible to the modeled translation readout and also visible to measured protein abundance after the declared composition and length controls.  The protein-abundance surface is independently consistent with the broader direct protein-omics run: the same direct $S^{QP}$ experiment reports all $9$ coordinates surviving in E. coli with $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ on $3739$ joined rows and all $9$ coordinates surviving in yeast with $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ on $5815$ joined rows.  The direct protein-omics run also reports high CDS-abundance join rates, $0.9978649586335735$ for E. coli and $0.9156038419146592$ for yeast, so the present two-organism discriminant is not operating on a hidden tiny join.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite classification output is sharply concentrated in case $D$.  In E. coli the case count vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(A,B,C,D,\mathrm{positive\ positive\ without\ drop}) &= (0,0,0,7,2),
\end{aligned}
$$
with no unclassified coordinates.  Thus seven of the nine residual coordinates satisfy the full positive-positive-plus-attenuation condition under modeled tAI, while two coordinates are positive against both $T$ and $P$ but do not show the required mediation drop.  In yeast the vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(A,B,C,D,\mathrm{positive\ positive\ without\ drop}) &= (0,0,0,9,0),
\end{aligned}
$$
again with no unclassified coordinates.  Yeast therefore gives the cleanest finite readback: all nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates are classified as $D$ under this modeled-tAI/protein-abundance discriminant.

\paragraph{Relation to measured ribosome-TE rows.}
The modeled-tAI four-case result should be read together with, but not identified with, measured ribosome-TE computations.  A separate measured-TE survival run reports $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$ in E. coli on $3415$ TE genes and $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast on $5128$ TE genes, with all nine coordinates surviving at the stated positive threshold.  The measured mediation decomposition gives measured mediated fractions $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  These measured-TE numbers make clear that the modeled-tAI discriminant is biologically anchored by a translation-layer route, but the chapter's own four-case table remains a modeled-tAI classification over the two finite organism panels, not a direct ribosome-profiling mechanism proof.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The verified finite statement is that, after amino-acid composition, CDS length, $GC3$, and $M$-density residualization, the two reported panels contain a nondegenerate $9$-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ surface whose modeled-tAI and protein-abundance readouts separate into objective four-case labels.  E. coli gives seven full $D$ calls and two positive-positive calls without the mediation drop.  Yeast gives nine full $D$ calls.  This supports the narrower claim that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are not only direct protein-omics predictors on these panels; they also align with a translation-conditioned modeled-tAI readout strongly enough to absorb the direct protein-abundance signal in most or all coordinates, depending on organism.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome dwell mechanism, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, phenotype claim, causal claim, or universal biological law follows from this finite discriminant alone.  The $D$ label records a controlled statistical attenuation pattern under modeled tAI and measured abundance.  It does not establish that synonymous editing would rescue abundance, that elongation is the unique mediator, that protein abundance is isolated from mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, or pathway context, or that the same labels must hold across all taxa and conditions.  Any such stronger assertion would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
