\subsection{Translation-conditioned four-case discriminant for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourcase-discriminant}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite cross-layer matrix over nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, two rank-powered organism rows, one bounded translation readout, and one matched protein-abundance readout.  The coordinate list is
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The translation-side readout in this finite row is not a ribosome-profiling measurement: it is the per-protein modeled tAI average
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g)=\frac{\sum_{c} n_{g,c}\,w_c}{\sum_{c} n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{g,c}$ is the CDS codon count and $w_c$ is the GtRNAdb and dos Reis wobble-weight tAI value for codon $c$.  The two organism rows are \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The joined row counts are $3739$ CDS/protein rows for \emph{E. coli} with the tRNA organism key \texttt{escherichia\_coli}, and $5815$ rows for yeast with the tRNA organism key \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.

\paragraph{Residual design.}
For each organism, $Q$, $T$, and $P$ are residualized against the same covariate family: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline attached to the nine-coordinate support.  The residual-control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in both organisms.  The residual $Q$ design has rank $9$ against both the $T$ and $P$ targets, so the four-case readback is not a rank-degenerate all-fit artifact.  The finite row therefore works with the controlled quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT} &= R^2(T_{\perp Z}\sim Q_{\perp Z}),\\
S^{QP} &= R^2(P_{\perp Z}\sim Q_{\perp Z}),\\
M^{QTP} &= \text{the attenuation of the }Q\to P\text{ signal after conditioning on }T.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification threshold is $10^{-12}$, so a positive entry means a strictly positive finite partial-$R^2$ signal above numerical zero, not a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row energies.}
For \emph{E. coli}, the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $3716$, the residual protein energy is $4020.333671802061$, and the residual modeled-tAI energy is $0.4728468725814029$.  The $Q$ surface explains $432.89050065533684$ energy units on $P$ and $0.256128714706293$ energy units on $T$.  Equivalently, the two headline controlled scores are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QT}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.5416736993691318.
\end{aligned}
$$
For yeast, the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $5792$, the residual protein energy is $2958.1865890925337$, and the residual modeled-tAI energy is $6.90537888244409$.  The $Q$ surface explains $882.6526210803958$ energy units on $P$ and $5.671703404506426$ energy units on $T$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QT}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.8213457220900503.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are large enough to assign every coordinate to a four-case outcome in both organisms.  No coordinate is left unclassified.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The four-case table separates translation-null, protein-null, direct-protein, and translation-conditioned behavior.  The powered rows give the following finite module classification.  In \emph{E. coli}, the counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=7$, with two additional positive-positive coordinates in which the direct $Q\to P$ association remains positive without the required mediation drop.  In yeast, the counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, and $D=9$, with zero positive-positive no-drop coordinates.  Thus the finite matrix does not merely say that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts protein abundance: it separates the protein-abundance association by whether the same controlled coordinate also survives against the modeled translation readout and whether conditioning on that readout reduces the direct association.

\paragraph{Measured-TE comparison.}
A separate measured-ribosome TE row checks how the same nine-coordinate $Q$ surface behaves when $T$ is defined as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$ from local ribosome-footprint divided by mRNA records.  That row is broader than the modeled-tAI four-case row and includes danio, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast.  The controlled measured-TE survival values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each row has nine surviving coordinates.  The measured mediation row then reports measured-TE mediated fractions of $0.0$ for danio after clipping a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  These numbers sharpen the interpretation of the modeled-tAI discriminant: yeast is the strongest common row across modeled and measured translation-side readouts, while \emph{E. coli} has a positive modeled-tAI four-case signal but only a small measured-TE mediation fraction.

A joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition further shows that the direct abundance signal is not exhausted by one readout.  The joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. \paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite claim is ready at the level of a bounded four-case discriminant.  The matrix has nondegenerate rank, matched CDS/protein rows, concrete residual energies, positive $S^{QT}$ and $S^{QP}$ values for both modeled-tAI organism rows, and a complete A/B/C/D assignment for all coordinates.  The strongest row is yeast, where all nine coordinates enter case $D$ and the measured-TE mediation companion row gives a mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$.  The \emph{E. coli} row is still positive on both modeled-tAI and protein abundance, but two positive-positive coordinates fail the mediation-drop criterion in the modeled-tAI discriminant and the measured-TE companion row gives only $0.04091158340950673$ mediated fraction.  Therefore the chapter may state a finite translation-conditioned readback, not a universal law.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter does not infer observed ribosome dynamics from modeled tAI, and it does not infer folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, perturbation response, or causality from the four-case table.  Protein abundance is a steady-state omics readout and can absorb effects of transcription, translation, turnover, localization, degradation, post-translational modification, and measurement bias.  Case $D$ is therefore a controlled readout label: it records that a $Q\to P$ association is reduced after conditioning on the stated $T$ readout in this finite design.  A mechanism claim would require an additional matched contact, such as perturbation, rescue, time ordering, direct ribosome kinetics, protein-state measurement, or functional assay, evaluated on the same coordinate surface under its own controls.
