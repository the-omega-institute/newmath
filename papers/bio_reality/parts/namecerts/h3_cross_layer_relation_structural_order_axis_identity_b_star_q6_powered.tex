\subsection{B-star Q6 structural-order axis identity}\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-structural-order-axis-identity}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite cross-layer surface whose coordinate rows are the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed question is not whether these coordinates are a biological law. It is narrower: after the residual coordinate basis is fitted against a structural-order readout under the registered finite-row design, does one coordinate become the unique carried axis of the held-out structural-order increment? The run identified the claim as \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.structural\_order\_axis\_identity.b\_star\_q6\_powered}, used experiment run \texttt{fac1206f6516}, required a minimum joined block size of $300$, and evaluated $5$ folds. The reported conclusion was \texttt{structural\_order\_carried\_by\_f3\_stress}.

\paragraph{Axis scores.}
The fold-level sign-flip audit gives a concrete ordered list of coordinate increments. The held-out weighted $\Delta R^2$ values and one-sided sign-flip probabilities were:
$$
\begin{aligned}
K\_AAA &: \Delta R^2=0.0005307046179682497,\quad p=0.2294921875,\\
Arg\_AGR &: \Delta R^2=0.0020139270616115154,\quad p=0.142578125,\\
Ile\_AUA &: \Delta R^2=0.0013067592718559024,\quad p=0.0693359375,\\
Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR &: \Delta R^2=-0.0003205780368125179,\quad p=0.779296875,\\
Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG &: \Delta R^2=0.00017615626369389472,\quad p=0.24609375,\\
Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY &: \Delta R^2=0.0010134090056701938,\quad p=0.2158203125,\\
Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG &: \Delta R^2=0.00004390882136492317,\quad p=0.4873046875,\\
Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY &: \Delta R^2=-0.00045340330387383447,\quad p=0.529296875,\\
f3\_stress &: \Delta R^2=0.0031744502294756994,\quad p=0.03125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only $f3\_stress$ passed the fold-level sign-flip gate. It was also the dominant loading coordinate, with weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, rank $1$ by weighted increment, and positive dominance both as the increment axis and as the loading axis. Thus the finite-row verdict is an axis-identity statement: within this controlled structural-order readout, the detectable $B^{*}_{Q6}$ signal is carried by the third-position stress coordinate rather than by the leucine, serine, lysine, arginine, isoleucine, or threonine residual contrasts.

\paragraph{Readout interpretation.}
The readout is a structural-order association surface, not a molecular-structure assay. The supported statement is therefore:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\exists q\in Q\ \text{such that}\ q=f3\_stress,\\
&\Delta R^2(q)=0.0031744502294756994,\quad p(q)=0.03125,\\
&\text{and no other coordinate in }Q\text{ passes the same sign-flip gate.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The negative and nonpassing coordinates are part of the conclusion. $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$ and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ had negative weighted held-out increments, while the remaining positive coordinates did not meet the sign-flip threshold. The result therefore does not license a broad statement that every $Q6$ residual carries structural order. The finite result isolates one coordinate, $f3\_stress$, as the observed carrier under this design.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance rows.}
Separate finite-row runs show that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates can participate in translation-conditioned protein-abundance decompositions, but those runs do not by themselves turn the present axis call into a translation, folding, or mechanism claim. In the twelve-organism mediation run, the joined protein counts were $4042$ for \textit{Bacillus subtilis} strain 168, $11661$ for \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \textit{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. The corresponding modeled mediation fractions $M^{QTP}$ were $0.6084594553474505$, $0.4872268357227724$, $0.3157053953614641$, $0.6165167748739756$, $0.612922252793667$, $0.1976903964724546$, $0.27206289160119157$, $0.15488799907323988$, $0.08397602919861291$, $0.17489681798543982$, $0.9491551041216973$, and $0.01850668564589326$, respectively. These values are statistical decompositions using modeled per-protein tAI and measured protein abundance; they are not ribosome-footprint measurements and are not folding measurements.

\paragraph{Yeast structure and mass-balance checks.}
The yeast five-way structure-mediated abundance run joined $4759$ rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and overall join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$. In that run, the total residual association was $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, conditioning on the structure proxy alone gave $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, conditioning on measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability gave $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and adding the structure proxy to those controls gave $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$. The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the incremental structure fraction beyond $T,M$ was $0.0005507778820193332$, and $S\_Q$ structure $R^2$ was $0.003774248455422586$ with right-tail permutation probability $0.027972027972027972$. This supports using structure-related readouts as bounded association surfaces, but it does not provide a direct folded-state observation for the present axis identity.

A dynamic mass-balance run also cautions against over-promotion. In a fixed subsample of $120$ genes from $4434$ eligible genes, using $718$ intervals, the $M$-only model had $R^2=0.9960628201518571$, the $M,E_{stat}$ model had $R^2=0.996410628851051$, while later blocks dropped to $R^2=0.9712443377743347$ and $0.8953999568421264$. The final $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.07584438093220836$, with $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{TEbin}=0.6831683168316832$, and time-shuffle probability $0.9306930693069307$. These rows are consistent with a bounded execution analysis rather than a universal mechanistic assertion.

\paragraph{Evolutionary and execution-side context.}
The cross-ortholog selection-imprint run used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species. Its positive selection-imprint gate did not pass: $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$, maximum null $95\%$ $\Delta R^2=0.05427157066522958$, and \texttt{positive\_selection\_imprint=false}. Thus the present $f3\_stress$ structural-order axis must not be restated as evidence of cross-ortholog selection. A different execution-decomposition run in yeast used $3289$ genes and returned \texttt{observable\_execution\_subblock}; that result records at least one observed rate-proxy slot passing its effective null classes, but it remains a separate rate-proxy statement.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter-level boundary is strict. The ready verdict says that, in the registered finite-row structural-order axis experiment, $f3\_stress$ is the unique significant and dominant $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate, with $\Delta R^2=0.0031744502294756994$, $p=0.03125$, and weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$. It does not say that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ geometry has been observed as translation, ribosome occupancy, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, adaptive selection, or a universal biological rule. Each of those stronger readings would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled finite-row statistic. Direct residual-to-protein or residual-to-structure wording is therefore disallowed unless the intermediate translation or structure-level readout is explicitly measured or otherwise bounded by a separate contact in the same analysis surface.
