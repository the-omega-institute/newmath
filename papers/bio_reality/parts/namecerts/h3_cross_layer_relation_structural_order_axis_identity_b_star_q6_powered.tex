\subsection{Finite axis identity for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ structural-order contact}
\label{subsec:bioreality-bstar-q6-structural-order-axis-identity}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The tested object is a finite matrix of residual coordinates, controlled readouts, and axis scores.  The residual side contains exactly the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates
$K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The structural-order audit ran with five folds and required at least $300$ joined rows per fold-level evaluation.  Its registered conclusion was that structural order, on this surface, is carried by the $f_3$-stress coordinate rather than by the full residual basis as an undifferentiated block.

\paragraph{Axis calculation.}
The finite decision was made by comparing held-out weighted increments and fold-level sign-flip probabilities across the nine coordinates.  The dominant coordinate was $f_3$-stress, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, rank $1$ by weighted increment, and sign-flip right-tail probability $0.03125$.  The audit reported it as both the dominant increment axis and the dominant loading axis, and it was the only coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate.  The remaining coordinates stayed below the axis-identity threshold: $Arg\_{AGR}$ had increment $0.0020139270616115154$ with $p=0.142578125$, $Ile\_{AUA}$ had $0.0013067592718559024$ with $p=0.0693359375$, $K\_{AAA}$ had $0.0005307046179682497$ with $p=0.2294921875$, $Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$ had $0.0010134090056701938$ with $p=0.2158203125$, $Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$ had $0.00017615626369389472$ with $p=0.24609375$, and $Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$ had $0.00004390882136492317$ with $p=0.4873046875$.  Two coordinates were negative on the same held-out scale: $Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$ gave $-0.0003205780368125179$ with $p=0.779296875$, and $Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$ gave $-0.00045340330387383447$ with $p=0.529296875$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{f_3} &= 0.0031744502294756994,\\
p_{f_3} &= 0.03125,\\
|u_{f_3}|_{w} &= 0.5109586606724208.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Readout interpretation.}
The positive result is an axis-identity result, not a promotion to a higher biological layer.  It says that among the finite $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates tested on this structural-order surface, $f_3$-stress is the unique coordinate that survives the registered fold-level sign-flip comparison.  It does not by itself establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality.  Those claims require their own layer-matched contacts and cannot be inferred from the sign-flip axis result.

\paragraph{Translation and protein-abundance guardrail.}
The broader cross-layer ledger contains a separate translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition over twelve organisms.  That audit used the same nine residual coordinates and joined per-protein modeled tAI to log$_{10}$ PAXdb abundance after amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, organism-level baselines, and the $M$-density baseline were controlled.  The joined protein counts were $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The corresponding mediated fractions $M^{QTP}$ were $0.6084594553474505$, $0.4872268357227724$, $0.3157053953614641$, $0.6165167748739756$, $0.612922252793667$, $0.1976903964724546$, $0.27206289160119157$, $0.15488799907323988$, $0.08397602919861291$, $0.17489681798543982$, $0.9491551041216973$, and $0.01850668564589326$, respectively.  These finite values show that a controlled statistical mediation surface exists, but the structural-order axis call remains narrower: the $f_3$-stress coordinate is identified on the structural-order audit, while protein-abundance mediation remains a separate statistical decomposition and not a causal mechanism.

\paragraph{Yeast structure-mediation comparison.}
A yeast five-way join supplies a useful negative-control shape for overinterpretation.  The join retained $4759$ genes, with a reported CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$ and an overall join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$.  In that joined surface, the residualized protein-abundance association gave $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, while conditioning on the 5-prime CDS MFE proxy gave $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$.  Conditioning on measured translation efficiency plus mRNA stability gave $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and adding the structure proxy produced $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction was therefore only $0.003284564452662019$, and the structure-incremental fraction beyond translation efficiency and mRNA stability was $0.0005507778820193332$, with permutation probabilities $0.027972027972027972$ for $S_Q$ structure association and $0.1258741258741259$ for the incremental structure fraction.  This supports the local boundary: the axis identity is compatible with a small sequence-derived structural proxy signal, but it is not a folded-structure measurement.

\paragraph{Modeled translation contact.}
The modeled tAI survival packet also remains bounded.  It used three organisms, not a powered multi-organism validation panel, and reported $81$ entries above the coupling threshold $0.01$ with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  The three-organism matrix is explicitly marked as insufficient for powered survival, even though all standard amino-acid families were represented in the local GtRNAdb payloads for \emph{E. coli} K12, human, and yeast.  Thus a modeled adaptation-weight contact can help define a translation-readout surface, but it is not ribosome profiling, protein output, folded structure, or mechanism evidence.

\paragraph{Independent boundary checks.}
Other finite audits keep the same separation.  The observable-execution decomposition in yeast used $3289$ genes and reached the verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  The mass-balance dynamic closure audit used $120$ genes and $718$ intervals, with $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628201518571$, $R^2_{L1}=0.996410628851051$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443377743347$, $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$, and a negative final increment $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ with $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits.  The cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; its positive-imprint gate was not supported because $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$ stayed below the maximum null95 value $0.05427157066522958$, while leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.0021477729853302424$.  These checks are consistent with the structural-order verdict: the finite data identify $f_3$-stress as the structural-order axis on the registered surface, while translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, selection, phenotype, and universality remain outside the claim unless a separate matched contact and statistic supplies them.
