\subsection{Finite axis identity for the $B^*_{Q6}$ structural-order readout}\label{sec:biorality-bstar-q6-structural-order-axis-identity}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record considered here is a finite mediation-and-axis surface whose rows are indexed by residual coordinates $q_i$, bounded translation readouts $t_j$, and downstream protein-abundance or structural-order readouts $p_k$. The nine residual coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The finite computation named the same nine axes in its computed coordinate list and evaluated them on $5$ held-out folds with a minimum joined-row threshold of $300$.

\paragraph{Axis verdict.}
The finite sign-flip audit identifies exactly one coordinate as passing the fold-level gate. The selected coordinate is $f3\_stress$, and it is both the dominant increment axis and the dominant loading axis. Its held-out weighted increment is positive, its unit loading is the largest reported loading, and its sign-flip tail probability is below the finite gate used in the run:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{f3} &= 0.0031744502294756994,\\
|\lambda_{f3}| &= 0.5109586606724208,\\
p_{\mathrm{signflip}}(f3\_stress > 0) &= 0.03125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit therefore supports the scoped statement that the observed structural-order signal in this finite row system is carried by the $f3\_stress$ axis, not by a diffuse collection of all $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates. The run also reports that the full $B^*_{Q6}$ signal is significant, but the coordinate-level decomposition assigns the positive axis identity to exactly one coordinate.

\paragraph{Rejected competing axes.}
The remaining eight coordinates do not pass the same fold-level sign-flip gate. Their reported increments and sign-flip probabilities are finite and are kept as negative or non-promoted coordinate evidence:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{Arg\_AGR} &= 0.0020139270616115154, & p &= 0.142578125,\\
\Delta R^2_{Ile\_AUA} &= 0.0013067592718559024, & p &= 0.0693359375,\\
\Delta R^2_{K\_AAA} &= 0.0005307046179682497, & p &= 0.2294921875,\\
\Delta R^2_{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR} &= -0.0003205780368125179, & p &= 0.779296875,\\
\Delta R^2_{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG} &= 0.00017615626369389472, & p &= 0.24609375,\\
\Delta R^2_{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG} &= 0.00004390882136492317, & p &= 0.4873046875,\\
\Delta R^2_{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY} &= 0.0010134090056701938, & p &= 0.2158203125,\\
\Delta R^2_{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY} &= -0.00045340330387383447, & p &= 0.529296875.
\end{aligned}
$$
This table is not read as evidence that the other axes are absent from biology. It says only that, under this finite row design and this fold-level null, they do not carry the accepted structural-order axis identity.

\paragraph{Mediation context.}
The structural-order result sits beside a separate translation-mediated abundance calculation in which the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates were joined to protein abundance and modeled per-protein tAI. That larger mediation run had $12$ organism-scoped panels. Examples include $19{,}053$ joined human proteins, $19{,}395$ mouse proteins, $13{,}295$ Drosophila proteins, $5{,}815$ yeast proteins, $4{,}042$ Bacillus subtilis proteins, and $1{,}656$ Sulfolobus solfataricus proteins. In that computation, the reported mediated fractions $M^{QTP}$ stayed in the unit interval for every organism. The largest reported fraction was yeast, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
R^2_{QP,\mathrm{total}} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,\mathrm{direct}} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895.
\end{aligned}
$$
Other finite fractions include $0.612922252793667$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.6084594553474505$ for Bacillus subtilis, $0.6165167748739756$ for Drosophila melanogaster, $0.4872268357227724$ for Caenorhabditis elegans, $0.3157053953614641$ for Danio rerio, $0.27206289160119157$ for Homo sapiens, $0.17489681798543982$ for Rattus norvegicus, $0.15488799907323988$ for Mus musculus, $0.08397602919861291$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, and $0.01850668564589326$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus. These mediation scalars justify treating the structural-order axis computation as a cross-layer finite-row question, while still keeping the axis identity separate from causal claims.

\paragraph{Translation readout boundary.}
A modeled tAI survival matrix was also computed for three organisms: Escherichia coli K12, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The contact contained all $20$ standard amino-acid families for each organism, with $89$ FASTA headers in E. coli K12, $432$ in human, and $275$ in yeast. The modeled tAI computation covered all $61$ sense codons in each organism. It produced $81$ entries above the bounded-contact threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute entry
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max |S_{ij}| &= 3801961.031607896.
\end{aligned}
$$
However, this three-organism modeled-tAI contact is not a powered measured translation-survival result. The finite record explicitly states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival when the requested condition requires at least $40$ matched species. The structural-order axis result is therefore not promoted to measured ribosome occupancy or universal translation realization.

\paragraph{Yeast structure-mediation comparison.}
A yeast five-way join gives a second bounded comparison surface for the same coordinate family. It joined $4{,}759$ genes across CDS, protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, mRNA half-life, and a $5'$ coding-region folding-free-energy proxy. The reported join hit rate was $0.8818572656921754$, and the CDS join hit rate was $0.9156038419146592$. After residualizing controls, the single and joint variance-partition values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}
$$
The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the structure-incremental fraction was $0.0005507778820193332$, and the coordinate-to-structure statistic was $S_Q$ structure $R^2 = 0.003774248455422586$ with right-tail permutation probability $0.027972027972027972$. This comparison is useful because it shows a finite structural proxy adjacent to the abundance and translation surfaces, but it is still a statistical partition over a CDS-derived proxy, not direct folding dynamics.

\paragraph{Independent finite-code calibration.}
The broader BioReality calibration surface includes a locked genetic-code contact in which $40$ of $40$ independent replication trials succeeded and $40$ of $40$ boundary-probe trials succeeded. Both exact one-sided binomial upper-tail probabilities were $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$. The same calibration reported $0$ unexpected successes in $20$ negative-control trials and $0$ unexpected successes in $20$ critical-ablation trials. These numbers are not used to infer protein structure or function here. They only establish that the coding-table level of the record has a finite, externally checked calibration before the higher-layer residual analyses are read.

\paragraph{Local admissible reading.}
The admissible readback is narrow. The finite row evidence supports the statement that, within the audited $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate set and under the reported held-out sign-flip design, $f3\_stress$ is the unique accepted axis for the structural-order readout. The accepted claim is an axis-identity claim over a controlled finite surface. It is not a claim that synonymous residual geometry by itself causes translation, determines folding, proves physical admissibility, realizes molecular function, supplies a mechanism, or states a universal biological law. Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic after the translation, abundance, mRNA-control, and structural-proxy boundaries have been kept distinct.
