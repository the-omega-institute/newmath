\subsection{Finite-row axis identity for the powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ structural-order readout}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
The claim is a finite-row axis-identification statement for the powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual panel.  The tested coordinate family contains nine residual coordinates:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The finite computation used five folds, required at least $300$ joined rows per fold surface, and evaluated whether the structural-order signal was carried by the whole residual panel or by a specific coordinate axis.  The registered conclusion is that the structural-order increment is carried by $f3\_stress$: the dominant increment axis and the dominant loading axis are both $f3\_stress$, and exactly one coordinate passed the fold-level sign-flip gate.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite conclusion is narrow: in the powered structural-order axis experiment, the only coordinate that survives the sign-flip criterion is $f3\_stress$, with $p=0.03125$ and held-out weighted increment $0.0031744502294756994$.  The full $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ panel is significant in that finite audit, but the coordinate-level interpretation is asymmetric: $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$ and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ are negative in held-out increment, several coordinates are positive but non-significant, and only $f3\_stress$ is promoted to the identified structural-order axis.  The result is therefore an axis-local structural-order readback over finite joined rows, not a universal synonymous-code law.
