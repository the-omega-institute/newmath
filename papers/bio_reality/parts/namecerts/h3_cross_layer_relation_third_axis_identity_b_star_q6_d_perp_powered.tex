\subsection{Powered third-axis identity for the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-third-axis-identity}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the third residual direction in the $B^*_{Q6}$ coding-sequence surface as a finite, organism-indexed coordinate question.  The carrier is the CDS-by-organism residual surface after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $f_3$-stress coordinate and to rows for which a matched translation readout can in principle be attached.  The finite codon boundary used by the record is explicit.  The saturated $f_3$ image of the $R$-set contains
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The comparison set $M=\mathrm{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains the same eighteen codons together with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty, so the finite topology leaves no separate unsaturated boundary codon set to explain the third axis.  The biological question is therefore not whether the codon list itself has an extra boundary, but whether the residual direction left after the amino-acid quotient has a reproducible readout identity across organisms.

\paragraph{Powered identity estimate.}
The powered third-axis identity assay was run with five folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms.  The resulting identity call is that $d_{\perp}$ aligns with within-family usage frequency.  Quantitatively, the mean cosine is $0.128$, and the one-sided sign-test probability is $p=0.000488$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathrm{axis}}(d_{\perp})&=\mathrm{usage\_frequency\_within\_family},\\
\overline{\cos}(d_{\perp},\mathrm{usage})&=0.128,\\
p_{\mathrm{sign}}&=0.000488.
\end{aligned}
$$
The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  It identifies the third residual coordinate as a usage-frequency-within-family direction in the powered finite panel; it does not by itself say that the direction has a measured ribosome effect, a protein-abundance effect, or a downstream cellular consequence.

\paragraph{Relation to the two-axis closure.}
The same finite row family contains a two-axis closure result in which the tRNA direction and the $f_3$-translation-selection direction do not exhaust the organism-level residual surface.  In that assay, the powered panel again used five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested.  The call was \emph{third\_axis\_present}: $d_{\perp}$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms.  A companion residual-optimality assay, with the same fold count, organism count, protein threshold, and synonymous design dimension, identified the dominant non-tRNA residual optimality axis as $f_3$ translation selection.  These two records place the present identity call in a three-direction picture: a tRNA-related axis, an $f_3$ translation-selection axis, and a remaining $d_{\perp}$ direction whose powered identity estimate is within-family usage frequency.

\paragraph{GC and positional controls.}
The third-axis mechanism assay separates the usage-frequency identity from a purely GC-driven explanation.  It was run on the same powered design scale: five folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested.  Its recorded conclusion is selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, with the CSC or positional identity still requiring a separate external row.  The reason attached to the run is that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.  Thus the present chapter can use the powered evidence to say that $d_{\perp}$ is not reduced to GC3 rank alone, while still refusing to promote the axis to a detailed ribosomal or positional mechanism without a measured translation contact.

\paragraph{Spectrum placement.}
The per-organism axis-strength spectrum supplies a useful scale check.  In the $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, with five folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns, the captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$.  The per-organism dominant counts were $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$, and the largest GC3 rank effect reported in that spectrum was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.  These numbers keep the third-axis claim in proportion.  The $d_{\perp}$ identity is statistically visible, but the broader spectrum says that the strongest captured direction in this finite panel is the fourth residual direction, not the third-axis identity itself.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
The biological readback is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ for a bounded translation row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The required asymmetry is contextual: after amino-acid projection, an $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance can become a dynamical translation distinction only where the corresponding tRNA or wobble weights break $f_3$ symmetry, written locally as $w_c\ne w_{c^{f_3}}$.  A separate tRNA-poverty assay gives finite support for this kind of supply asymmetry: over $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested, with $6$ skipped organisms, $1000$ permutations, and $\alpha=0.05$, the $R$ sense codons were called systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb gene-copy counts together with dos Reis wobble weights.  That result supports the plausibility of a tRNA-supply interface for the codon boundary, but it is still not a measured survival entry for the $f_3$-stress coordinate.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
This chapter therefore records a powered coordinate identity, not an unrestricted biological law.  No claim about measured translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, downstream mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from the $d_{\perp}$ identity alone.  Such claims require a separate layer-matched contact and an organism-matched controlled statistic.  In particular, a three-organism all-$1.0$ controlled surface is rank-degenerate and is treated as a null boundary rather than as survival.  Modeled tAI or stAI coupling may be used only as a bounded translation-layer readout unless it is paired with measured rows and controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA abundance.  The present record is falsified at the translation layer if the controlled entries $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ remain non-positive or rank-degenerate across a powered panel.
