\subsection{The $f_3$-stress axis as a bounded translation-readout question}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-translation-readout}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate being tested.}
This record isolates one finite coordinate of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface: the $f_3$-stress direction after synonymous codons have been compared within amino-acid families. The object is not a protein-level phenotype and not a claim about molecular function. It is a codon-coordinate question with a narrowly specified possible readout: whether the $f_3$-paired residual imbalance survives in a matched translation row after ordinary sequence and organism covariates have been controlled. The coordinate is meaningful only where third-position symmetry is broken by a context-dependent tRNA, wobble, or codon-adaptation weight asymmetry, written locally as $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$. If the paired weights remain symmetric, the coordinate is retained as geometry but has no licensed translation interpretation.

\paragraph{Saturation rows.}
The finite $f_3$ saturation calculation gives the row set against which the stress coordinate is read. The computed set $M = {\rm Med}_R \cup \{ACA,ACG\}$ contains the $20$ codons
$AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG$.
After applying $f_3$ saturation to $R$, the retained row contains $18$ codons,
$AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG$,
so the two codons $ACA$ and $ACG$ are present in $M$ but absent from $Sat_{f_3}(R)$. The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty. In finite-row terms this means that the stress coordinate is not introduced by an unfilled boundary row; it is a residual distinction inside a closed codon surface, with the $ACA/ACG$ exception explicitly visible.

$$\begin{aligned}
|M| &= 20,\\
|Sat_{f_3}(R)| &= 18,\\
M \setminus Sat_{f_3}(R) &= \{ACA,ACG\},\\
\partial_{f_3}R &= \varnothing.
\end{aligned}$$

\paragraph{Observed residual identity.}
The powered third-axis identity experiment identifies the residual direction $d_\perp$ with the candidate axis ``usage frequency within family.'' The run used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous-design columns. The reported mean cosine was $0.128$, with one-sided sign-test $p = 0.000488$. Thus the finite observation is not that $d_\perp$ is already a measured translation effect; it is that the third residual coordinate aligns weakly but systematically with within-family usage frequency across the powered panel.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding spectrum.}
Several neighboring finite rows constrain the interpretation. The universal residual-axis run reported that the non-tRNA residual optimality aligns most with $f_3$ translation selection, using the same $5$-fold, $11$-of-$16$ organism, $41$-column design. The two-axis closure run concluded that a third axis is present, because $d_\perp$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed organism directions. The three-axis sufficiency run concluded that a fourth mechanism remains present, so the three-axis description is not a complete biological explanation. The per-organism spectrum was led by $d_{resid4}$ on average, with dominant-count summary $\{d_{resid4}:11\}$, domain counts $\{archaea:1,bacteria:2,eukaryote:8\}$, and largest GC3 rank effect $\rho = 0.136$ for $d_{usage}$. The boundary-set tRNA-poverty run computed $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and found the $R$ sense codons systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. These rows make the stress coordinate biologically plausible as a translation-layer target, but they do not themselves supply a same-row ribosome or translation-efficiency statistic.

\paragraph{Required survival statistic.}
The admissible readout is the controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a bounded translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. A positive entry requires matched rows for coding-sequence codon counts, organism identity, wobble or codon-adaptation weights, a translation readout, and matched mRNA abundance when translation efficiency is formed as a residual or ratio. The control set must include amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA where applicable, rank checks, and simpler codon baselines. The local support condition is therefore not the sign of $d_\perp$ alone but a non-vacuous controlled survival inequality:

$$\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} &> 0
\end{aligned}$$

after the declared controls and baseline ablations. If every controlled entry is non-positive, or if the fitted surface is rank-degenerate, the translation-readout claim fails for this packet. In particular, an all-$1.0$ three-organism matrix is treated as rank saturation rather than evidence of survival.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The finite rows above license only a residual-coordinate and bounded-readout question. They do not establish observed translation output, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism, causation, adaptation, or universality. Modelled tAI or stAI weights may be used as bounded codon-adaptation readbacks, but they are not direct ribosome-footprint measurements. A downstream protein, structure, function, or phenotype statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own statistic after the same-coordinate translation step has first survived. Until such rows exist, the chapter records a sharp test: the $f_3$-stress coordinate is a candidate translation-readout distinction only in contexts where third-position wobble symmetry is broken, and it is falsified as a translation-readout distinction when the controlled $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ row remains absent, non-positive, or rank-degenerate.
