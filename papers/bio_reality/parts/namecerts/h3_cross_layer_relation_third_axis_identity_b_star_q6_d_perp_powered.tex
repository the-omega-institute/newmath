\subsection{Third-position stress as a bounded translation-readout candidate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-translation-readout}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The record considered here is the finite CDS-by-organism residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection and restriction to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ third-position stress coordinate.  Its purpose is narrow: it asks whether a synonymous residual direction that survives the amino-acid quotient can become visible to a translation-layer readout only when the organism-level tRNA or wobble-weight surface fails to respect the third-position $f_{3}$ pairing.  The coordinate is therefore not a protein-structure claim and not a phenotypic claim.  It is a finite relation among codon usage rows, tRNA or wobble-weight rows, and an explicitly bounded translation readout row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.

\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The finite $f_{3}$ saturation row fixes the codon support before any biological interpretation is attempted.  The recorded $M$ carrier is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_{3}$-saturated $R$ surface removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from that carrier:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_{3}}(R) = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_{3}$ boundary is empty, $\partial_{f_{3}}R=\varnothing$.  This matters because the conjectural readout is not being introduced through an unsaturated codon exception.  Any later translation signal must come from a controlled residual relation on the finite surface, not from an unaccounted codon outside the recorded support.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row identity.}
The powered third-axis identity assay passed on run $377671f96e96$.  It requested $16$ organisms and computed $11$ usable organism rows, with at least $500$ proteins per organism, $5$ folds, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  On those rows, the third residual direction $d_{\perp}$ was provisionally identified with usage frequency within synonymous family.  The reported mean cosine was $0.128$, and the one-sided sign-test value was $p=0.000488$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\cos}(d_{\perp},\mathrm{usage\ frequency\ within\ family}) &= 0.128,\\
p_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 0.000488.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite-row identity statement about the residual codon-usage coordinate.  It is positive evidence that the third axis is not merely a naming artifact, but it is still not a measured ribosome or translation-efficiency survival statistic.

\paragraph{Mechanism-relevant neighboring rows.}
The same finite panel records several constraints around this axis.  The third-axis mechanism row passed with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, $5$ folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns; its conclusion was that the axis is selection-aligned after GC orthogonalization, not explained as a GC-only direction, and not resolved as a wobble-clustered mechanism.  The universal residual optimality row also passed with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested and identified the dominant non-tRNA residual optimality axis as $f_{3}$ translation selection.  The two-axis closure row passed on the same $11$ of $16$ organism scale and concluded that a third axis remains present, with systematically positive held-out $R^{2}$ and same-signed $d_{\perp}$ directions across organisms.  The axis-strength spectrum row computed $11$ organisms, used $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns, and reported that $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ led the captured spectra on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; all $11$ per-organism dominant counts were assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, while the largest GC3 rank effect was only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  Finally, the three-axis sufficiency row passed with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested and concluded that a fourth mechanism remains present.  These rows keep the third-axis identity in a finite surrounding spectrum: the signal is reproducible enough to name, but not sufficient to close the translation-readout interface by itself.

\paragraph{Readout rule.}
The bounded readback for this packet is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a translation-layer readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The intended statistic is positive only if the $f_{3}$-stress coordinate improves the translation readout after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, matched mRNA abundance where applicable, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon baselines have been accounted for.  In schematic form, the admissible positive statement has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
under the declared controls.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface on a three-organism panel is a null boundary for this purpose, not survival evidence.  Likewise, a modeled tAI or stAI surface is at most a bounded codon-adaptation readout unless it is kept separate from observed ribosome or TE rows.

\paragraph{Falsification condition.}
The conjecture fails at the translation boundary if the controlled entries remain non-positive or rank-degenerate across a powered panel:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} \leq 0
\end{aligned}
$$
for every declared translation readout $T_j$, after the finite controls are applied.  It also fails in the stronger practical sense if the only apparent signal is explained by amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, length, M-density, transcript abundance, or simpler codon-usage baselines.  The finite third-axis identity row then remains a codon-usage residual fact, but it does not cross into measured translation readout.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter permits one local conclusion: in the finite powered rows, $d_{\perp}$ is identified with usage frequency within synonymous family, and its neighboring spectrum makes the $f_{3}$-stress translation-readout test worth posing.  Nothing stronger follows here.  No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, localization, stability, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, causation, adaptation claim, or universal biological law is licensed unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant rows and the corresponding controlled statistic is computed.  The current record is therefore a bounded finite-coordinate chapter with an explicit open translation-readout gate, not a downstream biological promotion.
