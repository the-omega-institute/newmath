\subsection{Third-axis identity of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-third-axis-identity}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-by-organism residual coordinate surface after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate family and, inside that family, to the third residual direction denoted $d_{\perp}$. The biological object is not a protein phenotype or a universal law. It is a rank-powered comparison between synonymous codon residual coordinates, organism-scoped codon usage, tRNA or wobble-weight annotations, and bounded translation-readout routes. The module class is a cross-layer relation, and the finite record is organized into three local roles: relation, spectrum, and trigger. Relation names the tested coordinate-to-readout pairing; spectrum records which residual directions dominate in the computed panel; trigger records the symmetry-breaking condition under which the third-position $f_3$ coordinate is allowed to become biologically testable.

\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The $f_3$ saturation calculation gives the local codon boundary used by this chapter. The finite set $M=\mathrm{Med}(R)\cup\{ACA\}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
After applying $\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}$ to $R$, the two codons $ACA$ and $ACG$ drop out, leaving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty. Thus the chapter does not treat the third-base transform as a free global degree of freedom. It is a residual coordinate that becomes empirically meaningful only when paired with an organism-context readout that can distinguish the two $f_3$-paired codon sides.

\paragraph{Third-axis identity.}
The powered identity assay for the third axis used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, requested $16$ organisms, computed $11$ organisms, and used $41$ synonymous design columns. In that finite panel, $d_{\perp}$ aligned with the candidate axis named usage frequency within synonymous family. The reported mean cosine was $0.128$, with one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. In local notation, the assay supports the bounded identification
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\perp} &\leadsto \text{usage-frequency-within-family},\\
 \overline{\cos}(d_{\perp},u_{\mathrm{family}}) &= 0.128,\\
 p_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 0.000488.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an identity statement about the finite residual coordinate surface, not a statement that the coordinate controls abundance, localization, folded structure, function, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding spectrum.}
The third-axis result sits inside a larger rank-powered spectrum. The two-axis closure assay, also run with $5$ folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, a $500$ protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns, recorded a third axis as present: $d_{\perp}$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms. The residual optimality assay on the same organism count and design width identified the non-tRNA residual optimality direction most strongly with $f_3$ translation selection. The per-organism axis-strength spectrum computed $11$ organisms and found that $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ led on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; all $11$ per-organism dominant counts were assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$. The same spectrum explicitly reported domain means as descriptive under an imbalanced panel, with counts $n=1$ archaea, $n=2$ bacteria, and $n=8$ eukaryotes, and the largest GC3 rank effect was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.

\paragraph{Mechanism boundary.}
A separate third-axis mechanism assay reported that the signal is selection-not-GC, not wobble-clustered, and that CSC or positional identity still needs an additional layer of measurement. Its stated reason was that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ kept significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. A related $R$-sense boundary assay used $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested, skipped $6$ organisms, and ran $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$; it found that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. These rows justify a local mechanistic pressure toward tRNA-supply asymmetry, but they do not by themselves establish a ribosome-occupancy, translation-efficiency, or protein-output effect for the $f_3$-stress coordinate.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
For the $f_3$-stress conjecture, the admissible readback is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a bounded translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The statistic must be evaluated after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where applicable, rank, and simpler codon baselines. The support condition is strictly local:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
for at least one nondegenerate translation row under the declared controls. The break condition is equally local: if all such controlled entries are non-positive, or if the fitted matrix is rank-degenerate, the coordinate has no positive translation-readout survival in this packet. In particular, an all-$1.0$ three-organism matrix is treated as saturation of rank, not as evidence for survival.

\paragraph{Symmetry-breaking requirement.}
The $f_3$ coordinate is allowed to become dynamical only when the readout surface can see an asymmetry between a codon $c$ and its $f_3$-paired codon $c^{f_3}$. The required trigger is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
 w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $w_c$ may be a context tRNA weight, a wobble-weight annotation, or a bounded adaptation weight in the organism-specific readout. If $w_c=w_{c^{f_3}}$ throughout the tested context, the third-base residual coordinate remains a geometric distinction without a translation-layer test. The chapter therefore separates codon topology from translation realization: the topology supplies the candidate coordinate, while a same-organism and same-CDS translation row is required before any positive survival statement can be made.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The finite rows support a bounded identity claim for $d_{\perp}$ and a bounded condition for testing $f_3$-stress against translation readouts. They do not imply that $f_3$-stress changes mature protein abundance, folded structure, localization, molecular function, physical admissibility, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universality. tAI or stAI weights are modelled translation-adaptation readbacks, not observed ribosome footprints. Ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows, when present, still require same-row mRNA control and the stated covariate adjustment. Protein, structure, physical, functional, localization, stability, interaction, perturbation, or phenotype claims each require their own layer-matched contact and their own recorded statistic. Without that separate contact, the present chapter stops at the finite residual-axis identity and the explicit translation-readout survival test.
