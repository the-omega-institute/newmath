\subsection{Third-position residual identity for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-third-axis-identity}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate under test.}
This record concerns the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface after amino-acid projection, restricted to the third-position stress coordinate and to translation-layer readouts that can be matched by organism or by coding sequence. The finite coordinate is not a claim about protein state. It is a row-level question: after removing amino-acid composition and ordinary sequence-scale covariates, does the residual direction $d_{\perp}$ have a reproducible identity, and can that identity later be tested against translation readouts only where the readout row is present?

The finite $f_{3}$ saturation memory fixes the local alphabet used by the coordinate. The row $M=\operatorname{Med}_{R}\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains the twenty codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_{3}$-saturated subset removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, leaving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_{3}}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_{3}$ boundary set is empty. Thus this chapter treats the third-position coordinate as a finite residual surface with a specified codon support, not as an inferred molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row identity.}
The powered identity assay for the third axis used five folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, and computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms. The synonymous design had $41$ columns. Under that finite design, $d_{\perp}$ aligned most directly with usage frequency within synonymous family: the mean cosine was $0.128$, and the one-sided sign-test value was $p=0.000488$. In the notation of this chapter, the finite-row statement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Id}(d_{\perp}) &= \text{usage frequency within synonymous family},\\
\overline{\cos}(d_{\perp},\text{usage-frequency}) &= 0.128,\\
p_{\mathrm{sign}} &= 0.000488.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an identity assignment for a residual direction inside the controlled codon-usage coordinate. It is not yet a translation-efficiency survival result. The data say that the third residual axis is reproducibly closer to within-family usage frequency than to the competing finite candidates in the powered panel.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding spectrum.}
The same powered family of assays gives the local context for the identity call. The residual optimality assay computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms with five folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns, and its dominant non-tRNA residual axis was recorded as $f_{3}$ translation selection. The two-axis closure assay, also over $11$ of $16$ organisms with the same fold count and design width, reported a third axis because $d_{\perp}$ had systematically positive held-out $R^{2}$ and mutually same-signed organism directions. The sufficiency assay then reported that a fourth mechanism remains present, so the $d_{\perp}$ identity is not a complete explanation of the whole residual spectrum.

The per-organism axis-strength spectrum computed $11$ organisms out of $16$, again with five folds and $41$ synonymous columns. Its descriptive summary placed $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ first on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; the dominant count was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$. Domain means were explicitly descriptive because the domain counts were imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms. The largest GC3 rank effect in that assay was only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$. Separately, the mechanism assay for $d_{\perp}$ concluded selection-not-GC and not-wobble-clustered, while marking CSC or positional identity as needing a further external row. Together, these finite rows make the third-axis identity biologically pointed but not biologically promoted.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
For the cross-layer reading, the only permitted readback is a bounded translation row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The intended statistic is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-stress},T_{j}}$, evaluated after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance when used, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations. A positive identity of $d_{\perp}$ with within-family usage frequency does not by itself supply such an entry.

The finite contact requirements are therefore asymmetric. Codon-usage tables can test sequence composition; tRNA gene-copy and tAI or stAI weights can supply bounded adaptation-weight readbacks; ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency rows can supply a measured translation row only when matched to the same organism or coding sequence; matched mRNA abundance is a control covariate, not a translation output. The current finite memory records the identity of $d_{\perp}$ but does not contain a same-organism and same-CDS joined matrix of codon counts, wobble weights, ribosome or TE rows, and mRNA controls for this exact $f_{3}$-stress packet.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The chapter therefore has a local no-promotion rule. The row identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\perp}\sim \text{within-family usage frequency}
\end{aligned}
$$
may be used as a finite codon-coordinate claim, and it may motivate a translation-layer test in contexts where $w_{c}\neq w_{c^{f_{3}}}$. It does not establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics. In particular, the three-organism all-$1.0$ controlled surface is a rank-degenerate boundary, not survival evidence, and modeled tAI or stAI coupling is not an observed ribosome-footprint result.

\paragraph{Falsification condition.}
The finite identity claim would be weakened if the powered residual direction ceased to align with within-family usage frequency under the same fold structure, organism threshold, and synonymous design width. The cross-layer translation conjecture has a stricter break condition: after attaching a rank-powered measured or explicitly bounded translation panel, every controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-stress},T_{j}}$ remains non-positive or rank-degenerate. In that case the $d_{\perp}$ identity would remain a codon-coordinate fact, but it would not survive as a translation-readout distinction.
