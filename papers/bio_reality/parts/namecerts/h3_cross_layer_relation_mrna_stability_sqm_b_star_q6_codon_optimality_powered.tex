\subsection{B-star Q6 codon residuals against yeast mRNA half-life}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mrna-stability-sqm}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate system retains measurable information about yeast mRNA stability after the usual coding-sequence covariates have already been removed.  The readout is $\log_{10}(t_{1/2}\,{\rm minutes})$ from the Neymotin 2014 yeast $4$tU metabolic-labeling half-life table.  The joined table contains $5085$ coding sequences.  The half-life side contributes $5379$ valid records keyed by the Neymotin \texttt{Syst} identifier, and the CDS side contributes $5815$ joined coding-sequence records after stripping the \texttt{4932.} prefix from the protein identifier.  The recorded CDS hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, with $730$ CDS rows missing a half-life match and no skipped rows from empty codon counts, invalid length, missing protein id, nonpositive abundance, unparseable yeast ORF, duplicate half-life key, missing half-life key, or nonpositive half-life.

\paragraph{Residual design.}
The tested coordinate block has the nine coordinates
$\texttt{K\_AAA}$, $\texttt{Arg\_AGR}$, $\texttt{Ile\_AUA}$, $\texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $\texttt{f3\_stress}$.  The control block has $24$ columns: an intercept, $\log({\rm cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$ computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  Its numerical rank is $23$, leaving $5062$ residual degrees of freedom for the stability regression.  Thus the reported score is not a raw codon-composition association; it is the incremental readout energy of the residualized $Q$ block after amino-acid composition, length, GC3, and the simpler codon-density baseline have been accounted for.

\paragraph{Measured survival score.}
Let $M$ denote the residualized yeast mRNA half-life readout and let $Q$ denote the residualized nine-column $B^{*}_{Q6}$ design.  The finite-row computation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QM} &= 0.05179429044975085,\\
\operatorname{rank}(Q) &= 9,\\
\|\widehat M_Q\|^2 &= 11.909960434207074,\\
\|M\|^2 &= 229.94736158731112.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dominant stability coordinate is $\texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$.  The permutation audit uses $1000$ permutations and reports $p=0.000999000999000999$ for $R^2_{QM}$.  On the same joined rows, the protein-abundance score is $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, and the cosine between the stability vector $S^{QM}$ and the same-row abundance vector $S^{QP}$ is $0.8040935468384264$.  The dominant abundance coordinate on the same join is $\texttt{f3\_stress}$, so the stability readout is aligned with, but not identical to, the protein-abundance readout.

\paragraph{Mediation comparison.}
The associated abundance-residual mediation row uses the same $5085$-row yeast join and the same $24$-column control design.  Before conditioning on mRNA half-life, the abundance association is $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$; after conditioning on residualized half-life it is $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$.  The absorbed portion is therefore $0.027596042074056304$, giving a mediation fraction of $0.09994436477872035$.  The permutation null for that mediation fraction has mean $0.00019674487584783138$, $95$th percentile $0.0014571003414947088$, and right-tail probability $0.000999000999000999$.  This comparison says that the yeast half-life coordinate explains a small but nonzero portion of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance association; it does not turn the stability association into a causal decay pathway.

\paragraph{Cross-organism orientation.}
Two additional mRNA-stability rows show that the yeast direction should be read as organism-specific rather than universal.  In \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, the matched half-life table at growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$ gives $n=2720$, $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, dominant coordinate $\texttt{Arg\_AGR}$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, and cosine $\cos(S^{QM},S^{QP})=-0.7784923375569599$.  In the human consensus relative half-life row, the join has $n=16165$, $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, dominant coordinate $\texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, and positive cosine $0.6351183883924848$.  The yeast value is therefore a concrete positive finite-row stability score, while the sign and dominant coordinate vary across the bacterial, yeast, and human rows.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The chapter supports a bounded statement: in the Neymotin yeast half-life join, $B^{*}_{Q6}$ codon residuals retain a positive, permutation-supported statistical association with residualized mRNA half-life after amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  It does not establish translation realization, ribosome dwell dynamics, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  It also does not show that codon optimality causes mRNA decay.  Those stronger readings would require distinct layer-matched measurements and separately computed controlled statistics.  The present result is a finite controlled association between a specified codon-residual coordinate block and one yeast mRNA-stability readout.
