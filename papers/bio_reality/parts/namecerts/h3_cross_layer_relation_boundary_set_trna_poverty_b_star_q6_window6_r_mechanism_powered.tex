\subsection{A tRNA-poor boundary for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-f3-trna-poor-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate being tested.}
The record concerns a finite coding-sequence coordinate surface restricted to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate after amino-acid projection. The relevant finite closure row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\partial_{f_3}R &= \varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the saturated $f_3$ surface removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from the displayed $M$ row, and the recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty. This chapter does not treat that empty boundary as a biological null by itself. It uses the finite row only to specify the codon-coordinate side of a later biological question: whether the $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance survives when matched to translation-layer measurements.

\paragraph{Observed powered boundary row.}
The powered finite-row assay for the boundary-set claim was run as experiment $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_boundary\_set\_trna\_poverty\_powered}$ with deterministic seed $\mathrm{sha256{:}b\_star\_q6\_boundary\_set\_trna\_poverty\_powered{:}R\mbox{-}sense\mbox{-}tRNA\mbox{-}poverty\mbox{-}permutation}$. It requested $46$ organisms, computed $40$, and skipped $6$. The test used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$ and returned status $\mathrm{passed}$. The finite conclusion recorded for this row is that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy summaries combined with the dos Reis wobble-weight scheme. In the language of this chapter, the row supports a local tRNA-supply explanation for avoidance of the $R$ sense boundary set in the sampled panel.

\paragraph{What the row says.}
The finite datum is not merely that a codon set was named. It states a cross-organism supply asymmetry: after the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate has been isolated on the synonymous residual surface, the $R$ sense side is depleted in the tRNA or wobble-weight support used by the assay. The result is therefore a boundary-set statement, not a universal translation law. Its force comes from the combination of a fixed codon coordinate, a finite organism panel, and an explicit permutation procedure. The recorded panel size, $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested, is large enough to make the boundary row qualitatively different from the rejected three-organism all-$1.0$ rank-saturated surfaces elsewhere in the same research memory.

\paragraph{Relation to nearby finite rows.}
The surrounding $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ rows keep this boundary result in a narrow regime. The axis-strength spectrum row computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns; its descriptive summary says that $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ led the captured spectra on average and that the largest GC3 rank effect was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$. The third-axis identity row, also on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns, identified $d_{\perp}$ with within-family usage frequency at mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The two-axis closure row concluded $\mathrm{third\_axis\_present}$, and the optimality-axis sufficiency row concluded $\mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present}$ on the same $11$-organism, $5$-fold, $41$-column design shape. These neighboring rows do not replace the tRNA-poor boundary assay. They only show that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate is not exhausted by a single codon-supply axis.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
The boundary-set result may be read as evidence for tRNA-supply pressure on the $R$ sense side of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$ coordinate. It is not yet a direct survival result for translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, localization, stability, function, phenotype, or physical structure. A positive translation-row claim would require same-organism or same-CDS rows joining codon counts, the $f_3$-stress coordinate, tRNA or wobble weights with $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$, a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, and controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, rank, and simpler codon baselines. In symbols, the promoted statement would require a non-vacuous positive entry
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j} &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
under those controls. The finite row supplied here does not contain that joined matrix. It therefore remains a codon-supply boundary result with a pending translation-readout gate.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No claim about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, downstream protein state, or universality follows from this chapter alone. The $1000$-permutation, $40$-organism tRNA-poverty row can support only the bounded statement that the $R$ sense side of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate is tRNA-poor under the named copy-number and wobble-weight model. Any stronger claim needs its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. In particular, modeled tAI or stAI weights are not observed ribosome footprints; ribosome profiling is not protein abundance; transcript abundance is only a control for translation-efficiency residualization; and a residual codon coordinate is not by itself evidence for folding, admissibility, biological function, or a global biological law.
