\subsection{A finite $f_3$ boundary where R-side codons are tRNA-poor}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-f3-boundary-trna-poverty}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a finite residual surface on coding sequences: the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate is restricted to the $f_3$-stress direction after synonymous codons have been compared inside their amino-acid classes.  The biological rows available to this record are organism-scoped codon counts, tRNA gene-copy summaries, dos Reis-style wobble-weighted tRNA supply, modeled tAI or stAI adaptation weights, and possible matched translation readouts.  The finite combinatorial boundary is sharp.  The marked set before removing the two non-boundary additions is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{\mathrm{Med}_R\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}}={}&\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
After the $f_3$ saturation step, the retained $R$-side set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)={}&\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the two entries removed from the twenty-row marked list are $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$.  The recorded $f_3$ boundary residue is empty:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{f_3}(R)=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the claim is not that an unpaired codon remains at the purely finite saturation boundary.  The claim is instead that, after amino-acid quotienting and finite saturation, the surviving biological asymmetry is carried by the tRNA or wobble-weight layer attached to the $R$-side coordinate.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row support.}
The powered assay for this boundary computed $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested organisms and skipped $6$ organisms.  It used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$ with deterministic seed \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_boundary\_set\_trna\_poverty\_powered:R-sense-tRNA-poverty-permutation}.  The finite-row conclusion is that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN combined with dos Reis wobble weights.  This supports a restricted tRNA-supply explanation for $R$ avoidance: the finite codon boundary is not merely a syntactic codon list, because the external tRNA-copy and wobble-weight rows distinguish the two sides after the $f_3$ pairing has been imposed.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding $Q6$ spectrum.}
The same finite data family records that, across the powered organism panel for the $B^*_{Q6}$ axes, spectra are led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ and then by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$.  In that spectrum run, $11$ organisms were computed from $16$ requested organisms, the per-organism dominant count was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$, and the largest GC3 rank effect reported for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ was $\rho=0.136$.  These numbers matter because the tRNA-poverty boundary does not stand alone as a free biological story: it sits inside a residual coordinate system where non-tRNA residual optimality was reported to align most with $f_3$ translation selection, while the two-axis tRNA plus $f_3$ span still left a third axis present.  The third-axis identity run computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms and identified $d_\perp$ with usage frequency within family at mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  Its mechanism run recorded the summary ``selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs\_external,'' with the reason that GC-orthogonalized $d_\perp$ retained significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.

\paragraph{What the mechanism statement does and does not say.}
The verified mechanism row licenses only a narrow conclusion: within the computed finite panel, the $R$-side codons are depleted in tRNA supply as measured by gene-copy and wobble-weight summaries, and this is consistent with a tRNA-supply account of $R$ avoidance.  It does not by itself assert ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  A stronger translation statement would require a same-organism or same-CDS readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, and matched mRNA controls.  In notation, the allowed translation-readout claim would have to pass through a non-vacuous controlled entry
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_j$ is an explicitly attached translation row.  Without that row, the finite tRNA-poverty result remains a supply-side boundary statement rather than a measured translation-output statement.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule for downstream layers.}
The boundary is deliberately local.  The all-geometry part says that $\partial_{f_3}(R)$ is empty after the finite saturation calculation; the observed biological part says that tRNA-copy and wobble-weight rows make the $R$ sense codons tRNA-poor in the powered panel.  Neither part promotes the result to protein structure, localization, stability, function, phenotype, or organism-level universality.  Separate contacts would be needed at each such layer.  In particular, tAI or stAI weights are bounded codon-adaptation readouts, not direct ribosome-footprint observations; ribosome profiling would be a translation contact, not protein abundance or folded-structure evidence; matched mRNA abundance is a control for translation-efficiency residualization, not a translation mechanism by itself.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite classifications.}
The broader fourth-axis tests keep the scope narrow.  The three-axis sufficiency run computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ and concluded that a fourth mechanism is present.  Candidate fourth mechanisms were then separated: the codon-pair assay concluded \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}; the translation-accuracy assay concluded \texttt{not\_accuracy} after $200$ permutations; the mRNA-stability assay concluded \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}; and the elongation-dwell assay in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} was marked \texttt{partial}.  These classifications do not weaken the tRNA-poverty result.  They prevent over-reading it.  The $R$-boundary row is a finite, powered supply-asymmetry record, and the remaining downstream biology must be established by its own matched rows and controls.

\paragraph{Falsification boundary.}
The record would fail as a cross-layer translation claim if the controlled translation entries remain non-positive or rank-degenerate across a powered panel.  The three-organism all-$1.0$ matrix is therefore a null rank boundary, not survival evidence.  The result would also fail as a tRNA-poverty mechanism if the $40$-organism permutation pattern disappeared under the declared tRNA-copy and wobble-weight construction.  At the current finite row, the strongest defensible statement is the following: the saturated $f_3$ codon boundary has no leftover combinatorial residue, while the attached organism-level tRNA and wobble rows identify systematic $R$ sense codon poverty in a powered panel of $40$ computed organisms, and no translation, folding, physical-admissibility, function, higher mechanism, or universality claim follows without a separate layer-matched contact.
