\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$ boundary set and tRNA-poverty mechanism}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-f3-boundary-set-trna-poverty}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The carrier for this record is the finite coding-sequence residual surface restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate that survives amino-acid projection as an $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance.  The coordinate is not interpreted as a protein-level observable.  It is a finite row object: codons are first grouped by the amino-acid quotient, then the third-position $f_3$ involution is used to expose which synonymous rows have a residual asymmetry after the ordinary amino-acid composition explanation has been removed.  In the saturation run, the ambient marked set was
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_3$-saturated $R$ surface removed the two $ACA/ACG$ rows and retained
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported $f_3$ boundary was empty, so the boundary set is not a residue of unsaturated codon enumeration.  It is a stress coordinate inside the already finite saturated codon surface.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row contact.}
The powered tRNA-poverty assay used the claim id $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.boundary\_set\_trna\_poverty.b\_star\_q6\_window6\_R\_mechanism\_powered$ and passed under experiment id $b\_star\_q6\_boundary\_set\_trna\_poverty\_powered$.  It requested $46$ organisms, computed $40$, skipped $6$, and used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$.  The deterministic seed was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{seed} ={}& \mathrm{sha256{:}b\_star\_q6\_boundary\_set\_trna\_poverty\_powered{:}}\\
&\mathrm{R\mbox{-}sense\mbox{-}tRNA\mbox{-}poverty\mbox{-}permutation}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite call was that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts together with the dos Reis wobble-weight model.  This licenses a narrow tRNA-supply reading of $R$ avoidance: the rows marked by the $B^*_{Q6}$ boundary are not merely topological labels, because their finite codon classes meet an organism-level anticodon-supply asymmetry.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding spectrum.}
The same finite panel family places this boundary record inside a broader residual-axis landscape.  In the axis-strength spectrum assay, $11$ organisms were computed out of $16$ requested, with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns; the captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and the per-organism dominant count was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  The domain means were explicitly descriptive because the panel was imbalanced with $1$ archaeon, $2$ bacteria, and $8$ eukaryotes; the largest GC3 rank effect was small, $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  In the residual-optimal-axis assay, the non-tRNA residual direction aligned most with $f_3$ translation selection across $11$ computed organisms, again with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The third-axis identity assay identified $d_{\perp}$ provisionally with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The mechanism assay for this third axis reported selection-not-GC and not-wobble-clustered, with GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ retaining significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.  These surrounding rows do not replace the tRNA-poverty permutation result; they show that the boundary-set call lies in a residual surface where tRNA supply, $f_3$ stress, and non-GC residual axes are separately visible.

\paragraph{Bounded readout.}
The mathematical object therefore has three finite parts.  First, the relation is the $f_3$-paired codon imbalance after amino-acid quotienting.  Second, the spectrum is the organism-indexed residual-axis behavior in which $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and $d_{\perp}$ separate under controlled synonymous designs.  Third, the trigger is the tRNA or wobble asymmetry $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$, which is the condition under which the $f_3$ coordinate can become a biological readout candidate rather than a purely internal codon-coordinate distinction.  The finite result here supports only the tRNA-supply poverty component: $40$ organism rows were sufficient for the permutation assay to pass, and the $R$ sense-codon set was called systematically poor in tRNA supply under the declared gene-copy and wobble-weight contact.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
This record does not by itself establish translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  A positive tRNA-poverty mechanism for $R$ avoidance is not the same as a measured $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ survival entry.  Any translation-readout claim still requires same-organism and same-CDS codon counts, tRNA or wobble weights, measured tAI, stAI, ribosome-occupancy, or translation-efficiency rows, matched mRNA controls, and controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, rank, and simpler codon baselines.  Downstream claims about protein output, localization, stability, structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism beyond the supplied tRNA-contact interpretation, or universality require their own layer-matched measurements.  In particular, a rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ matrix on three organisms would be a null surface rather than survival evidence, and codon topology alone cannot assign a general direction without the wobble-breaking condition and a non-vacuous controlled readout row.
