\subsection{Fourth-axis elongation-dwell identity for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate}
\label{sec:bior-eality-q6-elongation-dwell-fourth-mechanism}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate under test.}
The object in this chapter is a finite residual surface on coding sequences, restricted to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate that remains visible after amino-acid projection and after the third-position $f_3$ pairing is imposed.  The finite codon substrate is not treated as a biological output by itself.  It is the row-space on which a later translation readout may be tested.  In the saturation record, the marked set $M=\mathrm{Med}_{R}\cup\mathrm{ACA}$ contains the twenty codons
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.  The $f_3$-saturated set removes the two exceptional ACA/ACG entries and leaves eighteen codons,
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.  The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  Thus the coordinate being tested is not a free codon-usage contrast over all synonymous codons; it is a finite stress coordinate built after the third-position pairing has already removed its boundary residue.

\paragraph{Readout equation.}
For a coding sequence row $x$, let $q_{f_3}(x)$ denote the residual $f_3$-stress coordinate after amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, length, $M$-density, and simpler synonymous baselines are made available as controls.  Let $T_j(x)$ denote a bounded translation-layer row: modeled tAI, modeled stAI, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or a directly estimated elongation-dwell quantity when that row is explicitly attached.  The survival entry tested by this chapter has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3,T_j} &= \Delta\operatorname{MDL}\bigl(T_j \mid Z\bigr)-\Delta\operatorname{MDL}\bigl(T_j \mid Z,q_{f_3}\bigr),\\
Z&=\{\hbox{amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, }M\hbox{-density, mRNA controls when present}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive value is meaningful only as a controlled translation-row survival statement.  It is not a protein-abundance, folding, localization, stability, cellular-function, phenotype, or universal-law statement.  Those layers require their own matched measurement rows and their own controlled statistic.

\paragraph{Powered finite evidence.}
The direct assay attached to this chapter is the powered fourth-mechanism identity run for elongation dwell.  It passed under experiment id \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_elongation\_dwell\_powered}, with seed \texttt{sha256:f3d542c80eab2ed48839c11e4a4ef75c2286abfe31c9327f7afef47ce7335ab3}.  The finite run used five folds and was organism-specific: \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Its conclusion is \texttt{partial}.  This is the important scientific classification.  The record is stronger than a failed route, because the elongation-dwell candidate was not rejected by the powered test; it is weaker than an identity theorem or a cross-organism law, because the finite row currently names one organism and does not by itself supply a universal translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Discrimination against neighboring fourth-axis explanations.}
The partial elongation-dwell call is made in a family of fourth-axis tests, not in isolation.  The same finite evidence table records that the three-axis optimality surface is insufficient: the powered sufficiency run reported \texttt{fourth\_mechanism\_present} across $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested organisms, using five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  Competing fourth-mechanism identities were then checked.  The mRNA-stability run passed but concluded \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}; its stated reason was that the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction had low alignment with the abundance-side $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  The codon-pair run passed and concluded \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}, using five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null replicates.  The translation-accuracy run passed and concluded \texttt{not\_accuracy}, with $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested organisms, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $200$ permutations.  Against these alternatives, the yeast elongation-dwell result is a surviving partial identity call for the fourth mechanism, not a generic residual label.

\paragraph{Relation to the axis spectrum.}
The broader powered spectrum gives useful scale.  The per-organism axis-strength experiment computed $11$ organisms from $16$ requested organisms, used five folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, and used $41$ synonymous design columns.  In that table the captured spectra were led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; the per-organism dominant count was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  Domain means were explicitly descriptive because the domain sample was imbalanced, with $n=\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$.  The largest GC3 rank effect was small, reported as $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  The non-tRNA residual optimality experiment identified \texttt{f3\_translation\_selection} as the dominant residual axis over $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested organisms, again with five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The third-axis identity run also found a usage-frequency-within-family alignment for $d_{\perp}$, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  These scalar values keep the present chapter finite: the fourth mechanism is read as a residual translation-side candidate in a measured axis family, not as a free-standing biological principle.

\paragraph{Wobble asymmetry and the $f_3$ trigger.}
The $f_3$ coordinate becomes biologically testable only when the third-position pairing is broken by a translation-side asymmetry.  In the notation of this chapter, the required break has the form $w_c\ne w_{c^{f_3}}$, where $w_c$ is a context tRNA, wobble, tAI, stAI, or measured translation weight attached to codon $c$.  Without such a row, $q_{f_3}$ remains a coordinate contrast.  With such a row, the test is still bounded: one asks whether $S_{f_3,T_j}>0$ after the declared controls and simpler-baseline ablations.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ matrix in a three-organism surface is therefore a null boundary, not survival.  Likewise, modeled tAI or stAI weights may supply a bounded translation-readout surface, but they do not become observed ribosome occupancy merely by being named.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter's conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite evidence supports a partial elongation-dwell identity for the yeast fourth-mechanism assay, in the context where other tested fourth-axis identities were rejected or marked insufficient.  It does not assert that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ changes protein abundance, protein structure, localization, stability, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or a universal biological law.  It also does not assert a completed same-CDS ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency matrix for every organism in the powered panel.  Any such promotion must start again with matched rows for codon counts, tRNA or wobble weights, direct translation readouts, and mRNA controls, followed by the controlled statistic above.  The present result is therefore a finite, organism-scoped, translation-row identity signal: partial for elongation dwell in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, bounded by the absence of a broader measured survival matrix.
