\subsection{Fourth-Axis Elongation-Dwell Boundary for the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$ Coordinate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-elongation-dwell}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate under test.} The record concerns a finite coding-sequence residual surface after amino-acid projection. Its distinguished coordinate is the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress row: a paired synonymous-codon imbalance that is invisible to amino-acid composition but can remain visible to translation-layer readouts when the paired codons have unequal contextual tRNA or wobble weights. The finite saturation contact fixes the $f_3$ support explicitly. The set $M=\operatorname{Med}_R\cup\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$ contains
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$. Its $f_3$ saturation removes $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, leaving the $18$-codon set
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$. The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty, so the finite surface has no extra boundary codon outside this saturated support.

\paragraph{Readout row and controls.} The bounded biological question is whether this residual coordinate survives as a translation-readout entry after controls for amino-acid composition, organism identity, $\mathrm{GC3}$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA abundance where ribosome-efficiency data are used. A positive entry is not asserted from codon geometry alone. The row may be read against tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation-efficiency data, but only as a controlled survival statistic on the same finite coordinate surface. In this chapter the realized finite fact is narrower: the elongation-dwell assay was run for $\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}$ with $5$ folds and passed its computational gate with conclusion \emph{partial}. Thus the row is not null, but it is also not a completed universal identification of the fourth axis.

\paragraph{Discriminating the fourth-axis alternatives.} The surrounding finite assays make the elongation-dwell interpretation specific rather than decorative. A three-axis sufficiency test over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using $41$ synonymous-design columns and $5$ folds, concluded that a fourth component remains. The per-organism axis-strength spectrum was computed on the same $11/16$ organism panel with $41$ synonymous-design columns and $5$ folds; its captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ in all $11$ organism-dominant counts, while the largest recorded $\mathrm{GC3}$ rank effect was only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$. The third-axis identity test on the $11/16$ panel reported alignment of $d_\perp$ to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$; the companion mechanism screen retained significant cross-species alignment after $\mathrm{GC}$ orthogonalization and classified the result as selection-not-$\mathrm{GC}$ and not wobble-clustered, while leaving the CSC or positional identity as needing a separate contact.

\paragraph{Elongation dwell as the surviving candidate.} Against that background, the fourth-axis alternatives are sharply bounded. The mRNA-stability assay over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $5$ folds concluded \emph{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}; its reason was that the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates. The codon-pair assay, also using $41$ synonymous-design columns, $5$ folds, and a $50$-shuffle null, concluded \emph{not\_codon\_pair}. The translation-accuracy assay over $11/16$ organisms, $41$ synonymous-design columns, $5$ folds, and $200$ permutations concluded \emph{not\_accuracy}. By contrast, the elongation-dwell assay for $\textit{S. cerevisiae}$ concluded \emph{partial}. The most conservative reading is therefore that the fourth row has an elongation-dwell-compatible signal in the yeast assay, while other named alternatives have been rejected or not promoted under their own finite tests.

\paragraph{Wobble asymmetry and the $f_3$ trigger.} The proposed trigger is third-position symmetry breaking. The $f_3$ coordinate is inert when paired codons have equal contextual translation weights, because amino-acid projection has already removed the amino-acid identity and the residual row has no independent downstream meaning. It becomes testable only when a pair $c,c^{f_3}$ satisfies $w_c\ne w_{c^{f_3}}$ in the relevant organism or context. The tRNA-poverty screen supplies one finite supply-side contact: for the $R$ sense codons, $40$ organisms were computed out of $46$ requested, with $6$ skipped, $1000$ permutations, and $\alpha=0.05$; the screen passed and reported systematic tRNA poverty under GtRNAdb copy numbers plus dos Reis wobble weights. This does not prove elongation dwell by itself, but it gives a concrete finite route by which an $f_3$-paired residual imbalance can have unequal translation weights.

\paragraph{Null boundary.} The correct null is not biological irrelevance of $B^*_{Q6}$. The null is failure of the controlled row: after the stated covariates and simpler codon baselines, the entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ is non-positive or rank-degenerate for every allowed translation readout $T_j$ in a powered organism panel. A three-organism all-$1.0$ matrix is therefore not survival evidence; it is rank saturation. Likewise, a modeled tAI or stAI contact is a bounded codon-adaptation readout, not direct ribosome occupancy. A ribosome-profiling row becomes relevant only after same-organism, same-CDS codon counts, RPF rows, and matched mRNA rows are joined and the controlled statistic is computed.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.} This chapter asserts only a finite, controlled translation-readout boundary for the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$ residual coordinate, with a partial elongation-dwell-compatible result in yeast and finite exclusions for mRNA stability, codon-pair identity, and translation accuracy under the stated assays. It does not license claims about realized translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, protein stability, phenotype, a complete biochemical mechanism, or universality. Each such layer requires its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic before it can be interpreted.
