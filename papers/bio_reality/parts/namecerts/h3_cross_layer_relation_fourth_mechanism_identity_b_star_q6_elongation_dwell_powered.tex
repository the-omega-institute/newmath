\subsection{The fourth residual mechanism has a partial elongation-dwell identity}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-elongation-dwell}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The row studied here is the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate after amino-acid projection, restricted to a finite coding-sequence panel and read only through matched translation-layer quantities.  The relevant residual contrast is not a raw codon-count claim.  It is the part of synonymous codon imbalance that remains after the amino-acid quotient, and it becomes biologically interrogable only when an organism or codon context supplies asymmetric tRNA or wobble weights between an $f_3$-paired codon and its partner.  The finite saturation record fixes the $M$-surface as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&\qquad UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},\\
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&\qquad UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},\\
M\setminus \operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{ACA,ACG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty, so the finite obstruction is not an unfilled boundary cell.  The informative restriction is instead the survival requirement: the coordinate must retain a non-degenerate association with a translation readout after amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, and simpler codon baselines are accounted for.

\paragraph{Observed classification.}
The elongation-dwell experiment for the fourth mechanism passed its finite run and returned the classification \emph{partial}.  The run was the deterministic row $e4edcde459b7$ for experiment $b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_elongation\_dwell\_powered$, using $5$ folds and the organism $\mathit{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$.  This is a positive but limited identity statement: the fourth residual axis has detectable elongation-dwell content in the yeast row, but the finite record does not upgrade it to a universal elongation law or to a complete identity of the fourth mechanism.

\paragraph{Position among competing fourth-mechanism calls.}
The partial dwell classification is sharpened by neighboring negative and exclusion rows.  The mRNA-stability assay passed with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, but its conclusion was $\mathit{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}}$ direction had low alignment with the abundance-linked $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ gate.  The codon-pair assay also passed with $5$ folds, $50$ shuffle-null replicates, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, but its conclusion was $\mathit{not\_codon\_pair}$.  The translation-accuracy assay passed across $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with $200$ permutations and $41$ synonymous-design columns, and concluded $\mathit{not\_accuracy}$.  Thus the dwell row is not merely one of many equally open labels.  Within the recorded finite assays, mRNA stability, codon-pair structure, and translation accuracy have been disfavored as dominant fourth-axis explanations, while elongation dwell remains as a partial mechanism-level fit.

\paragraph{Relation to the residual-axis panel.}
The broader $B^*_{Q6}$ powered panel gives the local dwell result a controlled coordinate setting.  The three-axis sufficiency test passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, and concluded $\mathit{fourth\_mechanism\_present}$.  The axis-strength spectrum was also computed on $11$ organisms, with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous-design columns; its descriptive summary placed $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ first on average and reported per-organism dominance counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$, with imbalanced domain counts $\{\mathit{archaea}:1,\mathit{bacteria}:2,\mathit{eukaryote}:8\}$ and a largest GC3 rank effect of $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  The non-tRNA residual optimality assay passed on the same $11/16$ organism panel and identified $\mathit{f3\_translation\_selection}$ as the dominant axis.  The third-axis identity assay, also on $11/16$ organisms with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous-design columns, aligned $d_{\perp}$ to within-family usage frequency with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  Its mechanism companion retained significant cross-species alignment after GC orthogonalization and concluded $\mathit{selection\text{-}not\text{-}GC}$, $\mathit{not\text{-}wobble\text{-}clustered}$, with CSC or positional identity still requiring a further contact.  These rows make the yeast dwell call plausible as a residual translation-layer phenomenon, while preventing it from being read as a complete biological pathway.

\paragraph{Mechanistic boundary of the row.}
The $R$-sense tRNA-poverty test supplies a separate finite pressure on the translation interpretation: it passed on $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and called the $R$ sense codons systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene copy number with dos Reis wobble weights.  This supports a tRNA-supply route for $R$ avoidance, but it does not by itself prove that the $f_3$-stress coordinate changes ribosome motion in every organism.  The allowed readback is narrower.  A row may be interpreted as a bounded translation-readout survival entry only when codon counts, tRNA or wobble weights, and a direct or explicitly bounded translation quantity are joined under the declared controls.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface on a three-organism panel is a null boundary, not survival.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The conclusion of this chapter is therefore finite and conditional.  The current data support the statement that the fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ residual mechanism has a partial elongation-dwell identity in the recorded yeast assay, against several competing fourth-mechanism explanations.  They do not establish protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular localization, phenotype, causation, or a universal biological law.  They also do not establish observed translation dynamics from codon geometry or modeled tAI/stAI weights alone.  Any stronger assertion must be supplied by a separate layer-matched contact: measured ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency for translation, proteomics for protein abundance, structure or stability assays for physical state, functional or perturbation assays for biological role, and a powered cross-organism design before universality is claimed.
