\subsection{Human turnover as a test of the remaining protein-abundance residual}
\label{subsec:h-candidate-turnover-human-b-star-q6-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The human turnover test asks whether a one-dimensional protein half-life readout carries the part of the human protein-abundance signal that remains after the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates have already been compared with the modeled translation readout.  The finite row set is the intersection of human CDS codon counts, PAXdb whole-organism protein abundance, and Mathieson primary-cell protein half-life records.  The join contains $8057$ proteins.  The parent human CDS--abundance surface contains $19053$ joined CDS rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and $19483$ valid PAXdb abundance proteins.  The turnover source reports $8164$ valid half-life proteins, with no missing, duplicate, or nonpositive turnover records after parsing.  The identifier route is the finite map
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Mathieson gene name} &\longrightarrow \text{reviewed UniProt human primary gene} \\
&\longrightarrow \text{STRING }9606.\mathrm{ENSP},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the half-life column used is the median half-life across the measured primary-cell settings.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control basis.}
The source design has nine residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\quad Arg\_AGR,\quad Ile\_AUA,\quad Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\quad Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\quad Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\quad Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\quad f3\_stress.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control matrix has $24$ listed columns and rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  On the full human CDS--abundance join, the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component has squared norm
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the modeled-translation complement is still large: the modeled readout accounts for only a small part of the original $Q$-aligned abundance component, and the registered diagnostic gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same value is recovered in the turnover experiment against the full human join, with absolute difference $0.0$ from the complement reference.  A coverage-sensitive recomputation on the $8057$-row turnover join gives $0.7820306826175398$, so the chapter records both the stable full-join complement value and the coverage diagnostic.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The tested readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life.  The statistical question is not whether half-life causes the abundance residual, but whether the one-column turnover subspace absorbs a nontrivial part of the residual component $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$.  The fitted scalar quantities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{turnover,basis}} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two absorption computations agree to numerical precision, so the projection itself is internally stable.  Its magnitude, however, is nearly zero relative to the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null.}
The comparison null uses one-dimensional trial subspaces, matching the rank of the half-life readout.  Across $200$ deterministic trials the null summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{NullMean} &= 0.0001262572958089998,\\
\mathrm{Null}_{95} &= 0.0004342285066808464,\\
\mathrm{rank\ values} &= \{1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed absorbed fraction $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ does not exceed the $95$ percent null boundary.  Therefore the finite-row conclusion is negative for this candidate: human median protein half-life, in this joined primary-cell turnover source, does not explain the large modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding readouts.}
The negative turnover result is meaningful because the neighboring surfaces are not empty.  Direct human protein-omics survival against PAXdb abundance gives $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ on the full human abundance join, with all nine coordinates surviving the positive numerical threshold.  Measured human ribosome-TE survival gives $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$ on $11554$ TE genes, also with nine surviving coordinates.  The measured-TE abundance decomposition on the human three-way join reports $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$, and measured mediated fraction $0.0027966485036786744$.  A separate joint TE--mRNA-stability decomposition reports, for human, $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$, joint mediation fraction $0.16890712099923358$, TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, and stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These neighboring numbers show that the human residual surface is real enough to test, but the specific Mathieson half-life projection is not the absorber of the large remaining complement.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For this chapter the finite certificate is a completed negative H-candidate test.  The data support the statement that $B^*_{Q6}$ has a human protein-abundance association and a measured translation-readout association under the stated controls, but they do not support the stronger statement that median human protein turnover absorbs the large modeled-translation-complement residual.  The admissible readback is therefore: direct and translation-conditioned abundance structure persists, while this particular half-life readout does not pass the matched one-degree null as an explanatory post-translational candidate.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation mechanism, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, causal pathway, perturbation effect, evolutionary direction, or universal biological law follows from this chapter.  The turnover fit is a descriptive projection over a finite joined matrix.  Protein half-life is measured in Mathieson human primary-cell data and then matched to a whole-organism integrated abundance surface; the conditions and platforms are not a causal intervention.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as perturbation, rescue, condition-matched turnover, structural assay, localization assay, functional assay, or a direct mechanistic time-course, and would have to be evaluated under its own finite controls.
