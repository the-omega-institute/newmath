\subsection{Human Protein Turnover Does Not Absorb the Large Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The human turnover test places the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates on a finite, identifier-matched protein panel and asks whether measured protein half-life accounts for the large protein-abundance component left after a modeled translation readout has been removed. The coordinate side is the same nine-dimensional synonymous residual basis used throughout this packet: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. The control design contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline over the union support of the nine projected coordinates. The recorded control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Joined human rows.}
The turnover readout is Mathieson et al. 2018 human primary-cell protein half-life, using the median half-life across cell types and replicates. The finite join maps Mathieson gene names through reviewed UniProt human primary gene names to STRING $9606.ENSP$ identifiers. The turnover file reports $8164$ valid half-life proteins, with $0$ duplicate, missing, or nonpositive turnover records. The human CDS/protein-abundance background contains $19053$ joined CDS rows at a reported CDS join hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$, while the turnover-abundance-CDS intersection contains $8057$ rows. The same join reports $19483$ valid abundance proteins and skips $10996$ CDS records only because no turnover match is available. The PAXdb abundance source is recorded as H. sapiens whole-organism integrated protein abundance, publication year $2026$.

\paragraph{Residual being tested.}
Before turnover is considered, the human direct protein-abundance surface has a nontrivial $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ footprint. In the larger direct protein-omics computation, the human entry has $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving, after the same length, amino-acid-composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls. In the measured TE/protein-abundance decomposition on the human measured-TE join, the human values are $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S^{meas}_{QT}=0.018837911344127926$, and measured mediated fraction $0.0027966485036786744$. Thus the turnover test is not being applied to an empty abundance association; it is applied after a substantial human abundance component remains only weakly accounted for by the measured TE readout.

\paragraph{Modeled translation complement.}
The human modeled-translation complement calculation records $\|P_Q\|^2=1370.6490492302532$ and $\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=1262.2237678771598$. The modeled complement fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{modeled} &= 0.9208949355679455,
\end{aligned}
$$
so most of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component remains after the modeled tAI-like translation readout is removed. The diagnostic comparing the current turnover computation to the full human abundance-CDS complement gives absolute difference $0.0$ against the reference $d_{modeled}=0.9208949355679455$, even though the current turnover join has $8057$ rows rather than the full $19053$-row abundance join. A separate coverage recomputation gives $0.7820306826175398$, which is reported only as a coverage diagnostic and not as the operative complement value.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The tested turnover coordinate is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life. Projecting the large modeled-translation complement abundance residual onto this one-dimensional turnover readout gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{turn}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{turnover} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{turnover}^{basis\ check} &= 0.0000035740764900125626,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two absorption computations agree to numerical precision, but the magnitude is extremely small. In ordinary prose, the human half-life readout sees a detectable projection geometry, yet it does not remove an appreciable part of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses a one-dimensional rank family, with rank values $[1]$ and $200$ trials. Its mean absorbed fraction is $0.0001262572958089998$, and its $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$. The observed turnover absorption is $0.0000035740764900116538$, far below both the null mean and the null $95$th percentile; the recorded Boolean verdict is $\mathrm{exceeds\_null}=\mathrm{false}$. Therefore human protein half-life is not a positive $H$-candidate for this large residual under this finite test. The ready statement is a negative one: the computation closes the turnover check and leaves the large residual mostly unexplained by this turnover readout.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and mRNA stability.}
Other finite surfaces show that the human residual is not merely a rank artifact. The measured ribosome-TE survival computation reports, for human, $R^2_{QT,measured}=0.018837911344127926$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving on $11554$ measured-TE genes, while the corresponding rank diagnostics have $\mathrm{rank}(X_{\sim})=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, and residual degrees of freedom $11531$. The joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition on human gives $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$. The resulting human joint mediation fraction is $0.16890712099923358$, with measured-TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. This comparison matters because turnover is being judged against a residual that already has meaningful partial reductions by other measured readouts; the turnover half-life readout nevertheless fails its own null gate.

\paragraph{Dictionary context.}
The yeast partial-mediation dictionary gives a useful scale for nonzero but incomplete reduction. In that powered certificate, $n_{join}=1800$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $5$ folds, and $120$ permutations produce coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ over the readout family $[\mathrm{measured\ TE},\mathrm{mRNA\ stability},\mathrm{turnover}]$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. The selection-aware null $95$th percentile is $0.029149007242956115$, while the raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.2745002077400602$. That dictionary surface says that turnover can be listed as a candidate readout family, but the human turnover-only projection here is not a positive instance of that family.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The human turnover record is therefore classified as ready with a negative turnover verdict. It supports the statement that the large human $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual persists after modeled translation conditioning and is not materially absorbed by Mathieson 2018 median protein half-life on the $8057$-row matched human panel. It does not support a turnover mechanism, a causal mediator, a degradation-rate law, a translation mechanism, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype realization, or universality. Any such stronger conclusion would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic. The only local claim closed here is the finite-row one: under the stated controls and null comparison, human protein turnover is too weak to explain the large modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.
