\subsection{Human turnover as a candidate absorber for the large $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-large-q6-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether human protein half-life can account for the large protein-abundance component left after the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface is projected through a modeled translation readout.  The finite rows are human coding sequences with joined codon counts, PAXdb protein abundance, and Mathieson et al. primary-cell protein half-life.  The $Q$ side has nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block has $24$ columns: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The realized control rank is $23$.

\paragraph{Joined human rows.}
The full human abundance--CDS join contains $19053$ coding-sequence rows, with a reported CDS hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$ against the abundance table.  The turnover contact reports $8164$ valid human turnover proteins, and the three-way turnover--abundance--CDS join contains $8057$ rows.  The abundance source is the H. sapiens whole-organism integrated PAXdb table, reported with $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  The turnover readout is the median half-life across Mathieson primary-cell measurements, mapped by gene name through reviewed UniProt primary gene names to STRING identifiers.  The turnover payload has SHA256 $5e19833b82a7e31c05565f2a4c1797d52fcb477fccd0d13a856b58267a5ca54e$, and the source workbook has SHA256 $e0503f49230680cbf86fbc86db145ba439c03382e584fde8f0d83241c9826515$.

\paragraph{Residual geometry before the turnover test.}
On the full human abundance--CDS surface, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance projection has squared norm
$P_Q^2=1370.6490492302532$.  After removing the modeled translation component, the complement remains large:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{mod} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same value $d_{mod}=0.9208949355679455$ is recovered on the current human turnover join against the full human complement reference, with absolute difference $0.0$.  A coverage diagnostic recomputes $d_{mod}$ as $0.7820306826175398$ on the smaller overlap, so the chapter treats the exact-match check as a reference-projection consistency statement, not as proof that the turnover-overlap rows preserve every abundance-surface feature.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption statistic.}
The tested candidate readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life.  Projecting the large modeled-translation complement $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto this one-dimensional turnover readout gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{turn}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
a_{turn} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,\\
a_{turn}^{basis} &= 0.0000035740764900125626,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The basis check agrees to numerical precision.  The absorbed fraction is therefore positive as a descriptive projection, but extremely small on the scale of the remaining human complement.  Against a $200$-trial degree-of-freedom matched null with rank values restricted to $1$, the null mean is $0.0001262572958089998$ and the null $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  Since
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.0000035740764900116538 &< 0.0004342285066808464,
\end{aligned}
$$
the turnover projection does not clear the finite-row null gate.

\paragraph{Relation to the wider $Q6$ protein surface.}
The negative turnover result sits inside a protein-abundance surface that is not empty.  Direct $B^{*}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance survival is positive across the reported organism panel, including $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ in human and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving under the stated positive partial-$R^2$ threshold.  Measured translation mediation is also organism-specific rather than uniform: the measured $Q\to T\to P$ mediated fraction is $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, and clipped to $0.0$ in danio from a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$.  In a separate yeast readout-dictionary certificate, the best selected family using at most three measured readouts covers $0.24538211439730545$ of the residual, with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n=1800$, five folds, and a selection-aware null $95$th percentile of $0.029149007242956115$.  These companion rows show that the protein-abundance residual is a real empirical target, but they do not rescue the human turnover candidate: this chapter's half-life projection remains below its own null threshold.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is that human protein turnover, as measured by Mathieson median primary-cell half-life and joined to $8057$ human coding-sequence rows, is not a sufficient absorber for the large human $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation complement.  The correct readback is a bounded negative H-candidate result: turnover can be recorded as a measured post-translational covariate with a tiny positive projection, but not as an explanatory layer for the human residual under this test.  The remaining signal should be treated as open to other condition-specific, compartmental, stability, localization, complex-membership, transcript-state, or assay-specific surfaces, subject to their own finite joins and null gates.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
Nothing in this turnover projection establishes translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbative mechanism, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality.  Protein abundance is a steady-state omics readout, half-life is a descriptive stability-related readout, and the tested projection is observational.  A stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched contact, same-row controls, and a statistic showing that the proposed layer absorbs or attenuates the relevant $Q$ association beyond its null surface.
