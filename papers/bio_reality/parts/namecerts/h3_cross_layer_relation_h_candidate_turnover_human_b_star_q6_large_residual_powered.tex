\subsection{Human Turnover as a Test of the Large B$^*_Q6$ Abundance Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-turnover-human-b-star-q6-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The human turnover experiment asks whether a measured protein half-life readout carries any appreciable part of the large human abundance component left after the modeled translation readout has been separated from the B$^*_Q6$ protein-abundance signal.  The row set is the common human join among real CDS codon counts, PAXdb whole-organism protein abundance, and the Mathieson et al. 2018 primary-cell turnover table.  The CDS-to-abundance contact reports $19053$ joined human CDS rows out of the abundance surface, with a CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  The turnover table contributes $8164$ valid half-life proteins, and the all-readout intersection used for the projection contains $8057$ rows.  The half-life column selected by the loader is the median half-life across cell types, mapped by gene name through reviewed UniProt human primary gene names to STRING identifiers.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control basis.}
The B$^*_Q6$ side has nine residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control family has $24$ columns and rank $23$: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected B$^*_Q6$ vectors.  In the full human abundance join, the squared norm of the abundance component explained by the nine-coordinate B$^*_Q6$ design after these controls is
$$\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{mod} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}$$
Here $T_{mod}$ is the modeled translation readout used in the complement construction.  The recomputed current value matches the full-reference value exactly on the diagnostic field: the absolute difference is $0.0$, although the current turnover join has $8057$ rows rather than the $19053$ rows of the full abundance-CDS reference surface.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The tested H-candidate is residualized $\log_{10}$ protein half-life.  It is not used as a translation readout; it is a downstream protein-state candidate for explaining what remains after the modeled translation component has been removed.  On the $8057$-row human join, the turnover projection gives
$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{turn}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{Absorb}_{turn} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,\\
\mathrm{Absorb}_{turn}^{basis\ check} &= 0.0000035740764900125626,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}$$
The basis check agrees to numerical precision, so the reported absorption is not a column-order artifact.  Its magnitude is nevertheless tiny: only about $3.57\times 10^{-6}$ of the modeled-translation-complement abundance component is absorbed by this one-dimensional half-life readout.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses rank-one comparison columns, with rank values recorded as $[1]$ over $200$ trials.  Its mean absorbed fraction is $0.0001262572958089998$, and its $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  The observed turnover absorption is therefore below both the null mean and the null $95$th percentile:
$$\begin{aligned}
0.0000035740764900116538
&< 0.0001262572958089998\\
&< 0.0004342285066808464.
\end{aligned}$$
The finite-row verdict is consequently negative for this specific H-candidate: human primary-cell protein half-life was successfully joined and tested, but it does not explain the large human B$^*_Q6$ abundance residual beyond the rank-matched null level.

\paragraph{Relation to the direct protein-abundance surface.}
The negative turnover result is meaningful because the direct human protein-abundance surface is not empty.  In the powered direct protein-omics experiment, the human PAXdb join used $19053$ CDS rows with join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$, the same nine B$^*_Q6$ coordinates, rank $9$ after the rank-$23$ control basis, and residual degrees of freedom $19030$.  The direct human association had
$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP,human} &= 0.04454565118019448,\\
\mathrm{surviving\ coordinate\ count} &= 9.
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the half-life experiment is not a test of whether any human B$^*_Q6$ abundance association exists.  It is a sharper layer question: whether the portion of that abundance association left after modeled translation can be attributed to median human protein turnover.  The answer from these rows is no.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter records a finite negative readback for one downstream candidate, not a causal statement.  The turnover table is from human primary cells and is summarized by median half-life across cell types; PAXdb abundance is an integrated protein-abundance contact; the modeled translation component is not ribosome profiling.  The result therefore does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  It also does not show that turnover is irrelevant in all conditions.  It says only that, on this $8057$-row joined human surface with the stated controls, residualized median half-life absorbs $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the modeled-translation-complement abundance component, below a rank-one null $95$th percentile of $0.0004342285066808464$.  A positive claim at any higher biological layer would require a separate matched contact and a separate controlled statistic at that layer.
