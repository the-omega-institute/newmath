\subsection{Human Turnover Test for the Large $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-b-star-q6-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record tests whether human protein half-life accounts for the large residual protein-abundance component left after the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ codon-residual surface has already been compared with a modeled translation readout.  The finite rows are human CDS-protein rows for which codon counts, PAXdb protein abundance, and Mathieson et al. primary-cell protein half-life can all be joined.  The joined turnover table contains $8057$ rows, from $8164$ valid turnover proteins and $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  The CDS side reports $19053$ joined human CDS rows before the turnover restriction, with a CDS join hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$.  The turnover source is the Mathieson et al. 2018 human primary-cell half-life table, using the median half-life across cell types and replicates, mapped by gene name through reviewed UniProt primary gene names to STRING identifiers.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates and controls.}
The residual coordinate block has nine columns,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The test therefore asks a narrowly finite question: after these controls and after the modeled translation complement have been accounted for, does residualized human half-life absorb a detectable part of the remaining abundance residual?

\paragraph{Modeled translation complement.}
On the full human abundance-CDS join, the modeled translation comparison gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\rVert^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ value is recovered for the turnover-restricted test as $0.9208949355679455$, with absolute difference $0.0$ against the full-abundance complement reference.  A separate coverage diagnostic on the turnover subset gives $0.7820306826175398$, so the chapter keeps two facts distinct: the modeled-complement scalar is exactly matched to the human complement reference, while the turnover join covers only the rows for which half-life is available.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The turnover readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life.  Projecting the large modeled-translation-complement residual onto this one-dimensional turnover readout gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{basis\ check}} &= 0.0000035740764900125626,\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The basis check agrees with the main absorbed fraction to numerical precision, so the computed value is stable under the reported projection audit.  The size of the effect, however, is extremely small: the absorbed fraction is roughly $3.57\times 10^{-6}$ of the tested residual component.  This is a descriptive association with human half-life, not a biological mediation result.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom-matched null uses $200$ trials with rank-one surrogate readouts, and its rank list is the singleton $\{1\}$.  The null mean is $0.0001262572958089998$, and the null $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  The observed turnover absorption does not exceed that null envelope:
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.0000035740764900116538
&< 0.0001262572958089998 \\
&< 0.0004342285066808464.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the human half-life readout is not supported as an absorber of the large $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual in this finite row set.  The correct readback is a negative H-candidate result for turnover absorption, not a hidden positive mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation mediation.}
The separate measured-translation mediation table shows why this turnover result cannot be promoted to a translation claim.  In human, measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency gives $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$, $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, and measured mediation fraction $0.0027966485036786744$ on $11554$ three-way joined rows.  Yeast is the stronger measured-mediation case in that table, with $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, and measured mediation fraction $0.4793069656345167$ on $5128$ joined rows.  The human turnover computation is not that experiment: it uses half-life as a candidate downstream absorber of the modeled complement, and it does not supply a positive $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ chain.

\paragraph{Relation to localization absorption.}
The localization H-candidate table gives a useful scale comparison.  On the human localization join, $16008$ rows are matched against $16406$ localization records and $12$ localization columns.  The reported human localization absorption is $0.009254913329269913$, while its degree-of-freedom-matched null has mean $0.0008032852113983743$ and $95$th percentile $0.0013758521256775384$.  That comparison is layer-specific and descriptive; it is not imported here as a turnover result.  It only emphasizes that the turnover row is quantitatively different: the turnover absorbed fraction is far below its own rank-one null threshold.

\paragraph{Local readback.}
The four-case cross-layer reading for this chapter is therefore bounded.  The coordinate block has a real human protein-abundance residual after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, $M$-density, and modeled translation controls.  It also has a valid human protein-turnover contact with $8057$ joined rows.  But the turnover projection does not clear the matched null, so the chapter records direct residual abundance structure left unresolved by this half-life readout rather than turnover-mediated explanation.  In the four-case vocabulary, this is not a case in which measured translation survives and then absorbs protein abundance through turnover.  It is a finite negative test of one downstream H-candidate.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation realization, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, causal mechanism, evolutionary direction, phenotype claim, or universal biological rule follows from this turnover computation.  The Mathieson half-life table is a human primary-cell turnover readout, the abundance table is a steady-state protein-abundance readout, and the modeled translation complement is not a measured ribosome-occupancy mediator.  A stronger claim would require a separate matched contact at the appropriate layer and a positive statistic at that layer, especially a measured $q_i\to T_j$ step followed by attenuation of $q_i\to P_k$ after conditioning on $T_j$.  In the present finite rows, the admissible conclusion is narrower: human protein half-life was tested as an absorber of the large $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual, and it did not exceed the degree-of-freedom-matched null.
