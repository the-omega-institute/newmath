\subsection{Human turnover does not absorb the large $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance complement}\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-turnover-human-b-star-q6-large-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The tested surface is the human same-protein join between real CDS codon counts, PAXdb whole-organism protein abundance, and Mathieson et al. primary-cell protein half-life.  The residual coordinate system has nine $B^*_{Q6}$ columns,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control matrix has $24$ columns before rank reduction: an intercept, log CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from the same CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  On the full human abundance-CDS surface the control rank is $23$, the abundance-CDS join contains $19053$ proteins, and the reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9779294769799313$.

\paragraph{Why turnover is a legitimate candidate readout.}
The candidate readout is not a translation readout.  It is the log half-life of human proteins, loaded from the Mathieson et al. 2018 primary-cell half-life table and mapped by gene name through reviewed UniProt primary gene labels to STRING protein identifiers.  The turnover table reports $8164$ valid half-life proteins, and the three-way turnover-abundance-CDS join has $8057$ proteins.  The abundance side reports $19483$ valid proteins; after joining, $10996$ CDS records have no turnover match, while the remaining rows have no skipped abundance records, no skipped turnover records, and no invalid CDS lengths.  Thus the carrier is finite and row-level, but it is also visibly a restricted human subset rather than a whole-proteome equality.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the component of residualized log protein abundance explained by the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates after the stated control projection.  Let $T_{mod}$ denote the modeled tAI readout used only to define the abundance complement, not a measured ribosome-occupancy surface.  The human finite-row computation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{mod} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same $d_{mod}$ value is recovered on the current human turnover computation with absolute difference $0.0$ against the full abundance-CDS reference, although a separate coverage diagnostic on the turnover-restricted rows is $0.7820306826175398$.  This is the correct interpretation: the large modeled-translation complement is a fixed target from the human abundance surface, while turnover is tested as a possible explanatory readout on the $8057$-row overlap.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption test.}
The half-life readout was residualized against the same control family and used as a one-dimensional candidate absorber for $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.  The fitted human turnover statistic is small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(Q,T_{turn}) &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
A_{turn} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,\\
A_{turn}^{basis} &= 0.0000035740764900125626,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $A_{turn}$ is the absorbed fraction of the modeled-translation complement attributable to the residualized log half-life direction.  A degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$; the observed absorption does not exceed that null boundary.  The rank list for this null is $\{1\}$, so the comparison is dimension-matched to the single turnover readout.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein-abundance signal.}
The negative turnover result is not a negative result for the underlying $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance association.  A separate direct protein-omics computation on the human PAXdb abundance surface gives $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ with all nine coordinates surviving the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold after the same style of length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  The human measured-ribosome readout surface also has a bounded translation-efficiency association, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$ and nine surviving coordinates on $11554$ genes with measured TE.  The measured TE to abundance mediation computation on human rows reports $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$, and a measured mediated fraction $0.0027966485036786744$.  These numbers place the turnover test in context: human protein abundance contains a detectable $B^*_{Q6}$ residual component, but the present half-life readout is not the finite-row explanation of that component.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback is therefore case-specific.  Human turnover is a tested $H$-candidate because it is a same-protein post-translational readout and because the human abundance complement after modeled tAI is large.  However, the candidate fails the absorption gate: the observed absorbed fraction $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ is below the dimension-matched $95$th percentile null value $0.0004342285066808464$.  The correct finite-row statement is that Mathieson-style median human primary-cell half-life does not account for the large $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-translation complement on this joined panel.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This computation is a descriptive projection on observational rows.  It does not establish a causal turnover mediator, a translation mechanism, a folding route, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal biological rule.  Modeled tAI is used only to define the residual complement; it is not a measured ribosome trace.  PAXdb abundance is a steady-state proteomics readout; it is not by itself a translation-rate, localization, post-translational-modification, folding, or function assay.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as a perturbation or rescue design for turnover, a measured ribosome profiling panel for translation, or a direct assay at the relevant protein-state layer.
