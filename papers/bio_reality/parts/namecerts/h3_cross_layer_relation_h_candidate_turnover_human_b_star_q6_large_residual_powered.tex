\subsection{Human turnover as a candidate readout for the large residual component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-human-turnover-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite row set.}
The human turnover assay tests whether a measured protein half-life column accounts for the part of the human protein-abundance signal left after the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component is conditioned on a modeled translation readout. The coordinate surface has nine residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control design contains the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates. The reported control-column count is $24$, with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$. On the full human abundance-CDS join there are $19053$ joined CDS rows, and the codon-to-abundance join hit rate inherited from the direct protein-omics assay is $0.9779294769799313$. The direct human protein-abundance survival table gives $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ for Homo sapiens after these controls, with all nine coordinates surviving the positive-entry threshold in that direct, non-mechanistic readout.

\paragraph{Turnover contact and identifier join.}
The turnover contact is Mathieson et al. 2018 human primary-cell protein half-life data, using the median half-life across cell types and replicates. The identifier route maps Mathieson gene names through reviewed human UniProt primary gene names to STRING identifiers of the form $9606.\mathrm{ENSP}$. The turnover source contains $8164$ reported valid turnover proteins; after joining turnover, abundance, and CDS rows, the active assay has
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 8057,\\
 n_{\mathrm{abundance}} &= 19483,\\
 n_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 8164,\\
 n_{\mathrm{cds}} &= 19053.
\end{aligned}
$$
The skipped-CDS count records $10996$ CDS rows with no turnover match and no losses from missing abundance, invalid length, empty sense-codon counts, or missing protein identifiers. The turnover payload has byte size $276520$ and SHA256 digest $5e19833b82a7e31c05565f2a4c1797d52fcb477fccd0d13a856b58267a5ca54e$.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
The abundance component explained by the nine-coordinate $Q$ design in the full human abundance join has squared norm
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532.
\end{aligned}
$$
After projecting away the modeled translation readout, the remaining component has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the modeled translation readout removes a visible part of the direct abundance component but leaves a large residual fraction. The diagnostic fraction reported for the modeled complement on the full human join is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the current turnover assay reproduces the full-join complement value with absolute difference $0.0$. A coverage recomputation on the turnover-active rows gives $0.7820306826175398$, which is recorded as a join-specific diagnostic rather than as the defining residual fraction.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
On the $8057$ active human rows, the assay projects $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life. The turnover-only readout has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed\_by\_turnover} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{basis\ check} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
 d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The basis check agrees with the reported absorption to numerical precision. However, the degree-of-freedom matched null has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$ over $200$ trials, with rank list $\{1\}$. The recorded comparison is $\mathrm{exceeds\_null}=\mathrm{false}$. Consequently the turnover column is a computed candidate contact but not a positive explanation of the large human residual in this finite row set.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback is therefore negative at the H-candidate layer. The human $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association is real as a direct protein-omics correlation after the listed sequence and composition controls, and related measured-readout assays show that the same coordinate family can be conditioned against translation and transcript-stability columns. In the measured TE protein-abundance decomposition, Homo sapiens has $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S_{QT}^{\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$, and measured mediated fraction $0.0027966485036786744$. In the joint TE and mRNA-stability partition, Homo sapiens has $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$, and joint mediation fraction $0.16890712099923358$ with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. Those surrounding finite-row results explain why the turnover test is meaningful: there is a large residual object to interrogate, and some measured readouts reduce it. The half-life column tested here, however, absorbs only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the modeled-complement residual and does not clear its matched null.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This section makes no causal, mechanistic, or universal claim. The half-life data are observational human primary-cell measurements summarized across cell types; the abundance and codon rows are cross-sectional; the modeled translation coordinate is not a ribosome-occupancy experiment. A direct $q_i\to P_k$ association is not a translation mechanism, and a small projection onto turnover is not evidence for degradation control unless it exceeds the matched null under the same row set. No conclusion about translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, perturbation response, or a general biological law follows from this assay. Those layers require their own matched contacts and their own positive finite-row statistics.
