\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter records a finite statistical projection, not a causal or mechanistic result. The human half-life readout comes from primary-cell turnover measurements summarized across cell types, whereas the protein abundance and coding-sequence rows are joined cross-sectionally through identifier maps. A positive direct $q_i\to P_k$ association is not a translation mechanism, and the turnover projection is not a causal mediator. No claim about observed translation, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or biological universality follows from this row set. Such claims would require an additional layer-matched contact and a separate controlled statistic at that layer. The admissible conclusion is only that, on $8057$ matched human rows, residualized log half-life does not absorb the large modeled-translation complement beyond the degree-of-freedom-matched null boundary.
