\subsection{Human turnover as a candidate readout for the large residual protein-abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-large-q6-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether human protein turnover carries a measurable part of the large $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation readout has been removed.  The finite carrier is the joined human matrix whose rows are coding sequences with codon counts, measured protein abundance, and Mathieson et al. human primary-cell protein half-life data.  The active $Q$ surface has nine residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_{AAA},\ Arg\_{AGR},\ Ile\_{AUA},\ Leu\_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu\_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser\_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser\_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr\_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f3\_{stress}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
residualized against a $Z$ design of rank $23$ with $24$ columns: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions from the same CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The full human protein-abundance join contains $19053$ CDS rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$.  The turnover-limited three-way join contains $8057$ rows: $19053$ CDS-abundance rows are available, $8164$ turnover proteins are valid, and $10996$ CDS records are skipped only because no turnover match is present.  The turnover source is the Mathieson et al. 2018 human primary-cell half-life table, using the median half-life across cell types and the gene-name to reviewed-UniProt to STRING mapping.

\paragraph{Residual target and projection.}
The target is not total protein abundance.  It is the abundance component already attributed to $B^*_{Q6}$ after the control design, with the modeled translation readout removed.  On the full human abundance-CDS join the $Q$-explained abundance component has squared norm
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the complement after the modeled translation readout is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598.
\end{aligned}
$$
The modeled translation readout therefore explains a large share of the original $Q$-associated abundance component, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{modeled} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same value is recovered on the turnover experiment reference surface: the recorded difference between the full human complement value and the current experiment value is $0.0$, and the comparison passes the $0.02$ matching tolerance.  A separate coverage diagnostic on the reduced join gives $0.7820306826175398$, so the row restriction is visible, but it does not change the registered complement value used for the turnover projection.

\paragraph{Turnover readout.}
The candidate $H$ readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life from the Mathieson primary-cell turnover table.  Projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto the residualized half-life direction gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{turn}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ turnover} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{basis\ check} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The numerical basis check agrees to the displayed precision, so the projection itself is internally stable.  The substantive result is instead the scale: the half-life direction absorbs only a minute fraction of the large residual component.  A one-dimensional degrees-of-freedom matched null was computed with $200$ trials.  Its mean is $0.0001262572958089998$ and its $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  The observed absorbed fraction does not exceed this null boundary, and the rank list for the candidate readout is $\{1\}$.  Thus the finite human turnover readout is tested, but it is not a positive carrier of the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding readout geometry.}
The negative turnover result is meaningful because the upstream abundance and translation surfaces are powered rather than rank-degenerate.  In the direct human protein-omics survival computation, the human abundance surface has $19053$ joined proteins, $9$ residual coordinates, residual rank $9$, $Z$ rank $23$, and residual degrees of freedom $19030$; the direct human $Q\to P$ value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.04454565118019448.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the measured-ribosome translation mediation surface, the human three-way join has $11554$ rows, measured translation survival is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{meas}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.018837911344127926,
\end{aligned}
$$
and measured translation accounts for only
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0027966485036786744
\end{aligned}
$$
of the direct protein-abundance association in that readout.  A separate joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition on the human surface reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.04274178488511215,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.0355223930537993,\\
\mathrm{joint\ mediation\ fraction} &= 0.16890712099923358,\\
\mathrm{TE\ only\ fraction} &= 0.00549490864190343,\\
\mathrm{stability\ only\ fraction} &= 0.15314679870183337.
\end{aligned}
$$
These comparison numbers do not turn the half-life projection into a mechanism.  They locate the turnover test inside a larger finite-row pattern: human $B^*_{Q6}$ has a real direct protein-abundance association; measured translation explains very little of it in the available human readout; mRNA stability carries a larger statistical share than measured TE in the joint decomposition; and the primary-cell protein half-life readout tested here does not exceed its own one-dimensional null for the remaining modeled-translation complement.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a computed negative for this specific $H$ candidate.  Human turnover was attached to the large human modeled-translation-complement residual, the join was large enough for the declared projection, and the numerical readout was stable; nevertheless, the absorbed fraction $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ is far below the null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  The correct interpretation is therefore that this particular human half-life readout does not explain the large residual component on the available rows.  It does not follow that the residual is absent, nor that turnover is biologically irrelevant in other conditions; it only says that this finite human primary-cell median half-life contact does not carry the registered residual signal above the declared null.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation mechanism, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, causal mechanism, phenotype claim, or universal biological rule follows from this computation.  The rows are observational and cross-sectional, the half-life values are primary-cell medians, the abundance readout is steady-state proteomics, and the modeled translation complement is not a perturbation.  Any stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as perturbation or rescue data for causality, structural or physical assays for folding and admissibility, functional assays for molecular role, or phenotype measurements for organism-level consequence.
