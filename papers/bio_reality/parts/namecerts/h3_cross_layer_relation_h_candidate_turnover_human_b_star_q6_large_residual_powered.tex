\subsection{Human Turnover as an H-Candidate for the Large $B^*_{Q6}$ Protein-Abundance Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-h-candidate-b-star-q6}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether human protein turnover accounts for the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal that remains after a modeled translation readout has been removed. The row universe is the human CDS--protein join for which codon counts, PAXdb abundance, and Mathieson primary-cell protein half-life can all be read on the same protein identifier. The full human abundance/CDS panel contains $19053$ joined CDS rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$. The turnover contact contains $8164$ valid turnover proteins, and the common abundance--turnover--CDS panel contains $8057$ rows. The protein-abundance source is the PAXdb integrated whole-organism human readout with $19483$ valid abundance proteins; the turnover source is the Mathieson et al. human primary-cell half-life table, using the median half-life across cell types and mapping gene names through reviewed UniProt primary genes to STRING protein identifiers.

The controlled design uses nine synonymous residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$. The covariate surface has $24$ columns and rank $23$: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined from the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Primary turnover projection.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the $Z$-residualized human protein-abundance component explained by the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ design, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the part left after the modeled tAI translation readout is removed. The finite calculation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lVert P_Q\rVert^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\rVert^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the human abundance residual is large: the modeled translation readout leaves about $92.09\%$ of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component in the complement. The diagnostic recomputation on the current $8057$-row turnover join returns the same full-panel value $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455$ with absolute difference $0.0$ from the reference full-abundance/CDS join, while a coverage-only diagnostic on the turnover-available rows gives $0.7820306826175398$. The equality of the full-panel diagnostic keeps the target fixed: the turnover test is reading the same large human complement rather than changing the modeled-translation decomposition.

The turnover readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life. Projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto this one-dimensional half-life surface gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{basis\ check} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality of the absorption and basis-check values to numerical precision confirms that the reported turnover projection is internally stable. Its biological content is nevertheless small: the absorbed fraction is far below the degree-of-freedom matched null boundary. Across $200$ null trials with rank one, the null mean is $0.0001262572958089998$ and the null $95\%$ value is $0.0004342285066808464$, while the observed absorbed fraction is $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$. The finite verdict for this H-candidate is therefore negative: human half-life, as measured here, does not explain the large modeled-translation complement.

\paragraph{Relation to the cross-layer panel.}
The negative turnover result does not erase the protein-abundance signal. Direct protein-omics survival on the larger cross-organism panel is positive for all nine coordinates in all fifteen organisms. In human, the direct controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ to PAXdb abundance statistic is $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ on $19053$ joined proteins, with $9$ surviving coordinates after the same $24$-column control family and rank $23$ control design. The strongest direct abundance entry in the listed panel is yeast with $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, while human is a smaller but nonzero member of the same direct protein-omics pattern.

Measured ribosome-profiling TE gives a separate translation readout rather than a turnover readout. In human, measured TE survival is $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$ on $11554$ genes with TE, again with $9$ surviving coordinates. When protein abundance is decomposed by measured TE, the human measured-mediated fraction is only $0.0027966485036786744$, with $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$ and $S_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$. The broader TE/stability decomposition gives the human single and joint quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.04274178488511215,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04250692268197657,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.036196017359154814,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.0355223930537993.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting human joint mediation fraction is $0.16890712099923358$, with TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. This places the standalone turnover test in context: the Mathieson half-life projection is not the mediator of the large modeled-tAI complement, while a separate mRNA-stability/TE variance partition can still reduce a smaller controlled abundance association.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
For this record the finite readback is: direct $q_i\to P_k$ association is present in human protein abundance; measured $q_i\to T_j$ survival exists for human ribosome TE but is weak as a mediator of abundance; the modeled tAI complement in human remains large; and the tested turnover readout does not absorb that complement beyond its matched null. The appropriate class is therefore an H-candidate rejection for this specific turnover surface, not a rejection of the whole residual problem. A related yeast dictionary calculation reports coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ for the measured-readout family $\{\mathrm{measured\ TE},\mathrm{mRNA\ stability},\mathrm{turnover}\}$ with selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, five folds, and $120$ permutations. That dictionary result shows partial reducibility in a yeast context, but the human half-life calculation above remains locally negative for the large modeled-translation complement.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The contact tested here is a statistical projection against median human protein half-life. It is not a perturbation experiment, not a temporal causal mediation fit, and not a protein-state mechanism. The source combines human primary-cell half-life estimates, integrated PAXdb abundance, and CDS codon counts through identifier mapping, so condition, cell type, proteomics platform, and mapping coverage remain part of the finite surface. No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility statement, molecular function, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this turnover projection. Such claims would require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics. The local conclusion is narrower: Mathieson human half-life, under the stated controls and row join, does not carry enough of $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ to explain the large human $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual.
