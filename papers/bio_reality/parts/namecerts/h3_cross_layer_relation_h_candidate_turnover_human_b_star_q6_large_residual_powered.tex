\subsection{Human Turnover As A Candidate Readout For The Large Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The human turnover test asks whether the abundance component left after a modeled translation readout has been removed is carried by measured protein half-life.  The row set is the joined human coding-sequence, abundance, and turnover panel.  The coding-sequence side uses the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$.
The control design contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  This gives $24$ control columns and residualized control rank $23$.  The full human abundance-CDS join contains $19053$ coding rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ against the abundance surface.  The turnover readout is Mathieson et al. human primary-cell protein half-life, represented by the median half-life across cell types and replicates.  The turnover table reports $8164$ valid turnover proteins, and the all-readout join contains $8057$ rows.  On the skipped-CDS accounting, $10996$ coding records have no turnover match, while empty codon counts, invalid length, missing protein id, and missing abundance match are all zero in this joined audit.

\paragraph{Projection target.}
The target vector is the large modeled-translation complement of human protein abundance.  Let $Z$ be the control matrix, $Q$ the nine-coordinate residual design, $P$ the log protein-abundance vector, and $T_{mod}$ the modeled tAI-style translation readout used only as a bounded readout surface.  The reported residual abundance energy explained by $Q$ before removing the modeled readout is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the complement after removing the modeled translation component has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598.
\end{aligned}
$$
The modeled decomposition fraction on the full human abundance-CDS join is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{mod} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same value is recovered on the current full-human join used by the turnover test, with absolute difference $0.0$ from the reference complement calculation and $8057$ active rows.  This equality matters because the turnover readout is not being asked to explain the original abundance signal directly; it is being asked to explain the residual component that remains after the modeled translation readout has been separated.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption statistic.}
The finite test projects $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life.  The turnover fit gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{turn}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{turnover} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{turnover}^{basis} &= 0.0000035740764900125626,\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The basis check agrees to numerical precision, so the calculation is internally stable as a finite projection.  The scientific content, however, is weak: the absorbed fraction is about $3.57\times 10^{-6}$ of the target component.  A rank-one degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  The observed turnover absorption does not exceed that null boundary.  Thus the chapter records a computed negative result for turnover as an explanation of the large human complement, not a positive turnover-mediated account.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding readout surface.}
The same human abundance panel has direct protein-omics survival in the broader $B^*_{Q6}$ audit: for Homo sapiens, the direct $Q\to P$ controlled fit reports $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ with all nine coordinates surviving under the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold, using $19053$ joined coding rows and residual degrees of freedom $19030$ after the same family of controls.  A separate measured ribosome-TE surface gives human measured-TE survival $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$ on $11554$ TE genes, with ribosome-TE join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$ relative to the human CDS join.  In the measured translation-conditioned abundance decomposition, the human measured mediated fraction is only $0.0027966485036786744$, with $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$ and $S_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$ on the three-way TE, abundance, and CDS join.  These surrounding finite rows explain why the turnover calculation is framed as an $H$-candidate test: there is a real direct abundance association and a bounded translation readout, but the remaining human complement is not substantially absorbed by median protein half-life.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The chapter-specific readback is therefore case-limited.  The observed facts support a large human residual after modeled translation separation, a well-formed turnover join, and a computed turnover projection.  They do not support turnover as the missing explanatory layer for the human residual, because the absorption is far below the degree-of-freedom matched null threshold.  In finite-row language, turnover is an evaluated candidate readout with status computed but not promoted: it is present as a measured human protein half-life column, it has enough matched rows for a rank-one projection, and its fitted absorption is numerically positive, yet it fails the null comparison.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No stronger biological conclusion follows from this chapter.  The modeled translation readout is not measured ribosome occupancy; the turnover projection is descriptive rather than causal; and the protein-abundance association is a cross-sectional protein-omics relation.  The finite numbers above do not imply translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, organismal phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality.  Such claims would require their own layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  In particular, a direct $Q\to P$ association is not a mechanism claim unless a measured translation readout survives and conditioning on that measured readout removes or materially reduces the direct $Q\to P$ signal.  The turnover result here moves in the opposite direction: after the modeled translation complement is isolated, human median protein half-life accounts for essentially none of the remaining component above a matched null baseline.
