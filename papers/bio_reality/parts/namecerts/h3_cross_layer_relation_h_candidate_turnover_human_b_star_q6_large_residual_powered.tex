\subsection{Human turnover as a small-absorption test for the large $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-human-turnover-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite-row carrier.}
This chapter records a human protein-turnover contact for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after the amino-acid and sequence-composition controls have already been imposed.  The row set is the matched human CDS--abundance--turnover join.  The coordinate block has nine columns,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The control block has $24$ columns before rank reduction, with rank $23$, consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  On the full human protein-abundance CDS join the experiment reports $19053$ rows and a PAXdb CDS hit rate $0.9779294769799313$.  The turnover contact is Mathieson et al. human primary-cell protein half-life, using the median half-life across cell types and replicates.  The identifier route is gene name to reviewed UniProt primary gene and then to STRING $9606.ENSP$; it gives $8164$ valid turnover proteins and $8057$ rows in the simultaneous abundance--CDS--turnover join.

\paragraph{Residual quantity being tested.}
The tested object is not raw protein abundance and not raw half-life.  First, protein abundance is residualized against the control block $Z$ and projected onto the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate space.  Then the modeled translation component is removed, leaving the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.  The finite diagnostics are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{modeled} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same $d_{modeled}$ value is recovered against the full human complement reference with absolute difference $0.0$, so the turnover-row computation is anchored to the same large residual used by the human abundance-complement calculation, even though the turnover join is smaller: $8057$ rows instead of $19053$.

\paragraph{Turnover projection result.}
The turnover readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life.  Its one-dimensional turnover basis has rank $1$.  The computed direct turnover association with the large complement residual is small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{turn}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{Absorb}_{turn} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{Absorb}_{turn}^{basis\ check} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{combined} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
A degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials gives mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  Since
$$
\begin{aligned}
3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6} &< 0.0004342285066808464,
\end{aligned}
$$
the turnover coordinate does not exceed the local null envelope.  Thus, for this human primary-cell half-life contact, turnover is not an adequate explanation of the large modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding finite matrix.}
The negative turnover result is informative because the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are not generally silent against protein or translation readouts.  In the direct protein-omics surface, the human $R^2_{QP}$ is $0.04454565118019448$ with all nine coordinates surviving, while other organisms also show positive direct abundance association, for example $0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, and $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse.  In the measured ribosome-TE surface, the measured $R^2_{QT}$ list includes $0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, again with nine surviving coordinates in each of those measured-TE joins.  The measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition gives mediation fractions
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.4793069656345167,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{zebrafish}} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human measured-TE mediation value is therefore weak, while yeast supplies a much stronger translation-conditioned component.  A separate joint TE--mRNA-stability variance partition reports right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast, with joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, respectively.  The yeast readout dictionary certificate, using $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$ and $\rho_{join}=0.05$, covers $0.24538211439730545$ of the target and leaves $0.7546178856026946$ unexplained.  The $f3_{stress}$ dwell test in yeast gives an approximate $p$-value $7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$ and held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ against a null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$.  These neighboring finite rows show that the turnover null is not caused by a globally empty $Q6$ matrix; it is a layer-specific failure of human half-life to absorb this particular residual.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The available modeled-tAI four-case classification places yeast entirely in the translation-and-protein positive class without a direct leftover: $9$ of $9$ coordinates are classified as case $D$.  In \emph{E. coli}, $7$ coordinates are case $D$ and $2$ are positive-positive without a mediation drop.  The human turnover contact does not itself assign such a case, because the tested readout is half-life against the already formed modeled-translation complement rather than a same-row $Q_i,T_j,P_k$ mediation surface.  Its proper readback is narrower: human turnover has a measurable but null-level projection on $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and boundary.}
The finite-row conclusion is that the human Mathieson half-life contact is ready as a negative H-candidate test.  It validates the computation of the turnover projection, the null comparison, the row join, and the identifier route, but it does not promote turnover to a mediator of the large $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  The observed absorption $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ is below the degree-of-freedom matched null threshold $0.0004342285066808464$; therefore the residual remains largely outside this turnover readout.  No translation mechanism, folding order, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, causal pathway, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter.  Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own controlled statistic, and this half-life projection supplies only a descriptive human protein-turnover test.
