\subsection{Human protein turnover against the large $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-turnover-human-b-star-q6-large-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record tests whether human protein half-life explains any appreciable part of the large protein-abundance component left after the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates have already been compared with a modeled translation readout.  The finite surface is the human joined table over coding-sequence codon counts, PAXdb whole-organism abundance, and Mathieson et al. 2018 primary-cell protein half-lives.  The abundance contact reports $19053$ coding sequences joined to measured protein abundance, with a CDS join hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$ and $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  The turnover contact contains $8164$ reported turnover proteins and $8164$ valid turnover entries; after the gene-name to UniProt reviewed primary-gene to STRING mapping, the all-readout table has $8057$ rows.  The skipped CDS rows are dominated by missing turnover matches: $10996$ coding sequences have no turnover match, while the recorded counts for empty sense-codon vectors, invalid length, missing protein id, missing abundance match, and non-object rows are all $0$.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates and controls.}
The tested coordinate block has $9$ residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The nuisance design has $24$ columns and rank $23$, consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  On the full human abundance-CDS join, the projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component has squared norm $1370.6490492302532$.  Removing the modeled tAI component leaves a large complement with squared norm $1262.2237678771598$, giving the modeled-complement diagnostic
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 \|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
 \|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598.
\end{aligned}
$$
The turnover-specific table preserves the same modeled-complement value: the current full-human-join value and the full abundance-CDS reference are both $0.9208949355679455$, so their absolute difference is $0.0$.  A separate coverage recomputation gives $0.7820306826175398$ on the narrower turnover availability pattern; this is a coverage diagnostic, not the main complement scalar.

\paragraph{Turnover absorption result.}
The half-life readout is the median half-life across Mathieson primary-cell measurements, converted to a residualized $\log_{10}$ turnover coordinate on the same row set.  The descriptive projection of the modeled-translation complement onto this one-dimensional turnover readout gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
 R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
 \mathrm{Absorb}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
 \mathrm{Absorb}_{\mathrm{basis\ check}} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
 d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The degree-of-freedom matched null is rank one in every trial.  Across $200$ trials its mean absorbed fraction is $0.0001262572958089998$ and its $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  The observed absorbed fraction $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ is below that threshold, so the human turnover readout does not carry a positive H-candidate signal for the large modeled-translation complement.  In finite-row terms, protein half-life was testable here, but it did not explain the residual beyond the matched null baseline.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding cross-layer table.}
The negative turnover result is meaningful because the upstream abundance residual is not absent.  In the direct protein-omics survival table, the human entry has $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving after the same control family, on $19053$ joined CDS-abundance rows.  In the measured ribosome-TE mediation table, the human three-way join has $11554$ rows; the measured TE survival is $S_{QT}=0.018837911344127926$, the protein-abundance survival is $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, and the measured mediated fraction is only $0.0027966485036786744$.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition, the human values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 R^2_{QP} &= 0.04274178488511215,\\
 R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04250692268197657,\\
 R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.036196017359154814,\\
 R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.0355223930537993,
\end{aligned}
$$
with a joint mediation fraction $0.16890712099923358$, TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  Thus, in the human table, mRNA stability carries much more of the available variance partition than measured TE, while protein turnover, as tested by half-life, fails the matched-null absorption criterion.

\paragraph{Other readout contrasts.}
The localization H-candidate gives a useful scale comparison.  On $16008$ human localization-joined rows with $12$ localization columns of rank $12$, the localization absorption is $0.009254913329269913$, while the matched null has mean $0.0008032852113983743$ and $95$th percentile $0.0013758521256775384$.  That localization projection is positive relative to its null, whereas the turnover projection is not.  The yeast readout dictionary gives another benchmark: on a yeast context join with $n=1800$, $5$ folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, and $120$ permutations, the best selection-aware dictionary coverage is $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$, with unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ and selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.029149007242956115$.  The yeast $f3\_stress$ dwell test has $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1257$, base $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$, approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$, and permutation null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$.  Its positional refinement identifies the $5'$ segment as dominant, and the structural-order positional table classifies the outcome as $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$, with weighted ramp increment $0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, and paired ramp-minus-body value $0.001378137149572267$.  These contrasts show that the turnover null is not an artifact of a globally insensitive pipeline; other layer-matched readouts can exceed their finite null surfaces.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The correct reading is narrow.  Human half-life is a tested post-translational candidate for the part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal that remains after modeled tAI has been removed.  The row set is large enough to compute the statistic, the source mapping is explicit, and the result is below its one-dimensional degree-of-freedom matched null.  Therefore the large human residual should not be attributed to protein turnover on this evidence.  The result leaves open condition-specific turnover, cell-type-specific dynamics, nonlinear degradation models, localization, complex assembly, measurement platform effects, and other post-translational or regulatory readouts.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No translation mechanism follows from this turnover projection.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, causal mediation, or universal biological rule follows from the direct $q_i\to P_k$ association, from the modeled tAI complement, or from the negative half-life projection.  A stronger claim would need a separate layer-matched contact and a positive statistic on the relevant rows.  In particular, a mechanism claim would require a measured translation step $q_i\to T_j$ that survives the stated controls and a downstream reduction of the $q_i\to P_k$ signal after conditioning on that measured translation readout.  This chapter records a finite human protein-turnover test and its null outcome, not a causal explanation of the remaining abundance residual.
