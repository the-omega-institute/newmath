\subsection{Human turnover as a residual post-translation candidate}\label{subsec:bsk-q6-human-turnover-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite row tested here asks whether the large human abundance residual left after a modeled translation readout can be accounted for by protein turnover.  The objects are nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates on coding sequences, a control matrix $Z$, a modeled translation-complement readout $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$, a matched whole-organism protein-abundance readout $P$, and a human primary-cell half-life readout $T_{\mathrm{turn}}$.  The nine coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control list consists of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  In the recorded computation the control column count is $24$ and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.

\paragraph{Matched finite rows.}
The human turnover file reports $8164$ valid turnover proteins and is joined to $19053$ human CDS-protein rows and $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  The turnover-abundance-CDS intersection contains $8057$ rows, with $10996$ CDS records skipped because no turnover match was present.  The abundance join hit rate recorded for the human CDS table is $0.9779294769799313$.  Turnover is the median half-life across Mathieson 2018 human primary-cell measurements, mapped by $\mathrm{gene\_name}\to$ reviewed UniProt primary gene name $\to$ STRING $9606.ENSP$.  The abundance readout is the PAXdb human whole-organism integrated entry, recorded with publication year $2026$.  No duplicate, missing, or nonpositive turnover entries were retained in the matched turnover payload.

\paragraph{Residual construction.}
Let $Q$ denote the residualized nine-column $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate matrix after projection away from $Z$, and let $P$ denote the residualized protein-abundance vector on the same human rows.  The direct abundance component explained by $Q$ has squared norm
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P\_{Q}\|^{2} &= 1370.6490492302532.
\end{aligned}
$$
After removing the modeled translation-complement readout $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$, the remaining modeled-complement residual has squared norm
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P\_{Q\perp T\_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^{2} &= 1262.2237678771598.
\end{aligned}
$$
The modeled-complement diagnostic on the full human abundance-CDS join is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The turnover run recomputes the same full-human complement value with absolute difference $0.0$ from the reference complement experiment, so the turnover test is attached to the same large human residual rather than to a different abundance surface.  A separate coverage diagnostic on the current $8057$-row intersection gives $0.7820306826175398$; this is interpreted as a coverage effect of the turnover join, not as a replacement for the full-human complement value.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The half-life readout is residualized as $\log_{10}$ half-life against the same controls and used as a one-dimensional candidate absorber of $P\_{Q\perp T\_{\mathrm{mod}}}$.  The recorded turnover association with the $Q$ coordinates is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}(Q,T\_{\mathrm{turn}}) &= 0.004615408769189838.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fraction of the large modeled-complement abundance residual absorbed by turnover is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A\_{\mathrm{turn}} &= 0.0000035740764900116538,
\end{aligned}
$$
with an independent basis check of $0.0000035740764900125626$.  The combined diagnostic after adding the turnover readout is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the half-life column is a valid finite readout, but its observed absorption of the modeled-complement residual is essentially zero on the scale of the human $Q\to P$ abundance residual.

\paragraph{Null calibration.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses a rank-one readout, matching the single turnover column.  Across $200$ null trials the recorded mean absorbed fraction is $0.0001262572958089998$ and the $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  The observed turnover absorption $0.0000035740764900116538$ is below both the null mean and the null $95$th percentile, and the finite row records $\mathrm{exceeds\_null}=\mathrm{false}$.  The rank list for the null is the singleton $\{1\}$, so the comparison is not inflated by a multi-column turnover basis.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and stability rows.}
The measured-translation finite row gives the human measured-TE decomposition on $11554$ joined CDS rows.  In that row $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{measured}}=0.018837911344127926$, and the measured mediation fraction is only $0.0027966485036786744$.  The rank diagnostics are nondegenerate: $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(T)=1$, $\operatorname{rank}(X,T)=10$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $11531$.  A second human row that conditions the abundance residual on measured TE and mRNA stability uses $11322$ joined rows and records

\begin{aligned}
R^{2}\_{QP} &= 0.04274178488511215,\\
R^{2}\_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04250692268197657,\\
R^{2}\_{QP\mid M} &= 0.036196017359154814,\\
R^{2}\_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.0355223930537993.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding human joint mediation fraction is $0.16890712099923358$, with measured-TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These comparison rows show that the human abundance residual can be partially partitioned by mRNA stability in a measured-readout setting, while the separate protein-turnover half-life column tested here does not absorb the large modeled-complement residual above its own null.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
For this human turnover row the finite readback is a negative H-candidate result.  It is not a translation-surviving coordinate, because the turnover test is downstream of the modeled translation complement and does not itself supply a measured translation-efficiency survival step.  It is not a protein-omics mechanism claim, because conditioning on the turnover half-life column does not remove or materially reduce the direct $Q\to P$ abundance residual beyond a degree-of-freedom matched null.  In the four-case language for $Q,T,P$ surfaces, this row belongs on the direct-or-unexplained side rather than on the mediated side: the protein-abundance residual remains large, and the tested turnover contact fails to account for it.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result is a statistical projection over finite joined rows.  It does not establish causality, a ribosome mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  A claim at any of those layers would require a separate layer-matched contact and a positive controlled statistic at that layer.  In particular, direct $Q\to P$ association cannot be promoted to a mechanism unless a measured translation readout survives and conditioning on that readout reduces the protein-abundance signal.  The present row instead says something narrower and useful: within the $8057$-row human turnover-abundance-CDS intersection, median primary-cell protein half-life is not the finite readout that explains the large modeled-translation-complement $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual.
