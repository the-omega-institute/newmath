\subsection{Human turnover as a test of the large $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-human-turnover-large-residual}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record tests whether measured human protein turnover accounts for the large abundance component left after the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ codon-residual surface is compared with a modeled translation readout.  The finite matrix uses nine residual coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The covariate block has $24$ columns before rank reduction and rank $23$ after linear dependence is removed.  It contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline over the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Upstream abundance residual.}
On the full human abundance--CDS join, the direct $Q$-to-protein surface is powered rather than rank-degenerate: $19053$ CDS rows are joined, the reported CDS hit rate is $0.9779294769799313$, the residualized $Q$ design has rank $9$, and the protein-abundance residual energy after $Z$ is $30769.5367150478$.  In that direct omics readout the human partial surface has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{QP}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.04454565118019448,\\
\#\{\mathrm{surviving\ coordinates}\} &= 9.
\end{aligned}
$$
This upstream fact justifies asking what other measured readouts can absorb the residual, but it does not by itself identify a mechanism.

\paragraph{Modeled-translation complement.}
The turnover experiment starts from the human abundance component explained by the nine-coordinate $Q$ surface and removes the component accounted for by the modeled translation readout.  On the full human abundance join,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^{2} &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^{2} &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same modeled-complement value is recovered exactly on the current full-human join diagnostic: the absolute difference from the reference is $0.0$ and the tolerance check at $0.02$ passes.  Thus the turnover calculation is not testing the whole direct abundance signal; it is testing the large remainder after the modeled translation component has already been separated.

\paragraph{Turnover contact and join.}
The turnover readout is the Mathieson et al. 2018 human primary-cell protein half-life table, using the median half-life across cell types and replicates.  The identifier route maps Mathieson gene names to reviewed human UniProt primary gene names and then to STRING identifiers of the form $9606.ENSP$.  The turnover source reports $8164$ valid turnover proteins; the abundance source reports $19483$ valid abundance proteins.  After requiring turnover, abundance, and CDS codon rows together, the finite panel contains
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 8057,\\
 n_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= 8164,\\
 n_{\mathrm{abundance}} &= 19483,\\
 n_{\mathrm{CDS\ joined\ in\ abundance\ reference}} &= 19053.
\end{aligned}
$$
The skipped CDS rows are concentrated in the missing-turnover boundary: $10996$ CDS records have no turnover match, while the counters for missing protein identifiers, invalid lengths, non-object records, and empty sense-codon counts are all zero.  The turnover payload hash is $5e19833b82a7e31c05565f2a4c1797d52fcb477fccd0d13a856b58267a5ca54e$, and the source workbook hash is $e0503f49230680cbf86fbc86db145ba439c03382e584fde8f0d83241c9826515$.

\paragraph{Turnover projection result.}
The tested scalar is the projection of $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}(\mathrm{half\ life})$.  The resulting fit is small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ turnover} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{basis\ check} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses rank-$1$ draws and $200$ trials.  Its mean absorption is $0.0001262572958089998$ and its $95$th percentile is $0.0004342285066808464$.  Since
$$
\begin{aligned}
3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6} &< 0.0004342285066808464,
\end{aligned}
$$
the human turnover readout does not exceed the null boundary.  The finite verdict is therefore negative for turnover as an explanatory carrier of the large modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation conditioning.}
The measured-TE mediation experiment supplies a useful local comparison.  On the human three-way codon--measured-TE--abundance join, $11554$ rows are available.  The measured-TE readout has
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{QT}^{\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
S_{QP}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.04288464996165745,\\
M_{QTP}^{\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0027966485036786744.
\end{aligned}
$$
This says that a same-row measured translation readout exists for a restricted human panel, but it absorbs only a very small fraction of the abundance association.  The turnover test is still a distinct $H$-candidate test: it asks whether the residual that remains beyond modeled translation is carried by protein half-life.  The answer in this finite panel is no.

\paragraph{Local readback.}
The chapter-specific readback is a four-part statement.  First, the human $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface has a nontrivial direct protein-abundance association after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, and $M$-density controls.  Second, a modeled translation readout removes only a portion of that association, leaving $\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^{2}=1262.2237678771598$.  Third, measured human protein half-life is available on $8057$ matched rows and gives only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ absorption of that residual.  Fourth, that absorption is below the rank-matched null threshold, so turnover is not accepted as the finite carrier of the remaining human $H$ component.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The result is a statistical projection over a finite joined panel, not a causal experiment.  It does not show that codon residuals alter protein degradation rates, and it does not show that half-life mediates any synonymous-code effect.  The half-life data come from human primary-cell measurements summarized across cell types, while the abundance and CDS contacts use different source surfaces.  The calculation also does not justify claims about translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Those layers require their own matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.  Here the admissible conclusion is narrower: human turnover was tested as a candidate absorber of the large $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual, and in this finite matrix it failed the null-calibrated absorption criterion.
