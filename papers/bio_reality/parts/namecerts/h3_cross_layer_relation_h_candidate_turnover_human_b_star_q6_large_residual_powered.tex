\subsection{Human protein turnover against the modeled-translation residual}\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
The human turnover test asks whether measured protein half-life accounts for the part of human protein abundance that remains after the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate surface is compared with a modeled translation readout.  The active coordinate set has nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  These coordinates were residualized with a $24$-column control design whose effective rank was $23$.  The controls were the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.

\paragraph{Rows and readouts.}
The human abundance side used $19053$ CDS rows after the CDS-to-protein abundance join, with a reported CDS join hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$ and $19483$ valid abundance proteins in the PAXdb whole-organism integrated table.  The turnover side used Mathieson et al. human primary-cell half-lives, summarized by the median half-life across cell types and replicates.  The turnover table reported $8164$ turnover proteins and $8164$ valid mapped turnover proteins.  After requiring abundance, CDS, and turnover to meet on the same protein identifiers, the finite row table contained $8057$ rows; $10996$ CDS records were skipped only because no turnover match was available.  The stated identifier route was gene name to reviewed UniProt primary gene name and then to the STRING $9606.ENSP$ protein identifier.  No turnover record was discarded for duplicate protein id, missing protein id, or non-positive half-life.

\paragraph{Residual surface.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the human protein-abundance component projected onto the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ design, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the component left after removing the modeled translation readout.  The measured finite-row values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same modeled-complement value was reproduced on the current human turnover join: the reference complement experiment and the current full human join both gave $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455$, with absolute difference $0.0$.  A separate coverage diagnostic on the reduced turnover-available row set gave $0.7820306826175398$, which records the row-availability effect rather than a replacement of the full-join complement value.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
The H-candidate readout is residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life.  Projecting $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto this one-dimensional turnover readout gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{absorbed\ by\ turnover} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{basis\ check} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The basis check agrees with the reported absorbed fraction to numerical precision, so the computed quantity is internally stable.  The magnitude, however, is tiny.  The degree-of-freedom matched null used $200$ trials with rank list $\{1\}$, mean $0.0001262572958089998$, and $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  Since $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ is below this null boundary, the turnover readout does not pass the stated excess-absorption test.  Thus the human half-life table is a valid tested H-candidate, but the finite rows do not support turnover as the readout that explains the large human modeled-translation complement.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein-abundance surface.}
The same human abundance source has a direct $B^*_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance association after the sequence controls.  In the direct protein-omics survival computation, human had $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ with all nine coordinates surviving at the positive threshold.  This direct signal is not a mechanism statement.  In the measured translation mediation table, human joined $11554$ three-way CDS, measured-TE, and abundance rows; it gave $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S^{\mathrm{meas}}_{QT}=0.018837911344127926$, raw measured mediation fraction $0.0027966485036786744$, and clipped measured mediation fraction $0.0027966485036786744$.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability partition, the human row gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.04274178488511215,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.036196017359154814,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04250692268197657,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.0355223930537993.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding human joint mediation fraction was $0.16890712099923358$, with measured-TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These neighboring finite-row results explain why turnover was worth testing, but they do not rescue the present half-life projection: the specific human primary-cell turnover readout absorbs only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the modeled-translation complement and remains below its own null threshold.

\paragraph{Dictionary and positional neighbors.}
The broader yeast-centered readout dictionary also shows that residual abundance signal can be partly reducible without becoming fully explained.  In the partial-mediation certificate, the yeast join had $n=1800$ from $4758$ available rows, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.029149007242956115$, accepted coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell experiment, also on yeast, had $n=1257$, base $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, dwell-alone $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, full $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$, approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$, null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$, and $120$ permutations.  Its positional companion classified the leading dwell segment as $5'$ rather than $3'$, with $5'$ passing and $3'$ not passing.  A separate structural-order positional run classified the structural-neighbor module as $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$, with primary ramp length $50$, weighted ramp increment $0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, paired ramp-minus-body increment $0.001378137149572267$, ramp sign-flip probability $0.0478515625$, body sign-flip probability $0.54296875$, and paired ramp-minus-body probability $0.05859375$.  These rows show that other measured readouts can capture pieces of the residual surface, but the human half-life readout tested here is not one of the passing absorbers.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row verdict is a negative H-candidate result for human protein turnover.  The experiment successfully joined $8057$ human proteins, reconstructed the large modeled-translation complement with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455$, and computed a one-rank turnover projection.  The observed turnover absorption was $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, far below the degree-of-freedom matched null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  Therefore the residual human abundance component should be described as deeper than this median primary-cell half-life readout, not as explained by turnover.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This chapter records a finite statistical projection on matched rows.  It does not establish causal mediation, translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, or a universal biological law.  A direct $q_i\to P_k$ association remains only a protein-omics association unless a measured translation readout supplies a surviving $q_i\to T_j$ step and conditioning on that readout reduces the $q_i\to P_k$ signal.  Likewise, a half-life projection would need to exceed its own row-matched null before it could be treated as a positive H-candidate signal; here it does not.
