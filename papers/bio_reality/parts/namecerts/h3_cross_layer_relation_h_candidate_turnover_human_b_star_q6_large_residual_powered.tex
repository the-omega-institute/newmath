\subsection{Human Turnover as a Low-Absorption Candidate for the Large Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-turnover-large-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The human turnover test asks whether a protein half-life readout accounts for the part of the human $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal that remains after the modeled translation readout has already been removed.  The finite carrier is the joined table of human coding sequences, measured whole-organism protein abundance, Mathieson et al. primary-cell protein half-lives, the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, and the shared control design.  The nine coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block has $24$ columns, rank $23$, and consists of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The full human CDS--abundance join reports $19053$ joined coding units with a CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$; the turnover-specific all-readout join contains $8057$ rows, while $10996$ CDS records have no turnover match.  The turnover source contributes $8164$ valid protein half-life records, with the median half-life across cell types selected as the scalar readout and a gene-name to reviewed-UniProt to STRING mapping.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
The tested target is not raw abundance.  First, human protein abundance is residualized against the shared controls and projected onto the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_Q\|^2 &= 1370.6490492302532,\\
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2 &= 1262.2237678771598,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= 0.9208949355679455.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the modeled translation readout removes only part of the human $Q\to P$ component; the remaining target is still large.  The turnover chapter then asks whether residualized $\log_{10}$ half-life absorbs this remaining vector.  The recomputed human-complement diagnostic agrees with the full abundance-CDS reference: the current full-human value is $0.9208949355679455$, the reference value is $0.9208949355679455$, and the absolute difference is $0.0$.  A coverage recomputation on the turnover join gives the separate diagnostic value $0.7820306826175398$, which records that the smaller turnover table samples a restricted slice of the full human abundance surface.

\paragraph{Turnover projection.}
On the $8057$-row all-readout join, residualized human protein half-life has a measurable but very small alignment with the large modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  The fitted quantities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}} &= 0.004615408769189838,\\
\mathrm{Absorb}_{\mathrm{turn}} &= 3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6},\\
\mathrm{Absorb}_{\mathrm{turn,basis}} &= 3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6},\\
d_{\mathrm{combined}} &= 0.8901589776585174.
\end{aligned}
$$
The basis check agrees to numerical precision, so the projection itself is stable.  The substantive result is negative for turnover-as-explanation: the absorbed fraction is far below the degree-of-freedom matched null boundary.  The null calculation used $200$ trials, rank list $\{1\}$, null mean $0.0001262572958089998$, and null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$; the observed turnover absorption does not exceed that boundary.  In finite-row terms, human half-life is a computed candidate readout, but it does not explain the large residual component at the declared null standard.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein-abundance surface.}
The same human abundance surface is not empty.  In the direct protein-omics survival run, all nine residual coordinates survive against measured PAXdb abundance for human, with $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ and $9$ surviving coordinates on the $19053$-row human CDS join.  Across the full direct protein panel, the largest reported $R^2_{QP}$ is yeast at $0.29837624994140827$, while human sits in the moderate range; every listed organism has rank $9$ for the residualized coordinate block and rank $23$ for the controls.  This establishes that the human turnover result is not a failure to form the $Q\to P$ abundance surface.  Instead, it is a failed absorption attempt for one proposed post-abundance explanatory readout.

\paragraph{Translation and mediation context.}
The measured-TE mediation check gives the human three-way join $11554$ rows, $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, $S_{QT}^{\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$, and measured mediated fraction $0.0027966485036786744$.  The same run reports the human control ranks $\mathrm{rank}(X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(T)=1$, $\mathrm{rank}(X,T)=10$, and $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$.  In the joint TE--mRNA-stability variance partition, human has $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$, giving joint mediation fraction $0.16890712099923358$, TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and right-tail permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$.  These numbers place human turnover in a wider map: direct abundance association exists, measured TE absorbs very little in human, mRNA-stability absorbs more than TE in the available joint table, and protein half-life, as tested here, absorbs essentially none of the modeled-translation-complement residual.

\paragraph{Dictionary and positional controls.}
The yeast dictionary and dwell runs provide comparison points for what a positive absorption-style result looks like under the same finite-row discipline.  The partial mediation certificate on yeast used $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$ from $4758$ available rows, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$; its best selected readout family covered $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ above a selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell check used the same $1800$ base size and $5$ folds, joined $1257$ rows, and found held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ against a permutation null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$, with $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  Those companion finite checks show that the pipeline can detect nontrivial readout absorption when it is present; the human turnover result remains a specific low-absorption outcome rather than a general limitation of the test design.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
For this chapter, the finite readback is case $C$-like for the turnover candidate: a direct $Q\to P$ abundance surface exists, but the tested half-life readout does not provide a successful explanatory absorption of the large modeled-translation-complement residual.  It is not case $D$, because no measured translation-readout chain $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ is established here and the turnover projection does not pass its degree-of-freedom matched boundary.  It is also not a null statement about all post-translational variables; it only says that the Mathieson human half-life scalar, joined and residualized as specified, does not account for the residual at the registered threshold.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result is a finite statistical projection statement, not a causal or mechanistic claim.  The chapter does not infer translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates, from the PAXdb abundance surface, or from the half-life projection.  Protein turnover here is a descriptive scalar readout from human primary-cell data, summarized across cell types, and the observed absorption is below the null boundary.  Any stronger statement about translation mechanism, degradation machinery, folded state, compartmental context, function, perturbational rescue, or a general biological law would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic on the same units.
