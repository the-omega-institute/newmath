\paragraph{Licensed test.} The intended statistic compares high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels inside amino-acid-matched editable windows after all declared controls. A positive bounded bridge would require the RNA half-life residual to track measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, not merely the coordinate label. It must also survive a coordinate--occupancy decoupling test: if occupancy is experimentally separated from the residual-coordinate label, the half-life effect must follow occupancy rather than bare $B^{*}_{Q6}$ geometry. Finally, the effect must collapse under target-RBP loss and return under wild-type rescue, while binding-defective rescue or non-target control must fail to restore the signal. In compact form, the allowed bridge has the shape

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{effect}(Q, H \mid Z,T) &> 0,\\
 \mathrm{effect}(Q, H \mid Z,T,O) &\text{ follows measured } O,\\
 \mathrm{loss}_{\mathrm{RBP}} &\Rightarrow \mathrm{collapse},\\
 \mathrm{rescue}_{\mathrm{WT}} &\Rightarrow \mathrm{return},\\
 \mathrm{rescue}_{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &
Rightarrow \mathrm{return}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $Q$ denotes the residual-coordinate label, $H$ denotes the RNA half-life residual, $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout, $O$ denotes measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, and $Z$ denotes the full control set. The statistic is finite-panel only; it is not a universal sequence law.

If occupancy exists but does not follow the coordinate--occupancy decoupling test, the result is not an RBP mechanism. If target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, or wild-type rescue does not restore it while binding-defective or non-target controls remain negative, the bridge is not licensed.
