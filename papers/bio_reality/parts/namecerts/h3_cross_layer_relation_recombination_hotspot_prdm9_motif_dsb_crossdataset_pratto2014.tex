\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel for synonymously editable coding windows.  Each row carries a residual-coordinate label of type $B^{*}_{Q6}$, an amino-acid-matched coding-window identity, a same-row translation readout, a matched mRNA or transcription control, an RNA half-life readout, a local decay-factor RBP occupancy state when such a measurement is available, and a perturbation or rescue condition when such a row is available.  The biological question is not whether codon geometry alone predicts RNA decay.  The question is narrower: whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual can be treated as a bounded cross-layer relation only after it survives the translation-readout boundary and then follows a prespecified RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern.

The finite audit payload attached to this record is experiment run $a5e9df27ecd0$, verified at $2026$-$06$-$23T19{:}06{:}26{+}00{:}00$.  The available scalar row counts are
$$
\begin{aligned}

 n_c &= 21512,\\
 t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 187.044\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers certify that the present record is tied to a concrete finite computation for the associated claim identifier, but the attached facts do not include R lists, M lists, fitted $\lambda$ values, p-values, module labels, hit rates, or a same-window RBP perturbation matrix.  Consequently, the chapter can state the finite audit surface and the admissible test, but it cannot report a positive RBP-mediated RNA-decay effect.

\paragraph{Controlled null.}
For a row $w$, write $q(w)$ for its high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label, $T(w)$ for the same-row translation readout, $H(w)$ for the RNA half-life readout, $O(w)$ for measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, and $C(w)$ for the matched control vector.  The control vector includes transcription rate or matched mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state when relevant, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the declared translation readout.  The finite null is the exchangeability statement
$$
\begin{aligned}
 H(w) \perp q(w) \mid C(w), T(w)
\end{aligned}
$$
inside the declared window panel.  A departure from this null is only an RNA-stability association unless the occupancy and perturbation rows are also present.  In particular, a half-life residual that remains after the controls but is not accompanied by a measured change in local decay-factor RBP occupancy does not identify an RBP route.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
A positive cross-layer reading requires four finite-row conditions.  First, high- and low-coordinate synonymous windows must be amino-acid matched and editable without changing the encoded peptide.  Second, the RNA half-life contrast must remain after conditioning on $C(w)$ and $T(w)$.  Third, the contrast must track a prespecified local decay-factor RBP occupancy state under a design that can decouple coordinate from occupancy.  Fourth, target-RBP loss must collapse the effect, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target controls must fail to restore it.  In compact form, the required pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H(q \mid C,T) &\ne 0,\\
 \Delta_H(q \mid C,T,O) &\text{ is explained by the declared occupancy route},\\
 \Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
 \Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT}},\\
 \Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\not\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The criterion is intentionally conjunctive.  Translation readout, mRNA control, RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, and perturbation rescue are separate measured surfaces; naming one of them does not supply the others.

\paragraph{What the current finite facts support.}
The attached contacts support only bounded measurement routes.  The RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene half-life estimates with dataset provenance and join keys.  The ribosome-profiling contact supplies a possible translation-efficiency readout, typically expressible as $TE = RPF / mRNA$ once matched RPF and mRNA rows exist.  The matched mRNA contact supplies a transcription-layer control for residualization.  These contacts are sufficient to define the required finite test design, but not sufficient to assert that the design has been completed for this RNA-decay RBP question.

The record therefore licenses the following local statement.  In the finite panel with row counts $n_a=21636$ and $n_c=21512$, the admissible claim is a guarded boundary claim: a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate RNA half-life effect may be promoted to a bounded RBP-mediated cross-layer relation only if the same rows contain the translation readout, matched mRNA or transcription controls, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Since the present payload does not include the occupancy and perturbation/rescue matrix, the positive bridge is not established here.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from a coordinate-associated half-life residual.  A ribosome-profiling route does not become an RNA half-life route, an mRNA-abundance control does not become an RBP occupancy measurement, and a same-row translation readout does not by itself prove an RNA-decay mechanism.  The only allowed conclusion at the present data boundary is that the finite record specifies a testable RNA half-life and RBP-occupancy design whose positive bridge requires additional same-window occupancy and perturbation rows.
