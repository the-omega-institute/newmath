\paragraph{Licensed positive condition.}
The cross-layer RNA-half-life claim is licensed only under a conjunction of finite tests. First, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows must remain distinguishable for controlled RNA half-life after the translation-readout boundary and the matched mRNA controls have been applied. Second, the direction of the half-life residual must follow measured local occupancy of the specified decay-factor RBP when coordinate and occupancy are experimentally separated. Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life effect. Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect. Fifth, binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to restore it. In compact form, the positive call requires
$$
\begin{aligned}
  &\Delta H(q \mid Z,T,O,P) \ne 0,\\
  &\Delta H(q \mid Z,T,O_{\mathrm{decoupled}},P) \text{ follows measured occupancy rather than coordinate alone},\\
  &\Delta H(q \mid Z,T,O,P_{\mathrm{loss}}) \approx 0,\\
  &\Delta H(q \mid Z,T,O,P_{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}) \text{ returns},\\
  &\Delta H(q \mid Z,T,O,P_{\mathrm{binding\ defective/non\ target}}) \approx 0.

If target-RBP loss does not remove the effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it, the result is not a licensed perturbation-supported RBP bridge.
