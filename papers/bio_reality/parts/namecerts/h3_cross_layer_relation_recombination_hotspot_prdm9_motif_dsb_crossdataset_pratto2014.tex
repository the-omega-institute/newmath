\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RBP RNA Half-Life Boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
The record considered here is a finite-row BioReality packet for synonymously editable coding-RNA windows carrying residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, matched translation readouts, measured RNA half-life readouts, and a proposed decay-factor RBP boundary.  The packet is not a positive mechanism claim.  It is a bounded test specification: a coordinate-associated half-life contrast may be read as a cross-layer relation only if the same finite row also carries the translation boundary, the matched mRNA controls, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, and a failed rescue by binding-defective or non-target controls.  Without those rows, the object remains an RNA-stability association constrained by missing contact surfaces.

The finite verification payload is named by run $a5e9df27ecd0$.  It records two finite row counts, $n_a=21636$ and $n_c=21512$, and a runtime of $187.044$ seconds, with timestamp $2026$-$06$-$28$ $03{:}03{:}25$ UTC.  These numbers are sufficient to identify the finite computation that attached the packet, but they are not sufficient to turn the packet into a measured RBP-decay mechanism.  In particular, the payload does not contain an R list of occupied windows, an M list of perturbation modules, fitted decay coefficients, $\lambda$ values, $p$-values, hit rates, or a module classification for target-RBP loss and rescue.  Therefore the row counts are used only as finite support for the audit surface, not as biological evidence for the stronger bridge.

\paragraph{Carrier and statistic.}
A row of the intended panel has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x = (&q, a, t, h, o, u, r, b, z, s),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q$ is the residual coordinate label inside the amino-acid-matched editable RNA window, $a$ is the matched amino-acid context, $t$ is the same-row translation readout, $h$ is the RNA half-life readout, $o$ is the measured local occupancy state of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $u$ is the perturbation or rescue condition, $r$ records whether the rescue arm is wild type, binding defective, or non-target, $b$ is the matched mRNA abundance or transcriptional control, $z$ is the remaining control vector, and $s$ is the declared finite statistic for the matched null.  The control vector includes transcription rate, transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, batch, amino-acid composition, and the translation readout itself.

For a high-coordinate label $q_H$ and a low-coordinate label $q_L$, the admissible half-life contrast is not the raw difference $h(q_H)-h(q_L)$.  The admissible quantity is a residual contrast after the declared controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
  &= \widehat{\operatorname{E}}\bigl[h \mid q=q_H, z, t, b, o, u, r\bigr]
   - \widehat{\operatorname{E}}\bigl[h \mid q=q_L, z, t, b, o, u, r\bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
This statistic may support the packet only when it tracks measured RBP occupancy under coordinate--occupancy decoupling, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return in binding-defective or non-target controls.  The condition is deliberately stronger than an RNA half-life association, because the claimed object is a cross-layer relation involving translation conditioning, local RBP binding, and RNA decay.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The finite null is that high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life once the matched controls are imposed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_0:
  \operatorname{Law}\bigl(h \mid q_H,z,t,b,o,u,r\bigr)
  =
  \operatorname{Law}\bigl(h \mid q_L,z,t,b,o,u,r\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
The packet breaks this null only if the residual half-life contrast remains after the translation-readout boundary and if the occupancy and perturbation arms behave in the stated direction.  A half-life contrast without measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is weaker: it says that a finite RNA-stability signal survived some controls.  An occupancy contrast without coordinate--occupancy decoupling is also weaker: it does not separate coordinate geometry from an ordinary motif or structure explanation.  A perturbation effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target failures is weaker again: it does not license the named RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Observed finite support.}
The current audited packet supplies only the finite computation identity and row counts.  The two row totals, $21636$ and $21512$, show that the audit was not a one-row or empty-record event, and the $187.044$ second runtime fixes the finite calculation used by the packet.  However, no local RBP occupancy matrix is present, no target-RBP loss matrix is present, no wild-type rescue matrix is present, and no binding-defective or non-target control matrix is present.  Consequently the finite data do not yet instantiate the intended row tuple $x$ at the RBP-decay layer.  They instantiate a bounded review surface whose positive conclusion is withheld until the missing same-window rows and the declared statistic are supplied.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule.}
The chapter therefore records a negative licensing result.  The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate label may organize finite rows and may define matched high-versus-low coordinate comparisons, but it is not itself a biological measurement.  RNA half-life alone does not imply translation, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general sequence law.  Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency, if later attached, would only supply a translation readout; it would not by itself be RNA half-life evidence or RBP occupancy evidence.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not a substitute for translation or RNA decay mechanism.  Likewise, a measured half-life contrast becomes an RBP-mediated decay bridge only after the local occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and failed defective or non-target rescue arms are present in the same finite panel.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite record is ready as a self-contained boundary chapter: it states exactly what the present rows can support and what they cannot support.  The present audit supports the existence of a finite reviewed packet with counts $n_a=21636$ and $n_c=21512$ from run $a5e9df27ecd0$, but it does not support a positive RBP RNA half-life bridge.  The admissible present conclusion is therefore: the conjecture remains bounded at the RNA half-life and translation-conditioned review boundary until a same-organism, same-window panel supplies measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared controlled half-life statistic.
