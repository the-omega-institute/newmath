\subsection{Translation-conditioned RBP RNA half-life boundary}
\label{sec:translation-conditioned-rbp-rna-half-life-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a finite panel of synonymously editable coding RNA windows.  Each row is meant to carry a residual-coordinate label $B^\ast_{Q6}$, an amino-acid-matched low-coordinate or high-coordinate comparison state, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy by a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, perturbation or rescue state for that protein, and the matched controls needed to prevent transcript abundance or composition from being mistaken for a decay mechanism.  The intended row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_i = (&q_i, a_i, T_i, H_i, O_i, P_i, Z_i, N_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the residual-coordinate label, $a_i$ is the amino-acid-matched synonymous window class, $T_i$ is the translation readout, $H_i$ is the controlled RNA half-life residual, $O_i$ is measured decay-factor RBP occupancy in the local window, $P_i$ is the perturbation or rescue condition, $Z_i$ is the declared control vector, and $N_i$ is the matched-null assignment used for the finite statistic.  The verified finite run attached to this record is not such a complete RBP panel.  It reports experiment run $a5e9df27ecd0$ with two finite row counts, $n_a=21636$ and $n_c=21512$, and runtime $187.044$ seconds.  These numbers establish that the present data contact is finite and row-indexed, but they do not by themselves contain measured RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective controls.

\paragraph{Permitted reading.}
The chapter licenses only a boundary statement.  A positive cross-layer claim would require a statistic comparing high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows after transcription rate, abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, batch, and same-row translation readout have all been included in $Z_i$.  The minimum admissible comparison is not merely
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i &\sim q_i + Z_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
because such a regression can only describe a finite RNA-stability association.  The RBP bridge additionally requires that the coordinate-associated half-life residual track measured local occupancy, disappear under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under a binding-defective or non-target control.  In finite-row notation, the intended support pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H(q,O,P\!=\!\mathrm{wildtype}) &\neq 0,\\
  \Delta_H(q,O,P\!=\!\mathrm{target\ loss}) &\approx 0,\\
  \Delta_H(q,O,P\!=\!\mathrm{wildtype\ rescue}) &\neq 0,\\
  \Delta_H(q,O,P\!=\!\mathrm{binding\ defective}) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row counts $21636$ and $21512$ therefore support only the existence of finite tabular material for the named run, not the stronger occupancy-and-rescue pattern.  The observed runtime, $187.044$ seconds, is useful as a provenance scalar for the computation but has no biological interpretation.

\paragraph{Null statistic.}
The null hypothesis is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the full control set has been applied.  Let $G_i \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ denote the coordinate class and let $H_i^\perp$ be the RNA half-life residual after removing the permitted controls.  The chapter-specific null is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i^\perp \mid G_i=\mathrm{high} \quad \text{and} \quad H_i^\perp \mid G_i=\mathrm{low}
\end{aligned}
$$
being indistinguishable under the declared matched-null statistic.  If a difference remains without measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, the result is still only a finite RNA half-life association.  If occupancy is present but does not move under coordinate/occupancy decoupling, the coordinate label is not licensed as an RBP-mediated cause.  If target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, or wild-type rescue does not restore it, the record remains below the level of an RBP bridge.  Binding-defective and non-target controls are part of the same boundary: they prevent ordinary perturbation response from being re-described as specific local RBP mediation.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The translation readout is mandatory but not promotional.  It prevents an RNA half-life association from being read as a translation-layer result when translation is unmeasured or unmatched.  It also blocks the reverse mistake: a ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact does not measure RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, or binding specificity.  Translation enters here as a same-row covariate and boundary condition, not as evidence for protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, causation, or a universal sequence law.  Those stronger readings require their own finite contacts at the relevant layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For the attached finite data, the admissible conclusion is a scoped boundary verdict.  The verified run supplies finite row counts $n_a=21636$ and $n_c=21512$ under run id $a5e9df27ecd0$, but the available payload does not include the decisive rows for measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Therefore the chapter can state the exact acceptance rule for a translation-conditioned RBP RNA half-life bridge, and it can record why the present finite material does not yet satisfy that rule.  No translation realization, protein output, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, function claim, phenotype claim, mechanistic claim, adaptive claim, causal claim, or universal biological law follows from the current finite-row evidence.
