\subsection{Rank-admissible translation boundary for the Mercer branchpoint panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-branchpoint-q6-rank-admissible-translation-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite rows and the biological surface.}
The Mercer branchpoint-motif record is used here as a bounded finite-row contact for a cross-layer question: when a synonymous residual coordinate from $B^{*}_{Q6}$ is compared with a translation readout, the comparison must first pass a rank-admissibility test before it can be described as translation survival. The branchpoint panel itself contains $n=10584$ intron-overlap rows after joining to UCSC hg19 gtexGeneV8 expression, with expression represented as $\log(1+\operatorname{median})$ over $54$ tissue expression scores and joined on the same strand. The finite run is expression-controlled and records a wall time of $138.47155$ seconds. These numbers make the panel usable as a controlled biological row surface, but they do not by themselves provide a translation readout. They therefore support a boundary statement rather than a positive $S^{QT}_{ij}$ claim.

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
For each proposed coordinate--readout entry, the data object is a residualized design matrix with one candidate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate $q_i$, one translation-layer readout $T_j$, and a control block $Z$. The controls include amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, sequence length, and an M-density baseline; when the row source requires it, expression or matched mRNA abundance is also held as a control rather than promoted to translation. The admissibility condition is finite and row-level:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^{\perp Z} &= q_i - \Pi_Z q_i,\\
T_j^{\perp Z} &= T_j - \Pi_Z T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij} &> 0 \quad \hbox{is reportable only if }\operatorname{rank}(Z,q_i,T_j)\hbox{ leaves non-saturated held-out degrees of freedom.}
\end{aligned}
$$
If the post-control row space is saturated, if the held-out boundary is not falsifiable, or if the available readout is only a modeled or proxy contact, the entry remains a coupling record or a data request. An in-sample fitted value equal to $1.0$ is therefore treated as a warning sign when it comes from too few effective rows or a saturated projection.

\paragraph{What is already computed.}
The surrounding finite-row memory supplies several useful numerical anchors. A three-organism codon/tRNA proxy run loaded Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12, used a $61$-codon count space, and computed $9$ synonymous residual coordinates: $\operatorname{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\operatorname{Ile}_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, $\operatorname{Leu}_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $\operatorname{Leu}_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $\operatorname{Ser}_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $\operatorname{Ser}_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $\operatorname{Thr}_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. Its proxy contact was a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum. The break condition was not met: all $9$ entries were not below $0.01$, and the largest absolute proxy coupling was $1882464.1106377328$ with $9$ nonzero entries above the declared $0.01$ threshold. This is a real finite contact with codon and tRNA-copy rows, but it is not a translation-survival entry because true tAI/stAI, ribosome profiling, or protein-abundance readouts are not present in that matrix.

\paragraph{Residual-basis context.}
A separate residual-basis extraction run shows why the gate is needed. With the regularization value $\lambda=0.01$ and description-length penalty $\log_2(1+n_{col})$, the computed organisms remained persistent rather than compressible. Homo sapiens used $16008$ joined rows, selected $6$ candidates, and ended with a residual curve from $1.0$ down to $0.9723341658092314$. Saccharomyces cerevisiae used $3046$ joined rows, selected $9$ candidates, and ended at $0.9798667977250413$. Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 used $3415$ joined rows, selected $2$ candidates, and ended at $0.9979478314995476$. Danio rerio used $14410$ joined rows, selected $1$ candidate, and ended at $0.9791828971490305$. Gallus gallus and Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155 remain needs-data cases. Across the four computed organisms, the minimum final residual ratio was $0.9723341658092314$, so the negative result is informative: the existing readout blocks do not absorb the residual coordinate space enough to justify a direct biological promotion.

\paragraph{Nearby contacts.}
Other finite panels show that the broader cross-layer program has working row joins but not the specific rank-admissible translation entry required here. The uORF/Kozak ribosome-load sample reports $41862$ fixed rows, $16739$ varying rows, two-sample concordance, and a runtime of $131.941$ seconds. The codon-composition mRNA half-life run reports $16500$ human rows, $18924$ mouse rows, a raw correlation $\rho=0.126224$, a non-CSC tAI circularity guard, and a runtime of $46.349$ seconds. These panels confirm that finite biological surfaces can be attached and audited, but they remain distinct from the present $B^{*}_{Q6}$ branchpoint-rank question unless the same organism or same CDS rows are joined to a measured translation readout and the declared controls are applied.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The Mercer branchpoint panel is ready to support a rank-admissibility chapter because its own finite row count, expression-control status, source, and runtime are explicit. The translation-survival conclusion is not ready. The chapter's positive content is the refusal rule: a proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry cannot be reported from a rank-saturated or underpowered residual design, and modeled tAI/stAI weights, tRNA-copy spectra, proxy couplings $A^{QT}_{ij}$, or all-$1.0$ fits do not count as measured translation survival. No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this record unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant rows and statistic.
