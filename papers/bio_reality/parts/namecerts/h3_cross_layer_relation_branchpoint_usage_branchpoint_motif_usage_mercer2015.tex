The record considered here is the finite branchpoint-motif usage panel indexed by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.branchpoint\_usage.branchpoint\_motif\_usage\_mercer2015}. Its confirmed table has $n=10584$ joined rows. \paragraph{Coordinate and readout carrier.}
The cross-layer question is whether a residual coordinate $q_i$ from the synonymous $B^*_{Q6}$ family may be promoted to a translation-readout survival entry against a readout $T_j$. The relevant finite carrier is a residualized design matrix whose columns contain $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, organism- or row-indexed translation readout surfaces, and a control block $Z$. In the present record, $Z$ must at least preserve the branchpoint expression control already observed in the Mercer panel, and, for any translation claim, must also contain the declared codon-side controls: amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding length, and M-density baseline. The intended entry is not a fitted association in isolation. It is an admissibility certificate for $S^{QT}_{ij}$, recording residual rank, remaining degrees of freedom after $Z$, a held-out translation-readout boundary, and the failure condition under which an apparent in-sample fit is rejected as rank saturation.

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
For a proposed pair $(q_i,T_j)$, let $\widetilde q_i$ and $\widetilde T_j$ denote the residuals after projecting away the chosen control block $Z$. The entry is testable only when the residual design has enough independent rows left to make held-out survival falsifiable. A convenient local form of the rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{ij} \text{ may be reported} \quad &\Rightarrow\quad \operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X_{ij}) < n_{ij} \text{ and the held-out score is non-vacuous},\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X_{ij}) \text{ saturated or underpowered} \quad &\Rightarrow\quad S^{QT}_{ij}=\text{needs\_data rather than survival}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a boundary rule: a positive in-sample coefficient, a proxy coupling, or an all-$1.0$ fitted surface is not enough. The row surface must retain degrees of freedom after the controls, and the statistic must survive a held-out or otherwise falsifiable readout test.

\paragraph{Readback for this chapter.}
The chapter therefore records the Mercer branchpoint panel as a controlled finite row surface that can participate in a rank-admissibility test, not as a completed translation-survival result. A valid positive entry would need same-row or organism-matched codon counts, the chosen $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, a direct or explicitly bounded translation readout such as ribosome profiling, TE, tAI, or stAI, the Mercer-style expression control where relevant, and the full $Z$ block. If the post-control row space is saturated, if the degrees of freedom are insufficient, or if the only support is a proxy coupling matrix, the output remains \texttt{needs\_data} or coupling-only.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this branchpoint record by itself. The $n=10584$ expression-controlled Mercer surface supplies a finite controlled branchpoint panel. It does not supply a measured $Q_i\to T_j$ survival matrix, a $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ mediation chain, a structural assay, a functional assay, or a perturbation mechanism. Any such claim must be supplied by a separate layer-matched contact and by a non-saturated statistic computed on the joined rows.
