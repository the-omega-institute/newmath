\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The Mercer branchpoint record is used here as a controlled cross-layer finite-row surface, not as a protein-level or phenotype-level result.  The checked row table contains $n=10584$ intron-overlap rows after joining branchpoint-motif usage to expression annotation on the same strand. Thus the row surface is large enough to support a controlled branchpoint-usage readback, but its biological layer is still the splicing and expression-control boundary named by the data join.

\paragraph{Rank gate carried by the record.}
The rank-admissibility question is whether a proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry should be allowed to count as translation survival after residualizing both the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate $q_i$ and the translation readout $T_j$ against the control matrix $Z$.  The local test is finite and row-based:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^\perp &= (I-P_Z)q_i,\\
T_j^\perp &= (I-P_Z)T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij} &> 0 \quad \text{is reportable only when the residual design has non-saturated rank and held-out degrees of freedom.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The relevant controls are amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, and M-density, with expression entering the Mercer branchpoint surface as an explicit covariate rather than as a translation measurement.  A fitted score of $1.0$, or any in-sample positive coefficient on a saturated design, is therefore not a survival certificate.  It is at most a coupling or feasibility signal until the residual row space admits a falsifiable held-out readout.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The Mercer branchpoint finite-row record is ready as a rank-admissibility and no-promotion chapter.  It supports the following local statement: a proposed $B^*_{Q6}$ to translation-readout entry may be discussed only after residualizing against the declared controls and checking that the post-control row space is not rank-saturated.  The branchpoint table itself supplies $10584$ expression-controlled rows, but it does not supply a measured translation-readout survival matrix.  Consequently the correct classification is boundary-ready rather than translation-positive.  No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal rule follows from this record unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and the controlled statistic is recomputed.
