\subsection{Rank-Admissible Readout Boundary for Branchpoint-Indexed Cross-Layer Use}
\label{sec:bioreality-branchpoint-q6-rank-admissible-readout-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The branchpoint-contact record is a finite human transcript-row surface, not a universal biological assertion.  The verified branchpoint computation joins Mercer-style branchpoint or motif usage information to a UCSC hg19 \texttt{gtexGeneV8} expression control, using $\log(1+\operatorname{median})$ over $54$ tissue expression scores and joining by intron overlap on the same strand.  The resulting controlled table has $n=10584$ rows, with expression control recorded as present, and the run time was $138.47155$ seconds.  In this chapter the branchpoint table is used as a guarded cross-layer comparison site: it may indicate that a sequence-level or intron-level readout can be placed beside translation-oriented residual coordinates, but it does not by itself supply a translation-survival statistic.

\paragraph{Carrier and controlled rank test.}
Let $Q=\{q_i\}$ denote the finite list of residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinates available for the translation-boundary check, let $T=\{T_j\}$ denote organism-indexed translation readout columns, and let $Z$ contain amino-acid composition, organism, $GC3$, coding length, $M$-density, and the declared simpler codon baselines.  The admissibility question is not whether an in-sample fit can be made large.  It is whether the residualized design leaves enough independent support to make an out-of-sample failure possible.  For each proposed entry the local rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^{\perp Z} &= q_i - \Pi_Z q_i,\\
T_j^{\perp Z} &= T_j - \Pi_Z T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij} &> 0 \quad \hbox{is reportable only when } \operatorname{rank}(q_i^{\perp Z},T_j^{\perp Z}) \hbox{ is not saturated and held-out loss can increase.}
\end{aligned}
$$
If the post-control row space is rank-saturated, or if the remaining degrees of freedom are too small to make a held-out contrast falsifiable, the entry is retained only as a coupling or data-request record.  In particular, an all-$1.0$ fitted surface on a tiny controlled panel is treated as rank degeneracy, not as translation survival.

\paragraph{Observed q6 coordinate support.}
The finite $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coupling computation used $61$ codon-count dimensions and loaded three organism rows: \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, and \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12}.  The available synonymous residual coordinates were \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}.  Against the proxy readout recorded for that computation, the break condition was not met: not all nine coordinates had $|A_{ij}|<0.01$.  The largest absolute proxy coupling was $1882464.1106377328$, and all $9$ tested entries were nonzero above the bookkeeping threshold.  This is evidence that the coordinate list has finite contact with a proxy translation-adjacent surface; it is not evidence that any $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry survived the controlled rank gate.

\paragraph{Residual-basis pressure.}
The separate residual-basis extraction supplies a useful negative control for the rank gate because it reports persistent unexplained residual structure rather than collapse to a compact readout basis.  Four organisms were computed and classified as persistent: \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  The final residual ratios remained high, with a recorded floor of $0.9723341658092314$.  The joined-row counts were $14410$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \texttt{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $16008$ for human, and $3046$ for yeast; \texttt{gallus\_gallus} and \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155} remained in the needs-data state.  The human extraction had $6$ candidate blocks with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321191321$ and $d_{\mathrm{difference}}=0.018824203448813415$; yeast had $9$ candidates with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2199040339633322$ and $d_{\mathrm{difference}}=0.008171838236265161$; \texttt{E. coli} had $2$ candidates with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.6628211177352172$ and $d_{\mathrm{difference}}=0.00017488612263594217$; zebrafish had one candidate with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.874628721049266$ and $d_{\mathrm{difference}}=0.0$.  The declared regularizer in that extraction was $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length term $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.

The residual-basis curves make the finite-row warning explicit.  Human followed

\begin{aligned}

&0.9787609432955213,\ 0.9787233550315274,\ 0.9723341658092314,
\end{aligned}
$$
while yeast followed
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,&\ 0.9962498989663637,\ 0.9929843170647626,\ 0.9915730985485388,\ 0.9903314906684438,\\
&0.9894054695002641,\ 0.9885902293718664,\ 0.9882386501212531,\ 0.9881524582851271,\ 0.9798667977250413.
\end{aligned}
$$
The \texttt{E. coli} curve was $1.0,0.9982293045445091,0.9979478314995476$, and the zebrafish curve was $1.0,0.9791828971490305$.  These high terminal ratios are not failures of the method; they are the reason the chapter keeps the translation-survival claim behind a rank and held-out-readout gate.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
Two other finite-row computations help locate the boundary without crossing it.  The uORF/Kozak ribosome-load sample reported $41862$ fixed rows and $16739$ varying rows, with two-sample concordance recorded as true and run time $131.941$ seconds.  The codon-composition mRNA half-life computation used a non-CSC tAI circularity guard, with $n_{\mathrm{human}}=16500$, $n_{\mathrm{mouse}}=18924$, raw $\rho=0.126224$, and run time $46.349$ seconds.  These are translation-adjacent or RNA-stability contacts, but they do not replace the missing same-row, same-organism matrix joining $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates to a direct translation readout under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a guarded branchpoint-indexed cross-layer relation.  The finite branchpoint table has controlled expression rows; the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate table has proxy coupling and persistent residual structure; the nearby uORF and mRNA-stability computations show that relevant readout surfaces can be made finite.  None of these facts allows promotion to ribosome occupancy, translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Such claims require their own matched contact rows and a separate statistic at that layer.  The present result is the narrower statement that a proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry must first be rank-admissible after residualization against $Z$, and that any rank-saturated or underpowered entry remains coupling-only or needs data even when its in-sample score is positive.
