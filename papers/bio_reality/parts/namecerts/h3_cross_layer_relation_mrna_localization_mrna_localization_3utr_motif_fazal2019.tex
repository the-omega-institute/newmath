\paragraph{Coordinate and readout order.}
Let $Q_i$ denote a same-CDS synonymous residual coordinate, with the $f_3$-stress coordinate separated from the other $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  Let $T_j$ denote a same-row translation readout, such as a codon-indexed or local-window ribosome-occupancy statistic, or an explicitly bounded translation-efficiency residual once a matched mRNA control is present.  Let $L$ denote a protein-localization label, compartment score, fractionation enrichment, imaging score, or proximity-labeling readout after organism and protein identifier matching.  The admissible order is not $Q_i \to L$ first.  The admissible order is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &\longrightarrow T_j,\\
(Q_i,T_j) &\longrightarrow L,\\
Q_i &\longrightarrow L \mid T_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first arrow is mandatory.  If the finite table does not contain a nondegenerate $Q_i \to T_j$ survival test under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, codon-count, matched mRNA, coverage, mappability, rank, and simpler-baseline controls, then the later localization row is kept as a protein-level contact only.  In that null case, it does not lift the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate beyond the translation boundary.

\paragraph{Translation contact.}
The translation contact required here is position-resolved ribosome occupancy.  Its row unit is a same-CDS codon index or fixed local window keyed by organism, transcript or CDS identifier, codon position, and ribosome-profiling sample.  Such a row can represent per-CDS ribosome-footprint occupancy at codon or window resolution after an external ribosome-profiling resource is joined to ordered coding-sequence coordinates.  This contact is noisy in ways that matter mathematically: ribosome pausing, nuclease and fragment-length bias, frame-assignment ambiguity, mappability, start and stop edge effects, library normalization, condition specificity, and CDS annotation mismatch can all shift a local occupancy estimate.  For this reason, a contact name alone is not a statistic.  The table must contain the joined codon/window matrix and the declared $f_3$-stress window statistic before any translation survival statement is made.

\paragraph{mRNA control.}
The mRNA control is a same-condition transcript-abundance row, identifier-matched to the ribosome-profiling row.  It permits the separated translation-efficiency form
$$
\begin{aligned}
TE_i &= \frac{RPF_i}{mRNA_i},\\
T_j &= \operatorname{residual}(TE_i \mid Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ contains the finite controls used in the row design.  Transcript abundance alone is not translation realization.  It is a control surface that prevents a transcription-layer signal from being misread as a translation-layer signal.

\paragraph{Localization contact.}
The localization contact is a matched protein-level readout keyed to a UniProt, RefSeq protein, locus, or CDS-derived protein identifier.  It may be a curated compartment annotation, imaging measurement, fractionation profile, proximity-labeling enrichment, label, or score.  This contact has its own biases: cell type and condition specificity, antibody or tag artifacts, compartment coarseness, isoform ambiguity, fractionation contamination, proximity-labeling radius, and identifier-mapping loss.  Therefore a direct $Q_i \to L$ association is not promoted to a localization mechanism.  It is tested only after $Q_i \to T_j$ is established, and the direct term must be reported again after conditioning on $T_j$ and protein-abundance controls where such controls are used.
