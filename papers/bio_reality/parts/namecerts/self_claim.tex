\subsection{Self-Claim Boundary for ORF Eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-self-claim-orf-eligibility}

\paragraph{Claim packet.}
The record named \texttt{self.claim} is a boundary-level BioReality packet for open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration. Its biological layer is \texttt{orf\_eligibility}. The packet does not assert that a biological open reading frame has been observed. It says instead that a start/stop-window pattern is a finite candidate test only after an independent biological interval is supplied. In its minimal coordinate form, the carrier, distinction, and readback fields remain at reality-contact pending status, while the internal structure field is empty. Thus the packet is not a biological realization claim; it is a guard on what may and may not be inferred from codon-window geometry.

\paragraph{Boundary probe.}
The two probes attached to the packet enforce the same local rule. The first probe states that a start/stop-window eligibility seed must remain at boundary status unless a separate sequence, transcript, or curated-annotation contact supplies the observed biological interval. The second probe states that the packet cannot record an ORF eligibility claim while no sequence database, transcript evidence, or curated annotation can test the proposed interval. The required contact class is \texttt{curated.orf.interval.annotation}. Accordingly, a window pattern may guide a test, but it is not itself an observed ORF.

The self-check row records a boundary statement: no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers, and the checked gate count is $3$. \paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite rows support a precise boundary conclusion. Before contact, \texttt{self.claim} is only an admissibility guard for a possible ORF-eligibility test. After the curated interval contact is attached, the supported content is restricted to interval-level ORF eligibility for the finite curated dataset and its stated controls. The packet remains silent on translation, folding, physical chemistry, biological function, mechanism, and universality.
