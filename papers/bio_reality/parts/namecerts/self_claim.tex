\subsection{Self-Check Boundary for Open-Reading-Frame Eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-self-claim-orf-eligibility}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record concerns the biological layer called \emph{open-reading-frame eligibility}: the finite question of whether a nucleotide interval passes a start/stop codon enumeration test. The self-check row does not itself attach a sequence-database row, transcript observation, or curated interval annotation. Its own verified payload is therefore deliberately narrow: experiment run \texttt{0f47d2f0cf41} records the boundary statement that BioReality is performing only a self-check and that no codon geometry is carried upward across biological layers, with \texttt{checked\_gate\_count}=3. In this chapter the word eligibility means only the finite start/stop-window predicate; it is not a claim that a product is translated, folded, physically admissible, functional, mechanistically explained, or universal.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore separates three finite layers. First, $R$, $M$, saturation, and quotient geometry are codon-level combinatorial data. Second, a one-window enumeration row shows that the start/stop classifier can classify the supplied window while remaining at eligibility scope. Third, a curated interval contact with $168$ rows supplies a reality-facing dataset in which $72/72$ positives are accepted, $72/72$ matched decoys are rejected, three $24$-positive/$24$-decoy held-out folds pass, and $24/24$ boundary cases remain unpromoted. None of these facts entails translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality. Such claims would require their own observations and their own finite tests. The self-check row is valid precisely because it refuses to let start/stop enumeration or codon geometry substitute for those later biological contacts.
