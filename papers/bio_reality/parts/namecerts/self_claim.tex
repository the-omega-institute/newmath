\subsection{ORF eligibility as a contact-gated boundary record}
\label{subsec:bioreality-self-claim-orf-eligibility}

\paragraph{Finite claim under audit.}
The record named \texttt{self.claim} is a self-check for the layer \texttt{orf\_eligibility}. Its biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility obtained from enumeration of start and stop codons, but the record deliberately keeps that object behind a contact gate. The checked self-audit reports \texttt{checked\_gate\_count}=3 and records the boundary statement that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. Thus the datum is not an observed translation event and not an assertion that a sequence produces a protein. It is a finite boundary record saying that start/stop enumeration can describe what must be checked, while the biological interval itself must be supplied by a separate curated sequence, transcript, or annotation contact.

\paragraph{Window enumeration contact.}

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow. The finite rows support the statement that, under a frozen start/stop-window classifier and a curated interval contact, the recorded intervals satisfy the declared ORF-eligibility test while matched decoys and boundary cases do not receive promotion. The codon sets $R$ and $M$, the saturation result, the quotient geometry, the single-window manifest check, and the $168$-row curated interval contact are all compatible with that limited layer. They do not turn codon geometry into translation evidence. They also do not establish folded structure, energetic or physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, taxonomic generality, or a global law of coding biology. Any such stronger claim would require a distinct contact whose finite rows are designed to test that stronger biological layer.
