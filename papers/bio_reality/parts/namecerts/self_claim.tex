\subsection{Self-check boundary for ORF eligibility}\label{sec:self-claim-orf-eligibility-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim inspected.}
This record concerns the layer named $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$, understood only as eligibility obtained from enumerating start and stop codon windows.  The packet does not assert an observed open reading frame from the geometry alone.  Its own self-check reports a boundary-only status: no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers, and the finite gate count inspected for that self-check is $3$.  Thus the relevant statement is not that a nucleotide interval is translated, folded, physically admissible, functional, mechanistic, or universal.  The statement is narrower: a start/stop-window calculation may define a testable boundary condition, but the biological interval itself must be supplied by a separate contact with curated sequence annotation, transcript evidence, or another appropriate biological source.

It is only a finite indexing structure available to a later experiment.

\paragraph{Boundary-window enumeration.}
A separate finite-row result reports that one external nucleotide window was loaded and that all input windows were classified by start/stop enumeration: the classified count is $1$ and the input-window count is $1$.  Its scope is explicitly limited to external-window start/stop enumeration, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$.  The associated classification label is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  This is the correct local interpretation of the seed: start and stop boundaries can be checked for a supplied window, but this still does not establish that the interval is translated, that any protein product exists, that a folded structure is realized, that the product is physically admissible in vivo, or that it has a biological function.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact as a separate event.}
The later curated-contact row is materially different from the boundary seed.  It loads $168$ rows from a curated ORF interval contact dataset with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$, sourced from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$.  The required input classes are present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs.  The protocol was frozen before the external observation: the classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines are recorded as frozen, with protocol time $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$ and external evidence observed at $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The classifier identifier is $\mathrm{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}$, and its rules were not modified.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The self-claim is therefore admissible only as a bounded inspection of what the finite ORF-eligibility classifier may and may not say.  Before contact, the codon packets $R$, $M$, the saturation row, and the quotient rows supply a finite start/stop-window geometry but no biological realization.  After the curated interval contact, the paper may cite the $168$-row contact and its $72$ positive, $72$ decoy, and $24$ boundary-control counts as evidence for the scoped ORF-eligibility layer on that dataset.  Even then, the result is not a claim of translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Those stronger layers require their own contacts and tests.
