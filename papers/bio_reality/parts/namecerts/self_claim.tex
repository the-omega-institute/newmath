\paragraph{Record.}
This BioReality record concerns the layer named $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ for open-reading-frame eligibility under finite start--stop codon enumeration. The biological object is not an observed translated product and not a protein-level assertion. It is the narrower question of whether a finite nucleotide interval may be admitted as an ORF-eligibility candidate once the interval is supplied by a separate biological observation. In the row audited here, the direct contact list is empty, and the self-check therefore keeps the claim at boundary status. The recorded self-check has experiment run id $\mathrm{0f47d2f0cf41}$, reports $3$ checked gates, and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. Thus the row is not a biological discovery claim by itself; it is a finite audit of what the row is, and is not, permitted to assert.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The present self-claim therefore has a precise role. It records that the row is internally guarded against layer promotion: finite codon lists, saturation data, quotient geometry, a one-window enumeration check, and even the linked curated-interval statistics do not by themselves establish anything above $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$. A higher-layer assertion would require its own contact: translation would require translation-level evidence; folding would require structure-level evidence; physical admissibility would require physical or biochemical admissibility evidence; function would require functional evidence; mechanism would require mechanistic evidence; and universality would require evidence whose sampling frame and inference rule actually support such scope. Since this row's own contact list is empty, the local conclusion is a controlled boundary statement rather than an observed biological realization.
