\paragraph{Finite status of the record.}
The claim indexed as \texttt{self.claim} is a self-audit record for the biological layer \texttt{orf\_eligibility}.  Its verified boundary statement is deliberately narrow: the BioReality self-check reports that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers, and it records a checked gate count of $3$ under experiment run \texttt{0f47d2f0cf41}.  The corresponding conjectural packet is therefore not an assertion that an open reading frame exists in a genome, transcript, or translated product.  It is a boundary certificate for the question of what kind of contact would be needed before start--stop enumeration could be interpreted as eligibility for an open reading frame.

\paragraph{Start--stop enumeration before interval contact.}
The start--stop window experiment reports one external window loaded from a curated finite nucleotide-window manifest dated $2026$-$05$-$25$.  The run classified $1$ input window by start--stop enumeration, and the scope was explicitly limited to \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}.  Its own scalar record states \texttt{no\_geometry\_to\_translation\_promotion} with value \texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only}.  This is the scientific meaning of the seed boundary: enumeration can describe a window as having the relevant start and stop pattern, but the biological interval must come from an external sequence, transcript, or curated annotation before the packet may count the case as an eligibility contact.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact as a separate layer.}
A later curated-interval run supplies a distinct contact data set rather than deriving biology from codon geometry.  The data path \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json} loaded $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The classifier was \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified, and the protocol records that classifier rules, baselines, input classes, and held-out splits were fixed before the external evidence time $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The required input classes were \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}.  The positive interval test accepted $72$ of $72$ positives, while the matched decoy test rejected $72$ of $72$ decoys.  Both exact one-sided binomial tails are recorded as $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$, and the predeclared paired baseline sign-test advantage also reports $72$ wins out of $72$ pairs with the same tail probability.  Across three held-out splits, each split contains $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, with positive accept rate $1.0$, decoy reject rate $1.0$, and pass status true.  The boundary-control cap records $24$ boundary cases and $0$ promotions.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore separates two statements.  First, there is a finite codon and quotient record: $|R|=13$, $|M|=20$, $|\pi_M|=10$, and the self-check confirms that three gates were examined without promoting geometry across biological layers.  Second, there is an external curated interval contact with $168$ rows, exact positive and decoy behavior on $72+72$ matched cases, and three stable held-out splits.  Only the second statement can support the scoped layer \texttt{orf\_eligibility}, and even that scoped contact does not imply translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, causal mechanism, genome-wide completeness, or universality.  Any stronger assertion would require its own contact record and its own finite audit.
