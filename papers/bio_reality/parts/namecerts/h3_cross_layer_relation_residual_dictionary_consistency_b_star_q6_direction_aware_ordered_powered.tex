\subsection{Direction-aware residual dictionary for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance remainder}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-direction-aware-residual-dictionary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record treats the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation abundance remainder as a finite per-organism ledger rather than as a biological pathway.  For each organism with computable rows, the target vector is the $Q$-coordinate abundance component $P_Q$, and the reported decomposition first removes the measured translation readout $T$, then the available transcript-stability or transcript-level readout $S$, and only then tests a direction-aware $H$ dictionary.  The $H$ rows are scored from three separate quantities: absorbed residual energy $A_H$, coordinate-support agreement $C_H$, and a penalty for readout size and row loss.  The row score used here has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{score}(H) &= A_H C_H - 0.01\,DL(H) - \mathrm{null\ and\ join\ penalties},
\end{aligned}
$$
where the coefficient $0.01$ is a declared regularizer, not an estimated biological constant.  The cosine $C_H$ compares the support direction induced by the tested readout with the residual coordinate support; it is a geometric agreement statistic, not a molecular interaction.

\paragraph{Ordered residual ledger.}
The four emitted organism rows sum to one within floating-point precision.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $T=0.1127139450$, $S=0.0196274205$, and the unexplained remainder is $U=0.8676586345$ over $n=4759$ proteins with $\|P_Q\|^2=229.8645079401$.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $T=0.0003596477$, $S=0.0053579738$, and $U=0.9942823785$ over $n=2517$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=225.8455631995$.  In human, $T=0.0153650365$, $S=0.0047683426$, and $U=0.9798666209$ over $n=11354$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=334.2841143429$.  In zebrafish, $T=0.0243680958$, the $S$ row is skipped, and $U=0.9756319042$ over $n=13140$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168670$.  Thus the emitted dictionary is dominated by unexplained remainder: no positive $H_1$ or $H_2$ entry is kept in the final table for any of the four organisms.

\paragraph{Direction filter.}
The direction term materially changes the interpretation of high-energy rows.  Zebrafish localization has the largest direction-aware product among its tested $H$ rows, with $A_H=0.0176300654$, $C_H=0.7730452319$, and $A_HC_H=0.0136288380$, but its $DL=12$ and join shift $0.8521308980$ drive the final score to $-0.1598407912$; it is not kept.  Zebrafish PTM density is also substantial in raw absorption, $A_H=0.0145068517$, but $A_HC_H=0.0080204239$ and the score is $-0.0452022411$.  The complex-membership row is directionally opposed, with $C_H=-0.1033849441$ and $A_HC_H=-0.0000025883$.

Human gives the same lesson in a different numerical shape.  PTM density has $A_H=0.0143546489$, $C_H=0.4249168129$, and $A_HC_H=0.0060995317$, yet its score is still negative at $-0.0046843221$.  Human complex membership has a better direction cosine, $C_H=0.6463811418$, but much lower absorption, $A_H=0.0015225942$, so $A_HC_H=0.0009841762$ and the score is $-0.0098114213$.  Human turnover is both tiny and directionally reversed, with $A_H=0.0000008938$, $C_H=-0.3821588332$, and score $-0.0311420019$.  Yeast localization again has visible raw alignment, $A_H=0.0211730665$, $C_H=0.7169052533$, and $A_HC_H=0.0151790826$, but the $DL=12$ readout and join shift $0.1592771591$ yield score $-0.1180646698$.  Yeast PTM density is directionally compatible, $C_H=0.7323062921$, but the score remains $-0.0063465630$.  Yeast complex membership and domain count point the wrong way, with $C_H=-0.0756413652$ and $C_H=-0.6437764614$, respectively.  For \emph{E. coli}, the emitted $H$ dictionary contains no usable feature, localization, PTM, transmembrane, complex, or turnover rows, and the mRNA-abundance placeholder row has $A_H=0$, $C_H=0$, and score $-0.01$.

\paragraph{Relation to the wider finite panel.}
The companion direction-aware ranking table gives weak organism-specific best absorbers before the stricter ordered dictionary rejects them.  In that table, zebrafish measured TE has $A_H=0.0175942900$, $C_H=0.6573753096$, $A_HC_H=0.0115660518$, and score $0.0015660518$; zebrafish mRNA has $A_HC_H=0.0107345886$ and score $0.0007345886$.  Human complex membership and mRNA are both aligned but weak, with $A_HC_H=0.0065660301$ and $0.0062247772$.  Human PTM density has the larger absorption $A_H=0.0148848259$ but only $C_H=0.3604440742$, which demotes its aligned product to $0.0053651473$.  Yeast mRNA is the top raw ranked row there, with $A_HC_H=0.0011462622$ and score $-0.0088537378$.  These values explain why the ordered dictionary is not a failure of computation: it is a negative readback after adding direction, null, description-length, and join-loss discipline.

\paragraph{Auxiliary checks.}
Several independent finite checks agree that this should be read as a residual-accounting result, not as a pathway discovery.  The measured-TE mediation panel reports measured mediated fractions $0.4793069656$ for yeast, $0.0409115834$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485$ for human, and $0$ for zebrafish.  The minimal compilation test reports low cross-organism compression by the measured readout set: compressibility $0.1323413655$ for yeast, $0.0057176215$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0263557674$ for human, and $0.0243680958$ for zebrafish, with the stated cross-organism summary that the median is about $0.026$.  The $f3$ stress readout reach test in yeast finds best-subset coverage $0.2261844517$ against a selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.0269714130$, but still leaves $0.7738155483$ unexplained.  The boundary-set sign test for the Window6 $R$ set is strong in a different direction: $15$ of $15$ computed organisms are in the avoided direction, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  That $R$ result supplies a coordinate-level biological signature, but it does not identify an $H$ readout that explains the residual dictionary.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The valid conclusion is narrow.  The finite data support a direction-aware, ordered variance ledger for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual, and the ledger says that the tested readouts do not furnish a stable, retained $H$ dictionary under the declared score.  The best apparent absorbers are organism-specific and often lose support once direction, null scale, readout dimension, and row availability are considered.  Missing feature rows are not zeros, and negative scores are not evidence that no future readout could work.  No statement about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, pathway identity, organism-universal $H$, or a global law follows from these ranks or scores.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate matched measurement and a separate statistic for the relevant biological layer.
