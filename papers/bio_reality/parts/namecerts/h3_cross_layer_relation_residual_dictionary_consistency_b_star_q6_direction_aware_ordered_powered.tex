\subsection{Direction-aware residual dictionary for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-direction-aware-residual-dictionary}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The object studied here is a finite per-organism ledger attached to the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  For each organism with an available matched panel, the abundance component $P_Q$ is first partitioned in the fixed order measured translation efficiency, mRNA-stability where available, and then a direction-aware $H$ dictionary.  An $H$ row records its absorbed energy $A_H$, its coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, the signed alignment $A_H C_H$, a description length $DL$, a join-shift penalty, a permutation-null scale, and a final score.  The scoring rule is descriptive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda_0 DL(H) - \lambda_1 J(H) - \operatorname{null}_{95}(H),\\
\lambda_0 &= 0.01.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the companion ranking table without the extra null and join-shift subtractions, the same stated regularizer appears as $\operatorname{Score}=A_HC_H-0.01\log_2(1+n_{columns})$; single-column readouts therefore carry $DL=1$, while the twelve-column localization readout carries $DL=\log_2(13)=3.700439718141092$.  The table is a numerical readback of a finite projection computation, not a biological explanation.

\paragraph{Residual support before the dictionary.}
The $T/S/H$ cut was computed for four organisms.  The emitted partition sums are exactly normalized: the reported sum diagnostic is $1.0$ for danio, E. coli, human, and yeast.  Yeast has $T=0.11271394500088064$, $S=0.019627420469677935$, and residual $U=0.8676586345294415$.  E. coli has $T=0.00035964766234144823$, $S=0.005357973825835613$, and $U=0.9942823785118229$.  Human has $T=0.015365036507318318$, $S=0.004768342590437625$, and $U=0.9798666209022441$.  Danio has $T=0.02436809579891462$, no $S$ entry in this cut, and $U=0.9756319042010854$.  The corresponding $P_Q$ energies and row counts are $605.0092168669606$ on $13140$ danio rows, $225.84556319953032$ on $2517$ E. coli rows, $334.28411434290217$ on $11354$ human rows, and $229.86450794006544$ on $4759$ yeast rows.  Thus the table is dominated by residual mass: none of the four organisms receives a positive kept $H$ readout under the direction-aware scoring rule.

In human, PTM density has $A_H=0.014354648896209531$, $C_H=0.4249168128961486$, $A_HC_H=0.006099531659220571$, and score $-0.004684322133066079$, while complex membership has $A_H=0.0015225942297501104$, $C_H=0.6463811418186044$, and score $-0.009811421276914757$. In yeast, localization has the largest alignment, $A_H=0.021173066463797872$, $C_H=0.7169052532945601$, and $A_HC_H=0.01517908257625157$, but the twelve-dimensional and join-sensitive penalties give score $-0.11806466977343578$. E. coli has no usable feature, localization, or turnover rows in this ordered dictionary; its only explicit mRNA-abundance row is the zero row $A_H=C_H=A_HC_H=0$ with score $-0.01$.

\paragraph{Relation to the wider $H$ ranking.}
The less penalized direction-aware ranking table explains why a naive reading would overstate the result.  In that table, danio has positive single-column scores for measured TE and mRNA: measured TE has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and score $0.001566051846174291$; mRNA has $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and score $0.000734588577977589$.  Human's best raw organism-specific readouts are complex membership and mRNA, with $C_H=0.8723071163246565$ and $0.7921354915786852$, but their regularized scores are negative, $-0.0034339698846599055$ and $-0.0037752227820994546$.  Human measured TE is a useful warning case: it exceeds its Bonferroni threshold in absorbed energy, yet its direction is wrong, $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, giving $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$.  Yeast mRNA is the best single-column ranking entry there, with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, and score $-0.008853737772559931$.  Gallus has a feature-only partial table in which complex membership is best by direction-aware rank, $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$, $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, and score $-0.00575848521855683$.  These organism-specific leaders do not define a shared universal absorber.

\paragraph{Cross-checks from neighboring finite rows.}
The residual dictionary is consistent with the upstream measurements.  The modeled-translation complement residual is large in several organisms: $d=0.874628721049266$ for danio, $0.6629960038578532$ for E. coli, $0.9400341806302775$ for gallus, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, and $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast.  The measured-TE mediation fractions are heterogeneous: danio is clipped to $0.0$ from raw $M^{QTP}=-0.04801486048721136$, E. coli is $0.04091158340950673$, human is $0.0027966485036786744$, and yeast is $0.4793069656345167$.  The minimal compilation test is likewise negative as a general law: compressibility is $0.024368095798914635$ in danio, $0.005717621488177116$ in E. coli, $0.02635576736932188$ in human, and $0.13234136547055853$ in yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.  The $f3$ stress coordinate remains partially reachable by a larger independent readout set in yeast: best-subset coverage is $0.22618445168998694$ against a selection-aware null95 of $0.02697141300639046$, but $0.7738155483100131$ of that target remains unexplained after the full readout set.  Across organisms, the Window6 boundary $R$ set has an avoided abundance-preference signature: all $15$ computed organisms are avoided, with one-sided sign-test $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$; the $R$ sense-codon count is $10$ out of $61$.  Separately, optimal-codon directions are only clustered rather than universal, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ across $15$ computed organisms.

\paragraph{Classification and boundary.}
The finite row is best classified as a cross-layer descriptive relation with an organism-specific, mostly unexplained residual.  The organism-specificity survey supports that caution: the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has $n=12$, chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, but permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$; $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log tRNA gene count it has $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$; versus log proteome size it has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$.  The finite table may therefore report coordinate support, absorbed energy, direction cosine, null comparisons, penalties, row counts, and ranks.  It may not assert translation realization, folding state, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanistic pathway, phenotype, or universality.  A readout name such as localization, PTM density, turnover, mRNA, or complex membership denotes only the attached measured or curated column used in the projection.  Any stronger biological interpretation would require a separate layer-matched contact and a separate statistic; it is not supplied by $A_H$, $C_H$, score, rank, $H_o^{\ast}$, $H_{shared}^{\ast}$, or by the nine-coordinate geometry itself.
