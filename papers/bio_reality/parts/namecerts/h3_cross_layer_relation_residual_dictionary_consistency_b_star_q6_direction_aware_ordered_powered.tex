\subsection{Direction-aware residual dictionary for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-direction-aware-residual-dictionary}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record fixes a finite per-organism ledger for the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  The target vector is the residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component $P_Q=\Pi_{Q_e}P_e$, cut first by measured translation efficiency $T$, then by mRNA-stability where available, and finally by a direction-aware dictionary of candidate readouts $H$.  The readback stored for each organism is the tuple consisting of $\|P_Q\|^2$, row count, the ordered $T$ and $S$ fractions, the remaining unexplained fraction $U$, and the scored $H$ rows with absorbed energy $A_H$, direction cosine $C_H$, product $A_H C_H$, description length $DL$, join-shift penalty, null quantile, and final score.  The scoring convention is descriptive: the broader ranking table states the regularizer scale as $\lambda=0.01$, while this ordered dictionary also subtracts the finite null and join-shift terms.  No row is allowed to become a causal, mechanistic, or universal statement merely by ranking highly.

The four computed organisms show that the ordered $T,S,H$ dictionary leaves most of $P_Q$ unexplained.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $\|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544$ on $n=4759$ rows, with $T=0.11271394500088064$, $S=0.019627420469677935$, and $U=0.8676586345294415$.  Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 has $\|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032$ on $n=2517$ rows, with $T=0.00035964766234144823$, $S=0.005357973825835613$, and $U=0.9942823785118229$.  Homo sapiens has $\|P_Q\|^2=334.28411434290217$ on $n=11354$ rows, with $T=0.015365036507318318$, $S=0.004768342590437625$, and $U=0.9798666209022441$.  Danio rerio has $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606$ on $n=13140$ rows, with $T=0.02436809579891462$, no computed $S$ fraction in this row, and $U=0.9756319042010854$.  In all four cases the finite diagnostic sums are exactly $1.0$ to the reported precision.

The table therefore supports a negative descriptive conclusion: the measured low-dimensional dictionary does not compress the non-translation residual into a stable shared readout channel.

In Homo sapiens, PTM density is the largest absorber in this ordered row, with $A_H=0.014354648896209531$, $C_H=0.4249168128961486$, $A_HC_H=0.006099531659220571$, and score $-0.004684322133066079$; complex membership has $A_H=0.0015225942297501104$, $C_H=0.6463811418186044$, and score $-0.009811421276914757$; turnover is wrong-direction with $C_H=-0.38215883324320976$ and score $-0.031142001898485053$. \paragraph{Cross-check against the broader ranking table.}
The related finite ranking table confirms why the ordered dictionary should not be read as a shared $H$ discovery.  In the broader ranking, Danio rerio has a best raw direction-aware absorber at measured TE with $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$ and score $0.001566051846174291$, followed by mRNA with $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$ and score $0.000734588577977589$.  Homo sapiens has high direction cosines for complex membership and mRNA, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$ and $C_H=0.7921354915786852$, but their scores are still negative, $-0.0034339698846599055$ and $-0.0037752227820994546$.  Yeast is led by mRNA with $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$ and score $-0.008853737772559931$.  Gallus gallus is led by complex membership with $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$ and score $-0.00575848521855683$.  E. coli has no Bonferroni-exceeding ranked absorber; its mRNA row has $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$ and score $-0.008470202379021127$.  The best absorber is therefore organism-specific, and the cross-organism shared absorber is absent.

\paragraph{Boundary-set and mediation context.}
The finite codon-window boundary that supplies part of the coordinate background is strong but not sufficient for mechanism.  The $R$ boundary set is avoided in all $15$ computed organisms, with $15$ avoided, $0$ preferred, $0$ zero, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  This is a cross-organism abundance-preference signature for the fixed Window6 boundary set, not a route from the $H$ dictionary to translation, folding, function, or mechanism.  The measured-TE mediation rows are also organism-specific: the measured mediated fractions are $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0$ in Danio rerio after clipping the raw value $-0.04801486048721136$.  Separately, the optimal-codon direction comparison over $15$ organisms reports mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ and the classification ``clustered'', which is compatible with heterogeneous directional structure rather than a single universal channel.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  The direction-aware ordered dictionary is a finite statistical readback showing that, after measured translation and mRNA-stability cuts, the remaining $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual is mostly unexplained by the attached candidate readouts.  High raw absorption can be demoted by direction, description length, null behavior, or row-join instability; missing readouts stay absent; and no rank, score, or selected organism-specific absorber is promoted to a biological pathway.  Nothing in this table establishes translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, regulation, localization mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger assertion would require an additional layer-matched contact and a separate statistic for that specific claim.
