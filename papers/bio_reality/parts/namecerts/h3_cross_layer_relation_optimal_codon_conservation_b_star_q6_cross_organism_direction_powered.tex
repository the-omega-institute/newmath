\subsection{Direction-weighted conservation of the non-translation codon residual}
\label{sec:bq6-direction-weighted-conservation}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
This record studies the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual as a finite per-organism ledger rather than as a biological law.  For each organism with enough joined coding sequences, the row contains the absorbed energy $A_H$ of a candidate readout $H$, the residual coordinate support $a_{\perp}$ over the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates, the $H$-projected coordinate support $a_H$, the direction cosine
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_H=\frac{\langle a_H,a_{\perp}\rangle}{\lVert a_H\rVert\lVert a_{\perp}\rVert},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Score}(H)=A_H C_H-\lambda\operatorname{DL}(H),\qquad \lambda=0.01,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $\operatorname{DL}(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}})$.  The computation used five folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, assembled $41$ synonymous-design columns, and completed on $15$ of $16$ requested organisms.  The cross-organism direction test returned mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ and classified the conserved pattern as clustered, not universal.

\paragraph{Conservation readback.}
The conservation call is intentionally weak.  A clustered mean cosine near $0.263$ says that the finite codon-direction summaries have non-random recurrence across the computed organisms, but it does not say that one biological channel is shared by all organisms.  The separate Window6 boundary signature makes the same point from the codon-side direction: the fixed $R$ set contains $10$ sense codons, and across the $15$ computed organisms the directional sign test found $15$ avoided, $0$ preferred, and $0$ zero directions.  The one-sided avoided-sign probability was $3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and the two-sided probability was $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  Among individually significant organisms, $6$ of $6$ were avoided.  Thus the row has a real finite directional signal, but the signal is a codon-abundance boundary statistic, not a translation, folding, function, or mechanism statement.

\paragraph{Residual scale.}
The translation-complement residual is large in most metazoan and bacterial rows and small in yeast.  On the same nine-coordinate support, the modeled-complement fractions were $d=0.874628721049266$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $d=0.6629960038578532$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $d=0.9400341806302775$ for \textit{Gallus gallus}, $d=0.9208949355679455$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, $d=0.8972672018746489$ for \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, and $d=0.21173219572706703$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The residual coordinate supports remain nine-dimensional in every computed row, with dominant coordinates changing by organism: for example, danio is led by $f3\_stress$, human by $Arg\_AGR$, yeast by $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and \textit{E. coli} by $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$.  These organism-specific leading coordinates are the main reason the conservation label is clustered rather than shared-channel.

\paragraph{Direction-aware H ranking.}
The H-panel confirms that no single fetchable readout explains the residual in a cross-organism way.  In danio, measured TE has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, raw alignment $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and positive penalized score $0.001566051846174291$; mRNA is close behind with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, raw alignment $0.01073458857797759$, and score $0.000734588577977589$.  In human, complex membership is the top one-column absorber by direction-weighted rank, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, and raw alignment $0.006566030115340095$, but its penalized score is $-0.0034339698846599055$.  Human PTM density has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but the direction filter lowers it because $C_H=0.3604440741749213$, giving raw alignment $0.0053651472982577525$.  Human measured TE is a wrong-direction example: $A_H=0.002894714925796667$ but $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, so $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$ and the score is $-0.01031302107580362$.  Yeast has mRNA first among one-column readouts, with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, and raw alignment $0.0011462622274400686$, but score $-0.008853737772559931$.  In \textit{E. coli}, mRNA reaches only raw alignment $0.0015297976209788734$ and score $-0.008470202379021127$.  Gallus has a complex-member row with $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$ and $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, but score $-0.00575848521855683$.  The best absorber therefore changes by organism and remains weak in absolute scale.

\paragraph{Null and penalty behavior.}
The Bonferroni and description-length tests separate raw absorption from justified compression.  Danio measured TE, mRNA, PTM density, and measured tRNA-copy tAI exceed their degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds, but only measured TE and mRNA keep positive penalized scores under $\lambda=0.01$.  Human complex membership, localization, measured TE, mRNA, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, yet all reported penalized scores are negative once direction and description length are applied.  Yeast localization exceeds its Bonferroni threshold and has $A_H=0.011225105608516642$, but its $12$-column description length is $3.700439718141092$, producing score $-0.03223607310225219$.  This is why high-dimensional or wrong-direction readouts are not promoted: energy alone is not enough.

\paragraph{Related finite checks.}
Measured mediation through ribo-seq TE is also organism-specific.  The measured mediated fraction was $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0$ in danio after truncating the negative raw value.  Direct measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption was $0.020817102845637092$ in danio, $5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5}$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast.  Protein turnover was weak in both turnover rows: yeast half-life absorbed $0.0009086031889966327$ on $3640$ joined proteins, while human Mathieson half-life absorbed $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ on an $8057$-protein join and did not exceed its dof-matched null.  The f3-stress reach test in yeast did cross its selection-aware null, with best coverage $0.22618445168998694$ versus null95 $0.02697141300639046$, but $0.7738155483100131$ of that component remained unexplained after the full readout set.  The minimal compilation check reached the same conclusion at the whole-panel level: final unexplained fractions were $0.9756319042010854$ in danio, $0.9942823785118229$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.9736442326306781$ in human, and $0.8676586345294415$ in yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.

\paragraph{Local exclusion clause.}
The finite table supports only a descriptive statement: the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation abundance residual has clustered cross-organism codon-direction structure, and the currently attached readouts do not supply a stable shared absorber.  No translation realization, folding pathway, structural order, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, causal mechanism, shared pathway, universal $H$, or global biological rule follows from $A_H$, $C_H$, score, rank, codon-window geometry, or the conservation label.  Such a stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched measurement with its own statistic.  Missing rows stay missing rather than becoming negative evidence, and H-side supports must be supplied independently of the residual target before any future row is treated as an empirical absorber.
