\subsection{Direction-aware conservation of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ codon-support residual}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-optimal-codon-conservation-b-star-q6-cross-organism-direction-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record tested here is a finite cross-organism ledger for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement residual.  Each organism contributes residual coordinate support over the nine coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The conservation probe then asks whether the organism-level optimal-codon direction vectors are scattered, universal, or clustered after enforcing a minimum organismal support of $500$ proteins.  The finite run requested $16$ organisms and computed $15$ usable organisms, used $41$ synonymous-design columns, and evaluated the cross-organism direction geometry by $5$ folds under the deterministic seed $sha256:b\_star\_q6\_optimal\_codon\_conservation\_powered:deterministic$.

\paragraph{Conservation statistic.}
The readback is not a pathway model.  It is the scalar cross-organism directional summary
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \overline{\cos}(B^{\ast}_{Q6}) &= 0.2629282074265475,\\
  n_{computed} &= 15,\\
  n_{requested} &= 16,\\
  d_{synonymous} &= 41.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered finite call is therefore \emph{clustered}: the optimal-codon direction vectors are not identical across organisms, but they are also not treated as unstructured noise.  The word clustered is used only for this finite cosine geometry.  It does not assert a universal codon law, a translation mechanism, a protein-state mechanism, or a conserved biological program.

\paragraph{Relation to the residual table.}
The same finite coordinate system also supports a direction-aware $H$-candidate ledger.  For organism $o$ and readout $H$, the table records absorbed energy $A_H$, coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, raw directional support $A_H C_H$, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
  Score(H,o) &= A_H(o) C_H(o) - \lambda DL(H),\\
  \lambda &= 0.01,\\
  DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{columns}(H)).
\end{aligned}
$$
For one-column readouts $DL=1$, while the $12$-column localization readout has $DL=3.700439718141092$.  This penalty is a declared scoring convention, not a biological constant.  The strongest raw direction-aware row in the computed ranking is small: in Danio rerio, measured translation efficiency has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, and $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, giving $Score=0.001566051846174291$.  Danio mRNA follows with $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$ and $Score=0.000734588577977589$.  In Homo sapiens, the best one-column ranked row is complex membership with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, and $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, but its penalized score is $-0.0034339698846599055$.  Human PTM density has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its direction cosine is only $0.3604440741749213$, so the directional product is only $0.0053651472982577525$.  This is the local meaning of the direction filter: energy without coordinate agreement is demoted.

\paragraph{Organism specificity.}
The per-organism best absorbers are not a shared channel.  The ranking ledger gives $H_o^{\ast}=measured\_te$ for Danio rerio, $H_o^{\ast}=complex\_member$ for Homo sapiens, $H_o^{\ast}=mrna$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and no positive shared best absorber for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 in the penalized table.  Gallus gallus has a feature-only computed ranking headed by complex membership with $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$ and $Score=-0.00575848521855683$, but it is not a universal absorber.  The finite table therefore supports an organism-specific residual description, not a common causal layer.

\paragraph{Boundary signature.}
A separate finite boundary-set readback gives an unusually consistent sign direction for the Window6 $R$ set.  Across $15$ computed organisms, the $R$ set is avoided in all $15$ non-tie cases and preferred in $0$ cases, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided binomial $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  Among individually significant organisms, the avoided count is $6$ of $6$.  This supports a finite abundance-preference signature for the fixed $R$ set, while staying separate from any claim about translation dynamics, protein folding, function, phenotype, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Meta-level checks.}
The organism-level summary remains heterogeneous.  The domain-versus-dominant-coordinate contingency test used $12$ complete-feature organisms and gave Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$.  The $M^{QTP}$ fraction did not resolve into a strong continuous proxy: versus $GC3$, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; versus log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  Domain grouping gave a bacteria-minus-archaea direction by group mean, but the variance-ratio permutation result was $p=0.4155844155844156$.  Thus the clustered cosine call is not accompanied by a resolved phylogenetic, GC, proteome-size, or tRNA-pool explanation.

\paragraph{Residual size and compression.}
The modeled-translation-complement residual is substantial in several organisms.  On the complement projection, the residual fraction $d$ is $0.874628721049266$ for Danio rerio, $0.6629960038578532$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.9400341806302775$ for Gallus gallus, $0.9208949355679455$ for Homo sapiens, $0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, and $0.21173219572706703$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The minimal-compilation readback likewise does not compress the residual strongly into the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary: the reported compressibilities are $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio rerio, $0.005717621488177116$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.02635576736932188$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.13234136547055853$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with median compressibility about $0.026$.  This is consistent with the negative reading of the $H$-ranking table: the residual is finite and measurable, but the attached readouts do not collapse it into one short universal explanation.

\paragraph{Biological boundary.}
The only permitted conclusion is a bounded descriptive one.  The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ optimal-codon direction field is clustered across $15$ computed organisms with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$, and the associated residual ranking shows weak, organism-specific, direction-sensitive absorption rather than a shared absorber.  No translation realization, folding state, physical admissibility, biochemical function, localization mechanism, causal pathway, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from these rows.  Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own statistic; they are not supplied by $A_H$, $C_H$, $Score$, the clustered conservation call, the $R$-set sign test, or the finite codon-coordinate geometry.
