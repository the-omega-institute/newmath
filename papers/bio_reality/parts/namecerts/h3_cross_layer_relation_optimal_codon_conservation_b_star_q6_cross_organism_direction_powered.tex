\subsection{Direction-aware optimal-codon conservation and the non-shared absorber boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-optimal-codon-conservation-direction}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.} The record fixes a finite cross-organism ledger for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement residual.  Each organism row contains the absorbed energy $A_H$, the residual coordinate support $a_{\perp}$, the readout-projected support $a_H$ on the nine coordinates, the direction cosine $C_H=\langle a_H,a_{\perp}\rangle/(|a_H||a_{\perp}|)$, a degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni comparison, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Score}(H) = A_H C_H - \lambda\,\operatorname{DL}(H),\qquad \lambda=0.01,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $\operatorname{DL}(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}})$.  The row therefore reports a bounded ranking statistic, not a biological channel.  Single-column readouts have $\operatorname{DL}=1$, while the localization readout has $12$ columns and hence $\operatorname{DL}=3.700439718141092$.

\paragraph{Conservation signal.} The optimal-codon conservation run is finite and passed its declared computation: $16$ organisms were requested, $15$ were computed, each retained at least $500$ proteins, the design used $41$ synonymous columns, and the five-fold procedure returned mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$.  The resulting module call is \emph{clustered}.  This is a weak-to-moderate cross-organism directional clustering statement over codon-design columns; it is not a universal optimal-codon law and it does not identify a common downstream absorber.  The associated Window6 boundary set supplies a sharper directional check: all $15$ computed organisms placed the fixed $R$ set on the avoided side, with avoided count $15$, preferred count $0$, zero count $0$, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and two-sided binomial $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  Among individually significant organisms, the same direction held for $6$ of $6$ rows: Homo sapiens, Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, Mus musculus, and Rattus norvegicus.  Thus the finite table contains a real avoided-boundary signature, but that signature is separate from any claim about translation, folding, function, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Measured translation and residual size.} The measured-TE mediation rows show that the abundance-side relation is highly organism-specific.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has measured mediated fraction $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.4793069656345167$, with $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$ and $S_{QT}=0.13681931343730436$.  Escherichia coli has $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.04091158340950673$, Homo sapiens has $0.0027966485036786744$, and Danio rerio is clipped to $0$ from raw value $-0.04801486048721136$.  The modeled-complement fraction $d$ is also heterogeneous: Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $d=0.21173219572706714$, Escherichia coli has $d=0.6629960038578534$, Danio rerio has $d=0.874628721049266$, Homo sapiens has $d=0.9208949355679455$, Gallus gallus has $d=0.9400341806302775$, and Sulfolobus solfataricus has $d=0.988614641876908$.  These values say that the residual left after the modeled translation readout is large in many organisms but small in yeast; they do not say what biological process creates it.

\paragraph{Direction-aware absorber table.} The ranking table confirms the negative part of the result.  The largest raw direction product among the computed rows is Danio rerio measured TE, with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and score $0.001566051846174291$.  Danio mRNA is close, with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and score $0.000734588577977589$.  Both exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, respectively $0.000719994971498518$ and $0.000587830414454545$.  Homo sapiens has a different best single-column absorber: complex membership has $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, and score $-0.0034339698846599055$, while mRNA has $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$.  Human PTM density has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but the direction cosine is only $0.3604440741749213$, so the direction product is demoted to $0.0053651472982577525$.  Human measured TE is even more strongly filtered: it exceeds the Bonferroni threshold but has $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$ and $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae is led by mRNA, with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and score $-0.008853737772559931$.  Escherichia coli has no scored best absorber; its mRNA row has $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$ but remains below the Bonferroni threshold.  Gallus gallus is led by complex membership, with $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$, $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, and $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$, but its score is negative.  The table therefore has local weak absorbers, but no stable cross-organism $H$.

\paragraph{Description-length outcome.} The description-length penalty is not a cosmetic adjustment.  It turns many nominally detectable projections into non-justifying rows.  Human localization, for example, has $A_H=0.009254913329269913$ and $C_H=0.7450003925723202$, giving $A_HC_H=0.006894914063528885$, but the $12$-column description length lowers the score to $-0.030109483117882037$.  Yeast localization similarly has $A_H=0.011225105608516642$ and $C_H=0.4247910216132792$, but score $-0.03223607310225219$.  The minimal-compilation check agrees with this interpretation: the computed organisms are not substantially compressed by the measured readout dictionary.  The reported compressibilities are $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio rerio, $0.005717621488177116$ for Escherichia coli, $0.02635576736932188$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.13234136547055853$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with median approximately $0.026$.  The ordered direction-aware dictionary leaves unexplained fractions $0.9756319042010854$ for Danio rerio, $0.9942823785118229$ for Escherichia coli, $0.9798666209022441$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.8676586345294415$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

\paragraph{Coverage and contact limits.} Some data rows are real but join-limited.  The yeast turnover contact joins $3640$ proteins, with reported abundance count $6351$, CDS count $5815$, TE count $5128$, and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; its turnover absorption is $0.0009086031889966327$.  The human Mathieson turnover row joins $8057$ proteins out of $19053$ full human CDS-abundance rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and turnover absorption $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, below its null threshold.  These contacts are valuable finite rows, but they are not enough to promote a universal degradation, localization, complex-membership, or translation channel.  Missing organisms and unavailable readouts remain unavailable, not zero-valued negatives.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.} The correct reading is a finite negative-positive split.  Optimal-codon direction is clustered across organisms, and the fixed Window6 $R$ set has a strong avoided abundance-preference signature.  At the same time, the candidate absorbers of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual are weak, direction-sensitive, and organism-specific.  Danio rerio has the only positive penalized top rows in this table, Homo sapiens is led by complex membership but remains penalized below zero, yeast is led weakly by mRNA, Escherichia coli stays within null for its available readouts, and no shared $H$ is supported.  The chapter therefore records a bounded cross-layer ranking result: the residual has structured codon-side geometry and finite external contacts, but no translation realization, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, protein-function claim, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows without an additional layer-matched contact and a separate statistic designed for that stronger assertion.
