\subsection{Direction-filtered conservation of the $B^*_{Q6}$ codon residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-optimal-codon-conservation-direction}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record tested here is a finite cross-organism ledger for the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  Each organism contributes a codon-side residual vector over the nine named $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, together with any attached readout rows for mRNA, measured translation efficiency, measured tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, localization, and protein-feature annotations.  The conservation probe asks whether the codon-side directions form a repeatable cross-organism module rather than a single universal biological channel.  The direct conservation run computed $15$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, used $41$ synonymous-design columns, and evaluated $5$ folds.  Its finite module call was \emph{clustered}, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ under the deterministic seed \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_optimal\_codon\_conservation\_powered:deterministic}.  Thus the supported claim is directional clustering in the codon residual geometry, not a universal optimum or mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary-set contact.}
A separate fixed boundary-set check supplies a stronger sign-level descriptive fact for the Window6 $R$ set.  Across the $15$ computed organisms, all $15$ non-tie directional rows placed $R$ on the avoided side: avoided count $15$, preferred count $0$, zero count $0$, majority fraction $1.0$, one-sided avoided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and two-sided binomial $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$ at two-sided $\alpha=0.05$.  Among the individually significant organisms, the same direction held for $6$ of $6$ rows: Homo sapiens, Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, Mus musculus, and Rattus norvegicus.  This gives a finite avoided-preference signature for the fixed $R$ boundary, but it is still only an association test against abundance data.  It does not identify translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, or a global biological law.

\paragraph{Residual complement scale.}
The translation-complement residual is large in several organisms and small in yeast, so the conservation result must be read against a heterogeneous residual background.  The modeled-complement fractions were $d=0.874628721049266$ for Danio rerio, $d=0.6629960038578532$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $d=0.9400341806302775$ for Gallus gallus, $d=0.9208949355679455$ for Homo sapiens, $d=0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155, and $d=0.21173219572706703$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The complementary translation-mediated fractions in that same finite decomposition were therefore organism-specific rather than conserved as one scalar: $m=0.12537127895073397$ for Danio, $m=0.3370039961421468$ for E. coli, $m=0.05996581936972245$ for Gallus, $m=0.07910506443205445$ for human, $m=0.10273279812535108$ for Mycobacterium, and $m=0.788267804272933$ for yeast.  Measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency did not collapse this heterogeneity into a common readout: the measured $M^{QTP}$ fractions were $0$ for Danio after truncating a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.

\paragraph{Direction-aware readout ranking.}
The finite $H$ ranking uses absorbed energy $A_H$, residual-coordinate cosine $C_H$, and the declared score $A_H C_H-\lambda DL(H)$ with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}})$.  The raw direction filter is essential.  In Danio rerio, measured TE ranked first with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and Score $0.001566051846174291$; mRNA followed with $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$ and Score $0.000734588577977589$.  In Homo sapiens, complex membership had the best one-column penalized score among simple readouts, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, and Score $-0.0034339698846599055$; mRNA was close, with $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$.  Human PTM density had larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its lower direction cosine $C_H=0.3604440741749213$ reduced the directional product to $0.0053651472982577525$.  In yeast, mRNA was the best simple entry with $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$ and Score $-0.008853737772559931$.  E. coli had only weak rows, with mRNA at $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$ and Score $-0.008470202379021127$.  Gallus had feature rows but no strong penalized positive score: complex membership gave $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$ and Score $-0.00575848521855683$.

\paragraph{Null and missingness discipline.}
The table separates three facts that are easy to conflate: direction, absorbed energy, and null exceedance.  Danio measured TE, Danio mRNA, Danio PTM density, and Danio measured tRNA-copy tAI exceeded their degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds, yet only measured TE and mRNA survived the stated description-length penalty with positive Score.  Human measured TE exceeded its Bonferroni threshold but had wrong residual-coordinate direction, $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, so its directional product was negative, $-0.00031302107580362116$.  Human measured tRNA-copy tAI was also directionally opposed, with $C_H=-0.3998537725071249$.  The measured tRNA-copy tAI absorption itself was not uniformly explanatory: Danio absorbed $0.020817102845637092$, human absorbed only $5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5}$, and yeast absorbed $0.001530747712165342$.  Missing organisms and empty ranking lists remain unavailable rows, not zero effects and not negative evidence.

\paragraph{Cross-organism interpretation.}
The best absorber is organism-specific rather than universal.  The assembled ranking gives $H_o^*=\texttt{measured\_te}$ for Danio, $H_o^*=\texttt{complex\_member}$ for Homo sapiens and Gallus gallus, $H_o^*=\texttt{mrna}$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and no finite winner for E. coli under the penalized criterion.  The organism-specificity survey likewise did not resolve the pattern by simple organism-level covariates: for $12$ complete-feature organisms, $M_{QTP}$ versus GC3 had Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; $M_{QTP}$ versus log proteome size had $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and $M_{QTP}$ versus log tRNA-gene count had $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The domain test had variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These numbers support a clustered finite codon-residual module with heterogeneous organism-level readback, not a domain-level or GC-level law.

\paragraph{Compression verdict.}
The residual dictionary and minimal-compilation checks reinforce the same boundary.  The measured low-dimensional dictionaries did not highly compress $P_Q$ across computed organisms: compressibility was $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio, $0.005717621488177116$ for E. coli, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.  The direction-aware ordered dictionary table left unexplained fractions $0.9756319042010854$ for Danio, $0.9942823785118229$ for E. coli, $0.9798666209022441$ for human, and $0.8676586345294415$ for yeast.  Hence the chapter-level readback is negative in a precise way: the $B^*_{Q6}$ codon residual shows finite clustered direction and a strong avoided $R$ signature, but the available biological readouts do not provide a shared absorber or compact common explanation.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The accepted statement is only that a finite, externally checked table reports clustered $B^*_{Q6}$ codon-residual directions, avoided $R$ behavior, and weak organism-specific readout absorption under the declared scoring and null rules.  No translation-realization claim follows from codon geometry alone.  No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, pathway, causal mechanism, or universality follows from $A_H$, $C_H$, Score, rank, $H_o^*$, missingness, or the clustered conservation call.  Any such stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own statistic.
