\subsection{Window-6 boundary set abundance signature}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-window-six-boundary-r-signature}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record tested here is a finite cross-layer table attached to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  The fixed object is the Window-6 boundary subset $R$ of the sense-codon universe: the run reports $10$ $R$ sense codons inside a $61$-codon sense table.  For each organism, the finite question is whether the $R$ codons are abundance-preferred or abundance-avoided after the organism-specific codon and abundance rows have been joined.  The module class of the claim is \texttt{cross\_layer\_relation}; the run is an association test against protein abundance and not a statement about translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, cellular function, mechanism, phylogeny, or any universal biological law.

\paragraph{Observed cross-organism sign.}
The powered run computed all $15$ requested organisms with at least $500$ proteins per organism.  Every computed organism placed the Window-6 boundary set on the avoided side: $15$ avoided, $0$ preferred, and $0$ zero among $15$ directional non-ties.  The corresponding sign-test summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{dir}} &= 15, & n_{\mathrm{avoided}} &= 15, & n_{\mathrm{preferred}} &= 0, & n_{0} &= 0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{one\text{-}sided,avoided}} &= 3.0517578125\cdot 10^{-5}, &
 p_{\mathrm{two\text{-}sided}} &= 6.103515625\cdot 10^{-5}. 
\end{aligned}
$$
At the declared two-sided threshold $\alpha=0.05$, this is a strong finite sign pattern.  The organisms in the avoided list are \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{bacillus\_subtilis\_subsp\_subtilis\_str\_168}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, \texttt{caenorhabditis\_elegans}, \texttt{drosophila\_melanogaster}, \texttt{halobacterium\_salinarum}, \texttt{sulfolobus\_solfataricus}, \texttt{dictyostelium\_discoideum}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}.  The significant-organism subset is smaller but directionally identical: $6$ avoided, $0$ preferred, and $0$ zero among \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{gallus\_gallus}, \texttt{pseudomonas\_aeruginosa\_pao1}, \texttt{mus\_musculus}, and \texttt{rattus\_norvegicus}.  The individual diagnostic used $1000$ permutations and records $6$ individually significant organisms out of $15$ computed organisms; the older majority threshold recorded in the diagnostic was $8$ and is not used to promote the result beyond the finite association call.

\paragraph{Relation to the residual-ranking table.}
The Window-6 boundary signal sits beside, rather than inside, the direction-aware residual-ranking table.  The ranking carrier records per-organism tuples $(A_H,C_H,\mathrm{Score})$ over fetchable readouts, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
 C_H &= \frac{\langle a_H,a_{\perp}\rangle}{\lVert a_H\rVert\lVert a_{\perp}\rVert}, &
 \mathrm{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - 0.01\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
That table confirms the same restraint seen in the boundary-set record: high absorption is not automatically biological explanation.  For example, in \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{ptm\_density} has the largest recorded $A_H$ among the one-column feature readouts, about $0.0148848259$, but its direction cosine is only $C_H\approx0.360444$, giving $A_HC_H\approx0.00536515$ and a negative score after the stated $\lambda=0.01$ penalty.  In the same organism, \texttt{complex\_member} has $A_H\approx0.00752720$, $C_H\approx0.872307$, and $A_HC_H\approx0.00656603$, but still has $\mathrm{Score}\approx-0.00343397$.  In \texttt{danio\_rerio}, the best raw direction-aware row is \texttt{measured\_te}, with $A_H\approx0.01759429$, $C_H\approx0.657375$, $A_HC_H\approx0.01156605$, and $\mathrm{Score}\approx0.00156605$; \texttt{mrna} follows with $A_HC_H\approx0.01073459$ and $\mathrm{Score}\approx0.000734589$.  In \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, the top one-column row is \texttt{mrna}, with $A_H\approx0.00189761$, $C_H\approx0.604057$, $A_HC_H\approx0.00114626$, and a negative score.  These values show that the boundary-set avoided signature is not being converted into a single shared readout channel.

\paragraph{Translation and abundance context.}
The same finite ledger contains translation-side and abundance-side contacts, but their numbers do not authorize a mechanism claim.  Measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency was available on joined rows for \texttt{danio\_rerio} ($n=13140$), \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} ($n=3415$), \texttt{homo\_sapiens} ($n=11554$), and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} ($n=5128$).  The measured mediation fractions are heterogeneous: \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} has $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}\approx0.479307$, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} has $0.0409116$, \texttt{homo\_sapiens} has $0.00279665$, and \texttt{danio\_rerio} is clipped to $0$ from a raw suppressive value.  Measured tRNA-copy tAI rows also differ by organism: the joined row counts are $14410$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $19053$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $5815$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, with absorbed fractions approximately $0.0208171$, $0.0000542623$, and $0.00153075$, respectively.  These rows are useful contacts for bounded readback, but they do not turn the Window-6 boundary set into a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Fetchable H-readout checks.}
The residual-side H candidates are likewise finite descriptive projections.  The mRNA absorption values are about $0.0119730$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $0.00177070$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $0.00785822$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $0.00189761$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  Measured TE absorption is about $0.0175943$, $0.00109830$, $0.00289471$, and $0.000276278$ in the same organism order.  Localization uses $12$ localization columns and joins $1984$ \texttt{danio\_rerio} proteins, $16008$ human proteins, and $4656$ yeast proteins; the corresponding absorption values are about $0.00950886$, $0.00925491$, and $0.0112251$.  Human turnover joins $8057$ proteins and absorbs only $3.57407649\cdot10^{-6}$, below its degree-of-freedom matched null threshold; yeast turnover joins $3640$ proteins and absorbs about $0.000908603$.  Protein-feature rows are sparse and join-sensitive: the human feature join has $18335$ rows, the yeast feature join has $5682$, and the danio feature join has only $2242$, so feature effects are kept as row-bounded projections rather than organism-wide explanations.

\paragraph{Biological signature call.}
The finite conclusion is therefore narrow but nontrivial.  The Window-6 boundary set $R$ has a reproducible avoided abundance-preference signature across the computed organisms: all $15$ computed signs point to avoided, the two-sided binomial value is $6.103515625\cdot10^{-5}$, and the significant-organism subset has the same direction in all $6$ significant cases.  The result is a cross-layer association between a fixed internal codon-window boundary set and externally joined abundance rows.  It is not evidence that the $R$ codons cause lower abundance, encode a universal translation program, determine folding, determine physical admissibility, realize protein function, specify localization, or define a global biological law.
