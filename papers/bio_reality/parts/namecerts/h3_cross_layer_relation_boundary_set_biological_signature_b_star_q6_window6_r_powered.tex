\subsection{Window6 boundary avoidance as a finite cross-layer signature}
\label{sec:bioreality-window6-boundary-avoidance-signature}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object in this record is the Window6 boundary set $R$ extracted from the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate packet and tested against organism-matched protein-abundance rows. The module type is cross-layer relation: the internal side supplies a fixed codon-boundary subset, while the measured side supplies organism-scoped abundance preferences. The finite support contains $10$ sense codons inside $R$, compared against $61$ sense codons in the per-codon preference table. The run requested $15$ organisms and computed all $15$ with at least $500$ proteins per organism. Its declared biological call is narrow: Window6 boundary $R$ has an avoided abundance-preference signature across computed organisms.

\paragraph{Sign test and row ledger.}
Across the $15$ directional non-tie organisms, every computed organism placed $R$ on the avoided side: avoided count $15$, preferred count $0$, and zero count $0$. Thus the majority fraction is $1.0$. The one-sided binomial probability for the all-avoided outcome is $3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and the two-sided binomial probability is $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$ at the stated two-sided level $\alpha=0.05$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{directional}} &= 15,\\
 n_{\mathrm{avoided}} &= 15,\\
 n_{\mathrm{preferred}} &= 0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{one\ sided, avoided}} &= 3.0517578125\times 10^{-5},\\
 p_{\mathrm{two\ sided}} &= 6.103515625\times 10^{-5}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The avoided list is exhaustive for the computed table: \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, \emph{Mus musculus}, \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}. The individually significant subset has $6$ organisms, again all avoided and none preferred: \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Mus musculus}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}. The individual permutation diagnostic used $1000$ permutations, with $6$ significant organisms and the old majority threshold recorded as $8$.

\paragraph{Relation to the residual ranking panel.}
The boundary signature is not read as a mechanism. It is placed beside the direction-aware $H$-ranking panel for the non-translation abundance residual, where each row reports absorbed energy $A_H$, coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, raw alignment $A_H C_H$, and a penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda\,DL(H),\\
 \lambda &= 0.01,\\
 DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
That ranking table is useful as a boundary check because it shows how little of the residual is stably absorbed by a shared readout. In \emph{Danio rerio}, the top raw aligned readout is measured TE with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and Score $0.001566051846174291$ on $13140$ joined rows. The human PTM-density row has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its direction cosine is only $0.3604440741749213$, so the direction filter lowers its raw alignment to $0.0053651472982577525$. In yeast, mRNA is the top ranked entry with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and Score $-0.008853737772559931$ on $5128$ rows. In \emph{E. coli}, mRNA has $A_H=0.0017706954554909877$, $C_H=0.8639529831258776$, and $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$, but its Score remains negative. The per-organism best absorber is therefore not shared: danio selects measured TE, human and gallus select complex membership, yeast selects mRNA, and several organisms have no computed ranking rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite result is a strong abundance-preference asymmetry for the fixed Window6 boundary set $R$, not a universal biological law. The $15/15$ avoided direction and two-sided probability $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$ support the statement that this boundary set is not neutral in the computed abundance-preference readback. The individually significant organisms give a second finite layer: $6/6$ significant rows are avoided. However, the same packet also records that the best residual absorbers are weak and organism-specific, that no shared universal $H$ is found, and that score penalization removes nearly all complexity-justified readouts. The $f3_{stress}$ auxiliary readout illustrates the same bounded style: in yeast the best subset covers $0.22618445168998694$ of the target against a selection-aware null $95$th percentile of $0.02697141300639046$, leaving $0.7738155483100131$ unexplained after the full readout set. Cross-organism codon-direction conservation is only clustered, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ over $15$ computed organisms and $41$ synonymous-design columns. These numbers keep the signature finite: they show a reproducible avoided boundary signal, while preserving the residual and organism-specific character of the remaining cross-layer projections.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
This subsection asserts only a finite association between a fixed Window6 boundary codon set and abundance-preference direction under the stated joins, permutations, and sign test. It does not assert translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, function, pathway mechanism, causal mediation, phylogenetic independence, or universality. The names of the attached contacts do not create those stronger facts. A later claim of translation, structure, function, mechanism, or global rule would require a separate layer-matched measurement and statistic, on its own rows, beyond the $R$-avoidance sign test and beyond the direction-aware residual-ranking table.
