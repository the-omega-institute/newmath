\subsection{Window6 boundary R abundance signature}
\label{sec:bior reality-window6-boundary-r-abundance-signature}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
The Window6 boundary packet tests a fixed set $R$ of codons determined before looking at the abundance readout.  The finite row contains $10$ codons in $R$ inside the $61$ sense-codon universe, and the abundance-preference direction is then evaluated organism by organism.  The computation requested $15$ organisms and computed all $15$ with a minimum organism size of $500$ proteins.  The observed direction table has no ties:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{avoided}} &= 15,\\
 n_{\mathrm{preferred}} &= 0,\\
 n_{\mathrm{zero}} &= 0,\\
 n_{\mathrm{directional}} &= 15.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting majority fraction is therefore $1.0$.  Under the one-sided avoided-direction sign test, the reported $p$-value is $3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and the two-sided value is $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$ at the stated two-sided level $\alpha=0.05$.  The permutation diagnostic used $1000$ permutations for the individual-organism calls.  Six organisms pass the individual two-sided threshold: \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Mus musculus}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}.  The full computed avoided list is larger: \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Gallus gallus}, \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, \emph{Mus musculus}, \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}, and \emph{Rattus norvegicus}.

\paragraph{Classification of the observation.}
This is a cross-layer relation record, not a biological mechanism record.  The tested object is a fixed Window6/B$^*_{Q6}$ boundary subset $R$ compared with external protein-abundance preference summaries after the organism-specific data joins.  The finite verdict is that the boundary subset has a reproducible avoided-abundance signature across the computed organisms.  The row classification is deliberately narrow: it is an association test on a predeclared codon set against abundance data, with no promotion to translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, function, causal pathway, phylogenetic explanation, or universal biological law.

\paragraph{Relation to the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual ledger.}
The boundary signal sits beside the nine-coordinate B$^*_{Q6}$ residual ledger used elsewhere in the same finite packet family.  Those coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  In the modeled-translation-complement readback, the residual fraction $d$ is organism-specific: for example $d=0.9208949355679455$ in human, $d=0.874628721049266$ in zebrafish, $d=0.9400341806302775$ in chicken, $d=0.6629960038578532$ in \emph{E. coli}, $d=0.8972672018746489$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $d=0.21173219572706703$ in yeast.  These numbers show why the boundary result is not read as a single shared mechanism: the residual geometry and the abundance boundary can both be computed, but their biological interpretation remains constrained to finite descriptive association.

\paragraph{Direction-aware readout checks.}
The same finite family also ranks candidate readouts $H$ by absorbed energy $A_H$, coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, and the stated regularized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda DL(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The ranking table gives concrete examples of why the avoided-boundary observation is not collapsed into a mechanistic readout.  In zebrafish, measured TE has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and Score $=0.001566051846174291$ over $13140$ joined rows; mRNA has $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, and Score $=0.000734588577977589$.  In human, complex membership is the top raw single-column readout in the table with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, and $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, but its Score is still negative, $-0.0034339698846599055$, after the declared penalty.  Human PTM density has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its direction cosine is only $0.3604440741749213$, yielding $A_HC_H=0.0053651472982577525$ and Score $=-0.004634852701742248$.  This is a useful finite check: high energy alone is not allowed to become a directionally correct biological explanation.

\paragraph{Joined-row scale and contact limits.}
The readout rows are finite and join-sensitive.  The base ranking ledger reports full abundance/CDS joins of $19053$ rows for human, $14410$ for zebrafish, $12418$ for chicken, $5815$ for yeast, $3739$ for \emph{E. coli}, $4129$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $4042$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $19395$ for mouse, $13322$ for rat, $11661$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $13295$ for \emph{D. melanogaster}, $1656$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}, and corresponding rank-$23$ control residualizations where the base table is computed.  Measured-TE/mRNA rows are smaller where the translation dataset is the limiting join: human has $11554$ TE and mRNA rows, zebrafish has $13140$, yeast has $5128$, and \emph{E. coli} has $3415$.  The measured tRNA-copy tAI check uses full modeled-complement joins for human, zebrafish, and yeast, with $431$, $8846$, and $273$ tRNA genes respectively.  Human turnover is much more restrictive: the Mathieson half-life contact joins $8057$ proteins, reports $8164$ valid turnover proteins, and gives absorbed turnover energy $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, below its dof-matched null with null mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$.  The separate N-end turnover boundary check joins $3898$ rows with $12000$ actual permutation fits and a consistent-sign fraction of $0.5$.  These row counts and null outcomes keep the chapter at the level of bounded measurement contact rather than explanation.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The supported statement is therefore precise: the fixed Window6 boundary set $R$ shows a cross-organism avoided abundance-preference signature in all $15$ computed organisms, with six individually significant avoided calls and a two-sided sign-test value $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  The result is stronger than a single-organism anecdote and weaker than a biological law.  It says that the predeclared codon boundary has a reproducible avoided-direction association with the abundance readout under the finite row policy.  It does not say that the codons realize translation efficiency, determine folded structure, encode physical admissibility, cause protein abundance, identify function, define a pathway, prove a universal shared $H$, or establish a global biological principle.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own statistic, null model, row joins, and failure conditions.
