\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP occupancy gate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows carrying $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate labels after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The intended row has, on the same biological item, a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, local sequence and structure controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP measurement at the editable RNA window.  The coordinate by itself is only a label on synonymous residual variation.  The row becomes a cross-layer RNA-decay candidate only when the label is read together with translation-conditioned half-life behavior and an independently measured RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue panel.

The available finite reality contacts are uneven.  The linked recombination contact for PRDM9 motif and DSB-hotspot structure is numerically concrete, with $52963$ hotspot rows in run $e57a09da690c$ and runtime $168.443$ seconds, but it belongs to a DNA recombination surface rather than to RNA half-life or RBP perturbation.  The RBP-binding contact has $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run $b225890251c2$, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds; this supplies a useful finite example of RBP-row accounting, but not the same-window decay-factor occupancy and rescue panel required here.  A separate translation-contact run contains $6494$ rows in run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, with runtime $9.469$ seconds and a GEO readout source for the translation layer.  These three contacts establish that finite row surfaces exist for hotspot, RBP-binding, and translation readouts, while also showing that the present RNA-decay bridge is not licensed by any one of those surfaces alone.

\paragraph{Statistic to be read on the matched panel.}
Let $q_i$ denote the high-versus-low $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after amino-acid quotient, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ be the measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable window.  Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA-structure score, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and simpler codon-baseline terms.  A positive bridge requires the following finite-panel pattern:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& q_i \not\perp H_i \mid T_i,Z_i, \\
& q_i \not\perp O_i \mid T_i,Z_i, \\
& \Delta_H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss}, \\
& \Delta_H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \text{ returns under wild-type target-RBP rescue}, \\
& \Delta_H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\Delta_H$ is only a bounded finite-row contrast in RNA half-life.  It is not a protein-output statistic, not a folding statistic, and not a functional assay.  The translation readout is a conditioning boundary: without $T_i$ on the same row, an RNA half-life difference remains an RNA-stability association rather than a translation-conditioned claim.  Without $O_i$ and the perturbation-rescue pattern, the same half-life difference remains outside the RBP-mechanism bridge.

\paragraph{Null model and break conditions.}
The matched null exchanges high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels after controlling amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout.  Under this null, any residual half-life separation must disappear after conditioning unless the coordinate carries information beyond the matched controls.  Even if a half-life contrast survives, it still does not become an RBP bridge unless measured local occupancy changes in the prespecified decay-factor RBP and the effect follows target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue while failing under binding-defective or non-target controls.

Several apparent positives are therefore excluded.  A motif-density-only explanation is not an RBP bridge.  A local RNA-structure-only explanation is not an RBP bridge.  A transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, codon-pair, GC, dinucleotide, or batch explanation is not an RBP bridge.  A binding signal that is not experimentally decoupled from coordinate class is not enough.  A rescue that also works with a binding-defective construct or with a non-target control does not support the bridge.

\paragraph{Finite evidence ledger.}
The present ledger supports a bounded readiness statement rather than a positive biological mechanism.  The PRDM9 hotspot run contributes a concrete linked cross-layer dataset size, $52963$ hotspot rows, but its biological layer is meiotic recombination.  The RBP-binding run contributes $900$ RBP peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but does not provide same-row RNA half-life, translation, target-RBP loss, or rescue rows for this coordinate system.  The translation run contributes $6494$ translation-layer rows, but it does not measure RNA half-life or decay-factor RBP occupancy.  Consequently the finite surfaces needed for this conjecture are only partially represented across separate contacts, not joined into the required same-organism, same-window panel.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter licenses only the finite question: whether a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate, after amino-acid quotient and after same-row translation conditioning, is associated with RNA half-life through a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern.  It does not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It does not license protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the specified RBP-decay test, or a universal sequence law.  Each of those claims would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
