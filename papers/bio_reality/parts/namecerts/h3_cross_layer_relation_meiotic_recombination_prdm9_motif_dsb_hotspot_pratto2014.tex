\subsection{Finite hotspot rows and the RNA-decay bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-prdm9-hotspot-rna-decay-rbp-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The row named by this record is a meiotic-recombination contact: the finite run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.meiotic\_recombination.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_hotspot\_pratto2014} reports $52963$ hotspot rows, with runtime $168.443$ seconds.  This number is used here only as a finite row count for a PRDM9-motif double-strand-break hotspot surface.  It does not by itself provide coding-transcript rows, synonymous residual-coordinate labels, translation readouts, RNA half-life values, decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, or rescue measurements.  The biological identity of the source row is therefore narrower than the conjectural RNA-decay bridge named in the packet: the Pratto hotspot table can bound a recombination-contact inventory, while the proposed $B^*_{Q6}$ RNA-half-life bridge requires a different matched transcript-level panel.

\paragraph{Typed finite carrier for the bridge question.}
The bridge question is meaningful only after replacing a bare hotspot inventory by matched transcript or CDS rows carrying four kinds of same-row data: a synonymous residual-coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and a measured local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The relevant row type is finite and relational: each admissible row has a coordinate class, a readout relation, and a perturbation or rescue trigger.  High-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels are compared only after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been conditioned away.  In symbols, the intended null is the exchangeability of the high and low labels for the RNA half-life residual after these controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0: \quad \Delta \lambda_{\mathrm{RNA}}(B^*_{Q6}\text{ high},B^*_{Q6}\text{ low}\mid Z,T)=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes the non-translation controls and $T$ is the attached same-row translation readout.  A nonzero residual half-life contrast is still not a bridge unless the RBP evidence below is present.

\paragraph{Required RBP-decay pattern.}
The proposed cross-layer reading is bounded by an experimentally decoupled RBP condition.  The local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must change at the editable RNA window, the half-life effect must collapse after loss of that target RBP, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore the effect, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail to restore it.  Thus the admissible positive pattern is not merely a regression coefficient on half-life.  It is the finite conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta \lambda_{\mathrm{RNA}} \neq 0,\\
&\Delta \mathrm{Occ}_{\mathrm{RBP}} \neq 0,\\
&\Delta \lambda_{\mathrm{RNA}}\text{ collapses under target-RBP loss},\\
&\Delta \lambda_{\mathrm{RNA}}\text{ returns under wild-type rescue},\\
&\Delta \lambda_{\mathrm{RNA}}\text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Without this conjunction, the row remains an RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor rather than an RBP-mediated RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Other finite contacts used for calibration.}
Two linked finite runs give useful scale but do not fill the missing bridge rows.  The RBP-binding run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  This establishes that finite RBP-binding surfaces are tracked elsewhere in the same audit universe, but it is not the same as measured same-window occupancy for the decay-factor RBP in the transcript panel required here.  The mTOR-translation run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} reports $6494$ rows from the stated GEO readout source, with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  This establishes that finite translation-readout tables can be attached in the broader system, but it is not a same-row translation boundary for the present RNA half-life and RBP-occupancy rows.  These two facts are therefore supporting scale checks, not substitutes for the missing joined matrix.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
The hotspot count, RBP peak count, and translation-row count cannot be combined by naming alone.  A PRDM9 hotspot table is a meiotic recombination surface; an RBP peak table is a binding surface; a translation table is a translation-readout surface.  The RNA-decay bridge requires the same biological rows to carry coordinate, translation, half-life, occupancy, perturbation, and rescue fields.  Consequently, no translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone, no RNA-decay mechanism follows from same-row translation conditioning alone, and no RBP mechanism follows from an unchanged occupancy surface or from a perturbation lacking wild-type and binding-defective rescue contrast.  Likewise, no protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law is licensed by this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant measurement and statistic.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
The present row is ready as a bounded BioReality boundary chapter because it states exactly what the verified finite facts do and do not support.  The only direct finite fact for the named Pratto record is the $52963$-hotspot inventory.  The linked $900$-peak RBP-binding surface and $6494$-row translation surface show that the surrounding evidence system contains finite binding and translation tables, but the decisive same-organism, same-window RNA half-life and RBP perturbation/rescue matrix is not present in this record.  The scientifically admissible reading is therefore negative for the full bridge and positive only for a finite boundary specification: a future positive bridge would need a joined transcript panel with $B^*_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, mRNA abundance controls, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, evaluated under the stated controls before any cross-layer RNA-decay claim is allowed.
