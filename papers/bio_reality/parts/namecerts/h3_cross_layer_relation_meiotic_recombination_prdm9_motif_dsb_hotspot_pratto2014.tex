\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about coding transcripts, synonymous residual-coordinate labels after amino-acid quotienting, same-row translation readouts, RNA half-life measurements, and an externally specified decay-factor RNA-binding protein. Its carrier is not a free sequence law. It is a table whose admissible rows must carry a coding transcript or CDS identifier, a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, the amino-acid quotient under which high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows are compared, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy for the nominated decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcription, abundance, sequence composition, codon-pair context, local RNA structure, splice or isoform state, and batch.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The finite ledger attached to this chapter contains a meiotic-recombination run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.meiotic\_recombination.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_hotspot\_pratto2014} with $52963$ hotspot rows and runtime $168.443$ seconds. That number is useful only as an example of a finite cross-layer reality table already admitted elsewhere in the same BioReality ledger; it is not an RNA half-life or RBP-occupancy measurement. The same ledger also contains an RBP-binding run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} with $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant outcomes, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds. This second run is closer in biological layer because it touches RBP binding, but it still does not provide the required same-organism, same-window occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows for the present $Q6$ RNA half-life bridge. A translation-linked finite run is also present for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012}, with $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds, but this supplies a translation readout surface rather than an RNA decay mechanism. These three anchors show that the ledger stores finite biological readouts, while also showing that none of the supplied scalar facts is already the joined table demanded by the present claim.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is an exchangeability statement on the finite matched panel. After conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are treated as exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. In schematic form, the null boundary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0:\quad (Q_{\mathrm{high}}, Q_{\mathrm{low}}) \perp H
\mid A, T, C, S, G, D, P, I, B,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ is the RNA half-life readout, $A$ is the amino-acid quotient information, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $C$ collects transcription and abundance controls, $S$ is local RNA-structure control, $G$ and $D$ are GC and dinucleotide controls, $P$ is codon-pair context, $I$ is splice or isoform state, and $B$ is batch. A departure from this null is still only an RNA-stability association unless the RBP bridge conditions are met on the same finite rows.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
A positive bridge is permitted only when the RNA half-life contrast tracks an experimentally separated local occupancy change for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapses after target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue. The required finite pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(Q \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{target\ loss}) \approx 0,\\
&\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \approx \Delta H(Q \mid T,Z),\\
&\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}) \approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same failure required for a non-target control. Here $Z$ denotes the declared control block: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, translation readout, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline terms. The bridge is therefore not a statement that codon geometry selects an RBP by itself. It is a finite perturbation-and-rescue statement about a named decay factor measured at the RNA window.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The intended readback is a matched-panel report with five finite observations: a translation-conditioned RNA half-life effect, a local occupancy change for the specified decay-factor RBP, collapse of the effect under target-RBP loss, restoration under wild-type rescue, and non-restoration under binding-defective or non-target controls. The contact \texttt{matched-rna-half-life-readout} supplies only half-life rows keyed by gene or transcript. The contact \texttt{matched-mrna-abundance-control} supplies a transcription-layer denominator or covariate. The contact \texttt{ribosome-profiling-translation-efficiency} supplies translation efficiency or ribosome-occupancy information. These contacts define necessary surfaces, but their names do not themselves form the joined statistic. In particular, a transcript half-life table without measured local RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue rows cannot certify the bridge.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter licenses at most a bounded cross-layer candidate: a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate association with RNA half-life, conditioned on translation, can be investigated as an RBP-bridge hypothesis. It does not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone, and it does not license protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality. Those claims require their own finite layer-matched contacts. A motif-density model, RNA-structure-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, transcript-abundance model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, batch model, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue cannot be counted as support for the bridge. If the same-row translation readout is absent, the result remains outside the translation-conditioned claim. If local RBP occupancy is unchanged, target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life effect, or wild-type rescue does not restore it, the result remains a bounded descriptor or RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
The present finite rows are sufficient to state the BioReality boundary precisely, because the ledger gives concrete finite anchors at related biological layers: $52963$ PRDM9 hotspot rows, $900$ RBP-binding peaks with $3$ cross-RBP concordant outcomes, and $6494$ mTOR translation rows. They are not sufficient to assert a positive RNA half-life RBP bridge. The chapter therefore records the admissible scientific shape of the claim and its local falsification rule: the bridge becomes positive only on a same-organism, same-window joined panel containing $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls under the declared finite statistic.
