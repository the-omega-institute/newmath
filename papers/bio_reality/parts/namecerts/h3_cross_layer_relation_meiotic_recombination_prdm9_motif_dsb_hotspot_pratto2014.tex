\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as an RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The object recorded here is a finite matched panel of coding-transcript rows carrying synonymous residual-coordinate labels $B^*_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid sequence.  Each row is allowed to enter the proposed bridge only when four kinds of measured or controlled information are present on the same finite index: a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a local decay-factor RBP occupancy readout at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for abundance, transcriptional state, local sequence composition, local RNA structure, splice or isoform state, and batch.  The intended contrast is not a free comparison between codons.  It is a matched contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after the amino-acid quotient, with translation conditioning already attached to the row.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The present packet is not a completed positive RBP-decay claim.  It is a boundary record tied to finite reality contacts.  The meiotic-recombination benchmark linked to the packet has a verified run $\mathrm{run}=\mathrm{e57a09da690c}$ with $52963$ hotspot rows and runtime $168.443$ seconds.  This hotspot table is not an RNA half-life table and is not used as mechanistic support for the RNA claim; it is cited only as a finite-row cross-layer benchmark showing that the surrounding BioReality ledger uses row-counted, auditable contacts rather than unrestricted verbal generalization.  The RBP-binding benchmark has verified run $\mathrm{run}=\mathrm{b225890251c2}$ with $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  That contact demonstrates that RBP occupancy can be handled as a separate finite surface, but it does not by itself provide same-window decay-factor occupancy for the $B^*_{Q6}$ RNA-half-life rows.  A translation benchmark, $\mathrm{run}=\mathrm{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$, contributes $6494$ finite translation-readout rows from the reported source and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  It supplies the kind of same-row translation boundary required by this chapter, but it is not a half-life or RBP-rescue experiment.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
Let $i$ range over the finite matched transcript or CDS rows that survive identifier reconciliation.  Write $Q_i \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ for the residual-coordinate bin, $T_i$ for the attached translation readout, $H_i$ for the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ for measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and $Z_i$ for the matched controls.  A positive bridge can be reported only if the finite statistic is computed after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$, and only if the same effect follows the externally measured occupancy perturbation chain.  The schematic finite test has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \widehat{H}(Q_i=\mathrm{high}\mid T_i,Z_i)-\widehat{H}(Q_i=\mathrm{low}\mid T_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_O &= \widehat{O}(Q_i=\mathrm{high}\mid T_i,Z_i)-\widehat{O}(Q_i=\mathrm{low}\mid T_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0 \quad \text{after target-RBP loss},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wt\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defect}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first line alone is only a translation-conditioned RNA-stability association.  The second line alone is only an occupancy association.  The bridge requires the paired pattern: local occupancy moves with the coordinate in a decoupled assay, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.

\paragraph{Matched null.}
The local null is row exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout have been held fixed.  Under that null, any residual difference in $H_i$ is treated as non-bridging unless the measured occupancy and perturbation chain is present.  A half-life shift without $O_i$ movement is not an RBP mechanism.  An occupancy shift without coordinate/occupancy decoupling is not a decay explanation.  A loss effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target failure is not a completed bridge.

\paragraph{What the present evidence licenses.}
The finite facts currently license a narrow record: BioReality has row-counted contacts for a recombination benchmark with $52963$ hotspot rows, for an RBP-binding benchmark with $900$ peak rows and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases, and for a translation benchmark with $6494$ rows.  These numbers support the existence of auditable finite surfaces around the proposed claim.  They do not supply the missing same-organism, same-window RNA half-life plus decay-factor RBP occupancy plus loss/rescue matrix required for the claim itself.  Therefore the chapter treats $B^*_{Q6}$ as a bounded coordinate descriptor until the panel contains all bridge fields on matched rows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, RNA-decay mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate geometry, from codon-window notation, from a translation contact name, from mRNA abundance controls, or from a half-life association.  Those conclusions require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  In this chapter, the only admissible positive conclusion would be a bounded translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge, and even that conclusion is unavailable until measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control rows are joined to the same finite panel.
