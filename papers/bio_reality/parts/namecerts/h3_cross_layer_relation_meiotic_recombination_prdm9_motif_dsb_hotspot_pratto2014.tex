\subsection{A translation-conditioned RNA half-life boundary for a prespecified RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-prdm9-namespace}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record considered here is not a claim about protein output, recombination, or a universal sequence law.  It is a finite matched-panel claim about coding-transcript rows carrying $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate labels after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and perturbation controls.  The available finite verification attached to the surrounding BioReality ledger contains a recombination-hotspot row count and an independent RBP-binding row count, but it does not yet contain the joined same-window panel required for a positive RNA-decay bridge.  The hotspot packet reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{hotspot}} &= 52963,\\
t_{\mathrm{hotspot}} &= 168.443\ \mathrm{s},
\end{aligned}
$$
for the PRDM9/DSB hotspot surface.  The RBP-binding packet reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks,total}} &= 900,\\
N_{\mathrm{cross\text{-}RBP\ concordant}} &= 3,\\
t_{\mathrm{RBP}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A translation-readout contact is also present in the broader ledger, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{translation}} &= 6494,\\
t_{\mathrm{translation}} &= 9.469\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers establish that the file contains finite external row surfaces for hotspot, RBP-binding, and translation-readout directions.  They do not, by themselves, establish that the same transcript windows carry all variables needed for the RNA half-life RBP-bridge statistic.

\paragraph{Local null model.}
For the RNA half-life bridge itself, the finite rows must be compared only after conditioning away the obvious lower-layer explanations.  Let $q_i$ denote the high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotienting, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote RNA half-life, and let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and any rank-sufficiency covariates declared for the finite panel.  The matched null is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, Z_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
with high- and low-coordinate labels exchangeable for RNA half-life after these controls.  A departure from this null is still only an RNA-stability association unless the RBP bridge is also present.  In particular, a half-life difference without local measured occupancy, loss-of-target collapse, and wild-type rescue remains below the mechanism boundary.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
The intended positive claim is a bounded cross-layer statement, not a promotion of codon geometry to biology.  A finite panel supports the bridge only when five readbacks occur on matched rows.  First, $q_i$ separates high-coordinate from low-coordinate synonymous residual windows after amino-acid quotienting.  Second, the RNA half-life contrast remains visible only in the translation-conditioned comparison, so the comparison is not merely a transcription-abundance or isoform-state artifact.  Third, the local occupancy $O_i$ of the prespecified decay-factor RBP changes in the coordinate-associated window under an experimental design that decouples coordinate label from occupancy measurement.  Fourth, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect.  Fifth, wild-type target-RBP rescue restores the effect, while binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it.  In compressed notation, the support pattern has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_O(q \mid T,Z) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{target\ loss}) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) &\approx \Delta_H(q \mid T,Z),\\
\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The displayed conditions are not inferred from the current row counts; they define the finite statistic that a future same-window panel must instantiate.

\paragraph{Current finite evidence state.}
The available scalar facts are sufficient for a bounded audit statement and insufficient for a positive bridge.  The $52963$ hotspot rows belong to a meiotic recombination surface and do not measure RNA half-life, translation efficiency, or decay-factor RBP perturbation.  The $900$ RBP peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries show that an RBP-binding surface exists in the ledger, but they do not identify a same-organism, same-transcript, same-editable-window decay-factor occupancy panel joined to $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels and RNA half-life.  The $6494$ translation rows provide a candidate translation-readout scale, but a translation table alone is not an RNA-decay mechanism.  Thus the current record is best read as a scoped boundary result: finite row surfaces exist around the relevant layers, while the joined panel needed for the cross-layer RBP-decay claim is absent.

\paragraph{Excluded interpretations.}
No translation realization follows from an RNA half-life contrast alone.  No RBP-mediated decay mechanism follows from half-life data unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy changes in the declared window and the perturbation-rescue pattern is observed.  No binding-defective rescue, non-target rescue, RNA-structure-only model, motif-density-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, transcript-abundance model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model counts as positive support for the bridge.  The same restriction applies to the coordinate notation itself: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual geometry is a finite labeling device until an external matched contact supplies the relevant biological readout.  The chapter therefore licenses no protein abundance claim, no folded-structure claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no biological-function claim, no phenotype claim, no adaptation claim, no causation claim, and no universality claim.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
As a standalone BioReality entry, the claim is ready only as a finite negative-boundary and data-requirement statement.  It records that the current ledger has finite hotspot, RBP-binding, and translation-readout surfaces, with concrete row counts and runtimes, but lacks the joined same-window RNA half-life, translation, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows needed for a positive RBP bridge.  The scientifically admissible conclusion is therefore: high- versus low-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ labels remain exchangeable for the bridge claim until a matched finite panel verifies the full occupancy-loss-rescue pattern under the declared controls.
