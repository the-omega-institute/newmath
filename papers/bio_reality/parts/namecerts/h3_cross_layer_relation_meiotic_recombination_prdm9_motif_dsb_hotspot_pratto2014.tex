\subsection{PRDM9 motif and DSB hotspot rows do not instantiate the Q6 RNA half-life RBP panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-prdm9-hotspot-q6-rna-half-life-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite rows actually present.}
The available finite computation for the named record is the meiotic-recombination hotspot contact indexed by the identifier $\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.meiotic\_recombination.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_hotspot\_pratto2014}$. Its verified run reports $52963$ hotspot rows and a runtime of $168.443$ seconds. Thus the finite surface currently available to this chapter is a hotspot-counted recombination panel, not a transcript/CDS panel with synonymous residual-coordinate labels, translation readouts, RNA half-life measurements, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, and perturbation/rescue arms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{hotspot}} &= 52963,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 168.443\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

These two scalars certify that a finite recombination-side artifact was parsed and counted. They do not provide a row set whose coordinates are coding-transcript windows, whose response variable is RNA half-life, or whose mechanism probe is a measured decay-factor RNA-binding protein. The data therefore support only the narrow statement that the PRDM9/hotspot computation reached a concrete finite table size; they do not support the proposed Q6 RNA half-life RBP relation.

\paragraph{Requested carrier and the observed carrier.}
The requested carrier is a matched transcript or CDS table. Each row would need a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life value, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, and controls for transcription rate, abundance, isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and amino-acid sequence. The observed finite record instead consists of meiotic recombination hotspot rows associated with a PRDM9 motif or DSB-hotspot analysis. A hotspot row is a genomic recombination object, whereas the requested row is a coding-RNA stability object. The two row types are not interchangeable under a finite-row audit because their identifiers, molecular layer, response variable, and perturbation requirements differ.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
For the requested Q6 RNA half-life RBP relation, the operative null is that high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life once the same-row translation readout and the listed transcript-level controls are attached. A positive RBP-mediated claim would require the half-life effect to track measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, to collapse under target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and to fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue. None of those finite rows or statistics are present in the hotspot computation. Consequently the current chapter cannot even test the exchangeability null for the requested RNA table; it can only record that the needed RNA/RBP panel is absent from the finite evidence supplied here.

\paragraph{Finite statistic that would be admissible.}
An admissible RNA half-life statistic would have rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i = (&q_i,\ T_i,\ H_i,\ A_i,\ L_i,\ W_i,\ C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the synonymous residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ is the translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance or transcription control information, $L_i$ is measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, $W_i$ records perturbation and rescue state, and $C_i$ collects the declared covariates. A bounded bridge statistic would compare high-coordinate and low-coordinate strata only after conditioning on $T_i$ and $C_i$, and would then check whether the estimated half-life contrast is lost under target-RBP loss and restored only by wild-type rescue. The supplied finite record gives no $q_i$, $T_i$, $H_i$, $L_i$, or $W_i$ values, so the statistic cannot be computed from the present artifact.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The hotspot count does not license a translation claim, an RNA-decay mechanism claim, a protein-output claim, a folding claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a functional claim, a phenotype claim, or a universal biological-law claim. It also does not license the narrower statement that a decay-factor RBP mediates a Q6-coordinate RNA half-life difference, because the necessary same-window RBP occupancy and perturbation/rescue data are missing. PRDM9 motif and DSB-hotspot rows may be biologically meaningful for meiotic recombination, but they are not evidence for a coding-transcript RNA stability mechanism unless a separate finite contact supplies transcript/CDS identifiers, translation readouts, RNA half-life values, RBP occupancy, and rescue controls on the same matched panel.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The record is therefore held at a mismatch boundary. The only cited finite numbers are $52963$ hotspot rows and $168.443$ seconds of runtime for the recombination computation. Those values are concrete, but they belong to the PRDM9/DSB-hotspot layer and do not instantiate the requested Q6 translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP carrier. The chapter remains open pending a finite matched RNA panel with the required coordinates, readouts, controls, occupancy measurements, loss-of-function rows, wild-type rescue rows, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows.
