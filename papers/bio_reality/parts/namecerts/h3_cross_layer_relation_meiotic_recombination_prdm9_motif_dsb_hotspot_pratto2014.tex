\subsection{PRDM9 hotspot rows and the Q6 RBP-decay boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-prdm9-hotspot-q6-rbp-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite empirical row.}
The finite row attached to this record is the PRDM9 motif--double-strand-break hotspot contact from Pratto et al. The parsed run contains $52{,}963$ hotspot rows and completed in $168.443$ seconds. In the present BioReality entry this number is not treated as a generic recombination law. It is used as a scale certificate for a large, finite, joined genomic readout: each row is a bounded observation of a recombination-associated hotspot surface, not an observation of translation, RNA decay, protein state, phenotype, or evolutionary optimality.

A second finite contact in the same cross-layer family records RBP-binding reality through RBNS/eCLIP data: $900$ peak rows were parsed, with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases and runtime $223.648043$ seconds. This RBP-binding contact is relevant because the conjectural biological object is not PRDM9 itself but a Q6 residual-coordinate RNA panel whose positive interpretation would require a measured decay-factor RBP bridge. The distinction is essential. A PRDM9 hotspot table can certify that a motif-conditioned genomic phenomenon has a finite empirical surface, while the RBP table certifies that measured RBP occupancy can be represented as a separate finite contact. Neither table alone supplies the joined RNA half-life, translation-readout, occupancy-loss-rescue panel demanded by the Q6 RNA-decay claim.

\paragraph{Coordinate split.}
Let $q$ denote the high-versus-low $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate after quotienting by amino-acid identity. Let $T$ denote the same-row translation readout, $H$ the RNA half-life measurement, $O$ the measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, and $L$ the loss/rescue perturbation state. The intended finite panel has rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_i=(q_i,T_i,H_i,O_i,L_i,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline controls. The PRDM9 hotspot run supplies a finite hotspot row count, and the RBP-binding run supplies finite occupancy-style row evidence, but the row type above is stricter than either source by itself because all variables must be joined at the same organism, same transcript or CDS, and same editable RNA window.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after the required controls and after conditioning on the same-row translation readout. In symbols, the positive claim is not the bare contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H \mid q=\mathrm{high} \quad \text{versus} \quad H \mid q=\mathrm{low},
\end{aligned}
$$
but a constrained residual contrast that must remain visible only with the prespecified RBP-bridge pattern attached. The observed half-life contrast must follow experimentally decoupled measured local RBP occupancy, collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls. If any of these clauses is absent, the result remains a bounded RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor rather than an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{What the verified numbers permit.}
The hotspot run establishes that the PRDM9 contact is not merely verbal: it has $52{,}963$ finite rows. The RBP-binding run establishes that an occupancy-like contact can be represented with $900$ finite peak rows and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases. Additional linked finite rows set scale for nearby cross-layer surfaces: the methylation-flanking-context run used $20{,}000$ probes across $656$ individuals with baseline $R^2=0.0129735$, while the mTOR translation run used $6{,}494$ rows. These quantities support the paper-level discipline that cross-layer claims must be tied to explicit finite panels, declared controls, and measured readout surfaces. They do not, however, manufacture the missing Q6 RNA half-life bridge.

The required Q6 bridge would need a joined statistic of the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{Q6,RBP}
    &= \operatorname{contrast}(H \mid q,T,Z,O,L) \\
    &\quad - \operatorname{contrast}(H \mid q,T,Z,\text{binding-defective or non-target control}).
\end{aligned}
$$
This expression is only a description of the finite statistic to be computed once the corresponding rows exist. It is not licensed by the PRDM9 hotspot count, the RBP peak count, the methylation probe count, or the mTOR translation row count separately.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded cross-layer target rather than a positive mechanism claim. The PRDM9 hotspot contact gives a concrete recombination-scale row count; the RBP-binding contact gives a concrete finite RBP-binding scale; the RNA half-life contact names the half-life surface; and the translation-efficiency contact names the required same-row translation boundary. A positive Q6 RNA-decay bridge would require all of these roles to be represented in one matched panel with the perturbation and rescue clauses present. In the absence of that joined panel, the coordinate remains a finite descriptor whose association with half-life, if observed, cannot be promoted to an RBP-decay mechanism.

No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No folding, physical admissibility, protein output, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causal mechanism, or universal sequence principle follows from the PRDM9 hotspot rows, from the RBP-binding rows, from the Q6 residual-coordinate geometry, or from the presence of translation and mRNA-control contact names. Those stronger assertions require their own layer-matched finite contacts and their own declared statistics.
