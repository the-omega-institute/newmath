\subsection{Finite matched-row boundary for a PRDM9 hotspot contact and an RNA-half-life RBP bridge}
\label{subsec:bioreality-prdm9-hotspot-q6-rbp-half-life-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite-row BioReality contact, not an open-ended biological law.  The named hotspot measurement supplies a meiotic-recombination layer row set keyed to PRDM9 motif and double-strand-break hotspot evidence.  Its verified finite payload contains $52963$ hotspot rows, with the local run recorded as $168.443$ seconds.  The row count is used only as an externally measured recombination-contact scale: it does not, by itself, produce a statement about coding-sequence residual coordinates, translation, RNA decay, RBP mechanism, protein output, phenotype, or adaptation.

The conjectural bridge under audit is narrower and differently typed.  Its carrier consists of matched transcript or CDS rows carrying a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched perturbation and rescue controls.  The admissible finite rows therefore have the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_i = (&q_i, a_i, T_i, H_i, O_i, L_i, R^{\mathrm{wt}}_i, R^{\mathrm{bd}}_i, C_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, $a_i$ records the amino-acid-controlled coding context, $T_i$ is the mandatory translation readout, $H_i$ is the RNA half-life measurement, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy by the declared decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ is target-RBP loss, $R^{\mathrm{wt}}_i$ and $R^{\mathrm{bd}}_i$ are wild-type and binding-defective rescue arms, $C_i$ contains non-target controls, and $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and abundance controls.

\paragraph{Observed numerical anchors.}
The recombination contact is numerically concrete: the Pratto-style hotspot run contributes $52963$ hotspot entries.  A separate RBP-binding contact in the same finite ledger reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases after its own bounded audit, with a recorded runtime of $223.648043$ seconds.  A translation-readout contact reports $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation dataset, with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  These numbers are not pooled into a single analysis; they are cited to mark the distinct row surfaces that would have to be joined before a positive bridge could be claimed.  The current record has a recombination hotspot scale, an RBP-binding scale, and a translation-readout scale, but it does not yet contain one same-organism, same-window panel in which all required coordinates and controls occur on the same rows.

\paragraph{Tested relation.}
The intended positive relation is conditional.  A high-coordinate versus low-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual contrast may be read as an RNA-half-life RBP bridge only when the half-life effect remains after conditioning on the same-row translation readout and the declared controls, tracks an experimentally decoupled occupancy change of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  In finite-row terms, the bridge requires the same matched panel to support the pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
  q_i &\not\perp H_i \mid T_i, Z_i, \\
  q_i &\not\perp O_i \mid T_i, Z_i, \\
  q_i &\perp H_i \mid T_i, Z_i, L_i, \\
  q_i &\not\perp H_i \mid T_i, Z_i, R^{\mathrm{wt}}_i, \\
  q_i &\perp H_i \mid T_i, Z_i, R^{\mathrm{bd}}_i, C_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
This display is a finite-row decision form, not an assertion that the rows already exist.  The decisive feature is the joint occupancy/loss/rescue structure.  A half-life contrast without measured local RBP occupancy remains an RNA-stability association.  An occupancy contrast without coordinate/occupancy decoupling remains an RBP-binding association.  A loss effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target failure remains a perturbation association.  None of these weaker outcomes is enough to certify the claimed bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life once amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout are fixed.  The null is not defeated by the presence of a PRDM9 hotspot table, by an RBP-binding table, or by a translation table unless the corresponding records are joined into the same finite matched panel with the required controls.  The hotspot count $52963$ is therefore a recombination-layer measurement, while the $900$ RBP peak rows and $6494$ translation rows are separate boundary witnesses for what a future joined panel must actually contain.

\paragraph{Readback and refusal boundary.}
The admissible readback is a bounded finite-panel result: a translation-conditioned RNA half-life effect, a local occupancy change for the declared decay-factor RBP, collapse after target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target controls.  No protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological rule follows from this record.  The PRDM9 hotspot rows do not become an RNA-decay mechanism; the RBP-binding rows do not become a coordinate-mediated decay bridge; the translation rows do not become proof of RNA stability or protein output.  Each stronger biological layer requires its own layer-matched contact and finite statistic on the same relevant rows.
