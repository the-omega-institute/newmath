\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life and the RBP Bridge Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows carrying $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate labels after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The intended row has, on the same biological unit, a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a matched mRNA abundance or transcription control, a local occupancy measurement for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.  The coordinate contrast is not a sequence-law assertion.  It is the finite comparison between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after the amino-acid quotient, evaluated only after the translation readout is attached and conditioned.

\paragraph{Available finite contacts.}
The present evidence table contains a verified meiotic-recombination contact with $52963$ hotspot rows and runtime $168.443$ seconds.  That finite count is useful as a row-scale reality check for the linked BioReality registry, but it is not an RNA-decay or translation-contact measurement.  The same table also contains an RBP-binding contact with $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, computed in runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  This is closer to the required bridge class because it is an RBP occupancy-style surface, but it is still not the same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy/loss/rescue panel required here.  A translation-side finite contact is also present: the mTOR translation readout has $6494$ rows, runtime $9.469$ seconds, and a declared readout source at \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}.  These three finite contacts establish that the registry contains row-bounded surfaces for recombination, RBP binding, and translation, but they do not by themselves join into the same transcript-window matrix needed for the RNA half-life RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Statistic being licensed.}
The only positive statistic licensed by this packet has the form of a finite translation-conditioned half-life contrast.  For rows $i$ carrying coordinate label $q_i$, translation readout $T_i$, RNA half-life $H_i$, transcription and abundance controls $A_i$, local RBP occupancy $O_i$, and perturbation-rescue state $P_i$, the permitted target is a bounded residual comparison of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= E\bigl[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high}, T_i, A_i, O_i, P_i\bigr]
   - E\bigl[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low}, T_i, A_i, O_i, P_i\bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
A nonzero value of $\Delta_H$ is not enough.  The bridge requires the half-life contrast to track experimentally decoupled measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, to collapse after target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and to fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Without that pattern, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null exchanges high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels after conditioning on amino-acid composition, same-row translation readout, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  Under this null, any half-life difference that survives only as a transcript-level association is not promoted to an RBP mechanism.  The RBP contact must be measured at the editable RNA window, not inferred from motif density, RNA structure, or the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate itself.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
This subsection licenses no translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It also licenses no protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological rule.  The $6494$ translation rows show that a translation readout can exist in the finite registry; they do not prove that the RNA half-life rows are translation-conditioned.  The $900$ RBP peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases show that finite RBP-binding surfaces can be represented; they do not supply target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue for this RNA half-life subject.  The $52963$ hotspot count belongs to a distinct meiotic-recombination contact and cannot be reinterpreted as transcript decay evidence.  A positive bridge therefore remains conditional on a single joined panel containing the coordinate, translation, half-life, occupancy, perturbation, and rescue fields on the same finite row family.
