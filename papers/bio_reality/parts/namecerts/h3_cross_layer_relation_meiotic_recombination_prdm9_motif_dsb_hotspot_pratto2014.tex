\subsection{PRDM9 Motif and DSB Hotspot Placement}\label{sec:prdm9-motif-dsb-hotspot-pratto2014}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite hotspot witness.} The Pratto 2014 meiotic recombination record is treated here as a finite hotspot ledger with $n_{\mathrm{hotspots}}=52{,}963$ and runtime $168.443\,\mathrm{s}$. The point of this record is not to inflate a motif scan into a general recombination law, but to pin the statement to a concrete, bounded row set in which PRDM9 motif context and DSB hotspot placement co-occur. The empirical witness is therefore discrete: it is a count of hotspot rows, not a claim about every meiotic context.

$$\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{hotspots}} &= 52{,}963,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 168.443\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}$$

\paragraph{Assay separation.} The broader evidence ledger keeps different layers apart. One neighboring panel reports methylation variation over $20{,}000$ probes in $656$ individuals with baseline $r^2=0.0129735$; another reports translation efficiency over $6{,}494$ coding rows; another records RNA-binding signal with $900$ peaks and only $3$ cross-RBP concordant peaks. These figures are useful here only as a reminder that a DNA hotspot count should not be read as transcript abundance, translation output, or RNA-binding support. Each panel lives on its own observable surface and must be interpreted by its own statistic.

\paragraph{Local reading and limit.} The admissible reading of this chapter is narrow: PRDM9-associated hotspot structure is visible in a finite meiotic panel of $52{,}963$ hotspots, and the recorded runtime is $168.443\,\mathrm{s}$. From that finite witness alone, no conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological rule is licensed. Those claims would require a separate contact at the relevant layer, with matched rows and a layer-specific statistic. Here the claim stops at the DNA-scale hotspot boundary.
