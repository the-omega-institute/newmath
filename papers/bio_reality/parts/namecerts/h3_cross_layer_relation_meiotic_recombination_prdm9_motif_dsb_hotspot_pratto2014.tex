\subsection{A translation-conditioned RNA half-life bridge with an RBP perturbation gate}\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows carrying a $B^\ast_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance controls, and a separately measured decay-factor RBP surface at the editable RNA window. The intended biological object is not a protein-output claim and not a general sequence law. It is a bounded cross-layer question: after amino-acid quotienting, do high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels separate RNA half-life only in the presence of an experimentally decoupled decay-factor RBP bridge?

The row shape can be written as a finite table with entries
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_i = (&q_i, T_i, H_i, A_i, O_i, L_i, W_i, B_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after amino-acid control, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $W_i$ records wild-type rescue, $B_i$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue control, and $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline covariates. A positive bridge is allowed only if the half-life contrast is translation-conditioned and follows the measured RBP intervention pattern.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The available finite anchors are sufficient to state the boundary, but they do not by themselves close the RNA half-life RBP mechanism. The meiotic-recombination run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.meiotic\_recombination.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_hotspot\_pratto2014} contains $52963$ hotspot rows and completed in $168.443$ seconds. This number is useful here only as a checked finite-row precedent for motif-to-event testing: a large row count does not license transfer from DNA hotspot structure to an RNA decay mechanism. The RBP-contact run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} reports $900$ total peak rows, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and $223.648043$ seconds of runtime. This anchor shows that RBP occupancy and binding evidence can be represented as finite contact rows, but it is not the same as a same-window decay-factor RBP loss-and-rescue panel for this conjecture. The translation-readout anchor \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} reports $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds, with a readout source recorded at the GEO raw archive. It supplies a finite translation-readout precedent, not an RNA half-life mechanism.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
For this chapter the admissible statistic is a finite contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$. The bridge condition is stricter than an association test:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_O(q \mid Z) \text{ is present at the same editable RNA window},\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,L=\mathrm{target\ loss}) \text{ collapses},\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,W=\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \text{ returns},\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,B=\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target}) \text{ does not return}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A row set that satisfies only the first line is an RNA half-life association. A row set that satisfies the first line together with the same-row translation boundary is a translation-conditioned RNA half-life association. It becomes an RBP bridge only when measured local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls jointly satisfy the intervention pattern. The RBP cannot be chosen after inspecting the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ geometry; it must be externally prespecified or otherwise fixed before the bridge test is interpreted.

\paragraph{Matched null.}
The local null states that, after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout, high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. If half-life differs while measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy is unchanged, the result is not an RBP bridge. If occupancy differs but target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life contrast, the result is not a decay-factor bridge. If loss collapses the contrast but wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or if binding-defective or non-target rescue restores it, the result is not the claimed coordinate-to-RBP-to-decay relation.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no translation realization from RNA half-life alone. It licenses no protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality unless a separate finite contact at that layer supplies the corresponding measurement and statistic. Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not an RNA half-life readout. Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency is a same-row translation boundary, not an RBP occupancy assay and not a decay perturbation. RBP binding rows are occupancy or affinity evidence only when they match the same organism, same window, same transcript or CDS key, and prespecified decay-factor RBP required by the test. The current finite record therefore supports a disciplined boundary formulation: the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ label may be tested against translation-conditioned RNA half-life, but a positive RBP-bridge conclusion requires the full occupancy, loss, rescue, and negative-control panel on the same finite row universe.
