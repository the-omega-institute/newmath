\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows carrying $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate labels after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  Each eligible row must keep four data surfaces separate: a coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation surface.  The intended comparison is not a free association between codon geometry and RNA stability.  It is a bounded cross-layer claim whose positive form requires that a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual contrast survive the ordinary sequence and expression controls, remain conditioned on translation readout, and then follow the experimentally separated RBP bridge.

The finite-row null is exchangeability of the high- and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life once amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been attached.  In symbols, for matched rows $i$ with coordinate class $Q_i$, translation readout $T_i$, RNA half-life $H_i$, and controls $Z_i$, the bounded statistic must be read only after the conditioning variables are present:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i &\perp Q_i \mid (T_i,Z_i) \quad \text{under the matched null},\\
  Z_i &= \{\text{amino acid, transcription, RNA structure, motif density, composition, codon context, isoform, abundance, batch}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive RNA half-life residual without the RBP evidence below remains an RNA-stability association, not a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{External finite contacts.}
The available finite evidence is uneven across layers.  The meiotic-recombination contact identified by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.meiotic\_recombination.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_hotspot\_pratto2014} has a verified run \texttt{e57a09da690c} with $52{,}963$ hotspot rows and runtime $168.443$ seconds.  That row count is useful as a finite biological-contact calibration point: it shows that the BioReality table can bind a large, externally measured hotspot surface to a named cross-layer record.  It does not, however, contain RNA half-life, translation efficiency, decay-factor RBP occupancy, or rescue rows for this conjecture.

A nearer RBP-side contact is \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}, verified by run \texttt{b225890251c2}, with $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  This supplies a finite reminder that RBP binding evidence can be represented as measured rows rather than as sequence-motif speculation.  It is still not sufficient for the present bridge, because the required RBP must be a prespecified decay factor measured at the editable RNA window and then tested by loss and rescue.  A translation-side contact, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012}, has run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} with $6{,}494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds, giving an example of a finite translation-readout table.  It does not by itself supply the same transcript windows, half-life rows, or RBP perturbation rows required here.

\paragraph{Bridge rule.}
For this chapter, an RBP bridge is licensed only when the same matched panel contains all of the following data on the same organism and window system: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance or transcription controls, RNA half-life, measured local occupancy of a declared decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type target-RBP rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.  The intended positive pattern is finite and falsifiable:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H(Q \mid T,Z) &\neq 0,\\
  \Delta_{\mathrm{occ}}(Q \mid Z) &\text{ tracks the editable window},\\
  \Delta_H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{RBP\ loss}) &\to 0,\\
  \Delta_H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{WT\ rescue}) &\to \Delta_H(Q \mid T,Z),\\
  \Delta_H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target}) &\not\to \Delta_H(Q \mid T,Z).
\end{aligned}
$$
The bridge fails if the local RBP occupancy is unchanged, if RBP loss leaves the half-life effect intact, if wild-type rescue does not restore the effect, or if a binding-defective or non-target control restores it.  It also fails if the apparent signal is absorbed by RNA structure, motif density, transcript abundance, isoform state, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, amino-acid composition, batch, or a simpler codon baseline.

\paragraph{Readback boundary.}
The present record is therefore ready as a bounded chapter only in the following sense: it states the finite matched-row carrier, the null, the admissible RBP-bridge pattern, and the local rejection conditions.  The verified PRDM9 hotspot table, the RBP-binding table, and the mTOR translation table are cited as finite contact precedents with concrete row counts, not as evidence that this particular $B^*_{Q6}$ RNA half-life bridge has crossed its boundary.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA half-life contrast becomes a decay-factor RBP mechanism without measured local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate contrast, from translation conditioning, from an RBP contact name, or from the hotspot row count.  Each of those claims would require its own matched contact and its own finite statistic.
