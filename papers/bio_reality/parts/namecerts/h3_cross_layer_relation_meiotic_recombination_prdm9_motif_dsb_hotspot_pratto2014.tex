\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With a Decay-Factor RBP Bridge}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record considered here is the finite panel named \texttt{q6.translation-conditioned.rna-half-life-rbp-bridge.cross-layer}.  Its rows are matched transcript or coding-sequence entries carrying synonymous $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls.  The intended biological object is not a free-standing codon-usage law.  It is a bounded relation among four finite row coordinates: a synonymous residual label, a translation readout on the same row, an RNA half-life residual conditioned on that translation readout, and an experimentally separated RBP occupancy or perturbation surface.

The finite evidence ledger currently contains a meiotic-recombination contact under the identifier \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.meiotic\_recombination.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_hotspot\_pratto2014}, with $52963$ hotspot rows and runtime $168.443$ seconds.  That contact is useful as a sanity check on the BioReality row discipline: it shows that the system can bind a cross-layer biological claim to a concrete row count and a recorded run, but it is not an RNA half-life or decay-factor RBP panel.  The same ledger also contains an RBP-binding contact under \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}, with $900$ total peak rows, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  Those rows are closer to the RBP side of the present conjecture, but they still do not by themselves provide same-window decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, and RNA half-life on the same transcript rows.  For the translation boundary, the mTOR translation contact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} records $6494$ rows with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  A separate transcription-kinetics contact records $1176$ rows, $1137$ distinct entries, and runtime $79.75938606262207$ seconds.  These numbers are finite row anchors, not automatic support for the present RBP-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Row relation.}
A positive row is allowed only when the same transcript or coding-sequence row carries all required surfaces.  Let $q_i$ denote the high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life residual after the declared controls, and let $O_i$ denote measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  The intended finite statistic has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  q_i &\longmapsto T_i,\\
  (q_i,T_i,Z_i) &\longmapsto H_i,\\
  (q_i,T_i,Z_i) &\longmapsto O_i,\\
  (q_i,T_i,Z_i,O_i,\Delta_i^{\mathrm{loss}},\Delta_i^{\mathrm{rescue}}) &\longmapsto B_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ contains transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and amino-acid composition.  The bridge variable $B_i$ is admissible only when the half-life effect tracks experimentally separated local RBP occupancy, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.

This definition deliberately separates association from mechanism.  A difference in $H_i$ between high- and low-coordinate rows is only an RNA-stability association unless the RBP rows are present and behave in the required direction.  A difference in $O_i$ is only an occupancy association unless it is experimentally decoupled from the coordinate, connected to the same half-life window, and tested through loss and rescue.  A translation readout $T_i$ is only a conditioning boundary; it does not by itself make the half-life effect translationally realized, causal, or mechanistic.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the full control list and the mandatory same-row translation readout are attached.  In symbols, the bridge is not licensed unless a finite panel rejects the exchangeability claim in the half-life surface and simultaneously satisfies the RBP perturbation pattern:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i \mid (q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i) &\sim H_i \mid (q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i),\\
  O_i \text{ unchanged} &\Rightarrow \text{no RBP bridge},\\
  \Delta_i^{\mathrm{loss}} \text{ noncollapsing} &\Rightarrow \text{no RBP bridge},\\
  \Delta_i^{\mathrm{wildtype\ rescue}} \text{ nonrestoring} &\Rightarrow \text{no RBP bridge},\\
  \Delta_i^{\mathrm{defective\ or\ non target\ rescue}} \text{ restoring} &\Rightarrow \text{no RBP bridge}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row rule is asymmetric.  Missing RBP occupancy or missing perturbation data does not make the hypothesis false; it keeps the claim below the RBP-bridge level.  Conversely, an RNA half-life association that survives many covariates but lacks measured occupancy and rescue remains a bounded RNA-stability readout.

\paragraph{Contacts currently attached.}
The attached RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with externally curated half-life estimates or normalized consensus readouts.  It can support a half-life boundary at per-gene or per-transcript resolution, but its stated noise includes platform effects, growth condition, cell type, transcript-isoform ambiguity, identifier mapping loss, normalization differences, and half-life estimation method.  It does not supply same-window RBP occupancy or perturbation/rescue rows.  The matched mRNA-abundance contact supplies a transcription-layer denominator for translation-efficiency residualization, not an RNA half-life measurement and not a decay mechanism.  The ribosome-profiling contact supplies a per-CDS translation-efficiency or ribosome-occupancy boundary, not an RBP perturbation experiment.

The closest finite row counts in the current ledger therefore remain separated by layer.  The PRDM9 hotspot run has $52963$ hotspot rows, but it belongs to meiotic recombination rather than RNA decay.  The RBP-binding run has $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but it is not joined to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, same-row translation readout, and RNA half-life panel required here.  The mTOR translation run has $6494$ rows, but it does not contain the RNA half-life and target-RBP loss/rescue columns.  These finite values are useful evidence of available row-structured contacts; they do not yet form the joined matrix required for a positive RBP-decay conclusion.

\paragraph{Acceptance rule.}
A ready panel for this conjecture must contain, on the same finite row system, the tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
  (\mathrm{transcript}_i,\mathrm{CDS}_i,q_i,T_i,A_i,H_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,D_i,N_i,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, $L_i$ is target-RBP loss, $W_i$ is wild-type rescue, $D_i$ is binding-defective rescue, $N_i$ is a non-target control, and $Z_i$ is the declared control vector.  The bridge is accepted only if the coordinate-associated half-life residual is translation-conditioned, locally accompanied by the prespecified RBP occupancy change, collapsed by target-RBP loss, restored by wild-type rescue, and not restored by binding-defective or non-target controls.  The rank of the finite design must remain sufficient after controls; otherwise the apparent bridge is treated as an unresolved association.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no protein-output claim, no folding claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no biological-function claim, no phenotype claim, no adaptation claim, no causal law, and no universal biological law.  It also does not allow codon or window geometry to choose an RBP after the fact.  The RBP must be prespecified by an external occupancy and perturbation route.  Translation is used only as a same-row conditioning readout, and RNA half-life is used only as a bounded RNA-stability surface.  If the finite panel lacks measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, the coordinate remains a descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA half-life association under controls.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The record is ready as a standalone BioReality boundary chapter because it gives a precise finite-row object, a local null, a concrete acceptance rule, and a self-contained refusal to promote separated contacts into mechanism.  The current verified rows include $52963$ PRDM9 hotspot rows, $900$ RBP-binding peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, $6494$ mTOR translation rows, and $1176$ transcription-kinetics rows with $1137$ distinct entries.  Those scalars are cited as finite contacts already present in the ledger.  The positive RNA half-life RBP bridge itself remains conditional on a future joined same-row panel containing all required translation, abundance, half-life, occupancy, loss, rescue, defective-rescue, and non-target-control columns.
