\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as an RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{subsec:bior-reality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about synonymous residual coordinate windows in coding transcripts. Its carrier consists of transcript or CDS rows labelled by $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, with same-row translation readouts, RNA half-life estimates, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, matched transcript-abundance controls, and perturbation or rescue controls. The intended readback is not a statement about codon geometry by itself. It is the finite comparison of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after conditioning on the translation readout and on transcriptional, abundance, sequence-composition, isoform, and batch covariates.

\paragraph{Available finite facts.}
The current verified ledger contains a directly measured meiotic-recombination entry for the claim identifier named in the packet: run \texttt{e57a09da690c} reports $52963$ hotspot rows and runtime $168.443$ seconds for the PRDM9-motif DSB-hotspot surface. That scalar fact is a useful row-count anchor for the broader cross-layer catalogue, but it is not a same-transcript RNA half-life or RBP-perturbation matrix. The closest RBP-side verified contact in the present finite ledger is \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}, run \texttt{b225890251c2}, with $900$ total peak rows, $3$ cross-RBP concordant calls, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds. The translation-side catalogue also contains \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012}, run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, with $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds. These numbers show that finite RBP-binding and translation-readout surfaces exist in the project ledger, but they do not yet form the required same-organism, same-window RNA-half-life perturbation panel for this conjecture.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
A positive bridge would require a finite table in which every tested window carries the tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i,\,T_i,\,H_i,\,O_i,\,L_i,\,R_i^{\mathrm{WT}},\,R_i^{\mathrm{def}},\,C_i,\,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $R_i^{\mathrm{WT}}$ records wild-type rescue, $R_i^{\mathrm{def}}$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue, $C_i$ records matched transcript abundance or transcription-rate controls, and $Z_i$ collects local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler-baseline controls. The admissible finite statistic is therefore not merely $q_i \mapsto H_i$. It is the residual half-life contrast that remains after translation conditioning and then follows the RBP-specific intervention pattern.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life once the mandatory covariates have been attached:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, C_i, Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
Breaking this null is still insufficient for the RBP bridge. A half-life contrast that survives translation conditioning but lacks local occupancy change remains a bounded RNA-stability association. A contrast accompanied by occupancy but not by target-RBP loss collapse remains an occupancy association rather than a decay mechanism. A contrast that collapses after loss but is not restored by wild-type rescue, or is restored by binding-defective or non-target rescue, fails the specific RBP interpretation. The bridge is licensed only by the joint finite pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q,T,C,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta O(q,T,C,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta H \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss},\\
&\Delta H \text{ returns under wild-type rescue},\\
&\Delta H \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a deliberately narrow rule: it prevents an RNA half-life readout, an RBP-binding readout, or a translation readout from being promoted across layers unless they are joined row by row and pass the stated perturbation pattern.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the present packet.}
On the finite evidence supplied here, the packet is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter but not as a positive RBP-mediated RNA-decay result. The row count $52963$ for the PRDM9 hotspot surface belongs to a different biological measurement than transcript half-life. The $900$ RBP-binding peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant calls establish a finite RBP-binding surface elsewhere in the ledger, but they do not provide same-window decay-factor occupancy for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA half-life rows. The $6494$ translation rows show an available translation-readout scale, but they do not by themselves compute the joined $q_i,T_i,H_i,O_i$ statistic. Thus the present state is a boundary record: the conjecture specifies the exact finite panel that would be needed, and the current data show why the existing contacts are not enough.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA-decay mechanism follows from a translation-conditioned half-life difference unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are attached to the same finite rows. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, general mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry, from codon-window notation, from RBP peak catalogues, from transcript abundance controls, or from translation-readout names. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite statistics.
