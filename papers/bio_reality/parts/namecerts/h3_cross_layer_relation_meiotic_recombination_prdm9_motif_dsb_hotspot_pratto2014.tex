\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life and the RBP-Occupancy Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record concerns a finite matched-panel question about synonymous residual coordinate windows in coding transcripts.  The coordinate side is the amino-acid-quotiented $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ label: two coding rows may encode the same amino-acid sequence while occupying different residual coordinate windows.  The biological readout side is deliberately narrower than a protein-output claim.  A row is admissible only when it carries, on the same transcript or coding-sequence key, a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA or transcription controls, and an independently specified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation panel.  The intended bridge is therefore not ``coordinate implies decay''.  It is the finite statement that a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be called an RBP-linked cross-layer readout only when measured local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls all cohere on the same finite row family.

The available finite facts show why this chapter must be phrased as a bounded boundary record rather than as a positive RNA-decay mechanism.  The recombination hotspot packet associated with the present claim identifier has $52963$ hotspot rows and a recorded runtime of $168.443$ seconds, but those rows are meiotic hotspot rows, not matched transcript/CDS rows with RNA half-life, translation, and RBP perturbation fields.  A separate RBP-binding fact records $900$ total peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases and runtime $223.648043$ seconds; this is evidence that finite RBP readout tables can be represented, not that the required decay-factor RBP is measured at the same editable RNA window as the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate and half-life residual.  A translation-side fact gives $6494$ mTOR translation rows with runtime $9.469$ seconds, and a stoichiometry-side RNA methylation fact gives $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites, cross-condition concordance marked true, coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$, and runtime $78.316$ seconds.  These facts supply concrete examples of finite biological surfaces in the surrounding dossier, but none is the joined same-row panel required for the present RNA half-life RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Row predicate.}
Let $i$ range over the finite transcript or CDS rows of a candidate panel.  A row may enter the bridge statistic only if the following fields are all present: a residual coordinate label $q_i \in B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, a same-row translation readout $T_i$, an RNA half-life value $H_i$, a transcription or abundance control vector $A_i$, local RNA-structure and composition controls $C_i$, a measured local occupancy value $O_i$ for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a target-RBP loss condition $L_i$, a wild-type rescue condition $W_i$, and a binding-defective or non-target control condition $D_i$.  The finite comparison is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after amino-acid quotienting, not between unrelated amino-acid products.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{admit}}
= \{i :&\ q_i,T_i,H_i,A_i,C_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,D_i\ \text{are observed on the same row key}\\
&\ \text{and the coding rows remain matched after amino-acid quotienting}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The null model is row exchangeability after the declared controls.  In words, once amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout are conditioned on, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels must be treated as exchangeable for RNA half-life unless the prespecified RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern is also present.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid (T_i,A_i,C_i,\mathrm{AA}_i,\mathrm{batch}_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

is therefore the local null schematic.  A deviation from this schematic is still only an RNA half-life association unless the occupancy and rescue clauses also pass.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
A positive finite bridge requires four linked signs on the same panel.  First, the half-life residual must be translation-conditioned; the coordinate effect may not be read from half-life alone.  Second, local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must change in the editable RNA window in a way that is experimentally decoupled from the coordinate label.  Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must return the effect, while a binding-defective rescue or non-target control must fail to return it.  A compact way to describe the admissible pattern is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q\mid T,A,C) &\ne 0,\\
\Delta_O(q\mid T,A,C) &\ne 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{target\ loss}}(q\mid T,A,C) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ rescue}}(q\mid T,A,C) &\approx \Delta_H(q\mid T,A,C),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{defective/non\ target}}(q\mid T,A,C) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The formula is only a bookkeeping display for the finite-row gate.  It does not license a parametric model, a universal law, or an inference outside the measured panel.  The words ``approximately zero'' and ``returns'' must be fixed by the declared statistic, rank sufficiency, and tolerance before any claim is made.

\paragraph{Use of the verified finite facts.}
The $52963$ PRDM9-associated hotspot rows are useful here as a warning about layer specificity.  They are a large finite genetic-contact table, but they are not an RNA half-life table and do not contain the same-row translation and decay-factor RBP perturbation columns needed for this chapter.  The $900$ RBP peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases likewise show that RBP occupancy surfaces are finite and auditable, but a generic peak table does not become a decay-factor RBP bridge without same-window matching and perturbation/rescue readout.  The $6494$ translation rows from the mTOR translation fact can play the role of a translation-readout precedent only in the abstract sense that translation surfaces can be stored as finite rows.  They do not, by themselves, attach RNA half-life or RBP rescue behavior.  The $60000$-row RNA methylation stoichiometry fact, with $49668$ distinct sites and a measured coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$, is another finite cross-layer surface, but it lies on a different molecular modification axis.  These facts therefore support the conservative design of the chapter: finite biological contacts must not be silently substituted across layers.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This record permits at most a bounded RNA-stability association, and only after the joined statistic exists, a bounded RBP bridge.  It does not show translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It does not show protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  It also does not allow a motif-density-only, RNA-structure-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair-context, or batch explanation to be counted as support for the RBP bridge.  The decisive distinction is local: half-life, translation, occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and defective or non-target rescue must be observed together on the finite matched rows.  If any one of those components is missing, the coordinate remains a descriptive residual label or, at most, a finite RNA half-life association under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Status of the present packet.}
The present data are ready for inclusion as a negative boundary and finite-row specification.  They are not ready as a positive RBP-mediated RNA-decay result.  The exact missing object is a same-organism, same-window transcript/CDS panel containing $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA or transcription controls, RNA half-life, measured occupancy for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, local RNA-structure controls, composition controls, splice or isoform diagnostics, batch fields, and a declared finite statistic.  Until that object exists, the mathematically correct chapter conclusion is a boundary statement: the bridge condition is specified, the null is explicit, and the available verified row counts do not cross the RNA half-life RBP threshold.
