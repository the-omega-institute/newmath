\subsection{Modeled tAI Survival for B*\_Q6 Residual Coordinates}\label{subsec:b-star-q6-modeled-tai-survival}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} The record is a finite mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$. The first coordinate $q_i$ ranges over the nine computed B*\_Q6 synonymous residual coordinates
$\{\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\mathrm{f3\_stress}\}$. The second coordinate is a bounded translation readout, here modeled tAI codon weight $w_{\mathrm{codon}}$ computed from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights. The third coordinate is reserved for matched protein abundance when the row is extended to a downstream protein readout. The codon count space has $61$ sense codons. The finite organism panel for the modeled-tAI calculation has $3$ organisms: Escherichia coli K12, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.

\paragraph{Modeled translation contact.} The tRNA payload contains all $20$ standard amino-acid families in each modeled organism. The E. coli K12 payload has $89$ FASTA headers, with $87$ matched and $2$ unmatched headers. The human payload has $432$ headers and all $432$ are matched. The yeast payload has $275$ headers and all $275$ are matched. In each organism the modeled tAI readout covers all $61$ sense codons. The zero-raw-weight fallback count is $0$ for E. coli K12, $0$ for Homo sapiens, and $0$ for S. cerevisiae in the codon-level modeled-tAI computation. These numbers certify that the modeled readout is finite and complete at the codon-weight level for this panel; they do not turn the readout into observed ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Survival matrix statistic.} The bounded coupling threshold is $0.01$. The modeled-tAI survival matrix has $81$ entries above this threshold, with
$$\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|S_{ij}| &= 3801961.031607896,\\
\max_i |S_i| &= 3801961.031607896.
\end{aligned}$$
The recorded break condition is not met for the bounded modeled-tAI contact: the finite modeled matrix is not empty above the contact threshold. This is a positive modeled-coupling result, not a powered cross-species survival law. The same finite row explicitly states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival if the requested claim is population-level survival with at least $40$ matched species. Thus the correct readback is: B*\_Q6 has nonzero modeled-tAI bounded coupling in this finite panel, while powered translation survival remains outside the supported scope.

\paragraph{Matched protein-abundance extension.} A separate downstream matrix joins the same nine B*\_Q6 residual coordinates to per-protein modeled tAI and measured protein abundance. The readout $T$ is per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac{\sum_c n_c w_c}{\sum_c n_c},
\end{aligned}$$
where $n_c$ is the codon count for codon $c$ in the CDS and $w_c$ is the modeled tAI weight. The readout $Y$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts. The joined row counts are $3739$ proteins for E. coli K12 MG1655, $4129$ proteins for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155, and $5815$ proteins for S. cerevisiae. The per-protein tAI construction uses all-tRNA copy totals $90$, $49$, and $275$ respectively, with zero-raw-weight entries filled by geometric mean in $1$, $1$, and $0$ cases respectively.

\paragraph{Controlled ranks and residual dimensions.} The downstream decomposition residualizes against a control block containing amino-acid composition, organism-scoped covariates, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched transcript control where present. The control column count is $24$ in each organism and the residualized control rank is $23$. The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $3716$ for E. coli K12 MG1655, $4106$ for M. smegmatis, and $5792$ for S. cerevisiae. In all three organisms the residual rank of the nine-dimensional $X_Q$ block is $9$, the rank of the translation residual $T$ is $1$, and the joint rank of $(X_Q,T)$ is $10$. The rank condition is therefore sufficient for the fitted finite decomposition in each organism.

\paragraph{Mediation readback.} The fitted mediation scalar is reported only as a statistical decomposition of a residual-to-protein association through modeled tAI. For E. coli K12 MG1655, the total $Q \to P$ residual $R^2$ is $0.10767526677985896$, the direct $Q \to P$ residual $R^2$ after conditioning on $T$ is $0.04167869969498872$, and the indirect difference is $0.06599656708487024$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$. For M. smegmatis, the corresponding values are $0.03209009170649934$, $0.029395293228368185$, $0.0026947984781311544$, and $M^{QTP}=0.08397602919861291$. For S. cerevisiae, the values are $0.29837624994140827$, $0.0151709093608293$, $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$. All three mediation fractions lie in the unit interval. The strongest finite mediated fraction is therefore yeast, followed by E. coli, with M. smegmatis much smaller.

\paragraph{Energy audit.} The controlled energy fields give the local scale of each fit. For E. coli K12 MG1655, $T$ explains $582.5912438239341$ units of residual protein-abundance energy, while $X_Q$ explains $432.89050065533684$ on $Y$ and $0.256128714706293$ on $T$; the direct improvement from $X_Q$ after $T$ is $167.56227978068944$. For M. smegmatis, the corresponding quantities are $15.813547327443837$, $183.85037813566998$, $0.026187928294862578$, and $168.41135341317295$. For S. cerevisiae, they are $941.8858618613751$, $882.6526210803958$, $5.671703404506426$, and $44.87838061554362$. These finite values support a modeled decomposition but do not identify a causal effect.

\paragraph{Local boundary.} A direct $q_i \to p_k$ association is not admitted as protein realization unless the same residual coordinate first survives through a translation readout $q_i \to t_j$ under the stated controls. The present chapter therefore supports bounded modeled-tAI coupling and a controlled statistical protein-abundance decomposition through modeled tAI. It does not establish observed translation dynamics, ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbation mechanism, organismal phenotype, or a universal biological law. Those claims require separate layer-matched measurements and a new finite statistic at the corresponding layer.
