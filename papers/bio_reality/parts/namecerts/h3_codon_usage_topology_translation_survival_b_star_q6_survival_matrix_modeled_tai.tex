These numbers justify using the modeled tAI table as a finite translation-layer contact, while also fixing its scope: it is an organism-scoped codon-adaptation annotation derived from tRNA gene-copy information, not a direct observation of ribosome movement.

The appropriate readback is therefore a nonzero modeled-tAI coupling matrix under a small organism panel, not ribosome-profiling validation and not proteomics validation.

\paragraph{Mediation discipline.}
The finite row order is directional.  A residual coordinate is not allowed to support a protein-abundance statement by jumping directly from $q_i$ to $p_k$.  The admissible route is first $q_i\to t_j$ and only then, if matched protein rows exist, $q_i\to t_j\to p_k$.  In this run the available finite evidence reaches the modeled translation coordinate $t_j$ only.  It does not attach organism-matched protein-abundance rows, and it does not attach matched mRNA rows sufficient for a controlled translation-efficiency residual.  Consequently a direct residual-to-protein association, even if later observed in a separate table, would not count as a mediated protein-realization entry unless the corresponding $q_i\to t_j$ step survived under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Controls and separated coordinates.}
Amino-acid sequence, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the M-density baseline remain controls.  They are not promoted into new residual coordinates in this chapter.  Matched mRNA abundance is also treated as a control needed to separate transcription-layer variation from translation-layer variation.  A transcription-only edge is therefore different from a mediated translation edge: mRNA abundance may explain expression differences, but it does not by itself supply ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein abundance.  The modeled tAI table likewise remains separate from measured ribosome profiling.  It gives a codon-adaptation weight readback, whereas ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency would require per-CDS RPF and mRNA rows from matched experiments.
