\subsection{Modeled tAI survival boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{subsec:bioreality-bstar-q6-modeled-tai-survival-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The finite surface considered here has triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$, where $q_i$ ranges over nine synonymous residual coordinates, $t_j$ is a translation-layer readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout when such a downstream row is explicitly joined.  The modeled-tAI survival run used the coordinate list
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The codon-count ambient space had $61$ sense codons.  The readout $t_j$ was not a ribosome-footprint measurement: it was a modeled codon weight $w_c$ computed from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and the dos Reis wobble $s$-values.  The organism panel had only $3$ organisms, namely \textit{Escherichia coli} K12, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Their local tRNA payloads covered all $20$ standard amino-acid families; the FASTA header summaries were $89$ total and $87$ matched for \textit{E. coli} K12, $432$ matched out of $432$ for human, and $275$ matched out of $275$ for yeast.  The modeled-tAI computation produced $61$ sense-codon weights in each organism, with zero raw-$W$ fallback count equal to $0$ in all three organisms.

\paragraph{Observed bounded coupling.}
The modeled matrix was nonzero in the bounded-contact sense.  With coupling threshold $0.01$, there were $81$ entries above threshold, and the largest absolute entry was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j} |S_{ij}| = 3801961.031607896.
\end{aligned}
$$
The maximum absolute diagonal entry had the same value, $3801961.031607896$.  These numbers show that the finite modeled-tAI surface is not empty: the residual coordinates can be numerically coupled to the modeled codon-adaptation readout.  The important qualification is that this is a bounded modeled readout.  It is not yet an observed ribosome-occupancy statistic, not a translation-efficiency statistic derived from $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$, and not a protein-output experiment.

\paragraph{Rank and power boundary.}
The run did not satisfy the powered survival condition.  The recorded break condition says that additional data are needed if the finite modeled-tAI matrix has no $|S_{ij}|$ above the bounded-contact threshold, or if powered survival is requested without at least $40$ matched species.  The first alternative is not met, since $81$ entries exceed $0.01$.  The second limitation remains binding: the organism count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{organism}} = 3 < 40.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the correct mathematical status is modeled translation-layer contact under insufficient organism power.  The row supports a finite $Q_i \to T_j$ modeled-coupling statement, but it does not support a population-level translation-survival law.

\paragraph{Downstream protein-abundance comparison.}
A separate per-protein modeled-tAI/protein-abundance analysis joined codon counts, modeled tAI, and PAXdb protein abundance across $12$ organisms.  Its joined row counts were $4042$ for \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, $11661$ for \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for zebrafish, $13295$ for fruit fly, $3739$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for chicken, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  In that analysis the joint nine-dimensional $Q$ block was decomposed against a modeled per-protein tAI readout $T$ and log protein abundance $Y$.  The reported mediated fractions included $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.6084594553474505$ in \textit{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  Those numbers are useful as a downstream matrix audit, but their own source states that the decomposition is statistical and that modeled tAI from gene-copy counts is not ribosome profiling.  Consequently they do not repair the three-organism power deficit of the modeled-tAI survival row.

\paragraph{Matched controls.}
The control set stays outside the residual coordinate carrier.  Amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline are controls, not promoted coordinates.  In the yeast structure-mediation row, the five-way join had $4759$ joined genes and used $24$ control columns including an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  That row reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values reinforce the same discipline: conditioning variables may attenuate or partition residual association, but they do not transform modeled codon-adaptation weights into a measured mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The supported statement is therefore narrow.  The nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates have a nonempty finite modeled-tAI coupling surface over three organisms, with $81$ threshold-exceeding entries and complete standard amino-acid-family coverage in the local tRNA payloads.  The same record refuses powered translation survival because $3$ organisms are far below the declared $40$-species threshold.  No translation realization, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, mechanism claim, organismal phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter.  Any such stronger assertion requires its own layer-matched contact: observed ribosome profiling or measured TE for translation, matched proteomics for abundance, and separate structure, physical, functional, perturbational, or phenotype assays for higher layers.
