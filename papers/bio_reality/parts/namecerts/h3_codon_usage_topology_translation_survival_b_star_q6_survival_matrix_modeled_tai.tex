\subsection{Modeled tAI Contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Translation Boundary}
\label{sec:bior-tai-contact-bstar-q6-translation-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The finite object in this chapter is the row set obtained by pairing the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates with organism-scoped modeled tAI readouts.  The residual coordinates are
$\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$.  The codon space contains $61$ sense codons, and the modeled translation weights are computed from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts with dos Reis wobble $s$-values.  The organism panel is deliberately small: \emph{Escherichia coli} K12, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \emph{Homo sapiens}, so the panel has $n=3$ organisms.  The chapter therefore records bounded modeled contact, not a powered measured-translation result.

\paragraph{tRNA-family coverage and modeled weights.}
The local GtRNAdb payload covers all $20$ standard amino-acid families in each of the three organisms.  In \emph{E. coli} K12 there are $89$ FASTA headers, of which $87$ match the standard parsing route and $2$ remain unmatched; in human there are $432$ headers and all $432$ match; in yeast there are $275$ headers and all $275$ match.  For the modeled tAI readout, each organism contributes all $61$ sense codons with zero raw-weight fallback count equal to $0$.  Thus the contact is finite and codon-complete for the three organisms actually used, even though it is not species-powered.

\paragraph{Survival matrix status.}
With coupling threshold $0.01$, the modeled matrix contains $81$ entries above threshold.  The maximum absolute modeled entry is $3{,}801{,}961.031607896$, and the maximum absolute diagonal entry is the same number, $3{,}801{,}961.031607896$.  These large modeled magnitudes should be read as bounded coupling under the tAI construction, not as observed ribosome occupancy.  The registered break condition says that the matrix would require more data if the finite modeled-tAI matrix had no absolute entry above the bounded-contact threshold, or if powered survival were requested without at least $40$ matched species.  The first branch is not met, because $81$ entries exceed threshold; the second branch remains active for any powered claim, because $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival.

\paragraph{Controlled readback.}
The readback allowed here is a controlled matrix from $Q_i$ residual coordinates to modeled translation weights $T_j$.  It is not a direct path from $Q_i$ to protein abundance $P_k$, and it is not a claim about folding, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, physical admissibility, or a universal biological rule.  A residual-to-protein statement becomes admissible only after a same-row translation step is present and the downstream protein row is tested under the declared controls.  Those controls include amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, the $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance whenever the claim crosses from a translation readout to protein abundance.

\paragraph{Downstream protein-abundance comparison.}
A separate powered protein-abundance decomposition supplies the contrast case.  In that experiment the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates are joined to modeled per-protein tAI and PAXdb protein abundance across $12$ organisms.  The joined protein counts are $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} strain 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for chicken, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  In that downstream decomposition the total residual-to-protein $R^2$ and direct-after-tAI $R^2$ remain organism-specific.  For yeast, for example, $c_{\mathrm{total}}=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and the indirect difference is $0.28320534058057895$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  For human, $c_{\mathrm{total}}=0.04454565118019448$, $c'=0.03242643251185274$, and the indirect difference is $0.012119218668341744$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$.  For \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $c_{\mathrm{total}}=0.10767526677985896$, $c'=0.04167869969498872$, and the indirect difference is $0.06599656708487024$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$.

\paragraph{Rank and coordinate identity check.}
The structural-order axis audit computes the same nine coordinates and identifies $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$ as the dominant increment axis.  Its weighted held-out $R^2$ increment is $0.0031744502294756994$, with exact sign-flip right-tail probability $0.03125$.  The other coordinates do not pass that fold-level sign-flip gate: for example $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$ has increment $0.0013067592718559024$ with $p=0.0693359375$, $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$ has increment $0.0020139270616115154$ with $p=0.142578125$, and $\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$ has increment $-0.0003205780368125179$ with $p=0.779296875$.  This axis audit is useful as a coordinate identity check, but it does not upgrade the modeled tAI contact into measured translation or protein realization.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The conclusion is therefore narrow.  The finite three-organism modeled-tAI matrix has nonzero bounded coupling for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, with $81$ entries above $0.01$ and codon-complete modeled weights in all three organisms.  It supports only the statement that the residual coordinates have a finite, modeled translation-weight contact.  It does not by itself establish measured translation survival, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  Any such assertion requires a separate layer-matched contact and a same-row controlled statistic at that layer.
