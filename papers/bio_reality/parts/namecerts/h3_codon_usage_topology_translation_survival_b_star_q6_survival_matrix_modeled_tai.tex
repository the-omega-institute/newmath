\subsection{Modeled tAI Survival and Translation-Conditioned Protein Abundance for $B^*_{Q6}$ Residuals}\label{sec:biorality-q6-modeled-tai-protein-mediation}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record fixes a finite mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$.  The $q_i$ coordinate ranges over the nine computed $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{Q6}=\{&\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $t_j$ coordinate is a translation-layer readout; in the computed bounded-contact run it is modeled tAI, with codon weights obtained from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The $p_k$ coordinate is matched protein abundance; in the downstream finite run it is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ after joining real CDS codon counts to PAXdb protein abundance rows.  The permitted readout is therefore not a direct $q_i \to p_k$ promotion, but a controlled matrix in which the residual must first pass through a translation coordinate.

\paragraph{Modeled translation-contact computation.}
The modeled-tAI contact was computed on the $61$ sense-codon count space for three organisms.  The local GtRNAdb payload contained all $20$ standard amino-acid families in each organism.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12, $89$ FASTA headers were present, $87$ matched the standard tRNA-family parser, and $2$ were unmatched.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $432$ headers were present and all $432$ matched.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $275$ headers were present and all $275$ matched.  The modeled codon-weight readout covered all $61$ sense codons in each of the three organisms, with zero raw-$W$ fallback count $0$ in all three cases.

The bounded coupling surface used threshold $0.01$.  It had $81$ entries above threshold, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|S_{ij}| &= 3801961.031607896,\\
\max_i |S_{ii}| &= 3801961.031607896,\\
\tau &= 0.01.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite modeled-tAI matrix is nonzero under the declared bounded-contact threshold.  The break condition for the modeled contact is not met: the null trigger would require no finite modeled-tAI entry above threshold, or a request for powered survival without at least $40$ matched species.  Here the organism count is only $3$, so the positive entries support bounded modeled coupling, not a powered multi-species translation-survival law.

\paragraph{Matched protein-abundance rows.}
A separate cross-layer computation used the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and joined CDS, tRNA, and protein-abundance rows for three organisms.  The joined row counts were $3739$ proteins for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  Per-protein modeled tAI was computed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
T=\frac{\sum_c n_c w_c}{\sum_c n_c},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_c$ is the CDS codon count and $w_c$ is the modeled tAI codon weight.  The all-tRNA copy totals were $90$, $49$, and $275$ respectively.  The zero raw-$W$ values filled by geometric mean were $1$ for \emph{E. coli}, $1$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0$ for yeast.

The control set was residualized before estimating the $Q \to T \to P$ decomposition.  It included amino-acid composition, organism-specific row structure through the joined organism panels, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched mRNA-abundance control.  Each organism used $24$ control columns with residual rank $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.  After removing the control span, the residual degrees of freedom were $3716$, $4106$, and $5792$.  The residual ranks were stable across the three panels:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)&=9,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)&=1,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)&=10.
\end{aligned}
$$
The rank gate was therefore sufficient in all three joined panels, with the reported degenerate-$R^2$ cutoff $0.999999$ not reached.

\paragraph{Mediation decomposition.}
The cross-layer statistic was a joint nine-dimensional $X_Q$ mediation scalar with per-coordinate audit retained separately.  For \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, the total residual-to-protein $R^2$ was $0.10767526677985896$, the direct $R^2$ after conditioning on modeled tAI was $0.04167869969498872$, and the indirect difference was $0.06599656708487024$.  The mediated fraction was
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{E.coli}}=0.612922252793667.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \emph{M. smegmatis}, the corresponding values were $0.03209009170649934$, $0.029395293228368185$, and $0.0026947984781311544$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{M.smegmatis}}=0.08397602919861291.
\end{aligned}
$$
For yeast, the total residual-to-protein $R^2$ was $0.29837624994140827$, the direct $R^2$ after conditioning on modeled tAI was $0.0151709093608293$, and the indirect difference was $0.28320534058057895$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{S.cerevisiae}}=0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
All three mediated fractions lie in the unit interval.  The finite result is therefore a positive statistical decomposition for matched protein-abundance rows under modeled per-protein tAI conditioning, with strongest attenuation in yeast and weakest attenuation in \emph{M. smegmatis}.

\paragraph{Control-energy audit.}
The residual-energy quantities are consistent with a finite, nondegenerate matrix rather than an empty routing artifact.  In \emph{E. coli}, modeled $T$ explained energy $582.5912438239341$ on protein abundance, $X_Q$ explained $432.89050065533684$ on protein abundance, the incremental $T$ improvement given $X_Q$ was $317.26302294928666$, and the remaining direct $X_Q$ improvement given $T$ was $167.56227978068944$.  In \emph{M. smegmatis}, the same four energies were $15.813547327443837$, $183.85037813566998$, $0.37452260494680445$, and $168.41135341317295$.  In yeast, they were $941.8858618613751$, $882.6526210803958$, $104.11162139652299$, and $44.87838061554362$.  The translation residual energies were $0.4728468725814029$, $0.041191576348982066$, and $6.90537888244409$, and the protein-abundance residual energies were $4020.333671802061$, $5729.194538214237$, and $2958.1865890925337$.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates have a nonzero bounded coupling to modeled codon-level tAI weights in a three-organism finite contact, and a same-coordinate downstream run gives a controlled statistical $Q \to T \to P$ decomposition for matched protein-abundance rows in three organism panels.  This supports reporting a modeled translation-conditioned protein-abundance matrix, not a causal translation mechanism.  A direct residual-to-protein association is not treated as protein realization unless the same residual first has a translation-readout step available in the finite surface.  Matched mRNA abundance stays in the control set and separates transcription-level abundance from translation-conditioned readout.

No conclusion about observed ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, selection, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows alone.  Modeled tAI and stAI weights are codon-adaptation annotations, not ribosome-profiling measurements.  Steady-state protein abundance is not isolated from mRNA abundance, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, pathway context, or sample-specific measurement bias.  Any higher-layer biological statement requires its own layer-matched contact and a separately computed statistic after the same controls are imposed.
