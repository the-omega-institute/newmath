\subsection{Tissue dependence of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual projection against draft proteome abundance}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-tissue-dependence-functional-class}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record tests a finite human panel in which nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are paired with tissue-specific draft proteome abundance readouts.  The coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The response variable is $\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from the human Kim et al. 2014 SEQUEST adult-tissue PAXdb files, and the organism is Homo sapiens.  Each tissue fit uses the same control family: an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The recorded control-column count is 24 in every tissue, while the fitted residualized $Q$ block has rank 9 and the control block has rank 23.

\paragraph{Per-tissue finite rows.}
Thirteen adult tissues enter the joined panel, with no skipped tissue in the recorded run.  The joined row counts are 9244 for B cells, 7604 for CD4 T cells, 10914 for CD8 T cells, 8787 for NK cells, 5179 for monocytes, 8664 for liver, 6726 for kidney, 9695 for pancreas, 9301 for frontal cortex, 10373 for retina, 7948 for colon, 6577 for lung, and 6468 for heart.  The controlled $S^{QP}$ surface is small but nonzero across all listed tissues: $R^2_{QP}=0.03626117434944674$ in B cells, $0.033532527035959644$ in CD4 T cells, $0.03928541767885904$ in CD8 T cells, $0.02709444741593442$ in NK cells, $0.021350299623075428$ in monocytes, $0.026133456228248052$ in liver, $0.018499903301809693$ in kidney, $0.027616177587166925$ in pancreas, $0.025874798845290918$ in frontal cortex, $0.04805730109298326$ in retina, $0.023732563331619574$ in colon, $0.0232738640400933$ in lung, and $0.02320896321347296$ in heart.  The largest recorded value is the retina value $0.04805730109298326$, and the smallest is the kidney value $0.018499903301809693$.

\paragraph{Dominant residual coordinates.}
The fitted dominant coordinate is not uniform across tissue.  $Arg\_AGR$ is dominant in B cells, CD8 T cells, colon, kidney, NK cells, pancreas, and retina.  $Ile\_AUA$ is dominant in CD4 T cells, frontal cortex, liver, lung, and monocytes.  $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$ is dominant only in heart.  This distribution is the main chapter-level readout: the finite panel supports an idiosyncratic tissue dependence of the controlled residual projection, not a single universal residual coordinate.  The claim is therefore a descriptive cross-layer relation between synonymous-residual coordinates and a draft proteome abundance surface, with the protein-abundance surface used only as the measured downstream readout in this finite calculation.

\paragraph{Functional-class label test.}
The tissue labels used for the prior grouping are immune, metabolic, neural, and epithelial-or-other.  The cosine matrix contains 78 tissue pairs over 13 tissues.  Within-class similarity is only slightly above between-class similarity: the observed within-class mean cosine is $0.9015393659102923$, the observed between-class mean cosine is $0.8936124027169124$, and the observed within-minus-between delta is $0.007926963193379821$.  A class-size-preserving permutation test with 1000 permutations gives one-sided $p=0.3006993006993007$ for within-class similarity being greater than the label-shuffle null.  The null deltas range from $-0.043787962870935226$ to $0.08858262592759103$, with null mean $0.0007739569961830541$.  Thus the finite run does not support a strong functional-class structure in the human tissue panel.  The appropriate reading is that the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection has tissue-specific structure, but the immune, metabolic, neural, and epithelial-or-other labels do not explain that structure at the usual evidential strength in this run.

\paragraph{Relation to modeled translation readouts.}
A separate rank-powered modeled-readout sweep records 41 swept organisms and reaches the target of 40 organisms.  In that sweep the modeled tAI surface has $R^2_{QT}$ distribution minimum $0.1579271837024072$, first quartile $0.26353954700074694$, median $0.33934845811598124$, mean $0.38466555440501393$, third quartile $0.5143951545826744$, and maximum $0.8213457220900503$, with 1000 permutations.  That organism-level modeled surface is useful as a bounded codon-adaptation comparison point, but it is not the same observation as the PAXdb tissue abundance panel and it is not ribosome profiling.  The present tissue chapter therefore records a $Q$-to-proteome-abundance projection in a single human study, while keeping modeled tAI or stAI readouts, measured translation efficiency, and protein abundance as distinct readout surfaces.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation-realization claim follows from this chapter.  The PAXdb tissue files are draft proteome abundance readouts from one study, not ribosome-profiling or matched mRNA-controlled translation-efficiency data.  The calculation also does not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, post-translational state, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new controlled statistic on the corresponding finite rows.  In particular, an observed $S^{QP}$ value, a dominant residual coordinate, or a tissue-cosine pattern may be cited only as the bounded descriptive output of this finite panel, not as evidence that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual geometry causes translation, structure, function, or organism-level phenotype.
