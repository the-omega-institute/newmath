\paragraph{Translation-gated interpretation.}
A codon residual $q_i$ may approach a protein-localization-facing or splice-readout-facing interpretation only through a nondegenerate translation-readout step.  In finite-row terms, the relevant gate is a statistic of the form $S^{QT}(q_i,T_j)$, where $T_j$ is a same-row translation readout such as measured translation efficiency, bounded ribosome occupancy, or an explicitly declared codon-adaptation readout.  The admissible pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j,\\
(q_i,T_j,\mathrm{mRNA},Z) &\longrightarrow L_k \text{ or } \Psi_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ contains the declared baseline controls, $L_k$ denotes a localization label or score when localization is under discussion, and $\Psi_k$ denotes a splice-event readout when the VASTDB splice surface is under discussion.  A direct fitted relation $q_i \to L_k$ or $q_i \to \Psi_k$ is therefore not enough.  The translation step must survive first, and the downstream association must then be tested after conditioning on the same-row translation readout and mRNA control.
