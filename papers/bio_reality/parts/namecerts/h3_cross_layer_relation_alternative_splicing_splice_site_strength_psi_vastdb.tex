\paragraph{Local statistic.}
The chapter-level question is whether a synonymous residual coordinate can be allowed to face a higher-layer localization interpretation after passing through a same-row translation readout.  In a finite panel, this requires a nondegenerate translation statistic before any localization statistic is interpreted.  The intended gating can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j,\\
(q_i,T_j,\mathrm{mRNA},Z) &\longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the residual coordinate, $T_j$ is the matched translation readout, $L_k$ is the matched localization label or score, and $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, methionine-density baseline, splice or isoform diagnostics where applicable, and simpler baseline terms. The second arrow is not a direct promotion of $q_i$ to localization; it is a conditioning test asking whether the apparent localization association is reduced, absorbed, or otherwise explained once $T_j$ and the matched controls are present.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The default interpretation is conservative.  If $q_i$ is associated with localization labels but the same row lacks a nondegenerate $q_i \to T_j$ translation-readout step, the association is a localization-contact boundary, not translation mediation.  If $T_j$ is rank-saturated, underpowered, all-baseline, or only represented by modeled tAI or stAI weights without measured or explicitly bounded translation rows, the localization side remains unpromoted.  If the available evidence is only splice-site strength, PSI, mRNA abundance, codon usage, or protein abundance, then the table may support a bounded RNA, transcript-control, codon-composition, or protein-abundance readback, but it still does not supply protein trafficking, compartment mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.
