\paragraph{Gate.}
A localization-facing interpretation is allowed only through a nondegenerate translation step.  For a row family $D$, the admissible path has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j,\\
(q_i,T_j) &\longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the first arrow must survive the matched controls.  In the notation of the packet, this means that the translation-readout surface $S^{QT}(q_i,T_j)$ must be positive in a rank-powered, non-saturated design before any localization readback is tested.  If $S^{QT}(q_i,T_j)$ is absent, rank-saturated, underpowered, or indistinguishable from a baseline-only fit, then the downstream localization contact remains a boundary row rather than a biological mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Conditioning test.}
The permitted localization statistic is not a direct $q_i \to L_k$ association.  The relevant finite-row question is whether a localization association is reduced, absorbed, or otherwise explained when the same-row translation readout and the matched mRNA control are included.  A schematic admissible comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_k &\sim q_i + Z,\\
L_k &\sim q_i + T_j + \mathrm{mRNA} + Z,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, rank-sufficiency diagnostics, identifier-mapping diagnostics, and simpler codon baselines.  A translation-mediated localization proposal requires that the first-stage $q_i \to T_j$ signal be present and that the second comparison show a finite conditioning relation rather than merely preserving an unmediated codon-to-localization fit.
