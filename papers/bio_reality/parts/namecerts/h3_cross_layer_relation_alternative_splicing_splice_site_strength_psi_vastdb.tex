\paragraph{Required gating relation.}
For a row-level localization interpretation to be admissible, the residual coordinate must first pass through a translation-readout test.  Let $Q_i$ denote the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual coordinate for a row family, let $T_j$ denote a same-row translation readout, and let $L_k$ denote a matched localization readout.  The required ordering is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &\longrightarrow T_j,\\
(Q_i,T_j) &\longrightarrow L_k.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first arrow is not notation for a mechanism.  It means that the coordinate has a tested nonzero survival against the translation readout after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, methionine-density, matched mRNA abundance, and simpler codon baselines have been represented.  The second arrow is only evaluated after that first step is nondegenerate.  A direct association $Q_i \to L_k$ is not enough, because a localization label can be correlated with many sequence, expression, abundance, compartment, and annotation biases unrelated to translation.

\paragraph{Admissible readback.}
The admissible chapter-level statement is therefore conditional.  A $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual coordinate may be described as localization-facing only when a same-row $Q_i \to T_j$ translation readout survives and a subsequent conditioning or mediation test shows that the localization association is reduced, explained, or otherwise organized by $T_j$.  In finite-row terms, the usable statistic must distinguish the following cases: a null translation step, a rank-saturated translation fit, a direct localization association that remains after translation conditioning, and a translation-conditioned localization association.  Only the last case can be read as a translation-gated localization contact, and even then it remains a statistical contact rather than a claim about trafficking machinery.

The correct local conclusion is a bounded null: the packet is ready to state the gate that a future joined table must satisfy, but it is not ready to claim measured localization survival.

\paragraph{Controls.}
The control set is part of the claim rather than optional background.  Any future positive row must preserve the amino-acid quotient by comparing synonymous residual signal against amino-acid sequence and composition controls.  It must also account for organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, methionine density, matched mRNA abundance, and baseline codon models.  If protein abundance is used as an additional control, it must be kept distinct from localization, because bulk abundance is not a compartment readout.  If tAI or stAI weights are used, they remain modeled codon-adaptation annotations unless an observed ribosome or translation-efficiency row is separately attached.

\paragraph{Layer boundary.}
No translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, trafficking mechanism, compartment-identity mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from the current finite record.  A translation claim would require a matched translation measurement or an explicitly bounded translation-readout statistic.  A folding or physical-admissibility claim would require structural or physical-assay contact.  A function claim would require a functional assay, perturbation, or curated validation surface.  A mechanism claim would require intervention, rescue, perturbation, or comparable causal evidence.  The $29742$ events in run \texttt{1ce9f7f16dbe} mark the size of the finite audit surface; they do not replace any of those layer-specific measurements.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The chapter is therefore a ready boundary chapter rather than a positive discovery chapter.  Its scientific content is the finite rule for how a $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual coordinate may approach protein-localization interpretation: first require a nondegenerate same-row translation-readout survival step, then test localization only under translation and matched-control conditioning.  Without that joined matrix, the residual coordinate remains a bounded proposal at the cross-layer interface, and the localization contact remains a target for future finite-row testing rather than a biological mechanism claim.
