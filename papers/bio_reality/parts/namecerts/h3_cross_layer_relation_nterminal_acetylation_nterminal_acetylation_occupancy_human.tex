\subsection{Human N-Terminal Acetylation as a Bounded Cross-Layer Occupancy Record}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-nterminal-acetylation-occupancy}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The human N-terminal acetylation occupancy record is treated here as a finite cross-layer table, not as a general mechanism of protein fate. The verified run for the claim identifier \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_human} reports $n=1426$ human rows and runtime $5.372$ seconds. Its finite annotation says that the frozen yeast rule transfers to human under preregistered gates, while the abundance-control flag is false. Thus the admissible statement is sharply local: the row set supports a transfer of an N-terminal acetylation occupancy rule from yeast to human under the recorded gate conditions, but it does not by itself establish that abundance, folding, localization, function, degradation, phenotype, or universal regulation has been measured.

\paragraph{Carrier and coordinate.}
Each admissible row is read as a matched protein-entry record carrying an N-terminal residue or motif state, the human occupancy readout, and the gate outcome used to compare the human table with the yeast-derived rule. The row is finite because it has a definite protein identifier, a definite occupancy-side readout, and a definite inclusion or exclusion status. The associated coordinate is not a hidden causal variable; it is the controlled row label under which the occupancy rule is tested. In symbols, the chapter uses only the finite relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_i &= (\mathrm{protein}_i,\, \mathrm{Nterm}_i,\, A_i,\, G_i,\, B_i),\\
  A_i &\in \mathrm{human\ N\text{-}terminal\ acetylation\ occupancy},\\
  G_i &\in \mathrm{preregistered\ gate\ state},\\
  B_i &\in \mathrm{break\ or\ pass\ condition}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite result is therefore an occupancy-survival statement on $1426$ rows. The absence of abundance control is part of the claim, not an incidental omission: because abundance was not controlled in the verified run, no protein-output statement is licensed from this table.

\paragraph{Rule transfer.}
The biological content of the verified row is the reported conservation relation: a yeast-derived rule remains compatible with the human N-terminal acetylation occupancy table when the preregistered gates are applied. This is a cross-layer relation only in the restricted sense that a protein-sequence boundary state is tested against an occupancy readout. It is not a statement that the N-terminal rule determines translation, folding, stability, localization, or function. The transfer phrase is therefore read as a finite comparative result: within the accepted human rows, the same rule form that was frozen in yeast survives the human occupancy test.

\paragraph{Linked finite context.}
The same finite audit snapshot also records a separate protein-solubility entry, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol}, with $n=3198$ rows and runtime $60.0$ seconds. That entry is not used as evidence for N-terminal acetylation. It is cited only to delimit the local scope of this chapter: a separate cross-layer protein property can have its own finite table, row count, and runtime, but those numbers do not migrate into the acetylation claim. The acetylation result remains the $1426$-row human occupancy record, while solubility remains a different readout surface.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The claim breaks if the human occupancy result is promoted into a translation, folding, physical-admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality claim without a separate contact at that layer. It also breaks if the absence of abundance control is ignored. A protein-abundance claim would require matched abundance rows; a folding or stability claim would require a structure, stability, turnover, or perturbation readout; a mechanism claim would require an intervention or mechanistic measurement rather than a finite occupancy association. The present record allows only the bounded statement that the human N-terminal acetylation occupancy table, with $1426$ rows, agrees with the frozen yeast-rule transfer under its preregistered gates.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter is ready as a finite BioReality record because the row count, runtime, abundance-control status, and conservation annotation are explicit. It is not ready as a mechanistic biology claim. The correct interpretation is a constrained occupancy-table survival result: human N-terminal acetylation occupancy can be compared to the yeast-derived rule on the verified finite panel, and all downstream biological readings remain blocked until a separate matched contact supplies the corresponding finite data.
