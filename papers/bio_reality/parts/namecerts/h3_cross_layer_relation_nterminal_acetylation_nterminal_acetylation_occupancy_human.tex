\subsection{Human N-terminal acetylation occupancy as a bounded cross-layer row}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-nterminal-acetylation-occupancy}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The row named \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_human} is admissible here as a finite BioReality record, not as an unrestricted biological law.  Its verified finite contact is the human N-terminal acetylation occupancy panel with experiment run \texttt{90ea6b5f7fc7}.  The recorded row count is $n=1426$, the measured runtime is $5.372$ seconds, and the abundance-control flag is false.  The associated readback sentence says that a frozen yeast rule transfers to human under preregistered gates.  In this chapter that sentence is treated only as the outcome of the stated finite gate, because the row itself explicitly records that matched abundance control is absent.

\paragraph{Local object.}
The mathematical object carried by the packet is a finite table of matched coding-sequence windows.  Each row contains an organism and CDS or protein identifier, a codon-position bin, an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate after amino-acid projection, synonymous amino-acid composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry flag, a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residual, and a break condition.  The row therefore has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 x_i = (&\operatorname{org}_i,\operatorname{cds}_i,\operatorname{posbin}_i, q_i, a_i, z_i, t_i, b_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ denotes the $f_3$-stress coordinate, $a_i$ records whether the third-position wobble weights break the $f_3$ symmetry, $z_i$ is the finite control vector, $t_i$ is the bounded translation-window readout, and $b_i$ is the local stopping condition.  The intended control vector includes amino-acid-bin or position controls, amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, codon-count controls, matched mRNA abundance, coverage, mappability, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablation.

\paragraph{Verified scalar anchors.}
Two finite facts are used to keep the chapter grounded.  First, the N-terminal acetylation row gives $n=1426$, runtime $5.372$, and \texttt{abundance\_controlled=false}; hence it can support only an occupancy statement under the preregistered gates actually recorded by the row.  Second, the linked solubility control row \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol} gives experiment run \texttt{8742a224b538}, $n=3198$, and runtime $60.0$ seconds.  That second fact is not evidence for acetylation and not evidence for translation occupancy; it is cited only as a separate finite cross-layer panel showing that the BioReality table distinguishes one measured layer from another.  The distinction matters because a protein-solubility panel cannot be silently converted into a ribosome-occupancy panel, and an acetylation-occupancy panel cannot be silently converted into an $f_3$-stress translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Admissible test.}
The admissible statement is a conditional finite-row survival claim.  Among matched CDS windows with position-resolved ribosome-footprint occupancy, the $f_3$-stress residual may be tested for incremental prediction of a bounded translation-window readout only in wobble-asymmetric contexts and only after the declared controls are present.  A convenient local statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 S_{f_3}(T\mid Z,A) &= \Delta\mathcal{L}\bigl(T; Z, A, q\bigr)-\Delta\mathcal{L}\bigl(T; Z, A\bigr),\\
 A &= \mathbf{1}\{w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T$ is the position-resolved translation-window residual, $Z$ is the finite control matrix, and $A$ marks wobble asymmetry.  This expression is a specification for a joined finite table; it is not a declaration that the joined table already exists for the present row.  The positive case would require nondegenerate rank, a stable sign or bounded improvement under simpler-baseline ablation, and survival after matched mRNA control.  The null case is equally informative: if the positive controlled survival step fails, the $f_3$ coordinate remains a codon-topology boundary variable rather than a translation-layer variable.

\paragraph{N-terminal acetylation interpretation.}
For the human N-terminal acetylation row, the finite evidence says that the preregistered human panel contains $1426$ rows and records transfer of a frozen yeast rule under its gates.  Because abundance control is false, the chapter does not infer abundance-mediated acetylation, translation-mediated acetylation, or a causal route from codon-window geometry to N-terminal acetylation.  The row may be used as a bounded cross-layer occupancy record: it names a finite human occupancy surface and a finite rule-transfer outcome, while leaving abundance, translation dynamics, and downstream protein-state mechanisms outside the certified row.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The record breaks if review status, contact routing, modeled tAI or stAI weights, wobble asymmetry, $S^{QT}$ notation, or $f_3$ geometry is treated as if it were already a joined same-organism, same-CDS, same-window footprint matrix.  It also breaks if the acetylation row is used to claim protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, localization, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those claims require their own layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  In particular, no translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal rule follows from this row unless a separate contact supplies the corresponding readout.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter is therefore a bounded finite-row certificate.  It records a human N-terminal acetylation occupancy fact with $n=1426$, runtime $5.372$, and no abundance control; it also records a separate solubility panel with $n=3198$ and runtime $60.0$ seconds as a layer-separation check.  The $f_3$-stress construction remains admissible only as a proposed same-window translation-readout statistic requiring codon counts, wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint occupancy on the same finite rows.  Until that joined statistic exists, the row remains scientifically useful as a sharply bounded contact record and not as a promoted mechanism.
