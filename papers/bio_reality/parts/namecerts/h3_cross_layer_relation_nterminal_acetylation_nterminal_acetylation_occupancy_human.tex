\paragraph{Admissible readout.}
The admissible statement is narrow.  The row supports the assertion that, on the supplied human panel, a preregistered N-terminal acetylation rule has a bounded occupancy readout over $1426$ entries. \paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, phenotype, or universal rule follows from this row alone.  The finite table does not include a matched ribosome-occupancy statistic, a matched mRNA abundance denominator, a protein-folding measurement, a physical assay, a localization assay, or a perturbation experiment.  In particular, the absence of an abundance-controlled flag blocks any claim that the observed occupancy relation is independent of protein abundance.  A stronger claim would require a separate finite contact at the relevant layer, joined to the same identifiers and evaluated with its own rank, coverage, and break-condition checks.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The record breaks if the yeast-to-human transfer phrase is treated as a mechanism, if the $1426$-row occupancy panel is treated as a protein-abundance result, or if the occupancy association is promoted to folding, stability, localization, function, phenotype, or universality without an additional same-row readout.  It also breaks if the independent $3198$-row solubility run is used as support for acetylation occupancy rather than as a separate cross-layer comparison.  Within these limits, the finite row is usable as a self-contained BioReality chapter entry: a human N-terminal acetylation occupancy relation, verified under its recorded gates, with explicitly bounded scope.
