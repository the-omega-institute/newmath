\subsection{Human N-terminal Acetylation Occupancy as a Bounded Cross-Layer Record}
\label{sec:bioreality-human-nterminal-acetylation-occupancy-f3-stress-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The row under consideration is the BioReality record
$\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_human}$.
Its verified finite contact is a human N-terminal acetylation occupancy panel with $n=1426$ rows, experiment run id $90\mathrm{ea}6\mathrm{b}5\mathrm{f}7\mathrm{fc}7$, and recorded runtime $5.372$ seconds.  The row also records that the abundance-controlled condition is false.  Thus the admissible readout is not a protein-abundance-conditioned statement: it is only a finite occupancy row whose strongest local description is that the frozen yeast rule transfers to human under the preregistered gates.  The companion BioReality record
$\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol}$
has experiment run id $8742\mathrm{a}224\mathrm{b}538$, $n=3198$ rows, and runtime $60.0$ seconds.  It is cited here only as an independent finite cross-layer assay row in the same audit surface, not as evidence for acetylation, ribosome occupancy, or the $f_3$-stress statistic.

\paragraph{Coordinate being certified.}
The mathematical object certified by this chapter is a finite coding-sequence window table carrying a residual third-position coordinate $f_3$, synonymous amino-acid composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry surface, and a matched translation readout whenever such a readout exists.  A row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mathrm{organism},\mathrm{CDS},b,s_{f_3},a,w,\rho,\beta),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $b$ is a codon-position or local-window bin, $s_{f_3}$ is the residual after amino-acid projection, $a$ records the amino-acid-bin and composition controls, $w$ records whether the third-position wobble weight is symmetric or asymmetric, $\rho$ is the matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residual when present, and $\beta$ is the break condition.  The human acetylation occupancy row supplies a finite cross-layer biological measurement, but it does not by itself supply the joined position-resolved ribosome-footprint matrix required for $\rho$ in this coordinate.

\paragraph{Admissible relation.}
The admissible relation is conditional and finite.  The $f_3$ coordinate may be tested against a translation-window residual only after joining same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, bounded tAI or stAI wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and codon-indexed or window-indexed ribosome-footprint occupancy.  The statistic must be evaluated after amino-acid-bin or position controls, GC3, coding-sequence length, methionine-density baseline, codon-count controls, matched mRNA control, coverage filters, mappability filters, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablation.  In symbolic form, the target is not a free-standing biological law but a bounded conditional contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{f_3}
&= \mathrm{Err}(T_{\mathrm{window}}\mid Z)
   - \mathrm{Err}(T_{\mathrm{window}}\mid Z,s_{f_3},w),\\
Z
&= (\mathrm{amino\ acid\ controls},\mathrm{position},\mathrm{GC3},
\mathrm{length},M,\mathrm{codon\ counts},\mathrm{mRNA}),
\end{aligned}
$$
computed only on rows where the design matrix has sufficient rank and the break condition is not triggered.  The human acetylation occupancy result with $n=1426$ rows is therefore a separate finite biological success row, while the $f_3$ translation-window coordinate remains a requested joined-readout statistic unless the ribosome-occupancy surface is actually attached.

\paragraph{What the row supports.}
The human N-terminal acetylation row supports a narrow statement: within the recorded finite panel, the preregistered human occupancy rule is compatible with a conserved yeast-derived rule, and the audit records this without an abundance-control claim.  The solubility row with $n=3198$ is useful only as a second example that the BioReality table can contain concrete finite cross-layer readouts with explicit row counts and runtimes.  These two rows establish that the audit surface is not merely symbolic: it contains finite measurements.  They do not establish that the $f_3$ coordinate has survived a position-resolved translation-window test.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon-window geometry alone.  No ribosome-occupancy claim follows from modeled tAI or stAI weights alone.  No protein abundance claim follows here because the human acetylation row explicitly records $\mathrm{abundance\_controlled}=\mathrm{false}$.  No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, localization, stability, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal law follows from the $f_3$ coordinate, from wobble asymmetry, from the acetylation occupancy row, or from the solubility row.  Each such layer would require its own matched finite contact and its own controlled statistic.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The record breaks if review eligibility, contact naming, wobble-weight annotation, $S^{QT}$ notation, codon-window shape, or the $f_3$ residual is treated as a computed same-window ribosome-occupancy statistic.  It also breaks if the human acetylation result is used as a proxy for translation efficiency or if the solubility panel is used as evidence for acetylation occupancy.  The present chapter certifies only a bounded cross-layer record: a verified human acetylation finite row, a second finite assay row for audit anchoring, and a precise statement of what additional joined translation data would be required before the $f_3$ coordinate could be promoted beyond boundary status.
