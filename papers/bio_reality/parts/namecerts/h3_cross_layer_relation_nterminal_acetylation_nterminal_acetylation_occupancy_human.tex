The recorded scalar outcome is that the frozen yeast rule transfers to human under the preregistered gates, while the abundance-control flag is false.  Thus the admissible biological statement is deliberately narrow: the row supports a finite human N-terminal-acetylation occupancy comparison against the conserved yeast-derived rule, but it does not by itself certify protein abundance mediation, translation realization, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, functional consequence, phenotype, or universality.

The row is read as a same-subject occupancy surface, not as a general proteome law.  Its finite datum is the tuple consisting of the claim identifier, run identifier, human row count, elapsed runtime, conservation readout, and the explicit absence of abundance control.  In symbols the local audit state may be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{human}} &= 1426,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{run}} &= 5.372\ \mathrm{s},\\
A_{\mathrm{controlled}} &= \mathrm{false},\\
C_{\mathrm{readout}} &= \text{yeast-rule transfer under preregistered gates}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last line is a classification attached to the finite run; it is not a causal statement about why the human occupancy pattern appears.

\paragraph{Cross-layer interpretation.}
The chapter uses the N-terminal-acetylation row as a bounded cross-layer relation: a finite upstream rule, already frozen before this human check, is evaluated against a human occupancy surface.  The positive conservation readout is therefore a survival statement for this specific occupancy test.  It says that, on the $1426$-row human panel used by run $\mathrm{90ea6b5f7fc7}$, the preregistered yeast-derived rule remains compatible with the human N-terminal-acetylation occupancy classification.  Because $A_{\mathrm{controlled}}=\mathrm{false}$, no abundance-adjusted claim is made.  In particular, the run cannot distinguish whether any surviving occupancy association is independent of protein abundance, transcript abundance, translation efficiency, or other matched downstream covariates that were not included as controls in the finite row.

The same audit packet also carries a neighboring cross-layer comparison, $\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol}$, verified in run $\mathrm{8742a224b538}$ on $n=3198$ entries with runtime $60.0$ seconds. Its role here is narrower: it marks that the finite audit table separates cross-layer tests by biological object and by matched readout.  The acetylation row and the solubility row are both finite cross-layer records, but their readout surfaces are different.  Therefore the acetylation conclusion cannot borrow the solubility row count, solubility classification, or hydrophobic-clustering interpretation.

\paragraph{Boundary against the translation-window material.}
The conjecture context also mentions a possible $f_3$-stress translation-window coordinate, with same-CDS codon windows, wobble-weight asymmetry, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residuals.  That coordinate is only a proposed translation-readout boundary unless the required same-organism, same-CDS, same-window rows are actually joined and evaluated.  The present N-terminal-acetylation row does not supply those rows.  It supplies a human occupancy test for an N-terminal-acetylation claim, not a position-resolved ribosome-footprint statistic and not a translation-window survival result.

Consequently, the admissible implication is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{finite human acetylation occupancy row}\ +\ \text{preregistered yeast rule}\\
&\qquad \Longrightarrow\ \text{bounded conservation readout for this occupancy test},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no promotion to
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{translation realization},\quad \text{folding state},\quad \text{physical admissibility},\\
&\text{protein function},\quad \text{mechanism},\quad \text{phenotype},\quad \text{universal biological law}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Any one of those stronger readings would require its own matched contact: ribosome profiling for translation, abundance measurements for protein-output claims, structural or stability assays for folding and physical-state claims, perturbation or biochemical evidence for mechanism, and organism-appropriate phenotype data for system-level consequence.

\paragraph{Audit verdict.}
Within its finite scope, the row is usable.  The evidence is concrete enough to state that run $\mathrm{90ea6b5f7fc7}$ evaluated $1426$ human entries and recorded conservation of the frozen yeast rule under preregistered gates.  The same record also forces the main limitation: abundance was not controlled.  The clean statement is therefore a bounded human N-terminal-acetylation occupancy survival claim, not an abundance-adjusted, mechanistic, translational, structural, functional, or universal claim.
