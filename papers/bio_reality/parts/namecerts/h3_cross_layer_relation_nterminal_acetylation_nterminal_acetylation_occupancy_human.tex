\subsection{Human N-terminal acetylation occupancy as a controlled cross-layer readout}
\label{subsec:bioreality-human-nterminal-acetylation-occupancy}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
The human N-terminal acetylation occupancy contact is represented here as a finite cross-layer table, not as a general law about protein fate.  The row set has $n = 1426$ human protein records in experiment run \texttt{90ea6b5f7fc7}, with a recorded runtime of $5.372$ seconds.  The run records that the abundance-controlled gate is false, so the admissible interpretation is narrower than a protein-output claim: the table can report whether the preregistered N-terminal acetylation occupancy rule survives on the human occupancy surface, but it cannot by itself separate acetylation occupancy from protein abundance or proteome-scale sampling effects.  The finite statement carried by the run is that the frozen yeast rule transfers to human under the preregistered gates.  In this chapter that sentence is read literally as a row-level occupancy result under the gates used by the run, not as evidence for translation efficiency, folding, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.}
Each admissible row is a matched tuple containing a protein identifier, an N-terminal context, the rule-side occupancy decision, the human occupancy readout, and the break condition attached to the run.  The mathematical role of the coordinate is only to keep the rule-side object and the biological readout on the same finite index set.  If $i$ ranges over the $1426$ records, the allowed summary has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  i &\mapsto (N_i, A_i, O_i, B_i),\\
  N_i &= \text{N-terminal sequence context},\\
  A_i &= \text{rule-side acetylation prediction or class},\\
  O_i &= \text{human occupancy readout},\\
  B_i &= \text{declared break condition for the finite gate}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The point of this notation is deliberately modest.  It records a same-row relation between an N-terminal class and an occupancy readout.  It does not create a translation-rate variable, a folding variable, a stability variable, or a localization variable.  Any such variable would require another matched contact on the same biological objects.

\paragraph{Cross-layer status.}
The positive finite fact is the transfer statement recorded by \texttt{90ea6b5f7fc7}: the yeast-derived rule is observed to transfer to the human N-terminal acetylation occupancy surface under the preregistered gates.  Because \texttt{abundance\_controlled} is false, the chapter keeps the result at occupancy scope.  It is admissible to say that the finite human table supports a rule-to-occupancy relation on $1426$ rows.  It is not admissible to say that the same table establishes protein abundance, protein stability, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal mechanism, or an organism-wide law.  The distinction matters because an acetylation occupancy surface is a post-translational-state readout, while abundance, stability, folding, and function are separate layers with their own sampling designs and confounders.

\paragraph{Contrast contact.}
A second verified finite surface is useful as a scope check rather than as support for the acetylation claim.  The protein-solubility run \texttt{8742a224b538} has $n = 3198$ rows and a recorded runtime of $60.0$ seconds for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol}.  This larger table confirms that the BioReality ledger can carry another protein-layer contact, but it does not merge solubility with N-terminal acetylation.  The two run identifiers name different finite surfaces.  The acetylation chapter therefore cites the solubility table only to enforce layer discipline: a protein contact at one layer is not automatically evidence at another layer, even when both contacts are protein-indexed and finite.

\subsection{Translation-window boundary for the associated $f_3$-stress packet}
\label{subsec:bioreality-fthree-stress-translation-window-boundary}

\paragraph{Boundary object.}
The associated $f_3$-stress packet asks for a finite coding-sequence window table carrying a synonymous residual coordinate, amino-acid composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry surface, and a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy readout.  That object is not supplied by the N-terminal acetylation occupancy run.  The acetylation table is protein indexed and post-translational in scope; the requested $f_3$-stress table is coding-sequence-window indexed and translation-readout in scope.  The admissible row shape for the latter would be
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r &= (g, c, p, q_{f_3}, W, Z, T, b),\\
  g &= \text{organism},\\
  c &= \text{CDS or transcript identifier},\\
  p &= \text{codon-position or local-window bin},\\
  q_{f_3} &= \text{synonymous residual coordinate after amino-acid projection},\\
  W &= \text{bounded wobble-weight asymmetry flag},\\
  Z &= \text{amino-acid, GC3, length, M-density, codon-count, and mRNA controls},\\
  T &= \text{position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residual},\\
  b &= \text{rank, coverage, mappability, and break-condition status}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No such joined matrix is present in the finite facts supplied for this chapter.  The phrase $f_3$-stress therefore remains a codon-window coordinate request until the same-organism, same-CDS, same-window translation rows are attached.

\paragraph{What would count.}
The translation-window claim would become testable only after three contacts are joined on the same finite row set: organism-scoped tAI or stAI weights for wobble-sensitive codon weighting, position-resolved ribosome-footprint occupancy for the translation readout, and matched mRNA abundance for transcript-level control.  The statistic must then test whether $q_{f_3}$ improves a bounded translation-window residual only in wobble-asymmetric contexts after controlling for amino-acid composition or position bin, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density, codon-count baselines, matched mRNA, coverage, mappability, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablation.  A symbolic display of the admissible test is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{f_3} &= L(T \mid Z, W, q_{f_3}) - L(T \mid Z, W),\\
  \text{support} &\Longrightarrow \Delta_{f_3} \text{ is positive only where } W \text{ breaks third-position symmetry},\\
  \text{break} &\Longrightarrow \Delta_{f_3} \leq 0 \text{ or the joined matrix fails rank, coverage, or control checks}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This formula is a local description of the needed finite statistic.  It is not a claim that the statistic has already been computed for the acetylation run.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
The present chapter therefore has two different statuses.  First, the human N-terminal acetylation occupancy contact is ready as a finite post-translational occupancy result on $1426$ rows, with the explicit limitation that abundance was not controlled.  Second, the $f_3$-stress translation-window conjecture is still a boundary request because the required same-window ribosome-occupancy matrix is absent.  The chapter does not infer translation realization from codon geometry, modeled tAI or stAI weights, wobble asymmetry, or review routing.  It also does not infer protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality from the acetylation contact, from the solubility contrast run, or from the unsupplied $f_3$-stress translation-window table.  Those claims require separate finite contacts at their own layers.
