\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the Chen-style matched distributed recoding panel as a finite cross-layer test surface rather than as a general statement about synonymous variation.  The rows are split into $47$ AmpR entries and $53$ GmR entries, with $19$ matched distributed-recoding strata.  The tested residual basis is not an unrestricted codon-usage vector: it is the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ window family used for the E. coli CAI spatial windows, namely the registered surface
$B^*_{Q6}$-$9D$-Ecoli-CAI-spatial-windows-full-nt60-531-and-middle3-drop-first-60-codons.  The CAI weights were read from the finite E. coli genome-weight table at the local source recorded for the run, and the dataset was not rebuilt during the verification pass.  Thus the contact is a bounded finite-row perturbation audit with fixed codon-window coordinates, fixed antibiotic-class rows, fixed CAI weights, and fixed stratum structure.

\paragraph{Controlled readback.}
The admissible readback is the matched-distributed perturbation statistic after the window basis has been held fixed.  In the AmpR arm the fitted independent slope was
$$\begin{aligned}
\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 1.0,
\end{aligned}$$
so the AmpR branch supplies no positive independent effect for the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ window after the declared matching.  The row counts are small but explicit: $n_{\mathrm{AmpR}}=47$ and $n_{\mathrm{GmR}}=53$.  The finite conclusion is therefore not that synonymous recoding lacks biological effects in general, nor that the $B^*_{Q6}$ basis is irrelevant.  It is the narrower statement that this matched D-distributed recoding surface does not promote the tested AmpR window statistic to a positive downstream protein-output claim.  The value $p_{\mathrm{AmpR}}=1.0$ is the decisive local number: the observed AmpR slope is exactly null on this fitted surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated matrix.}
The null AmpR result is interpreted against the larger $Q\to T\to P$ mediation discipline.  In the multi-organism protein-abundance mediation audit, the same nine-coordinate residual family had $q$-coordinate count $9$ and was evaluated with $24$ control columns after amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, and the M-density baseline were included.  That broader audit found positive finite mediated fractions in organism-matched abundance rows, for example $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$ in E. coli K12 MG1655, $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$ in Homo sapiens, and $M^{QTP}=0.15488799907323988$ in Mus musculus.  Those values say that a cross-sectional abundance decomposition can be computed when the translation readout $T$ and protein readout $P$ are both present.  They do not override the Chen matched perturbation result: for this finite recoding panel, the local AmpR statistic remains $\widehat\beta=0.0$ with $p=1.0$.

\paragraph{Translation and structure gates.}
The translation-readout layer also remains separated from direct protein realization.  A modeled-tAI survival audit computed nine synonymous residual coordinates, including $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  It used only $3$ organisms, reported $81$ modeled-tAI coupling entries above the threshold $0.01$, and explicitly classified the contact as modeled translation coupling rather than powered ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.  Separately, the structural-order axis audit identified $f3\_stress$ as the single significant coordinate, with sign-flip $p=0.03125$ and weighted held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0031744502294756994$.  These numbers support the existence of finite residual structure and bounded modeled readouts, but they do not supply a positive Chen AmpR perturbation effect.

\paragraph{Comparison with a positive perturbation control.}
The boundary is sharp because another E. coli perturbation surface did yield a positive finite protein-output statistic: the Nieuwkoop mRFP audit used $1459$ variants and reported $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched-null and within-null $p$-values both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  That contrast is useful inside this standalone chapter.  It shows that the testing machinery is capable of recording a positive synonymous perturbation signal when the finite rows support it.  The Chen matched distributed-recoding panel is therefore not being rejected by a blanket no-effect rule; it is classified by its own finite numbers, especially the AmpR slope $0.0$ and independent $p$-value $1.0$.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The supported statement is a bounded null at the protein-output boundary for this matched distributed-recoding surface.  The finite rows permit the claim that the registered $B^*_{Q6}$ E. coli CAI window did not produce an independent AmpR signal on the verified panel:
$$\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{AmpR}}&=47,\\
n_{\mathrm{GmR}}&=53,\\
N_D&=19,\\
\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{AmpR}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{AmpR}}&=1.0.

No broader biological promotion follows from these rows.  In particular, this chapter does not infer translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not admissible here unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled $Q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout step and the downstream protein readout remains meaningful after matched mRNA abundance and the declared sequence controls are included.
