\subsection{Chen distributed recoding as a bounded synonymous-perturbation contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-chen-distributed-recoding-bstar-window}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns the finite perturbation surface obtained from the Chen distributed synonymous-recoding panel after projection to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window basis.  The computed basis used in the registered run is the nine-dimensional E. coli CAI-window surface named \texttt{B\_star\_window\_Q6\_9D\_Ecoli\_CAI\_spatial\_windows\_full\_nt60\_531\_and\_middle3\_drop\_first\_60\_codons}.  The finite rows are split into $47$ AmpR observations and $53$ gmR observations, with $19$ multi-row $D$ strata retained for the distributed-recoding comparison.  The CAI weights were read from the local E. coli genome weight table, and the dataset was not rebuilt during the verification run.  Thus the object being evaluated is not an unconstrained codon-usage summary; it is a specific finite panel of synonymous perturbation rows, a fixed nine-coordinate residual window map, and an explicitly recorded drug-resistance split.

\paragraph{Recorded statistic.}
The Chen panel did not provide a positive AmpR response along this bounded residual direction.  The registered finite-row statistic reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 47,\\
 n_{\mathrm{gmR}} &= 53,\\
 n_D &= 19,\\
 \widehat\beta_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 0.0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{AmpR,independent}} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The estimated AmpR slope is exactly zero in the recorded readout, and the independent AmpR test returns the largest possible $p$-value.  This is a bounded null result for the Chen distributed-recoding surface, not a weak positive effect.  In particular, the panel does not license a statement that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinate raises AmpR output, improves resistance, or transfers a synonymous perturbation signal into a protein-level or phenotype-level conclusion.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation boundary.}
The broader $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ translation-memory context contains nine residual coordinates: \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}.  A separate modeled-tAI contact over three organisms found $81$ entries above the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$; however, that same record states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that the contact is modeled tAI coupling rather than ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.  The Chen perturbation row must therefore be read under the stricter rule: a direct $Q_i \to P_k$ or $Q_i \to$ phenotype association is inadmissible unless the same residual first survives a controlled translation-readout step.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance comparison.}
A distinct cross-organism protein-abundance decomposition supplies useful scale but not a rescue of the Chen row.  In that decomposition, twelve organism panels were joined; for example, E. coli K12 MG1655 used $3739$ joined proteins, yeast used $5815$, and human used $19053$.  The modeled $Q \to T \to P$ scalar fraction was positive in each listed organism, including $0.612922252793667$ for E. coli K12 MG1655 and $0.9491551041216973$ for yeast, while the readout was explicitly modeled per-protein tAI and PAXdb protein abundance.  Those numbers show that a finite mediation computation can be made when codon counts, a bounded translation readout, and protein abundance are joined.  They do not convert the Chen distributed-recoding null into a protein-realization result, because the Chen row itself reports no positive AmpR slope and no matched $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ mediation matrix.

\paragraph{Nearby positive perturbation contact.}
The Nieuwkoop E. coli mRFP perturbation panel illustrates the difference between a bounded positive protein-output contact and the present Chen row.  That separate run used $1459$ variants, obtained $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched-null and within-null $p$-values of $0.004975124378109453$.  The Chen record has none of those positive finite-row signatures: its AmpR slope is $0.0$ and its independent $p$-value is $1.0$.  The comparison is useful only as a calibration of evidential strength.  It does not transfer evidence across datasets, organisms, resistance markers, reporters, or perturbation designs.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a scoped refusal of promotion.  The Chen distributed-recoding panel is admissible as a bounded synonymous-perturbation audit of the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window basis, with $47$ AmpR rows, $53$ gmR rows, and $19$ distributed-recoding strata.  The observed AmpR statistic is null.  Therefore this chapter records that the Chen contact remains at residual-window audit status: it does not establish translation realization, protein abundance realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, drug-resistance mechanism, organism-level phenotype, selection, or a universal biological rule.  Any future promotion would require a separate layer-matched contact supplying a same-row translation readout, matched mRNA control, matched protein or phenotype readout, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablation before the residual coordinate could be read beyond the bounded perturbation surface.
