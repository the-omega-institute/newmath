\subsection{Distributed recoding as a matched protein-output boundary}\label{sec:biorality-chen-distributed-recoding-boundary}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite mediation surface whose rows are distributed synonymous recoding designs and whose coordinates are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual axes used in the codon-window readout.  The run identified the concrete basis as $B^*_{Q6}$ with E. coli CAI spatial windows on the full nucleotide interval $60$--$531$ and on a middle-three-window summary after dropping the first $60$ codons.  The finite panel was not rebuilt during the run, so the chapter reads the reported rows as an already materialized assay table rather than as a new data-construction claim.  The observed row counts were $47$ AmpR rows and $53$ GmR rows, with $19$ multi-row $D$ strata.  The CAI weights were read from a local E. coli genome weight table, and the computation ran for $465.144$ seconds.

\paragraph{Controlled statement.}
The admissible question is not whether a synonymous recoding has biological meaning in general.  It is whether a residual coordinate that has already been kept distinct from amino-acid sequence, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline improves a downstream protein-output readout through a translation-attached route.  In symbols, the finite target is a controlled table of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ with $q_i$ one of the residual coordinates, $t_j$ a translation readout such as modeled tAI, observed ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, and $p_k$ a matched protein-abundance or protein-output readout.  The admissible positive pattern has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow t_j,\\
t_j &\longrightarrow p_k,\\
q_i &\not\rightsquigarrow p_k\text{ as an unmediated protein claim.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The middle arrow is essential: a direct residual-to-protein association is not promoted unless the same residual first survives a translation readout under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Observed outcome in the Chen distributed-recoding row.}
For the Chen distributed-recoding contact, the AmpR channel produced an independent-test value $p=1.0$ and an AmpR slope equal to $0.0$.  Thus this finite row is an honest null for an AmpR-directed residual effect in the registered window basis.  The relevant scalar summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 47,\\
n_{\mathrm{GmR}} &= 53,\\
n_D &= 19,\\
p_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 1.0,\\
\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{AmpR}} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null value is not a failure of the coordinate construction.  It says only that this assay table, in this windowing and control regime, does not support a positive AmpR protein-output readback for the tested residual axis.  The chapter therefore keeps the Chen distributed-recoding packet at the perturbation-boundary level rather than treating it as protein realization.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader mediation matrix.}
A separate multi-organism mediation run gives context for why the translation step is kept explicit.  Across twelve organism-matched protein-abundance surfaces, the joint nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ matrix gave positive statistical mediation fractions when conditioned on modeled per-protein tAI, for example $0.612922252793667$ in E. coli K12 MG1655, $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse.  Those values are bounded statistical decompositions, not causal mechanism claims.  They show that the route $Q\to T\to P$ is a legitimate finite readout shape when the rows contain codon counts, modeled translation weights, matched protein abundance, and controls.  They do not rescue the Chen AmpR null: the Chen row still reports $p=1.0$ and slope $0.0$ for its own finite perturbation surface.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
The modeled translation-survival contact computed the same nine residual coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  It used three organisms, all with standard tRNA-family coverage, and found $81$ modeled-tAI bounded-coupling entries above the threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute coupling $3801961.031607896$.  That run was explicitly underpowered for a broad survival claim because $3$ organisms are not enough for the requested powered translation-survival conclusion.  Hence the Chen row is read with the same discipline: modeled adaptation weights and codon-window geometry may organize the finite test, but they do not by themselves observe ribosome dynamics or protein realization.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
The only local conclusion is a bounded negative or non-promoting one for this distributed-recoding assay surface.  The data support the statement that the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ window basis did not yield an AmpR-associated effect in this finite matched table.  No translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this row.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic computed on joined rows at that layer.  In particular, a protein-output association cannot be inferred from $q_i\to p_k$ alone; the route must pass through a surviving $q_i\to t_j$ readout before it can be discussed as mediated protein abundance.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
This chapter therefore records the Chen distributed-recoding packet as a finite, controlled boundary test with an AmpR null result.  The positive content is the discipline of the readback: $47$ AmpR rows, $53$ GmR rows, $19$ multi-row $D$ strata, the declared $B^*_{Q6}$ E. coli CAI window basis, $p_{\mathrm{AmpR}}=1.0$, and $\widehat\beta_{\mathrm{AmpR}}=0.0$ together define a self-contained assay result.  The negative content is equally important: this row does not promote the residual basis into translation, protein realization, folding, function, mechanism, or universality.
