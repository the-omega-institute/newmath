\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and an RBP occupancy gate}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched transcript or coding-sequence panel in which each row is allowed to carry a synonymous residual coordinate label $q$, an amino-acid quotient class, a same-row translation readout $T$, an RNA half-life readout $H$, local abundance and transcript-state controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy readout $B$ at the editable RNA window. The coordinate contrast is not a nucleotide-sequence claim by itself. It is the comparison between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after amino-acid quotienting, evaluated only after the row has the appropriate biological measurements attached. The positive reading sought here is narrow: a translation-conditioned RNA half-life contrast may be called an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation only when it is accompanied by measured local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss of the effect, restoration by wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite audit surface.}
The row-level RBP binding audit currently available for this subject is finite and explicit. The RBP binding packet is recorded under run \texttt{b225890251c2}; it examined $900$ total peaks and found $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with a runtime of $223.648043$ seconds. These numbers are not yet the desired half-life bridge statistic. They only establish that an RBP-binding-side finite surface has been computed and that the bridge candidate is not being asserted from coordinate notation alone. A linked translation-readout surface is also present in the same research ledger: run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} for the translation packet contains $6494$ rows and completed in $9.469$ seconds. That translation surface is useful as evidence that translation readouts can be represented as finite rows, but it is not itself joined to the RBP occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and RNA half-life rows demanded here.

\paragraph{Statistic to be read.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate label for row $i$, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $B_i$ the measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, and $Z_i$ the control block containing transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch. The intended finite statistic has the following shape:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i &\sim q_i + T_i + B_i + Z_i,\\
B_i &\sim q_i + T_i + Z_i,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &= \Delta_H(q,T,B,Z \mid \text{target-RBP loss}),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT}} &= \Delta_H(q,T,B,Z \mid \text{wild-type rescue}),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{def}} &= \Delta_H(q,T,B,Z \mid \text{binding-defective or non-target control}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The bridge is supported only if the half-life contrast is present in the translation-conditioned rows, tracks measured local occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP, collapses when that target RBP is removed, returns under wild-type rescue, and does not return under binding-defective or non-target control. The declared readback is therefore a conjunction of row matches rather than a single association: coordinate contrast, translation conditioning, RNA half-life residual, local RBP occupancy, perturbation collapse, wild-type rescue, and failed defective or irrelevant rescue must all be available on the same finite panel or through a declared matched-panel join.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null model is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after amino-acid sequence and the control block are fixed. In prose, once transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout are accounted for, the coordinate label must not predict half-life unless the RBP bridge pattern is also present. A half-life difference without a measured local RBP occupancy shift remains an RNA-stability association. A local occupancy association without perturbation collapse and wild-type rescue remains an occupancy correlation. A target-RBP loss effect without failed binding-defective or non-target rescue remains an incomplete perturbation story.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the present rows.}
The present finite evidence is therefore sufficient for a bounded chapter record, but not sufficient for a positive mechanistic bridge. The $900$-peak RBP binding audit and the $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases show that the RBP-binding side has been instantiated as measured finite data. The separate $6494$-row translation audit shows that translation-readout rows exist elsewhere in the finite research surface. What is missing for a positive claim is the joined same-organism, same-window panel containing the residual coordinate label, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target control. Until that panel exists, the record remains a scoped candidate for a translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge, not an established biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Local negative scope.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA decay mechanism follows from coordinate geometry, codon-window notation, RBP binding peaks, or translation-readout routing alone. No protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law is licensed by this packet. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics. In particular, motif density, RNA-structure-only explanations, transcription-rate explanations, isoform-state explanations, transcript-abundance explanations, GC or dinucleotide explanations, codon-pair-context explanations, and batch explanations are not positive evidence for the RBP bridge; they are controls or competing null routes that must be defeated before the bridge wording is allowed.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The current chapter should be read as a precise bounded target: a high-versus-low $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate contrast may be reported at the RNA half-life layer only after same-row translation conditioning, and it may be called an RBP bridge only after the prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern is observed. The finite numbers currently attached to the RBP-binding side are $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds. These values support the existence of a finite RBP-binding audit surface, while the missing same-window half-life, occupancy, loss, and rescue rows prevent promotion to a completed RBP-mediated RNA decay claim.
