\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with a prespecified RBP occupancy bridge}\label{subsec:bior-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The object considered here is not a free-standing sequence law. It is a finite matched transcript or CDS panel whose rows carry three kinds of recorded information: a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout or an explicitly bounded translation surrogate, and an RNA half-life readout joined to matched transcription and abundance controls. The intended bridge to RBP biology is stricter than an RNA-stability association: it requires measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, an experimental loss condition for that target RBP, a wild-type rescue condition, and a binding-defective or non-target control that fails to rescue the effect. Without those rows, the chapter records only a bounded RNA half-life boundary, not an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Finite run evidence.}
The RBP-contact run attached to this record is the ENCODE/RBNS--eCLIP binding run with identifier $\mathrm{b225890251c2}$. Its finite payload contains $900$ total peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with run time $223.648043$ seconds. In symbols, the finite binding surface supplied by the run is only
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
C_{\mathrm{cross\mbox{-}RBP}} &= 3,\\
T_{\mathrm{run}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are enough to say that an RBP-binding contact exists as a finite measured surface. They are not enough to say that the same rows also contain RNA half-life, same-window target occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, and binding-defective control behavior. A separate translation-readout run in the linked finite ledger, $\mathrm{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$, reports $6494$ rows from the GSE36892 source with run time $9.469$ seconds. That run shows that translation readout can be represented as a finite measured table, but it is not itself a joined RNA half-life/RBP perturbation panel for the present claim.

\paragraph{Statistic that would license the bridge.}
Let $q_i\in\{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ be the residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ the target-RBP loss condition, $W_i$ the wild-type rescue condition, and $B_i$ the binding-defective or non-target control. Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline terms. The desired bridge is a finite-row relation of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i &\sim q_i + T_i + O_i + L_i + W_i + B_i + Z_i,\\
\Delta_q(H\mid T,O,Z) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_q(H\mid T,O,Z,L) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_q(H\mid T,O,Z,L,W) &\text{ returns toward the original sign},\\
\Delta_q(H\mid T,O,Z,L,B) &\text{ does not return}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The ordering matters. A coordinate-associated half-life residual is first conditioned on the translation boundary, then checked against local RBP occupancy, then tested for loss collapse and rescue specificity. If the half-life term survives without local occupancy change, if loss of the target RBP does not collapse it, or if a binding-defective or non-target control restores it, the proposed RBP bridge fails.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the control set and the translation readout have been attached. The RBP-binding run contributes $900$ finite peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but those scalar facts alone do not identify a same-window perturbation/rescue design. Therefore the current finite record supports the following conservative reading: RBP-binding evidence is present as a measured contact surface, while the RNA half-life bridge remains conditional on missing same-row occupancy, loss, rescue, and control rows. The observed binding table may motivate a prespecified RBP target list; it does not by itself prove that synonymous residual coordinates alter RNA decay through that RBP.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RBP-mediated decay mechanism follows from an RBP peak table alone. No protein output, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate labels, the codon windows, the RNA half-life contact, the translation contact, or the $900$-peak RBP table unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and a finite statistic is computed on joined rows. The present chapter is therefore a bounded cross-layer readiness record: it identifies the finite RBP-binding surface and the required RNA half-life/translation/RBP perturbation joins, while withholding the positive RBP-decay bridge until those rows exist in the same matched panel.
