\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and a decay-factor RBP bridge}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row object, not a general law about coding sequence.  A row consists of a transcript or CDS identifier, a synonymous residual coordinate label from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate system after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, a local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls for abundance, composition, isoform state, and batch.  The intended contrast is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels at editable RNA windows, with the amino-acid sequence held fixed.  The bridge claim is narrower still: a coordinate-associated half-life residual may be called an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation only when the finite panel also shows local target-RBP occupancy at the relevant window, loss of the half-life effect after target-RBP removal, restoration under wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite counts.}
The current RBP-binding run for the claim identifier $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode$ reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant hits, with a runtime of $223.648043$ seconds.  Thus the presently observed concordance fraction in this finite scan is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat h_{\mathrm{RBP}} &= \frac{3}{900} = 0.003333\ldots .
\end{aligned}
$$
This number is a contact-level occupancy concordance count, not by itself a decay mechanism.  It gives a finite surface on which an RBP bridge could be tested, but it does not yet contain the same-window perturbation and rescue rows needed to promote an RNA half-life association to a mechanism-level RBP statement.

\paragraph{Control geometry.}
The null model is row-exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and same-row translation readout.  In symbols, if $Q_i$ is the residual coordinate label, $H_i$ is the half-life residual, $T_i$ is the translation readout, $O_i$ is target-RBP occupancy, and $Z_i$ is the finite control vector, the positive bridge requires a pattern of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &\not\perp H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\\
Q_i &\not\perp O_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\\
Q_i &\perp H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{target\ loss},\\
Q_i &\not\perp H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue},\\
Q_i &\perp H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first line alone is only a bounded RNA-stability association.  The second line alone is only a local binding association.  The loss-and-rescue pattern is the finite discriminant that separates an RBP bridge from a coordinate descriptor, an RNA-structure explanation, a motif-density explanation, a transcription-rate explanation, or a batch artifact.

\paragraph{Comparison with nearby finite runs.}
Several linked finite contacts demonstrate why this chapter keeps the RBP statement bounded.  The mTOR translation run contains $6494$ rows and took $9.469$ seconds, but its readout is a translation-layer surface rather than an RNA half-life or RBP perturbation panel.  The poly(A)-tail run contains $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows and reports no cross-species concordance; it therefore cannot supply a decay-factor RBP bridge for this coordinate window.  The methylation flanking-context run contains $20000$ probes over $656$ individuals with baseline $R^2 = 0.0129735$, which is a separate DNA-methylation context surface rather than an RNA-binding or RNA-decay assay.  The meiotic recombination run contains $52963$ hotspot rows and is likewise a distinct contact class.  These runs are useful finite comparators because they show that row count and cross-layer naming do not determine mechanism: each contact must carry the right observable layer before it can support a claim at that layer.

\paragraph{Readback rule.}
The finite readback accepted by this chapter is the following ordered panel: translation-conditioned half-life effect, measured local target-RBP occupancy change, collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The current $900$-peak RBP run with $3$ concordant cross-RBP hits can enter the occupancy part of that panel, but it does not fill the perturbation and rescue coordinates.  Therefore the present record is a bounded RBP-binding contact aligned with a proposed RNA half-life bridge, not a completed mechanistic demonstration.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological rule follows from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate label, from the $900$ peak rows, from the $3$ concordant RBP hits, or from the presence of matched transcript contacts.  A separate layer-matched contact is required for each such assertion.  In particular, ribosome profiling is needed for translation readout, proteomics is needed for protein abundance, structure or physical assays are needed for folding and admissibility, perturbation or functional assays are needed for function, and phenotype assays are needed for organism- or system-level claims.  Until the same-window occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows are attached, the scientifically licensed statement is only that a finite RBP-binding surface is available for a stricter translation-conditioned RNA half-life bridge test.
