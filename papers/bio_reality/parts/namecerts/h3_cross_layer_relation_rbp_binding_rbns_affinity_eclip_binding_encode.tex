\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-occupancy boundary}\label{subsec:bior reality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.} The finite object in this record is a matched transcript or coding-sequence table whose rows carry a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout or an explicitly declared translation boundary, an RNA half-life readout, and a proposed local occupancy surface for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein. The relevant contrast is not merely high-coordinate versus low-coordinate sequence geometry. It is the conjunction of a coordinate contrast, a translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual, measured occupancy at the editable RNA window, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and a binding-defective or non-target rescue control.

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence.} The available finite RBP-binding run is the row set named by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}. Its recorded run id is \texttt{b225890251c2}. The run reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant rows, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds. Thus the present evidence can record that a finite RBP-binding surface exists and that only a small concordant subset was observed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
N_{\mathrm{concordant\ RBP}} &= 3,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{concordant}} &= 3/900 = 0.003333\ldots .
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a binding-row statistic, not by itself an RNA-decay mechanism. The same ledger contains a separate translation-readout row for an mTOR translation experiment, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012}, with $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds. It also contains a poly(A)-tail-length row, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.polya\_tail\_length.cpe\_grammar\_polya\_taillength\_subtelny2014}, with $8854$ human rows, $3990$ yeast rows, runtime $31.5212$ seconds, and \texttt{cross\_species\_concordant=false}. These neighboring facts are useful only as separated finite surfaces: they show that translation and RNA-stability-related measurements can be represented as finite tables, but they do not join the $900$ RBP peak rows into a same-window half-life perturbation-and-rescue panel.

\paragraph{Admissible readout.} A positive bridge in this chapter would require a row-wise statistic of the following form. Let $Q_i$ be the residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout or translation-conditioned residual, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, and $O_i$ the measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the relevant RNA window. A bridge claim is admissible only if the high-versus-low coordinate contrast in $H_i$ survives the declared controls after conditioning on $T_i$, tracks an experimentally decoupled change in $O_i$, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target controls. In compact notation, the intended finite test has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= E[H_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{high}, Z_i, T_i] - E[H_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{low}, Z_i, T_i],\\
\Delta_O &\neq 0 \quad \text{at the same editable window},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_i$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and any declared simpler baseline. Without all of these row joins, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Present verdict for the finite rows.} The current verified row values do not satisfy the positive bridge test. The $900$-peak RBP run and the $3$ concordant rows are enough to justify a finite binding-contact record, but they do not provide same-organism same-window RNA half-life, measured decay-factor occupancy at the edited window, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective and non-target rescue rows. The $6494$-row translation entry is not the missing half-life/RBP perturbation panel, and the poly(A) row with $8854$ human and $3990$ yeast rows does not create a coordinate-conditioned decay-factor RBP mechanism. Consequently, the scientifically licensed statement is negative and bounded: the record defines a testable cross-layer target and a finite RBP-binding surface, but it does not license a realized RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.} No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No measured half-life difference may be read as a decay-factor RBP mechanism unless the same-window RBP occupancy changes in the required direction, the target-RBP loss removes the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail. The present rows also do not establish protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
