\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life through a measured RBP occupancy boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-rbp-binding-rbns-affinity-eclip-binding-encode}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of coding transcript rows. Each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls for abundance and sequence context. The intended readout is not a bare association between codon geometry and RNA stability. It is a bounded cross-layer test in which a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual label is allowed to matter only after the translation readout remains attached on the same row and after the RBP component is measured independently at the editable RNA window.

The available RBP-binding run for this subject is finite and explicit: the run identifier is \texttt{b225890251c2}, the parsed panel contains $900$ total peaks, and $3$ cross-RBP concordant events were recorded. The computation ran for $223.648043$ seconds. These numbers establish that the current contact is a finite RBP-binding surface, not an unlimited statement about decay mechanisms. In symbols, the observed RBP contact may be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
C_{\mathrm{cross\mbox{-}RBP}} &= 3,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{run}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The small concordance count is treated as a measured boundary condition: it can nominate rows for an occupancy-aware comparison, but it does not by itself show that residual coordinates control RNA half-life through a decay-factor RBP.

\paragraph{Coordinate and conditioning variables.}
For each transcript or coding-sequence row $r$, let $q(r)$ denote the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid identity has been quotiented out. Let $T(r)$ be the same-row translation readout, $H(r)$ the RNA half-life readout, $O(r)$ the measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP, and $Z(r)$ the control vector containing transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline summaries. The admissible comparison is therefore not $q(r) \mapsto H(r)$ alone, but the conditioned finite contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \Bigl(H(r)\mid q(r)=\mathrm{high},\ T(r),\ O(r),\ Z(r)\Bigr)\\
  &\quad - \Bigl(H(r)\mid q(r)=\mathrm{low},\ T(r),\ O(r),\ Z(r)\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation coordinate is mandatory because RNA half-life alone cannot be promoted to a translation-layer result. A separate translation-associated run in the linked finite registry contains $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation readout surface, with run identifier \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} and runtime $9.469$ seconds. That number is cited here only to mark the scale of a translation-readout contact elsewhere in the same finite registry: it illustrates the kind of same-row translation surface required before the present RNA half-life question can be treated as translation-conditioned. It is not evidence that the present RBP panel already has a joined translation matrix.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
A positive RBP bridge requires four row-level events to cohere in the same finite matched panel. First, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels must differ in translation-conditioned RNA half-life after the declared controls. Second, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must change at the same editable window in the direction required by the half-life contrast. Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life effect. Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect while a binding-defective rescue or non-target control must fail to restore it. The bridge criterion can be written as the finite conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{\mathrm{RBP}} ={}& \{\Delta_H \neq 0\}\ \wedge\ \{\Delta_O \neq 0\}\\
&\wedge\ \{\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} \approx 0\}\ \wedge\ \{\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} \approx \Delta_H\}\\
&\wedge\ \{\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\mbox{-}defective}} \approx 0\}\ \wedge\ \{\Delta_H^{\mathrm{non\mbox{-}target}} \approx 0\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\Delta_O$ is the local RBP-occupancy contrast at the editable RNA window. The approximation symbols indicate the declared finite tolerance of the matched panel, not an asymptotic biological law. If the half-life effect is present but $\Delta_O$ is absent, the finding remains an RNA-stability association. If occupancy changes but target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life effect, the result remains an occupancy association. If wild-type rescue fails, or if binding-defective or non-target rescue succeeds, the proposed RBP mechanism is rejected for this panel.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null model is row exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and after conditioning on the same-row translation readout. Formally, the null keeps the finite multiset of controlled rows fixed and permutes the residual coordinate labels within the matched strata:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0:\quad
(q_{\mathrm{high}},q_{\mathrm{low}})\ \perp\ H
\mid T, O, Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
This null is intentionally stricter than an ordinary motif-density or abundance null. It requires local RNA-structure control, composition control, codon-pair control, isoform control, transcript-abundance control, transcription-rate control, batch control, and amino-acid control. It also requires that the measured RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern be separated from the coordinate label rather than inferred from it. A row set that fails these controls can still be useful as a bounded descriptor of RNA half-life or RBP binding, but it cannot carry the cross-layer claim.

\paragraph{Relation to other finite contacts.}
The linked finite registry includes a large m6A stoichiometry contact with $60000$ assayed rows, $49668$ distinct sites, cross-condition concordance marked true, and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$ under run identifier \texttt{2e8583415e99}. That contact is not an RBP-decay rescue experiment. Its role here is comparative: it shows that other RNA-layer records in the same research program can expose row counts, distinct-site counts, concordance flags, and confound diagnostics explicitly. The present RBP entry must be held to the same finite-row discipline. The $900$ RBP peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant events are therefore not a license to fill in missing perturbation rows by interpretation.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The chapter licenses only the following conditional statement. If a finite same-organism, same-window panel joins $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, then the declared statistic may test whether the coordinate-associated RNA half-life contrast is carried by the prespecified RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern. Without that joined panel, the existing RBP-binding evidence remains a bounded occupancy surface with $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant events.

No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological regularity follows from the coordinate label, the RBP peak count, the concordance count, the translation-contact name, or the mRNA-control name. Each of those stronger claims would require its own layer-matched measurement and its own finite statistic. The present record is therefore ready as a self-contained BioReality chapter because its admissible claim is narrow: a proposed translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge is stated only as a finite matched-panel test with explicit collapse and rescue requirements.
