\subsection{A finite RBP-occupancy boundary for translation-conditioned RNA half-life}
\label{sec:bioreality-rbp-binding-rbns-eclip-encode}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a bounded RNA-side cross-layer packet. Its rows are finite matched transcript or coding-sequence entries carrying a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout or translation-efficiency boundary, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein when such occupancy is available, and matched negative or reduced-binding controls. The biological question is not whether a synonymous residual coordinate exists: that is an indexing construction on coding windows. The question is whether a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label remains associated with RNA half-life after the ordinary transcript-level explanations have been held fixed, and whether that remaining signal is specifically routed through a measured decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.

The finite audit row for the RBP screen is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
C_{\mathrm{cross\mbox{-}RBP}} &= 3,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $N_{\mathrm{peaks}}$ is the total finite peak count inspected by the RBNS/eCLIP/ENCODE-linked RBP occupancy routine, and $C_{\mathrm{cross\mbox{-}RBP}}$ is the number of cross-RBP concordant outcomes retained by that run. These numbers license only a finite occupancy-contact statement. They do not by themselves supply RNA half-life mediation, RBP loss, rescue, or binding-defective control behavior.

\paragraph{Coordinate split and null model.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate label attached to row $i$, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the local window, and $Z_i$ the declared control vector. The control vector includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the translation readout itself. The matched null is the exchangeability statement
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \mid (q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i) &\sim H_i \mid (q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i),\\
O_i \mid (q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i) &\sim O_i \mid (q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
with the additional requirement that any apparent half-life separation must fail to count as an RBP bridge unless it follows the measured occupancy perturbation pattern. In words, a half-life difference is not enough. A local occupancy difference is not enough. The bridge requires a finite rowwise chain in which the coordinate-conditioned half-life residual tracks experimentally separated local RBP occupancy, collapses after target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and is not restored by binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Bridge statistic.}
A positive bridge would require a finite statistic of the following form. First compute a translation-conditioned half-life residual $\widehat{\Delta H}_{Q6}$ after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$. Then compute the local occupancy contrast $\widehat{\Delta O}_{RBP}$ for the prespecified decay-factor RBP in the same RNA window. Finally compare the target-loss and rescue panels:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\Delta H}_{Q6} &= \widehat{E}[H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i]-\widehat{E}[H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i],\\
\widehat{\Delta O}_{RBP} &= \widehat{E}[O_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i]-\widehat{E}[O_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i],\\
B_{RBP} &= \mathbf{1}\{\widehat{\Delta H}_{Q6}\neq 0\}\mathbf{1}\{\widehat{\Delta O}_{RBP}\neq 0\}\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{loss\ collapse}\}\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}\}\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{defective\ rescue\ fails}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite RBP audit supplies the occupancy side of this design only at the level of a bounded contact screen: $900$ peak rows were inspected and $3$ cross-RBP concordant outcomes were retained. It does not supply the full value of $B_{RBP}$, because the loss, rescue, and binding-defective control rows are not present in the supplied finite data.

\paragraph{Comparison scalar from the RNA-side audit ledger.}
The same finite survey contains an RNA-modification check with $n=31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct sites, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$, and concordance across cells. That companion scalar is useful only as a calibration of evidential discipline: a row-rich RNA-side measurement can report stoichiometric or occupancy structure without becoming a universal sequence law. The RBP chapter uses the same discipline. Its $900$ RBP peak rows and $3$ concordant cross-RBP outcomes are treated as a bounded screen, not as proof that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate causes RNA decay.

\paragraph{Readback rule.}
The permitted readback is therefore narrow. The chapter may say that an externally measured RBP occupancy screen exists for this conjecture family, with $900$ inspected peaks and $3$ retained cross-RBP concordant outcomes. It may also say that a positive translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge would require same-organism, same-window rows containing residual coordinate labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy separation, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. The present finite data do not contain all of those rows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RBP-mediated decay mechanism follows from a coordinate-associated half-life difference unless the same-window measured occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and failed binding-defective or non-target rescue conditions are attached. No binding-defective rescue, non-target control, RNA-structure-only model, motif-density-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, transcript-abundance model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model counts as support for the RBP bridge. The finite RBP screen also says nothing by itself about protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. Those would require separate layer-matched measurements and separate finite statistics.
