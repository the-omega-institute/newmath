\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life with a Decay-Factor RBP Gate}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel whose rows are coding transcript or CDS windows carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls. The coordinate contrast is not an amino-acid contrast: high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows are compared only after quotienting by amino-acid sequence and after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation boundary. The supplied RBP-contact run for this subject is $\mathrm{experiment\_run\_id}=\texttt{b225890251c2}$, with $900$ total peak rows inspected and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases reported in $223.648043$ seconds. These numbers are finite contact data for an RBP occupancy surface; by themselves they are not an RNA half-life experiment, a perturbation experiment, or a rescue experiment.

\paragraph{Claim tested by the panel.}
The admissible positive statement is deliberately narrow. A coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be read as a bounded cross-layer RBP-mediated RNA-stability bridge only when the same finite rows show the translation-conditioned half-life effect, a local measured occupancy change for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapse of the effect after target-RBP loss, return of the effect under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and failure of rescue under a binding-defective target-RBP or non-target control. In symbols, the row design asks for a finite statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q \mid T,Z),\quad \Delta O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(q \mid Z),\quad
\Delta H(q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{loss}),\\
&\Delta H(q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{WT\ rescue}),\quad
\Delta H(q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{defective\ rescue}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q$ is the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $H$ is RNA half-life, $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is measured local target-RBP occupancy, and $Z$ contains the transcriptional, sequence-composition, isoform, abundance, structure, batch, and simpler-baseline controls. The bridge is supported only if the signs and losses line up across these five finite readbacks: occupancy tracks the coordinate window after decoupling, target-RBP loss removes the half-life residual, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue does not restore it.

\paragraph{Observed contact strength.}
The RBP-binding evidence supplied here is a finite occupancy-oriented contact: $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases. That is enough to document that an RBP-binding surface has been touched by finite data, but it is not enough to certify the full RNA-decay bridge. A separate translation contact in the same evidence ledger, $\mathrm{experiment\_run\_id}=\texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$, reports $6494$ translation-readout rows for the mTOR translation subject in $9.469$ seconds. This second scalar matters only as a layer comparison: it shows that translation readouts can be represented as finite rows, while not supplying the same-row $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue matrix required here. Thus the present chapter keeps the RBP occupancy surface, translation boundary, mRNA abundance control, and RNA half-life boundary distinct until a single joined panel supplies all row coordinates simultaneously.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the amino-acid quotient and the full control set. More explicitly, after conditioning on $T$ and $Z$, the null requires
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(q_{\mathrm{high}} \mid T,Z) \sim H(q_{\mathrm{low}} \mid T,Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
up to the declared finite statistic and its rank-sufficiency checks. Even if this half-life null is rejected, the RBP-mediated interpretation remains false unless the measured local target-RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern also pass. A half-life difference with unchanged local RBP occupancy is only a bounded RNA-stability association. A local occupancy association without coordinate/occupancy decoupling is only an occupancy correlation. Target-RBP loss without wild-type rescue, or apparent rescue by a binding-defective or non-target control, breaks the RBP-decay reading.

\paragraph{Control exclusions.}
The local controls are part of the mathematical content rather than optional statistical decoration. RNA-structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair-context, and batch explanations remain live unless explicitly represented in $Z$ and defeated in the declared finite statistic. The RBP must also be externally prespecified as a decay-factor candidate; the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry is not allowed to choose an RBP after seeing the outcome. The supplied $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases among $900$ peak rows can motivate a finite occupancy boundary, but they do not replace target-specific loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA-decay mechanism follows from a same-row translation readout alone. No measured RBP bridge follows from an occupancy contact unless the same-window perturbation and rescue rows are also present. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from this chapter. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics. The present verdict is therefore a bounded design-level record: it specifies the finite row pattern under which a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate could be connected to translation-conditioned RNA half-life through a decay-factor RBP, and it records that the supplied finite facts currently reach the RBP-binding and translation-readout surfaces without yet supplying the complete joined loss/rescue RNA-decay panel.
