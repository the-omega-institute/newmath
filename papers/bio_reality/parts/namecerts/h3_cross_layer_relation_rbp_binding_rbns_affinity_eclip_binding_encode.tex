\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP occupancy gate}\label{sec:bioreality-rbp-binding-rbns-affinity-eclip-binding-encode}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row target for a possible cross-layer relation between synonymous residual coordinates and RNA stability.  A row is eligible only when it carries a coding-transcript or CDS identifier, an amino-acid-quotiented $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout or an explicitly marked translation-readout boundary, an RNA half-life readout, a measured local occupancy field for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls.  The relevant coordinate contrast is the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual label after the amino-acid quotient has already removed amino-acid identity as the direct explanation.  The biological question is not whether the coordinate alone predicts half-life, but whether a half-life contrast remains after translation conditioning and is carried through a measured RBP binding route.

The finite audit row for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} reports $900$ total RBP peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with a recorded runtime of $223.648043$ seconds.  These numbers are enough to identify a small finite RBP-binding surface, but they do not by themselves supply the perturbation and rescue rows needed for a mechanistic RNA-decay claim.  In the same audit bundle, the translation readout record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} reports $n=6494$ rows and a runtime of $9.469$ seconds.  That second row is used here only to mark the kind of translation surface that must be joined before RNA half-life can be interpreted as translation-conditioned; it is not treated as an RBP occupancy, RNA half-life, or rescue experiment.

\paragraph{Finite statistic and null model.}
Let $q_i\in\{0,1\}$ denote the low- or high-coordinate residual label for row $i$, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, and let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and any simpler baseline codon controls.  A bounded RBP bridge is permitted only when the finite panel supports the ordered pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow H_i \mid (T_i,Z_i),\\
q_i &\longrightarrow O_i \mid (T_i,Z_i),\\
\text{target-RBP loss} &\text{ collapses the } q_i\text{-associated half-life term},\\
\text{wild-type rescue} &\text{ restores the term},\\
\text{binding-defective or non-target rescue} &\text{ fails to restore it}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched null is stricter than an ordinary association null.  After conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$, high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to $H_i$.  If a half-life difference is present but $O_i$ is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it, the observation remains a bounded RNA-stability association rather than an RBP bridge.  If binding-defective or non-target rescue restores the effect, the proposed bridge is rejected because the putative binding route is no longer the discriminating cause in the finite panel.

\paragraph{Readback from the available rows.}
The available RBP-binding audit result is compatible with a finite binding screen: $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases show that the record has an RBP occupancy-facing surface rather than a purely symbolic coordinate entry.  This is useful but deliberately weak.  It does not certify that the same transcript windows also carry RNA half-life, same-row translation readout, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control rows.  The translation audit row with $6494$ entries supplies a separate example of a translation-scale measurement surface, but it is not joined here to the $900$ RBP peak rows at the same transcript windows.  Therefore the finite numbers support only a bounded candidate relation: an RBP-binding surface exists, and a translation-readout surface exists in the broader evidence bundle, but the required same-row bridge panel is not present in the supplied data.

This chapter therefore records a conditional rule rather than a positive mechanistic conclusion.  A positive call would require a joined table in which the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate contrast, translation readout, RNA half-life, measured local RBP occupancy, RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control all live on the same finite rows or on an explicitly justified matched-window join.  In such a table the statistic must remain finite: the row count, the effect estimate for $q_i$ on translation-conditioned $H_i$, the occupancy contrast, the target-RBP-loss dropout, the wild-type-rescue return, the defective-rescue failure, and the declared $p$-values or interval estimates must all be named in the table rather than inferred from narrative proximity.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA-decay RBP mechanism follows from an RBP peak list alone.  No protein output, folding behavior, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causal law, or universal sequence principle follows from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry, the $900$-peak RBP-binding surface, the $3$ concordant RBP cases, or the separate $6494$-row translation audit surface.  Those stronger conclusions would require their own layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  The present record is ready as a self-contained BioReality chapter because it states the finite carrier, the observed scalar data, the null, the required bridge pattern, and the exact reason the current rows stop short of a positive RBP-mediated RNA half-life claim.
