\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With a Prespecified RBP Bridge}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This BioReality record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate window is kept separate from amino-acid identity and is then joined to same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched control rows. The coordinate contrast is not a claim about protein output. It is a finite label contrast: high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows after quotienting by amino-acid sequence. The intended biological object is narrower than a general codon-usage or transcript-stability claim. It asks whether a residual coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference can be read as a bounded cross-layer RBP bridge only when the same finite rows also contain a translation boundary and an experimentally decoupled RBP-occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern.

\paragraph{Observed finite audit surface.}
The only directly verified finite RBP-binding audit number attached to this claim is the RBNS-affinity/eCLIP-binding/ENCODE run identified by \texttt{b225890251c2}. It records $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds. In compact notation, the recorded surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
C_{\mathrm{cross\text{-}RBP}} &= 3,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{run}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are sufficient to anchor a finite RBP-binding contact surface, but they are not sufficient by themselves to certify the RNA-half-life bridge required here. The record says that some RBP-binding concordance was observed in the finite binding audit; it does not say that the same rows carry RNA half-life, translation efficiency, local decay-factor occupancy at the editable window, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.

\paragraph{Translation boundary used as a conditioning surface.}
A separate translation-layer audit in the linked finite ledger is the mTOR translation run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, which contains $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds. This number is not used as evidence for the RBP mechanism. Its role in this chapter is only to emphasize the difference between a translation readout surface and an RNA-decay RBP-occupancy mechanism. The translation-side row count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{mTOR\ translation}} &= 6494,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{mTOR\ run}} &= 9.469\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A translation readout can condition an RNA half-life residual only after the rows are joined at the same organism and transcript or CDS identity. It cannot, merely by being present, turn an RNA half-life association into an RBP-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null model is row exchangeability between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows after controlling amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout. Written as a finite matched-row condition, the target contrast has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0:\quad &q_{\mathrm{high}} \sim q_{\mathrm{low}}\ \text{with respect to RNA half-life}\mid Z, T, A,\\
Z &= \{\text{transcription, structure, motif density, composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, abundance, batch, amino-acid identity}\},\\
T &= \text{same-row translation readout},\\
A &= \text{measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $A$ is not optional. If RNA half-life differs while measured local RBP occupancy is unchanged, or if the half-life effect remains after target-RBP loss, or if wild-type rescue fails to restore the effect, the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than the bridge claimed by this record.

\paragraph{Positive-readback criterion.}
A positive readback requires a finite matched panel with all of the following conditions on the same row family. First, the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate contrast must survive amino-acid quotienting and the declared transcript-level controls. Second, RNA half-life must be evaluated after conditioning on the same-row translation readout and matched mRNA abundance. Third, the local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must change at the relevant RNA window in a way experimentally decoupled from the coordinate label itself. Fourth, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life residual. Fifth, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore the residual pattern. Sixth, a binding-defective rescue or non-target control must fail to restore it. The bridge statistic therefore has the schematic finite-row form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H(q) &= H(q_{\mathrm{high}})-H(q_{\mathrm{low}}),\\
\Delta H(q\mid T,Z,A) &\neq 0\quad \text{only if local target-RBP occupancy participates},\\
\Delta H_{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta H_{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta H(q\mid T,Z,A),\\
\Delta H_{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta H_{\mathrm{non\ target}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The symbols describe the intended finite statistic, not a completed positive result. The verified RBP audit has $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, but it does not yet provide the loss/rescue rows needed to instantiate every line of this criterion.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the RBP-binding audit.}
The verified RBP-binding run is useful because it gives the chapter a bounded empirical surface rather than an entirely free biological narrative. It says that the RBP-binding side has a finite peak universe and a measured concordance count. The small concordance count, $3$ out of $900$ total peaks in the recorded audit, should be treated conservatively: it can motivate a prespecified bridge test, but it cannot be promoted into evidence for RNA decay, translation realization, or mechanism without the same-row perturbation and rescue panel. The chapter therefore records an eligible finite question, not a completed mechanistic conclusion.

\paragraph{Controls that block over-reading.}
The following alternatives remain live unless explicitly defeated on the finite matched panel: RNA-structure-only explanations, motif-density-only explanations, transcription-rate effects, isoform-state effects, transcript-abundance effects, GC or dinucleotide effects, codon-pair-context effects, batch effects, and mismatched translation-readout effects. A binding-defective rescue, a non-target rescue, or an occupancy association without perturbation collapse is not supporting evidence for the RBP bridge. Those outcomes instead return the coordinate to one of two weaker statuses: a bounded descriptor of synonymous residual geometry or a finite RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not license translation output, mature protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the specified RBP-decay test, or universality. A same-row translation readout is only a conditioning surface. An RNA half-life value is only a stability readout. An RBP peak or occupancy value is only a binding contact unless perturbation and rescue behavior are attached. No conclusion about protein structure, function, system phenotype, or a general biological law follows from the $900$-peak RBP audit, the $3$ cross-RBP-concordant count, or the $6494$-row translation audit without a separate layer-matched contact that directly measures the claimed layer.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The record is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because it has a concrete finite RBP-binding audit and a stated row-level test that separates coordinate labels, translation conditioning, RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, and rescue controls. Its scientific status remains conditional. The present finite numbers establish the existence and scale of the RBP-binding audit surface, while the positive RBP-mediated RNA-half-life bridge still requires a same-organism, same-window joined panel carrying the missing occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control rows.
