\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life boundary for a Q6 RBP bridge}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel in which each eligible row is a transcript or coding-sequence window with a synonymous $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid identity. The intended row also carries a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance or transcription controls, local sequence-composition controls, and an independently specified decay-factor RBP occupancy measurement at the editable RNA window. The carrier is therefore not a sequence law and not a protein-output claim. It is a finite relation among coordinate labels, translation-conditioned RNA stability residuals, measured RBP occupancy, and perturbation or rescue behavior on matched rows.

\paragraph{Observed finite contact already present.}
The available RBP-binding contact for this subject is the finite RBNS/eCLIP/ENCODE binding screen recorded under run $b225890251c2$. It contains $900$ total peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds. In local notation the available scalar summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
N_{\mathrm{concordant\ RBP}} &= 3,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{run}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support only the existence of a finite binding-readout surface. They do not by themselves identify an RNA-decay mechanism, because the run summary does not include same-window RNA half-life, same-row translation efficiency, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue rows.

\paragraph{Translation conditioning requirement.}
The translation boundary is mandatory because an RNA half-life association can otherwise be read as a transcript-level stability association rather than a translation-conditioned cross-layer relation. A separate translation-readout contact in the finite ledger, run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, reports $n=6494$ rows from a translation dataset and runtime $9.469$ seconds. This scalar confirms that translation readout can be represented as finite row data, but it is not yet joined to the RBP/half-life rows of the present subject. Thus the intended statistic may only be evaluated after a same-organism, same-row join of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= (q_r,T_r,H_r,A_r,O_r,L_r,C_r),\\
q_r &\in \{\mathrm{high\ }B^*_{Q6},\mathrm{low\ }B^*_{Q6}\},\\
T_r &= \mathrm{translation\ readout},\\
H_r &= \mathrm{RNA\ half\mbox{-}life\ readout},\\
A_r &= \mathrm{matched\ abundance\ or\ transcription\ control},\\
O_r &= \mathrm{measured\ local\ decay\mbox{-}factor\ RBP\ occupancy},\\
L_r &= \mathrm{target\mbox{-}RBP\ loss\ and\ rescue\ condition},\\
C_r &= \mathrm{composition,\ structure,\ isoform,\ codon\mbox{-}pair,\ batch\ controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The key restriction is that $H_r$ is interpreted only after conditioning on $T_r$ and the declared controls. Without $T_r$ on the same row, the result remains an RNA-stability descriptor.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
A positive RBP bridge requires a coupled pattern, not merely a significant half-life contrast. High-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels must differ in translation-conditioned RNA half-life after amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch have been controlled. In addition, the effect must track measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP. It must collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and fail to return under a binding-defective rescue or a non-target control. The local acceptance condition can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q\mid T,C) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_O(q\mid C) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H(q\mid T,C,\mathrm{target\ loss}) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H(q\mid T,C,\mathrm{wild\mbox{-}type\ rescue}) &\approx \Delta_H(q\mid T,C),\\
\Delta_H(q\mid T,C,\mathrm{binding\mbox{-}defective\ rescue}) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equations describe the required finite-row pattern only; the supplied run summary has not yet measured all five quantities on one matched panel.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null says that, after amino-acid sequence and the declared transcript, structure, composition, codon-context, isoform, abundance, batch, and translation-readout controls are attached, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. A half-life difference that lacks measured RBP occupancy change is not an RBP bridge. An occupancy difference that is not decoupled from coordinate and motif confounding is not enough. A target-RBP loss effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure is also insufficient. Under the current finite facts, the correct status is therefore a bounded candidate relation with an explicit missing perturbation and rescue panel, not a completed mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no translation realization from RNA half-life alone, no folding claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no biological-function claim, no phenotype claim, no adaptation claim, no causation claim, and no universal sequence law. The $900$ RBP peak rows and $3$ concordant RBP cases are binding-surface evidence only. The $6494$ translation rows in the separate translation run show that finite translation readout can be handled, but they are not a same-row proof for this RBP subject. Any stronger interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact supplying the missing measurement rather than an inference from coordinate geometry or from the presence of contact names.
