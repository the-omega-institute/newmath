\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP occupancy gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-rbp-binding-rbns-affinity-eclip-binding-encode}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row design for coding transcripts in which each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label from $B^*_{Q6}$, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and a proposed decay-factor RBP occupancy surface at the editable RNA window.  The residual coordinate is used only after quotienting by amino-acid identity: high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels are compared within rows that are already protected against amino-acid-sequence promotion.  The relevant finite observation currently attached to the RBP-binding surface is the run $b225890251c2$, which reports $900$ total RBP peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  In symbols, the attached RBP screen has the finite summary
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
N_{\mathrm{cross\mbox{-}RBP\ concordant}} &= 3,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 223.648043\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough to state a bounded RBP-binding contact surface, but it is not by itself an RNA-decay mechanism.  The translation side is represented separately by the finite translation readout run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, where $6494$ rows were available for a translation-response surface and the runtime was $9.469\ \mathrm{s}$.  The chapter therefore treats translation readout, RNA half-life, and RBP occupancy as three distinct row columns that must be joined before any cross-layer relation is asserted.

\paragraph{Claim tested.}
The admissible positive claim is deliberately narrow.  After controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout, a $B^*_{Q6}$-associated RNA half-life residual may be called an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge only if the direction of the half-life effect follows experimentally separated local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  In addition, the effect must collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and fail to return under a binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The row-level contrast has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}} &= E[\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}\mid q_{\mathrm{high}}, Z, T]-E[\mathrm{half\mbox{-}life}\mid q_{\mathrm{low}}, Z, T],\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{occ}} &= E[\mathrm{RBP\ occupancy}\mid q_{\mathrm{high}}, Z, T]-E[\mathrm{RBP\ occupancy}\mid q_{\mathrm{low}}, Z, T],
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes the non-translation controls and $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout.  The bridge is licensed only when $\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{occ}}$ are directionally compatible, the target-RBP perturbation removes the half-life contrast, and wild-type rescue restores the contrast while binding-defective or non-target rescue does not.

\paragraph{Observed status.}
The present finite evidence does not yet satisfy that bridge test.  The RBP-binding run supplies a bounded peak-level contact with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but the attached memory does not contain same-organism, same-window target-RBP loss rows, wild-type rescue rows, binding-defective rescue rows, or non-target rescue rows for the same $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate windows.  The translation run supplies a separate finite translation-response surface with $6494$ rows, but that run is not an RNA half-life experiment, not an RBP occupancy perturbation panel, and not a rescue assay.  Consequently the current finite table can support only a scoped statement: the RBP peak surface and the translation-readout surface are available as separate contacts, while the full RNA half-life RBP-bridge statistic remains uncomputed.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is row exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls are attached.  Formally, before the occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows are joined, the admissible null statement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0:\quad q_{\mathrm{high}} \sim q_{\mathrm{low}}\ \text{for RNA half-life conditional on } Z,T.
\end{aligned}
$$
A half-life difference alone would not reject the mechanistic null for the RBP bridge.  It would remain an RNA-stability association unless measured local occupancy changes, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure are all present in the same matched panel.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This record does not claim translation realization from RNA half-life alone, and it does not claim that a codon-coordinate label creates an RBP mechanism.  It also does not claim protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.  Those conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  Here the only defensible statement is that $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate windows define a candidate row partition, the RBP-binding run gives a finite occupancy-like binding surface with $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and the translation run gives a distinct finite translation readout with $6494$ rows.  The positive RNA half-life RBP bridge remains conditional on the missing same-window half-life, occupancy-decoupling, perturbation, and rescue matrix.
