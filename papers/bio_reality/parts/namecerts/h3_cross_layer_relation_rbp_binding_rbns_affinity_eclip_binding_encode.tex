\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP bridge}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The object of this chapter is a finite matched panel of coding-transcript rows.  Each row carries a synonymous residual-coordinate label of type $B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout when such a readout is actually joined, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a candidate local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein.  The proposed biological relation is not that the coordinate itself causes decay.  The proposed relation is narrower: a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous label may be read as a cross-layer RNA-stability bridge only when the finite rows also show a measured local RBP-occupancy change at the editable RNA window, collapse of the half-life contrast after loss of the target RBP, restoration under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite contact.}
The available RBP contact is finite but incomplete for the bridge asserted here.  The RBP-binding run for the present record is \texttt{b225890251c2}.  It reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  Thus the finite binding surface can be summarized locally as
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
N_{\mathrm{concordant\ RBP}} &= 3,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{concordant}} &= 3/900 = 0.003333\ldots .
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a measured binding surface, not yet a measured decay-factor bridge.  The low concordance count is still useful because it fixes the evidential scale: the chapter may discuss a finite RBP-binding contact, but it may not convert that contact into an RNA half-life mechanism without same-window decay occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
A separate translation-readout example in the finite record reports $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation contact, from run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, with runtime $9.469$ seconds and a GEO raw-data source recorded for the readout.  This number is cited only to mark the difference between a joined translation surface and the RBP-binding surface above.  A translation table of size $6494$ and an RBP peak table of size $900$ do not by themselves form the required same-row panel.  The bridge tested here needs transcript or CDS rows in which $B^{*}_{Q6}$, translation readout, RNA half-life, transcript abundance, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue are simultaneously addressable.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls are attached.  In finite-row language, let $q_i\in\{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ be the synonymous residual-coordinate label, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, let $O_i$ be measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline terms.  The null says that, conditional on $T_i$ and $Z_i$, the coordinate label is not informative for $H_i$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive half-life contrast is therefore not enough.  If $H_i$ differs by $q_i$ but $O_i$ is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss does not remove the contrast, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it, the result remains a bounded RNA-stability association at most.  If binding-defective rescue or a non-target control restores the contrast, the claimed RBP bridge is rejected for this panel.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
The finite statistic has four gates.  First, the coordinate contrast must survive the amino-acid quotient and the same-row translation conditioning.  Second, the local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must change in the editable RNA window in the same direction required by the half-life effect.  Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life contrast.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must return the contrast while binding-defective or non-target rescue fails.  Written schematically, the accepted pattern has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q\mid T,Z) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_O(q\mid T,Z) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}}(q\mid T,Z) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}(q\mid T,Z) &\approx \Delta_H(q\mid T,Z),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}}(q\mid T,Z) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite RBP-binding contact with $900$ peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases can populate only part of this pattern.  It does not supply target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target rescue, or a translation-conditioned RNA half-life statistic.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the present record.}
The current finite evidence is therefore a scoped record, not a completed mechanism claim.  The strongest licensed statement is that the RBP-binding surface has been instantiated with concrete finite counts and can be used to seek a same-window panel for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA-stability question.  The current counts do not justify promoting RNA half-life association to a translation realization, and they do not justify treating an RBP peak as a decay mechanism.  They also do not justify protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Each of those claims would require its own layer-matched empirical contact and a declared finite statistic at that layer.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter permits a positive cross-layer RBP bridge only for a finite matched panel containing all required rows and controls.  Without measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue, the coordinate remains either a bounded descriptor or a finite RNA-stability association.  Without same-row translation readout and matched transcript-abundance controls, an RNA half-life effect is not a translation-conditioned result.  Without a separate protein, structure, physical, functional, phenotype, or mechanism assay, no statement about translation output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or a general biological law follows from this record.
