\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rbp-binding-rbns-affinity-eclip-binding-encode}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$ is kept after quotienting by amino-acid sequence.  Each admissible row must carry a high-coordinate or low-coordinate residual label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcription, abundance, local sequence composition, and isoform state.  The biological question is not whether a codon-coordinate descriptor can be seen in RNA stability by itself.  The narrower question is whether a translation-conditioned half-life residual can be tied to an experimentally separated RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern.

\paragraph{Observed finite contact.}
The available finite RBP-binding run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds in run \texttt{b225890251c2}, verified at \texttt{2026-06-23T19:06:26+00:00}.  These numbers establish that the packet has a finite RBP-binding surface rather than a purely notational coordinate.  They do not, by themselves, establish an RNA-decay mechanism, because the row set named here is an RBP binding or affinity contact, not a same-window half-life perturbation-and-rescue panel.  For comparison, the linked translation-readout run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} supplies $6494$ rows from a translation readout source and runtime $9.469$ seconds, while the linked RNA-modification run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6a\_stoichiometry.m6a\_stoichiometry\_exon\_architecture\_glori} supplies $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites, cross-condition concordance marked true, and coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$.  Those linked finite surfaces show that the surrounding BioReality ledger can store translation and RNA-layer measurements at scale, but neither linked fact supplies the exact same-row RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue matrix required for this chapter's positive claim.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
Let $q_i\in\{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ denote the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate class for row $i$, let $T_i$ denote the matched translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  A positive bridge statistic is admissible only if it is computed on joined rows that also include target-RBP loss $L_i$, wild-type rescue $W_i$, and binding-defective or non-target control rescue $C_i$.  The intended finite pattern is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_H(q\mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_O(q\mid Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q\mid T,Z,L) \approx 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q\mid T,Z,L,W) \text{ returns toward the wild-type effect},\\
&\Delta_H(q\mid T,Z,L,C) \text{ does not return toward the wild-type effect}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ contains transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the stated rank-sufficiency and simpler-baseline controls.  The same-row translation readout is a conditioning boundary: it prevents an RNA half-life association from being promoted into a translation realization, and it also prevents a translation contact name from being mistaken for an RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null says that, after conditioning on $T_i$ and the control vector $Z_i$, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  Equivalently, a finite association between $q_i$ and $H_i$ is still only an RNA-stability association unless measured local RBP occupancy changes with the coordinate, the half-life effect collapses under target-RBP loss, the wild-type rescue restores the effect, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  A motif-density explanation, RNA-structure-only explanation, transcript-abundance explanation, isoform-state explanation, GC or dinucleotide explanation, codon-pair explanation, or batch explanation remains live unless the joined panel defeats it under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Present verdict from the finite rows.}
The finite binding result has the right kind of RBP-facing surface to motivate the chapter: $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries are concrete, bounded data.  The result is therefore stronger than a coordinate-only packet.  However, the positive cross-layer RBP bridge is not yet closed, because the provided row payload does not include the necessary same-window half-life, occupancy, target-loss, wild-type-rescue, and defective-control columns.  The current chapter should therefore be read as a precise finite test specification with a partial RBP-binding contact, not as a completed decay-factor mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No claim about translation, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality follows from this record alone.  The translation readout is required only to condition the RNA half-life residual in the same row.  The RBP-binding peak rows are required only to expose a bounded occupancy-facing surface.  A separate layer-matched contact is required before any statement about ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, protein structure, physical feasibility, organismal function, phenotype, or general biological law can be made.  In particular, the $900$ RBP peak rows, the $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries, the $6494$ linked translation rows, and the $60000$ linked RNA-modification rows are finite measurements, but their existence does not license an inference to a universal sequence law or to an unmeasured biological layer.
