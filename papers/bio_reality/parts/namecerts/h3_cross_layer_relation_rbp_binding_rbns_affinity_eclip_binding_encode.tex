\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life with a Prespecified RBP Binding Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-rbp-binding-rbns-affinity-eclip-binding-encode}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel whose rows are coding-transcript windows carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$, an amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and the matched negative and rescue controls needed to separate binding from surrounding transcript covariates. The coordinate contrast is the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented out. The biological contrast is not merely whether a window has a motif or a peak; it is whether the RNA half-life residual tracks a measured local occupancy change for the declared decay-factor RBP, disappears when that target RBP is lost, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under a binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The available finite evidence for the RBP binding surface is the RBNS-affinity/eCLIP-binding/ENCODE panel with experiment run $\mathrm{b225890251c2}$. It contains $900$ total peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds. This is a binding-surface observation, not an RNA-decay mechanism by itself. The chapter therefore records a bounded contact: the finite rows show that a small cross-RBP concordant subset exists inside a much larger measured peak set, but they do not yet supply the same-window half-life, target-loss, and rescue matrix required for a positive RNA-decay RBP bridge.

The same standalone audit bundle also contains other finite biological panels that calibrate the distinction between a layer-specific readout and a higher-layer claim. The mTOR translation panel $\mathrm{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$ has $6494$ rows from the reported translation readout source and runtime $9.469$ seconds. The N-terminal acetylation panel $\mathrm{f452e76f6363}$ has $657$ rows, including $426$ NatA rows, and reports AUROC $0.942081$ on $622$ high/low rows with runtime $1.1687$ seconds. These numbers are cited only to fix the local finite-row discipline: a translation readout, an acetylation occupancy readout, and an RBP binding readout are separate measured surfaces. Agreement on one surface does not license claims on another surface without the row join and statistic for that surface.

\paragraph{Local null model.}
The null model holds high- and low-coordinate residual labels exchangeable with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the mandatory same-row translation readout. In symbols, for a finite row set $\Omega$ and controls $Z$, the admissible positive statistic must compare the coordinate term only after the conditioning variables and RBP perturbation indicators are attached:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6,RBP}
&= \widehat{E}\{H \mid Q=\mathrm{high}, O_R=1, Z\}
 - \widehat{E}\{H \mid Q=\mathrm{low}, O_R=0, Z\},\\
\Delta_{loss}
&\approx 0 \quad \text{after target-RBP loss},\\
\Delta_{rescue}
&\approx \Delta_{Q6,RBP} \quad \text{under wild-type rescue},\\
\Delta_{defective}
&\approx 0 \quad \text{under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $H$ denotes the RNA half-life residual, $Q$ the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate class, and $O_R$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP. The statistic is not satisfied by a half-life difference alone, by a motif-density difference alone, by an eCLIP peak alone, or by a translation readout alone. The occupancy row, the perturbation row, and the rescue rows are all part of the claimed finite object.

\paragraph{Readback permitted by the present rows.}
The current finite RBP panel permits only the following bounded statement. A measured binding surface exists for the declared RBP-binding contact, and its finite summary contains $900$ peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases. This can be used to motivate a joined RNA half-life experiment, to specify row keys, and to predeclare the RBP whose occupancy must be tested. It cannot, by itself, be read as translation realization, RNA decay causation, protein-output evidence, folded-structure evidence, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, or a universal sequence law.

\paragraph{Failure modes.}
The claim fails locally if any one of the following finite-row requirements is absent. First, if the same-row translation readout is missing, an RNA half-life association remains an RNA-stability association and cannot be promoted to a translation-conditioned relation. Second, if measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP is unchanged, the RBP bridge is not supported. Third, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the coordinate-associated half-life residual, the bridge is not supported. Fourth, if wild-type rescue does not restore the effect, or if a binding-defective or non-target rescue restores it just as well, the proposed binding mechanism is rejected. Fifth, if local RNA structure, motif density, composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, transcription rate, amino-acid composition, translation readout, or batch explains the signal, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor rather than a cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite evidence is sufficient for a ready standalone chapter because the row counts and scalar summaries define a concrete RBP-binding contact and a precise missing join. The scientific status is boundary-positive but mechanism-negative: $900$ measured peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases establish a finite binding surface, while the absence of same-window RNA half-life, occupancy-decoupling, target-loss, wild-type-rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows prevents a positive RNA half-life RBP-bridge claim. The admissible conclusion is therefore a bounded BioReality record for a prespecified experiment design, not a completed mechanistic assertion.
