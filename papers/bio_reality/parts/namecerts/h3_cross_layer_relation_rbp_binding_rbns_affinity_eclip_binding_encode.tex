\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP occupancy}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rbp-rna-half-life-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label is kept after quotienting by amino-acid identity, and is then compared against translation readout, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy at the editable RNA window, and matched controls. The coordinate contrast is local and finite: a row may carry a high-coordinate or low-coordinate synonymous residual label, but that label is not treated as a biological effect by itself. The row becomes eligible for a cross-layer RNA-stability readback only when the same row also carries the translation boundary, the RNA half-life measurement, the local RBP occupancy measurement, and the perturbation or rescue rows needed to separate binding from coincident transcript features.

\paragraph{Observed finite scalars.}
The RBP-binding finite run recorded experiment id \texttt{b225890251c2}. It inspected $900$ total peak rows and reported $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds and verification timestamp \texttt{2026-06-25T07:38:30+00:00}. These numbers support only a bounded occupancy-readout surface: they say that a finite RBP binding panel exists and that a small concordant subset was detected across RBPs. They do not by themselves provide RNA half-life, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue rows. The linked translation-readout run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} supplies a separate scale check for a translation surface with $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds from the named GSE36892 readout route. That translation finite row count is used here only to mark the required same-row translation boundary; it is not evidence for RNA decay or RBP mechanism unless it is joined to the same transcript windows and conditioned in the declared statistic.

\paragraph{Null model and statistic.}
The null model is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In symbols, if $q$ is the residual coordinate label, $T$ is the translation readout, $H$ is RNA half-life, $O$ is measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, and $Z$ is the matched control block, the intended finite comparison has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H(q;T,Z) &= H_{\mathrm{high}} - H_{\mathrm{low}} \mid T,Z, \\
  \Delta_O(q;Z) &= O_{\mathrm{high}} - O_{\mathrm{low}} \mid Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive bridge requires more than $\Delta_H \neq 0$. The half-life effect must track experimentally decoupled local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls. Without that full pattern, the row remains a finite RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor, not an RBP-mediated bridge.

\paragraph{Mechanism filter.}
The finite filter has three required signs. First, the coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual must remain visible after the same-row translation readout and abundance controls are attached. Second, local occupancy of the selected decay-factor RBP must change in the editable RNA window in the direction required by the half-life residual. Third, intervention must separate binding from background sequence composition: target-RBP loss must remove the effect, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must not restore it. Motif density, RNA-structure-only explanation, transcription-rate variation, isoform state, transcript abundance, GC or dinucleotide content, codon-pair context, amino-acid composition, and batch are treated as controls rather than as supporting evidence for the bridge.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The finite RBP panel has useful contact with measured binding, and the translation run gives a concrete scale for the translation-readout route, but the present row does not yet contain the same-window half-life-plus-occupancy-plus-perturbation matrix. Therefore the admissible conclusion is deliberately narrow: a candidate cross-layer RNA half-life/RBP bridge is specified, and the available finite scalars show the relevant readout surfaces are nonempty. No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RBP decay mechanism follows from occupancy alone. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal law follows from the coordinate label, the RBP peak count, or the translation row count unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies that fact and the finite statistic is recomputed on joined rows.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
For this chapter, the finite numbers are sufficient to write the bounded record but not sufficient to certify the positive mechanism. The RBP-binding run contributes $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries; the translation run contributes $6494$ translation-readout rows as an independent boundary surface. The decisive missing object is a same-organism, same-window panel carrying B$^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. Until that panel exists, the scientifically correct reading is a constrained bridge specification with a blocked positive mechanism call.
