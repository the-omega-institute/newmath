\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP occupancy gate}
\label{sec:biro-rbp-binding-rbns-affinity-eclip-binding-encode}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label is attached to an RNA half-life readout only through a same-row translation boundary and a separately measured RNA-binding-protein occupancy boundary.  The coordinate label is the high-versus-low $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual class after quotienting by amino-acid sequence.  The proposed biological object is not a bare sequence statistic: it is a finite panel whose rows must simultaneously carry the coordinate label, a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a measured local occupancy call for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, and matched controls for transcription, abundance, RNA structure, motif density, composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch.

The finite RBP-binding run available for this chapter is the ENCODE/RBNS-style occupancy contact
$h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode$.  Its recorded run id is $b225890251c2$, with $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, computed in $223.648043$ seconds.  This is enough to state a bounded occupancy-surface contact, but it is not enough by itself to assert an RNA-decay mechanism.  The translation-side comparison contact $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012$ contributes a separate finite translation readout scale with $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  A third independent cross-layer finite table, $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6a\_stoichiometry.m6a\_stoichiometry\_exon\_architecture\_glori$, records $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance equal to true, coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$, and runtime $78.316$ seconds; it is used here only as an audit comparator showing that cross-layer records in this paper are finite-row readbacks with explicit scalar diagnostics, not as evidence for the RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Row predicate.}
For a row $r$, write $Q(r)$ for the synonymous residual coordinate class, $T(r)$ for the same-row translation readout, $H(r)$ for the RNA half-life residual after the declared controls, and $O_{B}(r)$ for measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP $B$ at the editable RNA window.  The positive bridge requires more than association of $Q$ with $H$.  It requires occupancy-tracking and intervention tracking on the same finite panel:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&Q(r) \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\},\quad T(r) \text{ observed or explicitly bounded},\\
&H(r) \text{ residualized against transcription, abundance, structure, motif, composition, context, isoform, and batch controls},\\
&O_{B}(r) \text{ measured locally for a prespecified decay-factor RBP},\\
&\Delta H(Q\mid T,Z) \text{ follows } \Delta O_{B},\\
&\Delta H(Q\mid T,Z) \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss},\\
&\Delta H(Q\mid T,Z) \text{ returns under wild-type rescue},\\
&\Delta H(Q\mid T,Z) \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $Z$ denotes the complete control bundle: amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, translation readout, and batch.  The current finite data give an RBP occupancy surface with $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, plus a separate translation readout table with $6494$ rows.  They do not yet provide the same-row joined panel containing RNA half-life, translation, local RBP occupancy, RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue arms.  The chapter therefore states the exact acceptance rule for a future bridge while keeping the present evidence at bounded contact scope.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after the full control bundle and after conditioning on the same-row translation boundary.  In finite-row form, the null says that the coordinate label may be permuted inside matched strata without changing the distribution of $H$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(r) \perp Q(r) \mid Z(r),T(r),
\end{aligned}
$$

unless the measured RBP-occupancy and perturbation pattern breaks that exchangeability.  A half-life difference without local occupancy change is not an RBP bridge.  A half-life difference with occupancy change but no target-RBP loss collapse is not an RBP bridge.  A loss effect without wild-type rescue is not an RBP bridge.  A rescue by a binding-defective construct or a non-target control defeats the bridge interpretation.  RNA-structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair, and batch explanations remain live unless the same finite statistic excludes them.

\paragraph{Finite statistic.}
The statistic to be reported for a positive result is not a single correlation.  It is a finite matched-panel readback with four coupled signs: the translation-conditioned half-life residual, the local RBP occupancy contrast, the collapse under target-RBP loss, and the rescue specificity contrast.  One acceptable schematic is

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{bridge}}
&= \bigl(\Delta H_{Q\mid T,Z},\Delta O_{B,Q\mid Z},\Delta H^{\mathrm{loss}}_{Q\mid T,Z},\Delta H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}_{Q\mid T,Z},\Delta H^{\mathrm{defective\ rescue}}_{Q\mid T,Z}\bigr),\\
S_{\mathrm{bridge}} \text{ supports the bridge}
&\Longrightarrow
\Delta H_{Q\mid T,Z} \text{ tracks } \Delta O_{B,Q\mid Z},\\
&\qquad \Delta H^{\mathrm{loss}}_{Q\mid T,Z} \approx 0,\\
&\qquad \Delta H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}_{Q\mid T,Z} \text{ restores the original sign},\\
&\qquad \Delta H^{\mathrm{defective\ rescue}}_{Q\mid T,Z} \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The available occupancy run supplies only the occupancy-contact side of this object: $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  The translation run supplies a separate readout scale of $6494$ rows.  The m6A stoichiometry comparator, with $60000$ rows and $49668$ distinct entries, demonstrates the expected accounting discipline for finite cross-layer tables but does not enter the RBP-decay bridge statistic.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a bounded cross-layer RBP-occupancy gate for a translation-conditioned RNA half-life question.  It does not infer translation output from RNA half-life alone.  It does not infer RNA decay mechanism from a coordinate-associated half-life difference unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls all appear in the same finite design.  It does not infer protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence law.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite rows and declared statistics.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The record is ready as a self-contained BioReality chapter because it has a concrete finite RBP-binding contact, a concrete finite translation-readout comparator, and an explicit row-level rule for when an RNA half-life association may be promoted to an RBP bridge.  The present numerical evidence supports the formulation of the boundary and the finite acceptance rule; it does not yet satisfy the rule for a positive mechanistic RNA-decay claim.
