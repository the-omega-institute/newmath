\subsection{Synonymous-family resolution of the $f_3$ stress coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-family-decomposition}

\paragraph{Question.}
The $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel contains a single aggregate coordinate called $f_3\text{-stress}$. In the survival table this coordinate is useful only if it can be read as more than an anonymous one-dimensional residual: the finite row must say which synonymous codon families supply the energy seen by the aggregate direction. The present record therefore decomposes $f_3\text{-stress}$ into family-local synonymous subcoordinates and tests whether the resulting pieces remain visible against the same controlled protein-abundance target used for the yeast abundance contact.

\paragraph{Finite design.}
The organism is $\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}$, with organism key \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}. The target is $\log_{10}$ protein abundance after residualization against the sibling control basis. The joined analysis uses $n=1800$ rows, drawn from a base pool of $4759$ records subject to a minimum organism gate of $500$ proteins. The control basis has rank $24$. The decomposition contains $21$ additive synonymous-family subcoordinates, and all $21$ were computed. Each family subcoordinate is formed by retaining only the corresponding synonymous-family block of the projected $f_3$ weights and normalizing by the full projected $f_3$ norm before applying the same residualization step. The aggregate $f_3\text{-stress}$ direction has rank $1$, squared norm $52.76911713166682$, and accounts for the fraction $0.12259712041843235$ of the target energy, with target squared norm $430.42705205115925$.

\paragraph{Energy statistic.}
For a synonymous family $a$, let $P_a$ denote the target projection onto the residualized family subcoordinate, and let $P_{f_3}$ denote the target projection onto the full residualized $f_3\text{-stress}$ coordinate. The reported family energy is
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_a &= \frac{\lVert P_a\rVert^2}{\lVert P_{f_3}\rVert^2},\\
\lVert P_{f_3}\rVert^2 &= 52.76911713166682,\\
\lVert y\rVert^2 &= 430.42705205115925.
\end{aligned}
$$
The computation used five held-out folds and $120$ deterministic target-label permutations. The pass condition is not that every family be active, but that the family-resolved table be complete and that at least one synonymous family exceed the held-out and permutation gate as a real abundance source under the stated residualization.

The two-family gap is also part of the finite result: a complete $21$-family decomposition was computed, but the recorded real-source list contains $19$ names. Thus the conclusion is family-resolved rather than uniformly family-positive. The aggregate verdict is that the $f_3\text{-stress}$ direction is not merely an algebraic compression; in this yeast abundance contact, most synonymous-family blocks retain detectable target-facing energy after the same controls are imposed.

\paragraph{Position-readout consistency.}
A separate position-controlled ribosome-occupancy contact gives a bounded translation-facing comparison for the same family of coordinates. \paragraph{Boundary.}
The finite row supports a controlled yeast protein-abundance association for a family-resolved decomposition of $f_3\text{-stress}$. It does not by itself assert translation realization, ribosome causation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or universality. A stronger layer claim would require a separate matched contact for that layer and a separately reported statistic under the relevant controls. In particular, the row should not be read as saying that synonymous identity causes abundance, that every organism has the same family ordering, or that a protein-level abundance association is already a structure or function result.
