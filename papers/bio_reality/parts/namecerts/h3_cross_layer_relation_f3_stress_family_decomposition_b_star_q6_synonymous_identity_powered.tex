\subsection{Synonymous family decomposition of the $f_3$ stress coordinate}

\paragraph{Finite row under test.}
This record isolates the $f_3$ stress member of the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual panel and asks whether its abundance-facing signal is concentrated in a small number of synonymous-family subcoordinates or spread across the codon table.  The computation is a finite yeast-row analysis on \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with $n=1800$ joined proteins after the minimum-protein gate and with a reported base pool of $4759$ candidate rows.  The target is residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance on the unified yeast context join.  The control basis has rank $24$ and includes the same sibling covariates used by the surrounding abundance-residual computations: coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The run identifier is \texttt{1e753e65044b}, and the deterministic seed is \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_f3\_stress\_family\_decomposition\_powered:deterministic}.

\paragraph{Decomposition statistic.}
The tested source design contains $21$ additive synonymous-family subcoordinates.  Each subcoordinate retains only one projected $Q9$ family block of the full $f_3$ stress vector, divides by the norm of the full projected $f_3$ coordinate, and then passes through the same residualization against the control basis.  The full residualized $f_3$ projection has rank $1$, squared norm $52.76911713166682$, and accounts for $0.12259712041843235$ of the residualized abundance target energy.  The total target squared norm is $430.42705205115925$.  In the notation of this chapter, the family energy score is
$$
\begin{aligned}

The audit produced all $21$ family entries, so the decomposition is not a partial listing or a rank-only diagnostic.  The reported permutation count is $120$, and the cross-validation envelope uses $5$ folds.

The family decomposition sits inside the broader nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance analysis. \paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is ready for inclusion as an abundance-residual decomposition claim.  The recorded computation completed all $21$ synonymous-family subcoordinates of the $f_3$ stress coordinate, used $n=1800$ joined yeast proteins, retained a rank-$24$ control basis, used $5$ held-out folds and $120$ deterministic permutations, and reported a full $f_3$ projection with $\|P_{f_3}\|^2=52.76911713166682$ explaining $0.12259712041843235$ of the residualized target energy.  The chapter therefore supports the statement that the yeast $f_3$ stress abundance component is decomposable into synonymous-family sources, with a substantial real-source list across amino-acid families.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This result is deliberately narrower than a biological mechanism claim.  It does not establish translation realization by itself, because the row is an abundance-residual family decomposition rather than a same-row measured TE or ribosome-profiling survival matrix.  It does not establish folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway membership, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  It also does not treat tRNA gene copy, modeled adaptation weights, mRNA half-life, or steady-state protein abundance as interchangeable readouts.  Any claim at one of those layers would require its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  The supported object here is only the finite, controlled decomposition of the $f_3$ stress abundance-facing residual into synonymous-family components in the reported yeast row.
