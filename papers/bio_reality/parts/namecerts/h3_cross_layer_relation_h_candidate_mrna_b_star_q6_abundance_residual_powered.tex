\subsection{B-star Q6 mRNA H-candidate projection after modeled translation complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-h-candidate-mrna-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding sequences in four organisms, with nine amino-acid-quotient-surviving $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, a modeled translation component, matched mRNA abundance, and protein-abundance readouts.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The common control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real coding-sequence codon counts, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism the control matrix has $24$ columns and numerical rank $23$, so the descriptive projection is not a three-row saturated fit.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank guard.}
The finite rows used for the mRNA H-candidate projection are organism-specific.  Danio rerio has $n=13140$ rows after requiring valid mRNA and abundance joins, with $14410$ coding sequences reported in the abundance join.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 has $n=3415$, with $3739$ coding sequences in the reported abundance join and $3747$ valid abundance records by protein.  Homo sapiens has $n=11554$, with $19053$ reported coding-sequence abundance joins and $19483$ valid abundance records by protein.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $n=5128$, with $5815$ reported coding-sequence abundance joins and $6351$ valid abundance records by protein.  Thus the calculation is a finite residual projection on thousands of rows, not a symbolic claim about all genes, all organisms, or all translation systems.

\paragraph{Projection definition.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the protein-abundance residual component associated with the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate block after the declared controls, and let $T_{mod}$ denote the modeled tAI or stAI translation component.  The tested H-candidate is residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA abundance, applied only after forming the modeled-translation complement $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.  The reported scalar $d_{modeled}$ is the fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{modeled} &= \frac{\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the mRNA absorption score records how much of this complement is removed by the residualized mRNA covariate.  The calculation therefore tests transcript-level absorption of a residual abundance component; it does not by itself establish a causal transcript mechanism.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement numbers.}
The observed finite-row norms are sharply organism-dependent:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio}:&\quad \lVert P_Q\rVert^2=1092.0114815505467,\quad \lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2=919.9445878158525,\quad d_{modeled}=0.8424312412078521,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad \lVert P_Q\rVert^2=393.78692095732885,\quad \lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2=261.0102870984463,\quad d_{modeled}=0.6628211177352172,\\
\mathrm{Human}:&\quad \lVert P_Q\rVert^2=565.3850201235074,\quad \lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2=484.31956581711796,\quad d_{modeled}=0.8566190269974241,\\
\mathrm{Yeast}:&\quad \lVert P_Q\rVert^2=675.3275479192903,\quad \lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2=151.18264752811882,\quad d_{modeled}=0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest modeled complement fractions occur in human and Danio, while yeast retains a much smaller complement after the modeled translation component is removed.

\paragraph{mRNA absorption.}
Residualized mRNA explains only a small additional part of the modeled-translation complement in this finite panel.  The absorption values are $0.011973033879985792$ for Danio, $0.0017706954554908886$ for E. coli, $0.007858222847072218$ for human, and $0.0018976054721605224$ for yeast.  The corresponding combined complement fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio}:&\quad d_{combined}=0.8322280386743373,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad d_{combined}=0.6615987644523418,\\
\mathrm{Human}:&\quad d_{combined}=0.8498079619223776,\\
\mathrm{Yeast}:&\quad d_{combined}=0.223360891719316.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers make the chapter a bounded descriptive result: matched mRNA is a candidate covariate for the leftover abundance residual, but the recorded absorption is small in all four organisms.

\paragraph{Join-sensitivity audit.}
A negative self-check compares the current all-readout join against the complement experiment's full abundance coding-sequence join.  Danio changes from $0.874628721049266$ on $14410$ reference rows to $0.8424312412078521$ on $13140$ current rows, with absolute difference $0.03219747984141397$, outside the $0.02$ tolerance.  Human changes from $0.9208949355679455$ on $19053$ reference rows to $0.8566190269974241$ on $11554$ current rows, with absolute difference $0.06427590857052146$, also outside tolerance.  E. coli is stable within tolerance, moving from $0.6629960038578532$ to $0.6628211177352172$, absolute difference $0.00017488612263594217$.  Yeast is also stable within tolerance, moving from $0.21173219572706703$ to $0.22386566043976477$, absolute difference $0.012133464712697745$.  The failed Danio and human comparisons are not biological contradictions; they show that availability of matched mRNA changes the protein-identifier join used by the projection.

\paragraph{Relation to the half-life mediation panel.}
A separate cross-domain mRNA-stability computation supplies a useful comparison but not a replacement for this H-candidate projection.  In that two-organism half-life panel, E. coli joins $2720$ stable identifiers and human joins $16165$ stable identifiers.  The joint nine-coordinate mediation fractions are $0.24697202727892137$ in E. coli and $0.1411299863319291$ in human, each with deterministic residual-mediator permutation right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$.  The corresponding $R^2_{QP}$ values are $0.11632311854858134$ and $0.04095215557813418$, while $R^2_{QP\mid M}$ falls to $0.0875945621412319$ and $0.03517257842112907$.  Those half-life numbers support a statistical variance partition for RNA-stability readouts; they do not convert the present mRNA-abundance projection into a causal RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row conclusion is that residualized mRNA abundance is a computed H-candidate covariate for the modeled-tAI complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual.  The calculation is real and rank-powered on the stated joined rows, but its mRNA absorption is small and join-sensitive.  It is therefore admissible as a bounded cross-layer readback of residual abundance geometry, not as a direct translation-survival matrix.  No translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, phenotype claim, causal pathway, or universal biological rule follows from these rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the appropriate controlled statistic.
