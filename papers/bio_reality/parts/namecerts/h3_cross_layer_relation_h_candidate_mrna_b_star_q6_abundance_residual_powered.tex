\subsection{Rank-powered mRNA absorption boundary for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{subsec:biorreality-q6-mrna-abundance-residual-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding sequences indexed by organism, a nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate system, a modeled translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, and a downstream protein-abundance residual.  The coordinate side is not an amino-acid sequence effect: the nine residual coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each organism the control matrix has $24$ columns and numerical rank $23$, so the residual readback is not an unconstrained fit to the raw abundance vector.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank check.}
Four organism panels were computed for the mRNA-abundance H-candidate test.  The usable row counts after the mRNA join are $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding protein-abundance CDS joins before the mRNA restriction report $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$ CDS rows, while the valid protein-abundance-by-protein counts are $14410$, $3747$, $19483$, and $6351$.  Thus the H-candidate projection is evaluated on a stricter all-readout join, not on the larger abundance-only surface.  This distinction matters: the modeled-complement self-check agrees within $0.02$ only for \emph{E. coli} and yeast, and fails that tolerance for zebrafish and human because mRNA availability changes the joined protein-id surface.

\paragraph{Modeled complement before mRNA absorption.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance residual component associated with the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate block after the shared controls, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the residual left after projecting away the modeled translation readout.  The finite computations give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=919.9445878158525,\quad d_{mod}=0.8424312412078521,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=393.78692095732885,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=261.0102870984463,\quad d_{mod}=0.6628211177352172,\\
\mathrm{Human}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=565.3850201235074,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=484.31956581711796,\quad d_{mod}=0.8566190269974241,\\
\mathrm{Yeast}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=675.3275479192903,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=151.18264752811882,\quad d_{mod}=0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers place yeast in a different descriptive regime from the vertebrate panels: the modeled translation component leaves only about $22.4\%$ of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual energy, while zebrafish and human retain more than $84\%$.

\paragraph{mRNA H-candidate projection.}
The mRNA test projects the modeled-translation complement $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA abundance on the same rows.  The absorbed fractions are small in all four organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad a_{mRNA}=0.011973033879985792,\quad d_{combined}=0.8322280386743373,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad a_{mRNA}=0.0017706954554908886,\quad d_{combined}=0.6615987644523418,\\
\mathrm{Human}:&\quad a_{mRNA}=0.007858222847072218,\quad d_{combined}=0.8498079619223776,\\
\mathrm{Yeast}:&\quad a_{mRNA}=0.0018976054721605224,\quad d_{combined}=0.223360891719316.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore a bounded descriptive statement: residualized mRNA abundance absorbs roughly $1.20\%$ of the modeled-complement residual in zebrafish, $0.18\%$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.79\%$ in human, and $0.19\%$ in yeast.  It does not certify a transcript-mediated mechanism.  In particular, a small absorbed fraction does not prove that an H-factor has been found; it only says that this mRNA covariate does not explain most of the remaining modeled-complement abundance residual on the joined panel.

\paragraph{Join sensitivity self-check.}
The abundance-only reference and the all-readout join differ by organism.  Zebrafish changes from $d_{mod}=0.874628721049266$ on $14410$ reference rows to $0.8424312412078521$ on $13140$ current rows, an absolute difference of $0.03219747984141397$.  Human changes from $0.9208949355679455$ on $19053$ reference rows to $0.8566190269974241$ on $11554$ current rows, an absolute difference of $0.06427590857052146$.  By contrast, \emph{E. coli} changes only by $0.00017488612263594217$, and yeast by $0.012133464712697745$.  The failed tolerance in zebrafish and human is not a biological contradiction; it is a row-set warning.  The H-candidate result must be read on the all-readout panel on which mRNA, codon-derived coordinates, controls, and abundance residuals coexist.

\paragraph{Cross-domain mRNA-stability comparison.}
A separate finite computation tests an RNA half-life or stability mediator in \emph{E. coli} and human.  It uses the same nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ block and the same $24$-column, rank-$23$ control surface, but the mediator is residualized mRNA half-life rather than same-condition mRNA abundance.  The \emph{E. coli} three-way join contains $2720$ stable ids from $3739$ joined CDS records and $2947$ valid half-life records, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$.  The human three-way join contains $16165$ stable ids from $19053$ joined CDS records and $19480$ valid consensus half-life records, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$; $16150$ human joins use Ensembl gene id and $15$ use the unique gene-symbol fallback.  The mediation fractions are $0.24697202727892137$ for \emph{E. coli} and $0.1411299863319291$ for human, with right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in both cases.  These stability rows show that an RNA-layer mediator can carry a measurable variance partition in a different panel, but they still do not convert the abundance-mRNA H-candidate projection into a causal pathway.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The finite statement is that residualized mRNA abundance was evaluated as a candidate absorber of the modeled-translation complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual, and the computed absorption was low in all four organism panels.  The statement is rank-aware, row-aware, and control-aware: it depends on the all-readout join, the rank-$23$ control matrix, the nine residual coordinates, and the projection sequence $Q \to T_{mod}$ followed by mRNA absorption.  It is not an assertion that $B^*_{Q6}$ controls mRNA abundance, nor that mRNA abundance causes protein abundance.  It is also not a translation-survival matrix $S^{QT}$, because no direct measured ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency survival row is computed here.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
No translation realization, ribosome mechanism, protein folding conclusion, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, phenotype claim, or universal biological rule follows from this record alone.  A direct translation claim would require an organism-matched translation readout and a controlled $S^{QT}$ computation.  A protein-readout mediation claim would require the ordered matrix comparison in which a surviving $Q_i\to T_j$ step is established before interpreting $Q_i\to P_k$ or $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$.  A structure, stability, localization, function, phenotype, or mechanism claim would require its own layer-matched measurement and controls.  The present record only supplies a finite, computed boundary for how much same-row residualized mRNA abundance absorbs from the modeled-translation complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual.
