\subsection{Rank-powered mRNA absorption of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-h-candidate-mrna-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Finite panel and coordinate scope.}
This record evaluates whether matched mRNA abundance absorbs any part of the protein-abundance residual left after a modeled translation readout has already been used. The finite carrier is a joined coding-sequence panel indexed by organism, protein identifier, nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, amino-acid-composition controls, GC3, CDS length, an $M$-density baseline, modeled translation weights, protein abundance, and matched mRNA abundance. The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and an $M$-density coordinate defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. Thus the calculation is not a raw codon-count association: every reported scalar is read after this fixed control geometry.

\paragraph{Joined-row geometry.}
The panel is large enough to avoid the rank-saturated three-row failure mode. For \emph{Danio rerio}, the all-readout join has $n=13140$ rows, with $14410$ CDS rows joined in the protein-abundance reference, $14410$ valid abundance rows by protein, $13140$ valid mRNA rows by protein, $24$ control columns, and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$. For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the corresponding values are $n=3415$, $3739$ CDS rows in the abundance reference, $3747$ valid abundance rows by protein, $3415$ valid mRNA rows, $24$ control columns, and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$. For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the joined mRNA-constrained panel has $n=11554$, compared with $19053$ CDS rows in the abundance reference and $19483$ valid abundance rows by protein; the control matrix again has $24$ columns and rank $23$. For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the panel has $n=5128$, with $5815$ CDS rows in the abundance reference, $6351$ valid abundance rows by protein, $5128$ valid mRNA rows, $24$ controls, and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Modeled translation complement.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the protein-abundance residual component carried by the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block after the fixed controls, and let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modeled translation-weight readout already projected into the same residual space. The finite calculation first removes the modeled translation component and reports the remaining relative complement
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed values are organism-specific. In \emph{Danio rerio}, $\|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467$, $\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=919.9445878158525$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412078521$. In \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $393.78692095732885$, $261.0102870984463$, and $0.6628211177352172$. In human, they are $565.3850201235074$, $484.31956581711796$, and $0.8566190269974241$. In budding yeast, they are $675.3275479192903$, $151.18264752811882$, and $0.22386566043976477$. These numbers mean that the modeled translation readout leaves a substantial residual component in all four organisms, with the smallest remaining fraction in yeast and the largest in human.

\paragraph{mRNA absorption statistic.}
Matched mRNA abundance is then tested only as an $H$-candidate covariate: residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA is projected against $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, and the result is reported as an absorption decrement rather than as a causal explanation. The finite readout is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{mRNA} &= d_{\mathrm{modeled}}-d_{\mathrm{combined}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $d_{\mathrm{combined}}$ is the remaining fraction after adding residualized mRNA to the modeled translation component. The values are small. \emph{Danio rerio} has $\alpha_{mRNA}=0.011973033879985792$ and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8322280386743373$. \emph{E. coli} has $\alpha_{mRNA}=0.0017706954554908886$ and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6615987644523418$. Human has $\alpha_{mRNA}=0.007858222847072218$ and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8498079619223776$. Yeast has $\alpha_{mRNA}=0.0018976054721605224$ and $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.223360891719316$. The row therefore supports a bounded descriptive statement: matched mRNA abundance accounts for a small fraction of the already translation-complemented protein-abundance residual in this panel.

\paragraph{Join-sensitivity self-check.}
The calculation also compares the all-readout mRNA-constrained join with the prior full protein-abundance CDS join. The absolute difference in $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ is $0.03219747984141397$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, where the comparison is $0.8424312412078521$ against $0.874628721049266$ on reference join size $14410$; this fails the $0.02$ agreement check. \emph{E. coli} differs by only $0.00017488612263594217$, comparing $0.6628211177352172$ with $0.6629960038578532$ on reference join size $3739$, and passes. Human differs by $0.06427590857052146$, comparing $0.8566190269974241$ with $0.9208949355679455$ on reference join size $19053$, and fails. Yeast differs by $0.012133464712697745$, comparing $0.22386566043976477$ with $0.21173219572706703$ on reference join size $5815$, and passes. The failed cases are not biological effects; they show that mRNA availability changes the protein-identifier join and therefore changes the finite panel on which the residual is computed.

\paragraph{Relation to RNA-stability mediation rows.}
A separate cross-domain half-life computation gives useful scale but does not replace the present mRNA-abundance absorption statistic. In the \emph{E. coli} half-life panel, the stable joined count is $2720$, the CDS join hit rate is $0.9978649586335735$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde M)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X\widetilde M)=10$. Its mediation fraction is $0.24697202727892137$, with $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.0875945621412319$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. In the human half-life panel, the stable joined count is $16165$, the CDS join hit rate is $0.9779294769799313$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde M)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X\widetilde M)=10$. Its mediation fraction is $0.1411299863319291$, with $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.03517257842112907$, and the same right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. These half-life rows show that RNA-side covariates can be finite and rank-powered, but they are statistical decompositions across dataset joins, not proof of an mRNA mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter-level conclusion is narrow. The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual-to-protein-abundance relation remains a protein-readout association after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, and $M$-density controls. Adding matched mRNA abundance changes the residual complement by about $0.0018$ to $0.0120$ in the four organism panels, so mRNA abundance is a measured descriptive covariate but not the missing mechanism for the modeled-translation complement. The correct ordering is therefore: estimate any $Q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout survival first; only then examine direct $Q_i\to P_k$ protein readout and the translation-conditioned component $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$. The present row does not establish translation survival, ribosome mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, causation, or universality. Each such claim would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled finite statistic.
