\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ mRNA H-Candidate Abundance Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mrna-hcandidate}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record studies whether an mRNA abundance covariate can account for part of the protein-abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation readout has already been removed from the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate signal. The finite carrier is a panel of coding sequences joined by organism, residual codon-coordinate values, modeled translation weights, mRNA abundance, and protein abundance. The coordinate block has nine rows: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The shared control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline computed as the fraction of sense codons lying in the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. In all four organisms the control matrix has $24$ columns and residual rank $23$, so the reported quantities are not all-ones rank artifacts from a saturated three-row design.

\paragraph{Joined rows and control ranks.}
The joined panels are organism-specific. For \texttt{danio\_rerio}, the all-readout mRNA join has $n=13140$ coding sequences, compared with $14410$ reported CDS-protein abundance joins, and both the control-column count and rank are $24$ and $23$. For \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, the joined mRNA panel has $n=3415$, with $3739$ reported CDS joins and $3747$ valid abundance rows by protein. For \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, the mRNA join has $n=11554$, compared with $19053$ reported CDS joins and $19483$ valid protein-abundance rows. For \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, the mRNA join has $n=5128$, compared with $5815$ reported CDS joins and $6351$ valid protein-abundance rows. These row counts matter because the statistic is a finite-row projection on the rows where the protein, modeled-translation, mRNA, and coordinate blocks can all be evaluated together.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the protein-abundance projection associated with the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate block after the declared controls, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the component remaining after the modeled translation-weight readout has been projected away. The descriptive complement ratio is reported as
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{mod} &= \frac{\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed finite-row values are sharply organism-dependent. In zebrafish, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=1092.0114815505467$, $\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2=919.9445878158525$, and $d_{mod}=0.8424312412078521$. In \textit{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $393.78692095732885$, $261.0102870984463$, and $0.6628211177352172$. In human, they are $565.3850201235074$, $484.31956581711796$, and $0.8566190269974241$. In budding yeast, they are $675.3275479192903$, $151.18264752811882$, and $0.22386566043976477$. Thus the residual left after modeled translation removal is large in zebrafish and human, moderate in \textit{E. coli}, and much smaller in yeast.

\paragraph{mRNA absorption readback.}
The H-candidate test then projects $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA abundance. The absorbed fractions are small in all four organisms: $0.011973033879985792$ in zebrafish, $0.0017706954554908886$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.007858222847072218$ in human, and $0.0018976054721605224$ in yeast. The combined residual ratios after adding the mRNA projection are $0.8322280386743373$, $0.6615987644523418$, $0.8498079619223776$, and $0.223360891719316$, respectively. The calculation therefore supports a bounded descriptive statement: mRNA abundance explains only a small part of the modeled-translation complement in this finite panel. It does not identify an H-factor, and it does not turn low absorption into positive evidence for any specific downstream turnover, localization, or stability explanation.

\paragraph{Join self-check.}
A complement self-check compares the all-readout join against a protein-abundance reference join. The comparison passes the $0.02$ tolerance in \textit{E. coli} and yeast, with absolute differences $0.00017488612263594217$ and $0.012133464712697745$. It fails that tolerance in zebrafish and human, with absolute differences $0.03219747984141397$ and $0.06427590857052146$. The failed cases have a direct finite-row interpretation: mRNA availability changes the protein identifier join used by the projection. They are not contradictions of the projection statistic, but they mark the row set as a substantive part of the claim.

\paragraph{Relation to RNA half-life mediation.}
A separate cross-domain half-life decomposition gives a useful boundary check. In the \textit{E. coli} half-life panel, the stable joined row count is $2720$, the reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9978649586335735$, the control rank is again $23$, and the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $2697$. The joint nine-coordinate mediation fraction is $0.24697202727892137$, with $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.0875945621412319$, and a right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. In the human half-life panel, the stable joined row count is $16165$, the reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9779294769799313$, the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $16142$, the mediation fraction is $0.1411299863319291$, $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.03517257842112907$, and the same finite permutation resolution gives $0.000999000999000999$. These figures show that RNA-stability readouts can partition variance in a separate finite design, but they do not retroactively make the mRNA-abundance H-candidate causal.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The admissible conclusion is only a controlled projection statement about joined finite rows. The mRNA covariate is condition-specific and observational, and the protein-abundance residual is a steady-state readout. No translation survival claim follows from this chapter unless a direct translation readout first supplies a positive, controlled $S^{QT}$ entry. No protein-output mediation follows unless the same-coordinate chain $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ is evaluated and conditioning on $T_j$ reduces the direct $Q_i\to P_k$ component. No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from these rows without a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
