\subsection{Rank-controlled mRNA absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-h-candidate-mrna-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer question for $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The finite rows are coding sequences joined by organism, a nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis, amino-acid-composition controls, GC3, coding-sequence length, an $M$-density baseline, modeled translation weights, protein abundance, and matched mRNA abundance.  The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; in every organism reported here the control column count is $24$ and the observed control rank is $23$.

\paragraph{Row counts and rank.}
The joined mRNA-controlled panels are large enough to avoid the rank-saturation pathology of the small three-organism toy surface.  The observed row counts are $n=13140$ for zebrafish, $n=3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for human, and $n=5128$ for budding yeast.  The corresponding CDS joins before the mRNA restriction are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, while the valid protein-abundance rows by protein identifier are $14410$, $3747$, $19483$, and $6351$.  Thus the present statistic is not a universal biological law; it is a four-organism finite-row projection with an explicit mRNA-availability gate.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement residual.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the protein-abundance residual component associated with the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate block after the stated controls, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the part not captured by the modeled translation-weight component.  The reported finite-row diagnostic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For zebrafish, $\|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467$, $\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=919.9445878158525$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412078521$.  For \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $393.78692095732885$, $261.0102870984463$, and $0.6628211177352172$.  For human they are $565.3850201235074$, $484.31956581711796$, and $0.8566190269974241$.  For budding yeast they are $675.3275479192903$, $151.18264752811882$, and $0.22386566043976477$.

\paragraph{mRNA absorption statistic.}
The H-candidate tested here is matched transcript abundance, not a direct translation-readout survival entry.  The statistic projects the modeled-translation complement $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA abundance after the same control family.  The resulting absorption values are small in all four organisms: $0.011973033879985792$ for zebrafish, $0.0017706954554908886$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.007858222847072218$ for human, and $0.0018976054721605224$ for budding yeast.  The combined residual fractions after adding the mRNA projection are $0.8322280386743373$, $0.6615987644523418$, $0.8498079619223776$, and $0.223360891719316$, respectively.  In this finite panel, matched mRNA abundance absorbs only a thin slice of the modeled-translation complement, so the correct reading is descriptive: the residual is not explained away by transcript abundance alone.

\paragraph{Join self-check.}
The same computation records whether the all-readout mRNA-restricted join reproduces the earlier full-abundance complement surface within a tolerance of $0.02$.  The check passes for \emph{E. coli}, where the absolute difference is $0.00017488612263594217$ between $0.6629960038578532$ and $0.6628211177352172$, and for budding yeast, where the absolute difference is $0.012133464712697745$ between $0.21173219572706703$ and $0.22386566043976477$.  It fails for zebrafish and human: the differences are $0.03219747984141397$ and $0.06427590857052146$, respectively.  Those two failures are not biological counterexamples; they say that requiring mRNA availability changes the protein-identifier join enough to move the descriptive projection.

\paragraph{Relation to RNA-stability mediation.}
A neighboring finite-row record tests whether residualized RNA half-life or stability accounts for a related $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance association in \emph{E. coli} and human.  Its row counts are $2720$ stable joined identifiers for \emph{E. coli} and $16165$ for human, with reported CDS join hit rates $0.9978649586335735$ and $0.9779294769799313$.  The mediation fractions are $0.24697202727892137$ for \emph{E. coli} and $0.1411299863319291$ for human, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These numbers show that an RNA-layer covariate can be computed as a variance-partitioning diagnostic, but they do not turn the mRNA absorption statistic above into a causal transcript mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row claim supported here is narrow.  A $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual remains after modeled translation weights and the declared controls, and matched mRNA abundance absorbs only a small additional fraction of that residual on the four joined panels.  The result is therefore a rank-controlled mRNA H-candidate projection, not a promotion to translation survival.  A positive $S^{QT}$ entry would require an organism-matched translation readout, such as ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, estimated before protein-readout interpretation.  A downstream $S^{QP}$ or $M^{QTP}$ interpretation would then require a separate same-row protein-abundance analysis conditioned on that translation readout.  No translation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal pathway, or universal biological principle follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched empirical contact supplies the corresponding measurement and statistic.
