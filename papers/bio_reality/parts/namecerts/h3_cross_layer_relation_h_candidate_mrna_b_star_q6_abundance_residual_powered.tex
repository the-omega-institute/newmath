\subsection{B-star Q6 mRNA absorption after modeled translation complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-mrna-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite coding-sequence panel in which each row is indexed by an organism, a coding sequence or protein identifier after the available joins, nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, a controlled protein-abundance residual, a modeled translation readout, and a matched mRNA abundance covariate when that covariate is present.  The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  These coordinates are treated as synonymous residual directions that survive the amino-acid quotient; they are not amino-acid sequence features and are not protein-state measurements.  The shared control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an M-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each of the four organisms the recorded control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank boundary.}
The finite rows are large enough to make this a powered residual projection rather than the degenerate three-row surface refused elsewhere.  The joined mRNA/protein panels contain $13140$ rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3415$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5128$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The reported CDS-abundance joins before imposing the matched mRNA row condition are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$ rows respectively, while valid abundance records by protein are $14410$, $3747$, $19483$, and $6351$.  The matched mRNA counts are exactly the analysis row counts: $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.  Thus the mRNA covariate changes the admissible join in zebrafish and human most strongly, and the record keeps this as a sampling fact rather than converting it into biology.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance residual component associated with the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate block after the stated controls, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the part left after removing the modeled translation readout component.  The recorded descriptive complement ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{mod}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.8424312412078521,\\
 d_{mod}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.6628211177352172,\\
 d_{mod}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.8566190269974241,\\
 d_{mod}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding squared energies are $\|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467$ and $\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=919.9445878158525$ in zebrafish, $393.78692095732885$ and $261.0102870984463$ in \emph{E. coli}, $565.3850201235074$ and $484.31956581711796$ in human, and $675.3275479192903$ and $151.18264752811882$ in yeast.  The statistic therefore says that the modeled translation component leaves a large residual complement in zebrafish, \emph{E. coli}, and human, but a much smaller complement in yeast.  It does not by itself identify a ribosome mechanism, protein turnover process, localization state, or functional consequence.

\paragraph{mRNA absorption test.}
The H-candidate tested here is matched mRNA abundance.  The operation is a projection of the modeled-translation complement abundance residual onto residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA, using the same row-level controls.  The recorded absorption fractions are small in all four organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 a_{mRNA}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.011973033879985792,\\
 a_{mRNA}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.0017706954554908886,\\
 a_{mRNA}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.007858222847072218,\\
 a_{mRNA}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.0018976054721605224.
\end{aligned}
$$
After adding the mRNA projection, the combined residual ratios are $0.8322280386743373$ for zebrafish, $0.6615987644523418$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.8498079619223776$ for human, and $0.223360891719316$ for yeast.  On this finite panel, matched transcript abundance accounts for only about $1.20\%$, $0.18\%$, $0.79\%$, and $0.19\%$ of the modeled-complement residual in those organisms.  This is a bounded descriptive result: high absorption would not prove a causal transcript mechanism, and low absorption does not prove that the missing component has been found elsewhere.

\paragraph{Join self-check.}
The same computation was compared against a full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The current all-readout join and the reference join match within $0.02$ in \emph{E. coli} and yeast, with absolute differences $0.00017488612263594217$ and $0.012133464712697745$.  They fail that tolerance in zebrafish and human, with absolute differences $0.03219747984141397$ and $0.06427590857052146$.  The reference values are $0.874628721049266$, $0.6629960038578532$, $0.9208949355679455$, and $0.21173219572706703$, whereas the mRNA-matched values are $0.8424312412078521$, $0.6628211177352172$, $0.8566190269974241$, and $0.22386566043976477$.  The failed checks are interpreted as join-shape sensitivity: availability of matched mRNA rows changes the protein-identifier panel used by the projection.  They are not evidence for or against translation survival.

\paragraph{Relation to RNA-stability mediation rows.}
A separate finite cross-domain row set records mRNA half-life or stability mediation for the same nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ block in \emph{E. coli} and human.  In \emph{E. coli}, the stable-id count is $2720$, the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $2697$, $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.0875945621412319$, and the reported mediation fraction is $0.24697202727892137$ with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In human, the stable-id count is $16165$, the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $16142$, $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.03517257842112907$, and the reported mediation fraction is $0.1411299863319291$ with the same right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The half-life joins have reported CDS hit rates $0.9978649586335735$ for \emph{E. coli} and $0.9779294769799313$ for human.  These rows support a descriptive variance partition for RNA stability; they do not turn mRNA abundance or half-life into a causal bridge from codon residuals to protein output.

\paragraph{Ordered cross-layer interpretation.}
The admissible reading is ordered.  A same-coordinate claim from $B^*_{Q6}$ to a protein readout must first establish a controlled translation-readout survival entry $S^{QT}$ on a nondegenerate panel.  Only after that may a direct protein-readout surface $S^{QP}$ or a translation-mediated surface $M^{QTP}$ be interpreted.  The present mRNA absorption computation does not supply $S^{QT}$; it supplies a transcript covariate projection on the residual left after a modeled translation readout has been removed.  Consequently the finite row can be reported as a measured constraint on the H-candidate mRNA explanation of the modeled-complement residual, not as a positive translation-survival result.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No claim about translation realization, ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality follows from these rows alone.  Protein abundance remains a steady-state downstream readout, mRNA abundance remains a transcript-layer covariate, modeled tAI or stAI remains a bounded translation annotation, and $B^*_{Q6}$ remains a synonymous residual coordinate system until a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the stated controls are applied.  If all future rank-powered $S^{QT}$ entries are nonpositive, then the $B^*_{Q6}$ block remains a code-read or codon-usage residual basis for this packet and no downstream protein-mediation claim is admissible.
