\subsection{Rank-Powered mRNA Boundary for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Abundance Residual}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-mrna-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite panel of coding sequences in which nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are evaluated against protein-abundance residuals, modeled translation weights, and matched transcript abundance. The coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. The control design is fixed before interpretation: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors. Thus the coordinate being tested is a synonymous residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting, not an amino-acid sequence component.

\paragraph{Rows and rank.}
The joined rows are large enough to avoid the three-row rank-saturation pathology. The protein and mRNA join for \texttt{danio\_rerio} has $n=13140$, with $14410$ CDS rows joined for protein abundance and $13140$ valid mRNA rows; \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655} has $n=3415$, with $3739$ CDS rows joined and $3415$ valid mRNA rows; \texttt{homo\_sapiens} has $n=11554$, with $19053$ CDS rows joined and $11554$ valid mRNA rows; and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} has $n=5128$, with $5815$ CDS rows joined and $5128$ valid mRNA rows. In every organism the design reports $24$ control columns and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$. These ranks make the row space a powered regression setting rather than a saturated descriptive table.

\paragraph{Modeled complement before mRNA.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance residual component associated with the nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate block after the declared controls, and let $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the component left after projecting away the modeled translation-weight component. The finite-row diagnostic reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.8424312412078521,\\
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{ecoli}) &= 0.6628211177352172,\\
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.8566190269974241,\\
 d_{modeled}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding squared norms are $(1092.0114815505467,919.9445878158525)$ for danio, $(393.78692095732885,261.0102870984463)$ for E. coli, $(565.3850201235074,484.31956581711796)$ for human, and $(675.3275479192903,151.18264752811882)$ for yeast, where each ordered pair is $(\|P_Q\|^2,\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2)$. These values say that the modeled translation-weight complement remains substantial in all four organisms, but the statistic is still a descriptive projection and is not an observed translation-survival matrix.

\paragraph{mRNA absorption readback.}
Matched transcript abundance is then used only as a control-side candidate for absorbing the modeled-translation complement. The measured absorption is small in all four organisms: $0.011973033879985792$ for danio, $0.0017706954554908886$ for E. coli, $0.007858222847072218$ for human, and $0.0018976054721605224$ for yeast. The combined residual distances are $0.8322280386743373$, $0.6615987644523418$, $0.8498079619223776$, and $0.223360891719316$, respectively. Hence matched mRNA abundance does not absorb most of the modeled-complement abundance residual in this run. The conclusion is intentionally narrow: transcript abundance is a covariate that can be projected against the abundance residual, not a translation measurement and not a causal transcript mechanism.

\paragraph{Join self-check.}
The comparison with the full abundance-CDS join gives a join-sensitive warning. Danio changes from $0.874628721049266$ on the full abundance-CDS reference join to $0.8424312412078521$ on the all-readout join, an absolute difference of $0.03219747984141397$, so it fails the $0.02$ matching tolerance. Human changes from $0.9208949355679455$ to $0.8566190269974241$, an absolute difference of $0.06427590857052146$, also failing the tolerance. E. coli changes only by $0.00017488612263594217$, and yeast by $0.012133464712697745$, so those two pass the same tolerance. The biological reading is therefore organism-specific and join-sensitive: availability of matched mRNA rows changes the protein identifier panel in danio and human, and those two panels cannot be promoted as if they were the same finite sample.

\paragraph{Cross-domain stability check.}
A separate stability-mediated audit supplies a useful boundary comparison without changing the interpretation of the mRNA-abundance run. In E. coli the half-life three-way join uses $2720$ stable identifiers, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and growth-rate readout $0.40\,h^{-1}$. In human the three-way join uses $16165$ stable identifiers, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$, $16150$ Ensembl-gene joins, and $15$ gene-symbol fallback joins. The mediation fractions are $0.24697202727892137$ for E. coli and $0.1411299863319291$ for human, both with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. Those values support a descriptive variance-partition statement for mRNA stability in those two panels; they do not convert the abundance-control projection into a causal pathway.

\paragraph{Ordered cross-layer boundary.}
The permitted ordering is translation readout first, direct protein readout second, and translation-conditioned protein mediation third. In symbols, a direct protein-omics surface $S^{QP}$ or a mediated surface $M^{QTP}$ may be interpreted only after a controlled translation-readout surface $S^{QT}$ has been estimated on organism-matched rows. The present finite rows compute modeled-complement distances and mRNA absorption against abundance residuals; they do not contain a measured ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency survival matrix. Consequently the record supports a bounded cross-layer residual audit, not a translation-realization claim.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy, folding pathway, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from these rows. Protein abundance and transcript abundance are steady-state or condition-specific readouts, and modeled tAI or stAI weights are annotations rather than observed ribosome dynamics. A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact: first a controlled $Q_i\to T_j$ survival statistic for a direct translation readout, then a controlled $Q_i\to P_k$ statistic, and finally a mediation comparison testing whether conditioning on $T_j$ reduces the direct $Q_i\to P_k$ component. If all controlled $S^{QT}$ entries are nonpositive, the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates remain codon-usage residual coordinates with no admissible downstream protein-readout promotion.
