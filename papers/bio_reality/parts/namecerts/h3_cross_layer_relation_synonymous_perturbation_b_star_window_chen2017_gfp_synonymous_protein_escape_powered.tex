\subsection{Chen GFP synonymous perturbation as a bounded protein-output contact}
\label{sec:biorality-chen-gfp-synonymous-protein-escape}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The Chen GFP packet is a finite perturbation readback for synonymous coding changes in a reporter setting.  Its observed row surface contains $3556$ synonymous variants split across two assayed regions, with $1124$ rows in region $1$ and $2432$ rows in region $2$.  The finite resampling budget is $B=200$, and the recorded scalar linking protein output to out-of-frame mRNA is
$\mathrm{slope}_{P\mid \mathrm{OOF\ mRNA}}=0.016523671594873352$.
This is enough to place the dataset on the protein-output side of the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-perturbation ledger, but only as a finite reporter-contact surface.  The carrier is not a free biological law: each row is read as a tuple containing a synonymous coding perturbation, its region assignment, a protein-output readout, and the available matched RNA-side control used to prevent direct promotion from codon residual geometry to protein realization.

\paragraph{Local coordinate interpretation.}
The relevant $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis is the nine-coordinate list
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The Chen GFP rows are therefore interpreted as a finite perturbation window for testing whether changes aligned with these residual directions can survive the controls and appear in protein output.  The packet does not assert that every coordinate is active in this assay.  It records that the assay is row-sufficient for a bounded contact: $n=3556$ variants is large relative to the nine-dimensional residual basis, and the two region counts remain explicit rather than being hidden inside a pooled statistic.

\paragraph{Mediation discipline.}
The standing cross-layer rule for this chapter is that a direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not a protein-realization claim unless the relevant $Q_i \to T_j$ translation step is observed or explicitly bounded.  In the wider finite-row record, the translation-mediated protein-abundance computation used the same nine $Q$ coordinates and joined $12$ organisms, with per-organism matched protein counts ranging from $1656$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $19395$ in mouse.  Its mediated fractions included $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ for yeast, $0.612922252793667$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ for human, and $0.01850668564589326$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  Those numbers justify the use of a mediated matrix language in this paper, but they do not transfer the organism-level abundance result into the GFP perturbation assay by naming alone.

\paragraph{What the Chen rows add.}
The specific finite contribution here is the reporter perturbation surface itself: $1124+2432=3556$ synonymous variants with a recorded protein-to-out-of-frame-mRNA slope of $0.016523671594873352$ under $200$ resampling blocks.  The scalar is small and positive, so the RNA-side relation is present as a control pressure rather than as an explanation of the protein-output signal.  This supports a cautious statement: the Chen GFP assay can be used as a bounded protein-output contact for synonymous perturbation windows, provided that any $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-output claim is phrased through the observed finite rows and the RNA-side check, not through codon geometry alone.

\paragraph{Comparison with a stronger perturbation contact.}
A separate E. coli mRFP synonymous-output contact supplies a useful scale check without being merged into the Chen result.  That assay had $1459$ variants, $B=200$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matched}}=0.004975124378109453$, $p_{\mathrm{within}}=0.004975124378109453$, and a real-CAI robustness flag equal to true.  By contrast, the Chen GFP record made available here gives the finite row split and the protein-on-out-of-frame-mRNA slope, but not a matched null $p$-value, a description-length gain, or a residualized $\Delta R^2$ for the Chen rows.  The correct reading is therefore asymmetric: Chen GFP is a valid finite reporter-contact chapter for the synonymous perturbation boundary, while the stronger inferential scalars are not imported from the mRFP packet.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
The modeled translation-readout audit also matters locally.  It computed $81$ modeled-tAI coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ across $3$ organisms, with $\max |S_{ij}|=3801961.031607896$, but it explicitly marked the setting as underpowered for powered survival because only $3$ organisms were present.  Thus the Chen GFP perturbation rows do not by themselves establish translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, selection, or universality.  They also do not turn a direct residual-to-protein association into mediated protein realization when the translation step is absent, null, or merely named.  The chapter's positive content is narrower: a finite synonymous perturbation assay with $3556$ rows and an RNA-control scalar is admissible as a bounded protein-output contact for $B^*_{Q6}$ window analysis.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The claim is ready as a finite-row, protein-output boundary record.  Its strongest local statement is that the Chen GFP synonymous perturbation surface contributes $3556$ reporter variants, split $1124/2432$ by assayed region, with $B=200$ and $\mathrm{slope}_{P\mid \mathrm{OOF\ mRNA}}=0.016523671594873352$.  This permits controlled discussion of synonymous perturbation protein output, while keeping translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and universal biological law outside the claim unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies those measurements.
