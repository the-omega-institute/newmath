\subsection{Chen GFP synonymous-window protein escape surface}
\label{subsec:bioreality-chen-gfp-synonymous-protein-escape}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation carrier.}
The Chen GFP synonymous-window contact is represented here as a finite perturbation surface rather than as a general law about synonymous coding.  The rows are synonymous variants in a GFP assay, split into two reported window blocks with $1124$ rows in the first block and $2432$ rows in the second block, for a total of $3556$ variants.  The bounded computation used $200$ effective resampling draws and completed in $447.6606512069702$ seconds.  The scalar protein-on-out-of-frame-mRNA slope was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{P\mid M_{\mathrm{oof}}} &= 0.016523671594873352 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This slope is small and is read only as a finite assay-specific coupling between the measured protein channel and the out-of-frame mRNA channel.  It is not promoted into a claim that synonymous residual coordinates directly realize translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality.

\paragraph{Controlled readback shape.}
The relevant biological object is a finite matrix whose rows are synonymous GFP variants and whose columns are the measured assay channels available in this perturbation contact.  The admissible readback is therefore constrained to finite associations among synonymous-window residuals, mRNA-side controls, and protein escape readouts.  In the notation of the cross-layer program, a direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not enough for a protein-realization interpretation unless a translation readout $T_j$ is present and survives the same controlled row set.  The Chen contact gives a protein-channel perturbation surface and an out-of-frame mRNA slope, but the finite record supplied here does not include a same-row ribosome-occupancy, translation-efficiency, tAI, stAI, or equivalent translation-readout column for this GFP assay.  Consequently the chapter records a bounded synonymous perturbation result, not a completed $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ mediation chain for this experiment.

\paragraph{Local admissible statement.}
The admissible statement for this chapter is therefore narrow.  The Chen GFP synonymous-window assay supplies $3556$ finite variant rows, a two-block row split of $1124+2432$, and a measured protein-channel relation whose out-of-frame mRNA slope is $0.016523671594873352$.  This is enough to register a bounded synonymous perturbation contact at the protein-readout boundary.  It is also enough to block a stronger interpretation: the protein signal cannot be treated as a translation-mediated B$^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-realization claim unless the same finite rows are joined to a translation readout and the residual coordinate first survives the $Q_i \to T_j$ step after amino-acid composition, organism or construct context, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched mRNA controls are accounted for.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
The contact is a perturbation assay over synonymous GFP variants, not a universal codon law.  It does not establish ribosome dynamics, cotranslational folding, mature folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selective advantage, or a general mechanism.  It also does not justify promoting CUN/UUR directionality, a single organism sign, or any one residual coordinate into a population-level rule.  A later positive claim would require a joined finite table containing the same synonymous-window coordinates, a direct translation readout such as ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, matched mRNA abundance, and matched protein abundance.  Until that table exists and the $Q_i \to T_j$ entry is positive under the declared controls, the Chen GFP record remains a bounded protein-channel perturbation surface with an explicit translation-contact gap.
