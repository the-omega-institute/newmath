$$
\begin{aligned}

 n_{\mathrm{region\ 2}} &= 2432,\\

\end{aligned}
$$

In the language of the residual-coordinate program, the admissible readback is not a naked map from a synonymous coordinate to protein output.  It is a controlled finite comparison in which a codon-window residual may be associated with protein output only after the relevant transcript and translation-facing controls have been kept visible.

The finite scalar reported for the direct protein-versus-out-of-frame-mRNA check is

\begin{aligned}
 \widehat{\beta}_{\mathrm{protein}\mid\mathrm{oof\ mRNA}} &= 0.016523671594873352.

It only says that, on the available rows, the protein output has a measured nonzero association with the out-of-frame mRNA control axis.

The scientifically honest conclusion is therefore not that the Chen panel independently proves a strong cross-layer law.  It is that the Chen panel contributes a finite perturbation surface with a concrete protein-output readback and a concrete transcript-control scalar.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The chapter-specific conclusion is a bounded one.  The finite matrix contains $3556$ synonymous GFP variants across two reported regions and a measured protein-control slope $0.016523671594873352$ against the out-of-frame mRNA axis.  This is enough to keep the record in the protein-output audit ledger and to motivate comparison with translation-mediated $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ analyses.  It is not enough to promote the residual coordinate to an autonomous biological mechanism.  A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not treated as protein realization unless a same-panel or explicitly bounded translation readout $T_j$ survives the declared controls.  The local controls include amino-acid sequence or composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, the residual-coordinate support baseline, and matched transcript abundance.  Without that separated translation contact, no claim follows about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, mechanism, or universality.
