\subsection{Chen GFP Synonymous Perturbation as a B-Star Window Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-chen-gfp-synonymous-protein-escape}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
This record treats the Chen GFP synonymous perturbation panel as a finite cross-layer test surface rather than as a direct claim about folding, function, or selection. The carrier is the set of variant rows on which a synonymous coding perturbation can be paired with a protein-output readout and with the local $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates used by the residual packet. The verified run records $3556$ synonymous variant rows, split into $1124$ rows in the first region and $2432$ rows in the second region. The resampling budget was $200$ blocks, and the run time was $447.6606512069702$ seconds. In this chapter the finite row set is written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= V_1 \sqcup V_2,\\
|V_1| &= 1124,\\
|V_2| &= 2432,\\
|V| &= 3556,\\
B &= 200.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each row is read only as a perturbation-indexed observation of a synonymous sequence choice and a downstream GFP protein-output measurement. The row does not by itself identify a ribosomal rate, a folded-state change, a molecular-function change, or an organism-level phenotype.

\paragraph{Local residual readback.}
The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ side of the record is a nine-coordinate residual basis inherited by the surrounding translation-mediated program: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. These are not promoted to biochemical causes. They are finite coordinates used to ask whether synonymous perturbations align with a measurable output after simpler codon-count and composition effects have been kept separate. The Chen GFP row set is therefore a perturbation-level test of whether a local synonymous window can carry protein-output information, not a proof that the same coordinate has survived through an observed translation-efficiency step.

\paragraph{Observed scalar contact.}
The available finite scalar linking the Chen GFP surface to transcript-level leakage is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\mathrm{protein}\mid \mathrm{oof\ mRNA}} &= 0.016523671594873352.
\end{aligned}
$$
This slope is small in absolute scale and is used here as a local audit number: it records how the protein readout aligns with the out-of-frame mRNA control channel in the verified run. It is not a replacement for a same-row ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency readout. The row count is large enough to support a finite perturbation audit, and the $200$-block budget supplies a bounded resampling design, but the scalar itself does not state a universal sign law, a codon-family population law, or a mechanistic ribosome interpretation.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
The broader protein-abundance mediation record supplies the relevant comparison class. In that finite matrix, $12$ organism-level panels were joined to real CDS codon counts and measured protein abundance, with joined protein counts ranging from $1656$ for $Sulfolobus\ solfataricus$ to $19395$ for $Mus\ musculus$. The same record reports a joint nine-dimensional mediation scalar in which the mediated fractions remain within the unit interval for all listed organisms; for example, the fractions include $0.612922252793667$ for $Escherichia\ coli$ K12 MG1655, $0.9491551041216973$ for $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, $0.27206289160119157$ for $Homo\ sapiens$, and $0.01850668564589326$ for $Sulfolobus\ solfataricus$. Those numbers show that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis can be placed in a controlled $Q \to T \to P$ matrix when a translation readout and protein abundance are both present. The Chen GFP perturbation panel is narrower: it supplies a controlled synonymous perturbation surface with protein output, but the provided finite row for this specific run does not include an observed same-row $T$ variable such as ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, tAI, or stAI.

\paragraph{Modeled translation boundary.}
The modeled tAI survival matrix gives a second boundary condition. It computed the same nine residual coordinates across $3$ organisms, used a coupling threshold of $0.01$, and found $81$ modeled entries above threshold, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$. The same record states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that powered survival would require at least $40$ matched species. Thus the Chen GFP result is not allowed to borrow modeled-tAI coupling as if it were observed ribosome dynamics. The perturbation run remains a finite protein-output audit until a matched translation readout is attached to the same variants or to a rigorously joined row system.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
The admissible statement is the following. On the Chen GFP synonymous perturbation surface, $3556$ finite variant rows, split as $1124+2432$, support a bounded protein-output audit for local $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates under a $200$-block resampling design. The recorded out-of-frame mRNA slope $0.016523671594873352$ is a finite control-facing scalar, not a mechanism. The result may be used as evidence that synonymous perturbation rows can carry a protein-output signal that deserves comparison with translation-mediated $Q \to T \to P$ matrices. It may not be stated as direct translation realization, folding realization, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
A direct $Q \to P$ association on this panel is not enough to certify protein realization through translation. A separate contact must supply the intermediate $Q \to T$ step, and that step must survive the appropriate controls before the downstream protein-output association can be described as translation-mediated. Matched mRNA abundance, out-of-frame mRNA behavior, modeled codon-adaptation weights, and local synonymous-window geometry are useful controls or bounded readbacks, but none of them is by itself a ribosome measurement. Likewise, a GFP protein-output change is not a folded-structure assay, a physical-admissibility assay, a molecular-function assay, a fitness assay, or a universal biological rule. The chapter therefore closes at a finite perturbation-and-protein-output boundary: it is ready as a controlled BioReality record, while the translation, structure, function, mechanism, and universality claims remain unavailable unless layer-matched measurements are supplied.
