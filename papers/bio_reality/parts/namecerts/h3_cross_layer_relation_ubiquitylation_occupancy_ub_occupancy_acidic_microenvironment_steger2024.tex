\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA-decay and RBP-occupancy boundary for the Q6 residual coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-occupancy-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry a $B^*_Q6$ residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, a matched mRNA-abundance control, and externally attached columns for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein when such columns exist. The intended biological question is not whether a synonymous coordinate can be named, but whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference can be promoted to a bounded cross-layer relation after the translation boundary and the decay-factor occupancy boundary have both been supplied. In the present finite memory, the closest completed computational contact on the linked ubiquitylation-occupancy side is the acidic-microenvironment Steger-style surface with $2883$ proteins and $10410$ sites evaluated under $2000$ permutations. The same verification snapshot also contains a protein-thermal-stability linked surface with $6961$ rows and a deterministic runtime bucket reported as $70.0$ seconds. These numbers show that finite row-counted surfaces exist in the surrounding BioReality memory, but they do not by themselves instantiate the RNA half-life, translation-readout, RBP-occupancy, perturbation, and rescue columns required for the RNA-decay bridge described in this section.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive statistic for this chapter would have to be computed on rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_i = (&q_i,\; T_i,\; H_i,\; A_i,\; O_i,\; L_i,\; R_i,\; C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^*_Q6$ residual coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance, $O_i$ is measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, $L_i$ is the target-RBP loss condition, $R_i$ is wild-type rescue, and $C_i$ records binding-defective or non-target controls. The finite readback is allowed to ask whether the half-life contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows follows measured RBP occupancy after amino-acid composition, local sequence composition, GC and dinucleotide content, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, local RNA-structure terms, motif density, batch, and same-row translation readout have been represented in the control set. A schematic admissible contrast is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
    &= \widehat{H}(q_i=\mathrm{high}, O_i=1, Z_i)
       - \widehat{H}(q_i=\mathrm{low}, O_i=0, Z_i),\\
  Z_i
    &= (T_i, A_i, \mathrm{composition}_i, \mathrm{structure}_i,
        \mathrm{motif}_i, \mathrm{batch}_i, \mathrm{isoform}_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
with the additional requirement that the same effect collapses under target-RBP loss and returns under wild-type rescue, while binding-defective or non-target controls fail to reproduce the rescue. Without those finite columns, the statistic remains only a proposed row shape rather than an observed RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Current finite evidence.}
The available verified scalar evidence for the linked ubiquitylation-occupancy surface is concrete but layer-mismatched for this RNA question: $n_{\mathrm{prots}}=2883$, $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=10410$, and $2000$ permutations are recorded for the acidic-microenvironment occupancy computation. Those values are useful as a finite-row audit anchor for the linked post-translational occupancy claim, not as evidence of RNA half-life control. The linked thermal-stability surface contributes a separate finite-row anchor with $n=6961$ and a reported deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds, but it also belongs to a different readout layer. Neither surface supplies same-organism same-window RNA half-life rows joined to measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, nor does either supply target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective controls for the $B^*_Q6$ RNA/CDS windows.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded null boundary rather than a positive cross-layer bridge. The finite coordinate label may still be used as a descriptor of synonymous RNA/CDS windows, and RNA half-life may still be read as an RNA-stability association when a half-life contact is present. However, a decay-factor RBP mechanism is not licensed unless occupancy and perturbation data are present in the same finite row system and the half-life effect tracks those data under coordinate/occupancy decoupling. Similarly, the same-row translation readout is a gate on interpretation, not a substitute for RBP occupancy or rescue. No translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the half-life boundary alone. Those higher-layer claims require their own matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
