\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA half-life and decay-factor occupancy}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The object considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels, local synonymous-window annotations, a same-row translation readout when available, a matched RNA half-life readout, a matched mRNA-abundance control, and, for any positive decay-factor interpretation, externally attached occupancy and perturbation columns for a prespecified RNA-binding decay factor. The intended row type is therefore not a free-standing codon geometry and not an isolated half-life table. It is a bounded table in which high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows can be compared only after amino-acid composition, local sequence composition, transcript abundance, and same-row translation boundary conditions have been made explicit.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The closest finite external audit attached to the present record is the protein-side ubiquitylation occupancy run keyed as h3.cross\_layer\_relation.ubiquitylation\_occupancy.ub\_occupancy\_acidic\_microenvironment\_steger2024. That run contains $2883$ proteins and $10410$ ubiquitylation sites, with $2000$ permutations recorded in the deterministic output. Its role in this chapter is not to supply RNA decay data. It instead fixes the standard of evidence expected for a cross-layer relation: a finite row set, declared readout columns, and an explicit resampling or control protocol. Two linked finite audits provide scale checks for adjacent cross-layer records: h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_thermal\_stability.scd\_charge\_patterning\_thermal\_stability\_meltome\_human has $6961$ rows in its recorded run, and h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_degron\_stability.degron\_terminal\_motif\_stability\_voutsinos2026 has $5303$ proteins and $5303$ C-terminal rows. These numbers are used here only as finite-row comparators. They do not transfer their protein-layer conclusions to the RNA half-life question.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive RNA-decay bridge would require a statistic on rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i, T_i, A_i, H_i, O_i, L_i, R_i, C_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout or an explicit missing-readout boundary mark, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ is target-RBP loss status, $R_i$ is wild-type rescue status, $C_i$ records binding-defective or non-target control arms, and $Z_i$ contains transcription-rate, local RNA-structure, motif-density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, batch, and rank-sufficiency controls. The bridge claim is allowed only if the coordinate-associated half-life contrast follows $O_i$ after coordinate and occupancy have been decoupled, collapses under target-RBP loss, and returns under wild-type rescue but not under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The present finite memory does not contain same-organism, same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy; it also does not contain target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective and non-target control rows for this RNA half-life subject. Consequently, the strongest local statement is a bounded null: after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation-readout boundary, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life unless a layer-matched occupancy and perturbation table breaks that exchangeability. In symbols, the permitted default is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid Z_i, T_i, A_i
\end{aligned}
$$
for the available half-life boundary, not a mechanistic decay-factor conclusion.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
Matched RNA half-life rows can identify an RNA-stability surface. Matched mRNA abundance can serve as a transcription-layer control. Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency can attach the same-row translation boundary. None of these contacts, separately or jointly, measures decay-factor RBP occupancy or proves an RNA-decay mechanism. The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate remains a finite descriptor unless the externally measured occupancy, perturbation, and rescue columns are present on the same window panel and the declared finite statistic passes the stated controls.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from RNA half-life alone. A same-row translation readout does not by itself explain decay. A coordinate-associated half-life difference does not identify an RBP. An RBP occupancy association does not become a bridge unless coordinate/occupancy decoupling is shown and target-RBP loss with wild-type rescue separates the target from binding-defective and non-target controls. The chapter therefore records a ready finite boundary statement, not a positive RBP-mediated cross-layer mechanism.
