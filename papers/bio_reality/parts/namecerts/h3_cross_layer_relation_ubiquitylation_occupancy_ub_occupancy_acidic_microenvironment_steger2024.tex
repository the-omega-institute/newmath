\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA half-life and decay-factor occupancy}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge-steger2024}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label, an RNA half-life readout, a matched mRNA abundance control, and a required same-row translation readout before any cross-layer interpretation is allowed. The intended positive case is not an RNA half-life association by itself. It is the stronger finite situation in which high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows remain distinguishable only through a prespecified measured decay-factor RBP occupancy surface, under explicit separation of coordinate state from occupancy state, and only after target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls have been read on the same experimental panel.

\paragraph{Current measured rows.}
The locally verified finite run attached to the Steger2024 ubiquitylation-occupancy claim is run $0b96a8e4da3c$. It contains $10410$ ubiquitylation sites on $2883$ proteins and used $2000$ actual permutations in the recorded computation. The deterministic report stores runtime as $0.0$ seconds because the stdout payload is fixed and wall time is measured by the surrounding harness. These numbers are real finite-row counts, but they belong to a protein ubiquitylation occupancy surface, not to a same-window RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP perturbation panel. Two adjacent cross-layer checks give scale but not mechanism for this chapter: the human charge-patterning thermal-stability run $8873440304f9$ reports $6961$ rows with a $70.0$ second runtime bucket, and the terminal degron stability run $1a932ef5684e$ reports $5303$ C-terminal rows on $5303$ proteins with runtime $40.3029$ seconds. They show that related cross-layer finite panels have been materialized, while the RNA-decay RBP bridge itself still requires its own matched rows.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
For a row $i$, write $q_i$ for the residual coordinate label, $T_i$ for the same-row translation readout, $A_i$ for matched mRNA abundance, $H_i$ for RNA half-life, and $O_i$ for measured decay-factor RBP occupancy when that contact is actually present. The finite bridge is permitted only for a declared statistic of the following shape:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A) &= H_{\mathrm{high}} - H_{\mathrm{low}},\\
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{loss}) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H(q,O \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{binding\ defective}) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline controls. The statistic must be evaluated on a single finite joined panel. A contact name for half-life, mRNA abundance, or translation efficiency is not enough unless the rows are actually joined and the control columns are present.

\paragraph{Null state.}
The null condition is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA windows with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and the same-row translation boundary are imposed. In that state, any observed half-life difference remains a bounded RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor. It does not become an RBP-mediated cross-layer result. The current verified Steger2024 finite counts do not supply same-organism, same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective and non-target rescue arms for this RNA-decay subject. Therefore the chapter records a constrained admissibility test rather than a positive RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Readout separation.}
RNA half-life, matched mRNA abundance, and translation efficiency occupy different roles. Half-life is the candidate RNA-stability readout. mRNA abundance is a transcription-layer control used to prevent transcript-level abundance from masquerading as translation or stability. Translation efficiency or ribosome occupancy supplies the same-row boundary that prevents RNA decay from being promoted to downstream protein realization. None of these, alone or in combination, measures decay-factor RBP occupancy unless an explicit occupancy experiment is joined to the row. Similarly, the ubiquitylation occupancy run with $10410$ sites and $2883$ proteins cannot be reinterpreted as an RNA half-life or RBP perturbation assay; it is useful here only as a neighboring verified protein-layer finite surface.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
This chapter permits the following statement and no stronger one: a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate-associated RNA half-life readout may be treated as a bounded cross-layer bridge only if same-row translation data, matched mRNA controls, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are all present on the finite joined panel and the declared statistic passes the stated control structure. Without those rows, no translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, RNA-decay mechanism, RBP mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate label or from RNA half-life. The present finite evidence is therefore suitable for recording the gate and its failure modes, while a positive bridge remains conditional on a future same-window occupancy and perturbation panel.
