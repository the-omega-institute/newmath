\subsection{Translation-Bounded RNA-Decay RBP Bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel in which each row carries a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and, for a positive RNA-decay bridge, prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and control columns. The coordinate label is not treated as a biological measurement by itself. It is a row classifier whose use is allowed only after the row has been joined to observed readout surfaces. The finite evidence table attached to the ubiquitylation-occupancy comparison supplies $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=10410$ modified sites over $n_{\mathrm{prots}}=2883$ proteins with $2000$ permutations recorded in run $0b96a8e4da3c$. Those numbers are useful here as a scale anchor for finite proteome-level contact, but they do not contain RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, or rescue rows. Two nearby cross-layer finite checks also delimit the scope: the charge-patterning thermal-stability table contains $n=6961$ rows in run $8873440304f9$, and the degron terminal-motif stability table contains $n_{\mathrm{cterm}}=5303$ rows over $n_{\mathrm{proteins}}=5303$ in run $1a932ef5684e$. These additional row counts show that the BioReality archive can support finite cross-layer tests when the correct layer-specific contact is present; they do not substitute for the missing RNA-decay RBP panel.

\paragraph{Claim form.}
The admissible claim is deliberately narrow. A coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference may be reported as a bounded cross-layer RNA-decay bridge only if the same finite row simultaneously carries the residual coordinate, the translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a coordinate/occupancy decoupling design, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. In symbols, the row-level admissibility condition is the conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_i={}&Q_i \wedge T_i \wedge H_i \wedge M_i \wedge O_i \\
&\wedge D_i \wedge L_i \wedge W_i \wedge C_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $M_i$ is matched mRNA abundance, $O_i$ is measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, $D_i$ is coordinate/occupancy decoupling, $L_i$ is target-RBP loss, $W_i$ is wild-type rescue, and $C_i$ denotes binding-defective or non-target controls. If any of $T_i,O_i,D_i,L_i,W_i,C_i$ is absent, the row can support at most a coordinate-indexed RNA-stability association or a bounded descriptor of half-life variation. It cannot be promoted to a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Required statistic.}
The finite statistic must ask whether the high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows remain exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls, and whether any residual half-life contrast follows the measured RBP-occupancy surface. The control set is not optional: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation-readout boundary must be present before the RBP bridge is evaluated. A positive bridge requires three signs on the same panel: first, the half-life contrast tracks measured decay-factor RBP occupancy after coordinate and occupancy have been experimentally decoupled; second, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect; third, wild-type rescue restores the effect while binding-defective or non-target controls do not. The finite readout can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(Q\mid Z,T,M) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H(Q\mid Z,T,M,O) &\text{ follows } O,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} &\text{ returns},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $Z$ denoting the full finite control matrix. These displayed relations are the chapter's target measurement shape, not a statement that the attached row data already satisfy it.

\paragraph{What the attached rows show.}
The verified ubiquitylation-occupancy row set is a legitimate finite biological contact at its own layer: $10410$ sites, $2883$ proteins, and $2000$ permutations establish that the corresponding protein-site occupancy analysis was executed as a bounded finite computation. The thermal-stability and degron finite tables likewise give real row counts at their respective layers, $6961$ thermal-stability rows and $5303$ terminal-motif rows. None of these three verified facts is an RNA half-life experiment, a same-window RBP occupancy assay, a target-RBP knockout, or a rescue experiment. Therefore the present chapter records a ready negative boundary for the RNA-decay RBP bridge: the coordinate can be carried forward as a finite label, and the half-life contact can be used as an RNA readout boundary, but the RBP-mediated decay bridge is not established by the available rows.

\paragraph{Local exclusion.}
No translation output, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal biological rule follows from RNA half-life alone. A same-row translation readout is only a gate for evaluating the cross-layer question; it is not proof that RNA decay realizes protein output. Likewise, matched mRNA abundance is a transcription-layer control, not half-life evidence by itself. Measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is indispensable for the mechanism-bearing interpretation, and perturbation plus rescue controls are indispensable for separating an RBP bridge from a coordinate-correlated association. Without those layer-matched rows, the scientifically correct conclusion is the null form: after the declared controls and the same-row translation boundary, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows remain exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life for the purpose of claiming an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.
