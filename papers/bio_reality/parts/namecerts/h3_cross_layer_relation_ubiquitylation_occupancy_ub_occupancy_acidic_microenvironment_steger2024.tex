\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA half-life and decay-factor occupancy}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge-steger-occupancy}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel in which a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$ is attached to RNA or CDS windows only as a row label, not as a biological observation by itself. The intended row has four measured or controlled faces: a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy or perturbation column. The row class is therefore not an unrestricted RNA-stability claim. It is a guarded cross-layer question: whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference remains interpretable after the translation boundary and abundance control are present, and whether the remaining half-life contrast follows a measured decay-factor occupancy rather than the coordinate label alone.

\paragraph{Available finite counts.}
The local verified table for the Steger-style ubiquitylation-occupancy comparison contains $2883$ proteins and $10410$ sites, with $2000$ actual permutations recorded for the deterministic finite statistic. The same verification bundle also carries two neighboring cross-layer calibration sizes: a protein thermal-stability panel with $6961$ rows and a C-terminal degron-stability panel with $5303$ proteins, equivalently $5303$ C-terminal rows. These counts matter only as finite audit anchors. They show that the BioReality packet is being evaluated against bounded tabular surfaces, not against an unlimited biological law. No verified $R$ list, $M$ list, lambda value, module class, hit rate, or p-value is present in the supplied row payload for the RNA-decay RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive bridge would require a same-organism, same-window finite matrix whose rows contain the coordinate label, translation readout, mRNA control, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. In such a matrix, the permitted readout is a bounded contrast of RNA half-life after the declared controls have been applied:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= H(\text{high } B^*_{Q6} \mid Z, T, A, O)
     - H(\text{low } B^*_{Q6} \mid Z, T, A, O), \\
Z
  &= \{\text{amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC, dinucleotide content, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, batch}\}, \\
T
  &= \text{same-row translation readout}, \\
A
  &= \text{matched mRNA abundance}, \\
O
  &= \text{measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation state}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The bridge is supported only if the coordinate-associated half-life contrast tracks the measured occupancy under coordinate/occupancy decoupling, collapses after target-RBP loss, and returns under wild-type rescue while failing under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Current readback.}
The supplied row data do not contain the required RBP occupancy surface, target-RBP loss rows, wild-type rescue rows, or binding-defective and non-target control rows. The attached contacts therefore bound the question but do not close it. The RNA half-life contact can say that a transcript or gene row has an organism-scoped half-life estimate. The matched mRNA contact can serve as a transcription-layer denominator or abundance covariate. The ribosome-profiling contact can supply a translation-efficiency or ribosome-occupancy boundary when a same-row join exists. None of these contacts, alone or in combination as named routes, supplies measured decay-factor occupancy or the perturbation/rescue logic needed for an RBP-mediated RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
Under the present finite evidence, the conservative interpretation is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life once transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the same-row translation boundary are imposed. The record may preserve a bounded RNA-stability association target, but it does not yet license an RBP mechanism. It also does not identify the RBP by geometry: the RBP must be prespecified by an independent occupancy and perturbation surface before the coordinate label can be tested against it.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation output follows from RNA half-life alone. No folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate, from codon-window geometry, from matched mRNA abundance, from RNA half-life, or from the Steger-style finite occupancy audit counts. A protein-level claim would require its own protein-output contact; a structure claim would require a structure-level contact; a mechanism claim here requires measured decay-factor RBP occupancy together with loss and rescue controls. Until those columns are present on the same finite rows, the chapter records a bounded boundary condition rather than a positive cross-layer mechanism.
