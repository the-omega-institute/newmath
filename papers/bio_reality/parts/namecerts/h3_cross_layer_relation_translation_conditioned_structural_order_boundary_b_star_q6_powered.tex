\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which each $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate is used only after amino-acid-quotient residualization and after ordinary sequence controls have been placed beside the coordinate matrix. The tested downstream readout is predicted structural-order confidence, not experimentally observed folding and not molecular function. The attached structural-order panel contains $3412$ joined coding-sequence rows for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ joined rows for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The computation used $5$ folds, required at least $300$ joined rows, and was recorded under run id \texttt{1b97387835f7} with seed \texttt{sha256:43d63f9983efeb9bb7ebddb12e57570dc862f180b10174a4e85a0e270a65cef6}. The resulting call is finite-row and descriptive: the verdict is \texttt{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer} for the measured panel.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control surface.}
The upstream structural-order calculation used the nine residual coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The control block contained an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, where $M$-density is the fraction of sense codons lying in the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. In the structural-order source panel, both organisms were rank-powered for the nine-coordinate design: \textit{E. coli} had $9$ residual coordinate columns, control-column count $24$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, and residual degrees of freedom $3713$ after $Z$; yeast had the same $9$ coordinate columns, control-column count $24$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, and residual degrees of freedom $5781$ after $Z$. \paragraph{Relation to the translation-gated structural-order source.}
The supporting translation-gated structural-order source used AlphaFold mean pLDDT on a $0$ to $100$ scale as a predicted-confidence readout only. In that source, the structural-order join was nearly complete for the matched codon panels: \textit{E. coli} used $3736$ matched proteins from $3739$ codon-joined entries, with join hit rate $0.999198$, while yeast used $5804$ matched proteins from $5815$ codon-joined entries, with join hit rate $0.998108$. The structural-order survival $R^2_{SO}$ values were $0.03059081709413562$ for \textit{E. coli} and $0.0715885627979685$ for yeast, with all $9$ coordinates surviving in each organism under the stated controls. These numbers explain why the present boundary is treated as a crossed structural-order layer for the finite panel: the signal is not a single-row or single-coordinate accident, and the controlled rank condition is nondegenerate in both organisms.

\paragraph{Comparison with nearby stability readouts.}
The same $B^*_{Q6}$ family does not license a general promotion from structural-order confidence to protein stability. In the separate translation-conditioned stability boundary, the reported direct stability association summary had $n=3$, minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, maximum $0.03602995605625481$, and positive count $3$, but the translation-conditioned full-control delta was not uniformly positive: $n=3$, minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, positive count $1$, negative count $2$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip probability $0.946441650390625$. That separate stability call was only \texttt{partial}. The contrast matters: the structural-order boundary is supported by a positive two-organism full-control delta surface, whereas the stability surface does not supply the same translation-conditioned support.

\paragraph{Minimal-compilation context.}
The surrounding residual-compressibility audit also prevents overinterpretation. Across four computed organisms, the minimal compilation law was not supported by the measured low-dimensional readout set. The median compressibility was $0.026$, with organism-level values $0.024368095798914635$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.005717621488177116$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast. Each organism-level permutation probability in that audit was $0.000999000999000999$. Therefore the structural-order result should be read as a finite controlled cross-layer association, not as evidence that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source has been compressed into a small universal biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Local scope restriction.}
The chapter permits exactly this statement: in the two-organism matched structural-order panel, amino-acid-residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates retain a positive translation-conditioned association with predicted structural-order confidence under the stated controls. It does not permit a direct claim about translation output without the translation readout, and it does not permit folding, physical admissibility, protein function, cellular phenotype, degradation, localization, interaction, causation, mechanism, or universality. A claim at any one of those layers would require its own layer-matched contact, its own joined finite rows, and its own controlled statistic. Bulk protein abundance, mRNA abundance, tAI or stAI weights, codon-usage tables, workflow eligibility, or residual coordinate geometry are not substitutes for such contacts.
