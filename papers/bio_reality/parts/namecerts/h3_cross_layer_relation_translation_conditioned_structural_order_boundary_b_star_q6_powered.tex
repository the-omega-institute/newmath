\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{sec:bior reality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel and admissible reading.}
This record concerns a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are first formed after amino-acid quotient residualization and are then tested against a downstream structural-order surface only through a translation-conditioned route. The admissible chain is not a direct route from codon geometry to protein state. It has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow O_k,\\
Z &= (\hbox{amino-acid composition},\ \hbox{organism},\ \mathrm{GC3},\ \hbox{CDS length},\ \hbox{$M$-density},\ \hbox{mRNA controls}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is a residual coordinate, $T_j$ is a matched translation readout, $O_k$ is the structural-order readout, and $Z$ is the declared control surface. The chapter-specific boundary is that a positive structural-order result may be reported only as a finite translation-conditioned residual association. It does not license claims of translation realization, folding kinetics, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies that claim.

\paragraph{Coordinate basis and rank gate.}
The powered structural-order screen used nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates: $K\_\mathrm{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Arg}\_\mathrm{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}\_\mathrm{AUA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}\_{\mathrm{CUN}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}\_{\mathrm{UUA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}\_{\mathrm{UCR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}\_{\mathrm{UCA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}\_{\mathrm{ACR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f_3$-stress. In the structural-order survival computation the control design used an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $\mathrm{GC3}$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. The powered rank check was nondegenerate in both organisms: for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, the control column count was $24$, and the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $3713$; for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, the control column count was $24$, and the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $5781$. Thus the nine-coordinate residual surface is not being read from a rank-saturated design in these two structural-order rows.

\paragraph{Observed structural-order rows.}
The layer readout in the supporting structural-order contact is AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale, used only as predicted structure-confidence signal. The codon-to-structure join was nearly complete in both organisms used for this route: \textit{E. coli} joined $3739$ codon rows to $3736$ matched proteins, with $3736$ structural-order proteins and hit rate $0.999198$; \textit{S. cerevisiae} joined $5815$ codon rows to $5804$ matched proteins, with $5804$ structural-order proteins and hit rate $0.998108$. In that same supporting screen, the structural-order residual association was finite in both organisms: $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ for \textit{E. coli} with $9$ surviving coordinates, and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ for \textit{S. cerevisiae} with $9$ surviving coordinates. These numbers establish only a controlled association with predicted confidence, not an experimental folded-state measurement.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned powered result.}
The finite powered boundary test for this claim was run under experiment id $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_translation\_conditioned\_structural\_order\_boundary\_powered}$ with run id $1\mathrm{b}97387835\mathrm{f}7$, five folds, minimum joined-row threshold $300$, and status passed. The structural-order joined counts in the powered test were $3412$ rows for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ rows for \textit{S. cerevisiae}. The direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$-to-order predictive summary was positive in both organisms, with $n=2$, minimum $0.03664544696481631$, maximum $0.1604655467663032$, mean $0.09855549686555976$, median $0.09855549686555976$, positive count $2$, and negative count $0$.

The decisive finite-row statistic is the full-control translation-conditioned $\Delta R^2$ surface. Across the two organisms the aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ had minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, median $0.005284160876443678$, $n=2$, positive count $2$, and negative count $0$. The weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $0.004764742175882462$. The sign-flip test for $\Delta R^2>0$ gave $p=0.0029296875$. The corresponding permuted full-control $\Delta R^2$ surface was negative in both organisms, with minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, maximum $-0.000779938262202795$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, median $-0.00212186632044159$, positive count $0$, and negative count $2$. The finite module call is therefore $\mathrm{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer}$, with organism call counts recording two full-control-positive entries.

\paragraph{Relation to stability and compression checks.}
The same coordinate family is not uniformly promoted to every downstream protein-state surface. In the translation-conditioned stability boundary, the powered run had status passed but only a partial verdict: the aggregate full-control stability $\Delta R^2$ had mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, $n=3$, positive count $1$, negative count $2$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$. This contrast matters for interpretation: the present structural-order boundary is supported as a finite translation-conditioned predicted-confidence association, while the related stability boundary remains only partial and must not be used as a protein-stability mechanism. The minimal-compilation check also constrains over-reading. Across computed organisms the median compressibility was $0.026$, with organism-level compressibilities $0.024368095798914635$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.005717621488177116$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $0.13234136547055853$ for \textit{S. cerevisiae}; all four reported permutation values were $0.000999000999000999$. Thus the residual source is not highly compressed by the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary, and the structural-order crossing should be read as a bounded finite association rather than as a general compilation law.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The supported statement is narrow. In the finite powered panel, $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain a positive full-control translation-conditioned association with predicted structural-order confidence in the two organisms tested. The result does not say that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ directly realizes translation, determines folding, controls degradation, explains physical admissibility, produces biological function, causes phenotype, or defines a universal biological law. A stronger statement would require an independently matched contact at the relevant layer, such as direct ribosome occupancy for translation realization, experimental folding or stability assays for protein-state claims, functional perturbation for function, and phenotype assays for organism-level consequence.
