\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record treats the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates as sequence-derived covariates that must pass through a measured or explicitly bounded translation layer before any structural-order readout is interpreted.  The finite computation used two organism panels for the structural-order boundary: \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 with $3412$ joined rows and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} with $5119$ joined rows.  The model was run with $5$ folds, a minimum joined-row requirement of $300$, and seed $\mathrm{sha256{:}43d63f9983efeb9bb7ebddb12e57570dc862f180b10174a4e85a0e270a65cef6}$.  The observed structural-order readout is a predicted-confidence surface, not an experimental folding assay.  In the upstream structural-order survival computation, the readout was AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale; the sequence-to-readout join used $3736$ matched proteins in \textit{E. coli} with hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched proteins in yeast with hit rate $0.998108$.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate design.}
The coordinate set consists of nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates: $K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f\_3$-stress.  Each coordinate is interpreted only after amino-acid-quotient residualization.  The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The rank diagnostics were nondegenerate in both organisms: the control column count was $24$, the control rank was $23$, the residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ rank was $9$, and powered rank sufficiency was true.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls were $3713$ for \textit{E. coli} and $5781$ for yeast.  The corresponding residual readout energies were $136607.52873622958$ and $620506.1490869389$, while the explained energies from the residual coordinate block were $4178.935925251874$ and $44421.14342043593$.

\paragraph{Observed structural-order association.}
Before translation conditioning is used as a boundary, the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ structural-order association is positive in both computed organism panels.  The summary statistic $B^{\ast}_{Q6}\to\mathrm{order}$ has $n=2$, minimum $0.03664544696481631$, maximum $0.1604655467663032$, mean $0.09855549686555976$, median $0.09855549686555976$, positive count $2$, and negative count $0$.  In the more basic structural-order survival run, the partial structural-order $R^2$ values were $0.03059081709413562$ for \textit{E. coli} and $0.0715885627979685$ for yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving in each organism.  These values justify reporting a finite cross-layer association, but they do not by themselves identify a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned readback.}
The boundary test asks whether the structural-order surface remains connected to $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ after the mandatory translation interface is included in the controlled panel.  The full-control increment is positive in both organisms: the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ has $n=2$, minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, median $0.005284160876443678$, positive count $2$, and negative count $0$.  The weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ is $0.004764742175882462$.  The sign-flip test for a positive full-control effect gives $p=0.0029296875$.  The permuted full-control comparison is negative in both organisms, with minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, maximum $-0.000779938262202795$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, median $-0.00212186632044159$, positive count $0$, and negative count $2$.  The finite verdict is therefore \emph{crosses to structural-order layer}: the rank-powered, controlled panel preserves a small positive translation-conditioned structural-order association.

\paragraph{Relation to low-dimensional compilation.}
The same residual source is not well compressed by the available low-dimensional downstream readout dictionary.  In the cross-organism compilation run, the median compressibility was $0.026$, and the per-organism compressibilities were $0.024368095798914635$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.005717621488177116$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, each with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  The corresponding residual-energy fractions were $0.9756319042010854$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9736442326306781$, and $0.8676586345294415$.  Thus the structural-order boundary should be read as a narrow finite association inside a controlled panel, not as evidence that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual source has been reduced to a simple downstream biological program.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
This chapter allows one statement: in the two powered organism panels, amino-acid-residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates retain a small positive association with a predicted structural-order confidence readout after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, $M$-density, organism-level design, matched mRNA controls where applicable, and the translation interface are accounted for.  It does not assert measured translation realization from codon geometry alone, nor does it assert folding kinetics, protein stability, degradation rate, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact: direct ribosome or translation-efficiency rows for translation realization, an experimental folding or stability assay for protein physical state, and independent functional or phenotype measurements for higher biological interpretation.
