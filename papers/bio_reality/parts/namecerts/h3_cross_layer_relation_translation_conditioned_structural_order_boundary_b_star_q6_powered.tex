\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}  This record concerns a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which each $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate is first residualized against amino-acid quotient structure before any downstream readout is interpreted.  The tested residual block contains nine coordinates, namely $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The structural-order contact used here is not a folded-structure experiment; it is a per-protein AlphaFold mean pLDDT surface on the $0$ to $100$ scale, read only as predicted structure-confidence.  The translation-gated structural-order run joined $3736$ Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined entries, with hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins out of $5815$ codon-joined entries, with hit rate $0.998108$.  In the translation-conditioned boundary run itself, the same layer was represented by $3412$ joined Escherichia coli rows and $5119$ joined Saccharomyces cerevisiae rows, with a minimum joined-row threshold of $300$ and $5$ folds.

\paragraph{Controlled design.}  The statistic is a gated readout, not a direct codon-to-structure assertion.  The covariate block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In Escherichia coli, the control matrix used $24$ columns, had $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and left residual degrees of freedom $3713$; the residualized $Q6$ block retained rank $9$, so the panel was rank-powered for the nine tested coordinates.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the same $24$-column control design had $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, and residualized coordinate rank $9$.  Thus the reported association is evaluated after length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls, with the translation gate kept prior to structural-order interpretation.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row signal.}  Before the translation-conditioning comparison, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ block had positive predictive contact with the order surface in both organisms: across the two organisms the observed $R^2$ summary was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min R^2 &= 0.03664544696481631,\\
\operatorname{median} R^2 &= 0.09855549686555976,\\
\operatorname{mean} R^2 &= 0.09855549686555976,\\
\max R^2 &= 0.1604655467663032,\\
n &= 2,\quad \#\{R^2>0\}=2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The earlier translation-gated structural-order surface gave organism-level structural-order residual associations $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ for Escherichia coli and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, again under amino-acid-composition-aware controls.  These numbers justify treating the structural-order readout as a finite contact surface for this boundary, but only at the level of predicted structure confidence.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary call.}  The decisive statistic is the added full-control contribution of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual block after the translation step has been accounted for.  Across the two joined organisms, the full-control $\Delta R^2$ surface was positive in both cases:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min \Delta R^2 &= 0.002688284523492457,\\
\operatorname{median}\Delta R^2 &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\operatorname{mean}\Delta R^2 &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\max \Delta R^2 &= 0.007880037229394898,\\
\#\{\Delta R^2>0\} &= 2\text{ of }2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The weighted full-control value was $0.004764742175882462$, and the sign-flip probability for a positive full-control effect was $p=0.0029296875$.  The permutation control moved in the opposite direction, with permuted full-control $\Delta R^2$ values summarized by minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, median $-0.00212186632044159$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, and maximum $-0.000779938262202795$, with $2$ of $2$ entries negative.  The finite-row call is therefore that this panel crosses to the predicted structural-order layer under the stated translation-conditioned controls.

\paragraph{Relation to other readouts.}  The structural-order call is not a minimal-compilation claim.  In the broader residual-compressibility test, the measured low-dimensional downstream readout set did not compress $P_Q$ strongly: the reported cross-organism median compressibility was $0.026$, with organism values $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio rerio, $0.005717621488177116$ for Escherichia coli, $0.02635576736932188$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.13234136547055853$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, each with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  The structural-order boundary is also not a protein-stability result.  The separate translation-conditioned stability run joined stability-like readouts in three organisms and returned only a partial verdict: the weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.001572140012981015$, the aggregate mean was $-0.0025553576666936064$, and the sign-flip probability for a positive effect was $0.946441650390625$.  Those stability numbers prevent importing the structural-order call into degradation, turnover, or folding-stability language.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}  The supported statement is narrow: after amino-acid quotient residualization and after the declared covariates, the rank-powered $B^{*}_{Q6}$ block has a positive translation-conditioned association with a predicted structure-confidence surface in two organisms.  This does not establish observed translation dynamics, folding quality, physical admissibility, protein function, degradation mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each such claim: ribosome profiling or another measured translation contact for translation realization, direct stability or turnover assays for protein stability, physical or structural experiments for folding and admissibility, and functional or perturbation assays for function or phenotype.  The present chapter records only the finite-row structural-order boundary that is actually measured by the supplied panel.
