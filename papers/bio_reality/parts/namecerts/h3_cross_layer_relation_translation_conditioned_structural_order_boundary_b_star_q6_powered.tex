\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}

\paragraph{Claim shape.}
The finite object considered here is an organism-by-CDS panel in which each $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate has already been residualized against first amino-acid quotient structure before any biological readout is examined.  The coordinate family contains nine entries,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f_3\text{-stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout is not an experimental folded-structure assay.  It is AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$--$100$ scale, used only as a predicted structural-order confidence surface.  The tested question is whether the residual codon-coordinate block contributes a small but reproducible increment to this structural-order confidence readout after the mandatory lower-layer controls have been placed in the model.  Thus the chapter records a translation-conditioned structural-order boundary, not a claim about folding dynamics, molecular function, phenotype, physical admissibility, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Rows and rank.}
Two organism panels reached the structural-order readout with enough rows for the powered test.  The structural-order boundary experiment joined $3412$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 and $5119$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  In the companion translation-gated structural-order computation, the codon and structural-order joins were nearly complete: \emph{E. coli} had $3739$ codon-joined proteins, $3736$ matched proteins used, $3736$ structural-order proteins, and hit rate $0.999198$; yeast had $5815$ codon-joined proteins, $5804$ matched proteins used, $5804$ structural-order proteins, and hit rate $0.998108$.  The control design used an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, all $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The rank diagnostics are nondegenerate: each organism retained $9$ residualized coordinate columns after controls, with $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $3713$ for \emph{E. coli} and $5781$ for yeast, so the reported positive entries are not an all-ones saturated small-rank artifact.

\paragraph{Structural-order readout.}
Before translation conditioning is summarized, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ association with the predicted structural-order surface is finite in both organisms.  The cross-validated $B^*_{Q6}$-to-order summary has $n=2$, minimum $0.03664544696481631$, maximum $0.1604655467663032$, mean $0.09855549686555976$, and median $0.09855549686555976$, with $2$ positive entries and $0$ negative entries.  In the translation-gated structural-order computation, the per-organism structural-order residual readbacks were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{SO}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.03059081709413562,\\
R^2_{SO}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.0715885627979685.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those values are interpreted only as cross-sectional predicted-confidence associations after the stated sequence and composition controls.  They do not say that synonymous coordinates determine a folded conformation, that the protein is physically admissible, or that any cellular function follows.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned increment.}
The powered boundary test used $5$ folds, required at least $300$ joined rows, and produced status \emph{passed}.  The full-control translation-conditioned increment in $R^2$ is positive in both organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &\in [0.002688284523492457,\ 0.007880037229394898],\\
\operatorname{mean}(\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}}) &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\operatorname{median}(\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}}) &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{weighted}} &= 0.004764742175882462.
\end{aligned}
$$
The organism call count is therefore $2$ full-control-positive calls out of $2$ tested organisms.  A sign-flip test for positive full-control increment gives $p=0.0029296875$.  The permutation comparison moves in the opposite direction, with permuted full-control increments ranging from $-0.003463794378680385$ to $-0.000779938262202795$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, median $-0.00212186632044159$, $0$ positive entries, and $2$ negative entries.  On these finite rows the module classification is \emph{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer}: the coordinate block reaches the predicted structural-order confidence surface after the translation-layer gate and controls are imposed.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring readouts.}
The structural-order result should be read beside, not collapsed into, the protein-stability boundary.  The stability-side experiment had $n=3$ direct $B^*_{Q6}$-to-stability entries with positive direct summaries between $0.018837736273789707$ and $0.03602995605625481$, but its translation-conditioned stability verdict was only \emph{partial}; the aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ had mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$.  This contrast matters: the structural-order boundary is supported on its own predicted-confidence readout, whereas stability, degradation, turnover, folding quality, and mechanism remain separate questions requiring their own matched contacts and attenuation tests.

\paragraph{Compression context.}
A broader low-dimensional readout-compression check also prevents overstatement.  Across four organisms, the minimal-compilation calculation did not support strong compression of $P_Q$ by the measured low-dimensional readout set.  The reported compressibilities were $0.024368095798914635$ for zebrafish, $0.005717621488177116$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, with median $0.026$.  The corresponding retained-readout dimensions were $DL_{D_o}=1,2,3,2$ against raw candidate dimensions $17,2,19,19$.  This says that the structural-order boundary is a localized finite-row signal, not a general reduction of the whole $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source to a compact biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  In the two powered organism panels, the amino-acid-residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ block has a positive, controlled, translation-conditioned association with AlphaFold mean pLDDT predicted structural-order confidence, with weighted full-control increment $0.004764742175882462$ and sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.  No claim follows here about observed translation unless a measured translation contact supplies the same-row translation readout; no claim follows about folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, degradation, localization, phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universality unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the corresponding controlled statistic.  Modeled adaptation weights, codon tables, workflow eligibility, and the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry are insufficient by themselves to promote the statement beyond this finite structural-order-confidence boundary.
