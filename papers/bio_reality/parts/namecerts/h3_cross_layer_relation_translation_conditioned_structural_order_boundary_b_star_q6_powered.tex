\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are first residualized against amino-acid quotient structure and then tested against a translation readout before any structural-order readout is allowed to enter the interpretation.  The encoded coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The structural-order surface used here is AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale, interpreted only as predicted structural-confidence score.  It is not treated as a folding assay, a physical-admissibility assay, a function assay, or a phenotype assay.

\paragraph{Control design.}
For each joined CDS row the residual-coordinate block is tested after the finite control set consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control matrix has $24$ columns, with rank $23$ in both evaluated organisms.  The residualized coordinate matrix keeps rank $9$ in both organisms, so the tested $Q$ block is not collapsed by the controls.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $3713$, with explained energy $4178.935925251874$ and response residual energy $136607.52873622958$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} the corresponding residual degrees of freedom are $5781$, with explained energy $44421.14342043593$ and response residual energy $620506.1490869389$.

\paragraph{Joined rows and readout coverage.}
The translation-gated structural-order computation joined $3739$ codon rows for \emph{E. coli}, used $3736$ matched proteins, and reported $3736$ structural-order proteins, giving a structural-order join hit rate of $0.999198$.  For yeast it joined $5815$ codon rows, used $5804$ matched proteins, and reported $5804$ structural-order proteins, giving a hit rate of $0.998108$.  The later translation-conditioned boundary run used $3412$ joined \emph{E. coli} rows and $5119$ joined yeast rows, with a minimum joined-row requirement of $300$ and $5$ folds.  Thus the positive boundary call is not an identifier-only event; it is attached to two finite organism-specific matrices with thousands of matched rows.

\paragraph{Boundary interpretation.}
The correct reading is a translation-conditioned crossing into a predicted structural-order readout, not a direct codon-topology mechanism.  A $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate may be discussed at this boundary only after the matched translation readout survives its own controls, and the downstream structural-confidence association is then reported as a residual association conditioned by that translation layer.  The local result is therefore a rank-powered, controlled, finite-row association of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow S_k,\\
 S_k &= \text{predicted structural-confidence readout},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is one of the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, $T_j$ is the matched translation readout, and $S_k$ is the AlphaFold mean-pLDDT surface.  The reported verdict is $\mathrm{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer}$ for experiment run $1b97387835f7$ with seed $\mathrm{sha256{:}43d63f9983efeb9bb7ebddb12e57570dc862f180b10174a4e85a0e270a65cef6}$.

\paragraph{Relation to nearby finite checks.}
The adjacent protein-stability boundary does not show the same clean positive pattern.  Its translation-conditioned stability run is marked partial, with weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=-0.001572140012981015$, mean full-control $\Delta R^2=-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, and sign-flip probability $0.946441650390625$.  This contrast matters: the structural-order boundary is not being generalized into all post-translational surfaces.  Likewise, the minimal-compilation test reports that the measured low-dimensional readout set does not compress $P_Q$ strongly across organisms, with median compressibility $0.026$ and organism-level compressibilities $0.024368095798914635$ for zebrafish, $0.005717621488177116$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast.  The structural-order call is therefore a specific finite-row crossing, not a claim that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source has been globally reduced to a small biological readout dictionary.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation mechanism follows from the structural-order statistic by itself.  No protein folding pathway, folding quality, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, degradation law, phenotype, adaptive law, or universal biological rule follows from this result.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights are not survival evidence for the structural-order surface, and AlphaFold mean pLDDT is not an experimental structure measurement.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched contact: direct ribosome or translation-efficiency rows for translation realization, direct stability or degradation rows for protein-stability interpretation, direct structure or biophysical measurements for folding or physical admissibility, and functional or phenotype assays for function-level or organism-level interpretation.  Within those limits, the present finite panel supports a controlled, translation-conditioned association between the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and predicted structural-confidence readout in the two evaluated organisms.
