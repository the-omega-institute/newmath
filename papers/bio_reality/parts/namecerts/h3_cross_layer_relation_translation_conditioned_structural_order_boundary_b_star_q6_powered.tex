\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:biorreality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which each $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate is used only after amino-acid-quotient residualization.  The tested coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The structural-order surface is not an experimental folding assay; it is the AlphaFold mean pLDDT score on the $0$ to $100$ scale, used here only as predicted structural-confidence readout.  The row joins are organism-specific: Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 contributes $3412$ joined structural-order rows in the translation-conditioned experiment, while Saccharomyces cerevisiae contributes $5119$ joined rows.  A companion structural-order loading pass reported $3736$ matched E. coli proteins from $3739$ codon-joined rows, with hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched yeast proteins from $5815$ codon-joined rows, with hit rate $0.998108$.

\paragraph{Control geometry.}
The statistic is evaluated after controls for coding-sequence length, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the structural-order survival pass, both organisms had $24$ control columns, $23$ control-rank columns, $9$ residual coordinates, and rank $9$ after residualization.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls were $3713$ for E. coli and $5781$ for yeast.  The controlled explained energies were $4178.935925251874$ and $44421.14342043593$, respectively, against residual readout energies $136607.52873622958$ and $620506.1490869389$.

\paragraph{Observed structural-order readout.}
Before translation conditioning is interpreted, the residual coordinates show a finite association with the structural-confidence surface in both organisms.  The direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$-to-order summary has $n=2$, minimum $0.03664544696481631$, maximum $0.1604655467663032$, mean $0.09855549686555976$, and median $0.09855549686555976$, with two positive organism entries and no negative entries.  The earlier gated structural-order computation gave $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ in E. coli and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ in yeast, with all $9$ coordinates surviving in each organism.  These values justify treating the panel as a finite structural-confidence boundary contact, not as a statement about native structure, folding kinetics, or protein function.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned increment.}
The decisive quantity is the incremental readout remaining after the matched translation layer and the stated controls are present.  In the five-fold experiment with minimum joined-row threshold $300$, the full-control increment is positive in both organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &\in [0.002688284523492457,\;0.007880037229394898],\\
\operatorname{mean}(\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}}) &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\operatorname{median}(\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}}) &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{weighted}} &= 0.004764742175882462.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign test for positive full-control increment gives $p=0.0029296875$.  The permutation comparator is negative in both organisms, with range $[-0.003463794378680385,-0.000779938262202795]$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, and median $-0.00212186632044159$.  The module-level call is therefore $\text{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer}$: the finite rows support a translation-conditioned structural-confidence association for this readout surface.

\paragraph{Relation to nearby downstream panels.}
The structural-confidence result should not be conflated with protein-stability evidence.  In a separate stability-boundary panel over three organisms, the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$-to-stability summary was positive with mean $0.02753157569086806$ and median $0.027727034742559664$, but the translation-conditioned full-control increment had mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$; its verdict was only partial.  The minimal-compilation companion also cautions against over-compression: across Danio rerio, E. coli, Homo sapiens, and yeast, the residual source was not highly compressed by the measured low-dimensional readouts, with median compressibility $0.026$ and organism values $0.024368095798914635$, $0.005717621488177116$, $0.02635576736932188$, and $0.13234136547055853$.  Thus the structural-confidence boundary is a local finite-row result, not a universal reduction of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ source.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
This chapter permits only the following claim: in the stated finite organisms, after amino-acid composition, length, $GC3$, $M$-density, and matched translation controls, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain a small positive association with an AlphaFold mean-pLDDT structural-confidence readout.  It does not license a claim of measured translation output unless the translation readout itself is separately present and controlled.  It does not license claims about folding mechanism, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, degradation, localization, or a general biological law.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact, joined rows, controls, and a separately reported statistic.
