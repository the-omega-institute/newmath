\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates in $B^*_{Q6}$ can reach a structural-order readout only after passing through a controlled translation-readout surface. The finite object is an organism-by-coding-sequence panel: every row carries the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate values, coding-sequence controls, a translation readout where available, matched transcript controls, and a structural-order readout. The structural-order surface used here is predicted structural confidence rather than a folded-state experiment. It is therefore a downstream readout boundary, not a claim about folding chemistry, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control surface.}
The powered $B^*_{Q6}$ panel contains nine residual coordinates:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. Each coordinate is evaluated after amino-acid composition has been included in the control surface, so the reported residual association is not an amino-acid-composition statement. The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from the observed coding sequences, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected coordinates. In the structural-order readout panel, the rank checks were nondegenerate: for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, the control rank was $23$, the residualized coordinate rank was $9$, and the residual degrees of freedom after controls were $3713$; for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the corresponding values were $23$, $9$, and $5781$.

\paragraph{Finite rows and primary statistic.}
The translation-conditioned structural-order experiment used five folds, required at least $300$ joined rows, and was run with seed $\mathrm{sha256:43d63f9983efeb9bb7ebddb12e57570dc862f180b10174a4e85a0e270a65cef6}$. The joined structural-order panels contained $3412$ Escherichia coli rows and $5119$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae rows. The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ structural-order predictive surface was positive in both organisms, with summary values
$$\begin{aligned}
\min &= 0.03664544696481631,\\
\mathrm{median} &= 0.09855549686555976,\\
\mathrm{mean} &= 0.09855549686555976,\\
\max &= 0.1604655467663032,\\
n &= 2.
\end{aligned}$$
The translation-conditioned full-control increment was also positive in both organisms:
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full},\min} &= 0.002688284523492457,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full},\mathrm{median}} &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full},\mathrm{mean}} &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full},\max} &= 0.007880037229394898,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{weighted}} &= 0.004764742175882462.
\end{aligned}$$
The sign-flip test for a positive full-control increment gave $p=0.0029296875$. Under permutation, the corresponding full-control increments were negative in both organisms, with mean $-0.00212186632044159$, median $-0.00212186632044159$, minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, and maximum $-0.000779938262202795$. The finite classification is therefore $\mathrm{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer}$ for this readout surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-gated structural-order panel.}
The linked translation-gated structural-order run gives the row-level context for the same coordinate family. In Escherichia coli, $3739$ coding-sequence rows were joined to codon data, $3736$ matched proteins were used, and the structural-order join hit rate was $0.999198$; the structural-order partial surface had $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ with all nine coordinates surviving. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $5815$ coding-sequence rows were joined, $5804$ matched proteins were used, the hit rate was $0.998108$, and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ with all nine coordinates surviving. These row counts make the result a finite matched-panel readout rather than an identifier-level or name-level assertion. The protein-level readout is still AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale, interpreted here only as predicted structure-confidence order.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The result supports a narrow statement: in the available finite panels, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain a positive translation-conditioned association with the predicted structural-order readout after the declared coding-sequence controls. The evidence does not say that codon topology causes folding, that translation dynamics have been mechanistically measured, or that a protein-stability, degradation, localization, function, phenotype, or physical-admissibility layer has been reached. A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each such promotion. The minimal-compilation comparison also cautions against over-reading the result: across computed organisms, the measured low-dimensional readout set did not highly compress $P_Q$, with median compressibility $0.026$ and organism-level compressibilities $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio rerio, $0.005717621488177116$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.02635576736932188$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.13234136547055853$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Thus the structural-order boundary is a positive finite readout crossing, not a reduction of the residual coordinate source to a simple downstream explanatory mechanism.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation-realization claim follows from this chapter unless an organism-matched translation contact supplies the measured or explicitly bounded translation statistic used in the conditioning step. No folding, protein-stability, degradation, localization, physical-admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, or universal biological conclusion follows from the reported $\Delta R^2$ values. The only accepted readback is the controlled finite-panel association $q_i \to T_j \to$ predicted structural-order confidence, with amino-acid composition, organism scope, $GC3$, length, $M$-density, and matched transcript controls kept in the conditioning surface.
