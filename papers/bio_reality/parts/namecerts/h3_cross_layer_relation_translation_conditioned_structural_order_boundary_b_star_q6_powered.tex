\subsection{Translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{subsec:biorality-q6-translation-conditioned-structural-order-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel and admissible question.}
This record concerns a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which each $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate is read only after amino-acid quotient residualization and after the ordinary sequence covariates have been placed in the control design.  The tested question is not whether synonymous geometry directly explains protein structure.  The tested question is narrower: after a matched translation readout has been used as the required intermediate surface, does the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ block retain a finite controlled association with a structural-order readout on the same coding entries?  The admissible carrier is therefore a row table of the form
$$\begin{aligned}
(q_i, T_j, O, Z) ,
\end{aligned}$$
where $q_i$ ranges over the nine residual coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$; $T_j$ is the translation-readout gate; $O$ is the structural-order surface; and $Z$ contains the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $q$ vectors.  The finite row rule is important: a downstream structural-order statistic is interpretable only after the same residual block is rank-powered under the translation and composition controls.

\paragraph{Rank and row sufficiency.}
The powered design was nondegenerate in both computed organisms.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 the controlled design used $24$ control columns with $20$ amino-acid-composition controls, had $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, retained $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ for the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, and left $3713$ residual degrees of freedom after $Z$.  The explained energy was $4178.935925251874$ against residual readout energy $136607.52873622958$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} the same coordinate count and control count were used, with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, and $5781$ residual degrees of freedom after $Z$; the explained energy was $44421.14342043593$ against residual readout energy $620506.1490869389$.  Thus the result is not an all-fit rank artifact: in both organisms the residual coordinate block keeps full nine-dimensional rank after the declared controls.

\paragraph{Structural-order contact and survival surface.}
The structural-order readout used here is AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale, interpreted only as predicted structural-confidence order.  It is not an experimental folding assay, not a functional assay, and not a phenotype.  The joined structural-order rows were large and nearly complete relative to the available codon/protein mapping: \emph{E. coli} had $3739$ codon-joined proteins, $3736$ matched proteins used, $3736$ structural-order proteins, and hit rate $0.999198$; yeast had $5815$ codon-joined proteins, $5804$ matched proteins used, $5804$ structural-order proteins, and hit rate $0.998108$.  On this translation-gated structural-order surface the finite association before the final conditioning step was positive in both organisms, with $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ for \emph{E. coli} and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ for yeast.  Each organism retained all nine surviving coordinates on that bounded structural-confidence readout.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary statistic.}
The powered structural-order boundary run used five folds, required at least $300$ joined rows, and passed with joined structural-order row counts $3412$ for \emph{E. coli} and $5119$ for yeast.  The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ prediction surface for order was positive across the two organisms: minimum $0.03664544696481631$, maximum $0.1604655467663032$, mean $0.09855549686555976$, median $0.09855549686555976$, and $2$ positive entries out of $2$.  After the full control and translation-conditioned comparison, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ remained positive in both organisms, with
$$\begin{aligned}
\min \Delta R^2 &= 0.002688284523492457,\\
\max \Delta R^2 &= 0.007880037229394898,\\
\operatorname{mean}(\Delta R^2) &= 0.005284160876443678,\\
\operatorname{median}(\Delta R^2) &= 0.005284160876443678.
\end{aligned}$$
The weighted full-control value was $0.004764742175882462$.  A sign-flip test for positive full-control $\Delta R^2$ gave $p=0.0029296875$.  The permutation comparison went the other way: the permuted full-control $\Delta R^2$ values were both negative, with minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, maximum $-0.000779938262202795$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, and median $-0.00212186632044159$.  The finite call is therefore $\mathrm{crosses\_to\_structural\_order\_layer}$ in the restricted sense that the translation-conditioned structural-confidence surface remains positive after the declared controls.

\paragraph{Relation to protein stability and residual compilation.}
The structural-order result should not be promoted into a general protein-state law.  A nearby protein-stability boundary run did not give the same strength of support: three organisms had positive direct $B^*_{Q6}$ stability-prediction values with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, maximum $0.03602995605625481$, and mean $0.02753157569086806$, but the translation-conditioned stability verdict was only partial.  Its full-control aggregate $\Delta R^2$ had mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$.  The available stability rows were also readout-dependent: \emph{E. coli} used $2517$ mRNA-half-life rows as the primary stability-side readout, human used $6697$ protein-turnover rows together with $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows, and yeast used $3537$ protein-turnover rows together with $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows.  Separately, the minimal-compilation calculation reported that the measured low-dimensional downstream dictionary did not compress $P_Q$ strongly across organisms: compressibility was $0.024368095798914635$ in zebrafish, $0.005717621488177116$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ in human, and $0.13234136547055853$ in yeast, with cross-organism median $0.026$.  Thus the structural-confidence crossing is a finite boundary result, not evidence that the residual source has been globally reduced to measured downstream readouts.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The supported statement is only this: in the computed \emph{E. coli} and yeast panels, the amino-acid-residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ block has a positive translation-conditioned association with a predicted structural-order confidence surface after length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, $M$-density, organism-scoped row matching, and baseline controls.  No claim of translation mechanism, ribosome causality, folding pathway, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, degradation mechanism, localization mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this table.  A stronger interpretation would require a separate layer-matched contact: direct translation measurements for translation mechanism, physical or experimental structure measurements for folding and admissibility, stability or degradation measurements for protein lifetime, localization measurements for trafficking, and perturbation or functional assays for mechanism and phenotype.  The present chapter records a positive finite crossing to a structural-confidence boundary and keeps every higher biological interpretation outside the claim.
