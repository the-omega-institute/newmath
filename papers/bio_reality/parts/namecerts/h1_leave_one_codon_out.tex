\subsection{Leave-one-codon recovery on a local window-six tile}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite display.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table only at the code-read layer.  Codons are placed in a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with the external table used only to attach amino-acid or stop labels to the finite codon vertices.  The local phenomenon is a boundary-tile split: a finite set $R$ of label-sensitive codons forms a punctured cubical pattern, while the leave-one-codon probe asks whether omission of one codon can be recovered from the coordinate closure and the observed label boundary.  The verified distinguished set has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports that this set has cubical face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the tile is not being inferred from vague biochemical analogy: it is a concrete subcomplex of the six-bit codon display.  The finite contact is the curated standard-code label table; it says which codons read as amino-acid or stop labels and does not say how translation is physically realized.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The coordinate construction has a recorded spectral profile.  The relevant numerical rows give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with cubic residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also recorded as the radial-polynomial residual.  The empirical shape test used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the low exact-match count should be read against an explicit finite sampling burden rather than as an asymptotic law.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class while randomizing the relevant finite arrangement.  In $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$, it found no exact match to the observed shape.  The observed size was $|M|=20$, the lower-tail count at or below that size was $7$, and the reported lower-tail probability was $0.0035$.  Thus the signal is not merely that the standard table contains amino-acid labels; the finite arrangement remains atypical after the label-class constraint is imposed.  The row does not supply the full member list of $M$, so the chapter records the scalar size and the tested probabilities without inventing an unobserved codon list.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon result.}
For the codon-level omission test, the finite row reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, and $0$ anchor cases.  The high-coverage closure count is $8$, and the recovery rate is exactly $1.0$, with the above-threshold recovery indicator also equal to $1.0$.  In the intended reading, a codon omitted from the tile is either recovered by the closure pattern or lies on the recorded boundary rather than requiring an independent anchor exception.  This is the strongest local result in the packet: every tested omission is accounted for inside the finite code-read geometry, but only as a local codon-label recovery statement.

\paragraph{Code-table comparison.}
The companion leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, five code families were recorded.  The above-baseline family hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  These rows support the same local reading: the codon-window coordinate pattern is robust as a code-label probe, while remaining tied to the curated table as the finite source of labels.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module-classification row assigns all $13$ members of $R$ and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite check says that every module has a predictable-or-boundary witness.  This matters because the recovery row is not only a count of codons; it is compatible with a complete module partition of the distinguished codon set.

\paragraph{Scope of the biological claim.}
The biological content is deliberately narrow.  The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label readback, so it can test whether a local split in $Q_6$ agrees with observed code labels.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence is not a protein fold, a CUN or UUR codon family notation is not a structural experiment, and a local face pattern in $Q_6$ is not a global biological law.  Any claim crossing from code labels into translation dynamics, molecular structure, admissible physics, organismal function, or universal mechanism would need a separate empirical contact designed for that layer.  Without such a contact, the verified conclusion is only this: the standard-code labels expose a finite local boundary pattern in the window-six codon cube, and the leave-one-codon omission test recovers every omitted case by predictable closure or by the recorded boundary.
