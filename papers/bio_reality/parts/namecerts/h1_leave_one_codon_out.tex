\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out boundary recovery in the standard code}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-read contact: each of the sixty-four codon vertices is read only as an amino-acid or stop label supplied by the curated table. The six-bit codon cube supplies the coordinate carrier, and the local question is whether a withheld codon label lies in a recoverable neighborhood of the observed boundary-tile structure. The claim is therefore deliberately narrow. It concerns codon-label recovery inside a window-six coordinate display; it does not assert translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law. Those claims require separate observations at the corresponding biological layer.

\paragraph{Exceptional codon region.}
The finite exceptional region has cardinality thirteen. The recorded set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that this region is the punctured-cube object used by the local analysis, with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the boundary computation is not attached to an arbitrary list of codons: it is tied to a finite cubical subshape with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-faces. The supplied data do not give an explicit vertex list for the complementary set $M$, but they do record $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null comparison, so the chapter uses the scalar $M$ data without inventing a missing codon list.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The associated spectral row gives three numerical coordinates for the shape: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic check is numerically zero at the recorded precision, with value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$. In this chapter that number is used only as a finite consistency check for the coordinate computation. It is not a biological force law and is not promoted into a statement about translation or selection.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
For the codon-level leave-one-out run, the measured recovery rate is $1.0$. The run reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor cases, and $8$ high-closure-coverage cases. In compact form, the observed finite row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{predictable}&=8, & \mathrm{boundary}&=5, & \mathrm{anchor}&=0,\\
\mathrm{high\ closure\ coverage}&=8, & \mathrm{recovery\ rate}&=1.0. &
\end{aligned}
$$
The absence of anchor cases is important: the row is not saying that a single privileged codon determines the standard table. It says that, after one codon is withheld from the finite code-read contact, the local tile and closure information classify the withheld case as predictable or boundary-covered in the observed run.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification covers all thirteen codons of $R$ with no residue. The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}, & \mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}, & \mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}, & \mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded completeness check has $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. Each module has either a predictable member or a boundary member. This is the local combinatorial content of the claim: the leave-one-codon-out behavior aligns with a complete module partition of the thirteen-codon exceptional region.

\paragraph{Table-level comparison.}
A related leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level breakdown records five families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, and bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline. These numbers place the codon-level recovery row inside a broader table-read pattern, but they remain code-label statistics rather than observations of protein structure or organismal function.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The shape-significance null run used $N=2000$ distinct samples, with $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found one exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ high-Jaccard matches with threshold $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$. The amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also at $N=2000$, found zero exact matches, lower-tail count $7$, observed $|M|=20$, exact-shape probability $0.0$, and lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These finite comparisons support the claim that the observed local tile is non-generic relative to the sampled nulls. They do not identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Reality contact and blocked promotions.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, and its resolution is codon-label readback. That contact is enough to test whether the coordinate calculation agrees with amino-acid or stop labels. It is not a translation assay, not a folding experiment, not a thermodynamic admissibility test, not a function assay, and not a universality proof. Consequently the valid conclusion is that the window-six codon coordinates expose a finite local boundary pattern for standard-code labels, with full recovery in the supplied leave-one-codon-out row. Any statement about translation realization, folded protein structure, physical feasibility, biological role, mechanism, or global necessity must wait for an independent contact at that layer.
