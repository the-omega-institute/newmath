\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Codon Boundary Packet}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This packet studies a window-six representation of the standard-code codon table.  The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube obtained from the three codon positions after each nucleotide is resolved into a binary display.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: the observed table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and the packet asks whether a local codon tile can recover held-out codon membership without treating that tile as a biological mechanism.

The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its observed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite geometry is not merely a count: it records thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the local tile extracted from the codon cube.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The associated finite operator row reports spectral scalars
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the finite computation treats the displayed spectrum as numerically closed to tolerance.  A separate shape experiment with $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts found exactly one exact shape match after $55377$ duplicate retries.  The reported exact-shape probability is $p=0.0005$, while the high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ produced $25$ matches and $p=0.0125$.  These numbers support the claim that the observed local shape is sparse under the sampled null, but they do not identify any biochemical cause.

The amino-acid-class-preserving null row is even more restrictive for exact shape: with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact match count is $0$, the observed $M$-size is $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$.  The corresponding values are $p=0.0$ for exact shape and $p=0.0035$ for the lower tail.  The packet therefore keeps two finite signals separate: a codon-cube tile signal and a label-class-constrained null signal.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
The codon-level holdout row is the primary claim here.  It reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison also records recovery-rate-above-threshold $1.0$.  In words, every codon tested by this row is recovered either through the high-coverage closure side or through the boundary side of the local tile; none is recovered by an anchor count.

This is a code-read result.  The computation supports a finite local prediction rule over codon labels, not a claim that the codon table is generated by the six-bit geometry.  The relevant comparison at the whole-code level evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The same row records hit rate $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M\setminus T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary.  These figures make the local boundary useful for readback, while preserving the difference between finite recovery and biological realization.

\paragraph{Module-level accounting.}
The module classification row assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ with no extras and no omissions.  The six named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness counters are $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, extra-module codons $0$, missing-from-modules $0$, and unassigned codons $0$.  Each module has at least one predictable or boundary codon, so the packet is not a single exceptional codon event; it covers the entire listed residual set by module.

The family-level table supplies a second finite check.  Across $5$ families, the hit-rate-above-baseline values are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  The nonunit value is important: it prevents the result from being stated as a universal perfect law across all family groupings, even though the codon-level row for this packet records perfect recovery under its own finite test.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table as a codon-to-label readback source.  It supplies label assignments for codons; it does not observe translation dynamics, folded protein structure, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal function, or a universal generative mechanism.  Therefore the local tile may be used to test codon-label splits and leave-one-codon-out recovery at the code-read layer, but no statement about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this packet unless an independent contact of that kind is added.

The operative conclusion is consequently finite and local.  The window-six codon display isolates a thirteen-codon residual tile with punctured-cube combinatorics, nontrivial spectral values, significant sampled shape behavior, complete module accounting, and perfect recovery in the stated leave-one-codon-out row.  The biological meaning remains bounded by the curated label table: the packet reads codon labels back against a finite table and marks where higher-layer claims must stop.
