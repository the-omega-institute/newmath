\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Boundary Recovery}
\label{subsec:leave-one-codon-out-boundary-recovery}

\paragraph{Finite codon frame.}
The record concerns the standard genetic-code table only at the label-readout level.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube, with each codon represented by a six-bit display of its three nucleotide positions.  The observed exceptional region $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the local object being tested is not an unlimited biological principle; it is a finite geometric split inside the codon cube, read against a curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.

Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$, the exact-shape count is $0$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, and the lower-tail count is $7$, yielding $p_{\mathrm{lower}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Codon-withheld test.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  In this finite test, every withheld codon that falls under the reported predictable-or-boundary accounting is recovered by the local closure-and-boundary classifier.  The absence of anchors is important: the recovery is not being certified by fixed singleton anchors inside the row, but by the way the local codon neighborhood and its boundary class constrain the missing label.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete for all $13$ codons in $R$: assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.  The six reported modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\

Each module has at least one predictable-or-boundary representative, which is the finite combinatorial reason the row can support a leave-one-codon-out recovery statement.  The module names are used here as finite labels for codon-table neighborhoods; they do not by themselves supply organismal usage, tRNA-copy number, translational dynamics, folded structure, or selective function.

\paragraph{Reality contact and limit of the claim.}
The only admitted contact is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact can test code-level label readout and local codon-tile splits.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the result should be read as follows: the window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table, with strong finite recovery numbers under the supplied probes.  No claim about protein folding, biochemical feasibility, organism-wide law, or biological function follows unless an additional observation of that higher layer is supplied separately.
