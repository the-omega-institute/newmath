\subsection{Leave-one-codon recovery on the window-six codon cube}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Object and contact.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read through a codon display in which local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes can be inspected as finite coordinates. The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. It is therefore a label-readback contact: it can test whether a codon-coordinate pattern agrees with a table of code labels, and it cannot by itself observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Residual row.}
The residual codon row $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified finite run, the row is exactly
$R=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
The same row is identified as a punctured-cube shape with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. This gives the leave-one-codon test a sharply finite domain: every claimed recovery or boundary assignment is an assertion about these thirteen listed codons, not about the entire molecular biology of coding. The module classification is complete on this row: $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The module audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The coordinate evidence is not merely a list of labels. The spectral audit gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, with the cubic radial check evaluating to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$ and the same value reported for the radial polynomial residual. The Monte Carlo shape audit used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$, $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$ and $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. In the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$. These numbers support the claim that the row and its surrounding coordinate pattern are non-generic under the tested finite null models.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon result.}
The direct leave-one-codon audit reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, and high closure coverage on $8$ codons. The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison also reports $1.0$. Thus every codon tested in the relevant recoverable part of the row is either recovered by the local coordinate rule or sits on the explicitly counted boundary. At the code-table level, this is a strong local result: the window-six geometry does expose a boundary-tile structure in the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Relation to leave-one-code tests.}
The broader leave-one-code audit evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate after adding the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level split covered $5$ families: alternative yeast had rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each had rate $1.0$. These supporting rows are not a replacement for the codon-level audit, but they show that the codon-level boundary behavior is consistent with the larger code-read pattern.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The result is a finite code-read statement. It says that, for the listed residual row in the six-bit codon cube, the curated standard-code labels align with a local tile-and-boundary decomposition whose finite audits give full recovery on the counted recoverable cases. It does not say that DNA is forced to obey this geometry as a biological necessity. It also does not infer a translation mechanism, a protein fold, structural order, physical admissibility, function, or a universal law from the codon-label table. Any such stronger claim would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer.
