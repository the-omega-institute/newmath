\subsection{Leave-one-codon local recovery in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} concerns a finite code-layer test on the standard-code codon table. The coordinate carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, used only as a finite display for codon positions and local adjacency. The biological contact is the curated standard genetic-code table, which assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The intended reading is therefore narrow: a local window-six tile may expose boundary behavior in the label table, but the labels themselves remain empirical contacts supplied by the curated table.

The distinguished recovery set has cardinality $13$. In the recorded run it is
$R=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
The same set is reported as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector data $(13,16,4)$. Thus the finite object being tested is not an arbitrary prose category: it is a thirteen-codon subset with a recorded combinatorial shape in the codon cube.

\paragraph{Finite-row recovery test.}
The leave-one-codon run \texttt{e9e1a6e41633} reports no anchor codons, five boundary codons, and eight predictable codons. The high-coverage closure count is also $8$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$, meeting the recorded threshold exactly as a complete recovery over the tested rows. The numerical summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R|&=13,\\
\text{anchor count}&=0,\\
\text{boundary count}&=5,\\
\text{predictable count}&=8,\\
\text{high-coverage closure count}&=8,\\
\text{recovery rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This supports the local statement that every held-out codon in the tested recovery set is accounted for either by the predictable part of the window-six closure structure or by the boundary part of the tile. It does not identify a causal translation process; it is a finite classification result inside the code-label table.

The accounting check is exact: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra, missing, and unassigned codons are empty. This is the main finite reason the claim is stated as ready: the leave-one-codon phenomenon is not merely a scalar recovery score, but also has a complete module partition over the recorded recovery set.

\paragraph{Shape and spectral checks.}
The shape-significance run \texttt{0b267c88f8e4} used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. There was one exact shape match, yielding $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, yielding $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The amino-acid-class-preserving null run \texttt{e8d5de747a0c}, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape $p$-value $0.0$, and lower-tail $p$-value $0.0035$.

These numbers do not by themselves create a biological mechanism; they document that the finite codon subset and its surrounding $M$-geometry have reproducible numerical structure under the stated probes.

\paragraph{Reality contact and negative boundary.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table. It can test code-layer readback and local codon-tile label splitting. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law. Consequently, the claim is deliberately confined to the code-read layer. A codon-to-label assignment may be read from the curated table, and the window-six tile may be checked against that assignment, but no statement about protein folding, functional realization, organismal mechanism, or physical admissibility follows from this chapter without a separate empirical contact designed for that higher layer.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The finite record supports the following chapter-level statement. In the standard-code codon table, the thirteen-codon set $R$ forms a recorded punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$, admits a complete six-module classification, and has leave-one-codon recovery rate $1.0$ with eight predictable and five boundary cases. The broader code-table comparison gives a $0.9841269841269841$ hit rate above a $0.19821739130434785$ baseline over $23$ tables and $63$ targets, while the shape and class-preserving null probes give small finite-sample tail values. This is enough for a ready code-layer local-boundary claim, and not enough for any claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
