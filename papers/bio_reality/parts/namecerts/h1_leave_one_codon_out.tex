\paragraph{Question.}
The record tests whether a six-coordinate codon display can recover the locally exposed codon-label boundary after one codon is hidden.  The biological input is deliberately narrow: a curated standard genetic-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments, and the finite construction asks only for code-layer readback.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  No assertion is made here about translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The contact is the curated standard-code table.  It supplies the observed fact that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels, at codon-label resolution.  This contact can test code readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or function realization.  The table is therefore used as a label bridge, not as a mechanism, not as a universality proof, and not as evidence for folded protein structure.
