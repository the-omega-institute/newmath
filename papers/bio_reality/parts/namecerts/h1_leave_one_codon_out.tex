\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Boundary Tile}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-boundary-tile}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} concerns a code-read phenomenon on the window-six codon cube $Q_6$.  The carrier is the finite codon display obtained from two-bit nucleotide coordinates over three codon positions.  Its distinguished data are a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The readback is not a biochemical process; it is the comparison between this finite coordinate object and the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The intended layer is therefore code readback only.

\paragraph{Finite residual set.}
The distinguished residual set has cardinality $13$.  In the verified row, the set is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this set is a punctured cube with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the codon subset is not being introduced as a vague enrichment of the genetic code.  It is a finite coordinate object with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the recorded local geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral measurements.}
The spectral row gives separate numerical readings for the label-compatible main component and the residual component.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, matching the independent cubic-polynomial residual at the same tolerance.  These numbers only certify that the finite coordinate construction has a stable numerical profile under the stated computation.  They do not by themselves identify a translation mechanism, molecular structure, or biological function.

\paragraph{Shape controls.}
The global shape control used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second amino-acid-class-preserving null model also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It produced $0$ exact matches, with observed $M$-size $20$ and lower-tail count $7$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control rows therefore support the claim that the observed coordinate pattern is rare under these two finite randomization regimes.  They do not convert rarity into necessity or into a universal property of biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one readback.}
At the code-table level, the leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row then records the sharper local boundary behavior:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{anchor count}&=0,\\
\text{boundary count}&=5,\\
\text{closure coverage high}&=8,\\
\text{predictable count}&=8,\\
\text{recovery rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recovery rate above threshold is also $1.0$.  In this chapter, the word predictable means predictable inside the finite code-read protocol after one codon is withheld; it does not mean that a ribosome, protein fold, physical environment, or organismal phenotype has been reconstructed.

\paragraph{Family and module accounting.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row records $5$ families.  The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  The module classification row assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ and reports no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Its modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has either a predictable member or a boundary member in the finite protocol.  This is the strongest local conclusion supplied by the row data: the residual set is completely accounted for by the displayed modules, and the withheld-codon recovery behavior remains inside that module boundary.

\paragraph{Contact and scope.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  It can test codon-label readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label sequences remain labels at the code layer.  A separate empirical contact would be required before any claim about folding, molecular admissibility, function, cross-organism mechanism, or universality could be made.  The present chapter therefore records a finite code-read alignment between a window-six coordinate tile and the standard-code label table, together with explicit limits on what the alignment is allowed to mean.
