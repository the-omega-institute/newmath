\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out recovery on the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table after each codon is displayed as a six-coordinate binary window. The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, with readout supplied by the curated standard genetic-code table. The tested layer is therefore only the code-label layer: a codon is compared with its amino-acid or stop label, and local cube structure is asked to recover or bound the missing label position. The row is not a claim about translation chemistry, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Boundary subset and local cell counts.}
The finite distinguished subset $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube computation gives the same list for $R$ and has face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object tested by the leave-one-codon-out row is not an unstructured collection of labels. It is a small boundary-shaped region of the codon cube, with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ local two-faces.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The associated spectral row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic check is numerically closed to tolerance, with polynomial value $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$. This gives a quantitative coordinate for the local tile, but the coordinate is still an internal finite computation over the codon display. By itself it does not assert that biological systems must implement the same geometry.

\paragraph{Null-model pressure on the shape.}
Against the unrestricted finite comparison, the run used $N=2000$ samples. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape occurred once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. High-overlap shape recovery at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. A second amino-acid-class-preserving comparison used the observed size $|M|=20$. In that comparison the exact shape count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, the exact-shape value was $p=0.0$, and the lower-tail value was $p=0.0035$. These finite rows support the claim that the observed local shape is not typical under the recorded randomization procedures, while still leaving the biological interpretation fixed at codon-label readback.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out context.}
The broader leave-one-code-out table evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, while the hit rate after adjoining the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. Family stratification kept this high recovery across $5$ families: bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families each had rate $1.0$, while the alternative-yeast family had rate $0.8333333333333334$. These numbers give the codon-level row a surrounding code-table context without turning the local rule into a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out result.}
For the codon-level row itself, the finite recovery summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\ count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\ count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\ coverage\ high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\ count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The threshold comparison also records recovery rate above threshold $1.0$. The absence of anchor cases is important: this row is not saying that a single fixed anchor codon determines the missing codon. The evidence is instead that every held-out codon in the recorded boundary modules is either recovered by the local closure behavior or is accounted for as a boundary case.

\paragraph{Module-level accounting.}
The module classification is complete for the $13$ codons in $R$: the assigned count is $13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each listed module has either predictable or boundary status. This is the direct finite content of the claim: the leave-one-codon-out calculation covers the entire $R$ row by local predictable cases and explicitly marked boundary cases.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The supported conclusion is narrow. Window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary-tile pattern in the standard-code label table, and the leave-one-codon-out experiment reports full recovery within that finite setup. The contact used here is the curated codon-to-label table; it supplies amino-acid and stop labels, not ribosomal dynamics, molecule-level realization, tertiary structure, energetic viability, organismal function, or a general biological law. Any step from this code-label pattern to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require an additional empirical contact targeted at that higher layer.
