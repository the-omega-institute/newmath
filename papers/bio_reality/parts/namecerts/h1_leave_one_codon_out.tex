\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite labeled object on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The coordinates are not taken as a biological cause of translation.  They are used only to ask whether a local six-bit codon display separates a small set of boundary labels in a way that can be checked against the curated standard-code table.  The external contact is therefore the assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels, with no additional observation of ribosomal realization, molecular folding, thermodynamic admissibility, organism-level function, mechanism, or universality.

The finite residue set selected by the coordinate test has cardinality $13$.  It is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run verifies that this set is the punctured-cube row under the chosen coordinate readout, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object being tested is finite and explicit: thirteen codons, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local two-faces, all read only through the standard-code label table.
