\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object in this record is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$. Each codon is treated as a finite display point, and the table supplies only the amino-acid or stop label attached to that point. The relevant recovered region is the thirteen-codon set

The leave-one-codon row removes one codon at a time from the visible local tile and asks whether the remaining coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation information still places the held-out codon in the permitted recovery region. The recorded run $\mathrm{e9e1a6e41633}$ gives

\mathrm{anchor\_count} &= 0,\\

The absence of anchors is also informative: the recovery is not being explained by fixed singleton anchors already built into the table. It is a tile-boundary phenomenon, not a memorized label lookup.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, which maps codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is enough to test codon-label readback and the finite local split above. It is not a contact with translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal function, or a universal biological mechanism. Consequently, this chapter licenses the following narrow statement: within the standard-code label table, the thirteen-codon region $R$ forms a recoverable local boundary tile in the six-coordinate codon cube, and leave-one-codon tests recover every member as predictable or boundary. No claim about protein folding, biochemical realization, physical viability, function, mechanism, or universality follows unless a separate experiment supplies that higher-layer contact.
