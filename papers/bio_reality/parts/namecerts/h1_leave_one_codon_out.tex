\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Boundary Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record fixes a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$ and asks what can be recovered when a codon label is withheld from the standard-code readback table. The relevant finite subset is the thirteen-codon boundary set
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
Thus the row check is not a claim about all biology; it is a finite code-layer test on a distinguished part of the codon cube. The same set is certified as a punctured-cube-shaped row with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R|&=13,\\
f(R)&=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
The readback contact is the curated standard genetic-code table, which supplies codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop labels. It supplies labels only; it does not supply translation dynamics, molecular structure, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanistic rule.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape diagnostics.}
The coordinate test separates two finite objects, the distinguished boundary row $R$ and the larger comparison shape $M$. The recorded spectral values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic radial check leaves numerical residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is within the reported tolerance for the finite computation. Against $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes, the exact observed shape appeared once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. A high-overlap check at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampler recorded $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the empirical null should be read as a finite randomized audit of this codon-coordinate construction, not as a distributional law of molecular evolution.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A stricter label-respecting null preserves amino-acid class while testing whether the same finite shape is reproduced. In that null model the observed comparison object has size $|M|=20$. Among $N=2000$ trials, the exact-shape count was $0$, reported as $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This supports the narrow conclusion that the coordinate-and-label packet is not behaving like an ordinary class-preserving randomization at the tested finite resolution. It still remains a code-read statement: the contact has labels, not folded conformations, catalytic roles, organism-level fitness measurements, or physical synthesis constraints.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon recovery.}
The central leave-one-codon row reports no anchor codons, five boundary codons, and eight high-coverage closure recoveries:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\ count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\ count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\ coverage\ high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\ count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The threshold comparison also reports $\mathrm{recovery\ rate\ above\ threshold}=1.0$. Read literally, every withheld codon in the tested boundary packet is either recovered by the local closure criterion or remains accounted for by the boundary designation. The outcome is therefore a local boundary-recovery fact about the thirteen listed codons, not a statement that window-six geometry itself performs translation or determines protein-level behavior.

\paragraph{Relation to leave-one-code tests.}
The broader leave-one-code experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the reported hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and including the boundary gave $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear ciliate, and candidate-other rows, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast rows. These finite comparisons strengthen the interpretation that the row is detecting a stable code-table pattern, but the result is still only as broad as the supplied code-table contacts.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification exhausts the thirteen-codon row with no missing or extra codons. The assigned modules are
$\mathrm{AGR}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$,
$\mathrm{AR/Lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$,
$\mathrm{CUN}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$,
$\mathrm{Ile/Met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$,
$\mathrm{SSN}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and
$\mathrm{Stop/Trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.
The classification reports $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, $13$ assigned codons, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. This gives a closed finite accounting of the row at the label-class level.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The scientific status is a constrained readback claim. A curated standard-code table can test whether the displayed codon coordinates, closure rule, spectrum, and module relation align with amino-acid or stop labels. It cannot by itself test whether ribosomes realize the pattern, whether any amino-acid sequence folds into a particular structure, whether the resulting molecule is physically admissible, whether it has biological function, or whether the pattern is universal across organisms and mechanisms. Crossing any of those boundaries requires an additional contact of the appropriate kind. The present record therefore supports a leave-one-codon boundary-recovery statement for the standard-code label table and blocks promotion to translation, folding, physics, function, mechanism, or universality.
