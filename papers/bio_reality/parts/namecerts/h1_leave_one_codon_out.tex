\paragraph{Claim and local object.}
The claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} concerns a window-six representation of the standard-code codon table.  The mathematical carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite coordinate display for codon labels.  The row does not assert that this cube is a mechanism of translation.  It records a local code-layer phenomenon: when the standard-code labels are placed on the finite codon display, a small boundary region is singled out by the leave-one-codon-out probe.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  These distinctions are internal coordinate objects until they are compared with the curated standard-code table, whose role is only to supply codon-to-label readback.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local boundary.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard-code table.  It supplies the fact that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact can test code readback and the local codon-tile label split, but it cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, or biological function.  The finite evidence therefore supports a bounded code-layer statement: the standard-code labels, when placed on the six-coordinate codon display, expose a leave-one-codon-out boundary pattern with full recovery in the recorded test and complete module accounting over the $13$-codon residue set.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row.  Amino-acid label readback is still label readback; it is not a protein-structure observation.  CUN, UUR, stop, tryptophan, arginine, lysine, isoleucine, and methionine labels appear here only as entries in a finite code table.  Any claim crossing from code labels to realized translation, molecular structure, physical viability, biological function, or a universal biological rule would require a separate empirical contact designed for that layer.  Until such a contact is supplied, the present chapter records a finite local boundary phenomenon in the codon table and nothing stronger.
