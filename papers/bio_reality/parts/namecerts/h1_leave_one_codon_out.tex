\subsection{Leave-one-codon local boundary test}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Object and scope.}
The object of this record is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with positions read as six binary coordinates after fixing the usual three-letter nucleotide notation.  The biological contact used here is only the curated table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label.  Thus the tested layer is code readback: the record asks whether a window-six coordinate model exposes a local boundary pattern for withheld codons.  It does not assert that the coordinate model is a law of translation, a protein-structure model, a physical-admissibility criterion, a function predictor, or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The observed list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row also reports that this set is a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the local tile used by the leave-one-codon test.  The point of the construction is not that $R$ replaces biochemical evidence, but that the curated label table can be interrogated along this finite coordinate boundary.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The accompanying spectral measurement separates the main window region from the boundary region by two reported radii,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also listed as the cubic-polynomial residual.  This near-zero residual is used only as a finite numerical consistency check for the recorded coordinate spectrum.  It is not used as evidence for folding, catalysis, expression, or organism-level function.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite comparison against random code-shaped samples used $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $|M|=20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, class-preserving exact-shape value $0.0$, and lower-tail value $0.0035$.  These numbers support the claim that the recorded local shape is not a typical output of the tested finite null models.  They do not by themselves promote the coordinate pattern to a causal biological process.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code context.}
The related leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The recorded hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the same finite evidence gives five families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  These rows provide the surrounding code-table evidence for the more specific leave-one-codon boundary test.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon row.}
For the claim identified here, the leave-one-codon-out run reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, recovery rate $1.0$, and recovery-rate-above-threshold value $1.0$.  In symbols, the recorded scalars are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchors} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{boundary} &= 5,\\
\mathrm{high\ closure\ coverage} &= 8,\\
\mathrm{predictable} &= 8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\ rate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row therefore says that every withheld codon counted as recoverable by the stated local procedure is recovered, and that the recoverable cases divide between closure-covered and boundary-supported behavior rather than anchor-supported behavior.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons of $R$: it reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported classifier says that every one of these modules has predictable or boundary status.  This is the most concrete finite description of the local tile: it is not merely a count of $13$ codons, but a complete partition of the boundary set into biologically legible codon-label neighborhoods.

\paragraph{Readback interpretation.}
The scientific reading is deliberately narrow.  The curated standard-code table supplies the codon-to-label contact.  Against that contact, the window-six coordinates expose a local boundary whose $13$ codons, punctured-cube face-vector, spectral radii, null-model rarity, and leave-one-codon recovery statistics are all finite and auditable.  The claim is therefore that this coordinate presentation is useful for code-layer boundary readback: withholding one codon can still leave enough local structure for the recorded recovery behavior.

\paragraph{Local exclusion clauses.}
No result in this record observes translation being realized in a cell, a protein chain folding, a molecule satisfying physical admissibility constraints, a biological function occurring, or a universal genetic-code mechanism.  Amino-acid label readback is not folded structure.  Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, and codon-family naming do not supply structure evidence unless a separate structure contact is attached.  The local tile may organize a code table, but it remains at the code layer until an independent higher-layer observation is supplied.
