\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Recovery as a Local Codon-Tile Boundary}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite row.}
This record tests whether the window-six codon coordinate picture gives a local recovery signal when a single codon is withheld from the standard-code label table. The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of codons together with curated amino-acid or stop labels. The relevant distinction is not the full biological act of translation. It is the smaller code-layer question: after one codon is removed, does the remaining local tile geometry place the missing codon in a predictable or boundary-determined position? The verified row for \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor-only cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. Thus every withheld codon in the tested finite row is accounted for by the local predictable-or-boundary split, with no case requiring an isolated anchor explanation.

\paragraph{The thirteen-codon region.}
The region under this test has exactly $13$ codons. The verified list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set with the punctured-cube region and gives the face-vector data $(13,16,4)$. This is the finite geometric content of the claim: there are thirteen vertices in the codon region, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local two-dimensional faces in the displayed tile complex. The test therefore concerns a small combinatorial object inside the codon cube, not an open-ended biological field.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete on the same thirteen codons. The assigned modules are $\mathrm{AGR}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{CUN}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{SSN}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The audit row reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. Moreover, every listed module contains a predictable or boundary member. This matters because the leave-one-codon-out result is not a scattered collection of accidental singleton hits; it is aligned with a complete partition of the finite region into biologically readable code-label modules.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The finite geometry also carries a spectral signature. The verified spectrum reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, with radial-polynomial residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$. In the finite row this residual is effectively zero at numerical tolerance, so the measured quantities are mutually consistent with the recorded cubic constraint. The shape-significance audit used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It found $1$ exact shape match after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, giving $p_\mathrm{exact}=0.0005$. It also found $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. A second null model preserving amino-acid classes found $0$ exact shape matches in $2000$ samples and lower-tail count $7$ against observed $M$-size $20$, giving lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These values support the finite local geometry as non-generic under the recorded null probes, while still leaving the interpretation at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Relation to leave-one-code-out rows.}
The broader code-table experiment gives a compatible boundary picture. Across $23$ tables and $63$ targets, the leave-one-code-out audit reports baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$. The family split has five families, with hit-rate-above-baseline values $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families. These rows do not prove that the codon geometry causes biological reassignment. They do show that the same finite boundary vocabulary remains predictive across the recorded code-table comparison task.

\paragraph{Reality contact and blocked promotion.}
The external contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table. That contact supplies codon-to-label readback: codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels. It does not observe ribosomal translation as a process, does not supply folded protein structure, does not test physical admissibility, does not measure biological function, and does not establish a universal mechanism. Consequently the local tile result must be read narrowly. It says that, for the recorded thirteen-codon region in $Q_6$, leaving out one codon remains recoverable through the finite predictable-or-boundary classification with recovery rate $1.0$. It does not say that DNA is forced by this geometry, that proteins fold according to this tile, or that a function follows from the amino-acid label sequence. Any claim beyond code-layer label readback would require a separate empirical contact designed for that higher layer.
