\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Boundary Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and readback.}
The record for \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} uses the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$ as its finite coordinate carrier. The biological readback is restricted to the curated standard-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. Thus the object under inspection is not translation in a cell and not a protein-level realization; it is the finite label table seen through a window-six coordinate display. The distinguished codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$, namely
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that $R$ is a punctured cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. This is the finite boundary object used by the leave-one-codon probe: each codon removal is tested against the local closure and boundary structure already visible in the coordinate cube.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon measurement.}
The direct leave-one-codon run has experiment id \texttt{e9e1a6e41633}. It reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, and $0$ anchor cases. The high-coverage closure count is $8$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$, with the threshold comparison also equal to $1.0$. In finite terms, all thirteen codons in $R$ are accounted for by either predictable closure behavior or by an explicit boundary classification. The result should be read as a local recovery statement: removing one codon from the finite display does not destroy the ability of the coordinate-and-boundary record to place the missing row inside the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary of the claim.}
The contact named \texttt{curated.standard.code.table} supplies codon-to-label assignments only. It can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function realization, or a universal biological mechanism. Consequently, the present chapter claims only that window-six codon coordinates expose a strong local boundary-and-closure pattern in the standard-code label table. It does not claim that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological law, that a label sequence determines a protein fold, or that codon-tile recovery by itself supplies a mechanism of translation. Any movement beyond the code-label layer would require a distinct empirical contact carrying the corresponding biological observation.
