\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Boundary Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and readout.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the only observational contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The claim is therefore a code-layer statement: it tests whether local window-six coordinates expose a stable boundary-tile pattern around omitted codons. It does not treat the coordinate geometry as translation, molecular structure, physical admissibility, function, evolutionary mechanism, or a universal biological law.

The distinguished residual row $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the supplied finite run it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is reported as a punctured-cube object with face vector $(13,16,4)$. This is the finite shape against which the leave-one-codon test is read. The label table supplies the codon labels; the cube supplies the local coordinate and closure organization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon result.}
For claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.codon.out}$, the finite run reports no anchor cases, five boundary cases, and eight high-coverage closure cases. The predictable count is $8$, the boundary count is $5$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$. In compact form, the observed row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\_count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\_count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\_count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\_rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every omitted codon in the tested residual modules is either recovered by the local predictable component or remains on the explicit boundary component. The result is not that the omitted codon is biologically forced. It is that, relative to the standard-code label table and the six-bit coordinate display, the omitted row is not invisible to the local closure geometry.

The accounting therefore closes at the level of the supplied codon modules: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ entries are assigned, and the residual module table has no unclassified codon.

The radial cubic check is numerically zero to the supplied tolerance, with value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$. These numbers do not by themselves identify a biochemical mechanism. They record that the finite coordinate row has a stable numerical signature in the tested construction.

The shape test for $M$ used $N=2000$ sampled controls with seed $42$. It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampling run made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These controls support the claim that the tested local shape is not a routine artifact of the sampled label-preserving rearrangements.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The external contact is a curated standard genetic-code bridge at codon-label resolution. It observes that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels in the standard table. It does not observe ribosomal realization, folded protein structure, energetic admissibility, biological function, evolutionary cause, or cross-organism universality. The correct reading of the result is therefore narrow. Window-six geometry and the standard-code label table jointly support a local boundary and closure test for omitted codons in $R$. They do not license any claim that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological necessity, that an amino-acid label sequence determines a fold, or that the codon-tile split is a global rule of life.

The blocked structural interpretation is explicit. A codon-to-label table contains no folding contact, no physical measurement, and no functional assay. Any future promotion beyond code readback would require a separate observation at the relevant layer. Until such a contact is supplied, the present claim remains a finite code-layer record: $13$ residual codons are classified into complete modules, the leave-one-codon recovery rate is $1.0$, the boundary count is $5$, the predictable count is $8$, and the supporting shape and spectral checks remain confined to the local codon-coordinate construction.
