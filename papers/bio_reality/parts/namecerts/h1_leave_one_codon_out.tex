\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out recovery in the window-six codon cube}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This packet treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-reading contact against a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the $64$-vertex codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon is read through its six-bit coordinate presentation and then compared with the curated amino-acid or stop label assigned by the standard code.  The tested row is the leave-one-codon-out row with claim id $h1.leave.one.codon.out$.  Its purpose is narrow: after withholding one codon from the local code-read pattern, the procedure asks whether the missing codon falls back into the predictable or boundary portion of the same finite tile geometry.

\paragraph{The recovered boundary set.}
The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$\{AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU, UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}$.
The same row records $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite combinatorial contact is not merely a count: it identifies the particular codons, the number of local edges, and the number of two-faces seen by the residual tile.  In the leave-one-codon-out run itself, the observed values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{anchor\_count} &= 0,\\
\operatorname{boundary\_count} &= 5,\\
\operatorname{closure\_coverage\_high} &= 8,\\
\operatorname{predictable\_count} &= 8,\\
\operatorname{recovery\_rate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recovery threshold was also met with recorded value $1.0$.  In words, all withheld codons that were recoverable by this row landed inside the finite predictable-or-boundary compartment; none required an anchor class in this particular run.

\paragraph{Module resolution.}
The module classification is complete on $R$: it assigns all $13$ residual codons and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{AGR} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\operatorname{AR\_Lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\operatorname{CUN} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\operatorname{Ile\_Met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\operatorname{SSN} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\operatorname{Stop\_Trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has at least one predictable-or-boundary witness in the row.  The classification is therefore not an after-the-fact narrative grouping: it is a finite partition of the observed residual codons into named code-read modules, with $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\operatorname{assigned\_count}=13$.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The spectral companion row reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  Its cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical row is internally consistent to the displayed tolerance.  The sampled shape comparison used $N=2000$ null samples with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The run also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which is important because it shows that the finite null comparison was sampling a constrained combinatorial space rather than an unconstrained stream of independent labels.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, exact matches were $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$ and exact-shape value $0.0$ at the reported resolution.

These companion rows do not enlarge the biological conclusion; they show that the same local code-read geometry remains visible when the comparison is organized by table and family.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table: a finite table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The contact resolution is codon-label readback.  It tests whether a six-coordinate codon display and its local tile split align with the standard-code labels.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, molecular folding, energetic feasibility, organismal fitness, or biochemical function.  Consequently the successful row supports a code-layer statement: the withheld-codon test recovers a finite local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
No conclusion in this subsection promotes the local codon tile into a law of biology.  The window-six pattern does not by itself prove translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code origin, or universality across life.  Amino-acid label sequences remain label sequences until a separate contact supplies structural or functional evidence.  Cross-organism CUN/UUR wording and tRNA-Leu notation likewise stay outside this row unless they are attached to organism-matched data.  The present evidence is therefore strong but scoped: it is a finite, reproducible code-read alignment with recovery rate $1.0$, residual set size $13$, complete module assignment, significant null comparisons, and an explicit stop at the boundary of the curated label table.
