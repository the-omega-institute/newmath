\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out boundary recovery in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record uses the six-coordinate codon cube as its finite carrier and reads the standard genetic-code table only as a curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The carrier is therefore a code-layer object: it is a finite coordinate display equipped with label contacts, local faces, closures, and adjacency relations. The observation tested here is not that biology must obey this cube, but that the cube can expose a small local boundary pattern when one codon label is held out and then recovered from the surrounding finite row data.

\paragraph{The punctured residual row.}
The residual codon row $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
As a punctured cube-shaped object it has face-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
This is the finite row against which the leave-one-codon-out probe is evaluated. The row is not a universal rule for codons; it is a compact boundary witness inside the standard-code label contact.

\paragraph{Spectral row data.}
The associated spectral measurements separate the residual row from the ambient local geometry. The finite run recorded

\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The radial cubic check was numerically stable at

\end{aligned}$$
which is within the recorded tolerance for the spectral-radius computation. These numbers are used only as finite diagnostics of the row geometry; they do not supply translation, folding, biochemical feasibility, protein function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Shape and null comparisons.}
Against $2000$ distinct sampled comparison tables with seed $42$, the exact shape appeared once, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}$$
The high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
The distinct-sample procedure required $57377$ attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite sample is explicitly a sampled comparison rather than a closed enumeration of all possible labelings. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and the same seed, the observed comparison row size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$. Thus
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
These values support the claim that the row is unusually organized relative to the finite nulls that were actually run; they do not turn the row into a law of molecular realization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon recovery.}
The companion leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was
$$\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}$$
while the observed rate above baseline was
$$\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}$$
The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary row was again $0.9841269841269841$. For the stricter leave-one-codon-out record, the row has no anchor codons, five boundary codons, eight high-coverage closure codons, and eight predictable codons:
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\ count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\ count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\ coverage\ high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\ count}&=8.
\end{aligned}$$
The recorded recovery rate is
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}=1.0,
\end{aligned}$$
and the thresholded recovery flag is also $1.0$. The finite meaning is narrow: the held-out codon labels are recovered inside this local code-read experiment from closure and boundary information supplied by the standard-code contact.

\paragraph{Module coverage.}
The module classification covers all $13$ codons in $R$ with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The completeness row records $R$-size $13$ and assigned count $13$. Every module contains either predictable or boundary codons, so the leave-one-codon-out result is not concentrated in a single exceptional family. The family-level leave-one-code-out comparison also records five families, with hit-rate-above-baseline values $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and boundary.}
The claim is ready at the code-read layer. The standard-code label table supplies the reality contact, the finite cube supplies coordinates and closure tests, and the row data show complete module assignment together with perfect leave-one-codon recovery in the recorded run. The conclusion is deliberately local: a codon-label boundary tile is observed inside a curated label table. Nothing in this chapter establishes ribosomal translation, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a generating mechanism, or a universal biological principle. Any such stronger claim would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer rather than reusing codon-label readback.
