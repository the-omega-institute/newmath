\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out boundary recovery in the standard code}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite object and readout.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The finite display is not taken to be a biochemical engine.  It is a coordinate object whose rows are codons, whose visible labels are the curated amino-acid or stop labels, and whose tested question is whether a single omitted codon can be recovered from local boundary information at the code-read layer.  The reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table: it supplies codon-to-label assignments, and nothing stronger.  The four active distinctions are the six-bit codon display, the local tile face, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class.  The internal organization used by the computation is coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation.

\paragraph{The distinguished row set.}
The finite relation row set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run checks that this set is the punctured-cube row set and records the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tested object is a small, explicit subcomplex inside the codon cube rather than an inferred whole-genome principle.  The row count is important: all claims below are claims about these $13$ listed codons and their code-label behavior under the stated leave-one-codon-out test.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model checks.}
The spectral run supplies three scalar quantities:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic relation is numerically satisfied to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the radial polynomial value.  These numbers are not presented as biological constants; they are finite checks on the coordinate-and-relation representation used by the codon-window probe.

The shape-significance comparison used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found one exact shape match, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
It also found $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null model again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that constrained null, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$.  The reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite comparisons support the claim that the observed codon-row geometry is not typical under the stated randomizations, while still leaving the biological interpretation at the label-table level.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
The direct leave-one-codon-out computation for claim $h1.leave.one.codon.out$ reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\ count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\ count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\ coverage\ high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\ count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The above-threshold recovery scalar is also $1.0$.  In words, the run did not recover the omitted codon through anchor rows; it recovered the relevant cases through boundary and high-closure coverage.  The local conclusion is therefore a boundary-recovery conclusion: within the finite standard-code readout, the six-bit codon window exposes enough local structure for the tested omitted rows to be recovered by the stated criterion.

This codon-level result is consistent with the code-table leave-one-out aggregate.  Across $23$ evaluated code tables and $63$ total targets, the baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The comparison shows that the boundary rows materially improve recovery over the baseline, but it does not identify a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Module-level accounting.}
The module classification is complete on the same $13$-row set.  It assigns every row in $R$ and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{size}}=13,\qquad \mathrm{assigned\ count}=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\

\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\

\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has at least predictable or boundary support under the recorded classification.  The family-level leave-one-code-out run used $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  These family numbers give a broader row-wise consistency check, but they remain checks against code-label tables.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following bounded statement.  In the standard-code codon table, the $13$-row punctured-cube set $R$ exhibits a local boundary-and-closure pattern in the six-coordinate codon display.  Under the recorded leave-one-codon-out probe, the omitted-row recovery rate is $1.0$, with $5$ boundary cases and $8$ high-closure predictable cases.  The spectral quantities, null-model probabilities, and complete module assignment all point in the same direction: this is a genuine finite pattern in the codon-label readout, not merely a restatement of the raw label table.

\paragraph{Scope limit.}
No claim about translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows.  The contact used here observes codon-to-label assignments in the curated standard-code table.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, molecular folding, organism-level fitness, structural order, or functional consequence.  Any step beyond code-read recovery would require a separate contact whose observations live at that higher layer.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence obtained from this table is still only a label sequence; it is not a protein structure and not evidence of biological function.
