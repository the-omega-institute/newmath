\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Boundary Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim recorded here is that the standard-code codon table, when read through a six-coordinate codon display, contains a local boundary-tile signal strong enough to recover each held-out reassignment codon from its neighboring code-layer context. The carrier is the $Q_6$ codon cube: each codon is represented as a six-bit coordinate display, and the only readout allowed in this chapter is the curated amino-acid-or-stop label attached to that codon in the standard genetic-code table. The finite observation is not a statement about molecular translation, folded structure, physical viability, biological function, mechanism, or universality. It is a code-layer statement about whether local coordinate closure and label adjacency recover the held-out codon row inside a finite table.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The sole table-level contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code bridge assigning every codon to an amino-acid or stop label. This contact resolves codon label readback, not ribosomal realization or biochemical structure. The observed fact supplied by the contact is that the standard table maps codons to labels. Every calculation below stays inside that resolution: codons are rows, labels are readback symbols, and local tile membership is a finite combinatorial property of the six-bit display plus the curated label table.
