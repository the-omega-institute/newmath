\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Codon Tile Readback}\label{sec:biorreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.} The claim concerns a six-bit codon cube $Q_6$ equipped with the standard codon-label table as its only biological contact. The finite object under inspection is not a translation system or a protein system; it is a coordinate display whose rows are codons and whose readout is the curated amino-acid or stop label assigned by the standard code. In the verified row for this claim, the leave-one-codon-out probe has experiment run id $e9e1a6e41633$ and tests whether removing one codon from the locally distinguished region leaves enough coordinate closure and boundary information to recover the held-out codon at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Distinguished finite region.} The distinguished set $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its supplied codon list is
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row verifies that this set is the punctured-cube region used by the packet, with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the local object has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four square faces in the finite codon-cube geometry. The data do not say that these codons are biologically forced by geometry. They say that, after the standard labels are attached, this thirteen-row region is the finite tile on which the local readback test is performed.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape statistics.} The supplied spectral row records $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, within numerical tolerance for the claimed finite computation. The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$ and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, including $55377$ duplicate retries. It observed $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, used observed $M$-size $20$, found $0$ exact matches, and placed only $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size. Its reported values are $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035$. These rows make the local shape atypical under the stated finite null models, while still leaving the labels themselves as curated contacts rather than consequences of the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon recovery.} In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count is $0$, the boundary count is $5$, the high-closure-coverage count is $8$, and the predictable count is $8$. The reported recovery rate is
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}=1.0,
\end{aligned}$$
and the threshold comparison also reports $1.0$. The absence of anchors matters: the recovery is not being described as a direct singleton anchor effect. The finite row instead separates boundary codons from high-closure-coverage codons and then records that every tested held-out codon is recovered within the packet's code-read criterion. This is a local codon-label readback result: it concerns which codon rows can be recovered from the tile and its boundary behavior after a one-row deletion.

\paragraph{Module decomposition.} The module-classification row assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The complete decomposition is

The row also states that every module has a predictable or boundary representative. This supports a chapter-level reading in which the local codon tile is not merely a thirteen-row list: it is partitioned into six named modules, and every module is touched by the recovery-or-boundary classification. The decomposition is still finite and code-layered. It does not identify a folding module, a biochemical mechanism, or a cross-organism law.

These finite rows give context for the codon-level claim: the single-codon deletion test sits inside a larger pattern of code-table readback, but the present chapter only asserts the codon-row claim associated with the standard-code contact.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary.} The biological contact is the curated standard genetic-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code-read behavior and the local split of codon labels across the tile. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological mechanism. Therefore the chapter's conclusion is deliberately narrow: the finite six-bit coordinate packet, together with the curated standard-code labels, recovers the leave-one-codon-out targets at rate $1.0$ and organizes the thirteen-row region into predictable-or-boundary modules. No claim follows about protein folding, organismal function, molecular admissibility, translation dynamics, or universality unless an additional independent contact supplies observations at that higher layer.
