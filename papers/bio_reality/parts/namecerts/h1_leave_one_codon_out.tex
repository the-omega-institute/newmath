\paragraph{Finite setup.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table through the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon label is treated as a code-layer readout supplied by the curated table rather than as a biological mechanism. The finite row isolates a distinguished codon set $R$ of size $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this set has the punctured-cube face profile
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the codons are not merely listed labels; they form a finite local face pattern inside the six-bit display. The claim tested here is not that this pattern explains translation. It is the narrower assertion that leaving out one codon at a time still exposes a recoverable local boundary signature at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of the claim.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code label table: codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels, and the local tile can be checked against those labels. Therefore the result is a code-read statement about finite codon coordinates, module classes, and boundary recovery. It does not establish translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of origin, or a universal law of biology. Any step from this local codon-label pattern to translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires a separate observation of that higher layer. In the present row, no such higher-layer contact is supplied.
