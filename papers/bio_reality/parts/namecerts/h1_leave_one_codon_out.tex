\subsection{Leave-one-codon boundary recovery in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite setup.}
The object tested here is the standard-code codon label table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$.  Each codon is treated as a finite address, and the only biological readback used in this section is the curated assignment of a codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The claim is therefore deliberately local: a window-six coordinate can expose a boundary pattern among codon labels, but the labels themselves are supplied by the curated code table.  The section does not infer translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality from the coordinate calculation.

\paragraph{Distinguished recovery set.}
The finite row isolates a set $R$ of $13$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check reports $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen codons.  The punctured-cube check reports that the same set has face-vector data $(13,16,4)$ in the finite coordinate complex.  Thus the row is not only a list of codon names: it has a small local-cell signature, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces inside the chosen codon geometry.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon outcome.}
For the primary leave-one-codon test, the row reports no anchor codons, five boundary codons, and eight codons with high closure coverage.  In scalar form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\_count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\_count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\_count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\_rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recovery rate above threshold is also $1.0$.  This is the narrow positive content of the row: after withholding one codon at a time, the coordinate-and-closure calculation recovers the omitted codon as either predictable or boundary-accounted within the finite local pattern.  Since the anchor count is zero, the row is not being interpreted as a collection of fixed biological attractors.  Its positive evidence is instead a complete local recovery classification for this finite codon subset.

\begin{aligned}

Every module is reported as having either a predictable or boundary role.  This gives the leave-one-codon result a stronger finite interpretation than a bare aggregate rate: the result is not hiding unclassified codons outside the named module system.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model checks.}
The associated spectral row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial polynomial value is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the cubic-polynomial residual, so the finite spectral calculation is numerically closed to tolerance.  The shape-significance row used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$, after $57377$ distinct sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null row also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  There the observed comparison size was $20$, with $0$ exact matches and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size.  The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact\ shape}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the claim that the observed finite shape is uncommon under the sampled controls, while still remaining a statement about the coordinate-and-label experiment rather than a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to code-table leave-one-out rows.}
A broader leave-one-code-table row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  It reports a baseline hit rate of $0.19821739130434785$, a hit rate of $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M-T$, and a hit rate of $0.9841269841269841$ inside $M-T$ plus boundary.  The same $0.9841269841269841$ value is reported above baseline.  The family breakdown contains five families, with hit-rate-above-baseline values $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  These rows are consistent with the codon-level boundary interpretation: boundary allowance is carrying part of the predictive signal, and the local codon result should be read as a boundary-sensitive code-read calculation.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and permits local split checks in the code layer.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, or biological function.  It also does not justify converting this local tile into a global law about DNA or organisms.  Any claim across those boundaries would require a separate contact, such as a translation experiment, a structure measurement, an admissibility model tied to physical chemistry, or a function assay.  Without such additional contact, the correct conclusion is that the window-six codon coordinates recover a finite local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table.
