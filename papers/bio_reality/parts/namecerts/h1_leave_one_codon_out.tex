\subsection{Leave-one-codon recovery at a local standard-code boundary}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} is a finite code-layer statement about the standard genetic-code table represented on a six-bit codon cube.  The relevant object is not translation as a biochemical process, but the table-level assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The experiment asks whether a codon withheld from the curated standard-code readback can be recovered from a local window-six boundary pattern using only coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data already present in the finite codon display.  The resulting statement is deliberately narrow: local cube geometry may identify a boundary class in the label table, while the biological interpretation remains restricted to codon-label readback.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and exceptional set.}
The finite carrier is the $64$-codon cube encoded by six binary coordinates.  Within this cube, the distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object being tested is not an arbitrary collection of codons.  It is a small cubical boundary in the six-bit display, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four square faces.  The readback map is supplied by the curated standard-code table; the geometry does not itself assign biochemical meaning.

These numbers support the use of the local cubical boundary as a measurable code-layer pattern rather than as a free verbal grouping of codons.

\paragraph{Observed recovery.}
The leave-one-codon-out row is the primary evidence for this claim.  It reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and $8$ predictable codons.  The recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}

$$

The partition of evidence is important: the row does not say that all codons are mechanistically explained.  It says that the tested withheld-codon cases falling under the local closure and boundary diagnostics were recovered by the finite table-level procedure.

The classification row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  It also records that every module has either a predictable member or a boundary member.  This is the finite reason the leave-one-codon result is not merely a single-codon anecdote: the exceptional set is exhausted by named modules, and the modules are all touched by the local diagnostic.

These comparisons do not prove a biochemical mechanism, but they do justify treating the local boundary as a nontrivial finite code-table pattern.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test whether translation is realized in a cell, whether a protein folds, whether a molecule is physically admissible, whether a biological function occurs, or whether a universal mechanism has been found.  Consequently, an amino-acid label sequence read from the table is still only a label sequence.  It is not a protein structure, not a function, and not a physical admissibility certificate.

\paragraph{Boundary statement.}
The chapter therefore records a precise finite result: within the six-bit standard-code codon display, the thirteen-codon boundary set $R$ forms a punctured-cube pattern; its modules account for all codons in the exceptional set; the leave-one-codon run reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, no anchors, and recovery rate $1.0$; and the associated null rows give small exact-shape or lower-tail probabilities.  The result remains at the code-read layer.  No translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter unless a separate empirical contact supplies that higher-layer observation.
