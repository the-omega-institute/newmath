\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Local Boundary Test}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Question.}
The leave-one-codon-out packet asks whether a six-bit codon display of the standard code has enough local organization to recover withheld codon labels at the code-read layer. The object is the ordinary standard-code codon table, read only as a finite assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The coordinate carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with codon coordinates, local face relations, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The test is deliberately local: it inspects whether a missing codon lies in a predictable closure neighborhood or on a boundary tile, not whether codons biologically translate, fold, function, or obey a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The table contact used by this packet is the curated standard genetic-code bridge. It supplies one observed finite fact: each codon has a curated amino-acid or stop label. This contact has resolution at codon-label readback only. It does not observe ribosomal translation, molecular structure, physical admissibility, biological function, organism-level mechanism, or universality. Consequently, every numerical statement below is a statement about code labels and finite codon geometry. A higher-layer claim would require a separate contact of the corresponding kind.

\paragraph{Finite region.}
The distinguished codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records $R$ as a punctured cube with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the finite geometry is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it carries a small cubical face pattern with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces. The spectrum calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,\\
P(\mu)&=-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}.

$$
The residual value is numerically at zero for the recorded cubic relation, so the spectral summary is consistent with the same finite row, up to floating-point tolerance.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon outcome.}
For claim $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.codon.out}$, the verified run reports no anchor cases, five boundary cases, eight high-coverage closure cases, and eight predictable cases. The recovery rate is exactly $1.0$, and the above-threshold recovery value is also $1.0$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\_count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\_count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\_count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\_rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central finite-row result. Within the thirteen-codon region, every withheld codon is accounted for by the local closure-or-boundary criterion used by the packet. Since the anchor count is zero, the reported success is not a statement that a codon was already fixed by a direct singleton anchor. It is instead a statement that the local codon-tile structure, together with the finite label table, is sufficient for this leave-one-codon readback test.

Each module has at least one predictable or boundary codon under the recorded classification. This matters because the recovery result is not concentrated in an unlabelled residual bucket. The whole thirteen-codon region is partitioned by named codon modules, and the closure-or-boundary explanation reaches every module in that partition.

\paragraph{Comparison rows.}
The related leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. These rows do not prove the leave-one-codon result by themselves, but they put the codon-level recovery into the same finite experimental neighborhood: the boundary-augmented local region is where the strongest code-read signal appears.

\paragraph{Family split.}
The by-family summary records five families. The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. This supports a narrow robustness reading: under the tested family grouping, the local readback criterion remains strong across all five rows, with only the alternative-yeast row falling below perfect recovery. It does not license an organism-level mechanism claim, because the family labels here are still used as table-level groupings rather than as independent biological experiments.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Two null checks sharpen the finite interpretation. In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, there was one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. The run required $57377$ distinct sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. In the amino-acid-class-preserving null with the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, the exact-shape $p$-value was $0.0$, and the lower-tail $p$-value was $0.0035$. These rows say that the observed finite shape is uncommon under the sampled null procedures. They do not say that the geometry is necessary for biology.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The local conclusion is therefore precise: the six-bit codon cube exposes a finite boundary-and-closure pattern that recovers the withheld codon cases in the recorded standard-code readback test. The curated table supplies the label contact, while the cube supplies the finite coordinate and adjacency organization. Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, an organism-level causal mechanism, or a universal biological law. Amino-acid label sequences remain code-layer readbacks until a separate structure or function contact is attached. Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, and codon-family names likewise remain table annotations unless a distinct observation supplies the higher layer.
