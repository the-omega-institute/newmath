\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This record studies a window-six presentation of the standard-code codon table.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read at the code-label layer: a codon is treated as a six-bit display position, and the observed labels are the curated amino-acid or stop assignments of the standard genetic code.  The finite distinction tested here is not a biochemical process.  It is the following local question: when one codon-level row is withheld, does the remaining coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data locate the withheld row inside the same local boundary pattern that was visible before the deletion?

This is the finite geometric object whose stability is tested by the leave-one-codon-out probe.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
For the codon-level deletion test, the finite run reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-high coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The thresholded recovery flag is also $1.0$.  In the terms of this record, every recovered withheld codon is recovered through boundary or closure evidence rather than through an anchor singleton.  The result is therefore a local boundary result: deleting a row from the codon-label table does not erase the relevant finite neighborhood signal for the tested rows.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code-layer readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test whether translation is realized in a cell, whether an amino-acid string folds, whether a molecular state is physically admissible, whether a protein has function, whether a mechanism is universal, or whether the coordinate geometry is a law of biology.  The amino-acid label sequence is therefore not treated as a folded structure.  A separate structural, physical, organism-matched, or functional contact would be required before any such claim could be made.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The leave-one-codon-out row is ready as a finite code-layer record.  Its strongest statement is this: in the standard-code label table, the thirteen-row punctured-cube set $R$ has a stable local boundary signature under the tested deletion probe, with recovery rate $1.0$, predictable count $8$, boundary count $5$, and complete module accounting over the six listed modules.  Its weakest admissible interpretation is equally important: the result is a constrained codon-label regularity, not evidence for translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.
