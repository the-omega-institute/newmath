\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object and readout.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite labelled dataset on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is represented by a six-bit display, with the observed label supplied by the curated standard genetic-code table.  The resulting question is not whether the cube explains translation.  The question is narrower: after one codon label is withheld, does the local window-six geometry recover whether the missing row lies in the predictable part of the code-layer pattern or on its boundary?

The residual codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same rows form the punctured-cube support, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is not an arbitrary list of exceptional codons: it has a finite two-dimensional boundary profile inside the codon cube.  The amino-acid-side object used in the associated null comparison has observed size $20$, and the finite support $R$ is partitioned without leftovers by the six modules
$$
\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\}, & AR/Lys&=\{AAA\},\\
CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}, & Ile/Met&=\{AUA\},\\
SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\}, & Stop/Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing module codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out result.}
For the direct leave-one-codon-out probe, the verified run reports no anchor cases, five boundary cases, and eight predictable cases.  The high-coverage closure count is also eight.  In scalar form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\_count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\_count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{predictable\_count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\_rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The threshold comparison is likewise saturated at $1.0$.  This means that every withheld codon row tested by this finite procedure is accounted for either by the predictable part of the local closure pattern or by the boundary class.  The result is a code-layer recovery statement only: it concerns the codon-to-label table and the local split induced by the six-coordinate representation.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model checks.}
The same finite row family has a measured spectral profile
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the radial polynomial residual, so the numerical identity is within the recorded tolerance.  Against the shape randomization with $N=2000$, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, only one exact shape match is observed.  The exact-shape probability is therefore $0.0005$, while the count with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ is $25$, giving $p=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-match count is zero, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is seven, the exact-shape probability is $0.0$, and the lower-tail probability is $0.0035$.

\paragraph{Comparison with code-level table deletion.}
The codon-level recovery sits inside a broader leave-one-code-out finite audit.  That run evaluated $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, whereas the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline rates are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These numbers support the interpretation that the local codon-window split is repeatedly visible at the code-read layer, while still depending on the curated label table for biological names.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is a codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop assignment.  It tests label readback and local codon-tile splitting.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, molecular folding, structural order, energetic admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the finite recovery rate $1.0$ should be read as a local code-layer statement: in the tested window-six representation, withheld codon labels fall into predictable or boundary positions.  No claim about protein realization, folded structure, physical feasibility, function, or biological necessity follows without an additional empirical contact designed for that higher layer.
