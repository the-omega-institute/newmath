\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Boundary Test for the Standard Code}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object and coordinate frame.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with codons displayed as six binary positions induced by the three nucleotide slots. The observable labels are not generated by the cube itself: they are supplied by a curated standard genetic-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The local question is whether a leave-one-codon test inside this finite coordinate frame separates a small boundary region from ordinary closure recovery. The distinguished set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports the punctured-cube face data $(13,16,4)$, so the combinatorial object being tested is not a free biological mechanism but a finite local shape with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape measurements.}
The associated spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The cubic radial check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the radial polynomial evaluation, so the finite calculation is internally stable at the recorded tolerance. The shape-significance row used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. Among $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ were duplicate retries, $1$ exact shape match occurred, and $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$. Thus the reported exact-shape probability is $p=0.0005$, while the high-Jaccard probability is $p=0.0125$. A second amino-acid-class-preserving null used the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$: it found $0$ exact matches, $7$ lower-tail cases at or below the observed $M$ size, and an observed $M$ size of $20$, giving $p=0.0$ for exact shape and $p=0.0035$ for the lower tail.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon rows.}
The broader leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, appears after adding the boundary to $M-T$; without the boundary addition, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$. The specific leave-one-codon row has no anchors: $anchor\_count=0$. It reports $boundary\_count=5$, $closure\_coverage\_high=8$, and $predictable\_count=8$. The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$. These numbers support a local statement: every withheld codon in the tested row is either recovered by the closure signal or placed into the finite boundary region, but the row does not identify any anchor codon that would independently certify a mechanism.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete on the same $13$-codon set. It reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The six named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
agr&=\{AGA,AGG\}, & ar\_lys&=\{AAA\},\\
cun&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}, & ile\_met&=\{AUA\},\\
ssn&=\{UCA,UUA\}, & stop\_trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each listed module has a predictable-or-boundary witness in the supplied classification row. The family-level leave-one-code-out audit gives $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$ above baseline, while bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline. This family split is compatible with the same finite boundary reading, but it remains a code-table comparison rather than an organism-level account of translation.

\paragraph{Scientific scope.}
The supported conclusion is narrow. Window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local tile around the standard-code label table, and the leave-one-codon row records full recovery or boundary placement for the finite test set. The curated table supplies codon-to-label contact only. Nothing in these rows observes ribosomal translation, folded protein structure, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, evolutionary necessity, or a universal biological law. Amino-acid label readback is therefore not evidence of fold, function, or physical realization. Any such higher-layer claim would require a separate measurement source matched to that layer, not merely the present codon-label table.
