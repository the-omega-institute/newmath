\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Boundary for a Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object and contact.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read through a display in which each codon is represented by a six-bit word and then compared with a curated codon-to-label table. The biological contact used here is only the standard genetic-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. It is therefore a contact with symbolic code labels, not with ribosomal realization, folded protein geometry, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal fitness, or a universal biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Distinguished residue set.}
The finite row singled out by the window-six computation is a thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality row reports $|R|=13$. The same set is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the claim is not merely that a list of codons was selected; it is that the selected list carries a small local tile geometry inside $Q_6$. The point of the claim is local: the tile is a boundary-sensitive coordinate pattern in the code table, not a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectral computation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records the cubic or radial polynomial residual as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the finite numerical row is internally coherent to the stated tolerance. These numbers are used here only as finite coordinates of the codon-tile packet. They do not by themselves identify translation dynamics, a protein fold, a physical constraint, or an adaptive explanation.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The randomization row for the observed $M$-shape used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. The reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null preserving amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. In that row the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite comparisons support the claim that the code-label table is not behaving like the tested null rows with respect to this local tile. They still remain comparisons at the label-table level.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row.}
The direct leave-one-codon-out row is the primary finite test for this chapter. It reports $0$ anchor codons, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and $8$ predictable codons. Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the threshold comparison also recorded as $1.0$. The intended reading is that removal of a single codon from the local tile leaves enough coordinate information to recover the relevant code-layer placement in every tested case. Since the anchor count is $0$, the evidence is not an anchor-codon story; it is a boundary-and-closure story inside the finite codon cube.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module row assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing codons, no unassigned codons, and no extra codons outside the modules. The six named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified completeness row records $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$. Each module has either predictable support or boundary support, which is the finite reason the leave-one-codon-out result can be stated module-wise rather than as an isolated aggregate statistic.

\paragraph{Comparison with code-table leave-one-out.}
The neighboring leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. It reports a baseline hit rate of $0.19821739130434785$, a hit rate of $0.9365079365079365$ in $M-T$, and a hit rate of $0.9841269841269841$ in $M-T$ plus boundary. The hit rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the above-baseline hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate rows. These figures place the codon-level row in the same finite code-read regime: boundary information matters, and the improvement is measured against curated label tables rather than against direct molecular outcomes.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The claim supported by these rows is narrow. A window-six codon representation exposes a local boundary tile in the standard-code label table, and the leave-one-codon-out test recovers the relevant local placement with rate $1.0$ on the recorded finite row. The support comes from the listed codons, the punctured-cube face vector, the spectral values, the null comparisons, the module partition, and the code-table leave-one-out rates. Nothing in this chapter licenses a stronger biological statement. In particular, no translation process, folded structure, physical admissibility condition, biological function, mechanism, or universality claim follows from this contact unless a separate observation supplies that higher-layer information.
