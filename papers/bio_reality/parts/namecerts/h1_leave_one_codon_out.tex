\subsection{Leave-one-codon recovery in the window-six codon cube}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} records a finite code-layer experiment on the standard-code codon table.  Codons are treated as vertices of a six-bit codon display $Q_6$, and the observable labels are the curated amino-acid or stop assignments supplied by the standard genetic-code table.  The point of the record is not that codon geometry is itself a biological mechanism.  The point is narrower: when one codon is withheld from the local window-six construction, the remaining coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data still recover a sharply bounded set of code-layer positions.  The recovered pattern is a local tile phenomenon inside the codon-label table, with a separate guard against promoting it to translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and distinguished row set.}
The finite distinguished set $R$ has exactly thirteen codons.  The row set is
$$\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The cardinality check gives $|R|=13$.  The punctured-cube check identifies the same thirteen codons and records the face-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the object under discussion is not an inferred genome-scale rule.  It is a finite subcomplex of the codon cube whose vertices are read only through the curated standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape measurements.}
The associated spectral calculation reports
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.

The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the cubic-polynomial check, so the numerical witness is effectively at tolerance for the finite computation.  Against the sampled shape null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, there was one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and there were twenty-five samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling process attempted $57377$ distinct draws and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  These figures support a finite geometry signal, not a biological necessity claim.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null keeps amino-acid-class constraints while testing the same code-layer shape.  In that run, $N=2000$, the observed $M$-size was $20$, the exact shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$.  Hence
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
This comparison matters because it separates two claims that would otherwise be conflated.  The finite codon-label table supplies a real contact for label readback, while the null calculation asks whether the observed local shape is common under class-preserving reshuffling.  It is not evidence for folding, biochemical admissibility, cellular translation dynamics, or protein function.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope boundary.}
The sole biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or universal mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label readback remains a code-layer observation.  A sequence of labels obtained from the table is not a folded protein, not a physical admissibility certificate, and not a function assay.  Any future statement crossing those boundaries must supply a separate contact of the appropriate kind before it can be asserted.
