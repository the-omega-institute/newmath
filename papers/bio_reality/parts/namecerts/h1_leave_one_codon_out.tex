\paragraph{Claim record.}
The claim tracked here is that a six-bit codon display can expose a local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table, while the label table itself remains the empirical contact that supplies amino-acid and stop labels. The finite object is the codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary coordinate display for the $64$ codons. The tested row is the leave-one-codon-out row identified by $\mathrm{h1.leave.one.codon.out}$, with verification timestamp $2026$-$06$-$03T01{:}38{:}54+00{:}00$. The local result is not a claim about molecular translation, folding, admissible chemistry, organismal function, mechanism, or biological universality. It is a code-layer recovery statement: when one codon in the relevant region is hidden, the coordinate and closure structure recover the missing label position as predictable or boundary-visible within the finite table contact.

\paragraph{Code-table contact.}
The empirical contact used by this chapter is the curated standard genetic-code table. \paragraph{Boundary statement.}
The chapter-level conclusion is therefore narrow and testable. The codon cube $Q_6$, together with the curated standard-code labels, produces a finite local boundary row in which $13$ codons form a punctured cubical region, all $13$ are assigned to six complete modules, $8$ sites are predictable, $5$ are boundary sites, and the recorded leave-one-codon recovery rate is $1.0$. This is enough to record a code-read boundary phenomenon. It is not enough to infer that DNA or translation is governed by this geometry as a biological law. Any future claim about a translated product, a folded protein, a chemical feasibility constraint, a biological function, or a universal mechanism must enter through a separate reality contact that observes that higher layer directly.
