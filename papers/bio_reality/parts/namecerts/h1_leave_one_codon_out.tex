\paragraph{Leave-one-codon recovery.}
The direct leave-one-codon computation for this row evaluated the finite code-read boundary under claim identifier $h1.leave.one.codon.out$.  It reported anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, and high closure coverage $8$.  The recovery rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
with recovery above threshold also equal to $1.0$.  The absence of anchor codons is important: the result is not a statement that a single codon forces the table.  The evidence says instead that, inside the supplied six-bit local display and after one codon is withheld, the remaining finite boundary-and-closure pattern recovers the withheld codon-label placement in the tested row.

\paragraph{Local contact and blocked promotions.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, which supplies codon-to-label assignments at codon-label resolution.  It can test code-level readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It does not observe translation realization, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, or biological function.  Therefore the accepted conclusion is narrow: the six-bit codon display exposes a finite local boundary-tile pattern in the standard-code labels, with $13$ row codons, complete module assignment, significant shape statistics, and perfect leave-one-codon recovery in the supplied run.  No conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows unless an independent contact at that higher layer is supplied.
