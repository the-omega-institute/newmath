\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Boundary Recovery}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-boundary-recovery}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
This record studies the standard-code codon table through a six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon treated as a finite coordinate point and with labels supplied by a curated standard-code table. The object under test is deliberately narrow: the cube geometry, the local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes are used to ask whether omitted codons can be recovered at the code-label layer. The readout is the standard codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop label assignment. It is not a claim about translation dynamics, protein folding, physical chemistry, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Recovered row set.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified finite table it is
$R=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
The same run verifies that this set is the punctured-cube row under the chosen codon-coordinate display, with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the geometric datum is not merely a list of labels: it has a finite face count and edge count inside the coordinate cube. The associated measured $M$-shape has observed size $20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null experiment.

\paragraph{Spectral measurements.}
The numerical spectrum attached to the finite coordinate object separates the measured $M$-component from the boundary row $R$. The verified values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual was $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the polynomial identity was satisfied to the numerical tolerance of the run. The spectral numbers are used here only as finite diagnostics of the codon-coordinate table. They do not make the local tile into an organism-level law.

\paragraph{Randomization checks.}
Against $2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$, the exact $M$-shape match count was $1$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. The same randomization produced $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jac}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampling attempted $57377$ distinct draws and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the quoted probabilities are tied to a finite distinct-sample procedure rather than to an unrestricted continuum model.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid classes. In that model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact shape match count was $0$, so the finite estimate for exact shape recovery was $p=0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$. This check matters because it asks whether the observed tile behavior survives after the label-class structure is partly retained. The result supports a code-layer boundary pattern, not a structural or functional biological conclusion.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out comparison.}
The broader leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes. These numbers give finite comparative context for the codon-level omission test.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out result.}
For the claim indexed here, the leave-one-codon-out run found $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, and $8$ predictable cases. The recovery rate was $1.0$, and the thresholded recovery value was also $1.0$. The result says that every omitted codon tested in this finite row is recovered either through the predictable part of the coordinate closure or through the boundary part of the local tile. It does not say that the codon was translated in a cell, that a protein shape was realized, or that the coordinate rule is a biological necessity.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The $13$ elements of $R$ are completely assigned to six local modules. The modules are
$\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$,
$\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$,
$\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$,
$\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$,
$\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and
$\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.
The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing row codons, and no unassigned codons. Each module has either predictable or boundary status, so the recovery statement covers the full finite row rather than only a selected sublist.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The curated standard-code table is the only biological contact used here: it supplies codon labels at the code-read layer. The coordinate cube and its tile boundary organize those labels into a finite pattern, and the leave-one-codon-out experiment tests recovery after withholding one codon at a time. The admissible conclusion is therefore local: within the six-bit display and the curated label table, the row $R$ forms a punctured-cube boundary whose omitted codons are recovered by predictable or boundary mechanisms in the finite audit.

\paragraph{Excluded promotions.}
No translation realization follows from this chapter. No folded protein structure follows from amino-acid label readback. No physical admissibility, biological function, evolutionary mechanism, or universal genetic-code law follows from the local tile. Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact that directly observes the stronger layer. In the present record, the finite evidence remains exactly at the code-label and local-boundary level.
