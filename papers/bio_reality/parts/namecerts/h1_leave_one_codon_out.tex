\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Boundary Packet}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
This record studies the claim \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} at the code-read layer of the standard genetic code.  The object is the standard-code codon table, read only as a finite assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The coordinate carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with codon coordinates, local tile faces, singleton label corners, three-point label classes, closure data, spectral summaries, and table readback.  The question is whether a local window-six codon geometry gives a reproducible leave-one-codon-out signal while remaining confined to code-label evidence.

\paragraph{Finite codon region.}
The distinguished region $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The observed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that $R$ is a punctured-cube region with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite support is not merely a list of codons: it carries a small cubical boundary shape with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces.  The available table does not supply a complete codon list for $M$; it does supply the observed scalar size $|M|=20$ and the comparison statistics below, so $M$ is used here only at that reported resolution.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The finite spectral row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical polynomial check is at rounding scale rather than at visible macroscopic error.  In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The corresponding probabilities were $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$ and $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, found $0$ exact matches, $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $|M|=20$, and probabilities $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These values support the claim that the local finite geometry is not behaving like a typical sampled relabeling under the recorded nulls.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon result.}
For the codon-level omission test, the row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-closure-coverage codons, and $8$ predictable codons.  The recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the threshold comparison also recorded as $1.0$.  In finite-row terms, every tested omitted codon is recovered through the permitted local evidence classes; none requires a stronger biological reading.  The result therefore supports a code-layer boundary phenomenon: the table labels can be reconstructed locally in the tested leave-one-out sense, but the reconstruction remains a statement about codon labels and finite codon geometry.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module row partitions the thirteen codons without remainder.  The classes are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness counters are $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $\mathrm{assigned\ count}=13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The row also records that every module has a predictable-or-boundary representative.  This is the strongest finite support for the packet: the local boundary mechanism is not concentrated in one accidental codon family, because all six named modules are covered by the classification.

\paragraph{Cross-table comparison.}
The related leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level row has $5$ families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  These rows do not turn the codon packet into a biological law; they show that the same finite code-read signal survives several recorded table and family cuts.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot observe translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Therefore the correct conclusion is deliberately narrow: window-six coordinates expose a reproducible local label-boundary pattern in the supplied standard-code rows, and the curated table supplies the label readback needed to check it.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation process follows from these rows.  No protein fold, molecular admissibility condition, biochemical function, mechanism of code evolution, or universality theorem follows from them.  Amino-acid label readback is still only label readback.  Any later claim about translation, structure, physical viability, function, or general biological necessity would need its own independent observation at that layer.  Under the supplied finite data, the verdict is that \texttt{h1.leave.one.codon.out} is ready as a local code-read packet: it has a thirteen-codon punctured-cube support, complete module accounting, strong leave-one-codon recovery, nontrivial null comparisons, and a sharply bounded interpretation.
