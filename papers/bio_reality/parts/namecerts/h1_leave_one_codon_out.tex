\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record tests a window-six codon display on the codon cube $Q_6$.  The observed object is not a biochemical mechanism, but a finite labeled table: each codon is read as a six-bit coordinate and then compared with the curated standard-code assignment of amino-acid or stop labels.  The active exceptional row set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|R|=13$.  As a subcomplex of the codon cube, the same row set has the recorded face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Empirical contact and boundary.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, at codon-label resolution.  It supplies the mapping from codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  It does not observe translation dynamics, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Therefore the local tile split may be used as a code-layer readback test and as a boundary diagnostic, but no higher biological layer follows from this packet unless a distinct contact supplies the missing observation.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded proteins, CUN and UUR wording is not structural evidence, and a local codon-tile pattern is not a universal biological law.
