\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out recovery at a local codon boundary}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object, not as a mechanism of translation or as a structural model of proteins.  The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with codon-coordinate, closure, spectral, and relation data.  The relevant observable contact is a curated codon-to-label table for the standard genetic code.  In this setting the relation-side residue set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies the punctured-cube face count $f=(13,16,4)$ for this finite configuration.  Thus the local object is not a vague pattern in sequence space: it is a concrete finite subset of the codon cube with a specified boundary combinatorics.

\paragraph{Shape and spectral measurements.}
The shape comparison run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, indicating that the comparison was performed against a constrained finite population rather than against an unconstrained continuum.  Only $1$ exact shape match was observed, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  High-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jac}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The amino-acid-class-preserving null gave $0$ exact matches over $2000$ samples and only $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed $M$-size $20$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The spectral row reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  Its radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the polynomial check is numerically closed to the recorded tolerance.  These numbers support the narrower statement that the codon-coordinate construction exposes a stable local tile and boundary signature in the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out and family rows.}
The code-level leave-one-out experiment evaluated $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  Restricting to $M-T$ gave $0.9365079365079365$, and allowing the additional boundary region restored $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, $1.0$ for candidate-other, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These rows say that the coordinate boundary is not merely a single-table accident, but the interpretation remains at the level of codon-label readback.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row.}

In words, every tested missing codon in the recorded row is recovered through either predictable closure behavior or boundary placement, with no anchor codons counted in this particular finite test.

\paragraph{Local verdict and boundary.}
The ready claim is therefore a code-read claim: window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile structure whose missing-codon recovery is complete in the recorded finite row.  The contact used here is only the curated standard-code label table.  Nothing in this chapter establishes that the local tile is translation itself, a folded protein structure, a physical admissibility criterion, a biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Those stronger claims would require separate observations at the corresponding biological layer.  Amino-acid label readback is therefore retained as label readback, and the local codon tile is retained as a finite code-layer boundary result.
