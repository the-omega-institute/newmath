\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out recovery in a local codon tile}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object and scope.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer.  The finite object is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with the usual standard-code labels for amino acids and stop.  The experiment removes one codon at a time from the local tile under study and asks whether the remaining coordinate closure and boundary data recover the held-out codon as predictable or boundary-supported.  The evidence is therefore a finite label-readback experiment on curated codon assignments.  It is not an experiment on ribosomal translation, folded protein geometry, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, evolutionary mechanism, or universality across all code systems.

\paragraph{Distinguished codon row.}
The row singled out by the coordinate construction has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check reports $|R|=13$, and the punctured-cube check reports the same row with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a list of codons: it is also recognized as a finite local face pattern inside the six-bit display.  The local nature of this recognition is essential.  A face pattern in $Q_6$ may organize a codon-label table, but it does not by itself become a biological law.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The associated spectral probe gives the numerical values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, reported again as the radial polynomial value, so the numerical certificate is at floating-point zero for the intended tolerance.  These numbers provide a finite coordinate signature for the tile; they do not identify a biochemical energy, rate, or structural invariant.

\paragraph{Shape comparison against finite null samples.}
Two finite randomization checks record that the observed shape is atypical among the tested alternatives.  In the first check, $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$ produced one exact shape match after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact-shape frequency was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ gave $25$ matches, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed comparison used $|M|=20$.  The exact-match count was $0$, so the recorded exact-shape frequency was $0.0$.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite counts support the claim that the local codon tile has nonrandom shape under the tested shuffles.  They do not say that the geometry causes the standard genetic code or that the same pattern must appear in every biological context.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out result.}
For the codon-level holdout test itself, the run reports no anchor recoveries, five boundary recoveries, and eight high-coverage closure recoveries:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\ count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\ count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{closure\ coverage\ high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{predictable\ count}&=8.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the above-threshold recovery rate also equal to $1.0$.  Thus every tested codon in the distinguished row is accounted for by either predictable closure behavior or boundary placement.  The result is strongest when read exactly at this level: deleting one codon from the local standard-code tile does not destroy the finite coordinate signal needed to classify the missing site as an internal prediction or boundary member.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification covers all $13$ codons in $R$ with no extras, no missing entries, and no unassigned codons.  The finite partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\ count}=13$, while the lists of extra module codons, missing row codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The same record states that every module contains a predictable or boundary-supported member.  This gives a clean finite-row description of the local split: the distinguished codons are not scattered artifacts after module grouping, but remain organized by the codon-label classes used in the table readback.

\paragraph{Code-table comparison.}
A related leave-one-code-out comparison evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, whereas the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other groups, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These numbers are not the primary codon-holdout certificate, but they are consistent with the local boundary interpretation: the coordinate tile has strong code-label readback performance over the evaluated finite rows while still remaining a code-layer object.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, understood as a source of codon-to-label assignments.  It supplies the mapping from codons to amino-acid or stop labels and allows the local tile split to be tested against those labels.  It does not observe translation events, folded structures, admissible molecular states, organismal function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, a successful codon-label recovery is evidence about the organization of the code table, not evidence that an amino-acid string folds, functions, or is physically realized.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row conclusion is that the standard-code row $R$ forms a coherent local tile in $Q_6$: it has $13$ codons, punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, a stable spectral signature with $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, statistically rare shape matches in the tested null samples, complete module coverage, and full leave-one-codon-out recovery at the predictable-or-boundary level.  The claim stops there.  No statement about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality is licensed unless a separate empirical contact is added for that higher layer.
