\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Recovery in a Six-Bit Codon Window}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon window.}
This record studies the standard-code codon table after each codon is displayed as a point of the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The cube coordinate is used only as a finite organizing display: its vertices encode codon positions, its local faces encode small coordinate neighborhoods, and its labels are supplied by the curated standard genetic-code table.  Thus the object under test is not translation as a biochemical process, but code-layer readback from codons to amino-acid or stop labels.

The distinguished residual codon set has cardinality thirteen.  In the supplied finite row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the geometric support on which the leave-one-codon test is read.  Its interpretation is deliberately local: a missing codon may be recovered from the neighboring coordinate-and-label pattern, but that recovery is not a biological law for all organisms and is not evidence for protein structure.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The spectral summary separates the marked region $M$ from the residual region $R$ by finite numerical invariants.  The supplied computation gives

\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, reported also as the cubic-polynomial residual, so the displayed spectral relation is numerically closed to the stated tolerance.  The marked set has observed size $20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null comparison.  Its full codon list is not part of the supplied row, so the present chapter uses the available size, hit rates, and shape statistics rather than inventing an enumeration.

The shape test was run with $N=2000$ null samples and seed $42$.  Among $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ retries were duplicates.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-shape count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed marked-set size was $7$, and the corresponding lower-tail probability was $0.0035$.  These numbers support a finite local pattern at the code-label layer while leaving mechanism and physical realization outside the tested object.

$$

$$
\begin{aligned}

The recovery rate is therefore complete on the tested finite support:
$$
\begin{aligned}

The result should be read as follows.  When a codon is withheld from the finite standard-code display, the surrounding six-bit window and label neighborhood classify it either as predictable or as a boundary member.  The test does not say that codon reassignment is generated by cube geometry, nor that organisms implement the coordinate display as a causal device.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed content is a codon-to-label assignment for amino-acid and stop symbols.  That contact can test code-layer readback and local label splitting.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label sequences remain label sequences in this chapter.  A fold claim would require a separate structural contact; a function claim would require a separate functional contact; and an organism-level mechanism claim would require an organism-matched biological contact.

The local mismatch audit is therefore aligned at the code layer and blocked above it.  The code table supports the finite leave-one-codon recovery row, the residual thirteen-codon module partition, and the numerical shape and spectral summaries.  It does not supply evidence that DNA obeys the six-bit geometry as a necessity, that CUN/UUR wording carries structural information, that tRNA notation is a folded-structure observation, or that a codon-tile split determines protein behavior.  The ready conclusion is a bounded one: the six-coordinate codon display exposes a strong finite local pattern in standard-code labels, with complete recovery on the supplied leave-one-codon row, while every higher biological interpretation remains unavailable without an additional contact.
