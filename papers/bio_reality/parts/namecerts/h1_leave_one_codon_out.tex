\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Recovery in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed row.}
The finite object in this record is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the standard codon alphabet.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: it is a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The row tested here removes one codon from the finite code table and asks whether the remaining local tile, closure, and boundary information recover the withheld codon at the code-label layer.  For the reported run $\mathrm{e9e1a6e41633}$, the leave-one-codon row has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{predictable\_count} &= 8,\\
\mathrm{boundary\_count} &= 5,\\
\mathrm{anchor\_count} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high} &= 8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\_rate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every withheld codon in the tested row is recovered by either the predictable component or the boundary component, and none is recovered by a separate anchor class.

This is the local tile whose boundary behavior is being tested.  The statement is not that $R$ is a law of molecular biology; it is that this finite codon subset has a stable combinatorial profile under the supplied coordinate system and under the curated label table used for readback.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model checks.}
The finite spectrum associated with the row is numerically sharp.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, so the reported polynomial residual is effectively zero at the precision of the experiment.  Against the shape null model with $N=2000$ trials and seed $42$, the observed exact-shape count is $1$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampling log is also informative: $57377$ distinct-sample attempts were needed, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed module size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the finite code-layer separation: the tile is not a generic random remnant of the codon table under the nulls recorded in the packet.

Each listed module has at least one predictable or boundary recovery route.  This is the finite reason the leave-one-codon result is row-specific rather than a free-form narrative about the genetic code.

\paragraph{Scientific interpretation.}
The finite conclusion is restricted to code readback.  Window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile structure in the curated standard-code table, and the withheld-codon row is fully recovered in the reported experiment.  The recovery rate $1.0$, the split $8+5+0$, the complete module accounting over $R$, and the low null-model tail probabilities jointly support a finite code-layer pattern.  They do not establish that translation machinery realizes this geometry, that an amino-acid label sequence folds, that any physical conformation is admissible, that any biological function follows, that the pattern is a mechanism, or that it is universal across life.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact that directly observes translation, structure, physical constraint, function, mechanism, or cross-organism universality.  In this chapter the only admitted contact is the curated code-label table, so the claim stops at local codon-label recovery inside $Q_6$.
