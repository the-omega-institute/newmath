\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out recovery as a local codon-tile boundary}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The claim is evaluated on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon treated as a finite display row rather than as a biochemical mechanism. The row distinguishes the coordinate position, the closure test induced by nearby codon labels, the associated spectrum, and the relation between singleton-label corners and three-point label classes. The biological contact is only the curated standard-code table: it assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels and thereby supports code-layer readback. It does not observe translation in a cell, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, realized function, or a universal biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Recovered boundary set.}
The finite exceptional set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly the punctured-cube row reported by the computation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row has face-count data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the relevant object is not merely a list of codons but a small boundary-shaped subcomplex inside the codon cube. The leave-one-codon-out test reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, and $0$ anchor cases. Its recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$. Thus every withheld codon in the tested row is recovered either by direct predictability or by landing on the explicit boundary class, with no appeal to an additional hidden anchor.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, there are no missing codons from the modules, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},&
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},&
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},&
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},&
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has either a predictable codon or a boundary codon. This is the chapter-specific content of the claim: the test is not saying that the codon table is generated by geometry alone; it says that the finite coordinate display exposes a local split that remains visible when one codon is withheld.

\paragraph{Spectral and null comparisons.}
The spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the radial polynomial check, so the numerical spectrum is internally coherent at the stated tolerance. The shape comparison used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. Among those samples there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ high-Jaccard matches with threshold $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampling run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, which is relevant because it shows that the comparison is finite and enumerative rather than asymptotic. The amino-acid-class-preserving null also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$; it had $0$ exact matches, lower-tail count $7$, observed $M$ size $20$, exact-shape value $0.0$, and lower-tail value $0.0035$.

\paragraph{Relation to code-level leave-one-out evidence.}
The codon-level row is consistent with the broader code-table row. Across $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ targets, the baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the above-baseline rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast. These rows support a code-read pattern with family variation, not a claim that all organisms instantiate one global law.

\paragraph{Local boundary conclusion.}
The finite evidence supports the following restricted conclusion. In the standard-code contact, the six-coordinate codon display detects a leave-one-codon-out pattern in which the thirteen-codon set $R$ is fully classified by predictable or boundary modules, and the observed recovery rate is $1.0$. The curated table supplies the labels needed to test this statement, while the coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation rows supply the internal finite checks. Nothing in this evidence converts the local tile into translation realization, folded structure, physical possibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Any such stronger conclusion would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer.
