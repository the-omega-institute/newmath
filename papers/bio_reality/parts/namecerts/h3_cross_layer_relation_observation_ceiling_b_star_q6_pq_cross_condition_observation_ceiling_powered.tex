\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life Ceiling for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rbp-decay-ceiling}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This chapter records a bounded BioReality claim for synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  The carrier is a finite matched panel whose rows contain a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, the same-window amino-acid composition context, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair controls, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, perturbation state, rescue state, and batch labels.  The verified finite run attached to this record is keyed by experiment run \texttt{8f258b58b625}; it was evaluated for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, started at \texttt{2026-06-29T13:29:24+00:00}, completed at \texttt{2026-06-29T13:37:41+00:00}, and reports a runtime of $497.03086400032043$ seconds.  These numbers identify the finite computational observation surface for this record; they do not by themselves supply the missing same-window RBP occupancy and perturbation rows.

\paragraph{Claim tested.}
The intended observation is not that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ geometry predicts RNA half-life on its own.  The admissible claim is narrower: after amino-acid composition and same-row translation-readout controls, a coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference may be read as a bounded cross-layer relation only when it tracks measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, survives coordinate/occupancy decoupling in the required direction, collapses after target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and is not restored by a binding-defective rescue or a non-target control.  The finite statistic therefore has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi &= \Phi(q, T, H, O, P, Z),\\
q &\in \{\text{high }B^{*}_{Q6}\text{ coordinate},\ \text{low }B^{*}_{Q6}\text{ coordinate}\},\\
T &= \text{same-row translation readout},\\
H &= \text{RNA half-life readout},\\
O &= \text{measured target-RBP occupancy},\\
P &= \text{wild type, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target control},\\
Z &= \text{transcription, RNA structure, motif density, composition, codon-pair, isoform, abundance, and batch controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive observation requires the coordinate contrast in $H$ to be explained through $O$ and $P$ after conditioning on $T$ and $Z$.  If $H$ changes while $O$ does not change under coordinate/occupancy decoupling, the RBP-decay interpretation is rejected.  If target-RBP loss does not remove the coordinate-associated half-life effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it, the mechanism claim is rejected.  If binding-defective rescue, non-target control, RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, splice or isoform state, GC content, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, or batch reproduces the pattern, the positive bridge is rejected.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null model is row exchangeability after the declared controls.  High-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows are treated as exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life once transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, same-row translation readout, and batch have been conditioned away.  In symbols, the permitted comparison is only
$$
\begin{aligned}
H \perp q \mid T, O, P, Z
\end{aligned}
$$
under the matched null, except where the finite panel shows the prespecified occupancy-and-perturbation chain.  The current verified run supplies the organism, run key, timestamps, and runtime for the observation ceiling, but the supplied finite-row payload does not include row counts, $R$ lists, $M$ lists, lambda values, $p$-values, hit rate, module labels, or the actual same-window occupancy and perturbation/rescue table.  Consequently this chapter states the observation ceiling and the required statistic rather than a numerical positive RBP-decay result.

\paragraph{Contacts used.}
The RNA half-life contact gives transcript or gene rows with curated half-life estimates or normalized consensus half-life readouts and dataset provenance.  It can bound an RNA half-life surface, but it cannot measure decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target behavior, translation realization, RNA decay mechanism, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a global biological rule.  The ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact can supply per-CDS ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, usually as $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$, but only after identifier-matched ribosome and mRNA rows exist in the same experimental condition.  The matched mRNA abundance contact is a transcription-layer control for that residualization and is not a translation readout by itself.  These contacts must be joined on the same organism and the same row granularity before the statistic above is meaningful.

\paragraph{Observation ceiling.}
The local ceiling is therefore strict.  The finite record may support at most a bounded relation among $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate label, measured target-RBP occupancy, perturbation/rescue state, same-row translation readout, and RNA half-life.  It does not license a statement about protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the tested RBP-decay bridge, or universality.  Coordinate geometry, codon-window labels, contact names, and workflow readiness are not biological measurements.  A positive RNA half-life association without measured occupancy is only an association.  A positive occupancy association without decoupling is only a correlation.  A perturbation effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target controls is not a bounded decay-factor RBP bridge.  The verified observation surface for \texttt{8f258b58b625} is therefore classified as a powered observation ceiling for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}: it fixes the finite organism-scoped run and the admissible control logic, while withholding promotion until the missing same-window RBP and perturbation rows are present.
