\subsection{A wobble-broken translation boundary for the $B^*_{Q6}$ third residual axis}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-wobble-broken-translation-readout}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record concerns the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $f_3$-stress direction and to translation-layer readouts that remain paired with the same organism or coding-sequence rows. The underlying finite codon surface is not empty or informal: the verified $f_3$ saturation row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the saturated list has $18$ entries inside a $20$-codon marked set, and the recorded $f_3$ boundary list is empty. The finite row therefore supports a sharply delimited coordinate question: after amino-acid quotienting, does the $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance survive as a translation-readout distinction only when a context tRNA or wobble surface separates a codon $c$ from its $f_3$ mate $c^{f_3}$ by unequal weights $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$?

\paragraph{Observed residual geometry.}
The powered third-axis mechanism run $\mathrm{d0893337f0dd}$ passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. Its recorded conclusion is that the third residual axis is selection-associated rather than GC-driven, is not explained as a wobble cluster, and still needs an external contact for any CSC or positional identity. The reason attached to the finite row is that the GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ direction keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. A separate identity row, run $\mathrm{377671f96e96}$, also used $5$ folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns; it identifies $d_{\perp}$ provisionally with usage frequency within synonymous families, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. These numbers justify treating the third direction as a finite residual axis worth testing at the translation boundary, but they do not by themselves make it a measured translation effect.

\paragraph{Mechanistic pressure before translation promotion.}
The surrounding finite rows constrain the plausible route. The two-axis closure test reports a present third axis because $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms, again on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns. The residual optimality row names $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant non-tRNA residual axis under the same $11/16$ organism and $41$-column design. The axis-strength spectrum row states that captured spectra are led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, with per-organism dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$; its descriptive domain counts are imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms, and the largest GC3 rank effect is only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$. In parallel, the tRNA-poverty boundary row covers $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, uses $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and records that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb copy numbers with dos Reis wobble weights. Taken together, these rows make a concrete finite hypothesis: the $f_3$-stress residual should become dynamically visible only where the translation-weight surface breaks the third-position pairing, rather than where the codon topology alone is merely present.

\paragraph{Bounded readout.}
The admissible readout for this chapter is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a bounded translation-layer row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The intended estimation must condition on amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance when translation efficiency is used, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon baselines. In symbolic form, the local support and failure tests are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{support} &:\quad \exists j\; S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0\quad \text{under the declared controls and baseline ablation},\\
\mathrm{failure} &:\quad \forall j\; S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}\le 0\quad \text{or the fitted surface is rank-degenerate.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The current finite rows establish a selection-not-GC residual geometry and a wobble/tRNA supply pressure, but they do not yet contain same-organism and same-CDS joined codon, wobble-weight, ribosome or translation-efficiency, and mRNA-control rows for this exact $f_3$-stress statistic. A three-organism all-$1.0$ controlled surface must therefore be read as a rank boundary rather than as survival.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No statement in this record promotes the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate to protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universality. Codon usage, tRNA copy number, and modeled tAI or stAI weights may bound a translation-layer test, but ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency requires its own organism-matched measurement row, and any downstream protein, localization, stability, function, or phenotype interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact after a non-vacuous positive translation-readout step. Thus the chapter-specific claim is narrow: $f_3$-stress is a finite residual candidate for translation-readout survival under wobble-broken third-position symmetry, and it is falsified locally if the controlled $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ entries remain non-positive or rank-degenerate across a powered panel.
