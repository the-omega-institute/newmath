\subsection{The third residual axis is selection-retaining but not yet a closed translation readout}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-third-axis-mechanism}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The claim concerns a finite codon-residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection, with attention restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ third-position stress coordinate and to translation-facing rows that can be matched by organism. The $f_3$ saturation record fixes the finite codon boundary used by this chapter: the ambient set $M = \operatorname{Med}(R) \cup ACA$ contains
$$\begin{aligned}
&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG,
\end{aligned}$$
while $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ removes $ACA$ and $ACG$ and leaves
$$\begin{aligned}
&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG.
\end{aligned}$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty, so the relevant stress coordinate is not introduced by an unresolved codon-edge exception. It is a residual contrast left after quotienting by amino acid and then asking whether third-position pairing remains visible to a translation readout.

\paragraph{What the powered computation actually establishes.}
The directly attached computation for this packet is the run $d0893337f0dd$ of the experiment $b\_star\_q6\_third\_axis\_mechanism\_powered$. It used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous-design columns. Its recorded status is passed. The finite conclusion is: selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC or positional identity still needs an additional contact. The stated reason is that the GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ coordinate keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.

This should be read narrowly. The result says that the third residual direction is not erased by GC correction in the powered organism panel. It also says that the surviving direction should not be collapsed to a simple wobble-cluster explanation from this computation alone. The companion identity run $377671f96e96$ reported $d_{\perp}$ as provisionally aligned to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p = 0.000488$, again on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous-design columns. Thus the row-level picture is a finite residual-selection signal: the third axis is real enough to survive the controlled codon-usage geometry, but the biological channel that realizes it is not closed by the codon geometry itself.

\paragraph{Relation to the larger $Q6$ axis panel.}
The surrounding powered records make this interpretation more specific. The two-axis closure run reported a third-axis-present outcome: $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms. The residual-optimality run identified the dominant non-tRNA residual direction as $f_3$ translation selection. The per-organism spectrum record computed $11$ organisms, used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, and found captured spectra led on average by $d_{resid4}$, then $d_{tRNA}$; all $11$ per-organism dominant counts were assigned to $d_{resid4}$. These records support the local claim that $d_{\perp}$ is not a numerical accident of a single organism or a pure GC artifact.

The same panel also keeps the interpretation bounded. A separate boundary-set run over $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, with $1000$ permutations at $\alpha = 0.05$, found that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy number and dos Reis wobble weights. That supports a tRNA-supply explanation for $R$ avoidance, but it does not by itself identify the third residual direction with a measured ribosome, translation-efficiency, protein-abundance, localization, stability, or functional row. The present chapter therefore treats tRNA and wobble asymmetry as candidate translation-facing covariates, not as completed evidence of downstream realization.

\paragraph{Break condition and positive-contact condition.}
For this packet, the admissible positive row has the form $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} > 0$ after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where translation efficiency is used, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon baselines. The readout $T_j$ may be a bounded tAI or stAI surface, or a measured ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency surface, but the status differs across these choices. A modeled adaptation weight is only a bounded codon-adaptation readout. A ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact would be stronger only after same-organism and same-CDS rows are joined to codon counts, wobble or tRNA weights, and matched mRNA controls.

The falsifying finite-row outcome is equally explicit. If every controlled $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ entry is non-positive, or if the fitted surface is rank-degenerate, then the conjecture fails as a translation-readout distinction even though the internal codon-residual coordinate may remain well defined. In particular, an all-$1.0$ matrix from a three-organism rank-saturated fit is a null boundary, not survival evidence.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
The chapter does not infer translation realization from codon topology alone. It also does not infer protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality from the residual coordinate, from GC-orthogonalized alignment, from tRNA gene-copy summaries, or from tAI or stAI weights. Those statements require their own layer-matched contacts and their own controlled finite statistics. The present result is therefore a bounded cross-layer target: the third residual axis survives as selection-retaining and GC-resistant in the powered codon panel, while its CSC, positional, ribosome, or translation-efficiency realization remains open until a nondegenerate same-row translation statistic is supplied.
