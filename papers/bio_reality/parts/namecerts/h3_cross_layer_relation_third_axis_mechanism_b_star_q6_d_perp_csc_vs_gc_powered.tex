\subsection{Third-axis mechanism boundary for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-third-axis-mechanism-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The record considered here is the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate surface after amino-acid projection, restricted to the third-position $f_3$-stress direction and to the finite rows for which codon usage, tRNA or wobble annotations, and candidate translation readouts can in principle be matched. The first finite combinatorial support is the saturated $f_3$ row. In the verified saturation run, the marked set $M = \mathrm{Med}(R) \cup \mathrm{ACA}$ contains the twenty codons
$$\begin{aligned}
&M=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\qquad\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}$$
while the saturated $f_3$ image removes $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and leaves the eighteen-codon row
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same run reports an empty $f_3$ boundary, so the coordinate is not being introduced as an unsaturated edge residue. Its biological question is narrower: once synonymous imbalance has been quotiented by amino-acid identity, does the remaining $f_3$-paired contrast have any controlled translation-layer readback when third-position symmetry is broken by context tRNA or wobble weights?

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence.}
The powered third-axis mechanism run used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested, and used $41$ synonymous-design columns. Its recorded conclusion is selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, with the CSC or positional identity still requiring an additional reality contact. The finite reason is that the GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ coordinate retained significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. This makes the mechanism boundary sharper than a GC3 explanation: the third axis is not erased by orthogonalizing against GC, but the run also does not identify the axis as a direct codon-stabilization, ribosome, or positional-footprint mechanism.

The companion identity run gives the current numerical orientation of the same axis. On the same $5$-fold, $11$-organism, $41$-column design, $d_{\perp}$ is provisionally aligned to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The universal residual-axis run classifies the non-tRNA residual optimality as most aligned with $f_3$ translation selection, and the two-axis closure run reports that a third axis is present because $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms. The sufficiency run then records that the three-axis model is still not the whole story: the conclusion is fourth-mechanism-present, again on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous columns.

\paragraph{Axis spectrum and mechanism contrast.}
The per-organism axis spectrum gives the local geometry in which this chapter should be read. Across the computed organisms, the captured spectra are led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; the per-organism dominant-count table assigns all $11$ computed organisms to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ as the dominant axis. The domain split is descriptive rather than inferential because the panel is imbalanced, with $n=1$ archaeal organism, $n=2$ bacterial organisms, and $n=8$ eukaryotic organisms. The largest GC3 rank effect reported in that spectrum is $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$, which is small enough that the chapter should not recast the third-axis result as a GC-only phenomenon. The separate tRNA-poverty run also matters locally: for the $R$ sense-codon mechanism test, $40$ organisms were computed out of $46$ requested, $6$ were skipped, the permutation count was $1000$, and the decision threshold was $\alpha=0.05$. That run supports the statement that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb copy number plus dos Reis wobble weights. It supplies a plausible supply-side asymmetry, but it is not by itself a measured ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency row.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
The bounded readback for this chapter is the controlled survival entry $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ for a translation-layer readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The support condition is not merely that codon topology is nontrivial, nor that a modeled adaptation weight can be computed. A positive readback requires a finite joined panel in which the $f_3$-stress coordinate, organism identity, codon counts, wobble or tRNA weights, translation readout, matched mRNA control, amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, and $M$-density baseline are all present on the same rows, followed by a controlled statistic that remains positive after simpler codon baselines are removed. In symbols, the permitted local target has the form
$$\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j} > 0
\quad\text{after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, and mRNA.}
\end{aligned}$$
The current finite record does not contain that same-row measured translation matrix. It contains a powered residual-axis and mechanism boundary, plus external contact routes for codon usage, tRNA copy number, tAI or stAI weights, ribosome profiling or translation efficiency, and matched mRNA abundance. Therefore the correct status is a mechanism-constrained readiness statement: the third axis is finite, GC-orthogonalized, and aligned with a usage-frequency residual, but the translation survival entry remains conditional on the missing joined readout.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter therefore refuses every downstream promotion that would bypass the translation row. The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate, the empty $f_3$ boundary, the eighteen-codon saturated row, the $0.128$ mean cosine, the $p=0.000488$ sign-test result, the $11$-organism powered mechanism run, and the $40$-organism tRNA-poverty permutation run do not by themselves establish translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism in the biochemical sense, or a universal biological law. A modeled tAI or stAI surface may bound a codon-adaptation readout; it is not an observed ribosome-footprint measurement. Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency may test the next boundary only after same-row joining and control adjustment. Protein, structure, localization, stability, function, and phenotype require their own layer-matched contacts and cannot be inferred from this residual coordinate alone.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following self-contained claim. The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ third-axis mechanism record is not explained away by GC3 and is not currently resolved as a wobble-cluster identity; it is best read as a GC-orthogonal, selection-associated residual axis whose provisional orientation is usage frequency within synonymous family. The $f_3$-stress version of the claim becomes a translation-layer claim only where context tRNA or wobble asymmetry breaks the third-position pairing and a controlled $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ row survives. Until that row exists, the scientifically meaningful conclusion is a bounded mechanism boundary, not a downstream biological assertion.
