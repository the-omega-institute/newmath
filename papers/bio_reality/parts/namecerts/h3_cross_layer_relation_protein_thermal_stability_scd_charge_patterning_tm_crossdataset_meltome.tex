\subsection{A position-resolved translation boundary for the $f_3$ residual coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-position-resolved-translation-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The record considered here is not a protein-stability theorem, even though the surrounding audit table contains a thermal-stability contact.  Its biological object is the $f_3$-stress residual coordinate in coding sequence windows, tested only against a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout.  The finite carrier is a table whose rows are keyed by organism, coding sequence, codon index modulo $3$, local paired codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight surface, matched mRNA abundance, and a position-indexed ribosome-footprint response.  The admissible response is therefore local and translational: it is not steady-state protein abundance, folding, function, phenotype, or a global law of coding sequences.

\paragraph{Coordinate source.}
The internal coordinate is constrained by two finite combinatorial audits.  The saturated $f_3$ row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
M ={}& \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},

$$
and the $f_3$ saturation after removing the two ACA/ACG boundary elements records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) ={}& \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.

The reported $f_3$ boundary list is empty.  The quotient audit gives an $M$ face vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, a quotient face vector $(10,14,6,1)$, and $|\pi_M|=10$.  Thus the coordinate is finite and non-arbitrary, but these numbers are still code-geometry numbers.  They do not observe translation.

\paragraph{Required statistic.}
A positive claim requires a same-row residual statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_{\mathrm{pos}}$ is a codon- or window-indexed ribosome-occupancy readout, and $Z$ includes organism, amino-acid composition or amino-acid bin, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, positional coverage, mappability, and simpler codon-count baselines.  The statistic must be estimated only after the joined matrix has enough rank to test the residual term.  If the matrix is rank-degenerate, if the coefficient is non-positive after controls, or if a simpler baseline absorbs the signal, the $f_3$ coordinate remains a residual description of codon topology rather than a translation readout.

\paragraph{Existing finite contacts.}
The nearest positive finite rows in the present audit are adjacent contacts, not this statistic.  The thermal-stability Meltome row reports $n=8092$, runtime $512.940125$ seconds, and UniProt sequence match rate $0.963104$ for the charge-patterning thermal-stability surface.  That establishes a finite protein-level audit row for a different readout class; it does not provide codon-indexed ribosome footprints for the $f_3$ window statistic.  The positional structural-order row reports a ramp-positive but not localized conclusion.  At primary ramp length $50$, the weighted $f_3$ ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, the body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the paired ramp-minus-body increment is $0.001378137149572267$.  Its sign-flip probabilities are $0.0478515625$ for the ramp, $0.54296875$ for the body, and $0.05859375$ for the paired ramp-body contrast.  This is useful as a warning: even a positive local residual can fail the sharper localization gate.

\paragraph{Cross-check rows.}
Other cross-layer audits show that finite biological joins can be large enough to support a concrete readout when the response layer is actually present.  The alternative-polyadenylation row contains $24740$ genes and $296950$ PAS entries.  The ESE exon-inclusion row contains $n=1037$ with collinearity $-0.008429245619$ between $\Delta$ESEseq and $\Delta$MaxEnt.  The mouse VASTDB splice row contains $50633$ rows and reports all tissue signs positive.  These examples are not evidence for the $f_3$ translation boundary; they only calibrate what a populated finite-row contact looks like.  By contrast, the present $f_3$ translation packet still lacks the same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon, wobble-weight, mRNA-control, and position-resolved footprint matrix.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter therefore licenses only a bounded formulation: after amino-acid projection and baseline controls, the $f_3$ residual coordinate may be tested as a predictor of position-resolved ribosome occupancy in matched coding-sequence windows.  No translation realization follows from the coordinate itself.  No protein abundance, folding, thermal stability, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant rows and a controlled statistic survives.  In particular, modeled tAI or stAI weights may enter as bounded annotations, and matched mRNA abundance may enter as a control, but neither is an observed ribosome-occupancy matrix by itself.  The break condition is simple: whenever review status, contact naming, wobble-weight asymmetry, $S^{QT}$ notation, or codon-window geometry is treated as the missing joined statistic, the claim must be returned to null scope.
