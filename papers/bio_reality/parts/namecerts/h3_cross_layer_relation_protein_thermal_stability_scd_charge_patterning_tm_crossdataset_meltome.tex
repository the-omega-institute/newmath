\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record isolates a protein-thermal-stability contact in which the measured biological row is a cross-dataset Meltome melting-temperature table joined to UniProt sequences.  The finite run attached to the contact contains $8092$ sequence-level rows, ran in $512.940125$ seconds, and retained a UniProt sequence match rate of $0.963104$.  The finite coordinate being tested at this layer is not an abstract protein-function claim: it is a sequence-derived charge-pattern statistic, such as sequence charge decoration, compared with a measured thermal-stability readout.  The name certificate is therefore scoped to a row table of matched protein sequence and melting-temperature entries.  It does not license any inference from codon geometry alone to thermal stability, folded structure, physical realizability, molecular function, translation mechanism, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Layer distinction and the thermal-stability row.}
The protein thermal-stability fact is the Meltome-level row table, not the lower-layer translation or codon readout.  The admissible reading is: among $8092$ matched proteins, charge-patterning coordinates can be evaluated against a measured melting-temperature surface, with sequence matching high enough to make the row join nontrivial.  The contact remains bounded by its own row keys and controls.  Any statement about translation requires a separate translation readout such as position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency residuals; any statement about folding mechanism, physical admissibility, function, or phenotype requires its own layer-matched contact rather than the existence of a Meltome association.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
For this chapter, the admissible certificate is a finite cross-layer stability contact: sequence charge patterning is tested against matched thermal-stability measurements over $8092$ proteins with a $0.963104$ UniProt sequence match rate.  The contact may be used as a protein-stability readout boundary for the specified row table.  It may not be used to claim that $f_3$-stress, wobble weights, codon-window topology, or any translation-readout coordinate causes thermal stability.  A downstream promotion would require a new finite panel joining the relevant codon or translation coordinate to the same protein identifiers, together with matched mRNA controls, measured translation rows where translation is invoked, rank sufficiency, baseline ablation, and a separately declared statistic.  Without that extra panel, the present row table remains a thermal-stability contact only.
