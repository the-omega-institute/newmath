\subsection{Yeast structure-conditioned mediation of $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residuals}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-yeast-structure-mediation}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record fixes a finite yeast surface whose rows are matched coding-sequence, translation, mRNA-stability, local-structure, and protein-abundance entries. The joined table contains $4759$ genes after requiring a real CDS row, a matched protein-abundance row, a measured translation-efficiency row, an mRNA half-life row, and a 5-prime coding-region structure row. The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the five-way join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$. The structure coordinate is the ViennaRNA minimum free energy of the first $39$ nt of the coding region, keyed by yeast locus tag. Translation efficiency is the mean footprint normalized density divided by mean mRNA normalized density across two rich-medium replicates from the Ingolia yeast data surface, and mRNA stability is joined through $5379$ valid systematic-ORF half-life records.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control design.}
The $Q$ block has nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control block $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $Q$ vectors. After residualizing against $Z$, the rank diagnostics report $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde S)=1$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T\widetilde M)=2$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T\widetilde M\widetilde S)=3$, and residual degrees of freedom $4736$. The corresponding residual energies are $871992.3819246667$ for mRNA stability, $28554.997251412347$ for the structure readout, $645.3764418081507$ for translation efficiency, and $1724.6664873071313$ for protein abundance.

\paragraph{Variance partition.}
Let $P_e,Q_e,S_e,T_e,M_e$ denote the $Z$-residualized protein abundance, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block, structure proxy, translation-efficiency readout, and mRNA-stability readout. The reported quantities are ordinary finite-row regression energies and incremental $R^2$ values, not causal effects. The total $Q$ to protein-abundance residual association is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(Q_e\to P_e)&=0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2(Q_e\to P_e\mid S_e)&=0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2(Q_e\to P_e\mid T_e,M_e)&=0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2(Q_e\to P_e\mid T_e,M_e,S_e)&=0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the structure-only attenuation fraction is $0.003284564452662019$, while the additional structure attenuation after translation efficiency and mRNA stability are already present is $0.0005507778820193332$. The structure association of the $Q$ block itself is small but nonzero: $R^2(S_e\sim Q_e)=0.003774248455422586$, with right-tail permutation value $0.027972027972027972$. The incremental structure fraction after $T_e$ and $M_e$ has right-tail permutation value $0.1258741258741259$, so the finite surface supports a weak structure-associated residual component but not a strong independent structure-mediated channel beyond measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated abundance surface.}
A broader matched-protein surface with modeled per-protein tAI supplies a comparison point: in yeast, the joint nine-coordinate $Q$ block has $R^2(Q\to P)=0.29837624994140827$, direct $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.0151709093608293$, indirect difference $0.28320534058057895$, and mediation fraction $0.9491551041216973$ when the translation readout is modeled per-protein tAI. Across the twelve-organism panel, the same statistic ranges from $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. This comparison explains why the yeast structure row is kept as a conditioning and decomposition surface rather than as an independent route to protein realization: the translation-attached protein-abundance signal is much larger than the isolated 5-prime MFE attenuation in the five-way yeast table.

\paragraph{Coordinate identity and supporting audits.}
The structural-order audit identifies $f3\_stress$ as the only fold-level sign-flip-passing coordinate, with weighted held-out $R^2$ increment $0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip value $0.03125$. Other coordinates have weaker sign-flip values, for example $Arg\_AGR$ at $0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ at $0.0693359375$, and $K\_AAA$ at $0.2294921875$. A separate observable-execution decomposition on yeast reports $3289$ genes and the conclusion that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passes both effective null classes. In contrast, a dynamic mass-balance closure audit on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals reports $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, while a turnover residual audit on $3290$ genes reports $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$ and $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits. These companion rows keep the chapter's interpretation narrow: protein abundance and rate-proxy contact exist, but turnover and dynamic mass-balance promotion are not supported by the listed finite rows.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The admissible reading is a controlled yeast variance decomposition: $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals retain a substantial association with matched protein abundance after amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, and $M$-density controls; measured translation efficiency plus mRNA stability absorb a large part of that association; the added 5-prime CDS MFE term contributes only a very small further attenuation. No statement of translation mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mediation, or universality follows from these rows. Such claims would require a separate layer-matched measurement and a separate finite statistic at that layer. The present record therefore enters the BioReality paper as a yeast-specific, controlled cross-layer decomposition, not as a claim that synonymous residual geometry by itself realizes protein structure or biological function.
