\subsection{Yeast abundance residuals with translation and local CDS-structure conditioning}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-yeast-structure-mediation}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} This record tests a finite yeast surface whose rows join coding-sequence composition, B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, measured translation efficiency, mRNA half-life, a local coding-region structure proxy, and matched protein abundance. The usable join contains $4759$ genes. The reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$, and the five-way join hit rate is $0.8818572656921754$. The input surfaces are not merely symbolic: $5815$ CDS rows were joined before the final filters, $5128$ genes had translation-efficiency records keyed by protein identifier, $6027$ genes had valid local structure MFE records, and $5379$ mRNA half-life records were valid by systematic ORF. The main exclusions on the CDS side were $687$ rows without TE match and $369$ rows without mRNA half-life match; no rows were lost for missing structure, nonpositive abundance, nonpositive TE, nonpositive mRNA half-life, empty codon counts, duplicate stable identifiers, or invalid length.

\paragraph{Coordinates and controls.} The residual block $Q$ has nine coordinates:
$$\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The control matrix $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an M-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. The finite-rank audit reports $24$ control columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q)=9$, and $4736$ residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ residualization. The response residual energies are $28554.997251412347$ for the structure proxy $S$, $645.3764418081507$ for measured TE $T$, $871992.3819246667$ for mRNA stability $M$, and $1724.6664873071313$ for protein abundance $P$.

\paragraph{Readout definitions.} The translation readout is measured TE from the Ingolia et al. 2009 yeast rich-medium ribosome-profiling study, defined as mean footprint normalized read density across two replicates divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across two replicates. The mRNA-stability readout is a matched half-life surface. The structure readout is the ViennaRNA RNAfold minimum free energy of the first $39$ nucleotides of the CDS, keyed by locus tag and therefore still a CDS-only proxy. The protein readout is matched protein abundance on the same yeast ORF surface. Thus the tested pattern is not a free association $Q\to P$; it is a controlled variance decomposition in which $Q$ is examined after amino-acid composition, length, GC3, M-density, TE, mRNA stability, and the local MFE proxy are made explicit.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance residual fit.} After residualization by $Z$, the joint B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block explains a substantial fraction of the matched abundance residual:
$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}$$
The direct improvement energy of $Q$ for $P$ is $466.79849101702615$ after conditioning on $S$, $237.5550048307909$ after conditioning on $T$ and $M$, and $237.29705529470186$ after conditioning on $T$, $M$, and $S$ together. The corresponding finite ranks are $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde S)=1$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T\widetilde M)=2$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T\widetilde M\widetilde S)=3$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q\widetilde S)=10$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q\widetilde T\widetilde M)=11$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q\widetilde T\widetilde M\widetilde S)=12$. These ranks keep the reported decomposition in a nondegenerate finite linear surface rather than in a saturated memorization regime.

\paragraph{Structure component.} The structure-only fraction is small:
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid S}}{R^2_{QP}} &= 0.003284564452662019,\\
\frac{R^2_{QP\mid T,M}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}}{R^2_{QP}} &= 0.0005507778820193332,\\
R^2_{QS} &= 0.003774248455422586.
\end{aligned}$$
The right-tail permutation value for the $Q\to S$ structure association is $0.027972027972027972$, whereas the right-tail permutation value for the structure increment beyond TE and mRNA stability is $0.1258741258741259$. The finite-row verdict is therefore asymmetric: B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ carries a detectable but tiny association with the local CDS MFE proxy, while the extra structure-mediated fraction left after measured TE and mRNA stability is not strong on this surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader Q6 abundance surface.} A separate cross-organism protein-abundance mediation audit reports positive statistical mediated fractions through modeled per-protein tAI for twelve organisms, including $0.9491551041216973$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.612922252793667$ in Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.6165167748739756$ in Drosophila melanogaster, $0.4872268357227724$ in Caenorhabditis elegans, and $0.27206289160119157$ in Homo sapiens. For yeast in that audit, $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$, and the indirect difference is $0.28320534058057895$. The present yeast structure record is narrower: it asks whether a CDS-only MFE proxy accounts for residual abundance association beyond measured TE and mRNA stability. It does not replace the translation-mediated abundance record; it separates a local structure proxy from translation and transcript-stability readouts on a joined yeast panel.

\paragraph{Axis audit.} The same nine-coordinate family has an independent structural-order axis result in which $f3\_stress$ is the only coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate. Its weighted held-out $R^2$ increment is $0.0031744502294756994$, its right-tail sign-flip value is $0.03125$, and its weighted absolute unit loading is $0.5109586606724208$. The other coordinate increments are smaller or less stable: for example $Arg\_AGR$ has $0.0020139270616115154$ with sign-flip value $0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ has $0.0013067592718559024$ with sign-flip value $0.0693359375$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ is negative at $-0.00045340330387383447$. This supports using the nine-coordinate residual block as a finite structured object, but it does not license a single-coordinate biological law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.} The yeast data support a controlled abundance-residual decomposition with a large joint B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ association to protein abundance, a major attenuation when measured TE and mRNA stability are conditioned, and a much smaller local CDS-MFE component. Numerically, conditioning on the MFE proxy alone changes $R^2_{QP}$ from $0.2715520808117803$ to $0.2706601505000995$, whereas conditioning on TE and mRNA stability changes it to $0.13773967696311287$. Adding the MFE proxy after TE and mRNA stability changes it only to $0.13759011208318542$. The appropriate readback is therefore a finite variance-partition result over $Q$, $T$, $M$, $S$, and $P$, not a causal pathway claim.

\paragraph{Boundary.} No claim of translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality follows from this chapter by itself. The MFE variable is a sequence-derived first-$39$-nt CDS proxy, not a measured folding ensemble or a translation-initiation assay. The TE variable is measured from one yeast ribosome-profiling study, and the protein abundance variable remains a steady-state abundance readout affected by turnover, localization, post-translational state, and measurement bias. Any stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic showing that the same residual coordinate survives the appropriate readout under the same controls.
