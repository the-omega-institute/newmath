\subsection{Yeast abundance residuals conditioned by translation and local coding-region structure}
\label{subsec:bioreality-yeast-q6-structure-mediation-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The finite object studied here is a joined yeast table whose rows are systematic open reading frames with real coding-sequence codon counts, matched protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, matched mRNA half-life, and a sequence-derived coding-region structure proxy.  The coordinate block $Q$ consists of the nine projected $B^*_Q6$ residual coordinates
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the same coding sequences, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $Q$ vectors.  After these controls, the residualized finite table has $4736$ residual degrees of freedom, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and $\operatorname{rank}(Q\_{\perp Z})=9$.

\paragraph{Joined readouts.}
The five-way join is computed rather than inferred from contact names.  The final table contains $4759$ joined yeast genes, with a reported all-readout hit rate $0.8818572656921754$ and a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$.  The CDS side contributes $5815$ joined CDS records before the five-way intersection; the translation-efficiency side contains $5128$ valid protein-keyed entries; the mRNA half-life side contains $5379$ valid systematic-gene records; and the structure side contains $6027$ valid minimum-free-energy values.  The structure coordinate is the ViennaRNA RNAfold minimum free energy of the first $39$ nucleotides of the coding sequence, keyed by locus tag to the yeast proteomics, translation-efficiency, and half-life rows.  Translation efficiency is the mean ribosome-footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across the two rich-medium replicates of the Ingolia et al. yeast data.  This makes the table a same-organism, same-gene variance-partition surface, not a free association between an abstract residual coordinate and an unmatched protein list.

\paragraph{Residualized decomposition.}
Let $P$ denote residualized protein abundance, $T$ residualized measured translation efficiency, $M$ residualized mRNA stability or half-life information, and $S$ the residualized 5-prime coding-region MFE proxy.  The reported quantities use the following convention: $R^2\_{QP}$ is the $Q$-only explanatory fraction for $P$ after $Z$ residualization; $R^2\_{QP\mid S}$ is the remaining $Q$ increment after conditioning on $S$; $R^2\_{QP\mid T,M}$ is the remaining $Q$ increment after conditioning on $T$ and $M$; and $R^2\_{QP\mid T,M,S}$ is the remaining $Q$ increment after all three readout blocks are conditioned.  The finite values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2\_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2\_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2\_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2\_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the structure-only share of the $Q$--protein association is $0.003284564452662019$, while the extra structure share beyond measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability is $0.0005507778820193332$.  The direct association is large on this finite surface, but most of the reduction is associated with $T$ and $M$, not with the single MFE proxy.

\paragraph{Structure contact.}
The $Q$ block has a small but nonzero association with the residualized structure proxy: $S\_Q\_\mathrm{structure}\_R^2=0.003774248455422586$, with right-tail permutation probability $0.027972027972027972$ under the deterministic structure-residual permutation scheme.  The incremental structure fraction after $T$ and $M$ has a right-tail permutation probability $0.1258741258741259$, so the finite rows support a weak $Q$--MFE relation but do not support a strong structure-mediated explanation of the protein-abundance residual association.  In energy terms, $Q$ explains $468.33677333459536$ units on $P$ and $107.77365427073924$ units on $S$ after $Z$ residualization.  The residual energies are $1724.6664873071313$ for $P$, $645.3764418081507$ for $T$, $871992.3819246667$ for $M$, and $28554.997251412347$ for $S$.

\paragraph{Rank adequacy.}
The linear algebra is nondegenerate for the intended finite-row calculation.  The residualized one-column structure block has rank $1$; the joint $T,M$ block has rank $2$; and the joint $T,M,S$ block has rank $3$.  Adding the nine residual coordinates gives ranks $10$, $11$, and $12$ for $Q+S$, $Q+T+M$, and $Q+T+M+S$, respectively.  The direct improvement energies for $Q$ after conditioning are $466.79849101702615$ given $S$, $237.5550048307909$ given $T,M$, and $237.29705529470186$ given $T,M,S$.  These ranks and energies show that the computation is not an all-column saturation artifact: the $Q$ coordinates retain degrees of freedom after each conditioning block, and the observed drop from $R^2\_{QP}$ to $R^2\_{QP\mid T,M}$ is a real finite-row decomposition statistic.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated abundance surface.}
A broader twelve-organism protein-abundance computation gives the yeast translation-conditioned scalar for comparison.  In the yeast subset of that calculation, $5815$ proteins are used, the total $Q$--protein abundance fraction is $c=0.29837624994140827$, the remaining direct fraction after modeled per-protein tAI is $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and the indirect difference is $0.28320534058057895$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  The present yeast structure calculation is stricter in a different direction: it uses measured TE, mRNA stability, and the 5-prime CDS MFE proxy on $4759$ five-way joined genes.  The two surfaces agree on the local message that translation-associated information can absorb a large part of the direct $Q$--protein abundance association, while the single CDS-MFE proxy contributes only a small additional share on this row set.

\paragraph{Admissible reading.}
The chapter-specific conclusion is therefore a controlled variance partition.  On this yeast table, the nine $B^*_Q6$ residual coordinates have a substantial residual association with matched protein abundance after amino-acid composition, $GC3$, CDS length, and $M$-density controls.  Conditioning on measured translation efficiency plus mRNA stability reduces that association from $0.2715520808117803$ to $0.13773967696311287$.  Conditioning on the 5-prime CDS MFE proxy alone changes it only to $0.2706601505000995$, and adding the MFE proxy after $T$ and $M$ changes it only from $0.13773967696311287$ to $0.13759011208318542$.  The local structure proxy is therefore present as a small residual contact, not as the main mediator of the abundance relation.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, organismal phenotype claim, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from these numbers alone.  The MFE readout is a CDS-only first-$39$-nt minimum-free-energy proxy, not a measured initiation rate, not an ensemble structure, and not a folded-protein contact.  The computation is single-organism and depends on one matched set of yeast readouts.  A stronger biological conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact and a fresh statistic at that layer; the present result only licenses the finite statement that, in the supplied yeast rows, $Q$--protein abundance residual association is largely reduced by measured translation-efficiency and mRNA-stability conditioning, while the tested local coding-region MFE proxy accounts for only a small additional fraction.
