\subsection{uORF--Kozak translation-initiation contact as a bounded cross-layer readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-uorf-kozak-endogenous-te-ribonn}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The translation-initiation row named by the uORF--Kozak endogenous-TE contact is retained as a counted, externally attached readout rather than as a mechanistic explanation.  Its finite run reports $n=10982$ joined rows and a runtime of $68.392$ seconds.  Those two scalars are enough to certify that the row was executed on a nontrivial finite table, but they are not enough to promote the contact into a causal initiation model.  In this chapter the row is therefore used only as a bounded contact in a larger direction-aware ledger for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation abundance residual.

\paragraph{Residual object being tested.}
For each organism and candidate readout $H$, the ledger records the absorbed residual energy $A_H$, the residual coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda\,DL(H),\\
  \lambda &= 0.01,\\
  DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The residual coordinate vector is nine-dimensional, with coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The scalar $A_H$ measures projection energy in the finite table, while $C_H$ records whether the candidate readout points in the same coordinate direction as the residual support.  A large $A_H$ without aligned $C_H$ is treated as a wrong-direction absorber, not as a biological explanation.

\paragraph{Observed direction-aware rankings.}
The strongest raw aligned score in the assembled table is small.  In zebrafish, measured TE has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.001566051846174291$; mRNA follows with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.000734588577977589$.  In human, complex membership is the best one-column candidate after the penalty, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.0034339698846599055$; mRNA is close, with $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$ and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.0037752227820994546$.  Human PTM density has a larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but only $C_H=0.3604440741749213$, so its aligned product is $0.0053651472982577525$ and its score remains negative.  This is the central direction filter: high projection energy is not enough when the coordinate direction does not match the residual.

\paragraph{Null and missingness discipline.}
The table keeps the degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni null separate from rank.  In zebrafish, measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, mRNA, and PTM density exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, while localization, transmembrane count, domain count, and complex membership do not.  In human, complex membership, localization, measured TE, mRNA, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, while measured tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, and domain count do not.  In yeast, mRNA and localization exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, while measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, complex membership, PTM density, transmembrane count, and domain count do not.  Escherichia coli has neither mRNA nor measured TE above the finite Bonferroni threshold.  Empty rankings for organisms such as Bacillus subtilis, Caenorhabditis elegans, Drosophila melanogaster, Mus musculus, Mycobacterium smegmatis, Rattus norvegicus, and Sulfolobus solfataricus are missing readout rows, not zeros and not negative evidence.

\paragraph{Cross-organism conclusion.}
The organism-specific best absorbers disagree.  Zebrafish selects measured TE; human and chicken select complex membership; yeast selects mRNA; E. coli has no positive penalized winner.  No shared universal $H$ is supported by this finite table.  The uORF--Kozak contact therefore sits inside a negative cross-layer result: translation-initiation-adjacent data can be attached and counted, but the larger $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual is not compressed into one common fetchable channel.  The modeled-translation complement itself is large in several organisms: $d=0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish, $d=0.9208949355679455$ for human, $d=0.9400341806302775$ for chicken, and $d=0.6629960038578532$ for E. coli, while yeast is lower at $d=0.21173219572706703$.  These values indicate that much of the abundance component remains outside the modeled tAI projection in several organisms, but they do not identify a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{External contacts and positive controls.}
Other finite contacts show that the pipeline can detect structured signals without promoting them beyond their layer.  The Window6 boundary $R$ set is avoided in all $15$ computed organisms, with a one-sided binomial value $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and a two-sided value $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  The optimal-codon conservation contact reports $15$ computed organisms, $41$ synonymous design columns, and mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$, classified as clustered.  These contacts support the presence of finite coordinate structure, but they do not convert the uORF--Kozak row, the H-ranking table, or the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual into translation causation, protein folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, universality, or a global biological law.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The admissible statement is narrow.  The uORF--Kozak row supplies a finite translation-initiation contact with $10982$ rows, and the surrounding H-ranking table reports direction-aware projection quantities for candidate readouts against the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation abundance residual.  It is not evidence that uORFs or Kozak context cause the residual, not evidence that $C_H$ is a molecular mechanism, not evidence that $A_H$ is biological energy, not evidence of protein folding or physical admissibility, and not evidence for a universal cross-organism pathway.  Any stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite statistic.
