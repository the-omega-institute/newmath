\subsection{Cross-organism splice-MaxEnt exon-inclusion certificate}
\label{sec:bioreality-splice-maxent-crossorganism-mouse-vastdb}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The present record certifies a cross-layer exon-inclusion contact whose finite table is keyed by a mouse exon or transcript feature, a splice-strength coordinate, a tissue-resolved inclusion readout, and the organism-level join fields needed to keep the row inside a single curated biological surface. The concrete run for claim \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.exon\_inclusion.splice\_maxent\_crossorganism\_mouse\_vastdb} contains $50633$ rows and records \texttt{all\_tissue\_positive=true}; the computation took $410.672546$ seconds. This is the positive finite-row fact in the chapter: within the declared table, the splice-MaxEnt direction remains positive across all tissue partitions represented by the mouse VASTDB-style inclusion matrix.

\paragraph{Local mathematical object.}
Let $E$ be the finite set of joined exon rows, with $|E|=50633$. For each row $e \in E$, write $m(e)$ for the splice-MaxEnt coordinate and $\psi_t(e)$ for the inclusion readout in tissue or tissue-bin $t$. The certified finite condition is not a mechanistic law about splicing. It is the statement that the row-wise relation between $m(e)$ and the tissue-indexed inclusion surface has the same positive sign across the recorded tissue projections:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  |E| &= 50633,\\
  \operatorname{positive\_in\_all\_tissues}(m,\psi) &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This makes the object a finite relation certificate: it binds a splice-site scoring coordinate to an exon-inclusion readout under the rows actually present in the joined table. It does not assert that splice-MaxEnt is the only coordinate, that every organism follows the same law, or that a particular tissue response is causally forced by this score.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite controls.}
The same finite-row audit family contains two nearby splicing and transcript-processing checks. The ESE-hexamer exon-inclusion contact has $n=1037$ rows and reports $\operatorname{corr}(\Delta\mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta\mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619$ with runtime $4.569444656$ seconds. The alternative-polyadenylation contact has $24740$ genes and $296950$ PAS rows. These two numbers constrain the interpretation of the splice-MaxEnt certificate: a positive splice-strength relation in $50633$ mouse exon-inclusion rows is not interchangeable with an ESE-hexamer perturbation relation, and it is not a PAS-relative-usage relation. They are distinct finite surfaces with different row keys and different biological readouts.

They are useful for checking whether a proposed codon residual survives amino-acid projection, but they are not exon-inclusion measurements and do not substitute for VASTDB inclusion rows or splice-MaxEnt scores.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
A linked structural-order run illustrates the same separation. In the $B^*_{Q6}$ positional ramp audit, the primary ramp window is $50$, the fold count is $5$, and the minimum joined-row threshold is $300$. The weighted held-out $f_3$ ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, while the body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$; the paired ramp-minus-body increment is $0.001378137149572267$. The ramp sign-flip value is $0.0478515625$, the body value is $0.54296875$, and the paired ramp-minus-body value is $0.05859375$. The recorded conclusion is \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}. These scalars are deliberately not used to explain the splice-MaxEnt exon-inclusion result. They show that even a positive codon-position signal remains bounded by its own readout and localization gates.

\paragraph{Cross-layer comparison table.}
The wider finite audit contains a protein-thermal-stability contact with $8092$ rows, runtime $512.940125$ seconds, and UniProt sequence match rate $0.963104$. That table is a protein-stability surface, not an exon-inclusion surface. The splice-MaxEnt mouse VASTDB certificate therefore sits beside, rather than above, the APA, ESE, codon-topology, translation-ramp, and protein-stability records. The valid common operation is comparison of finite-row certificates; the invalid operation is promotion from one layer to another without a row-matched readout at the target layer.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
The admissible claim is narrow. In the finite mouse exon-inclusion table with $50633$ joined rows, the splice-MaxEnt coordinate has a positive tissue-wise relation to the inclusion readout in every recorded tissue projection. This supports a cross-layer relation between a splice-strength coordinate and exon inclusion on that finite surface. It does not, by itself, identify a universal splicing mechanism, a cross-species law, or a downstream translation effect.

\paragraph{Non-promotion boundary.}
No translation, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter unless a separate finite contact supplies the matching readout and the relevant controls. In particular, a codon residual such as $f_3$-stress would need same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight surface, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency rows before any translation-readout survival statistic could be reported. If that joined matrix is absent, rank-degenerate, or non-positive after organism, GC3, length, amino-acid composition, M-density, mRNA, position-bin, and simpler-baseline controls, the codon coordinate remains a sequence-topology residual only.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
This chapter is ready as a finite biological relation certificate for splice-MaxEnt and mouse exon inclusion. Its positive content is the $50633$-row all-tissue-positive splice-inclusion result. Its local limitation is equally explicit: the chapter does not turn splice-score survival, ESE perturbation, PAS usage, codon quotient geometry, ribosome-position signals, or protein-stability rows into one another. Each layer must be tested on its own joined finite table.
