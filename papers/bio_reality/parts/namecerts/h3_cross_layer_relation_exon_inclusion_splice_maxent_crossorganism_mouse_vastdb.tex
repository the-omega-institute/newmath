\subsection{Mouse exon inclusion and splice-MaxEnt survival}
\label{sec:bioreality-splice-maxent-crossorganism-mouse-vastdb}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite exon-inclusion table in which each row is keyed by a mouse splicing event and by the tissue-resolved inclusion readout attached to that event.  The biological coordinate used here is not a protein-output coordinate and not a translation coordinate: it is a splice-site strength coordinate, read through a MaxEnt-style splice score and tested against exon inclusion across the VASTDB-mouse surface.  The checked finite run for the present surface reports $n=50633$ usable rows, with the tissue-direction gate recorded as positive in every tested tissue and with runtime $410.672546$ seconds.  In compact notation, the empirical row certificate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{mouse,VASTDB}} &= 50633,\\
\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive} &= \mathrm{true},\\
T_{\mathrm{run}} &= 410.672546\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The role of this row certificate is limited but concrete: the finite table is large enough to make the splice-strength versus inclusion relation a data-bearing exon-inclusion claim rather than a naming convention, and the all-tissue positivity flag records that the tested tissue slices did not reverse the direction of the splice-MaxEnt association.

\paragraph{Coordinate and relation.}
For each admissible row $e$, let $M(e)$ denote the splice-MaxEnt coordinate attached to the relevant splice-site description, and let $I_t(e)$ denote the inclusion readout in tissue $t$.  The observed survival statement is that the fitted cross-row relation between $M(e)$ and $I_t(e)$ has the same positive direction for every tested tissue slice, after the row filters used by the finite run.  This chapter therefore records the bounded relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
M(e) \longrightarrow I_t(e)
\end{aligned}
$$
only at the exon-inclusion layer.  The arrow is not a molecular mechanism symbol.  It marks a finite-row statistical survival relation between a splice-site scoring coordinate and a measured inclusion readout.  The reported gate, $\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive}=\mathrm{true}$, says that no tested tissue slice in the retained mouse VASTDB panel supplied a sign-breaking counterexample to this bounded relation.

\paragraph{Comparison surface.}
A nearby exon-inclusion check based on ESE hexamer perturbation and Adamson-style exon-inclusion data gives $n=1037$ and the scalar collinearity
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt}) &= -0.008429245619,
\end{aligned}
$$
with runtime $4.569444656$ seconds.  That comparison is useful precisely because it is weak: the near-zero negative collinearity says that an ESE-hexamer perturbation surface should not be silently identified with the splice-MaxEnt surface.  The mouse VASTDB claim is therefore not a generic exon-regulatory claim.  It is the more specific observation that the splice-MaxEnt coordinate survives as a tissue-consistent exon-inclusion predictor on the $50633$-row mouse table.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The finite conclusion is therefore the following.  On the checked mouse VASTDB exon-inclusion surface, a splice-MaxEnt coordinate has a positive tissue-consistent association with exon inclusion across $50633$ retained rows.  The claim is cross-layer only in the narrow sense that a sequence-level splice-site score is compared with transcript-level exon inclusion.  It does not assert translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular mechanism, organismal phenotype, or universality.  Any such higher-layer statement would require its own layer-matched contact surface, its own finite row join, and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, the $f_3$ and quotient-geometry numbers listed above do not promote this exon-inclusion certificate into a translation-readout result.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
This chapter would fail if the row surface were replaced by a routing event, if the all-tissue positivity flag were treated as a mechanism, or if a codon-residual coordinate were claimed to explain exon inclusion without a same-event join and an explicit control design.  It would also fail if the splice-MaxEnt association were advertised as evidence for protein output or downstream phenotype.  The only ready statement is the bounded finite-row one: splice-MaxEnt direction survives across the tested mouse VASTDB tissue slices on the recorded $50633$-row panel.
