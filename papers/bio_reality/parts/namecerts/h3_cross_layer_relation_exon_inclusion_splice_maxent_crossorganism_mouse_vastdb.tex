\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The row family in this record is a bounded splice-layer table whose primary key is an exon event together with its organismal annotation, splice-site score coordinates, and tissue-resolved mouse inclusion readout. The realized finite panel contains $50633$ rows for the mouse VastDB-style exon-inclusion test, and the recorded pass condition is that every tissue-level direction checked by the finite procedure is positive. The computational run is identified by $70\mathrm{fafd}816698$ and reports runtime $410.672546$ seconds. In symbolic form the finite object can be written as a table
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{splice}}
  = \{(e,o,s_5(e),s_3(e),\psi_t(e),z(e)) : e \in E,\ t \in \mathcal{U}(e)\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $e$ is an exon event, $o$ is the organismal or cross-organism annotation available for that event, $s_5$ and $s_3$ are bounded splice-strength scores, $\psi_t$ is the tissue-indexed inclusion readout, and $z(e)$ denotes the finite controls retained by the row construction. The row count $|E|=50633$ is the scale at which the all-tissue positivity certificate is asserted; it is not a genome-wide law and it is not a mechanistic statement about spliceosome assembly.

\paragraph{Splice-score survival statement.}
The admissible claim is a cross-layer relation from splice-site sequence scoring to exon inclusion, not from coding-sequence residual geometry to protein output. The tested readback is local: a splice-strength coordinate is allowed to contribute only when the same event row also carries an inclusion measurement. The finite certificate says that the checked tissue directions are jointly positive, so the supported statement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \forall t \in \mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{checked}},\quad
  \Delta_t\bigl(\psi \mid s_5,s_3,z\bigr) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\Delta_t$ denotes the recorded finite-row direction statistic for tissue $t$. The value stored in the run is the Boolean condition $\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive}=\mathrm{true}$ rather than a tissue-by-tissue coefficient list. This distinction matters: the row certifies the sign pattern required by the packet, while it does not expose a full effect-size surface, confidence interval surface, or mechanistic kinetic model.

\paragraph{No downstream promotion.}
The splice-MaxEnt row is a splice-layer certificate. It does not imply translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, or universality. Such conclusions require their own row-matched contacts and their own statistics. In particular, the structural-order ramp result in the same finite-data bundle is deliberately not imported as support for this splice claim: its primary ramp window has $n=50$, ramp held-out $R^2$ increment $0.0011165640103326878$, body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, paired ramp-minus-body increment $0.001378137149572267$, ramp sign-flip probability $0.0478515625$, body sign-flip probability $0.54296875$, and ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability $0.05859375$, with conclusion \emph{ramp positive but not localized}. Those values belong to a distinct translation or structural-order boundary and cannot be used to strengthen the splice-inclusion claim.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
Within the finite evidence actually present, the chapter supports a bounded cross-layer statement: splice-strength scoring and mouse exon-inclusion rows form a non-vacuous positive relation over $50633$ checked rows, with all checked tissue directions positive in the recorded run. The certificate is finite, row-indexed, and readout-specific. If a later analysis wants a stronger conclusion, it must provide event-level coefficient magnitudes, tissue-resolved uncertainty, the exact control matrix rank, and matched perturbation or mechanistic measurements. Without those additions, the present result remains a splice-readout survival record and nothing downstream follows from it.
