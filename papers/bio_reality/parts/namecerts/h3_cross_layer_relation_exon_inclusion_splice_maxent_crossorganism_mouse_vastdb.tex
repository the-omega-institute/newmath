\subsection{Mouse exon-inclusion readback from splice-strength rows}\label{sec:biorality-splice-maxent-mouse-vastdb}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite certificate.}
The record certified here is a finite cross-layer table for mouse cassette-exon inclusion.  Each row is a splice event with a measured inclusion readout and a splice-strength coordinate derived from local sequence context.  The certified run for the splice-strength route contains $50{,}633$ rows and reports that the tissue-resolved inclusion direction is positive in every tissue class retained by the finite table.  In symbols, if $E$ is the retained set of mouse exon events, $T$ is the retained tissue index set, and $\Delta_{e,t}$ denotes the signed inclusion readback paired with the MaxEnt splice-strength coordinate for exon event $e$ in tissue $t$, then the finite certificate records
$$
\begin{aligned}
|E| &= 50{,}633,\\
\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is not a statement about every exon in every mouse annotation.  It is a statement about the joined finite table used by the certificate: splice-strength rows, VASTDB-style exon-inclusion rows, tissue labels, and the event identifiers needed to keep the readout attached to the same exon event.

\paragraph{Biological object.}
The biological object is not a protein, a phenotype, or a mechanism.  It is a row-level relation between a splice-site sequence score and exon inclusion.  The MaxEnt coordinate is used as a bounded sequence-derived splice-strength readout, while the inclusion side is a measured exon-inclusion surface indexed by mouse events and tissues.  The certificate is therefore a cross-layer relation only in the narrow sense that a local sequence coordinate is compared with an RNA-processing readout on the same event rows.  The admissible claim is that the finite table contains a consistently positive tissue-resolved association direction for the retained mouse exon-inclusion rows.

\paragraph{Control meaning.}
The finite assertion is intentionally weaker than a mechanistic splicing model.  It does not say that the MaxEnt score alone determines inclusion, nor that tissue-specific regulatory proteins, chromatin state, transcription rate, exon length, local RNA structure, or nonsense-mediated decay have been eliminated as biological contributors.  The certified row count and positivity flag say that the named finite surface passed its implemented direction check.  They do not by themselves supply a causal intervention, a perturbation rescue, or a universal rule for exon recognition.

\paragraph{Comparison with an independent exon-inclusion surface.}
A separate exon-inclusion certificate based on ESE hexamer perturbation data contains $1{,}037$ rows and reports a signed collinearity value
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{dESEseq},\mathrm{dMaxEnt}} &= -0.008429245619.
\end{aligned}
$$
The near-zero value in that independent finite surface is important for scope.  It prevents the mouse MaxEnt--VASTDB result from being read as a blanket assertion that all exon-inclusion coordinates collapse to one splice-strength axis.  The mouse table certifies a positive tissue-resolved direction for its own $50{,}633$ joined rows; the ESE-hexamer table shows that another exon-inclusion perturbation surface can be essentially unaligned with the same simple splice-strength direction.  The chapter therefore treats splice-strength association as a finite-row readback, not as a complete theory of exon inclusion.

They mark the surrounding coordinate discipline: coding-window residuals and splice-inclusion rows are distinct finite surfaces, and a positive splice-strength readback must not be promoted into a translation or coding-topology claim.

\paragraph{Name-certificate readback.}
The certified readback can be summarized as a finite implication with a local stop condition.  Let $X_e$ be the MaxEnt splice-strength coordinate for retained event $e$, let $Y_{e,t}$ be the inclusion readout for the same event in tissue $t$, and let $Z_{e,t}$ be the implemented row metadata used by the run.  The certificate supports the bounded statement
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Check}(X,Y,Z) &= \mathrm{positive\ in\ every\ retained\ tissue},\\
|E| &= 50{,}633.
\end{aligned}
$$
The certificate breaks if the event join is lost, if the inclusion readout is replaced by protein abundance or phenotype, if tissue positivity is inferred without the finite rows, or if the MaxEnt coordinate is treated as a sufficient mechanistic explanation of splicing.  It also breaks if a coding-coordinate statistic, such as the $f_3$ residual surface above, is substituted for the splice-event coordinate without a new joined table.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation readout follows from this exon-inclusion certificate.  No protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from it.  A downstream claim would require its own matched contact: for translation, a same-gene or same-transcript ribosome or translation-efficiency readout; for protein abundance, a matched proteomics surface; for folding or stability, a structure or stability surface; for function or phenotype, a separate assay or perturbation panel.  The present chapter certifies only the finite mouse splice-strength to exon-inclusion readback described above.

\paragraph{Audit verdict.}
The finite-row evidence is sufficient for a ready BioReality entry because the central claim is narrow, row-indexed, and numerically grounded: $50{,}633$ mouse exon events with an all-tissue-positive splice-strength relation.  The evidence is not sufficient for broader biological promotion.  The independent ESE-hexamer comparison, with $1{,}037$ rows and collinearity $-0.008429245619$, reinforces the need to keep the certificate local to its event table rather than generalizing it to every exon-inclusion coordinate.
