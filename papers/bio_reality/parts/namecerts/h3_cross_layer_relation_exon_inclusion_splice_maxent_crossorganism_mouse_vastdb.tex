\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The finite contact for this chapter is the mouse exon-inclusion table indexed by exon-level splice-strength coordinates and matched inclusion readouts.  The row audit for the packet
$\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.exon\_inclusion.splice\_maxent\_crossorganism\_mouse\_vastdb}$ reports $n=50633$ joined rows, a runtime of $410.672546$ seconds, and the Boolean tissue summary $\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive}=\mathrm{true}$.  Thus the contact is not merely a named biological topic: it is a finite matrix in which a splice-site scoring coordinate is joined to exon-inclusion behavior across the mouse VASTDB surface, and every tissue-level direction used by the audit is positive.

The row statement can be recorded as the finite assertion
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{splice}} &= 50633,\\
\mathrm{runtime}_{\mathrm{splice}} &= 410.672546\ \mathrm{s},\\
\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive}_{\mathrm{splice}} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a cross-layer relation only at the splicing layer: a sequence-derived splice-strength coordinate is compared with an exon-inclusion readout.  The result does not by itself mention ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Coordinate meaning.}
The admissible coordinate in this chapter is a splice-site strength statistic such as a MaxEnt-style splice score attached to exon rows.  The biological readout is exon inclusion, not a downstream protein-layer quantity.  The finite positive direction means that the splice-strength coordinate survives as an inclusion-associated signal over the audited row surface; it does not mean that every exon is explained by splice strength alone, nor that a splice-score gradient is causal without perturbation or rescue evidence.

The nearby exon-inclusion audit using ESE hexamer and MaxEnt coordinates gives a useful scale check.  In the Adamson-style exon-inclusion surface, the finite audit reports $n=1037$ rows and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{collinearity}(\Delta \mathrm{ESEseq},\Delta \mathrm{MaxEnt})=-0.008429245619,
\end{aligned}
$$
with runtime $4.569444656$ seconds.  The near-zero collinearity in that separate row surface supports the local interpretation that splice-strength and enhancer-hexamer coordinates should be treated as distinguishable finite coordinates rather than collapsed into one generic exon-inclusion score.  It is therefore legitimate for the present mouse VASTDB chapter to isolate the splice-strength direction while still refusing to identify it with all exon-regulatory information.

\paragraph{Controlled reading of the positive splice result.}
The phrase $\mathrm{all\_tissue\_positive}=\mathrm{true}$ is interpreted only as a finite audit outcome over the tissue partitions included in the mouse VASTDB splice-strength table.  It says that the signed splice-strength association did not reverse across those tissue partitions under the audit's row construction.  It does not certify that the effect is independent of every possible confounder, nor that splice strength is the sole driver of inclusion.  The chapter's positive statement is therefore bounded:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{splice-strength row coordinate} &\longrightarrow \text{exon-inclusion readout},\\
\text{finite support} &= 50633\ \text{mouse VASTDB rows},\\
\text{allowed conclusion} &= \text{positive cross-layer splice-inclusion contact}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Any stronger statement would require a new row surface with the corresponding readout and controls.

\paragraph{Why this does not promote the $f_3$ translation boundary.}
The same audit packet names a possible $f_3$-stress translation-readout boundary, but the splice-strength VASTDB table does not contain same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint occupancy.  Consequently the splice result cannot instantiate a statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-stress}}(\mathrm{position\mbox{-}resolved\ ribosome\ occupancy}\mid Z).
\end{aligned}
$$
It also cannot repair rank degeneracy in modeled translation panels.  For comparison, the structural-order ramp audit gives a small positive ramp residual with $\mathrm{weighted\_f3\_ramp\_delta\_held\_out\_r2}=0.0011165640103326878$, a body residual of $-0.00026157313923957926$, a ramp sign-flip value $p=0.0478515625$, and a paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value $p=0.05859375$; its conclusion is $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$.  That separate result illustrates the same discipline: a positive finite signal remains tied to its exact readout and gate, and it does not license unrelated downstream claims.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
This chapter therefore makes one positive claim and several explicit refusals.  The positive claim is that the finite mouse VASTDB splice-strength surface contains $50633$ joined exon rows with tissue-wise positive direction for the audited MaxEnt-style splice coordinate.  The refusals are equally local: no translation readout follows from this table; no protein abundance follows; no folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanistic causation, or universal biological law follows.  Such statements would require separate layer-matched contacts, such as position-resolved ribosome occupancy for translation, matched proteomics for protein abundance, structure-level measurements for folding, perturbation or rescue assays for mechanism, and phenotype measurements for system behavior.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The chapter is ready as a finite splicing contact because the row count, runtime, and tissue-direction Boolean are present and specific.  It remains deliberately silent about translation and protein-layer promotion.  The audited splice relation may be used as a BioReality record for exon inclusion, while the $f_3$-stress translation-readout packet remains a request for additional joined ribosome-occupancy and matched-control rows rather than a consequence of the mouse VASTDB splice-strength result.
