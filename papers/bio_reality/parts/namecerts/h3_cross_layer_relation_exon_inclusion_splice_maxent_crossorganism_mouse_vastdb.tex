\paragraph{What the splice table supports.}
The splice-MaxEnt result is a same-domain cross-layer relation between a splice-site scoring surface and exon-inclusion readout. Its finite positive status is local to the row table and the tissue-wise direction recorded there. It is strengthened as a BioReality contact because the table has a concrete row count, an explicit Boolean tissue summary, and a recorded runtime. It is not strengthened by the $f_3$ codon quotient, and the $f_3$ quotient is not strengthened by the splice table. The alternative polyadenylation contact in the same audit universe has $24740$ genes and $296950$ PAS rows, and the protein thermal-stability contact has $8092$ rows with UniProt sequence match rate $0.963104$; those numbers show that other finite biological surfaces exist in the surrounding ledger, but they are not splicing evidence for this chapter unless joined through a declared statistic. \paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
The $f_3$-stress translation conjecture in the packet is intentionally narrower than a biological mechanism claim. A valid translation-readout promotion would require a same-organism, same-CDS, position-resolved table joining ordered coding coordinates, codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight surface, matched mRNA abundance, and ribosome-footprint occupancy or an explicitly bounded translation-efficiency residual. The statistic would have the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z)>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_{\mathrm{pos}}$ is a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout and $Z$ includes amino-acid composition or position bins, organism, $GC3$, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, coverage, mappability, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablations. No such joined translation table is present in the audited finite-row data for this chapter. Consequently the $f_3$ coordinate remains a codon-topology residual and a request for a future translation-window statistic, not a translation result.

\paragraph{Rank and ablation discipline.}
The nearby structural-order ramp contact illustrates why the boundary is necessary. In the recorded structural-order run, the primary ramp length was $50$, the fold count was $5$, and the minimum joined size was $300$. At ramp length $50$, the weighted held-out $R^2$ increment was $0.0011165640103326878$ for the ramp and $-0.00026157313923957926$ for the body, with ramp-minus-body increment $0.001378137149572267$. The sign-flip values were $0.0478515625$ for the ramp, $0.54296875$ for the body, and $0.05859375$ for ramp minus body. The recorded conclusion was \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}: the ramp signal was positive, but the localization gate did not pass. This is the appropriate precedent for the present chapter: a positive scalar can be reported only at the layer and control set where it was computed, and failure of the stricter gate must remain visible.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The finite splice-MaxEnt table supports only the stated exon-inclusion contact. The $f_3$ quotient and saturated codon lists support only the existence of a finite residual coordinate. The chapter makes no claim that splice-site score, ESE hexamer score, $f_3$-stress, wobble-weight asymmetry, or codon-window geometry entails translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Any such statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a declared finite statistic at that layer. In particular, modeled tAI or stAI weights are not observed ribosome occupancy; matched mRNA abundance is a control covariate rather than a translation readout; and review eligibility or contact naming is not a biological measurement.
