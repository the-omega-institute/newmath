\paragraph{What the table supports.}
The supported local statement is the following finite implication: given the mouse exon-inclusion rows in the certified table, the MaxEnt splice-site coordinate has a positive tissue-wise inclusion orientation on $50633$ rows.  The statement is cross-layer only in the narrow sense that a sequence-derived splice-site score is tested against an exon-inclusion readout.  It is not a claim that codon topology causes inclusion, that synonymous residuals explain ribosome occupancy, or that splice-site strength determines downstream protein properties.

\paragraph{What the table refuses.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this certificate.  A translation statement would require same-organism and same-CDS codon rows, wobble or adaptation weights when used, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency rows.  A protein-abundance statement would require its own proteomics contact.  A structural, physical, functional, or phenotype statement would require a separate layer-matched assay or prediction table.  Until those rows exist and the appropriate controlled statistic is computed, the present certificate remains exactly a finite splice-site-strength versus exon-inclusion record.
