\paragraph{Translation-conditioned decision rule.}
A downstream stability association is admissible only when the same coordinate first survives a translation readout under the declared controls.  The finite statistic asks whether adding $q_i$ still improves the downstream stability readout after the translation readout has been included.  Equivalently, the reported quantity is the translation-conditioned change in held-out explanatory power, not a direct codon-to-stability mechanism.  In the run, the aggregate full-control change has three organism entries with two negative signs and one positive sign:
$$\begin{aligned}
\min \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &= -0.004953854025603666,\\
\operatorname{median} \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &= -0.0033500708066446228,\\
\operatorname{mean} \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &= -0.0025553576666936064,\\

\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{weighted}} &= -0.001572140012981015.

The sign-flip test for a positive full-control effect gives $p=0.946441650390625$. The finite call is therefore \emph{partial}: one organism is classified as a mediated blocked null and two as partial, rather than as a stable cross-organism positive mediation result.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The present stability result permits only the following bounded reading: in the available finite rows, $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals have a small direct association with the stability-surface readout, but the translation-conditioned evidence is mixed and has a negative weighted full-control change.  No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility statement, biological function, phenotype, degradation mechanism, or universal biological law follows from these numbers.  Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic evaluated after the translation readout, mRNA controls, amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, and protein-abundance controls where applicable.
