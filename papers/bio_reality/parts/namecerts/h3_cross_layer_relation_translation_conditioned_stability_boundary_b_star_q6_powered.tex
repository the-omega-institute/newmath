\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein-stability boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:biorreality-q6-translation-conditioned-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests whether synonymous residual coordinates from $B^*_{Q6}$ may be read at a protein-stability or degradation boundary only after passing through a matched translation readout. The finite carrier is an organism-by-CDS panel with amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates, matched translation measurements where available, matched mRNA controls, and a downstream stability surface when the row join is large enough. The powered run used five folds, required at least $300$ joined rows, and fixed the seed hash as \texttt{sha256:1deaaafe98cd16982d810af72f6389a20be0dc19cbd46bc966593dd507bad122}. Three organisms passed the row threshold. In \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 the available stability surface was mRNA half-life with $2517$ rows. In \textit{Homo sapiens} the primary stability surface was protein turnover with $6697$ turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA half-life rows. In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} the primary stability surface was protein turnover with $3537$ turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA half-life rows.

\paragraph{Gated statistic.}
The statistic is not a direct statement that $q_i$ controls protein stability. A coordinate $q_i$ is first tested against a translation readout $T_j$ under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, and matched mRNA controls. Only after that translation gate is available is the downstream stability surface interpreted, and then only as the residual association remaining after conditioning on the matched translation readout. The numerical summary separates the raw downstream association from the translation-conditioned increment. Across the three joined organism panels, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ stability prediction summary was positive in all three cases, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$. The downstream boundary test, however, is the full-control translation-conditioned change in $R^2$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &\in [-0.004953854025603666,\ 0.0006378518321674687],\\
\operatorname{median}(\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}}) &= -0.0033500708066446228,\\
\operatorname{mean}(\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}}) &= -0.0025553576666936064,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{weighted}} &= -0.001572140012981015.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only one of the three organism entries had positive full-control increment; two were negative. The one-sided sign-flip probability for a positive full-control effect was $0.946441650390625$, so the finite panel does not support a stable positive translation-conditioned protein-stability boundary. The permutation control was also negative in all three entries, with minimum $-0.004840148172275405$, median $-0.002926202164063474$, mean $-0.002753469686061827$, and maximum $-0.0004940587218466019$.

\paragraph{Call.}
The finite verdict is \texttt{partial}. The organism-level calls were two \texttt{partial} entries and one \texttt{mediated\_blocked\_null} entry. This means that the panel contains enough matched rows to evaluate the boundary and that the direct downstream association is nonzero, but the translation-conditioned residual does not become a cross-organism positive stability claim. The correct reading is a guarded boundary: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be placed next to stability or degradation readouts only after the translation step is represented, and the observed finite statistic blocks promotion from direct codon-topology association to protein-stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite checks.}
Two nearby readout families help calibrate the scope. In the minimal compilation test, the same residual source was not compressed by the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary: the reported organism compressibilities were $0.024368095798914635$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.005717621488177116$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, with median $0.026$ and permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$ in each computed organism. This supports the interpretation that the residual source is not merely absorbed by the available measured readouts. In the structural-order readout family, the translation-gated predicted-structure-confidence panel used $3736$ matched \textit{E. coli} proteins from $3739$ codon-joined rows with hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ yeast proteins from $5815$ codon-joined rows with hit rate $0.998108$. Its controlled structural-order readback had $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ in \textit{E. coli} and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ in yeast, while the translation-conditioned structural-order boundary later gave positive full-control increments in both organisms, with mean $0.005284160876443678$ and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$. The stability boundary therefore has a different empirical shape: the available stability and turnover rows do not reproduce the stronger positive structural-order crossing.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
The chapter does not license translation realization, folding quality, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality. A modeled tAI or stAI route is not a measured ribosome contact. Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not a translation readout by itself. Bulk proteomics abundance is not a protein-stability or degradation-rate assay unless a layer-matched stability, turnover, pulse-chase, degradation, or perturbation contact is joined on the same organism and coding-sequence identifiers. The present finite result permits only this statement: in the supplied organism panels, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates show small direct associations with available stability-like surfaces, but after the required translation-conditioned control step the aggregate increment is negative and the cross-organism stability boundary remains only partially supported.
