\subsection{Translation-conditioned stability boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which each $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate is first residualized against amino-acid quotient structure before it is allowed to touch a downstream protein-state readout.  The admissible readback has three ordered components: a residual coordinate $q_i$, a matched translation readout $T_j$, and then a stability or degradation-rate surface only when such rows are available.  The measured panel for this run used five-fold evaluation with a minimum joined-row threshold of $300$ and seed \texttt{sha256:1deaaafe98cd16982d810af72f6389a20be0dc19cbd46bc966593dd507bad122}.  Three organisms entered the stability-side join: \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 used \texttt{mrna\_half\_life} as its primary readout with $2517$ rows; \textit{Homo sapiens} used \texttt{protein\_turnover} as its primary readout with $6697$ turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows; \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} used \texttt{protein\_turnover} as its primary readout with $3537$ turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows.

\paragraph{Gated statistic.}
The statistic is not a direct codon-to-stability claim.  The admissible question is whether a residual coordinate that has survived the translation interface still carries a translation-conditioned association with the stability-side readout after controls.  In compressed notation, the tested comparison can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow P_k,\\
Z &= \{\text{amino-acid composition},\ \text{organism},\ GC3,\ \text{CDS length},\ M\text{-density},\ \text{matched mRNA controls}\},\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &= R^2(P_k \mid Z,T_j,q_i)-R^2(P_k \mid Z,T_j).
\end{aligned}
$$
The full-control aggregate did not show a positive stability boundary.  Across the three organism entries, the full-control $\Delta R^2$ summary had minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, with two negative entries and one positive entry.  The weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.001572140012981015$, and the one-sided sign-flip probability for being greater than zero was $0.946441650390625$.  The permutation control was also non-positive in all three entries, with minimum $-0.004840148172275405$, median $-0.002926202164063474$, mean $-0.002753469686061827$, and maximum $-0.0004940587218466019$.

\paragraph{Observed finite outcome.}
Before conditioning on translation, the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block had small positive predictive contact with the stability-side readout: over $n=3$ organism entries, the raw stability-prediction summary was minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, maximum $0.03602995605625481$, with three positive entries and no negative entries.  That finite association is therefore real as a descriptive row statistic, but it is not promoted to a stability mechanism.  After the required translation-conditioned test, the run verdict was \texttt{partial}: two organism calls were classified as \texttt{partial}, and one was classified as \texttt{mediated\_blocked\_null}.  The appropriate reading is that the panel detects weak residual contact with stability-side measurements, while the stricter translation-conditioned boundary does not pass as a positive mediated stability claim.

\paragraph{Context from the same coordinate family.}
The stability result should be read next to the behavior of the same residual family on other bounded readouts, not as an isolated universal law.  In the minimal-compilation test, the residual source was not highly compressed by the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary: the reported median compressibility was $0.026$.  The organism-level compressibilities were $0.024368095798914635$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.005717621488177116$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.02635576736932188$ for \textit{H. sapiens}, and $0.13234136547055853$ for \textit{S. cerevisiae}, each with permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$ under the recorded test.  By contrast, the translation-conditioned structural-order boundary crossed its layer in a separate two-organism panel: full-control $\Delta R^2$ had minimum $0.002688284523492457$, mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$, while the permuted full-control summary was negative in both entries.  The earlier structural-order readout also reported nine residual coordinates, namely \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}.  Its joined structural-order panels had $3736$ matched \textit{E. coli} proteins from $3739$ codon-joined rows, for hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched \textit{S. cerevisiae} proteins from $5815$ codon-joined rows, for hit rate $0.998108$.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
This chapter therefore records a narrow cross-layer boundary rather than a biological mechanism.  A direct $q_i \to P_k$ association is not enough: a downstream stability or degradation interpretation requires a surviving $q_i \to T_j$ translation entry and then a reduction or reallocation of the direct association after conditioning on $T_j$.  The present finite run is passed as a computed panel and classified as \texttt{partial}, but its full-control aggregate points away from a positive translation-conditioned stability boundary.  No claim about translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, degradation mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality follows from this record unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies that claim and the same-row conditioned statistic supports it.
