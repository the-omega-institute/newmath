\subsection{Translation-conditioned stability boundary for $B^\ast_{Q6}$}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The stability-boundary record is a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ coordinates are first residualized against amino-acid composition before any downstream readout is interpreted.  The tested coordinates are the nine-coordinate residual basis
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.
The control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline obtained from the union support of the nine projected $B^\ast_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the structural-order calibration where the same coordinate construction is rank-checked, the controlled design has $24$ control columns, $23$ control-rank columns, and full residual coordinate rank $9$ in both tested organisms; the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $3713$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5781$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Thus the stability boundary is not being read from a rank-collapsed codon table.

\paragraph{Gated statistic.}
For each organism, the admissible route is not a direct map from a residual coordinate $q_i$ to a stability readout.  A coordinate first has to survive a translation readout $T_j$ after the stated controls.  Only after that gate is evaluated is the downstream readout interpreted through the translation-conditioned increment
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{stab}\mid T}
  &= R^2\bigl(\mathrm{stability}\sim Z+T+q_i\bigr)
   - R^2\bigl(\mathrm{stability}\sim Z+T\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes the finite control block.  This quantity is a residual association after translation conditioning.  It is not a folding model, not a degradation mechanism, and not a phenotype statement.

\paragraph{Classification.}
The finite call is partial rather than promoted.  The organism-call ledger contains two partial calls and one mediated-blocked-null call.  The direct residual-to-stability surface is present but small, while the translation-conditioned full-control surface has a negative aggregate and lacks a positive sign-flip result.  In compact form, the observed boundary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{direct}\ B^\ast_{Q6}\to \mathrm{stability} &> 0\quad\text{in all three joined entries},\\
\mathrm{conditioned}\ B^\ast_{Q6}\to \mathrm{stability}\mid T,Z &\not> 0\quad\text{as an aggregate finite-row claim}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The stability packet therefore reaches a measured cross-layer boundary but does not certify a translation-mediated protein-stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter reports only a finite controlled association pattern.  No translation mechanism follows from modeled adaptation weights alone; no protein stability, degradation law, folding quality, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ coordinates alone; and no downstream interpretation is allowed unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding readout.  The current stability result is consequently a bounded partial contact: it records weak direct stability association, loss of aggregate positivity after translation conditioning, and a remaining requirement for stronger same-row turnover or degradation data before any mechanism-level conclusion can be made.
