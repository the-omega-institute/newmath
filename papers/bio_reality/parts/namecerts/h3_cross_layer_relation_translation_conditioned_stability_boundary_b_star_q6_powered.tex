\subsection{Translation-conditioned stability boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The stability-boundary record tests whether amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates in $B^*_{Q6}$ can be read at a downstream protein-stability or degradation surface only after the same coordinate has passed through a translation readout.  The tested carrier is finite: each row is a coding sequence or matched protein entry with a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual vector, organism annotation, amino-acid composition controls, $GC_3$, coding length, an $M$-density baseline, matched mRNA information, a translation readout, and, where available, a stability-like surface.  The experiment used five folds, a minimum joined-row threshold of $300$, and seed \texttt{sha256:1deaaafe98cd16982d810af72f6389a20be0dc19cbd46bc966593dd507bad122}.  The finite join supplied three organism-level stability surfaces: \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 contributed $2517$ rows with mRNA half-life as the primary readout; \textit{Homo sapiens} contributed $6697$ protein-turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows, with protein turnover primary; and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} contributed $3537$ protein-turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows, with protein turnover primary.

\paragraph{Gated statistic.}
The direct residual-to-stability screen is not interpreted as a mechanism.  Its role is only to decide whether there is any finite association left to condition.  Across the three joined organism panels, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ stability association was positive in all three cases, with summary
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min R^2_{Q\to P} &= 0.018837736273789707,\\
\operatorname{median} R^2_{Q\to P} &= 0.027727034742559664,\\
\operatorname{mean} R^2_{Q\to P} &= 0.02753157569086806,\\
\max R^2_{Q\to P} &= 0.03602995605625481.
\end{aligned}
$$
The admissible downstream question is then whether this residual-to-stability signal survives as a translation-conditioned association after the $q_i\to T_j$ gate and the full control set.  The full-control incremental statistic had $n=3$ organism summaries, one positive and two negative entries:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min \Delta R^2_{Q\to P\mid T,Z} &= -0.004953854025603666,\\
\operatorname{median} \Delta R^2_{Q\to P\mid T,Z} &= -0.0033500708066446228,\\
\operatorname{mean} \Delta R^2_{Q\to P\mid T,Z} &= -0.0025553576666936064,\\
\max \Delta R^2_{Q\to P\mid T,Z} &= 0.0006378518321674687,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{weighted}} &= -0.001572140012981015.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign-flip probability for a positive full-control effect was $0.946441650390625$.  The corresponding permuted full-control surface was entirely negative, with $n=3$, minimum $-0.004840148172275405$, median $-0.002926202164063474$, mean $-0.002753469686061827$, and maximum $-0.0004940587218466019$.  The organism-level calls were classified as two \texttt{partial} entries and one \texttt{mediated\_blocked\_null} entry, giving the overall verdict \texttt{partial} rather than a positive stability crossing.

\paragraph{Readout interpretation.}
The finite outcome is a boundary result.  The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates do show small positive stability-surface associations before the translation-conditioning test, but the conditioned statistic does not support a general residual-to-stability route after the declared controls.  The negative weighted value, the negative mean and median full-control increments, and the high sign-flip probability together force a conservative reading: the panel permits a partial contact with the stability surface, but it blocks promotion to a translation-mediated stability mechanism.  In symbols, the admissible statement is not $q_i\Rightarrow P_k$ and not $q_i\Rightarrow T_j\Rightarrow P_k$ as a biological mechanism; it is only the finite controlled readback
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\mapsto T_j \mapsto P_k,\\
\Delta R^2_{Q\to P\mid T,Z} &\leq 0 \text{ on the weighted aggregate,}\\
\operatorname{verdict} &= \texttt{partial}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ denotes the declared controls: amino-acid composition, organism, $GC_3$, coding length, $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA information, with protein-abundance controls used where the downstream surface requires them.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring finite facts.}
The stability boundary is consistent with the broader residual-compression test.  In the minimal-compilation panel, the measured low-dimensional downstream dictionary did not compress the $P_Q$ residual source strongly: the organism-level final residual fractions were $0.9756319042010854$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.9942823785118229$ for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.9736442326306781$ for human, and $0.8676586345294415$ for yeast.  The corresponding compressibilities were $0.024368095798914635$, $0.005717621488177116$, $0.02635576736932188$, and $0.13234136547055853$, with a cross-organism median of $0.026$ and permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ in each listed organism.  Thus the stability-boundary result should not be read as a failure of the coordinates to carry any signal; it says that this particular downstream stability interpretation is not established by the translation-conditioned statistic.

The same distinction is visible in the structural-order controls.  A separate structural-order boundary had a positive weighted full-control increment $0.004764742175882462$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, median $0.005284160876443678$, minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$ across two organisms.  The translation-gated structural-order readout also had high sequence-to-structure-confidence joins: \textit{E. coli} used $3736$ matched proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined entries, hit rate $0.999198$, and yeast used $5804$ matched proteins out of $5815$, hit rate $0.998108$.  Those numbers remain separate from the stability boundary: they show that another downstream surface can be finite and powered, not that stability, degradation, folding, or function follows here.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter therefore records a controlled finite-row boundary, not a biological law.  No translation mechanism, folding pathway, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, phenotype, degradation mechanism, or universal rule follows from the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, from modeled codon-adaptation weights, from mRNA controls, from bulk protein abundance, or from the stability labels alone.  Such a claim would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  In this panel the allowed conclusion is narrower: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be carried to a protein-stability or degradation-rate surface only under a mandatory translation gate, and the observed full-control aggregate is partial and mainly attenuating rather than a positive stability-crossing result.
