\subsection{Translation-Conditioned Stability Boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests whether amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates in the $B^*_{Q6}$ family can be admitted to a downstream stability or degradation surface only after the same coordinate has passed through a matched translation readout.  The carrier is a finite organism-by-CDS panel with residual $q_i$ coordinates, translation-layer readouts $T_j$, matched mRNA controls, and a stability-oriented readout surface when such a surface is available.  The experiment used five folds, a minimum joined-row requirement of $300$, and seed \texttt{sha256:1deaaafe98cd16982d810af72f6389a20be0dc19cbd46bc966593dd507bad122}.  Three organism entries entered the stability boundary table: \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 had $2517$ joined rows for \texttt{mrna\_half\_life} as its primary available readout; \textit{Homo sapiens} had $11322$ rows for \texttt{mrna\_half\_life} and $6697$ rows for \texttt{protein\_turnover}, with \texttt{protein\_turnover} used as primary; and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} had $4759$ rows for \texttt{mrna\_half\_life} and $3537$ rows for \texttt{protein\_turnover}, again with \texttt{protein\_turnover} used as primary.  Thus the panel is powered enough to report a finite boundary statistic, but it is not homogeneous across organisms: two entries have protein-turnover surfaces, while the bacterial entry is an RNA-half-life boundary surface rather than a direct protein-stability measurement.

\paragraph{Controlled readout geometry.}
Each $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate is interpreted only after residualization against amino-acid composition and the declared sequence controls.  The translation gate is prior to the stability surface: a direct $q_i$ to stability or turnover association is not promoted unless the corresponding $q_i$ to $T_j$ readout survives the controls.  The finite statistic reports the extra full-control explanatory contribution after conditioning on translation; in the aggregate table, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ prediction of the stability-oriented surface was positive in all three organism entries, with summary values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min &= 0.018837736273789707,\\
\operatorname{median} &= 0.027727034742559664,\\
\operatorname{mean} &= 0.02753157569086806,\\
\max &= 0.03602995605625481,\\
n &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This positive direct surface is not, by itself, the asserted biological conclusion.  The boundary asks whether that surface remains meaningful as a translation-conditioned association rather than as an unmediated codon-topology claim.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned statistic.}
After full controls and translation conditioning, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ summary changed sign at the panel level.  The observed full-control $\Delta R^2$ values had $n=3$, two negative entries, one positive entry, minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, and maximum $0.0006378518321674687$.  The weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.001572140012981015$.  A sign-flip test for a positive full-control effect gave $p=0.946441650390625$, so the finite table does not support a cross-organism positive translation-conditioned stability effect.  The permuted full-control comparison was also negative throughout: minimum $-0.004840148172275405$, median $-0.002926202164063474$, mean $-0.002753469686061827$, maximum $-0.0004940587218466019$, with three negative entries and no positive entries.  The organism-level calls were two \texttt{partial} entries and one \texttt{mediated\_blocked\_null} entry, giving the recorded verdict \texttt{partial}.  The scientific reading is therefore asymmetric: the direct residual-to-stability-oriented surface exists as a small finite association, but the translation-conditioned boundary does not license a protein-stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to adjacent finite checks.}
The same residual family has additional finite-row context that constrains interpretation.  In the minimal-compilation check, the measured low-dimensional readout set failed to compress $P_Q$ strongly across computed organisms: compressibility was $0.024368095798914635$ for \textit{Danio rerio} with $n=13140$ and $P_Q$ energy $605.0092168669606$; $0.005717621488177116$ for \textit{E. coli} with $n=2517$ and $P_Q$ energy $225.84556319953032$; $0.02635576736932188$ for human with $n=11250$ and $P_Q$ energy $286.3673197177688$; and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast with $n=4759$ and $P_Q$ energy $229.86450794006544$.  Each of those compression entries reported a permutation value of $0.000999000999000999$, but the cross-organism median compressibility was only $0.026$, so the residual source is not collapsed into the measured readout dictionary.  In the structural-order boundary check, the translation-conditioned order statistic was positive for two organisms, with aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ minimum $0.002688284523492457$, mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.  That order result is not imported as stability evidence; it only shows that the same residual family can cross a different readout boundary under a different finite contact.

\paragraph{Rank and row safeguards.}
The structural-order survival table also records the control geometry used to keep amino-acid composition from masquerading as a codon-residual effect.  For \textit{E. coli}, $3739$ codon-joined rows yielded $3736$ matched proteins and a structural-order hit rate of $0.999198$; the control matrix had $24$ columns, including $20$ amino-acid composition controls, with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, and residualized $Q$ rank $9$.  For yeast, $5815$ codon-joined rows yielded $5804$ matched proteins and a hit rate of $0.998108$; the same $24$-column control layout gave $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, and residualized $Q$ rank $9$.  These rank facts explain why the present stability boundary is treated as a gated finite-row test rather than as an identifier-level or notation-level assertion.  The coordinates are allowed to enter a readout surface only when the joined table has enough residual rank after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The outcome is a partial boundary, not a mechanism claim.  No translation realization follows from codon usage alone, from modeled tAI or stAI weights, from $S^{QT}$ notation, or from the residual-coordinate construction.  No folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, degradation mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from the observed direct association or from the translation-conditioned table.  The only supported statement is finite and conditional: in this joined panel, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates show a small direct association with the available stability-oriented surfaces, but after the required translation conditioning and controls the aggregate effect is not positive, with weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=-0.001572140012981015$ and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$.  A stronger protein-stability claim would require a separate organism- and CDS-matched stability or degradation-rate contact, a surviving same-coordinate translation readout, and attenuation of the direct $q_i$ to stability association after conditioning on that translation readout.
