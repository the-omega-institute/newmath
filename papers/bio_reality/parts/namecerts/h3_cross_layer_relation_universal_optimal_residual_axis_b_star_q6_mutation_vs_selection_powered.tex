\subsection{A wobble-broken $f_3$ residual axis for bounded translation readout}
\label{sec:biores-q6-f3-stress-wobble-broken-translation-readout}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record isolates the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate as a finite synonymous-residual surface on coding sequences. The carrier is not a protein-output object and not a phenotype object. It is the CDS-by-organism coordinate table obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the third-position $f_3$ paired imbalance and then compared only with matched translation-layer rows. The finite saturation check fixes the local codon boundary: the ambient $M = \operatorname{Med}(R) \cup \{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ row contains $20$ codons,
$$\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}$$
while the $f_3$ saturation of $R$ removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and leaves $18$ codons,
$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The verified boundary row is empty, $\partial_{f_3} = \varnothing$, so the chapter treats the coordinate as a closed finite residual contrast rather than as an unbounded codon-family search.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row claim.}
The powered residual-axis experiment reports status passed for the claim that non-tRNA residual optimality aligns most strongly with $f_3$ translation selection. The dominant axis is recorded as \texttt{f3\_translation\_selection}. The run used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous design columns. This supports a bounded axis statement: after the tRNA-adaptation component is separated, the leading remaining optimality coordinate in the tested panel is the $f_3$ translation-selection direction. The statement is deliberately weaker than a biological law. It does not say that every organism uses this axis, nor that every codon family has the same direction, nor that the axis produces a downstream protein phenotype.

\paragraph{Relation, spectrum, and trigger.}
The relation part of the record is the controlled association between the $f_3$-stress residual coordinate and a translation-layer readout $T_j$. The spectrum part is the rank-powered comparison against other residual axes. In the associated powered spectrum, $11$ organisms were computed out of $16$ requested, again with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The descriptive spectrum is led on average by \texttt{d\_resid4}, followed by \texttt{d\_tRNA}; per-organism dominant counts are $\{\texttt{d\_resid4}:11\}$. The same finite panel is imbalanced by domain, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms, and the largest reported GC3 rank effect is \texttt{d\_usage} with $\rho = 0.136$. The trigger part is therefore not the mere presence of a third-position codon involution. The coordinate becomes a candidate dynamical row only where context tRNA or wobble weights separate paired codons, expressed locally as $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$.

\paragraph{Controlled readout rule.}
The bounded readback is the survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ for a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The statistic is only meaningful after amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, M-density, matched mRNA abundance, rank, and simpler codon-baseline controls. In the current finite memory, modeled tAI or stAI weights may be used as bounded codon-adaptation rows, but they are not the same thing as observed ribosome occupancy. Likewise, a ribosome-profiling contact or a matched mRNA-control contact names a possible row source; it does not by itself create a positive $S^{QT}$ entry. The admissible support condition is local and numeric:
$$\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} > 0
\end{aligned}$$
under the declared controls and baseline ablation. If every controlled entry is non-positive, or if the readout matrix is rank-degenerate, the translation-readout claim fails for this packet.

\paragraph{Finite supporting neighborhood.}
Several nearby finite rows constrain the interpretation. The universal core tRNA-adaptation row passed on the same scale of $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The two-axis closure row reports \texttt{third\_axis\_present}, with the reason that $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms. The third-axis identity row identifies $d_{\perp}$ provisionally with within-family usage frequency, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The third-axis mechanism row reports \texttt{selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs\_external}, because the GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. These rows prevent the present $f_3$ statement from being collapsed into a generic GC3 or codon-usage statement.

\paragraph{Mechanism boundary.}
A separate powered boundary-set row reports that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. That assay computed $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations, and used $\alpha=0.05$. This row supplies a finite tRNA-supply pressure for $R$ avoidance, but it still does not prove observed ribosome dynamics for the $f_3$-stress coordinate. It says that a plausible supply-side asymmetry exists in a powered organism panel. The present chapter therefore keeps mechanism language conditional: wobble or tRNA asymmetry can activate the coordinate as a translation-readout target, but the actual translation survival row remains a controlled readout requirement.

\paragraph{Higher-layer non-promotion.}
The fourth-axis rows sharpen the local boundary. Three-axis sufficiency reports \texttt{fourth\_mechanism\_present}; codon-pair testing reports \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}; yeast elongation-dwell testing reports \texttt{partial}; mRNA-stability testing reports \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}; and translation-accuracy testing reports \texttt{not\_accuracy}. These classifications are useful for excluding several simple explanations, but none of them promotes the $f_3$ residual coordinate to protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality. Such claims require their own layer-matched contact and their own controlled statistic. In particular, protein abundance, localization, stability, interaction, structure, function, and phenotype are not read from $B^*_{Q6}$ geometry, from tAI or stAI weights, from codon tables, from the empty $f_3$ boundary, or from a workflow-ready route. The only claim made here is the bounded one: in the powered finite panel, non-tRNA residual optimality is led by the $f_3$ translation-selection axis, and its biological contact is limited to controlled translation-readout survival where a nondegenerate matched row is actually supplied.
