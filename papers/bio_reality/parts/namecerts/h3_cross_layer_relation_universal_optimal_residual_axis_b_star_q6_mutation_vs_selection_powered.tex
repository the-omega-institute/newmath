\subsection{The $f_3$ residual axis as a bounded translation-readout conjecture}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-residual-axis}

\paragraph{Subject.}
This chapter isolates a finite coding-sequence residual coordinate surface in which the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate is restricted to the $f_3$-stress direction after amino-acid projection.  The object is not a protein-state claim.  It is a claim about whether a synonymous residual coordinate, after removing amino-acid composition and standard sequence covariates, remains aligned with a translation-layer readout in a rank-powered multi-organism panel.  The observed finite computation for the named powered assay reports status passed with experiment run $2a526c9fb7f4$, $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous-design columns, $11$ organisms computed out of $16$ requested, and a minimum inclusion threshold of $500$ proteins per organism.  In that assay the non-tRNA residual optimality direction is classified as $f_3$ translation selection:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dominant\_axis}(B^{*}_{Q6}) &= \operatorname{f3\_translation\_selection},\\
N_{\mathrm{org,computed}} &= 11,\\
N_{\mathrm{org,requested}} &= 16,\\
N_{\mathrm{design}} &= 41.
\end{aligned}
$$
The statement is therefore a finite-row claim about the residual axis selected by the powered panel, not a free-standing biological law.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The $f_3$ saturation record supplies the finite codon boundary on which the residual direction is read.  The set $M=\operatorname{Med}_{R}\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains $20$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
After $f_3$ saturation the recorded $R$ surface removes $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and leaves $18$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary set is empty.  Thus the coordinate tested here is not introduced by an unaccounted boundary codon; it is read as a residual imbalance inside a finite saturated codon surface.  The biological interpretation is deliberately narrower than the coordinate construction: the coordinate is allowed to ask for a translation-readout row, but it is not itself a ribosome measurement.

\paragraph{Readout rule.}
The admissible readout is a controlled survival entry $S_{f_3,T}$ for a translation-layer observable $T$, such as bounded tAI/stAI weights or a measured ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency row when such rows are joined to the same coding sequences.  The local support condition is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3,T}>0
\end{aligned}
$$
under amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA control when translation efficiency is used, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon-baseline ablation.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ matrix over three organisms is treated as a null boundary, not as survival.  Likewise, a modeled tAI or stAI surface is a bounded codon-adaptation readout and is not the same datum as observed ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Linked finite evidence.}
The surrounding powered computations constrain the interpretation of the $f_3$ call.  The tRNA-poverty assay for the $R$ sense codons used $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested, with $6$ skipped organisms, $1000$ permutations, and $\alpha=0.05$; it reports systematic tRNA poverty for the $R$ sense codons under GtRNAdb copy number plus dos Reis wobble weights.  The universal core tRNA-adaptation assay passed with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous-design columns, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, and the same $500$ protein minimum.  The two-axis closure assay also used $5$ folds and reported a positive residual condition: the $d_{\perp}$ direction retains systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed organism directions, so a third axis remains present.  The third-axis identity assay provisionally identifies $d_{\perp}$ with within-family usage frequency, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  Its mechanism assay reports selection rather than GC as the retained direction, while also refusing a completed CSC or positional identity without additional contact.  These rows make the $f_3$ residual axis plausible as a translation-adjacent residual direction, but they do not by themselves supply a measured same-CDS ribosome or TE matrix.

\paragraph{Residual spectrum.}
The per-organism axis-strength spectrum was computed for $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and at least $500$ proteins per organism.  Its captured spectra are led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, then by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; the per-organism dominant count is $11$ for $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$.  The largest GC3 rank effect in that spectrum is $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with Spearman-type rank coefficient $\rho=0.136$.  The domain means are descriptive only, because the computed domain counts are imbalanced: $1$ archaeal organism, $2$ bacterial organisms, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms.  This spectrum supports a finite residual-axis ordering, not a universal taxonomy of translation selection across life.

\paragraph{Exclusions from fourth-axis probes.}
Several candidate downstream interpretations are already constrained by the finite rows.  A three-axis sufficiency assay reports a fourth mechanism present, with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and the $500$ protein threshold.  The fourth-mechanism probes do not license broad promotion: the mRNA-stability probe classifies the tested axis as not dominated by mRNA stability; the codon-pair probe reports $\operatorname{not\_codon\_pair}$ with $50$ shuffle nulls; the translation-accuracy probe reports $\operatorname{not\_accuracy}$ with $200$ permutations; and the elongation-dwell probe is only partial in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  These outcomes keep the $f_3$ residual axis inside a translation-readout boundary rather than turning it into a completed mechanism.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The claim is falsified at this layer if every controlled $f_3$-stress translation-readout score is non-positive or rank-degenerate.  Equivalently, for the relevant finite panel,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\forall T\quad S_{f_3,T}\leq 0
\end{aligned}
$$
after the declared controls and baseline ablations.  It is also blocked if the only available surface is a small rank-saturated matrix, or if the contact consists only of codon usage, tRNA copy number, or modeled adaptation weights without a measured or explicitly bounded translation row.  A positive modeled coupling can motivate the search, but it is not the same as a controlled survival statistic.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
Nothing in this chapter asserts protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universality.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  In particular, $B^{*}_{Q6}$ codon geometry, $f_3$ imbalance, wobble-weight asymmetry, a passed powered residual-axis assay, or the notation $S_{f_3,T}$ cannot be promoted into ribosome occupancy, protein output, folding, function, or a general biological law without the corresponding empirical row.  The present chapter records a bounded residual-axis result and the exact survival condition needed for its translation-readout interpretation.
