\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress residual axis as a bounded translation-readout conjecture}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-f3-stress-translation-readout}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This BioReality packet concerns a finite coding-sequence surface: organism-indexed synonymous codon residual coordinates, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate whose distinguished component is the $f_3$-stress direction, together with translation-layer rows that are allowed to be read only as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The coordinate is formed only after amino-acid projection, so the signal is not an amino-acid composition effect by construction. The finite saturation record fixes the relevant codon support. The set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_3$ saturation of $R$ removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from this displayed carrier:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the recorded $f_3$ boundary set is empty. Thus the chapter does not start from an unbounded biological narrative; it starts from a finite codon-coordinate surface and asks whether that surface leaves a controlled trace in translation-readout rows.

\paragraph{Residual-axis observation.}
The powered residual-axis assay reports status passed for the claim identifier $\texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.universal\_optimal\_residual\_axis.b\_star\_q6\_mutation\_vs\_selection\_powered}$. In that assay, the non-tRNA residual optimality direction aligns most strongly with $f_3$ translation selection. The dominant axis is recorded as $\texttt{f3\_translation\_selection}$, with $11$ organisms computed out of $16$ requested, $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. This is the central finite-row content of the chapter: after the tRNA-core component is separated, the residual optimum is not best described as a generic mutation coordinate; in the recorded assay it is led by the $f_3$ translation-selection direction.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding spectrum.}
The residual-axis result is coherent with the broader $B^*_{Q6}$ powered spectrum but does not collapse that spectrum to a single law. The universal core tRNA-adaptation assay also passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The two-axis closure assay passed with the conclusion that a third axis remains present, because the perpendicular residual component has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms. The third-axis identity assay reports provisional alignment to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The third-axis mechanism assay records the call $\texttt{selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs\_external}$, because the GC-orthogonalized perpendicular direction keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. The axis-strength spectrum further reports $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and per-organism dominance by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ in all $11$ computed organisms; its largest GC3 rank effect is small, $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$. These facts place the $f_3$ residual axis inside a finite rank-powered spectrum rather than inside an unconstrained biological assertion.

\paragraph{Mechanistic break condition.}
The $f_3$ coordinate becomes biologically testable only where the translation context breaks the third-position pairing symmetry. In finite-row terms, the relevant asymmetry is a context tRNA or wobble-weight inequality
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Without such an inequality, a paired synonymous codon contrast can remain a coordinate distinction while failing to become a translation-readout distinction. The boundary-set tRNA-poverty assay supplies one concrete mechanism-side support point: the $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. That assay passed with $40$ organisms computed out of $46$ requested, $6$ skipped organisms, significance level $\alpha=0.05$, and $1000$ permutations. This supports a tRNA-supply route for $R$ avoidance, but it is still not a measured ribosome or translation-efficiency survival row for the $f_3$-stress coordinate.

\paragraph{Translation-readout statistic.}
The bounded readout for this chapter is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is one of tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. A positive chapter-level readout requires same-organism and, where the row is CDS-level, same-CDS joins among codon counts, wobble or tRNA weights, translation readouts, and matched mRNA abundance. The control set includes amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, matched mRNA where the readout is translation efficiency, residualized rank, and simpler codon baselines. The local support and break conditions are therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{support} &:\quad \exists j\; S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0\ \text{after all declared controls and baseline ablation},\\
\text{break} &:\quad \forall j\; S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}\leq 0\ \text{or the fitted matrix is rank-degenerate}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The three-organism all-$1.0$ controlled surface is not counted as survival in this chapter, because it is rank-saturated rather than powered evidence. Likewise, modeled tAI or stAI coupling can define a bounded translation-weight row, but by itself it is not observed ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Negative promotion rule.}
This chapter makes no claim that the $f_3$-stress residual affects protein abundance, folded structure, localization, stability, molecular function, phenotype, physical admissibility, mechanism, or universality. None of those conclusions follows from codon topology, residual-axis geometry, tRNA-copy summaries, tAI or stAI weights, or workflow readiness. A downstream claim would require its own layer-matched contact and its own statistic after the translation-readout step is non-vacuous and positive. The fourth-mechanism assays reinforce the need for this local discipline: the mRNA-stability assay passed with conclusion $\texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$; the codon-pair assay passed with conclusion $\texttt{not\_codon\_pair}$ and $50$ shuffle nulls; the translation-accuracy assay passed with conclusion $\texttt{not\_accuracy}$ using $200$ permutations on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested; and the elongation-dwell assay is only partial in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. These results bound, rather than enlarge, the interpretation.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite-row evidence supports a precise residual-axis statement: in the powered $B^*_{Q6}$ assays, the non-tRNA residual optimum is led by $f_3$ translation selection, while the testable biological claim is restricted to controlled translation-readout survival in contexts where wobble or tRNA weights break $f_3$ symmetry. The current chapter is therefore a ready bounded record of the residual-axis claim and its readout gate. It remains deliberately silent about every higher biological layer until a separate measured contact supplies that layer.
