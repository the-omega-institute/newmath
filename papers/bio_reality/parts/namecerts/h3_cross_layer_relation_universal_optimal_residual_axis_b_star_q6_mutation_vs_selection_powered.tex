\subsection{The $f_3$-stress residual axis as a bounded translation-readout target}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-residual-axis}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns the finite CDS-by-organism residual coordinate surface obtained after amino-acid quotienting and then restricted to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate whose residual direction is the $f_3$-stress axis. The finite codon-side boundary is explicit. The ambient marked set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $20$ listed codons. The $f_3$-saturated $R$ surface removes $ACA$ and $ACG$ and leaves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
& UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the saturated side has $18$ listed codons and the supplied $f_3$ boundary residue is the empty list. Thus the chapter is not treating an unbounded codon universe: it is working on a finite residual coordinate surface with a named saturated codon row and a zero recorded boundary list.

\paragraph{Claim tested by the finite rows.}
The tested row states that non-tRNA residual optimality aligns most strongly with the axis named $f3\_translation\_selection$. The corresponding powered experiment used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, requested $16$ organisms, computed $11$ organisms, and used $41$ synonymous design columns. Its status is passed, and its dominant axis is recorded as $f3\_translation\_selection$. In the present chapter this is read narrowly: among the modeled non-tRNA residual alternatives available in the supplied design matrix, the leading residual optimality direction is the $f_3$-translation-selection direction. It is not, by itself, a measured ribosome-occupancy claim, a protein-output claim, or a general biological law.

\paragraph{Rank and axis context.}
The finite-row context is consistent with a multi-axis residual picture rather than a single codon-usage direction. The core tRNA-adaptation row was also passed with $5$ folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The two-axis closure row reported a third axis present, with the stated reason that $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms. The third-axis identity row identified $d_{\perp}$ with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The mechanism row for $d_{\perp}$ reported selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, and a CSC or positional identity requiring further contact, with the reason that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. These rows place the $f_3$-translation-selection call inside a finite residual spectrum: it is a leading residual direction after a tRNA core and alongside a distinct third direction, not a collapse of all residual biology into one scalar.

\paragraph{Per-organism strength spectrum.}
The axis-strength spectrum was computed for $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again under $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The captured spectra were led on average by $d_{resid4}$ and then by $d_{tRNA}$; the per-organism dominant counts were $\{d_{resid4}:11\}$. The row also records that domain means are descriptive only because the domain counts were imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms. The largest GC3 rank effect in this spectrum was $d_{usage}$ with $\rho=0.136$. This matters for the interpretation of the $f_3$-stress coordinate: the row supports a residual-axis ordering in the sampled panel, but its domain-level reading remains descriptive because the organism panel is not balanced across domains.

\paragraph{Wobble-breaking trigger.}
The biological trigger is not merely the topological presence of the $f_3$ coordinate. The coordinate becomes a candidate translation-readout distinction only when the paired contexts have unequal tRNA or wobble weights, written locally as $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$. The finite rows give one concrete supply-side boundary: the $R$ sense codons were systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts together with dos Reis wobble weights. That boundary experiment computed $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and passed. This supports a tRNA-supply route for $R$ avoidance, but the support remains at the supply and modeled-wobble layer. The translation-readout survival row still has to be supplied by organism-matched translation measurements or an explicitly bounded tAI or stAI readback kept separate from measured ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Downstream exclusions from the supplied rows.}
The finite-row packet also supplies negative or partial identity checks for possible fourth mechanisms. The three-axis sufficiency row concluded that a fourth mechanism is present, with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The mRNA-stability row concluded $fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability$, because yeast $d_{opt\_halflife}$ had low alignment with abundance $d_{resid4}$ under the fixed gates. The elongation-dwell row was partial for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The codon-pair row concluded $not\_codon\_pair$ using $50$ shuffle-null replicates, and the translation-accuracy row concluded $not\_accuracy$ with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested and $200$ permutations. These rows prevent the $f_3$-stress result from being inflated into a named downstream mechanism. They say that the residual spectrum contains structure beyond the first three axes, while the tested alternatives do not close that identity in the supplied data.

\paragraph{Bounded readout and refusal condition.}
The allowable readback is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ for a translation-layer target $T_j$, such as tAI, stAI, or a matched ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency row. The support condition is local and finite: a non-vacuous row must satisfy $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0$ after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls. A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface on only three organisms is therefore a refusal boundary rather than survival evidence. If the controlled entries remain non-positive, or if the design is saturated so that the apparent survival is only a rank artifact, the conjecture is not supported at the translation-readout layer.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter makes no claim that the $f_3$-stress residual coordinate changes protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism, or universality. Codon usage, tRNA-copy counts, and tAI or stAI weights can bound a translation-layer readout only within their own measurement class. Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency would supply a stronger translation contact only after same-organism and same-CDS joining with codon counts and matched mRNA controls. Protein abundance, structural order, physical admissibility, localization, stability, function, phenotype, and universal law each require a separate layer-matched contact and a separately recorded statistic. The present finite rows justify a chapter-level claim about a powered residual-axis alignment and its wobble-breaking translation-readout target; they do not promote that target beyond the bounded translation row.
