\subsection{The f3-Stress Residual Axis as a Bounded Translation-Readout Target}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-residual-axis}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The record studied here is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate restricted to the $f_3$-stress direction after amino-acid projection. The biological surface is finite: rows are coding-sequence by organism residual coordinates, and the admissible readout columns are translation-layer quantities such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, provided that the readout is matched to the same organism and coding-sequence rows. The finite saturation row fixes the relevant codon support. In the recorded $f_3$ saturation run, the set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The saturated $f_3$ image of $R$ removes exactly the two ACA/ACG entries and is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty. Thus the chapter does not begin from an open codon-topology edge; it begins from a closed finite codon support and asks whether the residual $f_3$ direction remains visible when translated into organism-level or CDS-level readout rows.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row outcome.}
The powered residual-axis experiment reports status passed for $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. Its conclusion is that non-tRNA residual optimality aligns most strongly with $f3\_translation\_selection$, and the dominant recorded axis is $f3\_translation\_selection$. This is a finite-row statement about the residual coordinate surface, not a general biological law. The experiment says that, after the tRNA-adaptation component is separated, the remaining optimality direction is best named as an $f_3$-coupled translation-selection axis within the assayed finite design. In the same family of runs, the boundary-set test gives a tRNA-supply contact for $R$ avoidance: $40$ organisms were computed out of $46$ requested, $6$ were skipped, $1000$ permutations were used, and the mechanism call says that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. This supports the biological plausibility of asking for a wobble-broken translation readout, but it does not itself supply measured ribosome or translation-efficiency rows.

\paragraph{Rank and spectrum context.}
The residual axis is not isolated from the rest of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ finite spectrum. The two-axis closure run computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested with $5$ folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns, and concluded that a third axis is present because $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed organism directions. The third-axis identity run, on the same $11$ of $16$ organism panel and the same $41$ synonymous design columns, provisionally identified $d_{\perp}$ with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The third-axis mechanism run then classified the signal as selection-not-GC, not wobble-clustered, with the CSC or positional identity still requiring an additional outside readout; its reason was that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. Finally, the per-organism axis-strength spectrum run computed $11$ organisms, used $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns, and found that captured spectra are led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; all $11$ per-organism dominant counts are $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, while the largest GC3 rank effect is only $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$. These rows make the $f_3$ residual-axis claim more specific: the axis is embedded in a finite, multi-axis residual spectrum rather than inferred from a single codon table.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
The admissible readback for this chapter is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a translation-layer row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The row is meaningful only after controlling for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, matched mRNA abundance where TE is used, and simpler codon baselines. The intended statistic has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0
\end{aligned}
$$
under the declared controls and baseline ablations. A positive bounded modeled-readout row may support a codon-adaptation distinction; a positive measured ribosome or TE row would support a measured translation-readout distinction. A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface on a three-organism panel is not counted as survival. Such a surface is a null rank boundary because it cannot separate the residual coordinate from the fitted design.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter-specific claim is therefore narrow. After amino-acid quotienting, the $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance is expected to be readable only where context tRNA or wobble weights break the third-position $f_3$ symmetry, and only as a translation-readout survival row under the finite controls. The recorded powered run gives the finite residual-axis result: $f3\_translation\_selection$ is the dominant non-tRNA residual optimality axis on the computed panel. It does not, by itself, say that the coordinate changes actual translation in vivo, changes protein abundance, determines folding, establishes physical admissibility, realizes molecular function, explains a mechanism, or states a universal law. Each of those stronger interpretations requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic. In particular, codon usage tables, tRNA gene-copy numbers, tAI or stAI weights, and ribosome-profiling contact names must remain separate kinds of evidence. The first three can justify bounded codon-adaptation and supply-asymmetry readbacks; measured ribosome or translation-efficiency rows are still required before the $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ entry can be promoted to measured translation survival.

\paragraph{Falsification boundary.}
The finite claim fails at the translation interface if the same-organism and same-CDS matrix, with codon counts, wobble weights, measured translation readouts, and matched mRNA controls joined, yields no positive controlled survival entry for $f_3$-stress. It also fails if the apparent signal vanishes after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, rank, and simpler-baseline controls, or if the only apparent success is a rank-saturated surface with no nondegenerate held-out distinction. Under those outcomes, the residual coordinate may remain a valid finite codon-geometric axis, but this chapter would not have evidence for a translation-readout relation.
