\subsection{Stop-Context Readthrough as a Cross-Dataset Boundary Record}
\label{sec:bioreality-stop-readthrough-crossdataset-wangen2020}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The stop-context readthrough record is a finite cross-dataset test on ordered nucleotide context rows rather than a general claim about translation termination.  The verified cross-dataset surface contains $n=55886$ rows and records an untreated rank association of $\rho=0.032718$ for the Wangen 2020 stop-context readthrough panel.  The associated run identifier is $7c85e126c888$, with a recorded runtime of $133.552797$ seconds.  This row count is the empirical carrier used here: each row is interpreted only as a finite stop-context observation with a matched readthrough score or rank-derived readback, not as a direct measurement of protein abundance, folding state, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or a universal termination rule.

\paragraph{Companion treatment surface.}
A second Wangen 2020 termination surface anchors the treated readthrough context.  In the G418-induced record, the joined table has $7658$ rows before filtering to nonzero RRTS values, and the nonzero subset contains $n=4982$ rows.  The nonzero fraction is $0.65056$, the endpoint is the G418-induced RRTS nonzero subset rank-averaged and scaled to $[0,1]$, the sheet is recorded as G418 $2.0\,\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{mL}$ at $24$ hours merged, and the runtime is $303.534$ seconds.  These values do not replace the cross-dataset untreated statistic; they show that the stop-context family has a separate finite treatment-conditioned readout whose row filtering, endpoint definition, and perturbation condition are explicitly different.

\paragraph{Statistic recorded locally.}
The cross-dataset entry can be summarized as a bounded rank-readout statement.  Let $X$ denote the stop-context coordinate used in the cross-dataset table and let $Y$ denote the untreated readthrough rank or score readback on the same finite row set.  The verified scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{cross}} &= 55886,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{untreated}}(X,Y) &= 0.032718,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{run}} &= 133.552797\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the companion induced surface, the recorded finite-row accounting is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 7658,\\
N_{\mathrm{nonzero}} &= 4982,\\
\frac{N_{\mathrm{nonzero}}}{N_{\mathrm{joined}}} &= 0.65056,\\
\tau_{\mathrm{G418}} &= 303.534\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mathematical content of the chapter is therefore not that stop-context geometry determines readthrough, but that a finite stop-context coordinate has been placed against a finite readthrough surface with a small observed untreated rank association and a separately documented perturbation-conditioned nonzero subset.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The observed untreated value $\rho=0.032718$ is a weak positive rank association on a large cross-dataset table.  It is not by itself a mechanism for stop-codon decoding, a causal estimate of release-factor failure, or a statement about all organisms or all stop contexts.  The G418-induced subset supplies a treatment-conditioned readout surface, but its nonzero filtering and rank-average scaling mean that it should not be collapsed into the untreated cross-dataset statistic.  The two finite records can be compared only as separate readout surfaces with different row definitions and conditions.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
The admissible claim is a narrow one: Wangen 2020 stop-context rows support a bounded cross-dataset readthrough record with $55886$ untreated rows and a measured untreated rank association of $0.032718$, while the companion G418-induced table supplies a distinct nonzero readthrough subset of $4982$ rows out of $7658$ joined rows.  This supports the existence of a finite stop-context/readthrough relation suitable for controlled boundary analysis.  It does not support any statement that codon context alone realizes translation output, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Required controls for promotion.}
Any stronger statement would require same-row controls beyond the finite row counts recorded above.  At minimum, a promoted translation-readout claim would need matched transcript abundance, condition labels, stop-codon identity, local nucleotide context, coverage and mappability checks, positional or sequence-composition controls, and simpler-baseline ablations on the same rows.  A mechanism claim would additionally require a perturbation design that separates release-factor availability, near-cognate tRNA effects, drug response, and transcript-specific measurement bias.  A protein-output claim would require a separate matched protein readout.  A folding, physical-admissibility, functional, phenotype, or universal-law claim would require its own layer-specific contact and cannot be inferred from the stop-context/readthrough table itself.
