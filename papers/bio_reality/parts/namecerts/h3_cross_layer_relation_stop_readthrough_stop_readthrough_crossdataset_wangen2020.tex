\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record considered here is the stop-context readthrough cross-dataset surface indexed by
\texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.stop\_readthrough.stop\_readthrough\_crossdataset\_wangen2020}.  Its finite table contains $n = 55886$ joined stop-context rows, with the verified run identifier \texttt{7c85e126c888}.  The measured untreated cross-dataset rank association is small but nonzero, with
$$\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{untreated}} &= 0.032718,\\
n_{\mathrm{cross}} &= 55886,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 133.552797\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}$$
This makes the object a weak but concrete readout boundary: the table is large enough to prevent a purely anecdotal interpretation, while the observed association is far too small to justify a broad mechanistic law.  The finite-row statement is therefore not that stop-context geometry determines readthrough, but that a bounded rank-level relation remains measurable when the readout is taken from a Wangen2020 cross-dataset stop-readthrough panel.

n_{\mathrm{joined,before}} &= 7658,\\

The two contacts do not have the same endpoint.  The cross-dataset untreated row set estimates a weak reproducible direction across a larger joined surface, while the G418 subset isolates a pharmacologically induced, nonzero readthrough response.  Their coexistence supports a conservative statement: stop-context readthrough is a valid finite-row cross-layer readout target, but its direction and effect size depend on endpoint definition, conditioning, and the chosen row filter.

\paragraph{Controlled mathematical reading.}
Let $T$ denote the finite stop-context table, and let each row carry a stop-context feature vector $x_r$, a dataset or condition tag $d_r$, and a measured readthrough score $y_r$.  The verified cross-dataset statistic is a rank association rather than a causal coefficient.  A compact form of the admissible statement is
$$\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{stop}}(T) &= \operatorname{rankcorr}(g(x_r,d_r),y_r\mid r\in T),\\
S_{\mathrm{stop}}(T_{\mathrm{cross}}) &= 0.032718,\\
|T_{\mathrm{cross}}| &= 55886.
\end{aligned}$$
Here $g$ is only a row-level stop-context score or learned ranking function supplied by the finite analysis.  The statistic may be used as evidence that the stop-context layer has a measurable readthrough boundary in this dataset family.  It may not be read as a universal termination mechanism, a protein-output law, or an unconditional rule for all genes and conditions.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
The chapter deliberately stops at measured readthrough.  No claim about translation efficiency in general, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality follows from $\rho_{\mathrm{untreated}}=0.032718$ or from the G418 nonzero subset.  Those claims would require separate row-matched contacts at the corresponding layer, with the same identifiers, controls, and held-out checks attached before promotion.  In particular, a stop-context readthrough score is not a ribosome-occupancy profile, not a proteomics abundance measurement, and not a folding or function assay.  It is a bounded readback of termination-context behavior.

\paragraph{Audit verdict.}
The finite evidence is sufficient for a standalone BioReality entry because it supplies a named measured surface, a large cross-dataset row count, a concrete rank statistic, and a nearby condition-specific Wangen2020 panel with explicit filtering numbers.  The supported conclusion is intentionally narrow: Wangen2020 stop-context rows provide a finite cross-dataset readthrough boundary with weak untreated rank signal and a separate G418-conditioned nonzero readthrough surface.  The record remains open only for stronger downstream interpretations, not for the existence of the finite readthrough contact itself.
