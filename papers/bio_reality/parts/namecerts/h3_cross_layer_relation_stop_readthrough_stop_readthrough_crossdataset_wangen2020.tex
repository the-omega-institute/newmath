\subsection{Stop-readthrough cross-dataset boundary for position-resolved $f_3$ stress}
\label{sec:bioreality-stop-readthrough-crossdataset-wangen2020}

\paragraph{Finite table.}
This record treats the stop-readthrough cross-dataset Wangen panel as a finite cross-layer test surface rather than as a general law of translation.  The relevant finite carrier is a table of coding-sequence windows whose rows must contain an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate, local synonymous-codon counts, a wobble-weight context, a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, an mRNA control, and amino-acid or position-bin covariates.  The currently recorded Wangen cross-dataset run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.stop\_readthrough.stop\_readthrough\_crossdataset\_wangen2020} has run id \texttt{7c85e126c888}, row count $n=55886$, runtime $133.552797$ seconds, and untreated rank correlation $\rho=0.032718$.  A related Wangen G418 stop-context readthrough run, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.translation\_termination.stop\_context\_readthrough\_g418\_wangen2020}, has run id \texttt{fe18fff6841f}; it joins $7658$ candidate rows before filtering, retains $n=4982$ nonzero RRTS rows, and records a nonzero fraction $0.65056$ for the endpoint described as G418-induced RRTS, rank-averaged and scaled to $[0,1]$.  These numbers are large enough to ground a finite row-level chapter, but they do not by themselves supply the full same-window $f_3$ survival statistic.

\paragraph{Statistic requested by the claim.}
The proposed statistic is a controlled survival entry for $f_3$ stress against a position-resolved translation readout.  Its admissible form is not an unrestricted association between codon geometry and biological effect.  It is the held-out improvement of an $f_3$ residual coordinate after the amino-acid quotient, with the wobble surface kept asymmetric only when the data actually support $w_c \ne w_c^{f_3}$.  In finite-row notation the intended comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)
  &= \Delta_{\mathrm{held\mbox{-}out}}\bigl(\mathrm{model}(T_{\mathrm{position}}; Z, f_3)\bigr) \\
  &\quad - \Delta_{\mathrm{held\mbox{-}out}}\bigl(\mathrm{model}(T_{\mathrm{position}}; Z)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes amino-acid composition or amino-acid bins, position bins, GC3, CDS length, M-density, local codon-count controls, matched mRNA abundance, coverage and mappability limits, and simpler translation baselines.  The stop-readthrough cross-dataset number $\rho=0.032718$ is therefore treated as a weak observed cross-dataset association on the recorded readthrough surface, not as the requested position-window survival entry.  The G418 subset supplies a second finite Wangen readout surface with $4982$ nonzero rows, but it is still a perturbation-specific stop-context endpoint rather than a completed $f_3$ window statistic.

\paragraph{Rank and comparison discipline.}
The cross-layer decision is allowed only after rank sufficiency and baseline ablation.  The chapter therefore separates three objects: the residual coordinate, the observed stop-readthrough or occupancy readout, and the controlled rank-powered survival relation between them.  A positive entry requires held-out improvement after the full control set and after simpler codon, position, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA, and amino-acid baselines have had their chance to explain the same readout.  A linked translation-conditioned structural-order run, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.translation\_conditioned\_structural\_order\_boundary.b\_star\_q6\_powered}, records a passed status with fold count $5$, joined structural-order counts $3412$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$ with sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.  That linked result shows how a controlled cross-layer surface is recorded when a completed statistic exists.  The present stop-readthrough cross-dataset entry is narrower: it has a finite Wangen readout and a measured cross-dataset scalar, but the same-CDS, same-window $f_3$ survival matrix remains the object that must be computed before any promotion is allowed.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite evidence supports a boundary claim: Wangen stop-readthrough data provide a concrete translation-adjacent readout surface on which an $f_3$-stress window statistic can be tested.  The observed untreated $\rho=0.032718$ is compatible with a small cross-dataset signal, while the G418 run shows that a perturbation-specific readthrough endpoint can be represented on thousands of finite rows after nonzero filtering.  The biological content of the claim is therefore conditional.  The $f_3$ member of the $B^*_Q6$ residual family may be discussed here only as a candidate coordinate whose survival must be checked against row-matched translation readouts under controls.  It is not a measured cause of readthrough, not a translation mechanism, and not a statement about protein output.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from the $f_3$ coordinate, the stop-readthrough association, the wobble-weight notation, or the codon-window geometry alone.  Such claims require a separate layer-matched contact and a finite statistic on the corresponding rows.  In particular, modeled tAI or stAI weights may serve as bounded codon-adaptation annotations, but they are not observed ribosome occupancy.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control covariate, not a translation readout.  The present chapter is therefore ready as a finite English TeX record of the stop-readthrough cross-dataset boundary, with the explicit remaining requirement that the same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon, wobble-weight, mRNA, and position-resolved footprint rows be joined before $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)>0$ can be reported.
