\subsection{Stop-readthrough cross-dataset boundary for an $f_3$-stress translation-survival claim}\label{sec:bioreality-stop-readthrough-crossdataset-wangen2020}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The finite object considered here is a coding-sequence or termination-context table in which each admissible row is allowed to carry three different kinds of coordinates: a synonymous residual coordinate such as an $f_3$-stress score after amino-acid quotienting, local codon or stop-context descriptors, and a matched readout drawn from a translation-side assay. The verified Wangen cross-dataset stop-readthrough run supplies a concrete finite surface with $n=55886$ rows, runtime $133.552797$ seconds, and an untreated cross-dataset rank association
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{untreated}} = 0.032718 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This number is usable as a bounded cross-dataset stop-readthrough datum, not as a general translation law. Its magnitude is small and its interpretation is tied to the row construction, endpoint definition, and cross-dataset join used by the run. A second Wangen-derived termination contact records a G418-induced readthrough surface: before filtering, $7658$ rows were inner-joined; after restricting to the nonzero RRTS subset, $n=4982$ rows remained, with nonzero fraction $0.65056$, runtime $303.534$ seconds, and endpoint described as the G418-induced RRTS nonzero subset rank-averaged onto $[0,1]$ from the sheet \texttt{G418 2.0mg|mL 24hr merged}. These two finite facts show that stop-context readthrough can be represented as a row-level biological readout, but they do not by themselves supply the same-window $f_3$-stress survival statistic demanded by the present cross-layer claim.

\paragraph{Coordinate to be tested.}
The proposed coordinate is the $f_3$ member of the residual basis after amino-acid information has been quotiented out. In a powered version of the test, each row would contain an ordered CDS or local window, synonymous codon counts, an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate $q_{f_3}$, a context-specific wobble-weight surface, and a translation readout $T_{\mathrm{pos}}$ such as position-resolved ribosome occupancy or an explicitly bounded translation-efficiency measurement. The survival statistic is local and conditional:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes amino-acid or position-bin controls, GC3, coding length, methionine density, matched mRNA abundance, codon-count baselines, coverage and mappability filters, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations. The Wangen stop-readthrough facts contribute finite readout discipline: they show row counts, filtering, rank scaling, and cross-dataset association for termination-context readthrough. They do not yet instantiate the required same-CDS, same-window tuple $(q_{f_3},T_{\mathrm{pos}},Z)$.

\paragraph{Wobble asymmetry requirement.}
The $f_3$ direction is not allowed to become a biological claim merely because a residual coordinate can be computed. The proposed test requires an observed asymmetry between the context weight $w_c$ and its $f_3$-shifted counterpart $w_c^{f_3}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_c \neq w_c^{f_3} .
\end{aligned}
$$
This asymmetry is a gate on whether the coordinate is even eligible for a translation-side readback. It is not a substitute for the readback. Modeled tAI or stAI weights may contribute a bounded codon-adaptation surface when they are explicitly row-matched, but they are not observed ribosome footprints and they are not protein abundance measurements. Likewise, the untreated Wangen cross-dataset value $\rho_{\mathrm{untreated}}=0.032718$ is evidence about a stop-readthrough association on its own finite panel, not evidence that a wobble-broken $f_3$ statistic survives position-resolved ribosome-occupancy controls.

\paragraph{Rank and break condition.}
The admissible positive result is not a raw correlation. It is a controlled finite-row improvement over the specified null family. In the present notation, a row panel passes only if the $f_3$ term gives held-out improvement after the controls and ablations have already absorbed amino-acid composition, position or stop-context bins, GC3, length, methionine density, mRNA abundance, and simpler codon summaries. Formally, the local claim has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{f_3}
  &= \operatorname{score}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z,q_{f_3})
     - \operatorname{score}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z),\\
\Delta_{f_3} &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
under held-out evaluation and adequate rank. The break condition is the failure of this strict improvement, or any attempt to replace the missing joined statistic with review status, contact naming, modeled wobble weights, or codon geometry alone. The available Wangen facts remain useful because they provide concrete finite readout surfaces: $55886$ rows for the untreated cross-dataset stop-readthrough association, and $4982$ nonzero rows after the G418 RRTS filter from $7658$ joined rows. Those facts support a termination-readthrough boundary record, not the stronger $f_3$ position-resolved translation-survival claim.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded cross-layer proposal with a strict non-promotion rule. The finite Wangen readthrough panels show that termination-context readout can be represented and filtered at row level, and they give numerical anchors for the stop-readthrough side of the subject. They do not establish translation realization for $f_3$-stress, do not establish protein abundance, do not imply folding or physical admissibility, and do not identify a mechanism. No function claim, phenotype claim, or universality claim follows from these rows. Such claims would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled finite statistics. The only local admissible statement is that the $f_3$-stress coordinate remains a testable candidate for a same-row translation-readout survival entry once the missing position-resolved occupancy or bounded translation-efficiency rows, matched mRNA controls, wobble-weight rows, and full control matrix are attached.
