\subsection{Stop-context readthrough as a bounded cross-dataset translation readout}\label{sec:bioreality-stop-readthrough-crossdataset-wangen2020}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite table of stop-context rows drawn from the Wangen 2020 readthrough setting and used only at the translation-readout boundary.  The row object is deliberately narrow: each row records a stop-codon context, the associated sequence features available to the stop-context analysis, and a readthrough or readthrough-related response.  The verified cross-dataset run for the present claim used $n=55886$ finite rows, completed in $133.552797$ seconds, and returned an untreated rank correlation of $\rho=0.032718$.  A separate G418-induced termination run on the same evidence family used the endpoint described as the nonzero G418-induced RRTS subset, rank-averaged and scaled to $[0,1]$; before filtering it joined $7658$ rows, after the nonzero filter it retained $n=4982$ rows, with nonzero fraction $0.65056$, and completed in $303.534$ seconds.  These numbers are the whole empirical payload used here: the chapter does not promote them to a general law of termination, translation, or protein production.

\paragraph{Coordinate and statistic.}
The coordinate under discussion is the $f_3$-stress residual member of the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ family, but in this chapter it is not treated as a measured biological object by itself.  It is a finite coordinate that becomes empirically meaningful only after a same-row translation readout has been attached.  The intended survival statistic has the form $S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$, where $T_{\mathrm{position}}$ is a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy, translation-efficiency, or stop-context readthrough readout, and $Z$ contains amino-acid or position-bin controls, GC3, length, M-density, codon-count controls, mRNA abundance where available, coverage and mappability limits, and simpler baseline models.  The cross-dataset Wangen stop-context run supplies a finite stop-readthrough response surface, not a complete position-window ribosome-occupancy matrix for $f_3$-stress survival.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) > 0
&\quad \text{means held-out improvement after the listed controls,}\\
S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) \leq 0
&\quad \text{means no surviving finite readout contribution is reported.}
\end{aligned}
$$

The observed untreated cross-dataset association, $\rho=0.032718$ over $55886$ rows, is therefore a weak finite readout contact.  It is compatible with the existence of a small stop-context signal, but it is not by itself a powered $f_3$-stress survival entry, because the required same-CDS, same-window codon counts, wobble-weight surface, matched mRNA control, and position-resolved footprint rows are not present in this record.

\paragraph{Readthrough-specific interpretation.}
The Wangen-style termination data are useful because stop-context readthrough is already located at a translation boundary.  The G418-induced run shows that a filtered response can be represented as a finite nonzero readout panel: $4982$ nonzero rows out of $7658$ joined rows, with the response rank-averaged into $[0,1]$.  That is enough to justify treating stop context as a real readout surface for finite-row testing.  It is not enough to infer that the $f_3$-stress coordinate controls readthrough, nor that readthrough behavior transfers to protein abundance, folding, localization, function, phenotype, or physical admissibility.

The distinction is important.  A stop-context table can support questions of the form: does a controlled sequence coordinate improve prediction of readthrough or readthrough-like response in held-out rows?  It cannot support claims of the form: the coordinate is a mechanism, a universal termination rule, or a direct determinant of downstream protein state.  The present verified cross-dataset statistic is a small untreated association; the G418 statistic is a separate finite response panel under drug-induced conditions.  Neither statistic supplies the full joined matrix required for an $f_3$-stress position-resolved translation-survival claim.

\paragraph{Relation to other bounded translation records.}
The broader finite-row audit contains a separate translation-conditioned structural-order boundary run with two joined organism panels: $3412$ joined rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ joined rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  In that run the weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $0.004764742175882462$, the aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ values were positive in both organisms with mean $0.005284160876443678$, and the sign-flip probability for being greater than zero was $0.0029296875$.  That separate record shows what a positive controlled cross-layer result looks like when a matched finite panel and full controls are present.  The stop-readthrough cross-dataset record in this chapter is not at that evidential level for $f_3$-stress position-resolved survival: it has row count and readthrough association, but it lacks the same-window $f_3$ coordinate/readout/control join required for the declared survival statistic.

\paragraph{Boundary and admissible conclusion.}
The admissible conclusion is therefore local and bounded.  The Wangen cross-dataset stop-readthrough result provides a finite translation-boundary contact with $55886$ rows and untreated $\rho=0.032718$, and the G418 readthrough panel confirms that a rank-scaled nonzero stop-context response can be represented on $4982$ filtered rows.  These facts make stop-context readthrough a legitimate candidate readout surface for future $f_3$-stress survival testing.  They do not show translation realization from codon or window geometry alone, do not make modeled tAI or stAI weights into observed ribosome occupancy, and do not justify protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
