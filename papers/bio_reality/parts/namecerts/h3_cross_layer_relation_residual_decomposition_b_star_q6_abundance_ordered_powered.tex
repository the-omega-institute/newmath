\subsection{Direction-aware residual decomposition for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance packet}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-residual-decomposition-direction-aware}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record studies the non-translation part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance signal as a finite ledger, not as a biological mechanism.  For each organism, the target quantity is the abundance component $P_Q=\Pi_{Q_e}P_e$ in the nine recorded coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The fixed ordered decomposition first removes measured translation-efficiency information $T$, then mRNA-stability information $S$ where available, and only then asks whether a small panel of downstream readouts $H$ absorbs a remaining part of the residual.  The module classification is therefore the cross-layer-relation module: codon-coordinate geometry is compared with measured or curated readouts, but the comparison is a projection ledger.

\paragraph{Ordered decomposition.}
The verified decomposition sums to one for all four computed organisms, with sum errors $0$ for Danio rerio, Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and $1.11\times 10^{-16}$ for Homo sapiens.  The retained ordered fractions are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Danio rerio:}        && T&=0.0243680958, & S&=\text{skipped}, & U&=0.9756319042,\\
\text{E. coli K-12:}       && T&=0.0003596477, & S&=0.0053579738, & U&=0.9942823785,\\
\text{Homo sapiens:}       && T&=0.0060119078, & S&=0.0061639914, & U&=0.9736442326,\\
\text{S. cerevisiae:}      && T&=0.1127139450, & S&=0.0196274205, & U&=0.8676586345.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding $P_Q$ energies and full-row counts are $605.0092169$ on $13140$ rows for Danio rerio, $225.8455632$ on $2517$ rows for E. coli, $286.3673197$ on $11250$ rows for Homo sapiens, and $229.8645079$ on $4759$ rows for S. cerevisiae.  Thus the finite result is mostly negative: the measured low-dimensional readouts leave $86.77\%$ to $99.43\%$ of the ordered residual unexplained.  The related minimal-compilation check reports median compressibility $0.026$ and explicitly rejects the strong claim that the measured dictionary compresses $P_Q$ across the computed organisms.

\paragraph{Scored $H$ dictionary.}
The $H$ stage uses absorbed energy $A_H$, a description-length term, null thresholds, and a join-shift penalty.  The direction-aware companion ranking also records $C_H$ and raw $A_HC_H$ with the stated regularizer $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL(H)=\log_2(1+n_{columns})$; that ranking is used here only to interpret whether high absorption points in the residual direction.  In the ordered decomposition table, Danio rerio keeps no $H$ readout: localization has $A_H=0.0176300654$ but $DL=12$, join shift $0.8521308980$, and score $-0.1560499134$; PTM density has $A_H=0.0145068517$, null95 $0.0017159597$, and score $-0.0388453333$; turnover is missing.  E. coli has only the mRNA-abundance placeholder row with $A_H=0$, $DL=1$, and score $-0.01$, while the other listed $H$ rows are unavailable.

In Danio rerio, measured TE has $A_H=0.0175942900$, $C_H=0.6573753096$, and $A_HC_H=0.0115660518$; mRNA has $A_H=0.0119730339$, $C_H=0.8965637854$, and $A_HC_H=0.0107345886$.  In Homo sapiens, complex membership has $A_H=0.0075272000$, $C_H=0.8723071163$, and $A_HC_H=0.0065660301$, while mRNA has $A_H=0.0078582228$, $C_H=0.7921354916$, and $A_HC_H=0.0062247772$.  Human PTM density has higher raw absorption, $A_H=0.0148848259$, but only $C_H=0.3604440742$, so its raw direction-aware product is $0.0053651473$.  In S. cerevisiae, mRNA leads the one-column ranking with $A_H=0.0018976055$, $C_H=0.6040571891$, and $A_HC_H=0.0011462622$.  In E. coli, mRNA has $A_H=0.0017706955$, $C_H=0.8639529831$, and $A_HC_H=0.0015297976$, but it does not exceed the Bonferroni threshold in the direction-aware table.  No shared universal $H$ is present; the best absorber is organism-specific and often penalized below zero after finite scoring.

\paragraph{Translation and residual context.}
Measured translation mediation is uneven across organisms.  The measured $M^{QTP}$ list is $0$ for Danio rerio after truncating a raw suppressive value $-0.0480148605$, $0.0409115834$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.4793069656$ for S. cerevisiae.  The modeled-translation complement is large in Danio, human, and gallus-like vertebrate rows: the complement fractions include $d=0.8746287210$ for Danio rerio, $d=0.6629960039$ for E. coli, $d=0.9208949356$ for Homo sapiens, $d=0.2117321957$ for S. cerevisiae, $d=0.9400341806$ for Gallus gallus, and $d=0.8972672019$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis.  The measured tRNA-copy tAI check gives Danio $A_H=0.0208171028$, human $A_H=5.4262293\times 10^{-5}$, and yeast $A_H=0.0015307477$, using $8846$, $431$, and $273$ tRNA genes respectively.  These numbers show a reproducible residual problem, not a single readout channel.

\paragraph{Boundary-set and meta-level diagnostics.}
The $R$ boundary set has $10$ sense codons.  Across $15$ computed organisms, the Window6 boundary $R$ set is avoided in all $15$ non-tie directional cases, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$; the six individually significant organisms are Homo sapiens, Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, Mus musculus, and Rattus norvegicus.  That boundary signature is a real finite association with abundance preference, but it is not a mechanism.  The organism-specificity meta table has $16$ rows, $12$ complete-feature organisms, and no resolved phylogenetically corrected law: the dominant-coordinate-versus-domain permutation test gives $p=0.5814185814$, while $M_{QTP}$ versus GC3 gives $r=-0.3062616500$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.3526473526$.

\paragraph{Reality boundary.}
The local conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite readback supports an ordered projection statement: after translation and mRNA-stability cuts, the measured $H$ dictionary explains little of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual, with one human PTM-density row passing the ordered score but no cross-organism absorber.  The row joins are part of the evidence: yeast turnover uses $3640$ joined proteins, reported join hit rate $0.8818572657$, and CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419$; human turnover uses $8057$ joined proteins from a larger $19053$ full human complement.  These finite joins justify the table, not a pathway.  No translation realization, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, protein function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from $A_H$, $C_H$, score, rank, $H_o^*$, or residual coordinate support unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that stronger statement.  Missing rows remain missing, weak rows remain weak, and high-energy wrong-direction rows are not promoted.
