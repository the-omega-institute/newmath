\subsection{Ordered decomposition of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-residual-decomposition}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.} The row studies the abundance component $P_Q=\Pi_{Q_e}P_e$ carried by the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates and then decomposes it by a fixed observational order: measured translation efficiency first, mRNA-stability where the joined table contains that readout, and finally a scored dictionary of downstream readouts $H$. The computation is per organism and per joined row set. It is therefore a finite ledger of residual energy, readout availability, description length, null comparison, join sensitivity, and unexplained mass; it is not a biological pathway diagram.

The computed $P_Q$ energies and row counts are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{Danio rerio}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606,\quad n=13140,\\
\textit{Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032,\quad n=2517,\\
\textit{Homo sapiens}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=286.3673197177688,\quad n=11250,\\
\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544,\quad n=4759.
\end{aligned}
$$
The decomposition diagnostics sum to one in every computed organism: exactly $1.0$ for danio, $1.0$ for E. coli, $0.9999999999999999$ for human with error $1.1102230246251565\times 10^{-16}$, and $1.0$ for yeast.

\paragraph{Ordered translation and stability terms.} The fixed-order partition leaves most of $P_Q$ outside the measured readouts. Danio has $T=0.02436809579891462$, no stability term on the emitted table, and unexplained fraction $U=0.9756319042010854$. E. coli has $T=0.00035964766234144823$, $S=0.005357973825835613$, and $U=0.9942823785118229$. Human has $T=0.006011907792542833$, $S=0.006163991439034291$, and $U=0.9736442326306781$. Thus the finite result is asymmetric. Yeast is translation-proximal in this table, but even there the residual mass remains $86.76586345294415\%$ unexplained. Human, danio, and E. coli remain mostly unexplained after the same ordered readout policy. The matching minimal-compilation calculation gives the same qualitative message: compressibility is $0.024368095798914635$ in danio, $0.005717621488177116$ in E. coli, $0.02635576736932188$ in human, and $0.13234136547055853$ in yeast, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ for each computed organism under that finite replay. The median compressibility is reported as $0.026$, so the panel does not support a compact universal measured-readout explanation of $P_Q$.

E. coli has only weak mRNA and measured-TE entries, both below the Bonferroni thresholds on that table. This is not a universal $H$; it is a set of organism-scoped finite readbacks.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.} The supported claim is narrow. The ordered decomposition is a reproducible variance partition on finite joined tables: measured TE, mRNA-stability where available, and scored $H$ readouts leave $U=0.9756319042010854$ in danio, $U=0.9942823785118229$ in E. coli, $U=0.9736442326306781$ in human, and $U=0.8676586345294415$ in yeast. The only kept entry in the non-directional $H$ dictionary is human PTM density, and even that is a descriptive residual absorber, not a mechanism. The stronger biological readings are excluded here: no translation realization beyond the measured contacts, no folding or physical-admissibility statement, no functional explanation, no causal pathway, no localization mechanism, and no universal cross-organism law follows from these scores. Any such promotion would require a separate layer-matched measurement and a separate statistic, not merely a positive absorption value, a favorable direction cosine, or a high rank in this finite table.
