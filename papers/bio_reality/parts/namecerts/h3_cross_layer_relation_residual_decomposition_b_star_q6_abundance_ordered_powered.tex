\subsection{Ordered decomposition of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{subsec:bioq6-ordered-residual-decomposition}

\paragraph{Finite object being measured.}
This record studies a finite per-organism decomposition of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component $P_Q$.  For each organism the same nine-coordinate residual basis is used, with coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The measured quantity is not a biological mechanism.  It is an ordered orthogonal variance partition: first a measured translation-efficiency readout $T$ is removed where available, then an mRNA-stability readout $S$ is removed where available, and finally a scored dictionary of finite candidate readouts $H$ is tested against the remaining residual.  The row therefore has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_Q &= P_T + P_S + P_H + U,\\
1 &= T_{fraction}+S_{fraction}+H_{fraction}+U_{fraction},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $U$ denoting the still-unexplained residual after the declared ordered cuts.  The order is part of the statistic: changing the order would change the attribution, so the numbers below are read only for this fixed protocol.

\paragraph{Rows and energy scale.}
The finite table was computed for four organisms.  The $P_Q$ energies and full-row counts were: Danio rerio, $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606$ on $13140$ rows; Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $\|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032$ on $2517$ rows; Homo sapiens, $\|P_Q\|^2=286.3673197177688$ on $11250$ rows; and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $\|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544$ on $4759$ rows.  The reported partitions close numerically: the diagnostic sums are $1.0$ for Danio, $1.0$ for E. coli, $0.9999999999999999$ for human with error $1.1102230246251565\times 10^{-16}$, and $1.0$ for yeast.  Thus the row is internally closed as a finite variance accounting exercise.

\paragraph{Ordered $T$ and $S$ cuts.}
The measured-TE cut is small in Danio, E. coli, and human, and largest in yeast.  The four $T$ fractions are $0.02436809579891462$ for Danio, $0.00035964766234144823$ for E. coli, $0.006011907792542833$ for human, and $0.11271394500088064$ for yeast.  The $S$ fraction is skipped for Danio, equals $0.005357973825835613$ for E. coli, equals $0.006163991439034291$ for human, and equals $0.019627420469677935$ for yeast.  The unexplained fractions after the ordered cuts are therefore large:
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{Danio} &= 0.9756319042010854,\\
U_{E.\ coli} &= 0.9942823785118229,\\
U_{human} &= 0.9736442326306781,\\
U_{yeast} &= 0.8676586345294415.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest translation-proximal case is yeast, where $T+S=0.13234136547055858$, but even there the residual remains mostly unexplained.  The minimal-compilation companion calculation gives the same message at the dictionary level: compressibility is $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio, $0.005717621488177116$ for E. coli, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, with median compressibility near $0.026$.  These are descriptive compression scores, not causal explanations.

\paragraph{Scored $H$ dictionary.}
The residual dictionary uses a score penalizing description length and join instability.  The finite rows include the absorbed energy $A_H$, a description-length charge $DL$, a join-shift penalty, a null $95\%$ reference, and a keep flag.  Danio has several nonzero absorptions but no kept $H$: localization has $A_H=0.017630065362242674$, $DL=12$, join shift $0.852130898021309$, null95 $0.011073433874378957$, and score $-0.15604991341320174$; ptm\_density has $A_H=0.014506851682279774$, $DL=1$, join shift $0.8327245053272451$, null95 $0.0017159596757307261$, and score $-0.03884533325981321$; tm\_count has $A_H=0.001295328773067384$ and score $-0.05209736705724147$; domain\_count has $A_H=0.001277188259514032$ and score $-0.05208455187690362$; complex\_member has $A_H=2.5035963817184764\times 10^{-5}$ and score $-0.053378360294640714$; turnover is unavailable.  E. coli has no computable feature, localization, or turnover rows in this dictionary, and its mRNA-abundance row is a zero-absorption bookkeeping row with score $-0.01$.

\paragraph{Direction and shared-channel check.}
A related direction-aware ranking shows why absorption alone is insufficient.  For human, ptm\_density has the largest raw energy among the single-column candidates in the ranking table, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its coordinate-support cosine is only $C_H=0.3604440741749213$, giving $A_H C_H=0.0053651472982577525$.  Human complex\_member is weaker in energy, $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, but better aligned, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, giving $A_H C_H=0.006566030115340095$.  Danio's top raw direction-aware row is measured\_te with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, and $A_H C_H=0.011566051846174291$, followed by mRNA with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, and $A_H C_H=0.01073458857797759$.  Yeast's largest direction-aware localization energy, $A_H=0.011225105608516642$, is penalized by $DL=3.700439718141092$ and is not a universal channel.  The best organisms differ: Danio favors measured\_te under the ranking rule, human favors complex\_member in the direction-aware table, yeast favors mRNA, and E. coli has only weak mRNA and measured\_te rows.  The record therefore supports organism-specific residual readback, not a shared biological absorber.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
The allowed conclusion is narrow.  The table says that, under a fixed ordered projection rule, most of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component remains unexplained by the measured low-dimensional readouts, with yeast showing the largest but still incomplete translation-proximal compression and with one human ptm\_density row passing the non-directional ordered score.  This does not establish translation realization, folding behavior, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, localization mechanism, phenotype, causation, or universality.  Missing rows remain missing, not negative evidence.  A nonzero $A_H$, a favorable direction cosine, a positive local score, or an organism-specific best readout is only a finite descriptive statistic.  Any stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched measurement and statistic designed for that stronger claim.
