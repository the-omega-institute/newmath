\subsection{Ordered residual decomposition of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-ordered-residual-decomposition}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite ledger.}
This record treats the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance signal as a finite per-organism ledger rather than as a biological explanation.  The measured object is the abundance component $P_Q=\Pi_{Q_e}P_e$ carried by the nine named $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates, after the same control block is removed: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The ordered decomposition then asks how much of $P_Q$ is absorbed first by measured translation efficiency $T$, then by mRNA-stability or transcript-side readouts $S$ where available, and finally by a small panel of candidate residual readouts $H$.  The $H$ panel is scored as a finite dictionary with absorbed energy $A_H$, dimensional cost $DL$, row-join shift, a dof-matched null95 value, and a keep/drop decision.  This is an orthogonal projection accounting scheme on matched rows; it is not a pathway model.

\paragraph{Ordered fractions.}
The four computed organisms all close numerically: the reported decomposition sums are $1.0000000000000000$ for Danio rerio, $1.0000000000000000$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.9999999999999999$ for Homo sapiens, and $1.0000000000000000$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The corresponding $P_Q$ energies and row counts are concrete and small enough to audit directly: Danio rerio uses $n=13140$ rows with $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$; E. coli uses $n=2517$ with $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$; human uses $n=11250$ with $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$; yeast uses $n=4759$ with $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$.  In the fixed order, the finite fractions are

For E. coli, the available dictionary is effectively absent apart from an mRNA-abundance row with $A_H=0$, $DL=1$, null95 $0$, and score $-0.01$; protein-feature, localization, and turnover rows remain missing rather than being treated as zero evidence.

Yeast has several above-null-looking absorptions but no kept row under the stated scoring rule: localization has $A_H=0.021173066463797872$, $DL=12$, null95 $0.005419222054817298$, and score $-0.11221001354437096$; PTM density has $A_H=0.006467661568183971$ and score $-0.0045079853396405925$; complex membership has $A_H=0.0026941809396506438$ and score $-0.008385088072707493$; turnover has $A_H=0.00232847477991429$ with a large join shift $0.3086782937591931$ and score $-0.02429227248076356$.  The result is not a shared residual channel: one human PTM-density row is selected in this ordered table, while Danio, E. coli, and yeast retain no selected $H$ row.

The broader finite context is consistent with an organism-specific residual.  The translation-complement residual fractions $d$ are $0.874628721049266$ for Danio, $0.6629960038578532$ for E. coli, $0.9400341806302775$ for gallus, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, and $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast.  A separate minimal-compression calculation reports compressibilities $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio, $0.005717621488177116$ for E. coli, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ in each of those four computed rows; the median compressibility is reported as $0.026$.  The Window6 boundary $R$ set also has an abundance-preference signature in a different finite test: all $15$ computed organisms fall in the avoided direction, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$, while only $6$ organisms are individually significant under the permutation diagnostic.  Optimal-codon direction conservation is classified as clustered with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ over $15$ computed organisms, and the organism-specificity meta test does not resolve a clean domain preference: the dominant-coordinate-versus-domain permutation right-tail $p$ is $0.5814185814185814$, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ on $n=12$ complete-feature rows.

\paragraph{Readback boundary.}
The scientific readback is therefore narrow.  The table says that under the declared order and penalties, measured TE and transcript-side terms leave most of $P_Q$ unexplained, and the remaining candidate dictionary does not yield a stable cross-organism absorber.  Human PTM density is a selected absorber in the ordered decomposition, but the corresponding direction-aware analyses and the absence of matching selections in Danio, E. coli, and yeast prevent promotion to a common biological channel.  The numbers support a bounded descriptive statement about variance partition and finite-row ranking only.  They do not establish translation realization beyond the attached TE and tAI contacts, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal regulation, trafficking mechanism, phenotype, or a universal law.  Any stronger interpretation would require a separate matched contact and a separate statistic directed at that stronger biological layer.
