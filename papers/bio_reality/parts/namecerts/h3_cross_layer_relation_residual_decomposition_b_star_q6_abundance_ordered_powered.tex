\subsection{Ordered residual decomposition for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-ordered-residual-decomposition}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record treats the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component as a finite per-organism ledger, not as a pathway model.  For each organism with the required joins, the measured protein-abundance component $P_Q$ is partitioned in a fixed order: first the measured translation-efficiency readout $T$, then an mRNA-stability or transcription-proximal readout $S$ where available, and finally a scored finite dictionary of candidate downstream readouts $H$.  The reported partition is an ordered orthogonal variance accounting.  Its local bookkeeping identity is the finite check
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_o+S_o+U_o+\sum_{H\in D_o} H_o &= 1,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $U_o$ is the remaining unexplained fraction after the ordered cuts.  The computed sums close to numerical precision: Danio rerio has sum error $0$, Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 has sum error $0$, Homo sapiens has sum error $1.11\times 10^{-16}$, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae has sum error $0$.

\paragraph{Primary decomposition.}
The finite rows show that the measured panel leaves most of $P_Q$ unresolved.  Danio rerio uses $n=13140$ full rows with $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092$, has $T=0.0243681$, skips $S$, and leaves $U=0.9756319$.  Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 uses $n=2517$ full rows with $\|P_Q\|^2=225.8456$, has $T=0.00035965$, $S=0.00535797$, and leaves $U=0.9942824$.  Homo sapiens uses $n=11250$ full rows with $\|P_Q\|^2=286.3673$, has $T=0.00601191$, $S=0.00616399$, and leaves $U=0.9736442$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae uses $n=4759$ full rows with $\|P_Q\|^2=229.8645$, has the largest translation-side cut, $T=0.1127139$, plus $S=0.0196274$, but still leaves $U=0.8676586$.  Thus even the strongest row in this table, yeast, retains roughly $86.8\%$ of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component after the ordered measured readouts.

\paragraph{Scored $H$ dictionary.}
The $H$ stage is intentionally conservative.  Candidate readouts are evaluated by absorbed energy $A_H$, a null scale, a description-length penalty with declared regularization $\lambda=0.01$, and a join-shift penalty for rows whose available identifiers change the target population.  The finite score is not a biological effect size; it is only the ledger value deciding whether a row is kept in this particular table.  In Danio rerio no $H$ row is kept: localization has $A_H=0.0176301$ but $DL=12$, join shift $0.852131$, null95 $0.0110734$, and score $-0.156050$; PTM density has $A_H=0.0145069$, null95 $0.00171596$, and score $-0.0388453$; turnover is unavailable.  Escherichia coli has no usable feature, localization, or turnover rows in this dictionary, and the mRNA-abundance placeholder carries $A_H=0$ and score $-0.01$.  Homo sapiens is the single organism where the unordered scored dictionary keeps one row: PTM density has $A_H=0.0143546$, $DL=1$, join shift $0.00915977$, null95 $0.000359019$, and score $0.00353764$.  The remaining human candidates are not kept, including complex membership with $A_H=0.00141424$ and score $-0.00938047$, localization with $A_H=0.00265791$, $DL=12$, join shift $0.117404$, and score $-0.125270$, and turnover with $A_H=0.000210474$, join shift $0.412454$, and score $-0.0309905$.  Yeast has no kept $H$ row: localization has the largest absorption, $A_H=0.0211731$, but its $DL=12$ and join shift $0.159277$ produce score $-0.112210$; PTM density has $A_H=0.00646766$ and score $-0.00450799$; turnover has $A_H=0.00232847$ and score $-0.0242923$.

\paragraph{Direction-aware cross-check.}
A separate direction-aware replay reaches the same qualitative endpoint.  In that replay no organism keeps a positive $H$ readout in the emitted dictionary table.  The emitted status labels Saccharomyces cerevisiae as translation-proximal, with $T=0.113$, $S=0.020$, and $U=0.868$, while Escherichia coli, Homo sapiens, and Danio rerio are classified as mostly unexplained, with unexplained fractions $0.994$, $0.980$, and $0.976$ respectively under the direction-aware table.  The direction-aware $H$ cosines also explain why large raw absorptions do not automatically become accepted readouts: in Homo sapiens PTM density has $A_H=0.0143546$ and $C_H=0.424917$ in the ordered direction-aware dictionary, while localization in yeast has $A_H=0.0211731$ and $C_H=0.716905$ but is still rejected after dimensional and join penalties.

\paragraph{Relation to the ranking table.}
The broader direction-aware ranking table reports organism-specific weak absorbers rather than a shared channel.  Danio rerio ranks measured TE first with $A_H=0.0175943$, $C_H=0.657375$, raw product $0.0115661$, and score $0.00156605$, followed by mRNA with $A_H=0.0119730$, $C_H=0.896564$, raw product $0.0107346$, and score $0.000734589$.  Homo sapiens ranks complex membership first by raw direction-aware support, with $A_H=0.00752720$, $C_H=0.872307$, raw product $0.00656603$, but its score remains negative after the stated penalty.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae ranks mRNA first with $A_H=0.00189761$, $C_H=0.604057$, and raw product $0.00114626$.  Escherichia coli has only weak mRNA and measured-TE rows, with mRNA raw product $0.00152980$ and measured TE raw product $0.000553666$.  The best absorber is therefore organism-specific, and no universal $H$ is identified.

\paragraph{Compression test and negative result.}
The companion compressibility ledger gives the same finite interpretation from a different angle.  Its permutation $p$ value is $0.000999001$ for each of Danio rerio, Escherichia coli, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae under the deterministic null, but the measured low-dimensional readout set still compresses little of $P_Q$: compressibility is $0.0243681$ for Danio rerio, $0.00571762$ for Escherichia coli, $0.0263558$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.132341$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The median compressibility is reported as $0.026$, so the measured panel is statistically non-random under this procedure while still failing to provide a compact shared explanation of the residual.

\paragraph{Data coverage notes.}
Coverage differs by readout and matters for interpretation.  Yeast turnover has $n=3640$ joined proteins, a reported CDS join hit rate $0.915604$, and a turnover join hit rate $0.881857$; its turnover absorption is $0.000908603$ in the standalone turnover test and remains unkept in the ordered dictionary.  Human turnover has $n=8057$ joined proteins, a CDS join hit rate $0.977929$, and absorption only $3.57408\times 10^{-6}$ against the large human residual, below its dof-matched null with null95 $0.000434229$.  These finite coverage facts prevent a missing or sparse readout from being silently converted into evidence for absence.

\paragraph{Boundary against stronger biology.}
This chapter licenses only the finite statistical readback just described.  The ordered fractions, $A_H$ values, direction cosines, scores, kept flags, null comparisons, row counts, and organism labels do not establish translation realization, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, regulation, pathway causation, phenotype, or universality.  A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each such promotion.  The boundary-set assay illustrates the distinction: the Window6 $R$ set has a cross-organism avoided abundance-preference signature with $15$ avoided directions out of $15$ directional non-ties, one-sided binomial $p=3.05176\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.10352\times 10^{-5}$, but that result is still an association test over a fixed codon-window set, not a mechanism.  The present residual-decomposition row is therefore best read as a precise negative result: the available measured readouts do not compress the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual into a single shared channel, and the remaining signal is mostly organism-specific and unresolved by this finite panel.
