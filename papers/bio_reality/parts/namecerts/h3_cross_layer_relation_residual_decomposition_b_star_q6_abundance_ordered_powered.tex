\subsection{Ordered Residual Decomposition for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Abundance Component}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-residual-decomposition}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record studies a finite per-organism decomposition of the abundance component carried by the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates.  For an organism $o$, the measured abundance vector is first reduced to the coordinate component $P_Q=\Pi_{Q_e}P_e$ after the fixed controls for coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The resulting vector is then decomposed in the fixed order measured TE, mRNA-stability where available, and a greedy scored $H$-dictionary.  The reported partition is therefore an ordered projection ledger, not an intervention, not a temporal pathway, and not a biological sufficiency claim.

\paragraph{Row universe.}
The computed decomposition rows are $o\in\{$Danio rerio, Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae$\}$.  The finite $P_Q$ energies and row counts are: Danio rerio $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606$ on $n=13140$ rows; Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 $\|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032$ on $n=2517$ rows; Homo sapiens $\|P_Q\|^2=286.3673197177688$ on $n=11250$ rows; and Saccharomyces cerevisiae $\|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544$ on $n=4759$ rows.  The decomposition sums close to one in every computed row: Danio rerio, Escherichia coli, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae have zero recorded sum error, while Homo sapiens has sum $0.9999999999999999$ with error $1.1102230246251565\times 10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{Greedy $H$ dictionary.}
The $H$-dictionary is scored as a finite absorber list with a description-length penalty and an explicit join-shift penalty.  In this decomposition table the score is not a mechanism score; it is a bookkeeping number for whether a candidate readout earns a place in the ordered residual dictionary under the declared penalties.  Danio rerio has no retained $H$ readout: localization has the largest listed $A_H=0.017630065362242674$ but carries $DL=12$, join shift $0.852130898021309$, null95 $0.011073433874378957$, and score $-0.15604991341320174$; PTM density has $A_H=0.014506851682279774$, null95 $0.0017159596757307261$, and score $-0.03884533325981321$; turnover is missing.  Escherichia coli has no available nonzero $H$ row beyond the zero mRNA-abundance entry, whose score is $-0.01$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae also retains no $H$ readout: localization has $A_H=0.021173066463797872$ but $DL=12$, join shift $0.15927715906703088$, and score $-0.11221001354437096$; PTM density has $A_H=0.006467661568183971$ and score $-0.0045079853396405925$; turnover has $A_H=0.00232847477991429$, join shift $0.3086782937591931$, and score $-0.02429227248076356$.

Homo sapiens is the one row in this decomposition table with a retained $H$ entry.  PTM density has $A_H=0.014354648896209531$, $DL=1$, join shift $0.009159767482825437$, null95 $0.00035901903371732323$, and score $0.0035376414883509363$, so it is marked kept. This retained human PTM-density row is not promoted to a conserved pathway or shared explanation, because the retained row is organism-specific and the other organisms do not reproduce it under the same finite rule.

\paragraph{Direction-aware comparison.}
The companion direction-aware ordered table strengthens the negative interpretation of the dictionary.  When the same residual decomposition is scored by $A_HC_H$ together with null, description-length, and join-shift penalties, all listed $H$ entries are rejected in the emitted organism dictionary.  The reported table gives $H_1=H_2=\varnothing$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Escherichia coli, Homo sapiens, and Danio rerio, with residual fractions $U=0.8676586345294415$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9798666209022441$, and $0.9756319042010854$, respectively.  This contrast is important: the unordered scored decomposition allows a narrow human PTM-density absorber, while the direction-aware dictionary does not yield a positive shared $H$ readout.

\paragraph{Boundary-set and conservation context.}
The finite geometry is not empty: the Window6 boundary $R$ set has a cross-organism avoided abundance-preference signature in the available rows.  In the directional sign test, all $15$ non-tie computed organisms are avoided rather than preferred, giving avoided count $15$, preferred count $0$, one-sided avoided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  The same packet reports $6$ individually significant organisms under the permutation diagnostic.  Separately, the optimal-codon conservation readout is classified as clustered, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ across $15$ computed organisms and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  These facts show that the coordinate system has finite biological contact, but they do not convert the residual dictionary into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Turnover boundary.}
A separate human N-end degron turnover check joins $3898$ rows in the Mathieson turnover boundary table and reports consistent-sign fraction $0.5$ with a negative-control null flag set to false after $12000$ actual permutations.  This turnover boundary is relevant because turnover is one of the candidate $H$ readouts, but the residual decomposition does not treat turnover as a shared absorber: yeast turnover has score $-0.02429227248076356$ in the ordered decomposition, and human turnover has score $-0.030990511076459067$ in the same table.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The scientific content of the row is therefore a negative, finite, cross-layer result.  The measured TE and mRNA-stability cuts remove only small fractions in most organisms, and the greedy $H$ dictionary does not supply a stable shared explanation.  The strongest exception is the human PTM-density row in the non-direction-aware ordered decomposition, but this exception is narrow, scoring-rule-specific, and not reproduced as a cross-organism absorber.  The direction-aware table gives the stricter reading: no positive $H$ readout survives as a compact residual dictionary entry across the computed organisms.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The decomposition is an empirical ranking and variance-partition record over finite joined tables.  It does not establish translation realization, protein folding, structural admissibility, biological function, phenotype, causal mediation, pathway mechanism, localization mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Missing readouts remain missing; high $A_H$ can be offset by direction, description length, null behavior, or join sensitivity; and a retained row is not a mechanistic explanation unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that stronger interpretation.
