\subsection{Ordered Residual Decomposition for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Abundance Signal}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-ordered-residual-decomposition}

\paragraph{Finite object under review.}
The object recorded here is a finite per-organism ledger for the abundance component $P_Q=\Pi_{Q_e}P_e$ carried by the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates.  The coordinate list is fixed throughout the computation:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  For each organism with a full finite row, the ledger first removes a measured-translation readout $T$ when available, then an mRNA-stability or transcript-side readout $S$ when available, and only then scores a small dictionary of downstream readouts $H$.  This ordering is a modelling convention for variance partitioning.  It is not a temporal order in the cell and it is not evidence for a pathway.

The row energies are finite and reported before interpretation: Danio rerio uses $n=13140$ rows and $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606$; Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 uses $n=2517$ rows and $\|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032$; Homo sapiens uses $n=11250$ rows and $\|P_Q\|^2=286.3673197177688$ in the ordered residual-decomposition row; Saccharomyces cerevisiae uses $n=4759$ rows and $\|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544$.  The decomposition sums are numerically closed: danio, E. coli, and yeast sum to exactly $1.0$, while the human row sums to $0.9999999999999999$, with error $1.1102230246251565\times 10^{-16}$.

Human and E. coli are still more residual-dominated: $U=0.9736442326306781$ and $U=0.9942823785118229$ respectively.

\paragraph{Dictionary scoring and the role of $\lambda$.}

The table also records join-shift penalties, because a readout whose identifier join changes the target row population is not allowed to masquerade as an equivalent full-row statistic.

\paragraph{Relation to other finite checks.}
The broader $B^*_{Q6}$ record is consistent with this residual-heavy conclusion.  The minimal-compilation audit reports median compressibility $0.026$ across computed organisms and states that the measured low-dimensional readout set does not highly compress $P_Q$.  Its per-organism compressibilities are $0.024368095798914635$ for danio, $0.005717621488177116$ for E. coli, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast, each with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ under that finite permutation design.  The measured-translation mediation row is also organism-specific: measured mediated fractions are $0.0$ for danio after a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The cross-organism boundary-set audit for the Window6 $R$ set reports $15$ avoided directions out of $15$ non-ties, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$; this supports an avoided abundance-preference signature for that fixed boundary set, not a downstream explanation of the residual dictionary.

The $f3_{stress}$ reach audit in yeast gives a best-subset coverage of $0.22618445168998694$ versus base coverage $0.1897720314893696$, an increment of $0.03641242020061733$, and a selection-aware null95 of $0.02697141300639046$ over $120$ permutations.  The optimal-codon conservation audit classifies the cross-organism direction pattern as clustered, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ over $15$ computed organisms and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The organism-specificity meta audit does not resolve a strong domain law: the dominant-coordinate versus domain test has $n=12$, chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$.  These rows explain why the decomposition should be read as organism-specific finite geometry plus external readout contact, not as a universal biological channel.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The finite conclusion is narrow.  The ordered decomposition shows that most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component remains outside the measured readout dictionary after the declared $T\to S\to H$ projection order.  Human PTM density is the only kept downstream readout in the ordered residual-decomposition table, and even that row is a small absorber under a chosen penalty rule.  Direction-aware ranking finds weak organism-specific leaders: measured TE and mRNA in danio, mRNA in E. coli, complex membership and mRNA in human, mRNA in yeast, and a feature-only gallus row that is not a complete ordered-decomposition row.  No shared universal $H$ is recovered.

Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization beyond the measured translation contacts, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  $A_H$, $C_H$, $A_HC_H$, null thresholds, ranks, and description-length scores are finite statistical readbacks over matched rows.  A stronger biological statement would require a separate same-layer contact and a statistic designed for that claim.  Until such a contact exists, the honest result is that the non-translation abundance residual is largely organism-specific and largely unexplained by the available finite panel.
