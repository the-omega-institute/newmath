\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life and a Decay-Factor RBP Bridge}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about coding transcripts whose synonymous residual labels have already been reduced modulo amino-acid identity.  A row is eligible only when it carries a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a measured local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and the control labels needed to separate the proposed bridge from transcription, abundance, isoform, local sequence, and batch effects.  In symbols, the intended row type is not a single codon statistic but a matched tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
r = (q_r, T_r, H_r, O_r, L_r, W_r, C_r),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_r$ is the high-versus-low residual coordinate class after the amino-acid quotient, $T_r$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_r$ is the RNA half-life readout, $O_r$ is local occupancy of the selected decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, $L_r$ records target-RBP loss, $W_r$ records wild-type rescue, and $C_r$ contains binding-defective or non-target controls together with transcription-rate, abundance, splice or isoform, RNA-structure, motif-density, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, and batch covariates.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts already present.}
The available finite evidence does contain useful numerical anchors, but they do not instantiate the full row type above.  The N-terminal acetylation contact from the Kron-style occupancy panel contains $n=657$ protein rows, including $426$ NatA-class rows, and gives a high-versus-low AUROC of $0.942081$ over $622$ eligible high/low rows.  This is a strong finite classification surface for an N-terminal acetylation occupancy claim, not a same-window RNA decay panel.  A separate RBP-binding contact contains $900$ total peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  A separate mTOR translation contact contains $6494$ rows.  These numbers show that finite biological readout tables exist at adjacent layers, but their keys and measurements are not the joined RNA-half-life, translation, occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows required for the present bridge.

\paragraph{Statistic intended by the conjecture.}
The bridge claim would require a bounded finite statistic comparing high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows after conditioning on the same-row translation readout.  One admissible schematic target is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{Effect}(H \mid q=\mathrm{high}, T, Z)
     - \operatorname{Effect}(H \mid q=\mathrm{low}, T, Z),\\
\Delta_O
  &= \operatorname{Effect}(O \mid q=\mathrm{high}, T, Z)
     - \operatorname{Effect}(O \mid q=\mathrm{low}, T, Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch.  A positive bridge is not licensed by $\Delta_H \neq 0$ alone.  It requires the half-life effect to track measured local occupancy of the externally selected decay-factor RBP, to collapse under target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and to fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Readback rule.}
The readback is therefore conjunctive.  The finite panel may support a bounded RNA-half-life RBP bridge only if the following row-level pattern is present in one joined table: high-versus-low coordinate labels differ in translation-conditioned RNA half-life; the same rows show the prespecified RBP occupancy contrast at the relevant RNA window; removing the target RBP removes the half-life contrast; wild-type rescue restores it; and binding-defective or non-target controls do not restore it.  The available finite numbers do not yet supply this conjunction.  The $657$-row N-terminal acetylation table supplies an acetylation occupancy classification surface, the $900$-peak RBP table supplies an RBP-binding surface, and the $6494$-row mTOR table supplies a translation-related surface, but none of these is the same-organism, same-window, same-row matrix needed to compute the RNA-half-life bridge statistic.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of the coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the controls and the translation readout are attached:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_r = \mathrm{high}) \perp H_r
  \;\mid\; T_r, Z_r.
\end{aligned}
$$
Even if this null is rejected in a future joined panel, the rejection remains an RNA-stability association unless it is accompanied by the measured RBP-occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern.  A half-life difference without local RBP occupancy change is not an RBP mechanism.  An occupancy difference without target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue is not a decay bridge.  A rescue by a binding-defective or non-target control breaks the proposed bridge rather than confirming it.

\paragraph{Finite classification of the present record.}
The present record is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because its admissible and inadmissible readings are determined by finite row requirements and by available scalar contacts.  Its current status is negative for the RBP-bridge claim: the necessary joined rows are absent.  The N-terminal acetylation AUROC of $0.942081$ over $622$ high/low rows is a concrete finite signal for a different occupancy problem, not evidence that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates control RNA half-life through a decay-factor RBP.  Likewise, the $900$ RBP-binding peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases do not by themselves provide local occupancy, loss, rescue, and binding-defective controls on the same transcript rows used for the RNA-half-life statistic.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not license a translation-realization claim from RNA half-life alone, and it does not license an RNA-decay mechanism from the presence of a translation contact.  It also does not license protein output, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite statistic.  Within the present evidence, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor and, at most, a target for a future translation-conditioned RNA-stability association; the decay-factor RBP bridge remains unclaimed until the same-row occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and control matrix exists.
