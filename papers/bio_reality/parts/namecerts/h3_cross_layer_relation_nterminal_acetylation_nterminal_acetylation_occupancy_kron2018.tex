\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and RBP-bridge boundary for the Kron occupancy row}
\label{sec:bioreality-kron2018-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched-row question rather than a universal biochemical law.  The rows are transcript or coding-sequence records carrying $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate labels after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, together with same-row translation readouts, RNA half-life readouts, measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and explicit perturbation or rescue arms.  The intended cross-layer claim is narrow: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be called an RBP-mediated bridge only when it survives the translation-readout boundary and then follows the measured RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls on the same finite panel.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The local positive finite scalar attached to the Kron occupancy row is not an RNA-decay RBP panel.  It is an N-terminal acetylation occupancy readout with $n=657$ rows, $n_{\mathrm{NatA}}=426$ NatA rows, $622$ high-versus-low rows in the reported AUROC comparison, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AUROC} &= 0.942081,\\
\mathrm{runtime}_{\mathrm{sec}} &= 1.1687.
\end{aligned}
$$
This scalar shows that the named finite row can carry a strong occupancy classification signal for the N-terminal acetylation surface, but it does not by itself measure RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, RBP loss, or rescue behavior.  Two linked finite anchors illustrate the separate contacts that would be needed for the proposed bridge.  The RBP-binding row supplies $900$ total peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases and runtime $223.648043$ seconds, while the translation-readout row supplies $n=6494$ translation rows with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  These numbers are useful as contact witnesses for RBP-binding and translation-readout surfaces, but their mere coexistence in the ledger does not join them into a same-transcript, same-window RNA half-life experiment.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive bridge would require a finite statistic computed only after joining the coordinate label, translation readout, transcript abundance control, RNA half-life, measured RBP occupancy, and perturbation arms on the same row set.  In such a panel, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels are compared after amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout have been controlled.  The bridge can be reported only if the half-life residual tracks the experimentally decoupled local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapses after target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Symbolically, the permitted readout has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q \mid T, Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta H(q \mid T, Z, \mathrm{RBP\ occupancy}) \text{ follows the measured local occupancy arm},\\
&\Delta H(q \mid T, Z, \mathrm{target\ RBP\ loss}) \to 0,\\
&\Delta H(q \mid T, Z, \mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \text{ returns},\\
&\Delta H(q \mid T, Z, \mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}) \text{ does not return}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $q$ denotes the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout, $H$ denotes RNA half-life, and $Z$ denotes the declared control block.  The notation is only a finite-row bookkeeping device; it is not an assertion that the present Kron occupancy scalar has already supplied the required RNA-decay experiment.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the full control block and the same-row translation readout are attached.  If half-life differs but measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy is unchanged, the result remains a bounded RNA-stability association.  If occupancy differs but the coordinate and occupancy are not experimentally decoupled, the result remains an occupancy association.  If target-RBP loss does not erase the effect, or wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or binding-defective and non-target controls also restore it, the proposed RBP bridge is rejected.  The verified Kron scalar, with $657$ rows and AUROC $0.942081$, therefore supports only the occupancy-classification surface for its own N-terminal acetylation row; it cannot be promoted into RNA half-life mechanism or RBP-mediated decay without a distinct joined panel.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA-decay mechanism follows from translation readout alone.  No RBP mechanism follows from a half-life difference unless measured local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue are all present in the finite design.  The record also makes no claim about protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  The finite RBP-binding count of $900$ peaks with $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases and the translation row count of $6494$ are separate reality contacts; they do not erase the need for same-window RNA half-life and perturbation rows.  Thus the chapter records a bounded, testable bridge criterion and a currently negative scope judgment for the requested RNA half-life RBP mechanism on this slug.
