\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about synonymous residual coordinate labels, translation readouts, RNA half-life measurements, and a proposed decay-factor RBP bridge. Its carrier is a table of transcript or CDS rows in which each row may contain a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, a matched mRNA abundance or transcription control, an RNA half-life readout, and, when available, local occupancy and perturbation information for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein. The intended contrast is not an unconstrained sequence law. It is the finite comparison between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after amino-acid sequence has been controlled, with any RNA half-life residual interpreted only through the additional boundary imposed by local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.

The finite evidence table currently contains a verified protein-layer contact for the named Kron-style N-terminal acetylation occupancy run, with $n=657$ rows, $426$ NatA-labeled rows, $622$ high/low rows used for the reported discrimination, and $\operatorname{AUROC}=0.942081$ in run $\mathrm{f452e76f6363}$. This scalar result is useful as a row-counted example of a layer-specific occupancy readout, but it is not itself an RNA half-life or RBP-decay measurement. The same evidence ledger also contains an RBP-binding contact with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant units in run $\mathrm{b225890251c2}$, and a translation contact with $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation run $\mathrm{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$. These numbers show that the surrounding BioReality ledger can hold finite contacts at the occupancy, RBP-binding, and translation layers. They do not by themselves create the joined same-row matrix required for the present RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Local claim form.}
Let $F$ be the finite set of rows that survive identifier matching and quality filters. A row $r\in F$ may carry a residual coordinate class $q(r)\in\{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$, a translation readout $T(r)$, an mRNA or transcription control vector $A(r)$, a local RNA-structure and sequence-composition control vector $C(r)$, an RNA half-life readout $H(r)$, and an RBP boundary tuple $B(r)$. The boundary tuple is nonempty only when it includes measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a target-RBP loss condition, a wild-type rescue condition, and a binding-defective or non-target control. The proposed bridge is therefore not the assertion that $q$ predicts $H$ in isolation. It is the narrower assertion that a translation-conditioned half-life residual follows the experimentally separated RBP boundary pattern.

The finite null is the exchangeability of high and low coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In compact notation, the positive bridge would require a nonzero residual term only inside the RBP boundary stratum:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(r) &= \alpha + \beta_q q(r) + \beta_T T(r) + \gamma_A A(r) + \gamma_C C(r) + \varepsilon(r),\\
\beta_q &\neq 0\quad\text{only when }B(r)\text{ records occupancy change, loss collapse, and rescue return.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The phrase ``only when'' is essential. A half-life association without the RBP tuple remains a bounded RNA-stability association. An RBP occupancy association without coordinate/occupancy decoupling remains an occupancy association. A target-RBP perturbation effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target failure remains an incomplete perturbation record.

\paragraph{Readout separation.}
The present record therefore has two layers of evidence and one missing join. First, the N-terminal acetylation occupancy run supplies a concrete finite occupancy-style scalar: $657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, $622$ high/low rows, and $\operatorname{AUROC}=0.942081$. Second, the RBP-binding ledger supplies a finite binding surface with $900$ peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP units. Third, the translation ledger supplies a row-counted translation surface with $6494$ entries. These are finite contacts, but they are not the same rows and not the same biological measurement. The claim at this subsection is therefore a boundary theorem for the BioReality record rather than a positive RNA-decay mechanism.

A valid positive instance would need a single finite table whose rows jointly contain
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl(q(r),T(r),A(r),C(r),H(r),O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(r),L_{\mathrm{RBP}}(r),W_{\mathrm{rescue}}(r),D_{\mathrm{control}}(r)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is measured local occupancy at the editable RNA window, $L_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is target-RBP loss, $W_{\mathrm{rescue}}$ is wild-type rescue, and $D_{\mathrm{control}}$ is the binding-defective or non-target control. The support pattern is finite and falsifiable: the coordinate-associated half-life residual must track measured occupancy, disappear under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue. Failure of any one of these row-level checks prevents promotion to an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not infer translation realization from RNA half-life alone. The same-row translation readout is a conditioning boundary, not proof of a translation mechanism. It also does not infer RNA decay mechanism from codon-window geometry, residual-coordinate notation, or the existence of a workflow-ready packet. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the finite scalars listed above. A separate contact at the relevant layer must supply each such readout before it can be stated.

The N-terminal acetylation scalar is consequently used only as a concrete row-counted occupancy-style fact for the named verified run; it is not reinterpreted as RNA half-life. The RBP-binding scalar is used only to show that finite RBP-binding contacts exist in the ledger; it is not treated as the prespecified same-window decay-factor occupancy for this claim. The translation scalar is used only to identify the kind of same-row translation surface required for conditioning; it is not a half-life or perturbation result. Under these restrictions, the record is ready as a self-contained finite boundary statement: the positive RBP bridge is blocked until the joined half-life, translation, occupancy, loss, rescue, and control matrix exists, while all higher biological claims remain outside the licensed scope.
