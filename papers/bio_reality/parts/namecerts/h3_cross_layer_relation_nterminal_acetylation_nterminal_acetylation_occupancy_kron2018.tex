\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row target for a cross-layer relation.  A row is eligible only when a coding transcript carries a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local measurements for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and controls for transcription, abundance, composition, isoform state, and batch.  The intended biological object is not an unconstrained sequence law: it is the bounded question of whether a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label is associated with RNA half-life only through a measured, experimentally separable RBP occupancy and perturbation route.

The finite audit table supplies a concrete numerical anchor from the neighboring N-terminal-acetylation occupancy run rather than a completed RBP-decay panel.  The run keyed by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_kron2018} reports $n=657$ rows, $n_{\mathrm{NatA}}=426$, a high-low comparison size of $622$, and $\operatorname{AUROC}=0.942081$ with runtime $1.1687$ seconds.  This is useful as evidence that a finite occupancy-style classifier can be evaluated on matched biological rows, but it is not itself an RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, or perturbation-rescue experiment for the present conjecture.  A second nearby contact, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}, records $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases in runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  It supports the existence of finite RBP-binding surfaces, not the requested same-window decay-factor bridge.  The translation-side comparison run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} contributes $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds; it is a translation-readout contact class, not an RNA decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Row variables.}
Let $i$ range over the finite eligible transcript/CDS rows that survive identifier matching.  The minimal row can be represented by
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_i = (&q_i, a_i, T_i, H_i, O_i, L_i, W_i, B_i, C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the high- or low-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual label after amino-acid quotienting, $a_i$ is the amino-acid and codon-context control block, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is local occupancy for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ is target-RBP loss status, $W_i$ is wild-type rescue status, $B_i$ is binding-defective or non-target rescue status, and $C_i$ collects transcription rate, transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, and batch.  The admissible comparison is therefore not $q_i \mapsto H_i$ alone.  It is a conditional finite test in which the half-life contrast must be aligned with $O_i$, must disappear under $L_i$, must return under $W_i$, and must fail to return under $B_i$.

A compact way to state the positive bridge requirement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H(q \mid T,C) &\ne 0,\\
 \Delta_O(q \mid T,C) &\ne 0,\\
 \Delta_H(q \mid T,C,L=\mathrm{loss}) &\approx 0,\\
 \Delta_H(q \mid T,C,W=\mathrm{wildtype}) &\approx \Delta_H(q \mid T,C),\\
 \Delta_H(q \mid T,C,B=\mathrm{defective\ or\ nonTarget}) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The symbols here name finite contrasts on matched rows, not asymptotic laws.  The bridge is accepted only when all five contrasts are present in one joined panel with the declared controls.  If the half-life term survives but the occupancy term is absent, the observation remains an RNA-stability association.  If occupancy is present but the target-RBP loss and rescue pattern fails, the observation remains an occupancy association rather than a decay-factor mechanism.

\paragraph{Matched null.}
The null model exchanges high- and low-coordinate labels after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout.  Formally, for the finite rows that survive matching,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 H_i \perp q_i \mid (T_i,C_i)
\end{aligned}
$$
unless the occupancy, target-loss, wild-type-rescue, and defective-or-non-target rescue pattern is simultaneously observed.  A half-life contrast without the perturbation-rescue chain does not falsify the RBP-bridge null; it only identifies a bounded RNA half-life contrast requiring a different explanation.  Likewise, an RBP-binding surface measured in another assay or at another window does not satisfy the bridge condition unless it joins to the same organism, same transcript window, same residual-coordinate label, and same readout rows.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The present record therefore licenses a sharply limited statement.  It may define the finite row structure, the conditioning set, the required RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern, and the null under which high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life.  It may cite finite companion runs showing that matched occupancy-like, RBP-binding, and translation-readout surfaces can be numerically audited: $657$ N-terminal-acetylation rows with $\operatorname{AUROC}=0.942081$, $900$ RBP-binding peaks with $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, and $6494$ translation-readout rows.  It may not convert any of those companion runs into the missing same-window RNA-decay RBP panel.

No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA decay mechanism follows from translation conditioning alone.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological rule follows from the residual-coordinate geometry, from codon-window notation, from contact names, from review readiness, or from the observed scalar values listed above.  Each such higher-layer assertion requires its own row-matched contact and statistic.  For this chapter the scientific content is the finite gate: a positive cross-layer RBP bridge exists only when the half-life effect, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure are all present in the same controlled panel.
