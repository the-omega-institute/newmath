\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor occupancy at a finite Q6 boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row design, not a free biological generalization.  A row is admissible only when it carries a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a transcript or coding-sequence identifier, a same-row translation readout or declared translation-efficiency boundary, an RNA half-life readout, matched abundance and transcription controls, and an externally specified decay-factor RNA-binding protein occupancy field at the editable RNA window.  The intended comparison is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels, after amino-acid identity has already been quotiented out.  The row is therefore a coordinate-conditioned RNA-stability panel with a translation boundary attached; it is not by itself a protein-output, folding, phenotype, or adaptation assay.

The available finite checks give useful scale but do not yet instantiate the full RNA half-life and decay-factor perturbation panel.  The N-terminal acetylation occupancy run reports $n=657$ rows, $n_{\mathrm{NatA}}=426$, and an AUROC of $0.942081$ over $622$ high--low rows in run $\mathrm{f452e76f6363}$.  This is a strong finite occupancy-style classification result for the N-terminal acetylation subject, but it is not an RNA half-life assay and it is not a measured decay-factor RBP loss-and-rescue panel.  A separate translation-readout finite run, $\mathrm{0c2bbfd3e2d9}$, reports $n=6494$ rows for the mTOR translation readout surface.  A separate RBP-binding contact run, $\mathrm{b225890251c2}$, reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases.  These three finite surfaces show that the repository has row-level occupancy, translation, and RBP-contact measurements in nearby BioReality records, but they are not yet joined into the same-organism, same-window matrix required for the present RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Target statistic.}
A positive bridge would require a finite statistic comparing a translation-conditioned half-life residual between the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels while conditioning on transcription rate, transcript abundance, batch, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout.  In symbolic form, the admissible contrast has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6} &= \widehat{\lambda}_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}(Q_{\mathrm{high}} \mid Z,T,O_{\mathrm{RBP}})
              - \widehat{\lambda}_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}(Q_{\mathrm{low}} \mid Z,T,O_{\mathrm{RBP}}),\\
Z &= \{\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{batch},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motif},\mathrm{GC},\mathrm{dinucleotide},
      \mathrm{codon\text{-}pair},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{amino\text{-}acid}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T$ is the same-row translation readout and $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The statistic is not licensed unless the same panel also contains a perturbation ladder: target-RBP loss must collapse the half-life effect, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail to restore it.  The bridge is therefore a finite conditional pattern across coordinate, translation, half-life, occupancy, loss, and rescue columns.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to the translation-conditioned half-life residual after the control set $Z$ and the same-row translation readout are fixed.  A half-life association that survives these controls but lacks measured local RBP occupancy remains an RNA-stability association, not an RBP bridge.  An occupancy association that is not experimentally decoupled from coordinate label, or that does not collapse under target-RBP loss, remains an occupancy correlation.  A perturbation response without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure remains an incomplete perturbation result.  The current finite facts do not report a row count, effect size, $p$-value, occupancy-change statistic, loss-collapse statistic, or rescue-return statistic for this exact joined RNA half-life design.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The N-terminal acetylation result supplies a concrete finite scalar surface, with AUROC $0.942081$ on $622$ high--low rows and $657$ total rows.  That number may be cited as a nearby occupancy-discrimination fact for its own biological subject.  It cannot be reinterpreted as an RNA half-life decay-factor bridge.  Likewise, the $6494$-row translation readout and the $900$-peak RBP-binding surface show that translation and RBP contacts can be represented as finite rows, but they do not by themselves provide same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue for the Q6 RNA half-life claim.  The present chapter therefore records a bounded cross-layer target whose finite carrier is specified, whose required perturbation pattern is explicit, and whose positive biological bridge is still blocked until the joined panel exists.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA half-life difference becomes a decay-factor RBP mechanism unless measured local RBP occupancy changes in the specified window and the loss/rescue ladder has the required direction.  No binding-defective rescue, non-target control, RNA-structure-only explanation, motif-density-only explanation, transcription-rate explanation, isoform-state explanation, transcript-abundance explanation, GC or dinucleotide explanation, codon-pair explanation, or batch explanation may be counted as support for the RBP bridge.  Finally, this record licenses no protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the specified decay-factor test, or universal sequence law.  Those conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
