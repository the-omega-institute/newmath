\subsection{A translation-conditioned RNA half-life bridge and the N-terminal acetylation control surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-nterminal-acetylation-control}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite matched-row claim about synonymous residual coordinate labels in coding transcripts, with the target biological question held at the RNA-decay layer.  A row is eligible only when it carries a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and an externally fixed route for testing decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy.  The carrier is therefore not a sequence-only table and not a protein-output table.  It is a joined panel in which high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are compared only after conditioning on amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the attached translation readout.

\paragraph{Numerical anchors.}
The available finite-row evidence contains a strong but different cross-layer benchmark from the N-terminal acetylation study: the parsed Kron-style occupancy table has $n=657$ rows, $n_{\mathrm{NatA}}=426$ NatA-class rows, and a high--low comparison size of $622$, with AUROC $0.942081$ for the recorded occupancy discrimination.  This is a genuine finite scalar surface, but it is not an RNA half-life perturbation panel and it is not an RBP-decay rescue experiment.  It is used here as a control example of how a measured occupancy surface can be finite and discriminative while still remaining at its own layer.

The record also contains an RBP-binding benchmark with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  That value shows that RBP contacts can be represented as finite measured rows, but it does not supply the same-window decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective control required for the present RNA half-life bridge.  A translation benchmark is separately present with $n=6494$ rows in the mTOR translation readout surface, and a poly(A)-tail benchmark records $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows with cross-species concordance recorded as false.  These linked facts establish that the BioReality table can carry row counts and layer-specific scalars across translation, binding, and RNA-processing surfaces; they do not collapse those surfaces into one mechanism.

\paragraph{Tested null.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the stated controls and after the same-row translation readout has been attached.  In finite notation, the intended comparison is bounded by
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{0}:\quad
&\Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}(B^{*}_{Q6,\mathrm{high}},B^{*}_{Q6,\mathrm{low}}\mid Z,T)=0,\\
Z=\;&\{\hbox{amino-acid composition, transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density,}\\
&\hbox{GC/dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, abundance, batch}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T$ denotes the same-row translation readout.  A departure from this null is still only an RNA-stability association unless the measured decay-factor RBP bridge is present.  The bridge requires local RBP occupancy at the editable RNA window, collapse of the half-life effect after loss of the target RBP, restoration by wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Positive criterion.}
The chapter licenses a positive cross-layer RBP bridge only under a four-part finite-row pattern.  First, the coordinate-associated half-life residual must remain after the mandatory controls and after translation-readout conditioning.  Second, the same local window must show experimentally decoupled occupancy by the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  Third, target-RBP loss must remove or sharply attenuate the coordinate-associated half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect while binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  Written compactly, the required readback is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}\neq 0,\\
&\Delta_{\mathrm{occupancy}}(\mathrm{target\ RBP})\neq 0,\\
&\Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}\mid \mathrm{target\ loss}\approx 0,\\
&\Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}\mid \mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}\hbox{ returns},\\
&\Delta_{\mathrm{half\text{-}life}}\mid \mathrm{binding\ defective/non\ target\ rescue}\hbox{ does not return}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The current finite memory names the half-life, translation, and abundance-control routes, but it does not yet contain that full same-window occupancy, perturbation, and rescue matrix for this subject.

\paragraph{Layer boundary.}
The N-terminal acetylation AUROC of $0.942081$ over $657$ rows is a strong finite occupancy discriminator for its own annotated surface, not evidence that synonymous residual coordinates control RNA decay.  The $900$-peak RBP table and the $6494$-row translation table likewise remain bounded contacts until they are joined to the same transcripts, same windows, and same perturbation design.  A translation readout is necessary here because the claim is translation-conditioned, but translation readout alone is not an RNA-decay mechanism.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control, not a half-life observation.  RNA half-life alone is not RBP occupancy.  RBP occupancy alone is not causal rescue.

Consequently, no claim about protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this record.  Those conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  The present chapter records a bounded criterion for when a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA half-life association may be promoted to an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge, and it records that the presently available finite rows stop short of that promotion unless the missing same-window occupancy, loss, and rescue rows are supplied.
