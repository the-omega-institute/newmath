\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite matched-panel question: whether a synonymous residual coordinate window, written here as a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ label after amino-acid quotienting, can be read as a bounded cross-layer relation to RNA half-life through a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein. The finite carrier is not a free sequence pattern. A row is eligible only when it carries a residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout or translation-efficiency boundary, an RNA half-life measurement, matched mRNA or transcriptional controls, local composition controls, and an independently measured occupancy and perturbation route for the named decay-factor RBP. The intended positive pattern is therefore a conjunction of finite events, not a single association:
$$
\begin{aligned}
& q_i \in B^{*}_{Q6},\quad T_i \text{ observed or explicitly bounded},\quad H_i \text{ an RNA half-life readout},\\
& O_i \text{ measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP},\\
& L_i \text{ target-RBP loss},\quad R_i^{\mathrm{wt}} \text{ wild-type rescue},\quad R_i^{\mathrm{bd}} \text{ binding-defective or non-target control}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive bridge requires the coordinate-associated half-life residual to track measured occupancy, disappear under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue. Without these rows, the record remains a bounded RNA-stability or coordinate-description record rather than an RBP-mediated decay claim.

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence already present.}
The current finite evidence table contains a strong but different cross-layer occupancy example for N-terminal acetylation: the run \texttt{f452e76f6363} reports $n=657$ protein rows, $n_{\mathrm{NatA}}=426$ NatA-class rows, and an AUROC of $0.942081$ on $622$ high-versus-low rows. This result is useful as a calibration point for what a finite occupancy-discrimination record looks like: it has a declared module, a bounded row count, and a scalar separation statistic. It does not, however, measure RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or rescue behavior at an editable RNA window.

A separate RBP-binding contact supplies a limited binding calibration surface: the run \texttt{b225890251c2} contains $900$ peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases. That fact confirms that finite RBP binding rows can be represented in this framework, but it is not the same as a decay-factor bridge for the present coordinate panel. In particular, generic binding concordance does not establish the required same-window occupancy change, does not supply RNA half-life residuals, and does not include the loss-and-rescue intervention pattern needed for a mechanistic RNA-decay interpretation.

The translation boundary is also represented elsewhere in finite form: the mTOR translation run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} reports $n=6494$ rows with a declared readout source and runtime $9.469$ seconds. This supports the feasibility of same-row translation readouts as finite tables, but it does not by itself supply RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, or perturbation-rescue rows for the present $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate question. Thus the three finite facts jointly show that occupancy discrimination, RBP binding surfaces, and translation readouts can each be recorded, while also showing that the required joined RNA half-life RBP bridge panel is not yet present in the supplied data.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null model is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after the declared controls. Let $Z_i$ collect amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. The null states that, conditional on $Z_i$, the coordinate label has no bridge-specific effect on RNA half-life:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
If a half-life difference remains but measured local RBP occupancy is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, or if wild-type rescue fails to restore it, the observation is not an RBP bridge. If binding-defective rescue or a non-target control restores the effect, the bridge interpretation is likewise rejected because the pattern no longer depends on the specified binding route.

\paragraph{Bridge statistic.}
The finite bridge statistic must be evaluated only after the joined panel exists. One convenient form is a signed residual contrast for RNA half-life after conditioning on translation and controls, paired with intervention checks:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high}, Z_i] - \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low}, Z_i],\\
\Delta_O &= \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[O_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high}, Z_i] - \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[O_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low}, Z_i],\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\quad
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wt\ rescue}} \approx \Delta_H,\quad
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{bd\ rescue}} \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The statistic is bridge-supporting only when the half-life residual and measured occupancy residual agree in the prespecified direction, the target-RBP loss removes the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails. The finite row counts cited above do not instantiate this statistic for the present subject; they only show nearby finite surfaces that must not be conflated.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no claim about protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law. It also licenses no translation realization from RNA half-life alone. A same-row translation readout is a necessary conditioning boundary, not a proof of an RNA-decay mechanism. A measured RNA half-life association is not a decay-factor RBP mechanism unless the same finite panel also supplies measured local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. Motif density, RNA-structure modeling, transcript abundance, codon-pair context, GC content, dinucleotide composition, isoform state, or batch effects may explain an apparent signal unless explicitly controlled. The present record is therefore ready as a bounded BioReality boundary chapter: it names the finite carrier, states the null, cites the available row-level facts, and refuses promotion beyond the missing joined RBP-decay evidence.
