\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life boundary for an RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about B$^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate windows in coding transcripts.  Each admissible row is required to carry a residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcript abundance, transcriptional state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, and batch.  The proposed cross-layer statement is not that a codon coordinate itself causes RNA decay.  It is the narrower assertion that a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be read as an RBP-mediated bridge only when the local occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows move with the same finite contrast.

The primary finite run attached to this record is the N-terminal acetylation occupancy panel with experiment identifier \texttt{f452e76f6363}.  It contains $657$ rows, of which $426$ are annotated NatA rows.  The high-versus-low contrast reports $622$ usable rows and an AUROC of $0.942081$ for the measured occupancy discrimination.  These numbers are useful here as a worked example of a finite occupancy surface with a declared row count and scalar statistic, but they do not by themselves instantiate the RNA half-life RBP bridge.  They show that this BioReality packet is allowed to cite finite occupancy evidence only when the measured surface, row universe, and statistic are explicit.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
Let $q_i \in \{0,1\}$ denote the high-coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting for row $i$, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ be measured local occupancy for the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The intended positive bridge is a finite contrast of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= E[H_i \mid q_i=1,Z_i,T_i]-E[H_i \mid q_i=0,Z_i,T_i],\\
\Delta_O &= E[O_i \mid q_i=1,Z_i]-E[O_i \mid q_i=0,Z_i],
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch controls.  A positive RNA half-life residual is not enough.  The same row family must also show that the local occupancy contrast is experimentally separable from the coordinate contrast, that target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, that wild-type rescue restores it, and that binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.

The RBP-binding comparison run \texttt{b225890251c2} supplies a second finite contact relevant to the boundary.  It contains $900$ peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  This is evidence that finite RBP occupancy panels can be represented with explicit row counts and cross-protein concordance checks, not evidence that the present RNA half-life bridge has already been measured.  The distinction matters: an occupancy surface can support the bridge only if it is joined to the same organism, same window, same coordinate panel, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life readout, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Translation conditioning.}
The conjecture is translation-conditioned.  RNA half-life cannot be promoted to a translation-layer realization unless a same-row translation boundary is attached and conditioned in the statistic.  The mTOR translation run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} records $6494$ rows from a translation readout source and finished in $9.469$ seconds.  That run is cited only to fix the kind of finite translation surface that can enter a joined panel.  It does not supply the RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or rescue rows required here.  In the present packet, $T_i$ is a conditioning variable that prevents transcript stability from being misread as translation output; it is not a protein abundance, folding, function, phenotype, adaptation, or mechanism assay.

\paragraph{Acceptance rule.}
The finite panel is accepted as an RBP-bridge record only under the following local rule.  High- and low-coordinate labels must be exchangeable for RNA half-life after the declared controls unless all bridge components align: a translation-conditioned half-life residual, a measured local occupancy change for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapse under target-RBP loss, restoration under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  If any one of these components is absent, unchanged, or contradicted, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA-stability association.  In particular, motif density alone, local RNA-structure alone, transcript abundance, transcription rate, codon-pair context, GC composition, dinucleotide composition, isoform state, batch, or a non-target RBP control cannot be counted as support for the RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This record licenses no claim about protein output, ribosome dynamics beyond the explicitly joined translation readout, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, system phenotype, evolutionary adaptation, causation, or universality.  It also does not allow the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry to select an RBP after the fact.  The RBP must be externally specified and measured at the local window, and the perturbation and rescue rows must be part of the finite panel.  Without those rows, the correct interpretation is a blocked null: the available contacts name RNA half-life, mRNA abundance, translation readout, and separate occupancy-style finite surfaces, but they do not yet form the same-window perturbation-rescue matrix needed for a positive RNA-decay mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Finite readback.}
The current finite evidence therefore supports the chapter as a sharply bounded BioReality record rather than as a completed mechanistic result.  The occupancy run contributes $657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, $622$ high-low rows, and AUROC $0.942081$ as a concrete finite occupancy example.  The RBP-binding run contributes $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases as a concrete finite RBP-contact example.  The translation run contributes $6494$ rows as a concrete translation-readout example.  None of these contacts alone closes the RNA half-life RBP bridge.  The bridge becomes positive only when a single matched panel joins B$^{*}_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls under the declared finite statistic.
