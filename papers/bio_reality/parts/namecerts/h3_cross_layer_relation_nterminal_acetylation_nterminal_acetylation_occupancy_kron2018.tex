\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with a decay-factor RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript and coding-sequence rows in which a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label is retained only after the amino-acid quotient, then joined to same-row translation readout, RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and perturbation controls.  The claim is not that a codon coordinate is itself a decay mechanism.  The claim is the narrower conditional statement: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be read as a bounded cross-layer RBP bridge only when the same finite panel also contains experimentally separated local RBP occupancy, collapse after loss of the target RBP, restoration by wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Numerical anchor.}
The finite audit table contains one fully computed cross-layer scalar for the named Kron2018 N-terminal acetylation occupancy record: $n=657$, $n_{\mathrm{NatA}}=426$, high-low comparison size $622$, and $\mathrm{AUROC}=0.942081$ with runtime $1.1687$ seconds.  Those numbers show that the row system can carry a strong finite occupancy classifier when the relevant occupancy surface is actually present.  They do not by themselves provide RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, or perturbation-rescue rows for the present RNA-decay question.  A separate RBP-binding audit supplies a different contact scale, with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries at runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  A translation-readout audit supplies another finite scale, with $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation record and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  These auxiliary scalars are used here only to keep the row vocabulary concrete: occupancy, RBP binding, and translation readout are distinct measured surfaces, not interchangeable labels.

\paragraph{Matched carrier.}
Let each retained row have fields
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_i = (q_i, T_i, H_i, O_i, L_i, W_i, B_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotient, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, $L_i$ is target-RBP loss state, $W_i$ is wild-type rescue state, $B_i$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue controls, and $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline terms.  The admissible statistic is therefore not a marginal contrast $q_i \to H_i$.  It is a conditioned finite readout that first requires $T_i$, then asks whether the remaining $q_i$-associated half-life term follows the measured RBP occupancy and the loss-rescue logic.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The positive bridge condition is the conjunction of four finite tests.  First, high- and low-coordinate labels must differ in translation-conditioned RNA half-life after the declared controls.  Second, the difference must track measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP rather than merely motif density, RNA structure, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, or batch.  Third, loss of the target RBP must collapse the half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect while binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  In compact form, the bridge requires
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H(q \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\qquad
  \Delta_H(q \mid T,Z,O) \text{ follows } O,\qquad
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} \approx 0,\qquad
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT}} \text{ returns},\qquad
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{defective}} \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Failure of any one term downgrades the record to a bounded coordinate descriptor or to a finite RNA-stability association without an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null makes high- and low-coordinate labels exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on translation readout and the stated controls.  A half-life difference without measured local RBP occupancy is insufficient.  An occupancy association without coordinate-occupancy decoupling is insufficient.  A loss effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target failure is insufficient.  Likewise, a structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, or batch explanation remains a live null unless defeated inside the same finite panel.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The present record therefore remains a translation-conditioned RNA half-life boundary until the missing RBP rows are attached.  The Kron2018 occupancy classifier establishes that a finite occupancy surface can be numerically sharp in the supplied audit table, but it is an N-terminal acetylation occupancy record, not a measured same-window decay-factor RBP perturbation-rescue panel for this RNA half-life conjecture.  The RBP-binding and mTOR translation rows likewise give finite contact scales, not the required joined statistic.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone; no RNA decay mechanism follows from translation conditioning alone; no protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the declared finite statistic passes there as well.
