\subsection{A finite-row certificate for a translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of transcript or coding-sequence rows.  Each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout or an explicit translation-efficiency boundary, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, local sequence-composition controls, and a prespecified route for measuring occupancy by a decay-factor RNA-binding protein.  The row type is deliberately narrow: it is not a sequence-law claim, not a protein-output claim, and not a functional claim.  Its only positive target is a bounded cross-layer relation in which a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual becomes interpretable as an RBP-mediated decay bridge only after the occupancy, perturbation, and rescue rows are all present.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The nearest completed finite-row evidence in the supplied ledger is not an RNA-decay bridge itself, but it fixes the scale of row-level cross-layer assays used by this chapter.  The n-terminal-acetylation occupancy run \texttt{f452e76f6363} contains $657$ finite rows, with $426$ NatA-labelled rows, and reports an AUROC of $0.942081$ on the high-versus-low subset of size $622$.  In the notation of this chapter this supplies the scalar packet
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{acetyl}} &= 657,\\
  n_{\mathrm{NatA}} &= 426,\\
  n_{\mathrm{hi/lo}} &= 622,\\
  \operatorname{AUROC}_{\mathrm{acetyl}} &= 0.942081 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This is used only as evidence that the repository already contains finite matched occupancy-style cross-layer tables with a declared bounded statistic.  It is not read as RNA half-life, RBP binding, translation, folding, mechanism, or organism-wide law.

A second row-level anchor comes from the RBP binding ledger.  The RBNS/eCLIP binding run \texttt{b225890251c2} reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{RBP\ peaks}} &= 900,\\
  n_{\mathrm{cross\mbox{-}RBP\ concordant}} &= 3 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This supplies a finite contact shape for measured binding or occupancy surfaces, but it still does not provide the specific same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls required for the RNA half-life bridge.  A third translation-scale anchor is the mTOR translation run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, which gives $6494$ rows from the declared readout source.  It is admissible here only as a reminder that translation contacts are finite same-row readouts, not as a substitute for RNA decay or RBP perturbation.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
Let each matched row $r$ carry a coordinate label $q(r)$, a translation readout $T(r)$, an RNA half-life readout $H(r)$, transcript and abundance controls $C_{\mathrm{tx}}(r)$, local RNA-structure and composition controls $C_{\mathrm{rna}}(r)$, and a measured local occupancy value $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(r)$ for a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  A positive bridge is allowed only when the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate contrast in $H$ remains after conditioning on the translation readout and all declared controls, follows experimentally decoupled occupancy change, disappears under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The intended finite decision surface can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H(q \mid T,C_{\mathrm{tx}},C_{\mathrm{rna}}) &\neq 0,\\
  \Delta_{O_{\mathrm{RBP}}}(q) &\neq 0,\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}}(q \mid T,C_{\mathrm{tx}},C_{\mathrm{rna}}) &= 0,\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}(q \mid T,C_{\mathrm{tx}},C_{\mathrm{rna}}) &\approx \Delta_H(q \mid T,C_{\mathrm{tx}},C_{\mathrm{rna}}),\\
  \Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\mbox{-}defective}}(q \mid T,C_{\mathrm{tx}},C_{\mathrm{rna}}) &= 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The null is row exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout have been controlled.  A half-life contrast without the occupancy-loss-rescue pattern remains an RNA-stability association, not an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Name certificate.}
The certificate name for this chapter is \texttt{q6.translation-conditioned.rna-half-life-rbp-bridge.cross-layer}.  Its carrier consists of finite matched transcript/CDS rows, residual coordinate labels, translation readouts, RNA half-life values, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation rows, rescue rows, and matched controls.  Its distinction set separates high-coordinate from low-coordinate synonymous residual labels, occupancy-present from occupancy-absent or occupancy-reduced windows, wild-type rescue from binding-defective or non-target rescue, and translation-conditioned RNA half-life residuals from transcriptional or abundance effects.  Its readback is the finite matched-panel pattern: half-life effect, occupancy change, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and control-rescue failure.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter licenses no claim about protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  It also licenses no translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  A same-row translation readout is a conditioning boundary, not proof of a translation mechanism.  Measured RNA half-life is a decay readout, not measured RBP occupancy.  RBP occupancy is not by itself a mechanism unless the target-RBP loss and rescue rows satisfy the finite contrast stated above.  The completed acetylation, RBP-binding, and translation ledgers provide finite-row precedents and contact shapes, but the positive RNA half-life RBP bridge remains tied to the stricter matched panel specified in this chapter.
