In that row, the captured spectra are led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, with per-organism dominant counts recorded as $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$. \paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter-level claim is therefore negative and bounded.  On the recorded finite surface, the fourth residual axis should not be named mRNA stability, because the yeast half-life direction $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ aligns only weakly with the abundance-linked fourth residual direction under the fixed gates.  The positive content is the exclusion of one proposed identity; the row does not by itself identify the fourth axis, does not establish a causal molecular pathway, and does not upgrade residual geometry into a downstream biological law.

\paragraph{Boundary of what follows.}
No translation-output claim follows from this row unless a separate translation contact supplies a non-vacuous controlled survival statistic for the same coordinate.  No folding, physical admissibility, protein function, localization, protein stability, phenotype, mechanistic causation, or universality follows from the stability exclusion.  mRNA abundance or half-life information can serve as a measured covariate or a candidate axis for a stated assay, but it is not a substitute for ribosome-profiling rows, protein-state rows, structure rows, perturbation assays, or organism-wide causal evidence.  The finite conclusion is exactly that mRNA stability is not the dominant fourth-axis identity in this assay, with the stated fold count, design width, protein floor, and seed; stronger biological interpretations require their own matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.
