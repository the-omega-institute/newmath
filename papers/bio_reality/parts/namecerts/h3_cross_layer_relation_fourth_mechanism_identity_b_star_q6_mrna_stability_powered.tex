\subsection{Fourth-axis mRNA-stability exclusion for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mrna-stability-exclusion}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This BioReality record concerns the finite CDS-by-organism residual coordinate surface obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $f_3$-stress coordinate inside the $B^*_{Q6}$ family and evaluated against matched translation-layer readouts.  The local question is not whether the coordinate has internal codon-topological structure, and not whether modeled adaptation weights can be written down.  The question is narrower: whether the powered fourth-axis assay identifies mRNA stability as the dominant biological identity of the fourth residual direction.  The finite row for this claim answers that question negatively.  The assay status is passed, with run id $377\mathrm{abd}67\mathrm{b}8\mathrm{b}3$, five folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  Its recorded conclusion is that the fourth axis is not mRNA stability: in yeast, $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ has low alignment with the abundance-side $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  Therefore RNA half-life is excluded here as the dominant fourth-axis identity in this assay, rather than promoted as a mechanism.

\paragraph{Finite codon boundary.}
The coordinate is anchored in a finite codon surface.  The verified $M=\mathrm{Med}(R)\cup \mathrm{ACA}$ carrier contains the twenty codons
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The verified $f_3$-saturation row removes $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, leaving the eighteen-codon surface
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  Thus the assay is not being read from an unbounded nucleotide grammar; it is read from a named finite codon set with a recorded zero boundary for the $f_3$ saturation step.

\paragraph{Controlled exclusion statement.}
The mRNA-stability row is an exclusion, not a positive cross-layer claim.  In the notation of the finite assay, the observed row can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{folds} &= 5,\\
\mathrm{minimum\ proteins\ per\ organism} &= 500,\\
\mathrm{synonymous\ design\ columns} &= 41,\\
\mathrm{conclusion} &= \mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reason is specific: the yeast half-life direction $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ does not align strongly enough with the abundance residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  Hence the finite assay rejects the interpretation that the fourth residual axis is primarily an mRNA-stability axis.  This does not say that mRNA decay is irrelevant to every codon-coordinate question; it says only that this powered fourth-axis identity test did not identify mRNA stability as the dominant identity of the fourth residual direction.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding powered rows.}
The exclusion sits inside a stronger finite pattern.  The three-axis sufficiency test was passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, and its conclusion was that a fourth mechanism is present.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum was also computed on $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms; the captured spectra were led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, then by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, with per-organism dominance recorded as $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  Domain means were explicitly marked descriptive because the domain counts were imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms, and the largest recorded GC3 rank effect was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.

\paragraph{Competing identities.}
Other powered rows constrain nearby explanations.  The codon-pair identity test passed with conclusion $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$, using five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null draws.  The translation-accuracy test passed with conclusion $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using $200$ permutations and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The elongation-dwell row was organism-specific for $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$ and returned $\mathrm{partial}$ rather than a complete identity call.  These rows make the mRNA-stability exclusion sharper: the fourth direction is present in the finite residual surface, but the tested identities do not collapse it to mRNA half-life, codon-pair composition, or translation accuracy, while elongation dwell remains only partial in the available yeast row.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The finite row also preserves a strict readout boundary.  A codon residual coordinate becomes a translation-layer statement only where a direct or explicitly bounded translation readout survives the declared controls.  The relevant controls include amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where translation efficiency is formed, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablation.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights may serve as bounded codon-adaptation readouts, but they are not ribosome-occupancy measurements by themselves.  The three-organism all-$1.0$ controlled matrix is treated as rank-degenerate and therefore as a null boundary, not as survival evidence.

\paragraph{tRNA and wobble context.}
The finite $R$-sense tRNA-poverty row supports the local mechanism pressure without promoting this mRNA-stability claim.  That row used $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and concluded that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights.  Separately, the non-tRNA residual optimality row passed on $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms and identified $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant non-tRNA residual axis.  The two-axis closure row concluded that a third axis is present because $d_\perp$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms.  The third-axis identity row then provisionally aligned $d_\perp$ to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$, while the mechanism row reported selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, and a CSC/positional identity still requiring a separate external row.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter-level verdict is therefore negative and bounded: the powered assay excludes mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis identity for this $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate under the stated gates.  The result is informative precisely because a fourth residual direction is present elsewhere in the finite panel, and because several nearby identity hypotheses are also constrained.  What remains open is the positive biological identity of the fourth direction, not the rejected mRNA-stability identity.

\paragraph{No promotion beyond the measured layer.}
No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row alone.  The mRNA half-life assay is an identity test for one proposed fourth-axis interpretation.  A stronger claim would require its own matched finite panel and its own statistic: for translation, same-row ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency; for protein abundance, matched proteomics after a surviving translation step; for RNA decay mechanism, decay-factor occupancy and perturbation or rescue rows; for structure, function, localization, stability, physical admissibility, or phenotype, a layer-matched contact at that layer.  The present record keeps those stronger readings outside the claim.
