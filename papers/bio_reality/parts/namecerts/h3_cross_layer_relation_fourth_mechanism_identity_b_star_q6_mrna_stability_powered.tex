\subsection{The fourth $B^{*}_{Q6}$ axis is not identified with mRNA stability}
\label{subsec:biorality-q6-fourth-axis-not-mrna-stability}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object considered here is the finite synonymous residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection of coding sequences, restricted to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate family and then tested against matched translation-layer or transcript-layer rows. The specific coordinate under review is the $f_3$-stress direction: it records synonymous third-position imbalance only after amino-acid identity has been quotiented away. The saturated $f_3$ construction has the concrete row support
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
M \setminus \operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\},\qquad \partial_{f_3}R=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tested coordinate is not a free transcript abundance marker. It is a finite codon-residual coordinate whose biological interpretation must pass through a separate row of translation or RNA measurements.

\paragraph{Assay row and verdict.}
The powered mRNA-stability test was run as \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered} with five folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns. Its recorded result is \texttt{passed}, with conclusion \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}. The finite reason is specific: in the yeast assay, the optimized half-life direction $d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}}$ has low alignment with the abundance-associated fourth residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates. Therefore this assay excludes mRNA stability as the dominant identity of the fourth $B^{*}_{Q6}$ axis. The statement is negative and local: it does not say that RNA half-life is irrelevant to every codon-residual question, only that the fourth residual direction isolated here is not explained by the tested mRNA-stability row.

\paragraph{Position among the other finite tests.}
The surrounding finite records make the exclusion sharper. A separate three-axis sufficiency assay, also using five folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, concluded \texttt{fourth\_mechanism\_present}; hence the fourth direction is not an artefact of merely stopping at the first three axes. The axis-strength spectrum requested $16$ organisms and computed $11$; across those computed organisms, $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ led the captured spectra on average, with per-organism dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$, while the largest GC3 rank effect reported there was $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$. The residual fourth direction is therefore visible in the finite panel, but the mRNA half-life row does not identify it.

\paragraph{Comparison with competing mechanism rows.}
Other mechanism probes keep the fourth-axis question bounded rather than collapsed into RNA stability. The codon-pair assay returned \texttt{not\_codon\_pair} under five folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and a shuffle null count of $50$. The translation-accuracy assay requested $16$ organisms, computed $11$, used $200$ permutations, and concluded \texttt{not\_accuracy}. The elongation-dwell row was run in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} with five folds and returned \texttt{partial}. These rows do not promote elongation dwell to a full mechanism, but they do show that the mRNA-stability exclusion is part of a finite differential mechanism screen rather than a one-row absence claim.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation residual geometry.}
The finite background also separates the fourth-axis question from the already resolved lower residual directions. The non-tRNA residual optimality assay requested $16$ organisms, computed $11$, and identified the dominant residual axis as \texttt{f3\_translation\_selection}. The two-axis closure assay, again with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested and $41$ synonymous-design columns, concluded \texttt{third\_axis\_present}. The third-axis identity assay then aligned $d_{\perp}$ to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. Its mechanism companion reported that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$, summarized as selection-not-GC and not-wobble-clustered. These facts are not used to infer the fourth mechanism directly; they only establish that the residual basis has already been tested against simpler mutation, GC, and usage explanations before the mRNA-stability row is asked to carry the fourth direction.

\paragraph{Readout rule.}
For the $f_3$-stress coordinate, the admissible positive biological readback would be a non-vacuous controlled survival entry $S_{f3,T_j}$ for a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The present mRNA-stability assay does not provide that positive translation row. In formal terms, the relevant gate has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3,T_j} &> 0
\quad\text{after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls.}
\end{aligned}
$$
When that inequality is absent or rank-degenerate, no downstream interpretation is licensed. A modeled tAI or stAI surface may bound a translation-weight question, and an mRNA row may serve as a transcript control, but neither one alone is an observed ribosome-occupancy row or a protein-output row.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
This chapter records a finite negative identification: the fourth $B^{*}_{Q6}$ direction is not mRNA stability in the powered yeast half-life assay under the fixed gates. From this result alone one must not infer translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, phenotype, causation, mechanism, or a universal biological law. Each such layer requires its own matched contact and its own controlled finite statistic. In particular, a positive or negative RNA half-life alignment cannot substitute for the mandatory translation-readout survival entry, and a rank-degenerate translation surface cannot be treated as evidence merely because the coordinate geometry is well defined.
