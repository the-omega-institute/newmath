\subsection{Finite exclusion of mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis identity}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourth-axis-mrna-stability}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This BioReality record concerns the finite residual coordinate surface obtained from coding sequences after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate and to translation-adjacent readout rows.  The question is not whether transcript stability can ever matter biologically.  The bounded question is narrower: whether the fourth residual direction detected after the tRNA, $f_3$, and usage-frequency axes should be identified with mRNA half-life in the powered assay that compares yeast half-life alignment against the abundance-side residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$.

\paragraph{Powered assay row.}
The direct finite-row result for this claim is the run $377\mathrm{abd}67\mathrm{b}8\mathrm{b}3$, with experiment id $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}$.  The assay passed its gates with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  Its deterministic seed was $\mathrm{sha256{:}22c2c4dca83559af532a45fe2777c3c21afc8cd39d329c602491409752243209}$.  The recorded conclusion is
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{conclusion} &= \mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}.
\end{aligned}$$
The reason is concrete: in yeast, $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ has low alignment with the abundance-side direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  Within this finite assay, mRNA stability is therefore excluded as the dominant identity of the fourth axis.  This is an exclusion statement about the tested fourth-axis interpretation, not a general denial that mRNA decay, transcript turnover, or RNA-binding proteins can influence expression in other panels or under other readout definitions.

\paragraph{Neighboring exclusions and partial positives.}
Other fourth-axis candidate rows sharpen the local interpretation.  The codon-pair assay, run $\mathrm{b}519\mathrm{b}5\mathrm{ebd}34\mathrm{d}$, passed with $5$ folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null replicates, but concluded $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$.  The translation-accuracy assay, run $0\mathrm{dc}44\mathrm{df}59\mathrm{ba}7$, passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, used $200$ permutations, and concluded $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$.  The elongation-dwell assay, run $\mathrm{e}4\mathrm{edcde}459\mathrm{b}7$, was restricted to $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$ and concluded $\mathrm{partial}$.  Thus the finite record separates a negative mRNA-stability identity call from a partial elongation-dwell signal and from two additional negative candidates.  The stable statement is the ordered one: mRNA half-life is not the dominant fourth-axis identity in the fixed powered assay; elongation-dwell remains only partial; codon-pair and accuracy are not supported as identities by their respective tests.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The row is deliberately bounded.  A low alignment between $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ and $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ excludes one candidate explanation for the fourth residual direction under the finite gates; it does not by itself establish a translation-efficiency effect, ribosome occupancy effect, protein-abundance effect, folded-structure consequence, physical admissibility property, molecular function, phenotype, causal pathway, or universal biological law.  A positive translation claim would require an organism- or CDS-matched translation contact, such as measured ribosome-profiling or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency rows, paired with codon counts and controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline ablation.  A higher-layer claim would require its own layer-matched contact after that translation row is non-vacuous.  In the present finite row, the accepted content is only the powered exclusion: the fourth residual axis detected in the surrounding $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ spectrum is not identified with mRNA stability by the yeast half-life alignment assay.
