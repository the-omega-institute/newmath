\paragraph{Local statistic and refusal form.}
The finite verdict can be written as a constrained identity test.  Let $q$ denote the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate row, let $d_4$ denote the fourth residual direction recovered from the abundance-side assay, and let $h$ denote the matched RNA half-life direction available in yeast.  The assay does not ask whether RNA decay ever matters biologically; it asks whether $h$ is the identity of $d_4$ on this finite surface.  The recorded outcome is the negative branch:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{IdentityCandidate}(d_4,h) &\longrightarrow \mathrm{low\ alignment},\\
\mathrm{DominantFourthAxis}(d_4) &= \mathrm{not\ RNA\ stability}\quad\text{in this assay.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The refusal is therefore positive information: it removes one tempting downstream interpretation without erasing the residual axis itself.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The empirical contacts used around this packet have different scopes.  Per-organism codon-usage tables summarize coding-sequence composition.  tRNA gene-copy tables summarize annotated anticodon supply.  tAI or stAI weights are modeled codon-adaptation readbacks, not observed ribosome footprints.  Ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows, when available, can test a translation-layer statistic only after organism and coding-sequence matching with mRNA controls.  Matched mRNA abundance belongs in the control set for translation-efficiency residualization and does not itself provide translation output.  Therefore the RNA-stability refusal here does not certify a translation mechanism, a protein-abundance mechanism, a folded-structure state, a physical-admissibility claim, a molecular function, a phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Those interpretations require their own layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The chapter-level conclusion is a bounded negative identification.  The powered finite row is ready for inclusion because it has a concrete run identifier, a passed status, five-fold evaluation, a $500$-protein organism threshold, $41$ synonymous design columns, and a stated finite conclusion.  Within this evidence envelope, the fourth residual axis associated with $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance behavior should not be called an mRNA-stability or RNA-half-life axis.  The remaining live statement is weaker and cleaner: a fourth mechanism is present in the powered residual panel, but its dominant identity is not RNA stability in the yeast half-life assay reported here.
