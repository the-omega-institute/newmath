\subsection{The fourth B*\_Q6 axis is not mRNA stability in the finite yeast half-life assay}\label{sec:bireality-q6-fourth-axis-not-mrna-stability}

\paragraph{Claim being tested.}
This record concerns the finite residual coordinate surface obtained after amino-acid projection of coding-sequence codon counts.  The specific coordinate is the $f_3$-stress component inside the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis: it compares synonymous codon imbalance that remains visible after the amino-acid identity has been quotiented out.  The proposed fourth mechanism was mRNA stability.  The finite test asked whether the abundance-aligned fourth residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ is explained by a yeast mRNA half-life direction, denoted here by $d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}}$, under the fixed powered gates.  The recorded conclusion is negative: the experiment classifies the fourth axis as $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$.

\paragraph{Powered mRNA-stability test.}
The mRNA-stability assay was run as experiment $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}$ with run id $377\mathrm{abd}67\mathrm{b}8\mathrm{b}3$.  The powered design used $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The deterministic seed was $\mathrm{sha256{:}22c2c4dca83559af532a45fe2777c3c21afc8cd39d329c602491409752243209}$.  Under these gates the yeast half-life direction had low alignment with the abundance-side $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ direction, so the assay excludes mRNA stability as the dominant identity of the fourth residual axis in this setting.  In compact form, the finite decision is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{class}(d_{\mathrm{resid4}},d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}})
  &= \mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability},\\
\operatorname{folds} &= 5,\\
\operatorname{design\ columns} &= 41,\\
\operatorname{minimum\ proteins} &= 500.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a negative identity result, not a claim that mRNA decay has no biological role.  It only says that, in the finite half-life comparison used here, the fourth residual direction is not dominated by the mRNA-stability candidate.

The negative mRNA-stability call is coherent with the surrounding finite-row evidence.  The larger axis-strength spectrum computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, used $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns, and found that $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ led the captured spectra on average; the per-organism dominant count was $11$ for $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$. Separately, the two-axis closure test over $11$ computed organisms reported a third axis as present, while the three-axis sufficiency test over the same $11$ computed organisms reported a fourth mechanism as present.  The fourth mechanism therefore remains a real residual target in this finite panel, but the yeast half-life assay says that mRNA stability is not its dominant identity.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The present chapter does not assert that the $f_3$-stress coordinate changes protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, organismal phenotype, or a universal biological law.  It also does not assert that mRNA stability is irrelevant in general.  The only finite conclusion licensed here is narrower: the powered yeast half-life comparison did not identify mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis mechanism. \paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The finite result is best read as a mechanism-exclusion row inside a larger cross-layer search.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ fourth residual direction is strong enough to survive as a target in the powered axis tests, but its dominant identity is not supplied by the yeast mRNA half-life direction under the fixed gates.  The remaining admissible interpretation is that the fourth axis should be pursued through translation-layer rows where wobble or tRNA-context asymmetries break $f_3$ symmetry, and through measured ribosome or translation-efficiency contacts when those rows are organism- and CDS-matched.  Until such a row produces a positive controlled $S_{ij}$ value, the result remains a bounded negative identity statement for mRNA stability rather than a downstream biological mechanism claim.
