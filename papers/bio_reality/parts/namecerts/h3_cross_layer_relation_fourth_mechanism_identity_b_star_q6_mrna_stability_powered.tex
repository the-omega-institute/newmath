\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The $f_{3}$-stress coordinate becomes a biological translation claim only when the relevant third-position symmetry is broken by context tRNA or wobble weights and when a matched translation readout survives controls.  The required survival row is a controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_{j}}$ for a bounded tAI, stAI, ribosome-occupancy, or translation-efficiency readout after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, and matched mRNA controls have been applied.  A three-organism all-$1.0$ matrix is not such a row; it is a rank-degenerate boundary.  Likewise, a modeled tAI or stAI surface is not an observed ribosome-footprint matrix unless a separate measured contact supplies that row.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter records a finite negative identity result for mRNA stability.  It does not assert that $f_{3}$-stress changes translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, organismal phenotype, or any universal biological law.  It also does not treat transcript abundance as a translation readout.  Matched mRNA abundance may enter the control set for translation-efficiency residualization, but it is not itself ribosome occupancy, protein output, or mechanism evidence.  Any claim above the bounded translation-readout row requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  Under the present finite facts, the stable conclusion is only this: within the powered fourth-mechanism assay, yeast mRNA half-life does not align strongly enough with $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ to identify mRNA stability as the dominant fourth mechanism.
