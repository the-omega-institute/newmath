\subsection{The mRNA-stability refusal for the fourth $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ mechanism}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourth-mechanism-mrna-stability-refusal}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The record considered here is the finite CDS-by-organism residual coordinate surface restricted to the $f_3$-stress component of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ and to matched translation-layer rows.  The starting codon packet is already finite.  The saturated $f_3$ image of the $R$ set contains
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\{AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\cr
&\quad UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas the corresponding $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup ACA$ finite list contains the same eighteen codons together with $ACA$ and $ACG$.  Thus the stored boundary $f_3$-boundary is empty in this finite run, while the two codons $ACA,ACG$ mark the additional $M$ entries not retained in the saturated $R$ image.  This makes the local object a residual coordinate test rather than a direct biological assertion about RNA decay, ribosome motion, protein state, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Powered negative call.}
The mRNA-stability experiment for the fourth mechanism was run as a five-fold finite assay with at least $500$ proteins per organism and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  Its status is passed, but the conclusion is negative for the proposed identity: $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ in yeast has low alignment with the abundance-linked $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ direction under the fixed gates.  In the language of this chapter, the fourth axis is therefore not identified with mRNA stability in this assay.  The claim is not that RNA half-life is irrelevant to coding biology; it is narrower.  The finite evidence says that the residual direction called the fourth axis is not explained as the dominant mRNA-stability direction by this powered half-life comparison.

\paragraph{Position among the neighboring finite tests.}
The refusal is informative because nearby powered runs do find structure in the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface.  A three-axis sufficiency test over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again with five folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, concluded that a fourth mechanism is present.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum was computed for the same $11$ of $16$ organisms and found $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ dominant in all $11$ organism-level calls; the descriptive domain sample was imbalanced with $1$ archaeon, $2$ bacteria, and $8$ eukaryotes, and the largest reported GC3 rank effect was only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  The fourth-axis signal is therefore not being dismissed as numerical noise; rather, one candidate identity, mRNA stability, fails the fixed gate.

\paragraph{Mechanism discrimination.}
Other fourth-mechanism probes sharpen the local interpretation.  The elongation-dwell assay in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} returned a partial call under five folds.  The codon-pair assay returned $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ with five folds, a $500$-protein minimum, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null samples.  The translation-accuracy assay returned $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ across $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using five folds, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $200$ permutations.  Thus the finite row does not license a positive RNA-stability mechanism, a codon-pair mechanism, or an accuracy mechanism; it leaves a partial elongation-dwell relation as a bounded neighboring signal and keeps the fourth-axis identity open beyond these exclusions.

\paragraph{Translation-readout condition.}
The coordinate becomes a biological readout only through a controlled translation row.  The relevant survival entry is $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ for a bounded readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA controls.  A modeled tAI or stAI surface may supply a codon-adaptation readout, but it is not itself an observed ribosome-occupancy row.  A ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact may test the translation layer only after same-organism and same-CDS rows are joined to codon counts and mRNA abundance.  The present mRNA-stability result does not by itself supply such a positive translation-survival matrix.

\paragraph{Background constraints from the $Q6$ panel.}
The larger panel gives the expected coordinate setting.  The universal core tRNA-adaptation run passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms with five folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The non-tRNA residual optimality run identified $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant residual axis under the same organism and design-column counts.  The two-axis closure run concluded that a third axis is present, and the third-axis identity run aligned $d_{\perp}$ with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The third-axis mechanism run further reported selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, with the CSC or positional identity needing outside data.  Separately, the $R$-sense tRNA-poverty run passed over $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms with $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$.  These facts support a finite translation-selection context, but none of them converts the mRNA-stability refusal into a positive RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict and boundary.}
The chapter-level readback is therefore a signed exclusion.  The fourth $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ mechanism is present in the powered surface, $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ is the dominant organism-level axis in the stored spectrum, and mRNA half-life does not identify that fourth axis under the yeast half-life gate.  No stronger biological conclusion follows locally.  In particular, this record does not establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, phenotype, causation, mechanism, or universality.  Any such claim requires its own layer-matched contact and a separately computed controlled statistic.  The only positive conclusion retained here is the finite negative identity call: in the declared assay, the fourth-axis signal is not dominantly mRNA stability.
