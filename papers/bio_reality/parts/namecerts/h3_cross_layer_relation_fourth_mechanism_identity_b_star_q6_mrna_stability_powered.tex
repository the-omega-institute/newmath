\subsection{A finite rejection of mRNA stability as the fourth $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ axis}
\label{subsec:bior-reality-q6-mrna-stability-fourth-axis}

\paragraph{Coordinate surface.}
This record concerns a finite coding-sequence surface in which synonymous codon residuals are first quotiented by amino-acid identity and then tested against organism-matched readout rows.  The local coordinate of interest is the $f_3$-stress component inside the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis: it distinguishes synonymous codon imbalance that remains after amino-acid projection, and it becomes biologically testable only when a context weight, tRNA supply, or wobble rule separates a codon $c$ from its third-position partner $c^{f_3}$.  The finite saturation check fixes the relevant codon support rather than treating the coordinate as a free biological story.  The set $M=\mathrm{Med}(R)\cup\mathrm{ACA}$ contains
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
while $\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from that list and has empty $f_3$ boundary.  Thus the finite row being tested is not a generic codon-usage covariate: it is a residual coordinate whose admissible readback is bounded by the listed codon support and by the requirement that a translation or stability row be matched on the same organismal panel.

\paragraph{Powering and surrounding axes.}
The powered cross-organism computations give the residual basis enough rank to ask whether a fourth direction is present and what it is not.  In the three-axis sufficiency run, $11$ of $16$ requested organisms were computed, with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns; the recorded conclusion was that a fourth mechanism is present.  The two-axis closure run on the same $11$ computed organisms concluded that a third axis is still present, because the perpendicular direction has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions.  The third-axis identity run identified $d_{\perp}$ with usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The corresponding mechanism run reported a selection-not-GC boundary: after GC orthogonalization, the $d_{\perp}$ direction retained significant cross-species alignment and held-out predictive power.  These rows matter locally because the mRNA-stability question is not whether a fourth direction exists at all, but whether the abundance-side fourth direction can be identified with an RNA half-life axis under the fixed gates.

\paragraph{mRNA-stability assay.}
The mRNA-stability identity test was run under the finite assay identifier \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}.  Its recorded run \texttt{377abd67b8b3} passed with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The finite conclusion is \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}: in yeast, the $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction has low alignment with the abundance residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the declared gates.  The local mathematical reading is therefore negative and specific:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alignment}(d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}},d_{\mathrm{resid4}}) \text{ is too small under the assay gates}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\mathrm{RNA\ half\!\mbox{-}\!life}\neq \mathrm{dominant\ fourth\ axis}
\end{aligned}
$$
for this finite test.  The conclusion does not erase RNA stability as a possible covariate in other panels, and it does not deny that mRNA abundance or half-life may be useful controls.  It says only that, in the recorded powered assay, the fourth abundance-side residual direction is not identified with the optimized RNA half-life direction.

\paragraph{Comparison with neighboring fourth-mechanism candidates.}
The rejection is also consistent with the neighboring finite mechanism screens.  The codon-pair powered run passed with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null samples, and its conclusion was \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}.  The translation-accuracy screen computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms, used $200$ permutations, and concluded \texttt{not\_accuracy}.  The elongation-dwell screen was organism-specific for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} and concluded \texttt{partial}.  These companion rows leave the mRNA-stability result in a precise position: the fourth direction is not explained by RNA half-life in the tested yeast stability assay, is not explained by codon-pair structure in the powered codon-pair screen, and is not explained by translation accuracy in the powered accuracy screen, while elongation dwell remains only partially aligned in its organism-specific assay.

\paragraph{tRNA and wobble boundary.}
The translation-facing side of the record remains bounded by tRNA and wobble evidence rather than promoted to a broad biological law.  The R-sense tRNA-poverty screen computed $40$ of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and passed with the call that R sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights.  The universal residual-axis run computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms and concluded that the non-tRNA residual optimality aligns most with $f_3$ translation selection.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum likewise computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms; all $11$ per-organism dominant counts were assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, with $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$ next on average, and the largest GC3 rank effect was only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  These rows justify asking whether $f_3$-stress survives against translation readouts, but they do not turn the mRNA-stability failure into a positive alternative mechanism.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The admissible readback of this chapter is the finite negative identity statement for mRNA stability.  Codon usage tables, tRNA-copy summaries, tAI or stAI weights, modeled wobble asymmetry, ribosome-profiling routes, mRNA controls, and RNA half-life rows are separate data surfaces and must remain separated unless a joined finite panel supplies the corresponding statistic.  No claim about translation realization, ribosome occupancy, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, phenotype, causal mechanism, or universality follows from this record alone.  A positive downstream statement would require a separate layer-matched contact: for example, a same-organism and same-CDS translation-efficiency row for translation survival, a direct half-life panel for RNA stability, a proteomics row for protein abundance, or an assay at the relevant structural, physical, functional, or phenotypic layer.  In the present finite row, the only supported verdict is narrower: mRNA stability is excluded as the dominant identity of the fourth $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual direction in the recorded powered assay.
