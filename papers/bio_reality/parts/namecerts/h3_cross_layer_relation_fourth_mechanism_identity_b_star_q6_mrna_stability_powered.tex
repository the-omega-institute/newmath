\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ fourth-axis assay excludes mRNA stability as the dominant identity}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourth-axis-not-mrna-stability}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and coordinate boundary.}
The record concerns a finite coding-sequence surface: synonymous codon residual coordinates are formed after amino-acid projection, then restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ direction that carries the $f_3$-stress comparison and its matched translation-layer rows.  The relevant coordinate is not a whole-transcript biological property; it is a residual direction on CDS-by-organism rows with organism, amino-acid composition, GC3, length, $M$-density, and mRNA controls kept separate from the coordinate itself.  The finite $f_3$ saturation input fixes the sequence-level boundary.  In the recorded run $76d3dc408a57$, the set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{ACA\}$ contains $20$ codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M={}&\{AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
while the $f_3$-saturated $R$ surface omits $ACA$ and $ACG$ and therefore contains $18$ codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)={}&\{AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  Thus the assay is not allowed to treat third-position topology alone as biological readout; the coordinate becomes an empirical translation-layer or stability-layer question only after a finite organism-matched readout is attached.

\paragraph{Question tested by the fourth-axis identity row.}
The finite test asks whether the residual fourth direction that remains after the established $B^*_{Q6}$ axes is principally an mRNA-stability axis.  The powered stability run is $377abd67b8b3$ with experiment id \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}.  It used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, and used $41$ synonymous design columns.  Its declared conclusion is \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}.  More explicitly, the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction has low alignment with the abundance-associated $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ direction under the fixed gates, so mRNA stability is excluded as the dominant fourth-axis identity in this assay.  The seed is recorded as \texttt{sha256:22c2c4dca83559af532a45fe2777c3c21afc8cd39d329c602491409752243209}, which makes the finite row reproducible at the level of the stored experiment identity rather than as a qualitative impression.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding powered rows.}
The exclusion is not isolated.  A separate three-axis sufficiency test, run $2436cae9d455$, computed $11$ organisms from $16$ requested organisms with $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns; it concluded \texttt{fourth\_mechanism\_present}.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum, run $0146012d8909$, used the same $11/16$ organism count, $5$ folds, $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns; its captured spectra are led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, with all $11$ per-organism dominant calls assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$.  The domain counts in that descriptive spectrum are imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms, and the largest GC3 rank effect is reported for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.  These numbers make the stability test a discriminant for a real residual direction, not a test against an absent fourth component.

\paragraph{Negative identity call.}
The finite conclusion is therefore a negative identity statement:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{FourthAxis}(B^*_{Q6}) &\not\equiv \mathrm{dominant\ mRNA\ stability\ axis}\quad\text{in the recorded assay},\\
\mathrm{evidence} &= \text{low alignment of }d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}\text{ with abundance }d_{\mathrm{resid4}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This does not say that mRNA half-life is unrelated to codon usage in every organism or condition.  It says only that, under the fixed powered gates used by this finite row, the fourth residual direction detected in the $B^*_{Q6}$ panel is not dominantly identified by the yeast half-life optimum direction.

\paragraph{Competing mechanism rows.}
Other powered rows constrain the interpretation.  The elongation-dwell assay, run $e4edcde459b7$, is restricted to \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} and returns \texttt{partial} with $5$ folds.  The codon-pair assay, run $b519b5ebd34d$, returns \texttt{not\_codon\_pair} with $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null draws.  The translation-accuracy assay, run $0dc44df59ba7$, returns \texttt{not\_accuracy} over $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested organisms, with $5$ folds, $200$ permutations, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  These rows collectively leave the fourth residual direction present but not explained by mRNA stability, codon-pair structure, or translation accuracy as dominant identities in their respective assays; elongation dwell remains only a partial positive route in yeast.

\paragraph{Placement among the lower axes.}
The third-axis identity row, run $377671f96e96$, identifies $d_{\perp}$ provisionally with usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$ across $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested organisms, using $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The third-axis mechanism row, run $d0893337f0dd$, classifies the same level as selection rather than GC, not wobble-clustered, with CSC or positional identity still needing a separate contact.  The residual optimality row, run $2a526c9fb7f4$, assigns the dominant non-tRNA residual optimality axis to \texttt{f3\_translation\_selection} over $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested organisms, again with $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  Against that background, the mRNA-stability row has a precise role: it rules out one plausible fourth-axis identity without collapsing the earlier residual axes into transcript decay.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The bounded readback for this chapter is the controlled survival or alignment entry attached to the finite mRNA-stability assay.  No stronger biological statement follows from it.  In particular, the row does not establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.  A positive claim at any of those layers would require its own organism-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  Conversely, the negative stability identity call is already meaningful at its declared layer: within the powered finite surface, the fourth residual component should not be described as an mRNA-stability mechanism unless a separate, stronger row later supplies a matched half-life or decay-factor statistic that overturns the low-alignment result under the same controls.
