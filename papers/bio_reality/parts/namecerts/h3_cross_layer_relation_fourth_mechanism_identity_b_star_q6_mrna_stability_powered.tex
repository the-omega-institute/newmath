\subsection{The mRNA-stability exclusion for the fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ mechanism}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mrna-stability-fourth-mechanism}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns the finite CDS-by-organism residual surface restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ fourth-axis search and, more narrowly, to the proposed identification of that axis with mRNA stability.  The row under audit is not a claim that codon geometry directly determines transcript half-life.  It is a discrimination test: after the already detected fourth mechanism signal is present, the question is whether the candidate direction obtained from yeast transcript half-life aligns strongly enough with the abundance residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ to name mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis identity.  The powered assay gives a negative identity call.  Its recorded conclusion is \emph{fourth-axis-not-mRNA-stability}: yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}}$ has low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates, so mRNA stability is excluded as the dominant fourth-axis identity in this assay.

\paragraph{Controlled finite row.}
The verified run for this claim is experiment $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}$ with run id $377\mathrm{abd}67\mathrm{b}8\mathrm{b}3$.  The design used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism where that threshold applied, and worked over $41$ synonymous design columns.  The deterministic seed was $\mathrm{sha256{:}22c2c4dca83559af532a45fe2777c3c21afc8cd39d329c602491409752243209}$.  The status is recorded as passed, and the finite conclusion is not a weak absence of annotation but a directional exclusion under the declared gates.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding fourth-axis search.}
The exclusion is meaningful because nearby powered rows establish that the fourth-axis problem is not empty.  The three-axis sufficiency row reports the conclusion \emph{fourth-mechanism-present} on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again using $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum also computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested; its captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, with per-organism dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$ and an imbalanced domain panel $\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$.  The same spectrum row reports the largest GC3 rank effect as $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.  Thus the mRNA-stability row is not dismissing the existence of a residual fourth direction; it is narrowing the identity of that direction by rejecting one candidate mechanism.

This finite asymmetry keeps the $f_3$ stress coordinate available as a residual codon-side distinction after amino-acid projection, but it does not by itself assign a downstream biological identity.  The mRNA-stability assay is therefore interpreted as an identity test on a candidate fourth mechanism, not as a direct reading of codon topology into transcript degradation.

\paragraph{Comparison with alternative exclusions.}
Other fourth-mechanism rows help locate the result.  The codon-pair powered row concluded \emph{not-codon-pair} with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $50$ shuffle nulls.  The translation-accuracy powered row concluded \emph{not-accuracy} on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, with $200$ permutations, $5$ folds, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The elongation-dwell powered row was weaker and organism-specific: in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} it concluded \emph{partial} under $5$ folds.  The pattern is therefore asymmetric: mRNA stability, codon pairs, and translation accuracy are not the dominant identity under their respective gates, while elongation dwell remains only partially implicated.

\paragraph{Third-axis context.}
The third-axis rows keep this fourth-axis exclusion from being overread.  The $d_{\perp}$ identity row provisionally aligned $d_{\perp}$ to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$, over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The corresponding mechanism row concluded selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, with CSC or positional identity still needing an outside contact; its reason was that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ kept significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.  These facts place the mRNA-stability exclusion downstream of a structured residual-axis program rather than in isolation.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The chapter makes only the bounded finite-row claim.  The passed mRNA-stability assay excludes mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis identity under the recorded gates; it does not establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universality.  Any such promotion would require its own matched measurement contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, tAI or stAI weights remain modelled codon-adaptation readbacks unless paired with measured ribosome or translation-efficiency rows, and matched mRNA abundance remains a control for translation-efficiency residualization rather than a translation output.  A positive fourth-axis existence row and a negative mRNA-stability identity row can both be true: together they say that the residual fourth direction survives the search but is not named by transcript half-life in the finite powered assay recorded here.
