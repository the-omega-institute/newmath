\subsection{The fourth residual axis is not mRNA stability in the powered yeast assay}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-mrna-stability-fourth-axis}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This record concerns the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface after amino-acid projection, restricted to the finite codon-coordinate panel in which third-position $f_3$ pairing can be compared against organism-scoped translation and transcript readouts. The saturation row fixes the underlying finite codon support. The set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_3$-saturated $R$ surface removes the two boundary codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and leaves the 18-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite boundary residue for this $f_3$ saturation is empty. Thus the fourth-axis question is not posed on an open-ended codon vocabulary: it is posed on a closed residual coordinate surface with an explicitly recorded codon list.

\paragraph{Powered finite rows.}
The powered axis-sufficiency assay computed 11 organisms out of 16 requested organisms, using 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous-design columns. Its conclusion was that a fourth mechanism is present beyond the three-axis closure. The per-organism axis-strength spectrum used the same 11-of-16 powered organism panel, again with 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous-design columns. In that spectrum, $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ led on average, and it was the dominant axis in all 11 computed organisms. The largest recorded GC3 rank effect was the $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ effect with $\rho=0.136$, so the observed fourth residual axis is not being summarized as a mere GC3 rank artifact in this finite table.

\paragraph{Local comparison against mRNA stability.}
The mRNA-stability identity test used experiment run $377\mathrm{abd}67\mathrm{b}8\mathrm{b}3$ with the fixed experiment id $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}$. The row passed its computational gate with 5 folds, a minimum of 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous-design columns. The finite conclusion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{fourth\ axis}\neq \mathrm{mRNA\ stability}
\end{aligned}
$$
for this assay. More precisely, the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction had low alignment with the abundance-linked $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ direction under the fixed gates. The recorded interpretation is therefore exclusionary rather than promotional: mRNA half-life does not identify the dominant fourth-axis direction in this assay.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring mechanism tests.}
The same finite evidence cluster distinguishes the mRNA-stability refusal from adjacent mechanism rows. The elongation-dwell row passed with conclusion $\mathrm{partial}$ in $\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}$, using 5 folds. The codon-pair row passed but concluded $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$, with 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, 41 synonymous-design columns, and 50 shuffle-null draws. The translation-accuracy row passed but concluded $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$, using 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested organisms, 5 folds, 41 synonymous-design columns, and 200 permutations. These rows leave the fourth-axis identity unresolved at higher biological layers, but they make the local mRNA-stability reading too strong: the finite powered assay does not support mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis identity.

\paragraph{Translation and wobble context.}
The residual surface sits inside a broader translation-readout boundary. The universal-core row passed on 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested organisms, with 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous-design columns. The non-tRNA residual optimality row identified $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant residual axis under the same organism count, fold count, protein threshold, and design-column count. The two-axis closure row concluded that a third axis is present, with the reason that $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms. The third-axis identity row aligned $d_{\perp}$ to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The third-axis mechanism row concluded selection rather than GC, not wobble-clustered, while leaving the CSC or positional identity as requiring outside evidence. Separately, the $R$-sense tRNA-poverty row computed 40 organisms out of 46 requested, skipped 6 organisms, used 1000 permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and supported a tRNA-supply mechanism for $R$ avoidance under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. These rows justify keeping wobble-weight asymmetry and translation-readout survival in view, but they do not rescue the mRNA-stability identity claim.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The bounded readout for the present claim is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ for tAI, stAI, or measured ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency rows. A positive biological statement about translation must first be supported by a non-vacuous, non-rank-degenerate, controlled translation row. A three-organism all-$1.0$ matrix is a rank boundary and is not survival evidence. Likewise, modeled tAI or stAI weights can define a bounded codon-adaptation readout, but they are not observed ribosome footprints. Matched mRNA abundance is a covariate for separating transcript abundance from translation efficiency, not a translation output by itself.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter-level conclusion is therefore negative and finite: within the powered yeast mRNA half-life assay, the fourth $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual axis is not identified with mRNA stability. This does not imply that the fourth axis is a universal translation law, a protein-abundance law, a folding law, a physical-admissibility law, a function law, or a phenotype law. No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, downstream mechanism, organismal phenotype, or universality follows from this row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the appropriate measured rows and the corresponding controlled statistic. The present result only removes mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis identity under the finite gates stated above.
