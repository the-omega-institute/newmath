\subsection{The Fourth Q6 Axis Is Not mRNA Stability in the Powered Half-Life Assay}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-fourth-axis-not-mrna-stability}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record tested here is the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface after amino-acid projection, restricted to finite CDS-by-organism rows and then compared with a yeast RNA half-life direction.  The relevant coordinate is the fourth residual abundance direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, which appears after the core tRNA and $f_3$-translation-selection directions fail to span the powered panel.  The finite design used $41$ synonymous design columns, $5$ folds, and a minimum organism inclusion threshold of $500$ proteins per organism.  In the axis-strength spectrum run, $11$ organisms were computed out of $16$ requested organisms, and the per-organism dominant-axis count was concentrated on $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ with count $11$.  The same spectrum was reported as descriptive across domains because the domain sample was imbalanced, with $1$ archaeon, $2$ bacteria, and $8$ eukaryotes; the largest GC3 rank effect in that summary was the $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ effect with $\rho=0.136$.

\paragraph{Codon boundary inherited by the test.}
The $f_3$ saturation contact fixes the finite codon support on which the residual coordinates are allowed to speak.  The set $M=\mathrm{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains
$$\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
After $f_3$ saturation of $R$, the remaining list is the same support with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ absent:
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The reported $f_3$ boundary list is empty.  Thus the half-life question is not being asked on an unconstrained codon universe; it is asked on the residual coordinate surface already filtered by the $f_3$ codon boundary and by amino-acid quotienting.

\paragraph{Powered fourth-axis question.}
The powered three-axis sufficiency run computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms and returned the call \emph{fourth mechanism present}.  That result says that the tRNA, $f_3$-translation-selection, and third residual directions do not fully account for the held-out residual signal.  The residual-axis run identified the non-tRNA residual optimum as $f_3$ translation selection on the same $11$ of $16$ organism panel, again with $41$ synonymous design columns and $5$ folds.  The two-axis closure run also returned \emph{third axis present}, with the reported reason that $d_{\perp}$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms.  The third-axis identity run then gave a provisional usage-frequency-within-family reading for $d_{\perp}$, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  These surrounding rows are not evidence for mRNA stability; they only locate the mRNA-stability assay inside a finite sequence of already separated Q6 residual axes.

\paragraph{Half-life result.}
The mRNA-stability identity test used experiment \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_mrna\_stability\_powered}, run \texttt{377abd67b8b3}, with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and the same $500$-protein minimum threshold.  Its finite conclusion was \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}.  The stated reason is that the yeast half-life optimum $d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}}$ had low alignment with the abundance fourth residual direction $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  In the notation of this chapter, the assayed identity was
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Identity}(d_{\mathrm{resid4}})&=\mathrm{RNA\ half\ life}\quad\text{tested by alignment with }d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}},\\
\mathrm{Result}&=\mathrm{rejected\ as\ the\ dominant\ fourth\ axis\ identity\ in\ this\ assay}.
\end{aligned}$$
This is a negative mechanism-identity readout, not a negative statement about the existence of the fourth axis itself.  The fourth axis remains present in the powered residual panel, while the yeast half-life direction fails the fixed-gate identity test for being its dominant explanation.

\paragraph{Comparison with other fourth-mechanism screens.}
The same finite family contains additional exclusion or partial rows.  The codon-pair powered screen used $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, a $500$-protein minimum, $50$ shuffle-null replicates, and concluded \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}.  The translation-accuracy powered screen computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, used $200$ permutations, $5$ folds, and $41$ synonymous design columns, and concluded \texttt{not\_accuracy}.  The elongation-dwell screen in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} returned \texttt{partial}.  The mRNA-stability screen therefore belongs to a discriminating family of fourth-mechanism probes: codon-pair and accuracy are rejected in their respective powered tests, elongation dwell is only partial in yeast, and mRNA half-life is rejected as the dominant identity for $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ in the fixed half-life assay.

\paragraph{Local readback boundary.}
The conclusion is bounded by the row actually computed.  It permits the sentence: in the powered Q6 residual panel, yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt,halflife}}$ does not align strongly enough with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ to identify mRNA stability as the dominant fourth-axis mechanism under the declared gates.  It does not permit a stronger biological promotion.  No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and statistic.  In particular, matched mRNA abundance is a control surface for translation-efficiency residualization, not by itself a translation or protein-output measurement; RNA half-life is a stability-layer readout, not by itself evidence for folded structure, physical feasibility, biological function, or universality.

\paragraph{Falsification and retention.}
The retained statement is narrow.  The fourth residual axis is retained as a powered residual object because the three-axis sufficiency run reported a missing fourth mechanism, and the spectrum run found $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ dominant across the $11$ computed organisms.  The RNA-stability identity is rejected for this assay because the yeast half-life optimum did not align with $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed design.  A future same-CDS panel could reopen an RNA-stability question only by joining Q6 residual labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, rank checks, and simpler-baseline controls in one finite table and then showing a positive controlled statistic.  Until such a row exists, the present chapter records a powered exclusion: the fourth Q6 residual axis is not identified with mRNA stability by the verified yeast half-life screen.
