\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns the $f3\_stress$ coordinate inside the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface.  The finite rows are the joined Saccharomyces cerevisiae CDS units for which codon-count residuals, PAXdb protein abundance, and local ribosome-occupancy dwell summaries are all present.  The active dwell experiment uses $n=1257$ joined genes, with the base design trained from a larger available yeast context of $1800$ rows.  The target vector is not total abundance itself: it is the component $P_{f3}$ obtained by projecting $Z$-residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance onto the $Z$-residualized $f3\_stress$ coordinate on the same join.  The control basis has rank $24$ and includes length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline used across the sibling $B^*_{Q6}$ assays. \paragraph{Dwell readout and statistic.}
The dwell readout is a per-gene summary of local relative ribosome occupancy, computed from file-provided $5'$ and $3'$ A-site occupancy windows.  The fitted comparison keeps the fold assignment fixed across base and full designs, with five folds.  The base model has held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, the dwell-only readout has $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, and the full base-plus-dwell model has held-out $R^2=0.20412261520502883$.  Thus the finite increment attributed to the dwell block is
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}} &= 0.20412261520502883 - 0.1855578757644517 \\
&= 0.01856473944057712.
\end{aligned}$$
The direct correlation between $f3\_stress$ and the dwell readout is $0.1615441450901653$, with approximate right-tail probability $7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  The rank of the tested $f3$ source is $1$, so the statistic is a single-coordinate dwell-mediated abundance-component test rather than a broad multicoordinate compression claim.

\paragraph{Permutation boundary.}
The null comparison is a deterministic row-label permutation of $P_{f3}$, with the base design, full design, folds, and dwell rows held fixed.  The experiment uses $120$ permutations and the deterministic seed \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_f3\_stress\_dwell\_mediation\_powered:deterministic}.  The observed increment exceeds the fixed null envelope: the null mean is $-0.0032911642620130563$, the null minimum is $-0.013799622026666476$, the null maximum is $0.005001350039772926$, and the null $95\%$ cutoff is $0.0013115362189692937$.  Equivalently,
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{obs}} &= 0.01856473944057712,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null},95} &= 0.0013115362189692937,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{obs}}-\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null},95} &= 0.017253203221607826.
\end{aligned}$$
The finite verdict of this chapter is therefore positive for the stated statistical object: the $f3\_stress$ abundance component in yeast is better predicted on held-out rows when the local ribosome-dwell readout is added to the base design, and the improvement is well above the fixed row-label null boundary.

\paragraph{Compression and irreducible remainder.}
The partial mediation certificate gives a useful scale for the present dwell result. The dwell result should be read against that scale: it supplies a positive, finite, row-tested increment for the $f3\_stress$ abundance component, not a complete reduction of the full $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface.

\paragraph{Local claim and exclusions.}
The chapter's admissible claim is exactly statistical and finite-row: in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the $f3\_stress$ component of residualized protein abundance has a positive held-out dwell increment, $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, exceeding a fixed row-label permutation cutoff of $0.0013115362189692937$ on $1257$ joined genes.  This permits a dwell-mediated abundance-component readback for the measured yeast panel.  It does not, by itself, establish a causal translation pathway, ribosome-speed mechanism, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, or universal biological rule.  Any such stronger interpretation would require an additional layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic that directly measures the proposed layer under its own controls.
