\subsection{Stress-Dwell Mediation of the Yeast $f_3$ Abundance Component}\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-mediation}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.} This record concerns the $f_3$ coordinate inside the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel, evaluated on a Saccharomyces cerevisiae join where codon-derived residual coordinates, local ribosome-occupancy dwell summaries, and measured protein abundance are present on the same gene rows. The tested response is not the full protein-abundance vector. It is the one-dimensional abundance component
$$\begin{aligned}
P_{f_3} &= \operatorname{proj}_{\widetilde f_3}\bigl(\widetilde{\log_{10} P}\bigr),
\end{aligned}$$
where both $\widetilde f_3$ and $\widetilde{\log_{10} P}$ have been residualized against the common control basis $Z$. The active control basis has rank $24$, the $f_3$ source rank is $1$, and the active base-plus-dwell join has $n=1257$ rows. The declared dwell readout is a per-gene local relative ribosome-occupancy summary computed from file-provided $5'$ and $3'$ A-site occupancy windows. Thus the observed surface is a finite matrix relation among a codon residual coordinate, a position-local ribosome dwell readout, and the $f_3$-projected protein-abundance component.

\paragraph{Held-out mediation statistic.} The base design uses $1800$ available rows before the active dwell join and is evaluated with $5$ fixed folds. On the active rows the base model explains $R^2=0.1855578757644517$ for $P_{f_3}$, while the base-plus-dwell model explains $R^2=0.20412261520502883$. The held-out gain is therefore
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}} &= 0.20412261520502883 - 0.1855578757644517 \\
&= 0.01856473944057712.
\end{aligned}$$
The dwell readout alone has $R^2=0.034336580953121976$. The direct finite-row association between $f_3$ and dwell is positive, with correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. A deterministic row-label permutation of $P_{f_3}$, holding the base and full designs and the folds fixed, gives $120$ null draws with mean $-0.0032911642620130563$, minimum $-0.013799622026666476$, maximum $0.005001350039772926$, and $95\%$ cutoff $0.0013115362189692937$. The observed gain exceeds both zero and the permutation cutoff by a large margin:
$$\begin{aligned}
0.01856473944057712 &> 0.0013115362189692937.
\end{aligned}$$
The finite-row verdict is therefore positive for a dwell increment on the $f_3$ abundance component in this yeast surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein-abundance and translation-readout panels.} The same nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ basis contains $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$. In the direct protein-abundance scan, yeast has a protein-abundance join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, $5815$ joined CDS rows, rank $\widetilde X=9$, residual degrees of freedom $5792$, and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving the positive threshold. The measured ribosome-TE panel for yeast has $5128$ genes with TE, join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, residual degrees of freedom $5105$, and $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$. In the measured TE-to-protein decomposition, yeast has $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, and measured mediation fraction $0.4793069656345167$. The dwell result in this section is narrower than that whole-panel mediation number: it isolates the $f_3$-projected abundance component and asks whether local ribosome-occupancy dwell adds held-out explanatory power for that component.

\paragraph{Positional and reduction context.} The paired positional dwell run uses the same $P_{f_3}$ norm squared, $44.20095750389323$, and the same fraction of the abundance target, $0.1999063916718794$. It reports $5'$ as the leading segment, with the $5'$ ramp passing and the $3'$ body not passing. In the separate structural-order ramp screen, the primary $50$-codon ramp window has weighted $f_3$ ramp held-out gain $0.0011165640103326878$ and weighted body gain $-0.00026157313923957926$, with conclusion $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$. These two finite surfaces agree on a restricted statement: $f_3$ has a detectable local-ribosome-position signature, strongest near the early coding region in the tested yeast readouts, but the evidence does not promote to a structural-order localization claim. The broader readout-dictionary certificate also keeps the residual open: with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n=1800$, and $120$ permutations, the best selection-aware coverage is $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. The irreducibility certificate uses the same $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $5$ folds, and $120$ permutations; its status semantics say that the registered source signal is real and at least one listed reduction family refutes the original irreducibility hypothesis, not that the coordinate is globally primitive.

\paragraph{Admissible reading.} The admissible claim is finite and local. In the yeast rows tested here, the $f_3$-projected protein-abundance component is better predicted when the base design is supplemented by local ribosome-occupancy dwell summaries, and the observed held-out gain exceeds a fixed row-label permutation cutoff. This supports a measured dwell-mediated abundance component for $f_3$ in the stated Saccharomyces cerevisiae matrix. It does not by itself establish a causal translation mechanism, a universal codon rule, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or evolutionary direction. Those stronger claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics. Even within translation-layer language, the word dwell here means the file-provided local relative ribosome-occupancy windows on the joined yeast rows; it is not a general statement about elongation kinetics, initiation control, collision dynamics, or perturbational rescue.
