\subsection{F3-stress dwell mediation for measured yeast abundance}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-mediation}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $f3\_stress$ coordinate in the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis has a measurable dwell-associated component in a matched yeast protein-abundance readout.  The active organism is \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The target is not total abundance itself, but the one-dimensional projection $P_{f3}$ of $Z$-residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance onto the $Z$-residualized $f3\_stress$ coordinate on the active base-plus-dwell join.  The control basis has rank $24$, and the coordinate rank for this test is $1$.  The finite joined table contains $1257$ usable rows inside a base design budget of $1800$ rows.  The dwell variables are per-gene local relative ribosome-occupancy summaries computed from file-provided $5'$ and $3'$ A-site occupancy windows.

\paragraph{Controlled regression readback.}
The held-out regression comparison gives the following finite list of explanatory values:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{\mathrm{base}} &= 0.1855578757644517,\\
R^{2}_{\mathrm{dwell\ only}} &= 0.034336580953121976,\\
R^{2}_{\mathrm{base+dwell}} &= 0.20412261520502883,\\
\Delta R^{2}_{\mathrm{held\ out}} &= 0.01856473944057712.
\end{aligned}
$$
The tested component has squared norm $\|P_{f3}\|^{2}=44.20095750389323$ and accounts for fraction $0.1999063916718794$ of the controlled target surface.  The direct correlation between the residual coordinate and the dwell readout is positive, with $r=0.1615441450901653$ and approximate probability $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  The comparison was evaluated over $5$ folds.

\paragraph{Permutation boundary.}
The null model is a deterministic row-label permutation of $P_{f3}$ with the base design, the full design, and the fold allocation held fixed.  Across $120$ permutations, the null distribution for the held-out increment has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\mathrm{null}} &= -0.0032911642620130563,\\
\min_{\mathrm{null}} &= -0.013799622026666476,\\
\max_{\mathrm{null}} &= 0.005001350039772926,\\
Q_{0.95,\mathrm{null}} &= 0.0013115362189692937.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed increment $0.01856473944057712$ is positive and exceeds the fixed $95\%$ permutation boundary by more than an order of magnitude.  The registered status of the finite test is passed: the $f3\_stress$ abundance component has dwell-increment evidence in this yeast panel.

\paragraph{Relation to surrounding finite rows.}
The same yeast abundance contact supplies a broader protein-omics context.  In the direct $B^{*}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance surface, yeast has a protein join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, with $5815$ joined coding-sequence rows and all $9$ residual coordinates surviving the direct $S^{QP}$ screen.  The direct controlled abundance score in that surface is $R^{2}_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$.  In the measured ribosome-TE surface, yeast has join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, $5128$ genes with TE, and $R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, again with all $9$ residual coordinates surviving the measured translation-efficiency screen.  These surrounding rows explain why the dwell test is biologically addressed to a translation-near readout, but they do not by themselves prove the dwell result; the dwell statement rests on the separate $1257$-row positional occupancy join and its fixed permutation comparison.

\paragraph{Positional refinement.}
A companion positional test on the same organism and target separates the dwell signal into $5'$ and $3'$ windows.  It uses the same $P_{f3}$ norm $44.20095750389323$, the same target fraction $0.1999063916718794$, the same base budget $1800$, $5$ folds, and $120$ permutations.  The leading segment by held-out increment is the $5'$ window.  Its finite classification is $5'$ ramp passed and $3'$ body not passed.  A structural-order ramp comparison using a primary ramp length of $50$ gives weighted held-out increments
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^{2}_{f3,\,5'\ \mathrm{ramp}} &= 0.0011165640103326878,\\
\Delta R^{2}_{f3,\,\mathrm{body}} &= -0.00026157313923957926,\\
\Delta R^{2}_{\mathrm{ramp-body}} &= 0.001378137149572267.
\end{aligned}
$$
The ramp sign-flip probability is $0.0478515625$, while the paired ramp-minus-body probability is $0.05859375$.  The finite conclusion is therefore more modest than a localization theorem: the ramp-side signal is positive, but the paired localization gate does not fully pass.

\paragraph{Dictionary and irreducibility context.}
The partial readout-dictionary certificate on the unified yeast context gives a separate finite constraint on over-reading the dwell result.  With $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, join budget $n=1800$, $5$ folds, and $120$ permutations, the best selection-aware coverage by at most three measured readouts is $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The selection-aware null boundary is $0.029149007242956115$, while the naive fixed-subset null boundary is $0.00598374993486675$.  This says that measured readouts carry a real but partial component of the yeast residual abundance signal.  It does not collapse the whole $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface into dwell, TE, stability, or turnover.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
On the finite yeast panel, the admissible claim is the following: after the stated residualization and fixed held-out comparison, local ribosome-occupancy dwell summaries add predictive information for the $f3\_stress$-aligned component of protein abundance, with $\Delta R^{2}_{\mathrm{held\ out}}=0.01856473944057712$ above the permutation boundary $0.0013115362189692937$.  This is a cross-layer statistical mediation-style readback for a measured dwell surface, not a causal pathway.  It does not license a claim of translation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  Those stronger statements would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled finite-row statistics.
