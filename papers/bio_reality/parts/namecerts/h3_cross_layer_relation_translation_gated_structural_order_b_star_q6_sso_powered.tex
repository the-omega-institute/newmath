\paragraph{Gate interpretation.}
The intended biological chain has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow SO,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is one of the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, $T_j$ is a translation-layer readout, and $SO$ is a structural-order readback.  The present finite row set supplies the $q_i$ surface and a predicted-confidence $SO$ readback, but it must not be read as proving the full chain unless the same coordinate first survives a translation-readout test under the declared controls and simpler-baseline ablation.  Translation efficiency, tAI or stAI, ribosome occupancy, and protein abundance are distinct contacts; none is interchangeable with AlphaFold mean pLDDT.  Conversely, AlphaFold mean pLDDT is not a ribosome measurement, not a translation-efficiency measurement, and not a protein-abundance measurement.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The finite positive $R^2_{SO}$ values report a controlled association between $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ codon-usage residuals and predicted structure confidence.  They do not establish folded experimental structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, localization, folding kinetics, pathway activity, causality, mechanism, or a universal biological law.  They also do not establish translation mediation by themselves.  A translation claim requires an organism-matched translation contact such as measured translation efficiency, ribosome profiling, or an explicitly bounded adaptation-weight readout, with the same amino-acid-composition, GC3, length, and $M$-density controls.  A folding or physical-admissibility claim requires a separate structure or physical assay contact.  A function or phenotype claim requires a separate function or phenotype contact.  Without those layer-specific contacts, the present chapter remains a finite predicted-confidence association and a boundary condition for later gated testing.
