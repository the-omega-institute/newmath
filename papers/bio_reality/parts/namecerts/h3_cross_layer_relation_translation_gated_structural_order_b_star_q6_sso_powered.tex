\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record fixes a finite cross-layer test surface rather than a general biological law.  The coordinate side contains the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Each row is interpreted as a codon-usage residual coordinate attached to an encoded protein, not as a direct physical measurement.  The intended gate is sequential: a coordinate must first survive a translation-readout test under the declared controls, and only after that may a structural-order readback be examined for the same coordinate while conditioning on translation and abundance covariates.  In the present finite run the attached structural readback is AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the interval $0$ to $100$, used only as predicted structure-confidence signal.  It is not an experimental folded-structure assay.

The nuisance surface $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same CDS codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. These numbers establish a bounded row-wise relation between codon-usage residual coordinates and AlphaFold mean pLDDT after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  They do not by themselves show why the relation exists.

\paragraph{Gate interpretation.}
The name of the chapter records the stricter intended route: $q_i \to T_j \to SO$, where $q_i$ is one of the nine residual coordinates, $T_j$ is a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, and $SO$ is a structural-order readback.  The finite run reported here supplies a predicted-confidence surface and a controlled $q_i \to SO$ association, but it must not be read as a completed measured translation chain.  tAI and stAI are modelled codon-adaptation annotations; ribosome profiling and translation-efficiency rows require organism-matched read counts and mRNA controls; protein abundance requires a separate proteomics table.  Therefore a positive $R^2_{SO}$ entry is not a translation-mediated mechanism unless the same coordinate first passes a same-row translation test and the structural readback remains interpretable after conditioning on that translation readout and protein-abundance baseline.
