\subsection{Window-Six Codon Boundary Tile}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and label contact.}
The record works over the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of codon positions together with a curated standard-code label table.  The finite row under audit isolates a local boundary set $R$ of cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the observed set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is not presented as a mechanism for translation.  It is a coordinate-and-label observation: the cube supplies adjacency and local tile geometry, while the curated table supplies amino-acid or stop labels.  The biological contact therefore tests code-layer readback and local label splitting only.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, cellular function, a universal biological mechanism, or any necessity law for DNA.

\paragraph{Punctured-cube geometry.}
The same finite row verifies that the selected $13$ codons form the recorded punctured-cube tile.  Its face-count summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the carrier is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has a local two-dimensional boundary profile inside the six-bit codon display.  The point of the construction is the coexistence of a singleton-corner behavior and a three-point label-class behavior within one local tile.  The row is therefore a finite code-read phenomenon: it explains where the boundary pattern is seen in $Q_6$ and which standard-code labels are contacted, without promoting the pattern beyond the code table.

\paragraph{Spectral and randomization checks.}
The spectral probe gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the radial polynomial check, so the numerical relation is satisfied to the stated tolerance.  Against the sampled shape null with $N=2000$, one exact shape match was observed after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts with $55377$ duplicate retries.  The corresponding probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null with the same $N=2000$ gave no exact shape matches, an observed $M$-size of $20$, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below that size.  Its reported values are $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These numbers support that the tile is statistically sharp relative to the tested finite nulls, but they still remain code-layer evidence because the nulls compare coordinate-label arrangements rather than biochemical realization.

\paragraph{Leave-out probes.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the value with the boundary included was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row reported $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These checks say that the local tile is robust under the tested omissions.  They do not say that an omitted codon is biologically generated by the geometry; they say that the coordinate-and-label pattern recovers the held-out code-layer placement in the audited finite experiment.

\paragraph{Family and module resolution.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out summary split the evidence into $5$ families.  The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The complete assignment count is $13$, matching $|R|=13$.  This module table is the most concrete readback form of the claim: it gives a finite decomposition of the local boundary tile into codon groups that can be checked against standard-code labels.  The decomposition is useful precisely because it is narrow.  It marks a stable code-read boundary and keeps separate any claim about translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality until an independent contact for that higher layer is supplied.
