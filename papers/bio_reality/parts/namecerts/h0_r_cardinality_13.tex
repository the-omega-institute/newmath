\subsection{Window-Six Codon Boundary Packet}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite display.}
The packet uses the six-bit codon cube as its finite coordinate display and reads the standard-code table only at the codon-label layer.  The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the verified row, the full membership of $R$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is not a biological mechanism.  It is a finite readout pattern inside the codon coordinate display, checked against a curated standard-code label table.  The contact records codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop assignments, and therefore it can test code-layer label readback and local tile splitting.  It does not observe translation in a cell, folding, biochemical admissibility, function, causal mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Punctured-cube geometry.}
The same verified row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube-shaped local tile with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the thirteen selected codons carry not only a row count but also a finite incidence profile: $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ square faces in the induced six-bit geometry.  The claim made here is exactly local: the codon display exposes a boundary-shaped subset whose labels can be read from the standard code.  Nothing in this finite incidence statement says that genomes must instantiate the pattern as a law, that a ribosome uses this geometry, or that the pattern determines any protein-level object.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral measurement separates the selected boundary set from the larger local mass $M$ numerically.  The supplied row gives $\lambda_M = 0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R = 0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu = 3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check is numerically closed at the reported tolerance, with polynomial value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$.  The available finite row reports the size of $M$ as $20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null experiment, but it does not provide the full codon membership list of $M$.  Consequently this chapter treats $M$ through its reported size, spectral scalar, and null-model behavior rather than asserting an unseen membership table.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Against $N=2000$ random samples in the shape-significance run, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The same run found $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler attempted $57377$ draws and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, using seed $42$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact shape match count was $0$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$.  The corresponding values are $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These numbers support the statement that the observed local geometry is rare under the supplied finite null procedures.  They do not by themselves promote the geometry to an explanation of translation, structure, or function.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the reported hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  Inside $M-T$ the hit rate was $0.9365079365079365$, and inside $M-T$ plus the boundary it returned to $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level refinement used $5$ families: alternative yeast had hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each had hit rate $1.0$.  These rows show that the local coordinate split has predictive strength for code-table labels under the tested leave-one procedures.  The tested target remains a label in a code table, not a translated molecule or folded structure.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon modules.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $anchor\_count=0$, $boundary\_count=5$, $closure\_coverage\_high=8$, $predictable\_count=8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification is complete for the thirteen codons of $R$: $agr=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  The bookkeeping row gives $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, and empty lists for extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons.  The local partition therefore accounts for every member of $R$ in this finite packet.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table.  It supplies codon labels and stop labels at codon-label resolution.  A successful local tile check therefore licenses the following narrow conclusion: in the six-bit codon coordinate display, the thirteen-codon set $R$ forms a verified local boundary pattern whose label behavior is testable against the standard code.  It does not license any statement that the geometry is the mechanism of translation, that amino-acid labels determine folded protein structure, that the pattern is physically admissible as a biochemical process, that it realizes biological function, or that it is a universal biological law.  Any such stronger conclusion requires a separate contact at the relevant layer, for example a translation experiment, a structural assay, a physical model with admissibility data, or a function-level measurement.
