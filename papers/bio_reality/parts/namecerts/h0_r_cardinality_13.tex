\subsection{Window-six codon tile with a thirteen-codon boundary set}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table after it is displayed as a six-bit codon cube. The carrier is the finite cube $Q_6$ of codon coordinates, while the observed labels are supplied by a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The finite boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the claim is not merely that some exceptional codons exist: the row fixes the complete list and the count simultaneously. The same set is reported as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local tile has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the induced finite geometry. This is the coordinate content used here: a window-six codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes, all read against the ordinary code-label table rather than against a translation or structure experiment.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The same finite packet records a spectral split between the larger module $M$ and the boundary set $R$. The verified numerical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check has residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also listed as the cubic-polynomial residual, which places the numerical witness within ordinary floating-point tolerance rather than presenting it as an exact symbolic law. The shape-significance row used $N=2000$ sampled null trials with seed $42$. Out of $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ retries were duplicates, one exact shape match was observed, and $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$. The reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
 p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null row, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $|M|=20$, zero exact matches, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed module size. Its probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the narrower statement that the displayed local tile is atypical under the recorded finite null procedures. They do not convert the tile into a biochemical necessity.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
The predictive checks stay at the code-label layer. In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. In the leave-one-codon-out row, there were no anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$. These row values say that the finite coordinate closure recovers many code-label positions under the stated leave-out probes. They do not say that a ribosome realizes this closure, that a folded protein follows from it, or that the physical admissibility of any molecule has been observed.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The six named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row gives $R$-size $13$ and assigned count $13$, with empty lists for extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons. A family-level leave-one-code-out check reports $5$ families. The hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families. This is a finite accounting of label-module behavior across the named family grouping, not a claim of organism-wide mechanism.

\paragraph{Local biological reading.}
The biological contact is the curated standard-code table. It supplies codon-to-label readback and allows the local split to be checked against amino-acid and stop labels. The contact does not observe translation dynamics, ribosomal kinetics, folded structure, thermodynamic stability, cellular function, or universal lawhood. Consequently the ready conclusion is deliberately local: the standard-code table contains a thirteen-codon boundary tile in the six-bit codon display, with the verified list $R$, face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, significant finite-null behavior, and complete module assignment. Any promotion beyond code-label readback requires a separate empirical contact at the higher layer. In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded proteins, CUN/UUR or tRNA-Leu wording is not structural evidence by itself, and no statement about function, admissibility, mechanism, or universality follows from this row alone.
