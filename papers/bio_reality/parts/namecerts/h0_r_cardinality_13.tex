\subsection{Window-six codon boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate packet.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is treated as a coordinate in a local window rather than as a biochemical event. The row under audit isolates a boundary set $R$ of cardinality $13$. The verified members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the concrete row records $|R|=13$ and does not merely assert a qualitative codon pattern. The associated punctured-cube computation gives the same $13$ codons and the face-count vector $(13,16,4)$, so the packet has a finite local tile shape rather than an unconstrained label list. This is the exact scope of the coordinate claim: a six-bit display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class are enough to describe the observed boundary tile at the codon-label level.

\paragraph{Spectral and closure numbers.}
The spectral row supplies numerical invariants for the same finite object. The reported values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, with the same value recorded for the cubic polynomial residual. The spectral radius row therefore does not add a biological mechanism; it gives a reproducible finite-coordinate signature for the local code table split. A separate shape-significance row used $N=2000$ random trials with seed $42$, requiring $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. These values support the claim that the observed tile is not a common output of that finite null experiment, while leaving the interpretation restricted to code labels.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null model supplies a second finite comparison. With $N=2000$ and seed $42$, it recorded observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$. The corresponding values are $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$. The data therefore separate the observed code-read tile from the sampled class-preserving alternatives under the stated generator. The input row does not provide the individual codons of $M$, so this chapter uses only the reported size and scalar null statistics for $M$. That limitation matters: the row supports a finite shape comparison, not an independent reconstruction of all amino-acid biology.

\paragraph{Readback contact.}
The only reality contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for checking the local split against the standard-code label table. It is not an observation of ribosomal translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, cellular function, or a universal mechanism. The experiment is therefore a code-layer audit: the finite coordinate packet is allowed to compare codon labels, but it is not allowed to promote a local tile into a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level row divides the evidence into $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$. These numbers show that the local boundary packet has strong code-label recovery behavior across the tested table families, but the row still measures label recovery rather than biochemical realization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out and module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons of $R$: $agr=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$. The completeness row records $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing module members, and no unassigned codons. This is a closed finite accounting of the boundary set: every member of $R$ has a module-level placement, and every module member is either predictable or boundary-supported in the recorded classification.

\paragraph{Local verdict and blocked promotions.}
The finite data justify the following scoped reading: window-six codon coordinates expose a nontrivial local boundary tile in the standard-code label table, with $|R|=13$, punctured-cube vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, significant shape rows under the stated finite nulls, and complete module assignment for the $13$ boundary codons. The conclusion stops there. No translation event, folded structure, physical admissibility claim, biological function, mechanism, or universality statement follows from this packet unless an additional contact of the appropriate kind is supplied. Amino-acid label sequences are readback labels in this chapter, not protein structures. Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, or code-family recovery rates do not turn the codon tile into evidence about folding or function. The packet is therefore ready as a finite code-read record with an explicit higher-layer boundary.
