\subsection{Window-Six Boundary Tile for the Standard Codon Table}
\label{subsec:h0-r-cardinality-13-window-six-boundary-tile}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
The claim records a window-six codon calculation on the codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is represented by a six-bit display and then read against the curated standard-code label table. The distinguished boundary set $R$ has exactly thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit reports $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen codons appearing in the expected-codon check. This is the narrow claim: a particular code-layer coordinate cut isolates a local boundary tile inside $Q_6$. The row does not assert that the tile is a law of biology, only that this finite set is recovered from the specified codon-coordinate display and can be compared with the curated standard-code labels.

\paragraph{Punctured-cube shape.}
The same $R$ is reported as a punctured-cube object with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local geometry is not merely a count of labels; it also carries a finite face structure with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-dimensional faces. The evidence is therefore combinatorial before it is biological. The biological contact enters only when the codons are read through the curated standard-code table as amino-acid or stop labels. The local-tile statement remains at the code-read layer and must not be promoted to translation, folding, admissible chemistry, realized function, mechanism, or universality without another contact that directly observes that higher layer.

\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.

These numbers give the tile an internal coordinate-and-spectrum profile: the boundary set $R$ is not only a named list of codons but also participates in a measured finite spectral relation. The spectral row should still be read as structure inside the codon-coordinate experiment, not as a measurement of protein structure or organism-level fitness.

\paragraph{Shape significance against finite null rows.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and saw $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape match count was $1$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$ recorded $0$ exact matches, an observed $M$ size of $20$, and $7$ lower-tail cases at or below that observed size. Its reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite null comparisons support the claim that the observed window-six shape is not a generic accident of the sampled comparison families. They do not identify a causal mechanism for genetic translation.

\paragraph{Recovery and family rows.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. These are finite code-read recovery statistics: they say that the local boundary-and-closure construction is useful for recovering codon-label behavior in the recorded rows. They do not say that a recovered amino-acid label is a folded protein, a viable molecular event, or a biological function.

\paragraph{Module assignment.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons of $R$ into six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
agr&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
ar\_lys&=\{AAA\},\\
cun&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
ile\_met&=\{AUA\},\\
ssn&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
stop\_trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification row gives $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. The leave-one-code-out family row records five families. Their hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. This module table is the strongest finite bookkeeping result in the packet: every codon in $R$ is accounted for, and the family rows show broad recovery behavior across the listed code-table families.

\paragraph{Local scope of the claim.}
The scientific content of the record is a finite codon-table phenomenon: a thirteen-codon boundary set in a six-bit codon cube has a punctured-cube face count, a numerical spectral profile, low finite-null matching rates, complete module assignment, and high code-read recovery in the recorded leave-one rows. The curated standard-code table supplies the codon-to-label contact. Nothing in this contact observes translation as a physical process, folded structure, molecular admissibility, organismal function, or a universal mechanism. Those would require separate experiments or separately curated observations aimed at those layers. Therefore the ready claim is bounded: window-six coordinates expose a local standard-code boundary tile, and the amino-acid or stop labels are read only through the curated code table.
