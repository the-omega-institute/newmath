\subsection{Window-Six Boundary Row for the Standard-Code Table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Coordinate object.}
The row under consideration is the finite window-six codon display associated with the standard genetic-code table.  A codon is read as a six-bit position in the codon cube $Q_6$, while the biological label attached to that position is taken from the curated standard-code table.  The geometric part of the record is therefore finite and internal to the coordinate display, but the amino-acid or stop label is not inferred from geometry alone.  The finite boundary set recovered in the row has cardinality $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the verified row says $|R|=13$, with no extra codons and no missing codons relative to this displayed list.  The local shape calculation also identifies the same set as a punctured-cube boundary pattern with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are row-level geometry.  They do not by themselves assert that a ribosome realizes this geometry, that a protein fold is present, or that the codon table is forced by a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling evidence.}
The associated finite spectrum separates the main window component from the boundary component.  The row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical check is at tolerance scale rather than a symbolic identity claim.  In the shape-significance run with seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled tables, the procedure required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed main-set size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and $7$ samples lay in the lower tail at or below the observed size.  The corresponding row values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite evidence therefore supports a non-random local split relative to the stated null models, but only at the level tested by those null models: codon-coordinate shape and code-label readback.

The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage codons, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These values say that the local code-label pattern is stable under the tested omissions; they do not observe translation kinetics, molecular structure, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal fitness, or biochemical function.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or stop label.  That contact is adequate for checking code-layer readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It is not a contact for translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, or universal mechanism.  Consequently, the row is ready as a code-read record: the finite codon cube pattern, the $13$-element boundary, the punctured-cube face vector, the numerical spectrum, the sampling $p$-values, and the omission-test rates may all be cited locally.  Any claim about folding, structure, function, physical realization, or biological universality must wait for a separate observation designed for that higher layer.
