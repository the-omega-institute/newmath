\subsection{Window-six codon boundary row}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a six-coordinate codon display whose entries are read at the code-label level of the curated standard genetic-code table.  The finite row isolates a boundary set $R$ of size $13$.  In the verified run, the set is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The accompanying row records $R\_cardinality\_equals\_13=13$ and $size=13$, so the claim is a finite cardinality assertion rather than a biological mechanism.  The same thirteen codons also appear in the punctured-cube check, where the recorded face data have $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row supports a local tile with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the selected codon-coordinate geometry.

\paragraph{Coordinate and spectral evidence.}
The spectral run supplies a numerical consistency check for the local split.  The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, matching the separately stored radial-polynomial value, and the spectral radius in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers do not identify a biochemical force; they say that the finite coordinate construction has a stable numerical signature for the selected standard-code readback.  The symbol $M$ is present in the statistics as a complementary or ambient shape measurement, with observed size $20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, but no explicit finite list of $M$ codons is supplied in this row.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The main shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled tables.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{exact}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  A second null model preserved amino-acid classes.  In that run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-shape count was $0$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p=0.0035$ for that lower-tail event.  The finite evidence therefore says that the observed codon-label pattern is uncommon under the recorded randomizations, including one randomization that respects amino-acid label classes.  It does not by itself say why the biological code has the pattern.

\paragraph{Predictive readback checks.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level version separates the same behavior across five code families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  These are code-table readback statistics.  They remain label-level observations even when the row generalizes well across the recorded families.

\paragraph{Codon-removal and module structure.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $anchor\_count=0$, $boundary\_count=5$, $closure\_coverage\_high=8$, $predictable\_count=8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all thirteen codons in $R$: $\mathrm{AGA}$ and $\mathrm{AGG}$ form the $agr$ module; $\mathrm{AAA}$ is the $ar\_lys$ module; $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, and $\mathrm{CUU}$ form the $cun$ module; $\mathrm{AUA}$ is the $ile\_met$ module; $\mathrm{UCA}$ and $\mathrm{UUA}$ form the $ssn$ module; and $\mathrm{UAA}$, $\mathrm{UAG}$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$ form the $stop\_trp$ module.  The classification row reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The finite packet therefore has a closed accounting at the code-label level: every codon in the boundary set is placed in exactly the recorded module inventory.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, understood as a codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop label table.  That contact is adequate for checking whether the thirteen codons, their labels, and the local tile split are present in the standard-code readback.  It is not a contact with translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility, organismal function, or a universal biological rule.  Consequently, the row supports the narrower statement that window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary pattern in the curated code labels.  No claim about translation realization, folding, structural order, physical feasibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless a separate experiment supplies the missing contact.
