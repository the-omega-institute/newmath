\subsection{Window-six codon coordinates and the thirteen-point boundary row}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon is displayed as a six-bit coordinate after the four nucleotide letters are resolved into two binary distinctions per position.  The biologically supplied part of the record is the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That assignment is used as a label table, not as a claim about ribosomal realization, protein shape, chemical admissibility, biological role, or universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed boundary row.}
In the verified finite row for claim $h0.R.cardinality.13$, the boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The observed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a scalar count: the count and the named codons agree in the same run, with $|R|=13$ and with the listed codons exhausting the recorded set.  The associated punctured-cube check gives the same $R$ and records the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This makes the local geometry concrete: the thirteen codon points form the recorded punctured tile, with sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies and four two-dimensional faces in the finite cube representation.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape comparison run used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$.  Its recorded probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These statistics support the claim that the observed local codon-label arrangement is uncommon under the sampled finite null models.  They do not by themselves identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}

Every module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification.  This is the local finite-row content of the claim: the thirteen-codon boundary is not an anonymous residue of the computation, but a fully assigned collection of codon-label modules.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  It observes codon-to-label assignment at code-read resolution.  It does not observe translation dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal molecular mechanism, or universality across biological systems.  Therefore the finite row may be used to state a local window-six codon-tile boundary and its label-table readback, but it may not be promoted beyond that layer without an independent contact that directly measures the higher layer in question.
