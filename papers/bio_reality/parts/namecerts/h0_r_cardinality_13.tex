\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-read contact on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The carrier is not a molecule, a ribosome, or a folding experiment.  It is a finite coordinate display whose observable labels are supplied by a curated standard genetic-code table.  Within this display, the distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row is therefore not a vague enrichment claim: the finite boundary packet contains thirteen named codons, with no omitted or extra codon in the stated list.  The same finite row reports the punctured-cube face count $f=(13,16,4)$ for this boundary carrier, so the codon set is being read as a local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.

The codon display separates singleton-corner behavior from a three-point label class, while keeping the readback at the code-label layer. The shape randomization row gives $N=2000$ samples, $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ duplicate retries, $1$ exact shape match, and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}

A second null that preserves amino-acid class reports $N=2000$, observed $M$ size $20$, $0$ exact matches, and $7$ lower-tail outcomes at or below the observed $M$ size. These rows support a finite local-pattern statement, not a claim that the geometry is a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out behavior.}
The code-table perturbation rows provide a second finite check on the boundary interpretation.  Across $23$ evaluated code tables and $63$ total targets, the leave-one-code-out row reports baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate in $M-T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline rates are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers say that the local boundary classification is stable under the listed finite omissions; they do not say that every organism realizes the same mechanism.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of inference.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed content is codon-to-label assignment.  That contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or universal mechanism.  Amino-acid label readback is therefore kept below the protein-structure layer.  A stop label, an amino-acid label, or a local tile adjacency is not a fold, not an admissibility certificate, not a functional assay, and not an organism-independent law.  Any promotion beyond code labels would require an additional contact of the corresponding kind: a translation contact for translation, a structure contact for folding, a physical contact for admissibility, and a functional contact for biological role.

\paragraph{Claim status.}
The finite claim is ready at the code-read layer.  The observed boundary set has $13$ named codons, the punctured-cube count is $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values and near-zero cubic residual are recorded, the randomization rows give small finite-sample probabilities, and the omission tests recover the local classification at high rates.  The chapter therefore licenses the following narrow conclusion: window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary tile in the standard-code label table.  The conclusion deliberately stops there.  No translation process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility condition, biological function, mechanism, or universality theorem follows from this finite row alone.
