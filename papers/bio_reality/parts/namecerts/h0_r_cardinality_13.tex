\subsection{A Thirteen-Codon Boundary Row in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display for the four RNA bases across three codon positions. The observed row is a distinguished boundary set $R$ inside that cube. Its role is not to state a biological mechanism, but to isolate a finite local pattern in which singleton label corners, three-point label classes, and local tile faces can be compared against the curated standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The verified row has exactly thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies $R$ with the punctured-cube row and gives the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object is not merely a cardinality count: it carries vertices, edges, and two-dimensional faces in the local codon-tile geometry. The row is concentrated on codons involving the $A$, $G$, $C$, and $U$ positions listed above, with a visible cluster around stop-related codons $\mathrm{UAA}$, $\mathrm{UAG}$, $\mathrm{UGA}$, the arginine pair $\mathrm{AGA}$, $\mathrm{AGG}$, the isoleucine--methionine point $\mathrm{AUA}$, the leucine block $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, $\mathrm{CUU}$, and the small $\mathrm{UCA}$, $\mathrm{UUA}$ pair.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The row also has a numerical spectral signature. The verified spectral values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated cubic and radial residuals are both recorded as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the displayed eigenvalue data satisfy the stated finite polynomial relation to numerical tolerance. These numbers should be read as coordinates of the local codon-tile computation, not as physical constants of translation.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the finite null sample for the shape statistic, the run with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$ recorded $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserving amino-acid classes evaluated $N=2000$ samples with the same seed. It observed $0$ exact matches and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed module size $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite evidence therefore supports the claim that the thirteen-codon row is unusually structured relative to these sampled alternatives. It does not by itself identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-out checks.}
The leave-one-code-out table evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$. In the leave-one-codon-out check, the recovery rate was $1.0$, with $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, and $8$ high-coverage closure cases. These figures say that the finite local row is stable under the stated code-read probes; they do not assert that an organism executes the geometry as a translation rule.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out run split the evidence into $5$ families. The hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial--plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast. The module classification assigns all $13$ row codons and has no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This decomposition is the strongest finite summary of the row: every codon in $R$ is accounted for by a named module, and each module has predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The reality-facing contact is the curated standard genetic-code table. That table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or a stop label, and it is sufficient for checking code-layer readback and local label splits. It is not a contact with ribosomal dynamics, protein folding, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal fitness, or molecular function. The local tile is therefore a code-table pattern with finite support in the row $R$ and in the module decomposition above.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The proper conclusion is narrow. The window-six codon coordinates expose a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary row with nontrivial face data, spectral coordinates, sampled-null significance, leave-out stability, and complete module assignment. From these data alone one may claim a finite code-read pattern in the standard-code table. One may not infer translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a global law of biology unless a separate empirical contact supplies that higher-layer information.
