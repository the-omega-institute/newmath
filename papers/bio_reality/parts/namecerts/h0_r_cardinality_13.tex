\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile and the Thirteen-Codon Boundary Row}
\label{subsec:biorality-h0-r-cardinality-thirteen}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate packet.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a code-layer contact with a window-six codon coordinate system.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of the four nucleotide letters after the usual two-bit expansion of each codon position.  The packet separates four local distinctions: the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  Its internal operations are coordinate selection, finite closure, spectral measurement, and relation extraction.  The biological object is therefore not translation itself, but the standard-code table as a curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.

\paragraph{Thirteen-row boundary set.}
The verified row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The observed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same thirteen codons appear in the punctured-cube check.  Its finite face count is recorded by the vector $(13,16,4)$, so the row is not merely a list of labels: it has a local cubical incidence profile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the extracted tile.  The singleton and three-point label effects are read only through the standard-code labels attached to these codons; the geometry by itself supplies no amino-acid chemistry.

\paragraph{Spectral row measurement.}
The spectral probe gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial relation is numerically stable in the finite run: the recorded cubic-polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the radial-polynomial value.  Thus the code-layer row is accompanied by a reproducible numerical signature, while the interpretation remains local: the spectral radius measurement is a property of the extracted codon-label geometry, not an observation of folding, physical accessibility, or biochemical function.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Against $2000$ shape-control samples with seed $42$, the exact shape match count is $1$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ has $25$ matches, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The distinct-sample process required $57377$ attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, which records that the comparison was performed in a small finite combinatorial space rather than an asymptotic model.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed comparison set has size $20$, the exact match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$.  The corresponding probabilities are $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These values support a non-random local label split relative to the finite controls used here, but they do not promote the split into a general law of biology.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out check evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline rates are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  The leave-one-codon-out check records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These rows say that the local tile is useful for code-label recovery experiments inside the curated table family, not that a missing codon is physically generated by the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen codons are completely assigned to six finite modules.  The $\mathrm{agr}$ module contains $\mathrm{AGA}$ and $\mathrm{AGG}$; $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$ contains $\mathrm{AAA}$; $\mathrm{cun}$ contains $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, and $\mathrm{CUU}$; $\mathrm{ile\_met}$ contains $\mathrm{AUA}$; $\mathrm{ssn}$ contains $\mathrm{UCA}$ and $\mathrm{UUA}$; and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$ contains $\mathrm{UAA}$, $\mathrm{UAG}$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$.  The module audit reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Every module has either predictable or boundary status, so the row is internally accounted for at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The curated standard-code table is the only biological contact used here.  It licenses codon-to-label readback and local split checks.  It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code origin, or universality across biology.  Any statement crossing one of those thresholds requires a separate contact of the corresponding kind.  In particular, reading $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{UUA}$, or a stop-related module in the thirteen-codon row is still a code-table operation; it is not evidence that a protein folds, that a tRNA process occurs, that a molecule is physically admissible, or that a universal biological rule has been found.
