\subsection{Window-Six Boundary Tile for the Standard Code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
This record studies a finite window-six presentation of the standard-code codon table.  The working object is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read at the code-label layer through the curated table that assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label.  The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality thirteen.  In the verified run $5d4a3459997d$, the set is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $|R|=13$, with no additional codons and no missing codons in the finite row used for this claim.  The point of the record is not that the cube geometry generates biology by itself.  It is that, after the standard labels are supplied by the curated code table, the thirteen displayed codons form a local tile boundary that can be checked as a concrete finite pattern.

\paragraph{Punctured-cube shape.}
The same thirteen codons reappear in the punctured-cube check $e19917012c60$.  Its face-count summary is the $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$: thirteen vertices, sixteen local one-dimensional incidences, and four local two-dimensional faces.  The row therefore supports a small combinatorial shape rather than merely an unordered list of exceptional labels.  The codons in $R$ also fall into the module partition reported by $bbe8a4d0455b$: $\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$ for the AGR module, $\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$ for the Arg/Lys-adjacent module, $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ for the CUN module, $\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$ for the Ile/Met module, $\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$ for the SSN-side module, and $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$ for the stop/Trp-side module.  The module audit is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and the lists of extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.

\paragraph{Shape significance against finite null rows.}
The shape-significance run $0b267c88f8e4$ used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was exactly one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null row, run as $e8d5de747a0c$ with the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$, recorded observed $M$-size $20$, no exact shape matches, and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size.  Its exact-shape value is $0.0$, and its lower-tail value is $0.0035$.  These rows support the statement that the observed local shape is rare under the stated finite null procedures.  They do not identify a universal biological cause.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  Its observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels, at codon-label resolution.  That is enough to test the code-read question and the local split of the codon tile.  It is not a measurement of ribosomal realization, protein folding, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal fitness, or biochemical function.  Consequently the supported claim is deliberately local: window-six coordinates expose a thirteen-codon boundary tile inside the standard code once the labels are supplied by the curated table.  No conclusion about translation, folded structure, physical possibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless a separate observation at that higher layer is attached.
