\subsection{Window-Six Codon Boundary Tile}\label{sec:bioreality-codon-window-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube, with each codon read as a word in $\{U,C,A,G\}^3$ and displayed through the induced six-bit coordinate window.  The observed packet is not a claim about translation chemistry.  It is a finite statement about the portion of the curated standard-code label table selected by the local tile boundary.  The distinguished boundary set is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The direct cardinality run gives $|R|=13$, with experiment id \texttt{5d4a3459997d}.  The same finite set is also the punctured-cube witness returned by the face-count computation, whose $f$-vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the code-layer object is a local tile with thirteen occupied codon vertices, sixteen edge incidences, and four square faces in the induced coordinate complex.

\paragraph{Label contact.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, understood as a codon-to-label readout.  It assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or a stop label, and it is used only to test whether the coordinate-selected codons form a structured label split.  This contact has resolution at the codon-label level.  It does not report ribosomal realization, molecular folding, physical admissibility, organismal fitness, or biological function.  Consequently the finite tile may be compared with amino-acid and stop labels, but no statement about protein structure or mechanism is licensed by this chapter.

\paragraph{Spectral data.}
The coordinate split also has a numerical spectral signature.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same residual is recorded for the cubic relation check.  Within numerical tolerance this places the local codon-tile split on the recorded spectral relation, with $\lambda_M$ larger than $\lambda_R$ for the measured pair.  This is still a coordinate-and-label phenomenon: the spectrum detects the finite row geometry of the selected tile, not a physical energy law.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the recorded shape null, the run with seed $42$ used $N=2000$ sampled tables.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape was matched once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap event at Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null with the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed set size was $|M|=20$.  There were no exact-shape matches, so the recorded exact-shape rate is $0.0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{lower}}=0.0035$.  These values support the claim that the selected tile is not behaving like a typical draw under the tested finite nulls.  They do not by themselves identify a causal biological process.

\paragraph{Recovery tests.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level breakdown used $5$ families: alternative yeast had hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each had hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.  The leave-one-codon-out run reported $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchors, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These tests are finite cross-checks of code-label recovery; they do not turn a local codon-coordinate success into a universal law of genomes.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen codons in $R$ are exhausted by six named modules.  The \texttt{agr} module is $\{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\}$; \texttt{ar\_lys} is $\{\texttt{AAA}\}$; \texttt{cun} is $\{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\}$; \texttt{ile\_met} is $\{\texttt{AUA}\}$; \texttt{ssn} is $\{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\}$; and \texttt{stop\_trp} is $\{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}$.  The module classification reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The accounting identity is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
2+1+4+1+2+3=13=|R|.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the local finite classification asserted by the chapter: the boundary tile is completely covered by these six code-label modules.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a thirteen-codon local tile whose shape, spectrum, module partition, and recovery statistics are nontrivial under the recorded finite tests.  The standard-code table supplies the labels needed to read that tile.  Nothing in this record proves that the tile is translation itself, a folded protein structure, a physically admissible molecule, a biological function, a universal mechanism, or a law applying across all organisms.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact at the corresponding layer, with its own observations and failure conditions.
