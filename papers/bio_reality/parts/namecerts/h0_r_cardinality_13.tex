\subsection{Window-Six Codon Boundary Tile for the Standard Code}\label{subsec:h0-r-cardinality-13}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record concerns the code-read layer of the standard genetic-code table. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube, with each codon treated as a finite word over the RNA alphabet and read only through its curated standard-code label. The finite row named $h0.R.cardinality.13$ isolates a boundary set $R$ of size $13$. The verified row gives the complete list
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The biological reading of this list is deliberately narrow: these are codon labels in a curated standard-code table, not realized translation events and not claims about folded products. The row therefore supports a local label-boundary description inside the codon display, while keeping the amino-acid and stop annotations as contacts supplied by the curated code table.

\paragraph{Finite geometry.}
The companion row $h0.R.punctured.cube$ reports the same thirteen codons and verifies the face-vector summary $(13,16,4)$. In this chapter the phrase local tile refers to that finite combinatorial object: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the induced codon-coordinate geometry. The point is not that biology must obey this geometry. The point is that, once the standard-code labels are placed on the six-coordinate codon display, the observed boundary set has a compact finite shape that can be counted and re-identified. The singleton-corner and three-point-class distinctions are thus treated as code-table readback phenomena, not as hidden biochemical mechanisms.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum row records three scalar values attached to the same finite display: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same number is listed as the radial-polynomial residual. This is numerically small at the stated precision, so the row supplies a reproducible finite spectral signature for the coordinate object. It does not by itself identify a molecular force, a ribosomal process, or any physical admissibility constraint. Its role here is only to distinguish the observed codon-label boundary from arbitrary relabelings of the same finite alphabet.

\paragraph{Null-model pressure.}
The shape-significance experiment used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape was matched once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, while $25$ samples reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null row, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape $p$-value $0.0$, and lower-tail $p$-value $0.0035$. These figures say that the observed tile is not easily reproduced by the sampled finite null procedures. They do not promote the tile into a universal law of coding, translation, folding, or function.

\paragraph{Leave-one checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the reported hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. These numbers support the use of the local boundary as a strong code-read predictor within the finite experimental setup. The leave-one-codon-out row is complementary: it reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. Thus, in this finite dataset, missing codon labels are recovered through boundary or closure behavior rather than through an independent biochemical observation.

\paragraph{Family and module resolution.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row has family count $5$. The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. The module classification row assigns all thirteen $R$ codons, with $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no missing module entries, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has a predictable or boundary status in the supplied row. This gives a complete finite partition of the thirteen-codon boundary set for this claim.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of use.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a general mechanism. Consequently, the chapter's conclusion is limited to the following: the standard-code labels, when placed on the six-coordinate codon display, produce a thirteen-codon boundary set with the listed finite shape, spectral scalars, null-model separation, leave-one recovery behavior, and complete module assignment. Any claim about translation, folding, admissible molecular structure, organismal function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that directly observes that higher layer.
