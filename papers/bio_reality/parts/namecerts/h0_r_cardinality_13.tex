\paragraph{Coordinate object.}
The object recorded here is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display.  The coordinate carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon read as a finite word over the four nucleotide labels and then placed in a binary window. &\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite check reports $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons are returned by the punctured-cube computation.  The associated face data have $f$-vector

\begin{aligned}

$$

\paragraph{Label contact.}
The reality contact used in this row is a curated standard genetic-code table.  It supplies codon-to-label readback: each codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label.  This contact is deliberately narrow.  It can test whether the thirteen codons above sit in a local split visible at the code-label layer, but it does not observe ribosomal realization, tertiary structure, thermodynamic admissibility, biological activity, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal rule.  Any statement at one of those higher layers would require a separate measurement or curated contact of that layer.

\begin{aligned}

These numbers support a finite-code-layer anomaly statement: the local row is unusually structured under the stated sampling regimes.  They do not by themselves identify a causal biological process.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The record supports a code-read statement and a local tile-boundary statement for the listed thirteen codons.  It does not support promotion to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
