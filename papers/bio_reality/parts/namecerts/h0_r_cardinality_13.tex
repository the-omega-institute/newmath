\subsection{Window-six local boundary for the standard code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Claim packet.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer.  The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as the $64$ codon positions obtained from three nucleotide coordinates with two binary distinctions per coordinate.  The packet isolates a local boundary set $R$ whose members are exactly the codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audited row count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R|=13,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same thirteen codons returned by the independent cardinality and punctured-cube checks.  The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Thus the observation is a statement about label readback in the code table, not a statement about translation dynamics or molecular realization.

\paragraph{Local geometry.}
The finite computation identifies $R$ as a punctured cubical boundary pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the local tile datum: thirteen displayed codon vertices, sixteen local one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local square faces.  The description is deliberately local.  It records a codon-window shape inside $Q_6$ together with the standard-code labels sitting on those vertices.  The singleton-label corner and three-point label-class distinctions are therefore part of the code-table reading, while the coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data are internal finite descriptors of the same local tile.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the recorded randomization test with $N=2000$, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, the computation reports $55377$ duplicate retries, $1$ exact shape match, and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The corresponding probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$-size $20$, exact match count $0$, and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size.  Its row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite rows support the narrower statement that the observed local code-table shape is uncommon under the recorded null procedures.  They do not by themselves identify an evolutionary cause, a translational rule, a folding constraint, or a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out audit evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out audit reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$, with the same $1.0$ value recorded for recovery above threshold.  These rows say that the finite code-table geometry has strong recovery behavior in the tested setting.  They still remain code-layer tests: no protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or organism-level mechanism is observed by these contacts.

The family audit groups the leave-one-code-out behavior into $5$ families.  The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families. Each module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification.  This is a complete finite partition of the thirteen-codon boundary set for the packet, not a claim that the listed modules determine a universal rule across all biological contexts.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The supported conclusion is that the six-bit codon display exposes a statistically nontrivial local boundary tile in the curated standard-code table, with $13$ boundary codons, face-vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral row above, strong leave-one recovery behavior, and a complete six-module classification.  The supported biological reading stops at codon-to-label assignment in the standard code.  Nothing in this packet establishes translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate biological contact designed for that layer, rather than reuse of the codon-label table.
