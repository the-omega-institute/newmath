\paragraph{Claim record.}
The record $h0.R.cardinality.13$ concerns a six-coordinate codon cube in which each codon is read as a six-bit display over the usual four-letter alphabet.  The relevant finite object is not the whole biological process of translation.  It is the local row $R$ selected by the window-six coordinate rule and then checked against the curated standard-code label table.  The computed row has exactly thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the cardinality statement is the finite equality $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen entries appearing in the punctured-cube calculation.  The associated cell-count profile is

The singleton-corner behavior and the three-point label-class behavior are both read at this code-label level, where the standard table supplies amino-acid or stop labels but supplies no dynamics.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, viewed as a codon-to-label table.  It allows the six-bit coordinate object to be compared with amino-acid and stop labels in the standard code.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, protein folding, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, evolutionary mechanism, or a universal law of biology.  Therefore the local tile may be stated as follows: the window-six codon coordinates isolate a thirteen-codon boundary row with a punctured-cube face profile, spectral consistency, nontrivial sampling behavior, and high code-read recovery under the recorded leave-one tests.  No conclusion about translation, folded structure, physical possibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless another contact, designed for that higher layer, is supplied separately.
