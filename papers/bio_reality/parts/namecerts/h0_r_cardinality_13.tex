\paragraph{Finite display.}
The record is a code-layer observation on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is treated here as a finite word over the four RNA letters, displayed through a six-bit coordinate presentation, and then read against the curated standard-code table only for label readback.  The distinguished row is the subset $R \subset Q_6$ with thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit gives $|R|=13$, and the independent size field also reports $13$.  The same row is certified as a punctured-cube tile with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the object being discussed is not an open-ended biological mechanism: it is a finite local tile in codon-coordinate space, together with a standard-code label lookup.

\paragraph{Local shape and label split.}
The tile has three visible levels of structure.  First, it contains a local face pattern in the six-coordinate cube.  Second, it contains singleton-corner behavior, represented in this row by isolated codon-level label positions inside the finite tile.  Third, it contains a three-point label-class phenomenon: the stop/Trp region contributes the codons $UAA$, $UAG$, and $UGA$, while other modules collect the remaining codons into biologically named code-read families.  The module audit assigns all thirteen elements of $R$ with no omissions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \{AAA\}_{ar\_lys}
\cup \{AGA,AGG\}_{agr}
\cup \{AUA\}_{ile\_met}\\
&\quad \cup \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}_{cun}
\cup \{UCA,UUA\}_{ssn}
\cup \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}_{stop\_trp}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification row reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, $missing\_from\_modules=\emptyset$, $extra\_module\_codons=\emptyset$, and $unassigned\_codons=\emptyset$.  This makes the chapter-specific point precise: the coordinate tile and the label partition line up for this finite row, but the biological labels enter through a curated table rather than through the cube geometry alone.

No biological dynamics are inferred from these numbers.  Their role is to show that the tile has a reproducible finite coordinate signature that can be compared with randomized or leave-out probes.

\paragraph{Randomization checks.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ randomized samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among those samples there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{exact} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{Jaccard\ge 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that run the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, the exact-shape probability was recorded as $0.0$, and the lower-tail probability was $0.0035$.  These finite-row numbers support the statement that the observed code-read tile is non-generic under the tested shuffles.  They do not identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Broken down by family, the hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, over $5$ families.  The leave-one-codon-out row reports $anchor\_count=0$, $boundary\_count=5$, $closure\_coverage\_high=8$, $predictable\_count=8$, and $recovery\_rate=1.0$.  These probes show that the local tile is recoverable in the tested finite sense, especially when boundary positions are admitted as boundary positions rather than forced into a stronger interpretation.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table.  It observes that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code.  Therefore the table can test code-read questions and local codon-tile label splits.  It does not test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, functional effect, or a universal mechanism.  In particular, reading an amino-acid label sequence from codons is still a code-layer operation.  It is not an observation of a folded protein, a physical admissibility proof, or a functional assay.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The row supports the following limited conclusion.  In the six-coordinate codon display, the thirteen-codon set $R$ forms a punctured-cube local tile with a complete module assignment, a reproducible spectral signature, significant rarity under the stated null probes, and strong leave-out recovery statistics.  The standard-code table supplies the label readback needed to call the elements codons with amino-acid or stop labels.  Nothing in this row upgrades the local coordinate pattern into translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Any such promotion would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer.
