\subsection{Window-six codon boundary row}\label{subsec:h0-r-cardinality-13}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table, read only at the codon-label level.  Each codon is placed in the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_{6}$, where a nucleotide triplet is displayed as a finite six-bit address and the biological contact supplies only the amino-acid or stop label attached to that address.  The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the recorded finite run it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed row count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R| = 13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row was also reported as a punctured cubical tile with face-vector data $(13,16,4)$.  This means that the finite coordinate presentation is not just a list of exceptional labels: the row has a local two-dimensional face pattern inside the window-six cube.  The table contact, however, remains a curated label table.  It identifies which codon receives which standard-code label, and it does not by itself observe translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The row sits inside a larger finite split whose two recorded spectral coordinates are $\lambda_{M}=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_{R}=0.4758352843197002$, with auxiliary scalar $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check returned
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mu,\lambda_M,\lambda_R)=-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the numerical residual is at tolerance scale rather than a visible obstruction.  The local reading is therefore that the boundary row is compatible with a finite spectral partition of the codon cube.  This is a coordinate fact about the displayed table and the derived probes; it is not a biochemical law about how organisms must implement coding.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
A null comparison with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$ found $1$ exact shape match.  The exact-shape probability estimate was $p=0.0005$.  The high-overlap comparison found $25$ cases with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The sampling record also reported $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  These numbers support the claim that the observed local tile is not a common outcome under that finite randomization scheme.  A second amino-acid-class-preserving null model used $N=2000$ samples and found $0$ exact matches, with lower-tail count $7$ at observed $M$-size $20$.  Its reported values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These tests strengthen the finite-row reading: the row is a detectable local pattern in the standard-code label table, not merely a restatement of the raw codon alphabet.

\paragraph{Leave-one probes.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  It reported baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate in $M-T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value $0.9841269841269841$ was recorded as the hit rate above baseline.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, across $5$ families.  The leave-one-codon-out experiment recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These finite rows say that the local boundary construction is stable under several deletion probes at the code-label layer.  They do not say that the deletion probes reconstruct translation machinery or protein realization.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The $13$ codons of $R$ were completely assigned to six named modules, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The finite module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row size is $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and every module contains a predictable or boundary codon under the recorded classification.  This partition is useful because it separates singleton corners such as $\mathrm{AAA}$ and $\mathrm{AUA}$ from multi-codon label families such as $\mathrm{CUN}$ and the stop/tryptophan group.  The partition also marks where a three-point label class occurs, namely in the stop/tryptophan boundary group $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite record supports the following bounded statement.  In the six-coordinate codon display of the curated standard-code table, there is a $13$-codon boundary row $R$ with the exact codons listed above, punctured-cube face data $(13,16,4)$, a small spectral residual, nontrivial null-model significance, and complete six-module classification.  This is enough for a local codon-window claim at the label-readout layer.  It is not enough for any assertion about realized translation, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility of a sequence, biological function, a causal mechanism, or a universal rule for life.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer in question.
