\subsection{A thirteen-codon boundary row in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  The ambient coordinate object is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is represented by its window-six display and compared through finite coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data.  The distinguished row $R$ is not asserted to be a biological mechanism.  It is a finite local boundary row selected inside the codon-coordinate display and then read against the curated standard-code label table.  The verified row has cardinality thirteen:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the direct finite content of claim $h0.R.cardinality.13$: the row contains exactly the thirteen listed codons and no additional codon in the recorded packet.

\paragraph{Local cube geometry.}
The same row is also recorded as a punctured-cube shaped boundary tile.  Its finite cell count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the thirteen displayed codons form the vertex set of a local tile with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four square faces in the derived cubical relation.  The language is deliberately local: this row is a coordinate-and-label pattern in $Q_6$, not a statement that DNA, translation, proteins, or organisms must obey this geometry as a law.

The finite relation attached to the row has a recorded spectral profile.  The measured values are

\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.

Thus, at the precision of the packet, the spectral calculation is internally consistent with the stated finite relation.  The role of this number is diagnostic: it witnesses that the row is not being reported only as a list of codon names, but as a finite coordinate relation with a reproducible spectral signature.

\paragraph{Shape significance checks.}
Against the sampled shape null for $M$, the run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null preserving amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that run the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The recorded probabilities were $p=0.0$ for exact amino-acid-class shape match and $p=0.0035$ for the lower tail.  These figures support the finite-row claim as a non-generic local code-table pattern under the stated sampling procedures, while still leaving biological causation outside the claim.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the recorded hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, across $5$ families.  The leave-one-codon-out test recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These tests say that the finite row is stable under the packet's code-read probes; they do not say that translation itself has been modeled.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The thirteen codons of $R$ are completely assigned by the module classification.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no unassigned codons, and no extra module codons.  This makes the row suitable for a finite code-read chapter: every codon in the row has a recorded module home, and the module list neither omits nor adds a codon.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test codon-label readback and a local label split.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label sequences obtained from this row remain code-layer outputs.  No claim about protein folding, structural order, organismal function, biochemical admissibility, or universality follows from the thirteen-codon row unless a separate contact supplies that higher-layer observation.
