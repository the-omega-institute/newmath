\subsection{Window-Six Codon Boundary Readback}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The claim \texttt{h0.R.cardinality.13} concerns a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$ whose rows are read only at the code-label level.  The finite row singled out by the computation is the thirteen-codon set
$R=\{\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}$.
The verified cardinality is therefore $|R|=13$, with experiment run \texttt{5d4a3459997d} reporting both \texttt{R\_cardinality\_equals\_13}=13 and \texttt{size}=13.  The same codon list is reported by the punctured-cube check, whose face-count vector is $(13,16,4)$.  In this chapter, these numbers are treated as finite code-table facts about a local window in codon-coordinate space, not as statements about translation dynamics or molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary shape.}
The punctured-cube witness says that the same thirteen codons form a local tile with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces.  Written as a compact finite certificate, the observed row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \{\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\},\\
|R| &= 13,\\
f(R) &= (13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
The biological contact attached to this row is the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  That contact is adequate for checking codon-label readback and local label splits.  It is not a measurement of ribosomal realization, folded structure, energetic feasibility, organismal fitness, or universal biological law.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The finite coordinate computation also reports a spectral signature.  The measured values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial check, so the row is numerically consistent with the expected local spectral equation at the displayed precision.  The spectral radius tolerance entry is $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$ in the row data.  These scalars support the internal coherence of the window-six tile computation, but they do not enlarge the biological interpretation beyond code-label readback.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The shape-significance run \texttt{0b267c88f8e4} used $N=2000$ samples with seed 42.  It required 57377 distinct-sample attempts and encountered 55377 duplicate retries.  Only one exact shape match occurred, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap criterion produced 25 matches at Jaccard at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  A second comparison preserving amino-acid classes, run \texttt{e8d5de747a0c}, again used $N=2000$ and seed 42.  It reported observed $M$-size 20, exact-match count 0, lower-tail count 7, $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0$, and $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  Thus the displayed tile is not a frequent outcome under the recorded finite controls, including the amino-acid-class-preserving control.  The data supplied here include the size of $M$ but not an explicit member list for $M$, so the chapter records only the scalar $M$ information.

\paragraph{Leave-one checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row, run \texttt{429e6e2c4eb4}, evaluated 23 tables and 63 targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level table splits the above-baseline rates over five families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  The leave-one-codon-out row, run \texttt{e9e1a6e41633}, reports anchor count 0, boundary count 5, high closure coverage 8, predictable count 8, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These leave-one rows say that the coordinate tile is useful for recovering code-table labels in the tested finite setting.  They do not say that the tile implements translation or determines protein behavior.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module-classification run \texttt{bbe8a4d0455b} accounts for all thirteen codons in $R$.  It assigns $\texttt{AGA}$ and $\texttt{AGG}$ to the \texttt{agr} module, $\texttt{AAA}$ to \texttt{ar\_lys}, $\texttt{CUA}$, $\texttt{CUC}$, $\texttt{CUG}$, and $\texttt{CUU}$ to \texttt{cun}, $\texttt{AUA}$ to \texttt{ile\_met}, $\texttt{UCA}$ and $\texttt{UUA}$ to \texttt{ssn}, and $\texttt{UAA}$, $\texttt{UAG}$, and $\texttt{UGA}$ to \texttt{stop\_trp}.  The completion record gives $R$-size 13, assigned count 13, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The row therefore supports a closed local module account for this thirteen-codon boundary set.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The chapter's claim is deliberately narrow.  A window-six coordinate display, a thirteen-codon boundary row, the face vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values, the null-model p-values, and the leave-one recovery rates together support a local code-label pattern in the curated standard-code table.  They do not establish that DNA is forced to obey this geometry, that a codon-label sequence becomes a folded protein structure, that the label sequence is physically admissible, that it has biological function, that it is realized by translation in a particular organismal setting, or that the observed tile is a universal mechanism.  Any such higher-layer statement would require an additional contact whose observed object is at that layer rather than a codon-to-label table.
