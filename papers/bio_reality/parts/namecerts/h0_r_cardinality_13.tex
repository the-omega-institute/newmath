\subsection{Window-six boundary tile in the standard code}\label{sec:h0-r-cardinality-13}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed boundary set.}
The carrier for this record is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of the $64$ codons of the standard code.  The tested object is not a translation machine, a folding model, or a biochemical dynamical system; it is the standard codon-label table viewed through a finite coordinate window.  In this window the distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite run records the size again as $13$, so the cardinality claim and the explicit membership list agree exactly.  The biological contact used here is the curated standard-code label table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and it supplies no independent observation about realized translation, molecular structure, admissible chemistry, phenotype, or function.

\paragraph{Local shape of the boundary.}
The boundary set is also recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with the same thirteen codons.  Its finite face count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed object is not merely a list of labels: it has a local tile shape inside the six-coordinate codon display.  The phrase ``local'' is essential.  The face vector describes the combinatorics of the selected codons in this coordinate presentation; it does not assert that biological DNA is forced by this geometry, nor that the tile is a universal law for genetic systems.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Against a sampled shape null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the run reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and there were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null that preserves amino-acid classes, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$-size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$.  Its reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite comparisons support the claim that the observed local shape is not typical under the two sampled controls.  They still do not promote the result beyond the code-label layer, because both controls are built around the codon table and its labels.

The codon-level leave-one run reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers say that the local boundary and its neighboring closure structure have predictive content for code-label readback under the finite tests supplied here.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The claim $h0.R.cardinality.13$ is ready at the code-read layer.  The finite evidence establishes the explicit thirteen-codon boundary, its punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, its spectral consistency row, sampled rarity under the two reported null models, strong leave-one recovery rates, and complete module assignment.  The result is a disciplined codon-table statement: window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern in the curated standard-code labels.  Nothing in this record establishes realized translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality.  Any such promotion would require a separate contact that observes the higher layer directly.
