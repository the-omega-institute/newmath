\subsection{Window-six residual tile for the standard code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-thirteen}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim concerns a six-coordinate codon display for the curated standard genetic code.  The finite object under inspection is the codon cube with standard-code labels attached as observed table entries.  Within that display, the residual boundary set $R$ has exactly thirteen codons.  The observed members are \texttt{AAA}, \texttt{AGA}, \texttt{AGG}, \texttt{AUA}, \texttt{CUA}, \texttt{CUC}, \texttt{CUG}, \texttt{CUU}, \texttt{UAA}, \texttt{UAG}, \texttt{UCA}, \texttt{UGA}, and \texttt{UUA}.  Thus the finite row asserts $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen entries appearing both in the cardinality computation and in the punctured-cube reconstruction.

\paragraph{Local geometry.}
The punctured-cube calculation gives the face data of the residual tile as
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)&=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This records thirteen vertices, sixteen local adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the selected residual region.  The point of the record is not that codon biology is forced by cube geometry.  Rather, the window-six coordinate system isolates a small labelled boundary pattern that can be compared against the curated code table.  The label table is the contact point: it says which codons carry amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code, while the coordinate calculation says how the thirteen selected codons sit inside the six-bit codon cube.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The same finite packet carries a spectral summary.  The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius entry repeats $0.6752479308830213$ within the recorded tolerance.  These numbers are useful as a reproducible fingerprint of the selected finite construction.  They do not by themselves identify a biological mechanism, but they make the local computation rigid enough to be tested against alternative labelled tables and null rearrangements.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The unconstrained shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and twenty-five high-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The run also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  A second null model preserving amino-acid class again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It found zero exact shape matches, with $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$, and seven lower-tail samples at or below the observed $M$-size of $20$, giving $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  The available finite row gives the size of $M$ but not the member list of $M$, so the chapter treats $M$ as a measured surrounding set with recorded cardinality rather than as a displayed codon list.

\paragraph{Leave-out tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary term is $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row records recovery rate $1.0$, recovery rate above threshold $1.0$, eight predictable cases, five boundary cases, eight high-coverage closure cases, and zero anchors.  These rows support the narrower statement that the local residual tile has predictive structure for code-layer readback under these tests.

\paragraph{Family and module rows.}
The family-level code-out row contains five families.  The hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  The module classification assigns all thirteen residual codons with no extras, no omissions, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are: \texttt{AGR} with \texttt{AGA}, \texttt{AGG}; \texttt{ar\_lys} with \texttt{AAA}; \texttt{CUN} with \texttt{CUA}, \texttt{CUC}, \texttt{CUG}, \texttt{CUU}; \texttt{ile\_met} with \texttt{AUA}; \texttt{SSN} with \texttt{UCA}, \texttt{UUA}; and \texttt{stop\_trp} with \texttt{UAA}, \texttt{UAG}, \texttt{UGA}.  The row therefore partitions $R$ into six named local modules while preserving the total count $13$.

\paragraph{Biological reading.}
The finite contact is the curated standard-code table, interpreted only as codon-label readback.  It licenses statements about the labels attached to the thirteen codons and about the local split visible in the six-coordinate display.  It does not observe translation in a cell, folded structure, energetic feasibility, organismal function, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Any statement crossing one of those boundaries would require an additional contact suited to that layer, such as a translation assay, structure observation, physical model, or functional experiment.  In this chapter the conclusion is therefore deliberately local: the standard-code label table aligns with a thirteen-codon residual boundary tile in the window-six codon cube, and the numerical tests make that alignment non-generic under the sampled null models, but the claim remains confined to code-layer readback.
