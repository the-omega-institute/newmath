\subsection{A thirteen-codon boundary row in the window-six codon cube}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-thirteen}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table after it is displayed as a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The biological contact used here is only the curated codon-to-label table: each codon is read as an amino-acid label or a stop label, and no ribosomal realization, folding event, physical model, or functional assay is included in the row.  Within this finite display, the distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality thirteen.  The verified row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is not a claim that these thirteen codons form a law of biology.  It is a finite code-layer observation: when the standard-code table is placed in the window-six coordinate display, these codons occupy the locally selected boundary row.

\paragraph{Tile shape and punctured-cube count.}
The same row is independently reported as a punctured-cube tile with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observation is not merely a list of labels; it also carries a small combinatorial shape.  The point of the shape is local.  It says that the selected codons close as a finite boundary tile in the coordinate presentation, while the table labels themselves still come from the curated standard-code contact.  The six-bit carrier supplies coordinates, adjacency, and local closure; the label table supplies biological names.  Neither component alone supplies translation dynamics, structural order, chemical admissibility, protein function, or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral summary gives three scalar values tied to the same finite display:

\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.

$$
The reported cubic, or radial, residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the polynomial check is numerically at tolerance for the stored run.  These numbers should be read as internal diagnostics of the coordinate-and-closure presentation.  They do not turn the codon table into a physical theory, and they do not identify an amino-acid string with a folded protein.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
A randomization row with $N=2000$ samples used seed $42$ and required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$.  The corresponding empirical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $|M|=20$, exact match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$.  Its stored values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These rows support the narrower statement that the local boundary tile is not a common outcome under the tested finite null procedures.  They do not remove the need for separate biological contacts before promoting the row to mechanism or function.

\paragraph{Readback stability.}
Two leave-out checks describe how the local row behaves when information is withheld.  The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  It reports baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate above baseline $0.9841269841269841$, hit rate in $M-T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These checks are still code-layer checks.  They measure finite recovery behavior for codon labels in the coordinate presentation, not successful translation or realized protein structure.

\paragraph{Family and module rows.}
The family split contains $5$ families.  The stored hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  The module classification assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module row is exact for this finite packet: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the residual lists are empty.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded code-read claim.  In the standard-code table, the window-six coordinate presentation selects a thirteen-codon boundary row $R$ with punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, nontrivial spectral diagnostics, strong empirical separation under the recorded null samples, and complete module accounting.  The supported conclusion is therefore local: codon coordinates can expose a compact boundary-tile pattern in the curated label table.  The same data explicitly do not establish translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or any general biological law.  Any one of those stronger conclusions would require an additional contact that observes that layer directly.
