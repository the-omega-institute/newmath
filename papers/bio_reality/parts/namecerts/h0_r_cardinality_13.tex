\subsection{The thirteen-codon boundary row in the window-six code display}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed contact.}
The finite carrier for this record is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read through a curated standard-code table that assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The contact is therefore a code-label contact: it records codon-to-label readback in the standard genetic code, and it does not by itself observe ribosomal realization, molecular structure, energetic admissibility, protein activity, or a universal biological mechanism. Within that scope, the row named $R$ is the local boundary set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|R|=13$. This is the primary finite observation for the claim: the window-six coordinate display isolates exactly thirteen codons in the boundary row, and the same thirteen names are recovered by the independent punctured-cube check.

\paragraph{Combinatorial shape of the row.}
The punctured-cube computation gives the same set $R$ and reports the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a list of labels: it has a finite tile shape in the local cube, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces. This shape statement remains local. It says that the standard-code labels, when placed in the six-coordinate codon display, expose a boundary tile with a specific finite incidence profile. It does not say that the tile is a law of DNA, that translation must realize it, or that amino-acid strings produced from the labels have any folded form.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The associated spectral run records $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial polynomial residual is numerically negligible at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, recorded again as the cubic-polynomial residual. The larger set $M$ is also used in the sampling checks below; its observed size is $20$, but the present row packet supplies no explicit codon-by-codon list for $M$.

\paragraph{Randomization checks.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, including $55377$ duplicate retries. Among those samples, exactly one matched the observed shape exactly, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. The high-overlap threshold produced $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. A second null run preserving amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It found zero exact shape matches, so the recorded exact-shape frequency is $0.0$, and it found $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size $20$, giving a lower-tail value of $0.0035$. These finite counts support the claim that the observed local tile is not a common outcome of the sampled label shuffles, while still keeping the interpretation at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The by-family summary records five families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, and bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline. These figures show that the local boundary row remains predictive under code-table omissions at the label level. They do not turn the row into evidence about protein folding, organismal function, physical constraints, or a general mechanism of translation.

\paragraph{Codon omission and module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out run reports no anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and there are no extra module codons, no missing row elements, and no unassigned codons. The modules are

\begin{aligned}

Each module has either a predictable or boundary status in the finite classification. This gives a complete row-level partition of the thirteen codons and explains how the singleton, pair, triple, and four-codon parts of $R$ sit inside the local code display.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The verified conclusion is narrow and finite: in the standard-code codon table, the six-coordinate codon display exposes a thirteen-codon local boundary row with the listed codons, face vector $(13,16,4)$, the reported spectral values, nontrivial sampled-shuffle rarity, strong leave-out recovery, and a complete six-module classification. No conclusion about translation in cells, folded protein structure, chemical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this packet unless a separate observation explicitly supplies that higher-layer contact.
