\subsection{A thirteen-codon boundary row in the six-bit codon cube}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studied here is a code-layer object: the standard genetic-code table is used only as a curated assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The BEDC-side carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read through local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. In this finite display the distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality thirteen. The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local boundary packet is not a vague geometric impression: it is a concrete subset of the sixty-four codon vertices, with verified size
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R| = 13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated punctured-cube computation gives the same thirteen codons and records the local face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R) = (13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row carries thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional tiles in the reported finite complex.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The same run-family reports a spectral separation between the twenty-label row $M$ and the thirteen-codon boundary row $R$. The supplied numerical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check is numerically closed to tolerance: the reported polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same number is listed as the cubic-polynomial satisfaction value. This is a finite numerical certificate for the row geometry, not a biochemical mechanism. The shape-randomization comparison used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. It found one exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and twenty-five alternatives with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampling log also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the null comparison should be read as a finite empirical test over a constrained discrete population rather than as an asymptotic theorem.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information. In $N=2000$ trials, again with seed $42$, it reported $0$ exact matches to the observed shape, while $7$ trials fell in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size. The observed $M$ size is $20$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite record therefore supports a code-table regularity: the observed local tile and label split are atypical under the supplied class-preserving shuffle. It does not by itself say that the codon cube forces biology to realize translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$; the hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$; and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary row is $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the above-baseline hit rates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\

$$
The leave-one-codon-out probe records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ predictable cases, and $8$ high-coverage closure cases, with recovery rate $1.0$. These numbers show that the finite code-read packet has predictive stability under the supplied deletion tests. They do not observe ribosomal realization, molecular dynamics, folded geometry, organismal fitness, or any universal causal law.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen codons of $R$ are completely assigned to six reported modules. The module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module-classification row reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. This closes the local bookkeeping of the boundary row: every codon in the row is placed in a named code-read module, and the module list contributes no additional codon outside the row.

\paragraph{Contact boundary.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observable content is the codon-to-label assignment. That contact is sufficient for checking whether the listed codons, labels, local faces, and module memberships are reproduced at the code layer. It is insufficient for stronger biological readings. In particular, no claim is made here that the local tile is a global law of DNA, that six-bit codon geometry alone implements translation, that amino-acid labels determine folded protein structure, that the row supplies physical admissibility, that it establishes biological function, or that it identifies a universal mechanism. Any such promotion would require a separate contact whose observed fact lives at that higher layer.
