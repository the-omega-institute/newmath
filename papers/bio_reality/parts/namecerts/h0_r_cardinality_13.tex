\subsection{Window-Six Codon Boundary Tile}\label{subsec:h0-r-cardinality-13}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon row.} The finite object in this record is the six-coordinate codon cube with a distinguished boundary set $R$ read against the curated standard-code label table. The row-level computation identifies $R$ as a thirteen-codon subset, not as a statement about translation machinery, protein folding, physical feasibility, function, or biological universality. The verified set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality row reports $R\_cardinality\_equals\_13=13$ and $size=13$ under experiment run \texttt{5d4a3459997d}. Thus the claim is not merely that a local picture exists; the exact codon names and the exact row count are part of the finite record.

\paragraph{Punctured-cube shape.} A second row checks the same thirteen codons as a punctured-cube boundary shape. Its face-count summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This supplies the local tile geometry used by the chapter: thirteen vertices, sixteen local adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the extracted boundary tile. The computation is still code-layer geometry. It does not assert that the codon cube is a causal model of cell biology; it says that the curated label table, when placed on the six-bit codon display, exposes a particular finite boundary subset.

\paragraph{Spectral row.} The spectrum row gives a numerical check on the same finite split. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,\\
p(\mu)&=-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The near-zero polynomial residual is reported both as the cubic-polynomial residual and the radial-polynomial value. Within the tolerance of the computation, the spectral radius row therefore supports the local distinction between the main tile component $M$ and the boundary set $R$. The row does not include an explicit element-by-element list for $M$; the later null-model row records the observed size of $M$ as $20$.

\paragraph{Null-model checks.} Two finite randomization rows test whether the observed geometry is easy to obtain from label-preserving or shape-preserving alternatives. The shape-significance row uses $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$, after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It reports one exact shape match and twenty-five matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving row also uses $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It records $observed\_M\_size=20$, zero exact-shape matches, and seven samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite rows make the tile nontrivial as a code-table pattern. They do not promote it to a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one readback rows.} The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The by-family row records five families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$. These numbers support a robust code-label readback pattern across the tested finite table family, with the important restriction that the tested object is still a table of labels.

The completeness row gives $R\_size=13$ and $assigned\_count=13$, with $extra\_module\_codons=\varnothing$, $missing\_from\_modules=\varnothing$, and $unassigned\_codons=\varnothing$. Every module has a predictable or boundary status, so the thirteen-codon boundary is fully accounted for at the module level.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.} The sole contact used here is the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for checking whether the local boundary split agrees with the displayed table. It is not an observation of translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. In particular, an amino-acid label sequence is not a folded protein, and a local codon tile is not a global biological law. Any claim crossing from code labels into translation, structure, chemistry, function, or universality would require a separate contact designed for that layer.
