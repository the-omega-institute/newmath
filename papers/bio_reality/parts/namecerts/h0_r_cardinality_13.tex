Thus the finite object under discussion is not the whole genetic code as biology; it is a local subset of the standard-code label table, read through a six-bit coordinate window and then checked against label classes supplied by the curated table.

\paragraph{Punctured-cube shape.}
The same thirteen codons are recorded as a punctured-cube boundary row with face-count vector $(13,16,4)$. \paragraph{Randomization evidence for shape specificity.}
The shape comparison was run with $N=2000$ random trials and seed $42$.  The run attempted $57377$ distinct samples and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and it found $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, recorded observed $M$-size $20$, exact match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite-row tests indicate that the observed local shape is unusual under the sampled nulls.  The conclusion remains statistical and code-layered: the data support a nontrivial local pattern in the curated label table, not a direct inference to molecular realization or biological function.
