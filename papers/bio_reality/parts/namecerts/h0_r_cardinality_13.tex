\subsection{Window-six boundary codons in the standard-code table}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-cardinality-13}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim site.}
The claim concerns the subset $R$ selected by the window-six codon display on the standard genetic-code label table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$: each codon is treated as a finite coordinate word, and the only biological readout used here is the curated assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The observed boundary set has exactly thirteen codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
   &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a code-layer statement.  It says that a finite coordinate construction isolates a local boundary tile whose labels can be checked against the standard-code table.  It does not say that the coordinate construction causes translation, realizes a folded protein, imposes physical admissibility, determines biological function, supplies a mechanism, or holds universally across all biological regimes.

\paragraph{Finite geometry.}
The same thirteen codons occur in the punctured-cube computation.  The verified row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{punctured}}=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},\qquad f=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite tile has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the recorded combinatorial summary.  The point of the row is not that the codon table becomes a geometric law.  The point is narrower: under the six-bit display, the codons listed above form a stable local object whose membership is finite, explicit, and reproducible from the recorded run $5d4a3459997d$ and the punctured-cube run $e19917012c60$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the sampled null tables, the exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null row is sharper for exact shape: with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, it recorded $0$ exact matches and $7$ samples in the lower tail at the observed $M$-size.  The observed module size was $20$, the exact-shape probability was $0.0$, and the lower-tail probability was $0.0035$.  These are evidence for nontrivial finite-row structure relative to the selected null models, not evidence for a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Leave-out checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the recorded hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables, $1.0$ for the candidate-other family, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast tables.  The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closures, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These rows support the claim that the finite boundary pattern remains informative under local deletion tests at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ with no extras and no omissions: $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification.  This is the strongest finite bookkeeping statement available in the row: the thirteen boundary codons are not merely counted, but fully partitioned into named local modules.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used by this chapter is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and local split tests.  It is insufficient for claims about translation execution, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are still labels; they are not protein structures.  Any future claim crossing from code labels into structure, function, or physical realization must bring a separate empirical contact designed for that layer.
