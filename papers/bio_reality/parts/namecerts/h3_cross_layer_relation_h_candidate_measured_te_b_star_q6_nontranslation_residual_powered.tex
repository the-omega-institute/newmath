\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{subsec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-measured-te-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests a narrow cross-layer question: after amino-acid composition and sequence-composition controls have been removed, can a measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readout absorb any part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual component that remained outside the modeled codon-adaptation projection?  The object under test is not protein abundance, folded structure, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal translation rule.  It is the finite readout surface obtained by joining coding-sequence codon counts, nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, matched translation-efficiency values, and matched transcript-abundance controls.

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The tested residual basis has coordinate count $9$ and consists of
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control design contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$ computed from real CDS codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism the reported control-column count is $24$ and the observed rank is $23$, so the calculation is a high-rank controlled projection rather than an under-rank three-row saturation.

\paragraph{Joined rows.}
The joined measured-TE rows are organism-specific.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$, with $14410$ CDS rows reported in the abundance join, $14410$ valid abundance rows by protein identifier, and $13140$ valid translation-efficiency rows.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=3415$, with $3739$ CDS rows reported in the abundance join, $3747$ valid abundance rows, and $3415$ valid translation-efficiency rows.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=11554$, with $19053$ CDS rows reported in the abundance join, $19483$ valid abundance rows, and $11554$ valid translation-efficiency rows.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=5128$, with $5815$ CDS rows reported in the abundance join, $6351$ valid abundance rows, and $5128$ valid translation-efficiency rows.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P\_{Q}$ denote the fitted $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance-residual projection after the declared controls, and let $P\_{Q\perp T\_{mod}}$ denote its component left after the modeled translation-adaptation projection.  The descriptive complement ratio is reported as
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\_{modeled} &= \frac{\lVert P\_{Q\perp T\_{mod}}\rVert^{2}}{\lVert P\_{Q}\rVert^{2}} .
\end{aligned}
$$
On the measured-TE join, \emph{Danio rerio} has $\lVert P\_{Q}\rVert^{2}=1092.0114815505467$, $\lVert P\_{Q\perp T\_{mod}}\rVert^{2}=919.9445878158525$, and $d\_{modeled}=0.8424312412078521$.  \emph{E. coli} has $393.78692095732885$, $261.0102870984463$, and $0.6628211177352172$.  \emph{H. sapiens} has $565.3850201235074$, $484.31956581711796$, and $0.8566190269974241$.  \emph{S. cerevisiae} has $675.3275479192903$, $151.18264752811882$, and $0.22386566043976477$.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
Measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency was then used as the first candidate readout for the remaining $P\_{Q\perp T\_{mod}}$ component.  The absorbed fractions are small in all four organisms: $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  The corresponding combined residual ratios are $0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$.  Thus the measured-TE contact is present and finite-row powered, but it does not strongly remove the non-modeled translation-complement residual in this run.

\paragraph{Join self-check.}
The measured-TE join is not identical to the fuller abundance-CDS join used by the complement reference calculation.  The absolute shifts in $d\_{modeled}$ are $0.03219747984141397$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.00017488612263594217$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.06427590857052146$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.012133464712697745$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  Under the stated $0.02$ tolerance, \emph{E. coli} and \emph{S. cerevisiae} match the complement reference, while \emph{Danio rerio} and \emph{H. sapiens} fail the tolerance because measured-TE availability changes the protein-identifier join.  This is a data-shape warning, not a biological contradiction.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite calculation supports only the following bounded statement.  The matched ribosome-profiling TE contact can be evaluated against the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates under amino-acid composition, CDS length, $GC3$, $M$-density, organism-specific row joins, and matched mRNA abundance controls.  In the reported run, the descriptive measured-TE absorption is low in all four organisms, so the residual component left after the modeled adaptation projection remains mostly unexplained by measured TE.  This does not license a causal mediation statement: the statistic is a projection summary for a single measured-TE contact class and does not establish that TE mechanistically carries the residual signal.

\paragraph{Negative scope.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome-occupancy law, protein-abundance realization, folded-structure statement, physical-admissibility statement, functional statement, phenotype statement, or universal biological principle follows from this entry.  Such claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.  Matched mRNA abundance is used here only as a transcription-layer control boundary; it is not itself a translation readout, protein readout, or mechanism measurement.
