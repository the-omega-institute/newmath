\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-complement}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer question: after the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates have already been separated from amino-acid composition and elementary CDS covariates, how much of the remaining modeled-tAI-complement abundance component is described by measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency?  The finite coordinate family has nine columns,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control surface $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, where $M$-density is the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control matrix has $24$ columns in each organism and numerical rank $23$ in each joined panel.  Translation efficiency is used only as a measured readout joined to the same protein identifiers, not as a license to infer protein amount, folding state, biological function, or a universal rule.

\paragraph{Joined panels and rank check.}
Four organism-specific panels pass the finite-row rank requirement for this descriptive projection.  The joined measured-TE sample sizes are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{Danio}}&=13140, & n_{\mathrm{Ecoli}}&=3415, & n_{\mathrm{Human}}&=11554, & n_{\mathrm{Yeast}}&=5128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding CDS-join reports before imposing measured-TE availability are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$.  The valid abundance counts by protein are $14410$ for zebrafish, $3747$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $19483$ for human, and $6351$ for budding yeast, while the valid measured-TE counts are exactly the four joined measured-TE sample sizes above.  Thus the contact is powered as a finite regression surface, but it is not identical to the broader abundance-CDS join in every organism.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
For each organism, let $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the abundance residual component that remains after the modeled tAI direction has been removed from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual projection.  The measured-TE contact is evaluated by projecting this component onto residualized measured translation efficiency under the same control surface $Z$.  The descriptive fraction reported as $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=\frac{\|\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^{2}}{\|\widehat P_{Q}\|^{2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the measured-TE joins, the observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Danio})&=0.8424312412078521,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Ecoli})&=0.6628211177352172,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Human})&=0.8566190269974241,\\
 d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Yeast})&=0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The underlying squared norms are likewise finite-row quantities: zebrafish has $\|\widehat P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467$ and $\|\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=919.9445878158525$; \textit{E. coli} has $393.78692095732885$ and $261.0102870984463$; human has $565.3850201235074$ and $484.31956581711796$; yeast has $675.3275479192903$ and $151.18264752811882$.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
The measured translation-efficiency projection absorbs only a small part of the modeled-tAI-complement component on these joins.  The absorbed fractions and combined residual fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Danio}:&\quad 0.01759429001506974,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8276090159036916,\\
 \mathrm{Ecoli}:&\quad 0.0010982956489726373,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6620871850159149,\\
 \mathrm{Human}:&\quad 0.0028947149257963897,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.854133502300174,\\
 \mathrm{Yeast}:&\quad 0.0002762783754375593,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest measured-TE absorption in this finite table is therefore the zebrafish value $0.01759429001506974$, while yeast contributes only $0.0002762783754375593$.  These values support a negative reading for the proposed first $H$-candidate: measured TE, at least in these joined panels and conditions, does not by itself account for most of the remaining non-modeled-tAI residual component.

\paragraph{Join-stability self-check.}
The measured-TE join changes the abundance-CDS population in two organisms enough that the chapter must report the join effect rather than silently treating the contact as the same surface.  Relative to the full abundance-CDS complement reference, the absolute differences in $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{Danio}}&=0.03219747984141397,\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{Ecoli}}&=0.00017488612263594217,\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{Human}}&=0.06427590857052146,\\
 \Delta_{\mathrm{Yeast}}&=0.012133464712697745.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the stated $0.02$ tolerance, \textit{E. coli} and yeast match the complement reference, while zebrafish and human do not.  The failed matches do not invalidate the computed measured-TE projection; instead, they identify a population boundary.  The measured-TE availability filter changes the target set for zebrafish and human, so those two rows are read as all-readout-join estimates rather than as stable full-join replacements.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring finite contacts.}
The measured-TE result sits next to several other bounded readouts.  A measured tRNA gene-copy tAI contact on full joins reports $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, and $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast, with tRNA absorption values $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$.  A yeast turnover contact has $n=3640$, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2232704509412724$, and turnover absorption $0.0009086031889966327$.  Reviewed feature contacts are sparse and candidate-specific: for example, human feature rows have $n=18335$ and Bonferroni excess for complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count, whereas yeast feature rows have $n=5682$ and no candidate exceeds its Bonferroni threshold.  The organism-specific meta table remains exploratory: its complete-feature row count is $12$, the dominant-coordinate versus domain permutation right-tail value is $0.5814185814185814$, and the continuous tests report two-sided permutation values $0.35264735264735264$ for $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3, $0.913086913086913$ versus log proteome size, and $0.8621378621378621$ versus log tRNA gene count.  These neighboring contacts make the same point from different angles: the residual is measurable, but the observed finite contacts do not close it as a single translation-efficiency phenomenon.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The computed contact is ready as a descriptive cross-layer record.  It supports the statement that measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency was joined to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface under explicit CDS and composition controls, and that the resulting measured-TE absorption is small in all four organisms.  It does not support a causal mediator claim.  It also does not support a general translation-realization claim, a protein-abundance claim, folding or structural-order evidence, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger conclusion requires its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic beyond the measured-TE projection reported here.
