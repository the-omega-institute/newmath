\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record considered here is a bounded cross-layer measurement: nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are tested against ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readouts after the coding-sequence controls have been fixed. The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}stress$. The controls used in the finite computation are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. In every organism row the control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, so the tested surface is not the unconstrained codon table; it is the residual codon-coordinate component left after these covariates have been taken out.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
The finite organism panel contains four measured translation-efficiency joins. For \emph{Danio rerio}, the all-readout join has $n=13140$ rows, while the CDS-abundance join reports $14410$ rows and $13140$ valid TE rows. For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the corresponding counts are $n=3415$, $3739$ CDS-abundance joined rows, $3747$ valid abundance rows, and $3415$ valid TE rows. For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the measured-TE join has $n=11554$ rows, with $19053$ CDS-abundance joined rows, $19483$ valid abundance rows, and $11554$ valid TE rows. For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the measured-TE join has $n=5128$ rows, with $5815$ CDS-abundance joined rows, $6351$ valid abundance rows, and $5128$ valid TE rows. These row counts matter because the measured-TE filter changes the protein identifier panel in some organisms; the computation therefore reports the join itself rather than treating the translation-efficiency vector as if it were available on the full abundance surface.

\paragraph{Modeled complement before measured TE.}
The measured-TE contact is applied to the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$, not to a free-standing biological interpretation of a synonymous coordinate. The pre-contact complement sizes are organism-specific. In \emph{Danio rerio}, $\|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467$, $\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2=919.9445878158525$, and $d_{modeled}=0.8424312412078521$. In \emph{E. coli}, $\|P_Q\|^2=393.78692095732885$, $\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2=261.0102870984463$, and $d_{modeled}=0.6628211177352172$. In human, $\|P_Q\|^2=565.3850201235074$, $\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2=484.31956581711796$, and $d_{modeled}=0.8566190269974241$. In yeast, $\|P_Q\|^2=675.3275479192903$, $\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2=151.18264752811882$, and $d_{modeled}=0.22386566043976477$. Thus the residual left after modeled tAI is large in zebrafish, bacteria, and human, but much smaller in yeast before the measured-TE contact is applied.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
Projecting the modeled-tAI-complement residual onto residualized measured translation efficiency gives a small descriptive absorption in all four organisms. The absorbed fractions are $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast. The corresponding combined residual fractions are $d_{combined}=0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$. In formula form, the finite readback is the following descriptive update:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(d_{modeled},d_{combined},a_{TE})_{Dr} &= (0.8424312412078521,0.8276090159036916,0.01759429001506974),\\
(d_{modeled},d_{combined},a_{TE})_{Ec} &= (0.6628211177352172,0.6620871850159149,0.0010982956489726373),\\
(d_{modeled},d_{combined},a_{TE})_{Hs} &= (0.8566190269974241,0.854133502300174,0.0028947149257963897),\\
(d_{modeled},d_{combined},a_{TE})_{Sc} &= (0.22386566043976477,0.2237928123367028,0.0002762783754375593).
\end{aligned}
$$
The contact therefore supports a narrow statement: measured ribosome-profiling TE can be attached as a first $H$-candidate readout and its additional absorption can be computed. The observed absorptions are modest, especially in yeast and \emph{E. coli}, so the non-translation residual is not dissolved by this measured-TE surface.

\paragraph{Join stability checks.}
The computation also compares the modeled-complement value on the measured-TE all-readout join to the full abundance-CDS complement reference. The absolute differences are $0.03219747984141397$ for zebrafish, $0.00017488612263594217$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.06427590857052146$ for human, and $0.012133464712697745$ for yeast. Under the $0.02$ tolerance recorded with the experiment, \emph{E. coli} and yeast match the reference join, while zebrafish and human do not. The failure is not treated as a biological contradiction; it records that measured-TE availability changes the protein identifier join. Consequently, a population-level statement about a universal measured-TE residual cannot be read from the four rows without carrying the join-change boundary.

\paragraph{Relation to measured tRNA-copy tAI.}
A separate measured tRNA gene-copy tAI contact was computed on the full CDS-abundance joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast. Its current joins are $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ rows, with tRNA gene counts $8846$, $431$, and $273$, and anticodon table sizes $57$, $47$, and $41$. Unlike the measured-TE contact, this tRNA-copy contact exactly preserves the complement reference in those three organisms: all absolute differences are $0.0$. Its absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast. This comparison is useful because it separates two readout surfaces. The measured tRNA-copy tAI readback is a modeled adaptation proxy grounded in gene-copy tables; the ribosome-profiling TE readback is a condition-specific measured translation-efficiency surface. Neither one, by itself, licenses a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Turnover contrast.}
The yeast turnover contact gives a further negative control on promotion. It joins $3640$ turnover proteins to the yeast CDS-abundance surface, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$. On that join, $d_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$, close to the full yeast complement reference $0.21173219572706703$ with absolute difference $0.011538255214205373$. The turnover absorption is $0.0009086031889966327$, and the combined value is $0.22306503721327192$. Thus, for yeast, measured TE and turnover both have bounded descriptive absorptions, but neither erases the residual. This is a cross-layer accounting fact, not a causal mediator test.

\paragraph{Organism-level heterogeneity.}
The organism-specific meta surface reinforces the same restraint. Across the complete-feature organism set, $12$ organisms are available for the meta tests, while the assembled row count is $16$ and four organisms still need data for the $M^{QTP}$-style summary. The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The $M_{QTP}$ fraction has two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$ against $GC3$ with $r=-0.3062616500469447$, $p=0.8621378621378621$ against log tRNA gene count with $r=0.06029405206037823$, and $p=0.913086913086913$ against log proteome size with $r=-0.033628115789504955$. The domain variance-ratio test reports $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$. These p-values do not resolve a domain law; they say that the current cross-organism surface is heterogeneous and exploratory.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
This chapter therefore records a measured-contact result with a conservative reading. The finite data show that a ribosome-profiling TE surface can be joined to the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel in four organisms, and that the additional measured-TE absorption of the modeled-tAI-complement residual is positive but small: about $1.76\%$ in zebrafish, $0.11\%$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.29\%$ in human, and $0.028\%$ in yeast. The stronger statement that some individual coordinate $Q_i$ has positive $S^{QT}_{i,TE}$ after matched mRNA control is not separately promoted here unless the per-coordinate controlled survival table is present. The present computation is a bounded projection accounting of measured TE against the residual complement.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
No translation mechanism, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mediation, or universal biological rule follows from these rows alone. Ribosome-profiling TE is condition-specific and has its own library, isoform, normalization, and stalling biases. Matched mRNA abundance is a required transcription-layer boundary for interpreting TE, but transcript abundance is not itself translation realization. Any claim above the measured TE projection requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. The local conclusion is only that the measured-TE contact has been computed on the stated joins, with small absorption and explicit join-instability warnings.
