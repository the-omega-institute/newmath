\subsection{Measured Translation-Efficiency Contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Residual Complement}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-residual-complement}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency gives a bounded readout for the non-translation component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual abundance signal. The carrier is a finite CDS-by-organism table with nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, joined to organism-matched measured translation-efficiency values and abundance controls. The control block $Z$ has 24 declared columns and rank 23 in each measured-TE organism. It contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The finite joins are not uniform: zebrafish contributes $n=13140$ measured-TE rows from $14410$ CDS-abundance joins, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 contributes $n=3415$ from $3739$ reported CDS joins, human contributes $n=11554$ from $19053$ reported CDS joins, and budding yeast contributes $n=5128$ from $5815$ reported CDS joins.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
For each organism, the measured-TE row asks how much of the modeled-tAI-complement residual component remains after projecting against residualized measured TE. In the finite run this is summarized by a modeled complement fraction $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, a combined residual fraction $d_{\mathrm{combined}}$, and an absorbed fraction $a_{\mathrm{TE}}$. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{model}}=0.8424312412,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8276090159,\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0175942900,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli\ K12}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{model}}=0.6628211177,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6620871850,\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0010982956,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{model}}=0.8566190270,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8541335023,\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0028947149,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{model}}=0.2238656604,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123,\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0002762784.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus measured TE absorbs a small positive fraction in every joined organism, with the largest descriptive value in zebrafish and the smallest in budding yeast. The norm entries show the same scale directly: zebrafish has $\|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505$ and $\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=919.9445878159$; E. coli has $393.7869209573$ and $261.0102870984$; human has $565.3850201235$ and $484.3195658171$; budding yeast has $675.3275479193$ and $151.1826475281$.

\paragraph{Join stability check.}
The measured-TE join is itself part of the object being measured. Relative to the full abundance-CDS complement reference, the same residual complement is stable within $0.02$ in E. coli and budding yeast, but not in zebrafish or human. The absolute differences are $0.0321974798$ for zebrafish, $0.0001748861$ for E. coli, $0.0642759086$ for human, and $0.0121334647$ for budding yeast. This prevents the row from being read as a single organism-invariant magnitude. It is instead a finite controlled contact: the measured-TE availability filter changes the protein identifier join in some organisms, and the resulting residual target must be reported on the actual all-readout rows rather than silently promoted to the full abundance join.

\paragraph{Relation to modeled tRNA readback.}
A separate measured-tRNA gene-copy tAI surface gives a useful scale comparison without changing the measured-TE claim. On the full CDS-abundance joins, the measured-tRNA packet reports $a_{\mathrm{tRNA}}=0.0208171028$ for zebrafish, $0.0000542623$ for human, and $0.0015307477$ for budding yeast, with exact complement-reference agreement on the full joins for those three organisms. The TE row differs because it is conditioned by measured ribosome-profiling availability: it uses $13140$, $11554$, and $5128$ TE-valid rows for zebrafish, human, and yeast, respectively, rather than the corresponding full abundance-CDS joins $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$. The contrast is important: modeled adaptation and measured TE are distinct readout surfaces, even when both are projected against the same nine-coordinate residual basis.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The broader organism-specific summary also argues against an over-strong reading. In the finite cross-organism meta surface, complete feature rows are available for 12 organisms out of 16 assembled rows. The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has Pearson $\chi^2=9.4$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814$ over 1000 trials. The $M_{QTP}$ fraction has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500$ against GC3 with two-sided permutation $p=0.3526473526$, $r=-0.0336281158$ against log proteome size with $p=0.9130869131$, and $r=0.0602940521$ against log tRNA gene count with $p=0.8621378621$. These finite values do not erase the measured-TE absorption entries, but they keep the cross-organism interpretation descriptive: the current panel does not resolve a universal domain, GC3, proteome-size, or tRNA-pool law for the residual contact.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The row is ready as a bounded measured-translation-efficiency contact. It supports the statement that, on the joined measured-TE rows, residualized measured TE removes a small positive fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement $B^*_{Q6}$ residual component in each of the four reported organisms. It does not support a causal mediator claim, a general translation-realization claim, a protein abundance claim, a folding or structural-order claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a functional claim, a mechanism claim, or a universal biological law. Any such assertion requires a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.
