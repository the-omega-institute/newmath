\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-residual-complement}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-by-organism surface in which the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are evaluated against measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency after matched mRNA abundance has been used to form the readout boundary.  The coordinate side has nine named directions,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f3_{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control matrix used in the computation contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each organism the recorded control-column count is $24$ and the numerical rank of the control matrix is $23$.  The organism-specific joined TE rows are $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  These are not inferred sample sizes; they are the rows surviving the CDS, abundance, and measured-TE joins.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The measured-TE calculation is framed as a bounded H-candidate test: the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ is projected against residualized measured TE.  The upstream modeled-complement fractions on the all-readout joins are
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{mod}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.8424312412078521,\\
d_{mod}(\mathrm{ecoli}) &= 0.6628211177352172,\\
d_{mod}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.8566190269974241,\\
d_{mod}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding absorbed fractions by measured TE are small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{TE}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.01759429001506974,\\
a_{TE}(\mathrm{ecoli}) &= 0.0010982956489726373,\\
a_{TE}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0028947149257963897,\\
a_{TE}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.0002762783754375593.
\end{aligned}
$$
After adding the measured-TE projection, the combined residual fractions are $0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$ in the same organism order.  Thus the measured TE contact is real and computable on the finite panel, but it absorbs only a small part of the modeled-tAI complement residual.  The strongest absorption in this panel is in \emph{Danio rerio}, where the reported decrease is about $0.0176$; the yeast value is nearly zero at this resolution.

\paragraph{Join stability check.}
The computation also records whether the all-readout join preserves the full-abundance CDS complement reference within a $0.02$ tolerance.  The check passes for \emph{E. coli} and yeast, with absolute differences $0.00017488612263594217$ and $0.012133464712697745$.  It fails for \emph{Danio rerio} and human, with absolute differences $0.03219747984141397$ and $0.06427590857052146$.  The failure is not a biological reversal; it is a row-set warning.  Measured-TE availability changes the protein-id join, so the projection target is not identical to the full-abundance complement surface in those two organisms.  The chapter therefore reports the computed projection quantities, but does not turn the TE absorption values into a stable cross-organism resolution of the non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific summary gives a useful guardrail for interpreting the finite panel.  In the associated cross-organism table, complete-feature rows exist for $12$ organisms out of a $16$-organism metadata assembly, with four organisms still needing data for the $M^{QTP}$ summary.  The dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous comparisons are similarly exploratory rather than decisive: $M^{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$; versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These metadata numbers support a bounded reading: the residual behavior is organism-dependent and not resolved here into a domain law, GC3 law, tRNA-pool law, or proteome-size law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The measured ribosome-profiling TE contact is admissible as a descriptive projection readout for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement.  It is not, on these rows, a strong absorber of the residual component left after the modeled-tAI projection.  The finite result is therefore a positive computation with a negative promotion rule: measured TE is evaluated and bounded, but the remaining residual is still open to other contacts such as matched mRNA structure beyond the TE denominator, turnover, localization, complex membership, or condition-specific assays.  No translation-realization claim beyond this measured-TE projection, no folding claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no biological-function claim, no mechanism claim, and no universality claim follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and statistic.
