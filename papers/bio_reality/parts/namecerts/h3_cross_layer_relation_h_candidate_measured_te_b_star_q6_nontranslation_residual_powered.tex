\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records the measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates before any protein-level or phenotype-level interpretation is admitted.  The finite carrier is the organism-matched coding-sequence readout surface on which nine residual coordinates are evaluated against measured translation efficiency after the usual sequence and composition controls.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control design has 24 columns, with rank $23$ in each organism after including an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The measured-TE joins contain $n=13140$ rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  These rows are the operative finite panel for this contact; larger abundance or CDS joins are reported only to show what the measured-TE availability removes.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The tested object is not direct codon usage and not raw ribosome occupancy.  It is the component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance signal left after the modeled-tAI direction has been separated, denoted here by $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$, and then projected onto residualized measured TE.  The modeled-complement fractions on the actual all-readout joins are $d_{modeled}=0.8424312412078521$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.6628211177352172$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.8566190269974241$ for human, and $0.22386566043976477$ for yeast.  In squared-norm form the same rows give $(\|P_Q\|^2,\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2)=(1092.0114815505467,919.9445878158525)$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $(393.78692095732885,261.0102870984463)$ for \emph{E. coli}, $(565.3850201235074,484.31956581711796)$ for human, and $(675.3275479192903,151.18264752811882)$ for yeast.  The measured-TE absorption is small in every organism: $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  The combined residual fractions are respectively $0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$.

\paragraph{Join diagnostics.}
The measured-TE contact is powered by row count, but not all organisms preserve the full abundance-CDS join.  For \emph{D. rerio}, the measured-TE join has $13140$ rows against a reference abundance-CDS join of $14410$, and the current modeled complement differs from the full-join complement by $0.03219747984141397$, exceeding the $0.02$ self-check tolerance.  Human is more strongly affected: $11554$ measured-TE rows against $19053$ reference rows, with absolute difference $0.06427590857052146$.  The \emph{E. coli} and yeast measured-TE joins are stable under this check: \emph{E. coli} has $3415$ current rows against $3739$ reference rows with absolute difference $0.00017488612263594217$, and yeast has $5128$ current rows against $5815$ reference rows with absolute difference $0.012133464712697745$.  Thus the finite result is best read as a measured-TE projection on its actual joined surface, not as an organism-universal statement about the full abundance-CDS panel.

\paragraph{Comparison contacts.}
The same residual-complement program has nearby finite contacts that help locate this result.  The measured tRNA-copy tAI contact uses full joins for \emph{D. rerio}, human, and yeast: $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ rows, with tRNA gene counts $8846$, $431$, and $273$ and anticodon table sizes $57$, $47$, and $41$.  Its absorption values are $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$, respectively.  The yeast turnover contact joins $3640$ turnover rows, reports a CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$ and a turnover join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$, and absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ of the modeled complement.  Protein-feature contacts are sparse and join-dependent: \emph{D. rerio} has $2242$ joined feature rows out of $3180$ feature records, human has $18335$ out of $18842$, and yeast has $5682$ out of $6156$.  In those feature tests, Bonferroni excess is not uniform: \emph{D. rerio} exceeds the null only for PTM density with observed absorption $0.014211240113472575$ against threshold $0.003775603188484381$; human exceeds for complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count; yeast exceeds none of the four feature candidates.  These comparison contacts support a narrow reading: measured TE, tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, and coarse protein features each absorb only a small part of the residual complement under their own finite joins.

\paragraph{Organism-level checks.}
The cross-organism meta rows do not convert the measured-TE projection into a universal law.  The complete-feature meta panel has $12$ organisms, with four organisms still needing data for the M-QTP fraction summaries.  Domain grouping of M-QTP fraction gives group means $0.01850668564589326$ for archaea, $0.4351192457799102$ for bacteria, and $0.3960177769015294$ for eukaryotes, but the permutation right-tail value is $0.4155844155844156$.  Correlations of M-QTP fraction with GC3, log tRNA gene count, and log protein count have $r=-0.3062616500469447$, $0.06029405206037823$, and $-0.033628115789504955$, with two-sided permutation values $0.35264735264735264$, $0.8621378621378621$, and $0.913086913086913$.  Dominant-coordinate enrichment by domain has Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ but permutation value $0.5814185814185814$ on $n=12$.  A separate localization boundary row exists for the Itzhak-style mitochondrial propensity check, with $8696$ joined human rows, $657$ mitochondrial rows, and $2000$ permutations; that row is a localization boundary, not a license to interpret this measured-TE statistic as localization, trafficking, or compartment mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
On the finite measured-TE surface, the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-tAI complement remains largely present after projection onto residualized translation efficiency.  The strongest measured-TE absorption among the four organisms is the \emph{D. rerio} value $0.01759429001506974$, followed by human at $0.0028947149257963897$, \emph{E. coli} at $0.0010982956489726373$, and yeast at $0.0002762783754375593$.  The appropriate conclusion is therefore a bounded descriptive one: measured ribosome-profiling TE is a real contact for the residual-complement question, but in this finite panel it explains only a small fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual, and the interpretation is join-sensitive in \emph{D. rerio} and human.  No causal mediation claim follows from these projections.  No translation mechanism, protein abundance statement, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, phenotype statement, or universal biological rule follows here unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and statistic.  Matched mRNA abundance is part of the TE construction and control boundary; it is not itself a translation realization or a protein-output measurement.
