\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency absorption of the residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ complement}
\label{sec:bior-q6-mrna-controlled-measured-te-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer contact between nine synonymous residual coordinates and organism-matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency values.  The coordinate side is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface
$\{\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\mathrm{f3\_stress}\}$.
The empirical side is per-CDS measured translation efficiency from ribosome profiling, joined to matched mRNA abundance so that transcript abundance remains a control boundary rather than a translation conclusion.  The control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every organism listed here.  It contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 from real CDS codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Matched rows.}
The finite panel is rank-powered for the stated control design: each organism has $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ against $24$ control columns, and the joined TE rows are well above the control rank.  The measured-TE joins are $n=13{,}140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3{,}415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11{,}554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5{,}128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding CDS joins before measured-TE availability are $14{,}410$, $3{,}739$, $19{,}053$, and $5{,}815$, respectively.  Matched abundance availability by protein is $14{,}410$, $3{,}747$, $19{,}483$, and $6{,}351$, while valid measured-TE availability by protein is $13{,}140$, $3{,}415$, $11{,}554$, and $5{,}128$.  Thus the measured-TE contact changes the row set in all four organisms, and the self-check below treats row-set change as part of the finite observation rather than as a hidden equivalence assumption.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance residual component carried by the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after the declared control block.  Let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modeled tAI translation-readout direction and let $T_{\mathrm{meas}}$ denote the measured ribosome-profiling TE readout after the same control boundary, including matched mRNA abundance.  The statistic reported here asks how much of the modeled-tAI-complement component $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ is descriptively absorbed when measured TE is added as a candidate readout:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}} &= P_Q - \Pi_{T_{\mathrm{mod}}}P_Q,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}} &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|_2^2}{\|P_Q\|_2^2},\\
\mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{measTE}} &= d_{\mathrm{modeled}}-d_{\mathrm{combined}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a descriptive projection calculation.  It is not a causal mediation test, and it is not a statement that ribosome profiling explains the biological source of the residual coordinate.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement magnitudes.}
For \emph{Danio rerio}, $\|P_Q\|_2^2=1092.0114815505467$, $\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|_2^2=919.9445878158525$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412078521$.  For \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, the corresponding values are $393.78692095732885$, $261.0102870984463$, and $0.6628211177352172$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, they are $565.3850201235074$, $484.31956581711796$, and $0.8566190269974241$.  For \emph{S. cerevisiae}, they are $675.3275479192903$, $151.18264752811882$, and $0.22386566043976477$.  These numbers show that the modeled-tAI complement is not uniformly small: it remains especially large in zebrafish, human, and bacterial rows, while the yeast complement is much lower before measured TE is tested.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
Measured TE absorbs only a small fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement residual in this run.  The absorbed fractions are $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  The combined distances after adding measured TE are $0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$.  In the finite-row interpretation, the measured-TE contact is therefore present and computed, but it does not strongly absorb the non-modeled translation residual.  The residual remains an open cross-layer component rather than being promoted to a solved translation explanation.

\paragraph{Join self-check.}
The measured-TE row set changes the modeled-complement distance in two organisms beyond the $0.02$ comparison tolerance.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the full abundance-CDS complement reference has join size $14{,}410$ and $d=0.874628721049266$, whereas the measured-TE join has size $13{,}140$ and $d=0.8424312412078521$, giving absolute difference $0.03219747984141397$ and failing the $0.02$ match check.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding sizes are $19{,}053$ and $11{,}554$, with $d=0.9208949355679455$ versus $0.8566190269974241$, absolute difference $0.06427590857052146$, again failing the $0.02$ check.  The bacterial and yeast panels pass this check: \emph{E. coli} has absolute difference $0.00017488612263594217$, and \emph{S. cerevisiae} has absolute difference $0.012133464712697745$.  The negative cases are not discarded; they mark that measured-TE availability changes the protein-id join used by the projection.

\paragraph{Relation to measured tRNA-copy tAI.}
A companion finite-row contact tests whether measured tRNA gene-copy tAI absorbs the same modeled-complement abundance component on full joined panels.  That contact uses $14{,}410$ zebrafish rows, $19{,}053$ human rows, and $5{,}815$ yeast rows, again with $24$ control columns and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.  The parsed tRNA gene counts are $8{,}846$ in zebrafish, $431$ in human, and $273$ in yeast, with anticodon table sizes $57$, $47$, and $41$.  Its absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast.  These values are close in scale to the measured-TE absorptions: both contacts are useful bounded readbacks, but neither supplies a large descriptive absorption of the residual component.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite panel supports the following restricted statement.  The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis, after amino-acid composition, organism identity through separate panels, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA abundance controls, has been tested against measured ribosome-profiling TE as a first candidate readout for the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual.  The computation is complete for four organism panels and reports small measured-TE absorption values.  This licenses a bounded cross-layer relation entry: measured TE is a real joined readout and its absorption of the non-modeled translation residual is weak in the observed rows.  It does not license a claim of general translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger assertion would require an additional layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
