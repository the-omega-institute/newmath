\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-measured-te-contact}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface against measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency on the rows where coding-sequence counts, matched abundance measurements, and translation-efficiency readouts can be joined.  The residual basis has nine named coordinates,
$\texttt{K\_AAA}$, $\texttt{Arg\_AGR}$, $\texttt{Ile\_AUA}$, $\texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $\texttt{f3\_stress}$.  The control design contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism reported here the control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, so the readout is not a rank-saturated three-row summary.

The usable measured-TE joins are finite and organism-specific: $13{,}140$ rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3{,}415$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $11{,}554$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5{,}128$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding CDS joins before the measured-TE restriction were $14{,}410$, $3{,}739$, $19{,}053$, and $5{,}815$, respectively.  Thus the measured ribosome-profiling contact changes the available row set, most visibly in human and zebrafish, and the statistic must be read as a joined-readout statistic rather than as a property of the full abundance-CDS panel.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The tested quantity is the descriptive absorption of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual by residualized measured translation efficiency.  Let $P_Q$ denote the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ component after the declared covariates, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the part not accounted for by the modeled tAI direction on the same joined rows.  The finite norms and complement fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Danio rerio:}      &\quad \|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=919.9445878158525,\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412078521,\\
\text{E. coli K-12:}     &\quad \|P_Q\|^2=393.78692095732885,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=261.0102870984463,\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.6628211177352172,\\
\text{Homo sapiens:}     &\quad \|P_Q\|^2=565.3850201235074,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=484.31956581711796,\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8566190269974241,\\
\text{S. cerevisiae:}   &\quad \|P_Q\|^2=675.3275479192903,\quad \|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=151.18264752811882,\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
Measured translation efficiency then absorbs only a small part of this complement on the available rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Danio rerio:}      &\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.01759429001506974,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8276090159036916,\\
\text{E. coli K-12:}     &\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0010982956489726373,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6620871850159149,\\
\text{Homo sapiens:}     &\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0028947149257963897,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.854133502300174,\\
\text{S. cerevisiae:}   &\quad a_{\mathrm{TE}}=0.0002762783754375593,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028.
\end{aligned}
$$
On this finite readout, measured TE is therefore a bounded H-candidate contact, not a strong absorber of the non-modeled residual.  Zebrafish has the largest measured-TE absorption among the four organisms, about $1.76\%$ of the modeled-complement residual, while the other three organisms are below $0.3\%$ and yeast is below $0.03\%$.

\paragraph{Join-stability check.}
The complement fraction was also compared against the full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The comparison passes the $0.02$ tolerance in \emph{E. coli} and yeast but not in zebrafish or human:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Danio rerio:}      &\quad 0.874628721049266\ \text{on }14{,}410\text{ rows versus }0.8424312412078521\ \text{on }13{,}140\text{ rows},\quad \Delta=0.03219747984141397,\\
\text{E. coli K-12:}     &\quad 0.6629960038578532\ \text{on }3{,}739\text{ rows versus }0.6628211177352172\ \text{on }3{,}415\text{ rows},\quad \Delta=0.00017488612263594217,\\
\text{Homo sapiens:}     &\quad 0.9208949355679455\ \text{on }19{,}053\text{ rows versus }0.8566190269974241\ \text{on }11{,}554\text{ rows},\quad \Delta=0.06427590857052146,\\
\text{S. cerevisiae:}   &\quad 0.21173219572706703\ \text{on }5{,}815\text{ rows versus }0.22386566043976477\ \text{on }5{,}128\text{ rows},\quad \Delta=0.012133464712697745.
\end{aligned}
$$
This matters for interpretation.  The measured-TE availability filter is nearly neutral for \emph{E. coli} and yeast, but it changes the target residual in zebrafish and human.  Consequently, the result is a finite contact statistic on the TE-available rows, not a row-invariant cross-organism law.

\paragraph{Relation to adjacent H-candidate contacts.}
The measured-TE contact is consistent with the broader H-candidate picture.  The measured tRNA gene-copy tAI contact, evaluated on full CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast, absorbed $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$ of the modeled complement, respectively.  The yeast turnover contact absorbed $0.0009086031889966327$ on $3{,}640$ joined turnover rows.  Protein-feature contacts were sparse and feature-dependent: zebrafish joined only $2{,}242$ feature rows from $14{,}410$ CDS rows, human joined $18{,}335$ from $19{,}053$, and yeast joined $5{,}682$ from $5{,}815$.  In those feature panels, the largest Bonferroni-exceeding descriptive absorptions were PTM density in zebrafish at $0.014211240113472575$, PTM density in human at $0.014884825920540636$, complex membership in human at $0.0075271999877804495$, and transmembrane count in human at $0.0015569064882873212$; yeast feature candidates did not exceed their reported Bonferroni thresholds.  These adjacent contacts show that the residual is not automatically promoted by naming a downstream readout.  Each contact has its own row set, rank condition, and descriptive statistic.

The organism-specific meta table also keeps the cross-organism scope modest.  It assembled $16$ organisms, with $12$ complete feature rows for the domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, and proteome-size summaries.  The dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency had $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M^{QTP}$ fraction correlations were weak under the reported permutation tests: versus GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; versus log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  A localization boundary contact was also computed on $8{,}696$ joined rows with $657$ mitochondrial entries and $2000$ permutations, but it remains a separate localization boundary rather than evidence for measured TE survival.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite measured-TE panel supports the following bounded statement: after the declared sequence-composition, length, GC3, and $M$-density controls, measured ribosome-profiling TE can be projected against the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-tAI-complement residual on four organism-specific joined panels, and the resulting absorption is small in all four organisms.  The computation is meaningful because the control rank is $23$ on all four panels and the row counts are in the thousands, but it is not a positive translation-efficiency survival theorem for individual coordinates.  The available finite rows do not contain a per-coordinate $S^{QT}_{i,\mathrm{TE}}$ matrix with a declared positive entry after matched mRNA control and simpler-baseline ablation.  They instead provide a bounded H-candidate readout: measured TE explains little of the residual left after modeled tAI, while the residual itself remains open to other layer-matched contacts.

No translation-level realization, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this contact alone.  Such a stronger statement would require a separate layer-appropriate contact and its own finite statistic on the joined rows.  Here the licensed conclusion is only that the measured-TE projection was computed on the stated finite panels, that its absorption values were bounded, and that low absorption leaves the non-translation residual unexplained rather than refuted.
