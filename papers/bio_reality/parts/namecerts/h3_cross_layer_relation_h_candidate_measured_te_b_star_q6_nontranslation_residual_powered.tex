\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-contact}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object tested here is a finite coding-sequence readout surface carrying nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency values, and matched abundance controls. The residual coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.
The readout is not a protein, structure, function, or phenotype variable. It is a controlled projection of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual onto residualized measured translation efficiency, with the claim row recorded under
$h3.cross\_layer\_relation.h\_candidate\_measured\_te.b\_star\_q6\_nontranslation\_residual\_powered$.

\paragraph{Control design.}
For every organism-specific row, the control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors. The reported design has $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each measured-TE organism panel. The joined measured-TE row counts are $13{,}140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3{,}415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $11{,}554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5{,}128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The corresponding CDS-joined counts before measured-TE availability are $14{,}410$, $3{,}739$, $19{,}053$, and $5{,}815$, so the translation-efficiency contact changes the joined surface in every organism and especially in human and zebrafish.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption values.}
Let $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ denote the residual fraction of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ panel remaining after the modeled tAI complement, and let $d_{\mathrm{combined}}$ denote the residual fraction after adding the measured-TE projection. The finite rows report
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8276090159,\quad \mathrm{absorbed}=0.0175942900,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.6628211177,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6620871850,\quad \mathrm{absorbed}=0.0010982956,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8566190270,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8541335023,\quad \mathrm{absorbed}=0.0028947149,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2238656604,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123,\quad \mathrm{absorbed}=0.0002762784.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers show a small descriptive reduction in the modeled-complement residual after measured TE is introduced. The largest reported absorption is the zebrafish value $0.0175942900$; the bacterial, human, and yeast values are much smaller. Thus the measured-TE contact is a bounded statistical readback, not a resolution of the remaining non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Join-stability self-check.}
The same row includes a self-check against the full abundance-CDS complement experiment. The current measured-TE join matches the complement reference within $0.02$ for \emph{E. coli} and yeast, with absolute differences $0.0001748861$ and $0.0121334647$. It does not match within that tolerance for zebrafish and human, with absolute differences $0.0321974798$ and $0.0642759086$. The interpretation is consequently asymmetric: the yeast and bacterial measured-TE surfaces preserve the complement target closely enough for a local descriptive comparison, while the zebrafish and human surfaces are visibly changed by translation-efficiency availability. This is not a failure of computation; it is a finite-row warning that the available measured-TE data define a different protein-id join for those organisms.

\paragraph{Relation to the measured tRNA-copy contact.}
A separate finite row,
$h3.cross\_layer\_relation.measured\_trna\_tai.b\_star\_q6\_modeled\_residual\_absorption\_powered$,
uses measured tRNA gene-copy tAI as a modeled readout on the full CDS join for zebrafish, human, and yeast. Its complement-reference equality is exact on those full joins: $14{,}410$ zebrafish rows, $19{,}053$ human rows, and $5{,}815$ yeast rows, all with absolute difference $0.0$ against the complement reference. The tRNA-copy absorption values are $0.0208171028$ for zebrafish, $0.0000542623$ for human, and $0.0015307477$ for yeast. This comparison is useful because it separates two surfaces: the measured-TE surface is a smaller ribosome-profiling availability join, whereas the tRNA-copy tAI surface is a full-join modeled-adaptation readback. Neither surface licenses the other.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a narrow descriptive statement: measured ribosome-profiling TE has been joined to the nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface under the declared controls, and the added measured-TE coordinate absorbs only a small fraction of the modeled-complement residual in all four organisms. The result is therefore a bounded cross-layer relation, not a promotion to biological mechanism. No general translation-realization claim, folded-structure claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, phenotype claim, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and statistic. The remaining residual should be read as open for other bounded contacts such as turnover, localization, or protein-state readouts, not as explained by measured translation efficiency alone.
