Protein-feature contacts also remain descriptive: reviewed-feature joins are $2242$ in zebrafish, $18335$ in human, and $5682$ in yeast, and the only large-looking feature absorptions are sparse annotation projections such as zebrafish PTM density at $0.014211240113472575$ and human PTM density at $0.014884825920540636$, not mechanism measurements. \paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite calculation supports a bounded statement: measured ribosome-profiling TE can be used as a first descriptive $H$-candidate for the modeled-tAI-complement portion of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface, and all four organisms have a positive measured-TE absorption value under the declared controls. The result is not strong enough to identify measured TE as the explanation of the non-translation residual. The largest measured-TE absorption is about $1.76\%$ in zebrafish, while the residual complement remains large in every organism, and the join self-check fails the tolerance in zebrafish and human. Therefore this chapter records a positive but limited contact: it permits a controlled TE-projection entry, while leaving most of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ complement outside this measured-TE readout.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation mechanism, complete translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causation, or universal biological rule follows from these projection entries. The TE statistic is a same-table descriptive projection under the specified covariates. Any stronger biological interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic; in particular, protein abundance, structural order, localization, turnover, function, and organismal phenotype cannot be inferred from the measured-TE absorption values alone.
