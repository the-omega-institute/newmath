\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ complement residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer question: after the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual basis has been projected away from amino-acid composition and elementary coding-sequence covariates, does measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency explain any part of the remaining abundance-side complement? The carrier is a finite CDS-by-organism table with nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. The control design contains an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 computed from the same codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. Each organism-specific design has $24$ control columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Joined organisms and rank boundary.}
The measured-translation-efficiency contact is rank-powered in the finite sense that every joined organism has many more rows than the control rank. The joined row counts are $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The corresponding CDS joins before measured-TE filtering are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, while the valid measured-TE protein counts are exactly the final joined counts. The test is therefore not the three-organism saturated surface that would make every controlled score vacuous; it is a four-organism, high-row-count readout with organism-specific loss of rows caused by measured-TE availability.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance-side projection onto the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after the declared controls, let $T_{mod}$ denote the modeled-tAI direction already tested as a translation-adaptation candidate, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the residual component of $P_Q$ perpendicular to that modeled direction. The measured-TE candidate asks how much of $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ is descriptively absorbed when residualized measured TE is added to the readout. The finite computation records
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{modeled} &= \frac{\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2},\\
 d_{combined} &= \text{the remaining fraction after adding residualized measured TE},\\
 a_{measTE} &= d_{modeled}-d_{combined}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported values are small absorptions rather than a strong measured-TE explanation. For \emph{Danio rerio}, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=1092.0114815505467$, $\lVert P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\rVert^2=919.9445878158525$, $d_{modeled}=0.8424312412078521$, $d_{combined}=0.8276090159036916$, and $a_{measTE}=0.01759429001506974$. For \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $393.78692095732885$, $261.0102870984463$, $0.6628211177352172$, $0.6620871850159149$, and $0.0010982956489726373$. For human, they are $565.3850201235074$, $484.31956581711796$, $0.8566190269974241$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.0028947149257963897$. For yeast, they are $675.3275479192903$, $151.18264752811882$, $0.22386566043976477$, $0.2237928123367028$, and $0.0002762783754375593$.

\paragraph{Self-check against the full abundance join.}
The measured-TE join is not identical to the abundance-CDS join used by the modeled-complement calculation, so the packet carries a join-sensitivity check. In \emph{Danio rerio}, the full abundance-CDS complement reference is $d=0.874628721049266$ on $14410$ rows, while the measured-TE join gives $d=0.8424312412078521$ on $13140$ rows; the absolute difference is $0.03219747984141397$, outside the $0.02$ tolerance. In \emph{E. coli}, the reference is $0.6629960038578532$ on $3739$ rows and the measured-TE join gives $0.6628211177352172$ on $3415$ rows; the absolute difference is $0.00017488612263594217$, within tolerance. In human, the reference is $0.9208949355679455$ on $19053$ rows and the measured-TE join gives $0.8566190269974241$ on $11554$ rows; the absolute difference is $0.06427590857052146$, outside tolerance. In yeast, the reference is $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ rows and the measured-TE join gives $0.22386566043976477$ on $5128$ rows; the absolute difference is $0.012133464712697745$, within tolerance. Thus the measured-TE analysis is numerically stable for \emph{E. coli} and yeast under this check, but row availability visibly changes the projection surface for zebrafish and human.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring readouts.}
The measured-tRNA gene-copy tAI contact uses the same nine-coordinate residual basis and the same $24$-column, rank-$23$ control shape on the full CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast. Its absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast. These values are of the same descriptive scale as the measured-TE absorptions and do not convert the residual into an observed ribosome-occupancy mechanism, because tRNA gene copy number is only a proxy for abundance and tAI depends on a wobble-weighting model. A separate yeast turnover contact joins $3640$ turnover rows, with $d_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$, $d_{combined}=0.22306503721327192$, and turnover absorption $0.0009086031889966327$. The turnover row set has a CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$ and a turnover join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$, using SGD locus systematic names mapped to STRING identifiers by prefixing $4932.$. This nearby yeast result reinforces the local interpretation: the residual complement is not substantially absorbed by the measured post-transcriptional candidates tested here.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite result is a descriptive cross-layer contact, not a causal promotion. Measured TE absorbs about $1.76\%$ of the modeled-tAI complement in zebrafish, about $0.11\%$ in \emph{E. coli}, about $0.29\%$ in human, and about $0.028\%$ in yeast. The result therefore licenses the following local statement only: on the joined finite panels, residualized measured translation efficiency was computed against the declared controls and has small organism-specific descriptive absorption of $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$. It does not establish that a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate causes translation, nor that translation efficiency mediates protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality. Any such assertion would require its own matched readout layer and its own controlled statistic. If the measured-TE absorption remains low under stronger panels, the correct interpretation is that the non-modeled residual remains open to other bounded contacts, such as matched mRNA structure, protein turnover, localization, complex membership, condition-specific regulation, or other post-transcriptional measurements; it is not a license to treat the residual as biologically irrelevant.
