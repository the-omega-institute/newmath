\subsection{Measured Translation-Efficiency Contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Residual Basis}
\label{sec:q6-mrna-controlled-measured-te-contact}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
The contact studied here is a finite CDS-by-organism matrix in which nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are tested against matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency values after sequence and transcript controls. The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.
The control design has 24 columns before rank reduction and uses an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. In all four measured-TE organisms the reported control rank is $23$, so the readout is not an under-rank three-row surface.

\paragraph{Joined rows and control rank.}
The measured translation-efficiency contact joins four organisms. For \emph{Danio rerio}, the TE-valid panel has $n=13140$ rows, compared with $14410$ CDS rows joined to abundance, and the rank of $Z$ is $23$. For \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the TE-valid panel has $n=3415$ rows, with $3739$ CDS rows joined to abundance and $3747$ abundance-valid protein records. For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the TE-valid panel has $n=11554$ rows, compared with $19053$ CDS rows joined to abundance and $19483$ abundance-valid proteins. For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the TE-valid panel has $n=5128$ rows, compared with $5815$ CDS rows joined to abundance and $6351$ abundance-valid proteins. These row counts make the contact an actual measured-readout test rather than a rank-saturated three-organism proxy.

\paragraph{Projection quantities.}
The measured-TE statistic is a descriptive projection of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ onto residualized measured TE. The modeled complement ratios on the all-readout join are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{modeled}(Danio) &= 0.8424312412078521,\\
 d_{modeled}(E.\ coli) &= 0.6628211177352172,\\
 d_{modeled}(Homo) &= 0.8566190269974241,\\
 d_{modeled}(Saccharomyces) &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding squared-norm inputs are $1092.0114815505467$ and $919.9445878158525$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $393.78692095732885$ and $261.0102870984463$ for \emph{E. coli}, $565.3850201235074$ and $484.31956581711796$ for human, and $675.3275479192903$ and $151.18264752811882$ for yeast. Thus the upstream residual target is large for vertebrate and bacterial panels, but substantially smaller in yeast before the measured-TE projection is considered.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
Measured translation efficiency absorbs only a small part of the modeled-tAI-complement residual in this contact. The absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{measTE}(Danio) &= 0.01759429001506974,\\
 A_{measTE}(E.\ coli) &= 0.0010982956489726373,\\
 A_{measTE}(Homo) &= 0.0028947149257963897,\\
 A_{measTE}(Saccharomyces) &= 0.0002762783754375593.
\end{aligned}
$$
After adding the measured-TE direction, the combined residual ratios are $0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$ in the same organism order. The largest measured-TE absorption is therefore in zebrafish, about $1.76\%$ of the modeled-complement target, while the other three panels are near or below $0.3\%$. The finite-row conclusion is not that the $B^*_{Q6}$ basis realizes translation efficiency strongly; rather, measured TE is a weak descriptive absorber for this particular residual target.

\paragraph{Join-stability check.}
The self-check against the full abundance-CDS complement reference is mixed. In \emph{E. coli}, the current all-readout join gives $d_{modeled}=0.6628211177352172$ against the reference $0.6629960038578532$, an absolute difference of $0.00017488612263594217$, which is inside the $0.02$ tolerance. In yeast, the current value $0.22386566043976477$ differs from the reference $0.21173219572706703$ by $0.012133464712697745$, also inside tolerance. In zebrafish, the current value $0.8424312412078521$ differs from $0.874628721049266$ by $0.03219747984141397$, and in human the current value $0.8566190269974241$ differs from $0.9208949355679455$ by $0.06427590857052146$; both fail the $0.02$ join-stability band. The vertebrate TE availability filter therefore changes the protein-id join enough that their measured-TE absorptions should be read as all-readout-join projections, not as stable full-join complements.

\paragraph{Comparison with neighboring bounded contacts.}
The tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact uses the full abundance-CDS join in three organisms and has exact complement-match checks: $14410$ zebrafish rows, $19053$ human rows, and $5815$ yeast rows, all with absolute difference $0$ from the complement reference. Its absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast. The yeast turnover contact joins $3640$ half-life rows, has a complement difference $0.011538255214205373$ within tolerance, and absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$. Protein-feature readouts are much more availability-sensitive: zebrafish has only $2242$ joined feature rows and its feature-join complement differs from the full reference by $0.1384608720516286$, while human has $18335$ joined rows and yeast has $5682$. Bonferroni-excess feature absorptions appear for zebrafish PTM density, human complex membership, human PTM density, and human transmembrane count, but the sparse and uneven feature joins prevent those feature contacts from repairing the measured-TE boundary.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific table contains $16$ assembled rows, of which $12$ have complete feature rows for the reported meta tests. The domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The $M_{QTP}$ fraction correlations are also descriptive rather than resolved: against GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; against log protein count, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; against log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$. The domain variance-ratio test gives $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$. These finite numbers support heterogeneity bookkeeping, not a universal organism-level rule.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
This chapter records a measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis under matched mRNA and sequence controls. The strongest local fact is that the computation was performed on rank-sufficient finite joins and that measured TE absorbs only small fractions of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual. The result is a bounded cross-layer relation: it measures how much a residualized TE direction accounts for a residual abundance component after the declared covariates. It does not license a claim that the residual coordinates cause translation, encode a ribosome mechanism, determine protein abundance, imply folding or structural order, establish physical admissibility, realize biological function, produce phenotype, or state a universal biological law. Any such higher-layer assertion would need its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
