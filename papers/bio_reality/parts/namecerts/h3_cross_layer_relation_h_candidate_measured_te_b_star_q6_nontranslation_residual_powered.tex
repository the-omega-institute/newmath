\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-residual-complement}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface leaves a measurable contact with ribosome-profiling translation efficiency after the usual coding-sequence controls and after the matched transcript layer has been kept inside the boundary of the readout.  The tested coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_{3}$-stress.  The covariate matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the same real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying in the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  Matched mRNA abundance is not treated as a positive translation observation; it fixes the denominator-side boundary needed to interpret translation efficiency rather than transcript abundance.

\paragraph{Joined finite rows.}
The measured-TE test is a finite joined-row calculation over four organisms.  The joined sample sizes are $n=13140$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  In all four cases the control design has $24$ columns with rank $23$, so the readout is not an under-rank three-row artifact.  The corresponding reported CDS joins before TE availability are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, respectively, while the valid TE-by-protein counts are exactly the four joined row counts above.  Thus the calculation is powered as a row-wise measured-readout contact, but its row set is still the TE-available subset rather than the full abundance-CDS join.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement target.}
The quantity projected onto measured TE is the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual, written here as $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.  On the TE-available joins the complement fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{mod}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.8424312412078521,\\
 d_{mod}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.6628211177352172,\\
 d_{mod}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.8566190269974241,\\
 d_{mod}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The underlying squared norms are also finite-row quantities: for \textit{D. rerio}, $\|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467$ and $\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^2=919.9445878158525$; for \textit{E. coli}, $393.78692095732885$ and $261.0102870984463$; for \textit{H. sapiens}, $565.3850201235074$ and $484.31956581711796$; and for yeast, $675.3275479192903$ and $151.18264752811882$.  The self-check against the full abundance-CDS complement join passes within $0.02$ only for \textit{E. coli} and yeast: the absolute differences are $0.03219747984141397$, $0.00017488612263594217$, $0.06427590857052146$, and $0.012133464712697745$.  The zebrafish and human failures do not invalidate the finite TE projection, but they show that TE availability changes the protein-id join enough that the target residual is not identical to the full abundance-CDS complement target.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
After the same residualization boundary, the measured translation-efficiency contact absorbs only a small part of the modeled-tAI complement residual:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{TE}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.01759429001506974, & d_{combined} &= 0.8276090159036916,\\
 A_{TE}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.0010982956489726373, & d_{combined} &= 0.6620871850159149,\\
 A_{TE}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.0028947149257963897, & d_{combined} &= 0.854133502300174,\\
 A_{TE}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.0002762783754375593, & d_{combined} &= 0.2237928123367028.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest measured-TE absorption is therefore the zebrafish value, about $1.76\%$ of the residual target under this descriptive projection.  The bacterial, human, and yeast values are below $0.3\%$ except for the zebrafish case.  This supports a narrow conclusion: measured ribosome-profiling TE was successfully joined to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate system and produces bounded absorption numbers, but the measured TE readout does not collapse the modeled-tAI complement residual in any organism in this panel.

\paragraph{Comparison with the measured tRNA-copy readout.}
A separate measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact uses the full abundance-CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast: $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ rows, respectively, again with a $24$-column, rank-$23$ control design.  Its absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast.  This comparison is useful only as scale calibration.  It says that the modeled-readout and measured-TE contacts are not interchangeable: the zebrafish tRNA-copy absorption is slightly larger than measured TE, human measured TE is larger than the tRNA-copy value but still small, and yeast tRNA-copy absorption exceeds measured TE while both remain very small.  Neither contact supplies a causal mediator test.

\paragraph{Cross-organism scope.}
The broader organism-specific table also blocks a universal interpretation.  In the finite meta table, the dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  Continuous tests for the $M^{QTP}$ fraction similarly do not yield a resolved global law: versus GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and versus log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These numbers keep the measured-TE result in the local contact class rather than promoting it to a domain-level or organism-universal rule.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row result is ready as a bounded cross-layer relation: the measured ribosome-profiling TE surface was joined, residualized, and projected against the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual on four rank-powered organism panels.  The positive absorption values are descriptive survival-style readbacks of the measured TE contact, with the strongest value in zebrafish and near-zero values in the other three organisms.  They do not establish a causal translation mechanism, mature protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  Such conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics; the present record licenses only the finite measured-TE projection and the associated non-promotion boundary.
