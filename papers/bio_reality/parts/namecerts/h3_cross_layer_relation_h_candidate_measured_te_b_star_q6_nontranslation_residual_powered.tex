\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-contact}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a deliberately narrow contact between the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency.  The tested coordinate list is $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control table has 24 columns and rank 23 in each organism: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from real CDS codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The organism-specific joined row counts are 13,140 for $Danio\ rerio$, 3,415 for $Escherichia\ coli$ K12 MG1655, 11,554 for $Homo\ sapiens$, and 5,128 for $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$.  These counts are the measured-TE joins, not the larger CDS-abundance joins: the corresponding reported CDS joins are 14,410, 3,739, 19,053, and 5,815.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The measured-TE experiment asks whether the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ is further shortened by residualized measured translation efficiency.  The finite calculation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{modeled}}(Danio) &= 0.8424312412078521, & D_{\mathrm{combined}}(Danio) &= 0.8276090159036916, \\
D_{\mathrm{modeled}}(E.\ coli) &= 0.6628211177352172, & D_{\mathrm{combined}}(E.\ coli) &= 0.6620871850159149, \\
D_{\mathrm{modeled}}(Human) &= 0.8566190269974241, & D_{\mathrm{combined}}(Human) &= 0.854133502300174, \\
D_{\mathrm{modeled}}(Yeast) &= 0.22386566043976477, & D_{\mathrm{combined}}(Yeast) &= 0.2237928123367028.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the absorbed fractions attributable to measured TE on this joined surface are 0.01759429001506974 in $Danio\ rerio$, 0.0010982956489726373 in $E.\ coli$ K12 MG1655, 0.0028947149257963897 in human, and 0.0002762783754375593 in budding yeast.  All four entries are positive as descriptive projections, but their magnitudes are small; the strongest finite entry is the fish contact, and even there the reduction is only about 1.76 percent of the modeled-complement residual norm on the TE-available join.

\paragraph{Join stability check.}
The contact is not a population-level law because the measured-TE availability changes the row set in two organisms.  Against the full abundance-CDS complement reference, the absolute differences in $D_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ are 0.03219747984141397 for $Danio\ rerio$, 0.00017488612263594217 for $E.\ coli$ K12 MG1655, 0.06427590857052146 for human, and 0.012133464712697745 for budding yeast.  The $0.02$ self-check is passed in $E.\ coli$ and yeast, and fails in fish and human.  Thus the measured-TE contact is a real finite calculation, but its cross-organism interpretation is bounded by row-set movement.  The conclusion is not that translation efficiency explains the residual complement; it is that the TE readout produces a small, positive, organism-specific descriptive absorption on the available joined rows.

\paragraph{Comparison with adjacent readouts.}
The adjacent measured-tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact used the full CDS-abundance joins for fish, human, and yeast: 14,410, 19,053, and 5,815 rows, with $D_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ matching the complement reference exactly in those three organisms.  Its absorbed fractions were 0.020817102845637092 in fish, 0.00005426229300865148 in human, and 0.001530747712165342 in yeast.  The yeast turnover contact, by contrast, used 3,640 joined half-life rows and reported $D_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2232704509412724$ with turnover absorption 0.0009086031889966327.  These adjacent finite contacts place the measured-TE result in scale: the TE absorption is of the same small descriptive order as measured tRNA and turnover readbacks, and none of the three supplies a causal mediator test by itself.

\paragraph{Cross-organism status.}
The organism-specificity survey remains exploratory.  Its complete-feature table contains 12 organisms, with four additional organisms needing data for the $M^{QTP}$ comparison.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ against GC3 with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ against log proteome size with $p=0.913086913086913$, and $r=0.06029405206037823$ against log tRNA-gene count with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The dominant-coordinate-versus-domain contingency has $n=12$, $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$.  These values do not license a domain, GC3, tRNA-pool, or proteome-size mechanism for the measured-TE contact.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The supported statement is restricted to a finite projection calculation: measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency was joined to CDS-derived $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and controls, and it yielded small positive absorbed fractions on the joined rows.  This does not imply ribosome-level mechanism, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, universality, or a global biological rule.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, a $Q_i$ coordinate that survives this measured-TE projection remains only a translation-efficiency association on a finite surface; it is not a protein-output, structure, function, or mechanism claim.
