\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-residual-complement}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record concerns the finite CDS-by-organism surface on which the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are joined to ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readouts.  The coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each organism, the projection uses $24$ control columns with numerical rank $23$.  The controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 computed from real CDS codon counts, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The measured translation-efficiency variable is the ribosome-footprint readout normalized by matched mRNA abundance, so the contact is treated as a bounded translation-efficiency surface rather than as raw transcript abundance or mature protein abundance.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank condition.}
The powered rows are finite and organism-specific.  The joined translation-efficiency panels contain $n=13140$ CDS rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding CDS joins before the measured-TE restriction are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, respectively.  Valid translation-efficiency counts match the restricted panels: $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.  Valid abundance-by-protein counts are $14410$, $3747$, $19483$, and $6351$.  Thus the readout is not a rank-degenerate three-row surface: each organism has rank $23$ in the declared control design and thousands of same-organism joined observations.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement target.}
The target being tested is not a direct $Q_i$ to phenotype claim.  The computation first forms the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual, denoted here by $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, and then asks how much of that complement is descriptively absorbed by residualized measured translation efficiency.  On the measured-TE join, the finite squared norms and complement fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=1092.0114815505467,\quad
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=919.9445878158525,\\
&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412078521,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=393.78692095732885,\quad
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=261.0102870984463,\\

\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}:&\quad \|P_Q\|^2=565.3850201235074,\quad
\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2=484.31956581711796,\\
&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8566190269974241,\\

\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers show that the residual complement is large in zebrafish, human, and \emph{E. coli}, but much smaller in yeast on this particular all-readout join.

\paragraph{Measured-TE absorption.}
The measured translation-efficiency projection absorbs only a small fraction of the modeled-tAI complement in every organism.  The computed absorption entries are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad a_{\mathrm{measTE}}=0.01759429001506974,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8276090159036916,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad a_{\mathrm{measTE}}=0.0010982956489726373,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6620871850159149,\\
\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}:&\quad a_{\mathrm{measTE}}=0.0028947149257963897,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.854133502300174,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\quad a_{\mathrm{measTE}}=0.0002762783754375593,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123367028.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the strongest measured-TE absorption in this packet is the zebrafish value, about $1.76\%$ of the complement, while the other three values are near zero on the same descriptive scale.  The computation therefore supports a bounded statement: measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency was tested as an $H$-candidate, the relevant projections were numerically evaluated, and the observed measured-TE absorption is small rather than closure-producing.

\paragraph{Join-stability self-check.}
The measured-TE restriction changes the protein identifier join in two of the four organisms enough to alter the modeled-complement fraction relative to the full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The finite self-check compares the all-readout join against the complement reference join.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ changes from $0.874628721049266$ on $14410$ reference rows to $0.8424312412078521$ on $13140$ measured-TE rows, an absolute difference of $0.03219747984141397$, which exceeds the $0.02$ tolerance.  For \emph{E. coli}, it changes from $0.6629960038578532$ on $3739$ reference rows to $0.6628211177352172$ on $3415$ rows, an absolute difference of $0.00017488612263594217$, within tolerance.  For human, it changes from $0.9208949355679455$ on $19053$ reference rows to $0.8566190269974241$ on $11554$ measured-TE rows, an absolute difference of $0.06427590857052146$, outside tolerance.  For yeast, it changes from $0.21173219572706703$ on $5815$ reference rows to $0.22386566043976477$ on $5128$ measured-TE rows, an absolute difference of $0.012133464712697745$, within tolerance.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The organism-specificity meta table supplies exploratory context rather than a universal law.  It assembled $16$ organism rows, with $12$ complete-feature organisms for the $M^{QTP}$ summaries.  The dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M^{QTP}$ fraction has weak finite correlations with GC3, tRNA gene count, and proteome size: $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with GC3 and two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$; $r=0.06029405206037823$ with log tRNA gene count and value $0.8621378621378621$; and $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with log proteome size and value $0.913086913086913$.  The domain comparison reports a variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $0.4155844155844156$.  These values are useful as finite diagnostics of heterogeneity, but they do not establish phylogenetically corrected, causal, or universal organism-level structure.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row computation is ready for the standalone BioReality paper as a bounded cross-layer relation.  It says that a measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency surface was joined to the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis under a rank-$23$ control design, and that the descriptive measured-TE absorption of the modeled-tAI complement is small: $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$ across the four tested organisms.  The result is therefore a measured-contact boundary, not a promotion to downstream biology.  No translation mechanism, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causation, or universality follows from these entries unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant rows and a separate statistic is computed for that layer.
