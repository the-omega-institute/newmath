\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record fixes a finite cross-layer readout surface whose rows are coding sequences with matched codon counts, protein identifiers, abundance rows, and ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency values.  The residual side is the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ panel
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Each coordinate is read only after removing the declared control surface: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The organism-specific design matrices all have 24 control columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Joined translation-efficiency rows.}
The measured-TE contact is powered on four organism panels.  The joined row counts are $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding CDS joins before the measured-TE restriction are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, while valid TE rows are exactly $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.  These numbers make the contact a measured all-readout join rather than a full CDS-abundance join.  The self-check therefore records whether the modeled complement remains stable under the TE availability restriction: \emph{E. coli} differs from the full-join complement by $0.0001748861$ and yeast by $0.0121334647$, both within $0.02$, whereas zebrafish differs by $0.0321974798$ and human by $0.0642759086$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
For each organism, the tested quantity is the projection of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto residualized measured TE.  The finite values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{model}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.8424312412,& a_{TE}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.0175942900,& d_{combined}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.8276090159,\\
 d_{model}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.6628211177,& a_{TE}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.0010982956,& d_{combined}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.6620871850,\\
 d_{model}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.8566190270,& a_{TE}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0028947149,& d_{combined}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.8541335023,\\
 d_{model}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.2238656604,& a_{TE}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.0002762784,& d_{combined}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.2237928123.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $a_{TE}$ is the descriptive fraction absorbed by measured TE after the residualization boundary.  The largest measured-TE absorption in this panel is zebrafish, $0.0175942900$, and the smallest is yeast, $0.0002762784$.  None of these values is a causal mediation estimate; they are finite projection summaries on the stated joins.

\paragraph{Comparison contacts.}
A measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact supplies a separate bounded adaptation readout on full CDS joins: zebrafish has $14410$ joined rows, $8846$ tRNA genes, and $57$ anticodon-GCN table entries; human has $19053$ joined rows, $431$ tRNA genes, and $47$ entries; yeast has $5815$ joined rows, $273$ tRNA genes, and $41$ entries.  Its absorbed fractions are $0.0208171028$ for zebrafish, $0.0000542623$ for human, and $0.0015307477$ for yeast.  This comparison shows that the measured-TE contact is not just a restatement of the modeled or gene-copy adaptation contact: the TE-restricted joins and absorbed fractions have their own finite profile.  A yeast turnover contact gives another downstream check with $3640$ joined turnover proteins, a reported CDS-join hit rate $0.9156038419$, a turnover join hit rate $0.8818572657$, $3537$ rows jointly carrying measured TE, turnover, abundance, and CDS information, and turnover absorption $0.0009086032$.  The turnover result is also not promoted into the measured-TE statistic; it only marks a separate candidate surface.

\paragraph{Organism-level context.}
The cross-organism meta table remains exploratory.  Among $12$ complete-feature organisms, the dominant-coordinate-by-domain permutation test has right-tail $p=0.5814185814$ with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785$, and the continuous tests give $p=0.3526473526$ for $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3, $p=0.9130869131$ versus log proteome size, and $p=0.8621378621$ versus log tRNA-gene count.  The domain variance-ratio test has right-tail $p=0.4155844156$.  These values do not supply a domain law or a phylogenetically corrected inference; they only report how heterogeneous the organism summaries look in the available finite table.  A localization-boundary run supplies still another layer with $8696$ joined rows, $657$ mitochondrial labels, and $2000$ permutations in $11.034382$ seconds, but that contact does not replace the measured-TE join or license a localization conclusion for this chapter.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The measured ribosome-profiling TE row set gives a real, finite test of whether the modeled-tAI-complement residual is reduced by a matched TE readout after the stated controls.  The answer is bounded rather than decisive: the descriptive absorption is positive in all four organism panels, but small, and two organisms fail the $0.02$ full-join complement stability self-check because TE availability changes the row set.  Thus the record supports a narrow statement: measured TE can be evaluated as a first candidate contact for the non-translation residual complement, and the computed absorption is reported organism by organism.  It does not establish a general translation mechanism, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, causation, or universality.  Any such interpretation requires a distinct layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
