\subsection{Measured translation-efficiency contact for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-measured-te-contact}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The row object in this chapter is a finite coding-sequence panel in which nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are joined to organism-matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readouts and matched transcript-abundance controls.  The coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{AUA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,\mathrm{vs}\,UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,\mathrm{vs}\,UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,\mathrm{vs}\,AGY}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,\mathrm{vs}\,UCG}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,\mathrm{vs}\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}$.  The control design has twenty-four columns and numerical rank $23$ in each joined organism panel.  The controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The matched row counts are $13{,}140$ for zebrafish, $3{,}415$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $11{,}554$ for human, and $5{,}128$ for budding yeast.  The corresponding CDS joins before measured-TE restriction were $14{,}410$, $3{,}739$, $19{,}053$, and $5{,}815$, so the measured readout contact changes the usable finite surface rather than merely adding a column to an unchanged abundance panel.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
For each organism, the measured-TE contact was evaluated by projecting the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto residualized measured translation efficiency.  The reported complement fraction $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ and the combined residual fraction after the measured-TE projection were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{zebrafish}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8424312412,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8276090159,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.6628211177,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.6620871850,\\
\mathrm{human}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.8566190270,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8541335023,\\
\mathrm{yeast}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2238656604,\quad d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.2237928123.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the measured-TE absorption fractions were $0.0175942900$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ in human, and $0.0002762784$ in yeast.  These are finite descriptive projection quantities, not causal mediator estimates.  The largest measured-TE absorption in this panel is therefore zebrafish at about $1.76\%$ of the modeled-complement residual target; the other three organisms have substantially smaller measured-TE absorption on the joined readout surface.

\paragraph{Join stability check.}
The same computation records whether the measured-TE all-readout join preserves the full-abundance complement target within an absolute tolerance of $0.02$.  The self-check passes for \emph{E. coli}, where the absolute difference is $0.0001748861$, and for yeast, where the absolute difference is $0.0121334647$.  It fails for zebrafish and human, with absolute differences $0.0321974798$ and $0.0642759086$.  Thus the positive finite computation is best read as a contact-specific measured-TE absorption table, not as a stable cross-organism theorem about the unchanged abundance complement.  In particular, the human measured-TE join drops from $19{,}053$ CDS-joined rows to $11{,}554$ measured-TE rows, and the zebrafish join drops from $14{,}410$ to $13{,}140$ rows; those restrictions are already large enough to alter the target residual geometry in the self-check.

\paragraph{Relation to adjacent readouts.}
The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact supplies a nearby modeled-readout comparison on the full CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast.  Its measured-tRNA absorption fractions are $0.0208171028$, $0.0000542623$, and $0.0015307477$, respectively, with exact agreement between the current full-join $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ and the complement reference in all three organisms.  The yeast turnover contact, by contrast, uses $3{,}640$ joined half-life rows, has a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419$ and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572657$, and absorbs only $0.0009086032$ of the yeast modeled-complement residual.  These adjacent finite contacts sharpen the interpretation of the measured-TE table: translation-efficiency, tRNA-copy tAI, and turnover are separable descriptive readouts, and none of them licenses promotion to protein amount, folding, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law without an additional layer-matched contact and statistic.

\paragraph{Population scope.}
A broader organism-specificity table remains exploratory.  Among the complete-feature rows, the domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency used $n=12$ organisms and gave Pearson $\chi^2=9.4$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M^{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 comparison had Pearson $r=-0.3062616500$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.3526473526$, while comparisons against log proteome size and log tRNA-gene count had $r=-0.0336281158$ with $p=0.9130869131$ and $r=0.0602940521$ with $p=0.8621378621$.  These numbers do not support a domain-level or continuous-proxy population law for the measured-TE contact.  They only record that the row-wise $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis can be carried into finite organism panels and tested under explicit rank and control boundaries.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
This chapter is ready as a bounded cross-layer relation record.  The finite computation shows that measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency can be joined to the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis with matched mRNA control and standard CDS covariates, and that the resulting measured-TE absorption is small but computable in four organisms.  The correct conclusion is not that $B^*_{Q6}$ determines translation, protein abundance, structure, function, mechanism, or universality.  The correct conclusion is narrower: on the stated finite panels, measured TE removes a quantified fraction of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual, with join instability in zebrafish and human and low absorption in all four organisms.  Any stronger biological statement would require a separate finite contact at the relevant layer, with its own same-row joins, covariates, rank check, and null comparison.
