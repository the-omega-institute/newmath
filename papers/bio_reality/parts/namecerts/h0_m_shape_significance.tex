\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The biological contact used here is deliberately narrow: a curated table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The finite coordinate packet then asks whether the local window-six arrangement singles out a stable boundary tile around the label readback, rather than whether the code table itself explains translation or protein realization. In this row the distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records the punctured-cube face data by the vector $(13,16,4)$, so the relevant tile is not being described only by a codon count. It carries a small incidence profile: thirteen vertices, sixteen local adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the chosen codon-cube coordinates.

\paragraph{Shape test against shuffled label structure.}
The main finite-row test compares the observed local shape with $2000$ distinct sampled alternatives under seed $42$. The sampler required $57377$ sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, which is a useful warning that the null space being explored is finite and highly repetitive. Within the accepted sample, exactly one sampled table had the same shape as the observed row. Thus the exact-shape tail reported by the run is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A looser overlap criterion, Jaccard score at least $0.9$, had $25$ matches among the same $2000$ accepted samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both numbers support the modest reading that the observed local codon-label tile is not a common outcome of this sampled label-preserving geometry. They do not by themselves identify a molecular cause, an evolutionary route, or a necessary law for biological coding.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row keeps amino-acid class information in view. In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$-region has size $20$. No exact class-preserving sampled table matched the observed shape, so the reported exact count is $0$ and the displayed exact sampled tail is $0.0$. The lower-tail check counted $7$ sampled tables with $M$-size at most the observed $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite claim in a specific way: the signal is not erased when the null model respects the amino-acid class partition recorded in the table. The row still remains a code-layer observation, because the contact being tested is the codon-to-label table and not a translation apparatus, ribosomal dynamics, folded structure, energetic admissibility, or organism-level function.
