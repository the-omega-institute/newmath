\subsection{Window-six codon shape and local label boundary}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is represented as a finite coordinate point and the only biological labels admitted into the record are the curated amino-acid or stop labels of the standard code.  The claim is deliberately local: window-six coordinates may expose a small boundary-tile pattern in the code-label readback, but the label table itself remains an empirical contact rather than an internally generated biological mechanism.

The finite boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The enumerated codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is reported as a punctured-cube shape with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite evidence is not merely a count: it includes the explicit codon membership of the boundary row and a local tile signature with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo shape test.}
The observed $M$-shape row has size $20$.  In the unrestricted finite sampling test, $N=2000$ distinct comparison samples were retained.  Because the sampling was constrained to distinct rows, the run made $57377$ sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only one sampled row had the exact observed shape, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold was also sparse: $25$ sampled rows reached Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both scalars are finite-row p-values for the coordinate shape, not claims about translation, protein structure, viability, or organism-level function.

A second null test preserves amino-acid class information.  In that comparison, again with $N=2000$, there were no exact shape matches.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, with observed size $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving null therefore agrees with the unrestricted test that the displayed local shape is unusually constrained among the tested finite alternatives.  This conclusion is about the code-read geometry of the curated table; it does not turn the coordinate pattern into a biological necessity.

\begin{aligned}

\mu&=3.051487585298128.

The cubic residual for the radial relation is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  This supplies an internal numerical check on the finite coordinate construction: the local shape, its boundary row, and the spectral summary cohere to numerical precision.  It does not supply a physical Hamiltonian, folding energy, ribosomal process, or fitness model.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
Two finite leave-one probes measure how stable the code-layer boundary is under local omissions.  In the leave-one-code test, $23$ tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  These figures indicate that the local coordinate boundary is predictive for held-out code-label positions relative to the tested baseline, while staying entirely within the codon-label layer.

In the leave-one-codon test, the recovery rate was $1.0$.  The row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, and $8$ predictable cases.  The statement supported by these counts is narrow: omitted codons from the tested finite row can be recovered through the recorded local boundary and closure conditions.  The counts do not observe peptide synthesis, folded conformation, physical admissibility, biological activity, or cross-organism universality.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for checking whether the six-coordinate local tile splits the labels in the reported way.  It is not a contact for translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or an organism-independent law.  Any promotion beyond the code-label layer would require a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.  In particular, reading an amino-acid label sequence from the table is not evidence that a protein folds, is physically admissible, performs a function, or is realized by a biological process in any specified organism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite rows support a ready code-layer claim.  The boundary row $R$ is explicitly enumerated, has size $13$, and carries punctured-cube data $(13,16,4)$.  The observed $M$-shape of size $20$ is rare under both the unrestricted and amino-acid-class-preserving tests, with exact-shape p-values $0.0005$ and $0.0$ respectively, and the high-overlap unrestricted p-value is $0.0125$.  Spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$ give a consistent numerical summary of the same finite coordinate object.  Leave-one and module rows show strong finite recovery and complete assignment for the reported codon modules.  The resulting statement is that window-six codon coordinates expose a significant local boundary-tile structure in the curated standard-code label table, while all higher biological interpretations remain outside the supported scope.
