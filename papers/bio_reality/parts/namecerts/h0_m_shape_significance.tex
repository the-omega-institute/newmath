\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile and Standard-Code Label Readback}
\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The claim concerns a six-coordinate codon display, written here as a finite cube $Q_6$, whose observed row is the standard genetic-code table at the label level. The biological datum used in this chapter is only the curated assignment of each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The carrier has four relevant distinctions: the six-bit codon coordinate, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class. These distinctions let the codon table be read as finite combinatorial data, but they do not by themselves provide a biochemical mechanism. In particular, the code-layer observation is not a translation experiment, not a folding experiment, not a physical-admissibility assay, not a functional assay, and not a universality theorem.

\paragraph{The punctured local region.}
The distinguished region $R$ has cardinality $13$. The verified codon list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this region has punctured-cube face data with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the finite pattern is not merely a count of codons: it also carries a low-dimensional boundary shape inside the window-six coordinate system. The supplied row does not include a complete list for the companion set $M$, but it does give an observed size $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class preserving null test. The chapter therefore treats the concrete $R$ list, the $f$-vector, and the scalar $M$ measurements as the finite evidence actually present in the row.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the sampled null model for the $M$-shape row, the experiment used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Only $1$ sampled case exactly matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\ge 0.9}&=0.0125,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the high-overlap count was $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$. The amino-acid-class preserving null row is even sharper for exact shape: among $N=2000$ samples, the exact-match count was $0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed size $|M|=20$ was $7$. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers justify the local statement that the observed code-label shape is unusually placed relative to the tested finite nulls. They do not justify promoting the pattern beyond code-label readback.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral computation supplies three scalars for the same finite geometry:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial check reports a polynomial residual of approximately $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic relation. This is small enough to function as a numerical consistency check on the finite coordinate calculation. The spectral row is therefore evidence that the local partition has a stable finite linear signature; it is not evidence that a codon string has acquired three-dimensional protein structure.

\paragraph{Leave-one readback tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and adding the boundary again gave $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level version grouped the tested rows into $5$ families. The reported family hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast. The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. These finite rows support the claim that the local window-six geometry has real predictive contact with code-label placement under the tested deletions.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The six reported modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification row reports $R$-size $13$ and assigned count $13$. Each module has either predictable or boundary status in the supplied finite run. This is the most concrete form of the chapter claim: a finite codon-window partition sees a local boundary organization in the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The contact used here is the curated standard-code table. It supports codon-to-label readback and local split checks. It does not observe ribosomal realization, amino-acid chain production, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, biological activity, organism-level function, or a universal rule for all possible code systems. The correct conclusion is therefore narrow: the window-six coordinate system exposes a statistically nontrivial local tile and boundary pattern in the standard-code label table, with $R$ exactly the $13$-codon punctured region listed above and with finite null rows giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, $p_{J\ge0.9}=0.0125$, and amino-acid-class lower-tail value $0.0035$. Any claim about translation, folding, physical possibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality requires an additional observational contact not present in this row.
