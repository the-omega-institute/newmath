\subsection{Window-six codon boundary tile for the standard code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
This record treats the standard genetic-code table as a code-layer contact with a finite coordinate object. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a six-bit display of codon positions. The displayed data are not a mechanism of translation; they are a finite codon-label table together with a local tile extracted from that table. The relevant distinctions are the coordinate itself, the closure operation used to detect the local face, the spectrum of the finite relation, and the standard-code label readback. The biological contact is therefore narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label, and the present record asks whether one finite local shape is unusual under finite relabellings that preserve the declared sampling rules.

\paragraph{Boundary set and punctured-cube shape.}
The boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports that $R$ is a punctured-cube object with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local witness is not merely a count of codons. It has a four-face, sixteen-edge, thirteen-vertex combinatorial profile inside the codon cube. The module classification accounts for all thirteen codons in $R$: $\mathrm{AGR}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{AR\_Lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{CUN}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_Met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{SSN}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The classification row reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo shape significance.}
The finite sampling audit for the shape claim used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ accepted samples. The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the $2000$ accepted samples, exactly one sample matched the observed shape exactly, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap statistic counted $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite row therefore supports the modest code-layer statement that the observed local tile is rare under the recorded null experiment. A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It found $0$ exact matches for the observed shape, while $7$ samples lay in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size of $20$. The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers justify a shape-significance reading at the label-table layer. They do not justify any claim about physical chemistry, protein folding, expression, fitness, or universality.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
Taken together, the rows support the following narrow claim. A six-coordinate codon display of the standard-code table exposes a local boundary tile with a thirteen-codon punctured-cube profile, a complete module accounting, rare exact-shape behavior under the recorded sampling experiments, and compatible spectral numerics. The curated table supplies the codon-to-label contact; the coordinate calculation supplies the local geometry. These are distinct roles. The table gives label readback, while the coordinate and closure operations organize the finite rows.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
No translation process follows from this packet. No folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, global mechanism, or universal law follows from the local codon tile. Amino-acid label readback is not protein structure. CUN, UUR, tRNA-Leu wording, or cross-organism phrasing cannot be promoted into structure evidence without a separate organism-matched contact. Any future claim above the code-read layer must bring its own external observation, such as a translation assay, a structural contact, a physical-admissibility test, or a functional measurement. Until such a contact is present, the result is confined to a finite code-table geometry: a rare and well-accounted local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table.
