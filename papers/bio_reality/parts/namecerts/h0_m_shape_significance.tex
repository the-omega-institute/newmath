\subsection{Window-six codon shape significance at the code-read layer}\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object and scope.} The packet studies the standard-code codon table through a six-coordinate codon display.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary window for the three nucleotide positions.  The biological contact used here is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop.  This contact is enough to test codon-label readback and local tile boundaries, but it is not a measurement of translation dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Any promotion to those higher layers would require an independent contact of the appropriate kind.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.} The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the observed set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite set is certified in the punctured-cube calculation.  Its face data are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local codon tile is not merely a list of exceptional labels; it carries a small cubical boundary profile inside $Q_6$.  The shape is nevertheless local.  It describes a code-layer split in the finite codon table and does not assert a global biological law.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo significance of the observed shape.} The main finite-row test used $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes with seed $42$.  Because the sampler rejected duplicates, it made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only one sampled comparison matched the exact observed shape, and $25$ sampled comparisons reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  Thus the empirical tail values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers make the local tile non-random relative to the stated finite null experiment.  They do not identify a biochemical cause.  Their meaning is that the standard-code labels, when placed on the window-six cube, produce a boundary shape that is rare among the sampled alternatives.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null comparison.} A second null model preserved amino-acid class information and again used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed tile size was $20$.  Under this constrained null, the exact shape count was $0$, while $7$ sampled cases lay in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The constrained test is important because it checks that the effect is not explained merely by preserving broad label classes.  Even after that preservation, the observed local organization remains unusually tight in the finite sample.

\paragraph{Spectral readout.} The adjacency-spectrum calculation separates the main tile from the residual boundary.  The recorded spectral quantities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial evaluation is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial residual.  This is numerically at tolerance scale, so the finite spectral check is internally consistent with the recorded tile decomposition.  The spectral numbers are used only as finite code-table invariants; they are not evidence for a folding spectrum or a physical energy model.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.} The code-family perturbation test evaluated $23$ comparison tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  In formula form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathrm{baseline}}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
h_{\mathrm{above\ baseline}}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
h_{M-T}&=0.9365079365079365,\\
h_{M-T+\partial}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The family-level decomposition used $5$ families.  Their above-baseline hit rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  This supports the claim that the local window-six tile has predictive stability for code-label perturbations, within the explicitly tested code-read setting.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.} The codon-level recovery test reported anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison also reported $1.0$.  The result says that the finite local boundary can recover the tested codon-label omissions in the recorded setting.  Since the anchor count is zero, the recovery is not being described as a direct fixed-anchor mechanism; it is a closure-and-boundary readback property of the finite table.

\paragraph{Module classification.} The $13$ residual codons are completely assigned into six named modules with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The recorded module table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row gives $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$, with empty lists for extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons.  Thus every codon in $R$ is accounted for by a predictable-or-boundary module, and no codon outside the recorded module partition is silently imported.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.} The finite evidence supports a precise code-layer statement: in the standard-code codon table, window-six coordinates expose a rare local boundary tile whose residual set has $13$ codons, punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, significant finite-sample shape statistics, stable spectral readout, strong leave-one-code-out performance, complete leave-one-codon-out recovery, and a complete six-module residual classification.  The conclusion is intentionally bounded.  It concerns codon labels in a curated code table.  It does not claim translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal necessity without a separate contact measuring that layer.
