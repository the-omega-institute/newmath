\subsection{Window-six codon tile shape and code-layer significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studies the standard genetic-code table only at the codon-label layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary window presentation of the $64$ codons. The observed table is not treated as a consequence of the coordinate system. It is a curated contact assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The internal packet asks whether the window-six presentation exposes a local tile with a sharply delimited boundary when those labels are read against the coordinate geometry.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports that $R$ is the punctured-cube boundary with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the boundary is not merely a list of codons selected after the fact: it is a $13$-vertex finite subcomplex with $16$ one-dimensional incidences and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the window-six coordinate presentation.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The main significance test compares the observed local shape against randomized samples. With seed $42$ and $N=2000$ accepted samples, the sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. Exactly one sampled table matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count was $25$ among the $2000$ accepted samples. These numbers support a code-layer claim: the standard-code labels determine a local tile whose shape is uncommon under the stated finite null ensemble. They do not supply a biological mechanism for why the table has that shape.

A second null model preserves amino-acid class information. In that class-preserving ensemble, again with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the observed set has size $|M|=20$. The exact observed shape occurred $0$ times, so the finite Monte Carlo estimate for exact recurrence is $0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the same narrow conclusion: the local tile is not a common artifact of the sampled class-preserving rearrangements. The row data supplied here give $|M|=20$ but not the full codon membership list for $M$, so this chapter states the finite size and the measured probabilities without inventing an absent list.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral summary attaches numerical shape data to the same finite object. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius row repeats $0.6752479308830213$ as the in-tolerance value. The near-zero residual records numerical consistency for the finite spectral calculation. Its role is diagnostic: it identifies the shape as a stable coordinate object inside the code readback, not as evidence that translation, folding, admissibility, or biological function has been derived from coordinates alone.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code tests.}
The leave-one-code-out rows test whether the local tile remains informative when related code tables are withheld. Across $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ total targets, the baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The reported above-baseline hit rate is also $0.9841269841269841$. By family, $5$ families are present: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline. These finite rows indicate that the window-six tile is predictive for code-table label variation in the sampled family grouping, while remaining a code-layer predictor rather than a claim about protein realization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon tests and modules.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification covers all $13$ boundary codons with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The modules are

\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\

$$
The summary row gives $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, empty extra-module codons, empty missing-from-modules codons, and empty unassigned codons. Therefore the boundary tile is fully partitioned by the named modules in the supplied finite data.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The empirical contact used here is the curated standard-code table. It supports codon-to-label readback and local split checks. It does not observe ribosomal translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, or a universal biological mechanism. Consequently the chapter's positive claim is confined to this statement: in the standard-code label table, the window-six codon presentation exposes a statistically uncommon local boundary tile with $13$ boundary codons, a punctured-cube $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, significant finite-sample shape tests, coherent spectral diagnostics, and strong leave-one-out code-layer recovery. Any promotion from this code readback to translation, structure, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that is not present in these rows.
