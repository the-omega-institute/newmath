\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The claim concerns a finite code-read phenomenon in the standard genetic code. Codons are represented as points of a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, and the observed object is the curated standard-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The packet tests whether the window-six coordinate display exposes a local boundary-tile pattern whose label split is unusually specific under finite null models. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: it is a codon-to-label table. It does not observe translation as a cellular process, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility of a molecule, biological function, a mechanism of code origin, or universality across all organisms.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. The codons recorded in $R$ are
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row is verified in the punctured-cube calculation, where the finite face data have $f$-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the observed object is not merely a count: it is a thirteen-codon subset with a four-face local tile profile inside the six-bit display. In code-read language this marks a local label boundary, not a global biological law.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo shape test.}
The primary shape-significance run used seed $42$ and sampled $N=2000$ distinct randomized objects, requiring $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and producing $55377$ duplicate retries. Among these samples, exactly $1$ matched the observed shape exactly, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
Both numbers are finite-row empirical probabilities from the recorded run. They support the statement that the observed local tile is rare under the tested randomization, while leaving the result tied to the stated sampling scheme and to the code-label contact.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A stricter null model preserves amino-acid class information. In that row the observed $M$ size is $20$. With seed $42$ and $N=2000$ samples, the exact-shape count is $0$, so the empirical exact-shape probability is
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
This second null test is important because it asks whether the same type of shape could be obtained while respecting coarse label-class constraints. The answer in the finite run is again negative at the displayed scale: the observed size and shape sit in a small lower tail rather than appearing as an ordinary class-preserving arrangement.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectrum records three scalars:
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The radial cubic residual is
$$\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}$$
and the same value is recorded as the cubic-polynomial residual. The spectral radius row therefore agrees with the finite tile calculation to numerical tolerance. This is a numerical consistency check on the coordinate representation; by itself it is not a claim about molecular dynamics or selection.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.}
The leave-one-code-out evaluation considered $23$ tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary allowance is $0.9841269841269841$. The same $0.9841269841269841$ is recorded as the hit rate above baseline. Family-level rows further split the result across $5$ families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have rate $1.0$. These rows show that the local coordinate packet has predictive readback strength for code-table labels under the tested leave-one-code-out task, with one family below perfect recovery but still above the baseline row.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out modules.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor cases, and high closure coverage $8$, with recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ and leaves no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The modules are
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The classification completeness row has $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and assigned count $13$. Hence every codon in the boundary set is accounted for by one of the displayed modules, and every module has either predictable or boundary support in the leave-one-codon-out record.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite rows justify a code-layer statement: window-six codon coordinates expose a rare local tile associated with the standard-code label table, with $R$ a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary and with strong leave-one-out readback under the supplied probes. The rows do not justify any higher-layer promotion. A codon-label split is not a translation experiment. A label sequence is not a folded protein. A numerical spectrum is not a physical admissibility proof. A module table is not a biological function assay. Any statement about translation, folding, molecular feasibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that directly observes that layer.
