\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Significance}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-layer contact, not as a complete biological mechanism. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each nucleotide triplet is represented by a six-bit display and then read against the curated standard genetic-code table. The table contact supplies label assignments for codons, with amino-acid and stop labels as observed code-layer data. The mathematical object being tested is therefore a local tile boundary in codon-coordinate space: coordinates, closure, spectrum, and relation data are compared with the finite label table, while translation chemistry and organismal function remain outside this packet.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished relation row $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. This means that the finite tile is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the local coordinate complex. The module classification also exhausts this row. The six modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The assigned count is $13$, with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Observed shape rarity.}
The shape test sampled $N=2000$ distinct random comparison rows using seed $42$. Because the sampler rejected repeats, it made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. Only one sampled row matched the observed shape exactly, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. A high-overlap threshold also remained uncommon: $25$ samples had Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. These values support the local statement that the standard-code labels line up with a rare window-six tile shape. They do not by themselves identify a physical cause, a translational pathway, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Label-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid class information. Under this control, $N=2000$ samples produced zero exact shape matches, so the finite estimate for exact-shape agreement is $0.0$. The observed local tile had $M$-size $20$, and only $7$ sampled rows lay in the lower tail at or below that observed size, giving a lower-tail value $0.0035$. Thus the local geometry remains non-generic even when the comparison respects label-class structure. The point is narrower than a mechanistic claim: the evidence says that the codon-label table carries an unusually constrained local pattern in the chosen six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The finite spectrum records $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic check has residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius value is recorded in tolerance at $0.6752479308830213$. In chapter terms, this supplies an independent numeric signature for the same tile: the boundary row is not only combinatorial but also has a stable spectral profile at the recorded tolerance. The residual is close enough to zero for the finite run to regard the radial relation as satisfied, while still being reported as a numerical computation rather than an exact algebraic theorem.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
Across $23$ evaluated code tables and $63$ targets, the baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. Within $M-T$ the hit rate was $0.9365079365079365$, and within $M-T$ plus the boundary it returned to $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the above-baseline rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate tables. These rows say that the local tile is predictive for code-table perturbation tests at the code-label level. They still do not observe protein folding, biochemical admissibility, or function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The codon-removal probe recorded anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The recovery-rate threshold check is also $1.0$. This supports the view that the local boundary is not a single isolated coincidence: when individual codons are withheld, the coordinate and closure data recover the relevant code-layer position in the tested cases. The result remains a finite readout of codon labels. It is not a statement that the codon cube forces translation in cells, selects folded structures, or determines biological function.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The supported claim is that window-six codon coordinates expose a rare and recoverable local boundary tile in the curated standard-code label table. The contact is the standard code table itself, and its resolution is codon-label readback. No higher layer follows from this contact alone. In particular, this record does not claim translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Any such assertion would require a separate empirical contact directed at that layer, with its own finite rows and its own null comparisons.
