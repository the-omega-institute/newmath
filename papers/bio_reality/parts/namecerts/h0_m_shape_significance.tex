\subsection{Window-Six Codon Shape Significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon coordinate record.}
This record treats the standard genetic-code table as a finite label contact on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The object under test is not DNA chemistry, ribosomal translation, protein folding, or biological function.  It is the much narrower code-layer question of whether a six-bit codon display, when compared with curated amino-acid and stop labels, exposes a local boundary-tile pattern with measurable finite-row structure.  The working distinctions are: a six-bit codon coordinate, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The internal data used here are coordinate position, closure behavior, spectral scale, and codon-label relations.  The only readout is the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite boundary set $R$ has exactly $13$ codons.  The supplied row verifies
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite shape being compared against randomized alternatives.  The record supplied for the main set $M$ gives its observed size, $|M|=20$, but it does not provide a codon-by-codon listing of $M$ in this row bundle; for that reason this chapter uses the cardinality and the verified shape statistics, not an unstated membership list.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
In the shape-significance experiment, $N=2000$ sampled alternatives were evaluated with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, which is itself a warning that the finite sampling space is being searched under a strong combinatorial constraint.  The exact-shape count was $1$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
For high-overlap comparison, $25$ samples reached Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are reported as significant in the finite row.  The conclusion is therefore local and statistical: the observed standard-code boundary tile is rare under the tested finite reshuffling regime.  This does not turn the tile into a law of biology; it only says that this finite coordinate-and-label comparison has a nontrivial shape signal.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-match count was $0$, so the recorded exact-shape value was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, with observed size $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the interpretation that the signal is not merely an artifact of preserving coarse amino-acid classes.  It still remains a code-read statistic: the contact is a table of codon labels, not a physical experiment on translated molecules.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The spectral row supplies three scalar quantities attached to the same finite geometry:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial or cubic residual is reported as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically within the tolerance used by the row.  Thus the boundary tile is not only counted combinatorially; it also carries a stable finite spectral signature in the recorded computation.  The spectral number is evidence for a reproducible coordinate pattern, not evidence for molecular realization beyond codon-label assignment.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The row also records $0.9365079365079365$ for hits in $M\setminus T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ for hits in $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary.  These numbers support the claim that the finite coordinate pattern has predictive retention at the code-label layer.

The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  In symbols, the row can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor}=0,\quad \mathrm{boundary}=5,\quad
\mathrm{closure\ high}=8,\quad \mathrm{predictable}=8,\quad
\mathrm{recovery}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a recovery statement for codon-label placement under finite omission.  It is not a claim that omitted codons are biologically generated by the geometry.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family row divides the code-out evaluation into $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline are: alternative yeast $0.8333333333333334$, bacterial plastid $1.0$, candidate other $1.0$, mitochondrial $1.0$, and nuclear ciliate $1.0$.  The module-classification row assigns all $13$ codons of $R$, with assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The complete module allocation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the supplied finite row.  This gives a clean local decomposition of the $13$-codon boundary set.

\paragraph{Scope of the conclusion.}
The verdict of this packet is that the standard-code label table supports a rare local codon-tile shape in $Q_6$, with $|R|=13$, $|M|=20$, exact-shape probability $0.0005$, high-Jaccard probability $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail probability $0.0035$, and a stable spectral row led by $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$.  The claim is deliberately confined to code-layer readback from a curated codon table.  No translation process, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or cross-organism law follows from these rows unless a separate contact supplies evidence at that higher layer.
