\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the $Q_6$ codon cube: each codon is read as a six-bit position, and the only biological labels admitted into the record are the curated standard-code amino-acid or stop labels.  The investigated object is therefore not translation itself, nor protein realization, but a code-layer label table equipped with a finite coordinate system.  The local question is whether the label pattern around the codon tile exposes a statistically sharp shape split.  The observed tile separates a distinguished $R$ region from its complementary label neighborhood, with $R$ containing exactly thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed $R$ set has size $13$ and its punctured-cube face count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers are treated as finite table facts: they are read from the standard-code labels after the six-coordinate display has been fixed, rather than inferred as biological necessity.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The primary shape experiment used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ distinct sampled comparison tables.  Because the sampler rejected repeated draws, it made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  The observed local shape occurred exactly once among the $2000$ sampled alternatives, while high-overlap alternatives with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times.  Thus the empirical tail values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape value is small enough to mark the tile as non-generic under this finite null experiment.  The high-Jaccard value is larger but still limited: it says that near-copies of the boundary geometry are uncommon, not absent.  The chapter therefore records a local codon-table regularity, not a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid class information and again used seed $42$ with $N=2000$.  In that comparison there were no exact shape matches, so the exact-shape count was $0$ and the empirical exact-shape value was $0.0$ at the displayed sampling resolution.  The observed $M$ side had size $20$, and the lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This class-preserving test strengthens the interpretation that the local window-six arrangement is not merely a consequence of retaining broad amino-acid classes.  It still does not identify a biochemical mechanism.  The only contact used here is the curated codon-to-label table, so the conclusion remains at the level of code readback and finite tile comparison.

\paragraph{Spectral signature.}
The same finite object carries a coordinate-spectrum summary.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check returns $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial residual.  This residual is numerically near zero at the stated precision and supports the internal consistency of the spectral calculation for the displayed tile.  The spectral datum is a compact fingerprint of the finite coordinate object; it is not a structural-biology measurement and it supplies no claim about folding, physical admissibility, or function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adding the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$.  These figures say that the local tile plus boundary is highly informative for recovering code-layer targets within this finite evaluation.

The leave-one-codon-out experiment supplies a finer boundary check.  It recorded anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The thresholded recovery value was also $1.0$.  Thus every tested held-out codon in this packet was either recovered through the local prediction route or accounted for by boundary placement.  Since there are no anchors in this run, the evidence is not a claim that fixed biochemical anchors determine the table; it is a statement about the finite local geometry of the standard-code readback.

\paragraph{Local interpretation and boundary.}
The evidence supports a narrow conclusion: in the standard-code table, after the six-coordinate codon display is fixed, the observed local tile and its thirteen-codon $R$ side have a rare shape under the sampled null models, a coherent spectral summary, and strong recovery behavior in leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.  The contact is a curated codon-label table, so the observed facts remain code-layer facts.  No translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this record.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate observation that directly measures the higher layer.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded structures, cross-organism wording is not structure evidence, and a local codon tile is not a global biological law.
