\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object and contact layer.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes read against the curated standard genetic-code table.  The contact used here is only the codon-to-label assignment table: it records which codons receive amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code.  It does not record translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, thermodynamic admissibility, biological activity, or a general law of life.  The claim is therefore restricted to the code-read layer: a window-six coordinate system exposes a local tile boundary pattern, while the label table supplies the finite biological readback.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its observed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite rows identify this set as a punctured-cube boundary with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary is not being introduced as a vague visual analogy: the finite record fixes the $13$ codons and the associated combinatorial counts.  The supplied rows do not list the individual members of the complementary shape $M$; they do give the observed size $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class preserving test, so this chapter treats $M$ numerically rather than pretending that an absent member list has been supplied.

\paragraph{Shape significance under finite sampling.}
The primary significance run used seed $42$ and sampled $N=2000$ distinct null draws, requiring $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  In that run there was exactly $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The reported finite p-values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape event is therefore rare in this sampled null population, and the high-Jaccard event is also uncommon.  These numbers support the narrower claim that the standard-code labels occupy an unusual local tile arrangement in the six-coordinate codon display.  They do not by themselves turn the geometry into a causal model of translation or a physical explanation of proteins.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class structure.  It again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  For the observed value $|M|=20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding finite p-values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This sharper control is important because it checks that the tile signal is not merely a restatement of label-class multiplicity.  Within the finite rows supplied here, even after preserving amino-acid class, the observed shape remains exceptional by the reported exact and lower-tail tests.

\paragraph{Spectral readout.}
The spectral row gives the local numerical profile
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial and cubic polynomial checks both report the residual value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  These quantities provide a numerical consistency check for the local coordinate construction.  They should be read as finite spectral features of the codon-tile computation, not as measurements of molecular mechanics.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the reported rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes.  These rows say that the local tile geometry has strong code-table recovery behavior across the tested table family.  They still remain table-level readbacks: no organism-level translation system or phenotype is observed in this contact.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery and modules.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification is complete for the $13$ boundary codons: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and there are no extra module codons, no missing module members, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the supplied finite row.  This supports a local modular interpretation of the $R$ boundary, with the CUN block, AGR block, stop--tryptophan block, and singleton modules all accounted for.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and boundary of use.}
The finite evidence supports the following restricted conclusion.  In the six-coordinate codon cube, the curated standard-code labels determine a $13$-codon boundary set with punctured-cube counts $(13,16,4)$, rare sampled shape behavior under the reported nulls, stable spectral values, and strong leave-one-code and leave-one-codon recovery statistics.  The observation is therefore a significant code-layer tile phenomenon.  It is not, on these rows alone, a claim about actual translation chemistry, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Any such promotion would require a separate contact that directly measures the higher layer in question.
