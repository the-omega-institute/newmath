\subsection{Window-six local codon tile and standard-code readback}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and contact.}
The finite object in this record is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a codon display with two bits per nucleotide position.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard genetic-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label.  This contact is enough to test whether a window-six coordinate construction recovers a local label split, but it is not an observation of translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, or universality.  The claim therefore lives at the code-read layer.  It says that the standard-code table exhibits a statistically sharp local boundary-tile pattern in the six-bit codon coordinates, while the labels themselves remain supplied by the curated code table.

\paragraph{Boundary codons.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the verified list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records that this set is the punctured-cube boundary selected by the coordinate construction, with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite evidence is not merely a count: it identifies the exact codon names, the size of the selected set, and the small two-dimensional tile profile carried by the selected boundary.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The observed local-tile shape was compared against $N=2000$ sampled alternatives using seed $42$.  The sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ sampled alternatives, the exact observed shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap test counted $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These two numbers support the finite claim that the standard-code label table has a rare local tile boundary in the tested coordinate class.  The result is a code-table regularity, not a biological law about what translation or proteins must do.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null comparison.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class structure.  In that model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$-shape has size $20$.  The exact-shape count was $0$, so the recorded exact-shape probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This reinforces the interpretation that the observed local-tile profile is not an ordinary outcome of the class-preserving comparison used here.  It still does not identify a mechanism; it only sharpens the finite code-layer discrepancy between the observed standard table and the specified null population.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The same finite record includes a spectral summary of the local split.  The verified values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual and the cubic-polynomial residual are both recorded as approximately $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers give an internal numerical signature for the observed split: the $M$ side, the $R$ boundary, and the auxiliary scalar $\mu$ occupy a stable finite spectral row up to the stated tolerance.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
The code-family stress test evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Broken down by family, the hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  The stress test therefore says that the local window-six pattern remains predictive under leave-one-code-out evaluation at the code-label level.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery and modules.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchor codons, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ codons with high closure coverage, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Its module classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ selected codons are assigned, and there are no extra module codons, missing selected codons, or unassigned selected codons.  The module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification.  This supports the finite claim that the selected boundary is organized by codon modules rather than by an unstructured thirteen-element accident.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The evidence supports a local statement: within the six-bit codon cube, the standard-code labels determine a rare and recoverable boundary-tile pattern, with $R$ equal to the thirteen codons listed above and with a twenty-codon observed $M$ side in the class-preserving comparison.  The curated table supplies codon-to-label readback only.  No inference is made here from codon labels to protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, translation realization, universal mechanism, or a global law.  Any such stronger statement would need an additional independent contact that observes the higher layer directly.
