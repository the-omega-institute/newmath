\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Significance}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table after it is displayed in the six-bit codon cube. The finite object under inspection is not a biochemical mechanism. It is the code-level readback obtained by placing the curated codon labels on the $Q_6$ coordinate cube and asking whether the induced local tile has a statistically unusual shape. The result is a finite record about codon-label geometry: singleton corners, three-point label classes, local faces, closure behavior, spectrum, and relation data can be computed inside the coordinate presentation, while the amino-acid and stop labels themselves are supplied by the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the code-layer shape being tested. It is a local combinatorial boundary inside the six-bit display, not an assertion that biological translation must obey this geometry.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ accepted samples. Producing those distinct samples required $57377$ sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the accepted samples, the exact observed shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed local tile is not behaving like a typical draw from the sampled comparison class. The finite conclusion is deliberately narrow: the standard-code labels, when read through the window-six coordinate display, expose a rare local shape.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving comparison.}
A second null comparison preserved amino-acid class data. In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$. The exact-shape count was $0$, so the empirical exact-shape value was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This comparison is important because it tests against a label-class-aware background rather than only against an unconstrained coordinate shuffle. The signal remains finite and code-level: it says that the observed tile is rare under the stated finite resampling rule.

\paragraph{Spectral record.}
The spectral finite row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is also the reported cubic-polynomial residual. The value is numerically at tolerance scale for the finite computation. This spectral datum supplies an additional coordinate invariant for the same local tile; it does not by itself add a biochemical layer.

\paragraph{Leave-one evidence.}
The leave-one-code-out evaluation considered $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. Broken down by family, the five reported family rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon evidence.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure coverage high $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The same $1.0$ value is reported for recovery above threshold. The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons of $R$: assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the finite row. This supports the local tile reading as a stable code-layer phenomenon across the reported deletion probes.

\paragraph{Interpretation limit.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is enough to test codon-label readback and the local split of the window-six tile. It is not a contact for translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanistic causation, or universality across life. Amino-acid label sequences are labels in a code table; they are not observed protein folds. Any claim beyond this code layer requires a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The finite evidence supports the following constrained statement. In the six-bit codon display of the curated standard code, the boundary set $R$ of size $13$ forms a punctured-cube local tile with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, statistically rare exact shape with $p=0.0005$, high-overlap rate $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail value $0.0035$, and coherent deletion-module behavior. The result is a significant local codon-label geometry record, not a biological law and not a promotion from code readback to translation, structure, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal necessity.
