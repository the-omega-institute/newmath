\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-layer contact over the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is read as a six-bit display after choosing the usual nucleotide-to-coordinate presentation, and the biological table supplies labels only at the level of codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop assignment.  The object under test is therefore not translation as a cellular process, and not protein structure.  It is the finite shape made visible when the standard-code labels are placed on the codon cube and a local boundary tile is extracted from the displayed table.

\paragraph{Observed residual set.}
The finite residual set $R$ has size $13$.  The reported codons are $AAA$, $AGA$, $AGG$, $AUA$, $CUA$, $CUC$, $CUG$, $CUU$, $UAA$, $UAG$, $UCA$, $UGA$, and $UUA$.  The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the contact is not merely a count: it specifies a concrete thirteen-codon locus together with its local cubical incidence data.  This is the part of the claim that can be checked directly against the curated standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The finite-row experiment for $h0.M.shape.significance$ used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparison tables.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled table matched the observed shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  A high-overlap test with Jaccard threshold $0.9$ found $25$ matches, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The row therefore records two separate finite signals: an exact-shape rarity at $0.0005$, and a near-shape rarity at $0.0125$.  Both are code-layer statistics over the finite codon-label table; neither is a claim about a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class structure.  In that test, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size is $20$.  The exact-shape count is $0$, recorded as $p=0.0$ in the finite sample.  The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size is $7$, yielding a lower-tail value $0.0035$.  This strengthens the interpretation that the observed local tile is not a typical artifact of the class-preserving randomization.  The full membership list of $M$ is not included in the finite row, so the prose uses the reported size and shape statistics rather than inventing a missing codon list.

These numbers give an internal numerical profile for the same finite codon tile: the singleton-corner behavior, the three-point label-class behavior, and the residual set geometry are tied to explicit scalar coordinates rather than to an informal visual impression.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The code-level predictive check evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the reported hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  These rows say that the local codon-tile signal is stable under the stated finite leave-one-code-out comparisons, while still remaining a code-read signal.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The codon-level deletion test reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, and the recorded above-threshold recovery rate is also $1.0$.  The module classification assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing or extra module codons.  The modules are $agr=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  The assigned count is $13$, the reported $R$ size is $13$, the extra-module list is empty, the missing-from-modules list is empty, and the unassigned-codon list is empty.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite evidence supports the following narrow statement.  When the curated standard-code labels are placed on $Q_6$, a particular local tile around $M$ and $R$ has statistically rare shape behavior under the sampled nulls, a concrete thirteen-codon residual locus, a punctured-cube incidence profile, stable leave-one-code-out behavior, and complete module accounting.  This is enough to treat the tile as a finite code-layer regularity in the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Scope restriction.}
No stronger biological conclusion follows from this row alone.  The contact used here is a curated codon-label table, so it observes label readback and local split structure only.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a law forcing DNA to realize the displayed geometry.  Any claim at those higher layers would require a separate contact that actually measures that layer.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded structures, and CUN/UUR or tRNA-Leu wording does not become structure evidence merely by appearing near the same codon family.
