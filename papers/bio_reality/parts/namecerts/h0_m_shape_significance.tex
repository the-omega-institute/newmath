\subsection{Window-six local tile significance for the standard-code table}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the tested layer is code readback: codons are compared only as table entries carrying amino-acid or stop labels.  The relevant local distinction is a boundary tile whose exceptional region $R$ consists of thirteen codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
   &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit reports $|R|=13$.  As a cubical subcomplex inside the window-six display, this same set has face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the table is not being summarized merely by a count.  The observation being tested is the shape of the code-label split around a singleton label corner and a three-point label class, with coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data all read against the curated standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo comparison.}
The shape test for $M$ used $N=2000$ sampled comparison tables with seed $42$.  To obtain the distinct samples used in the comparison, the run made $57377$ sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicates.  Among the $2000$ retained trials, the exact observed shape occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run counted $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local geometry of the observed table is a rare event under this finite comparison rule.  The record does not claim that rarity alone explains the biological origin of the code; it only says that the window-six coordinates expose a sharply localized table pattern.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid class information and again used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  In that class-preserving comparison the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding finite rates were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This null strengthens the reading that the observed local tile is not explained by an undifferentiated relabeling check.  It still remains a code-table statement, because the tested contact is the curated table of codon-to-label assignments, not a translation apparatus, a protein structure measurement, or a functional assay.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The same finite packet records a spectral signature for the local split.  The numerical row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial evaluation is reported as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the cubic-polynomial residual.  This near-zero residual is treated as a numerical consistency check for the finite coordinate computation.  It is not promoted to an independent biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Predictive checks.}
Leave-one-code-out testing evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-resolved rows record five families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  These numbers support the claim that the local boundary is useful for code-label recovery inside the tested table family, not that it governs translation in organisms.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out and module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module accounting is complete: all $13$ codons in $R$ are assigned, with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This gives a finite module-level description of the boundary tile.  The three stop-related codons, the four CUN codons, the two AGR codons, the singleton AAA and AUA positions, and the two SSN codons are all accounted for inside the same code-read layer.

\paragraph{Local scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Consequently the chapter establishes a finite code-layer readback: a rare local tile, its thirteen-codon boundary set, its cubical face vector, its spectral row, and its recovery behavior under leave-one-out probes.  It does not establish translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or an organism-level law.  Any claim crossing one of those boundaries must bring an additional contact of the relevant kind.  In particular, amino-acid label readback is not a fold observation, CUN or UUR wording is not structure evidence, and a local coordinate pattern in $Q_6$ is not by itself a biological necessity.
