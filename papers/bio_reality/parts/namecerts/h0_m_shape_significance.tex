\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
The packet tests whether the standard genetic-code table, when read only as codon-to-label data on a six-coordinate codon cube, contains a local boundary shape that is difficult to reproduce under finite randomized controls.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$: each codon is displayed as a six-bit coordinate, with a local face tile, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class used as the visible distinctions.  The observed biological object is not translation as a biochemical process, but the curated standard-code label table: codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels, and the experiment asks whether this label table exposes a statistically unusual local shape in the coordinate layer.

\paragraph{Observed boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\\
&\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this row as a punctured cube with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the chapter records a concrete local combinatorial object: thirteen codons, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the relevant tile.  This is a code-layer shape statement.  It does not assert that DNA or translation must obey the coordinate geometry as a biological necessity.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo shape test.}
The primary significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ randomized samples.  To obtain distinct samples it made $57377$ attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one randomized sample matched the observed shape, and $25$ samples reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The resulting empirical rates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded significance fields repeat these same values as $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$ and $p_{\mathrm{high\ Jaccard}}=0.0125$.  The exact-shape event is therefore rare in the sampled null ensemble, while high-overlap local shapes occur more often but still at a small empirical rate.  The claim is consequently a finite-row significance claim about a codon-label tile, not a claim that the tile is a universal law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving control.}
A second null test preserves amino-acid class structure.  In that control the observed set size was $|M|=20$, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  There were no exact matches, so the recorded exact-shape rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=7/2000=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the local reading: even after class-preserving randomization, the observed finite shape is not reproduced exactly in the reported samples, and only a small lower-tail fraction is seen.  The data supplied here do not include the full membership list of $M$, so this paragraph records the scalar $M$ facts and does not reconstruct an unprovided codon list.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The spectral calculation records three scalar values:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same value is recorded as the cubic-polynomial residual.  The spectral-radius entry in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$.  These numbers provide a numerical consistency check on the coordinate reading: the local shape has a stable spectral summary, with the polynomial residual effectively at numerical zero relative to ordinary floating-point tolerance.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.}
The leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported hit rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the five family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes.  These are code-table readback facts only: they say that the local tile and its boundary organize many held-out label changes better than the baseline in the sampled comparison.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out readback.}
The leave-one-codon-out run records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, $8$ predictable codons, and a recovery rate of $1.0$.  The threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all thirteen codons in $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons: $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and the assigned count is $13$.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the supplied classification.  This makes the local row readable as six finite codon modules rather than as an undifferentiated set of thirteen labels.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact can test code-layer readback and the local codon-tile label split.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, or a universal mechanism.  Amino-acid label-sequence readback is therefore not protein structure.  CUN/UUR wording, tRNA-Leu notation, and cross-organism usage remain separate research questions unless supplied with organism-matched observations.  In particular, no conclusion about folding, function, physical admissibility, translation dynamics, or universality follows from the present finite rows.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a narrow statement: the standard-code label table, displayed on the six-bit codon cube, contains a statistically uncommon local boundary tile with $|R|=13$, $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, exact-shape Monte Carlo rate $0.0005$, high-Jaccard rate $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail rate $0.0035$, and coherent spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The predictive probes show strong code-layer recovery across held-out code tables and held-out codons.  The result is ready as a local codon-label shape claim, provided it remains confined to the code-read layer and does not cross into mechanism, structure, physics, function, or universality without an additional empirical contact.
