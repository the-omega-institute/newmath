\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Boundary}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The standard-code codon table can be read as a finite labelled pattern on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The point of the claim is deliberately narrow: the coordinate display exposes a local tile with a boundary-sensitive amino-acid label split, while the labels themselves are supplied by the curated standard-code table.  The biological object under discussion is therefore the code-read layer of the standard genetic code, not translation as a molecular process and not protein structure.  In this finite record the distinguished boundary set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row check records $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons are certified again by the punctured-cube computation.  Its face data are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the finite contact is not merely a count but a small cubical shape with vertices, edges, and two-dimensional faces explicitly accounted for.

\paragraph{Finite coordinates and label readback.}
The carrier is the six-bit codon display $Q_6$, with codons treated as finite coordinate labels rather than as a mechanistic account of ribosomal decoding.  The row data separate four ingredients: coordinate placement in the codon cube, closure under the local tile operation, spectral measurements on the induced finite relation, and readback through the curated standard-code label table.  The verified tile has observed size $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class preserving null calculation.  The member list of $M$ is not included in this finite row, so the present text uses only the reported size and the reported comparisons against shuffled label assignments.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape-significance experiment used seed $42$ and evaluated $N=2000$ null samples.  To obtain these distinct samples it made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, of which $55377$ were duplicate retries.  Against this finite null ensemble, the observed shape had exactly one exact match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The relaxed comparison counted $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are small at the scale of this row.  The exact-shape value records that the full observed local geometry is rare under the sampled null, while the high-Jaccard value records that near-shapes are still uncommon but less exceptional than exact equality.  The result supports a local codon-tile signal at the code-read layer; it does not by itself identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid class information and again used seed $42$ with $N=2000$ samples.  It recorded $0$ exact shape matches, so the finite-row estimate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$, with observed size $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a sharper test than an unconstrained label shuffle because it keeps a coarse biological label class fixed while testing whether the same kind of local tile remains typical.  In this row it does not: no exact shape appears among the $2000$ class-preserving samples, and only $7$ samples fall into the lower tail at or below the observed size.

\paragraph{Spectral signature.}
The finite relation also has a numerical spectrum.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check returned
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is within numerical tolerance for zero in this finite computation.  Thus the spectrum supplies a compact numerical fingerprint of the observed tile and its boundary relation.  The spectral radius recorded in tolerance is again $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$ in the row data.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out evaluation tested $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  This says that the local tile and its boundary recover most of the held-out code-layer targets in the finite experiment.  The family split is also explicit: among $5$ families, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, and $8$ predictable codons.  It also records closure coverage high at $8$ and recovery rate
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recovery-rate-above-threshold value is likewise $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$: $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either predictable or boundary support in the finite row, so the local classification is complete for the observed boundary set.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is enough to test codon-label readback and local label splits on $Q_6$.  It is not a contact with translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a general law of living systems.  Amino-acid label sequences are still code-layer readbacks; they are not folded structures.  Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, and code-family comparisons remain separate finite questions unless matched to their own organism-specific observations.  The present claim is therefore: the standard-code table exhibits a statistically uncommon local codon-tile boundary in window-six coordinates, with explicit boundary set $R$, punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, and strong finite leave-one-out behavior at the code-read layer.  Nothing stronger follows from this row without an additional contact designed for the stronger biological layer.
