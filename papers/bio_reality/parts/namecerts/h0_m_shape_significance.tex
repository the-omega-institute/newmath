\subsection{Window-six codon geometry and the local boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-level object: each codon is read as a six-bit point in the codon cube $Q_6$, and the observed labels are the curated amino-acid or stop labels attached to the standard table. The coordinate object supplies a finite carrier and adjacency geometry; the biological contact supplies only the label assignment. The contact does not observe ribosomal translation, folding, energetic feasibility, organismal fitness, biochemical mechanism, or universality. Consequently the result is a local statement about a labeled finite cube, not a law about DNA, proteins, cells, or evolution.

The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}

The punctured-cube check returns the same list and the face-count signature

This makes the row more specific than a mere subset size: the codons form a finite local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces under the window-six coordinate geometry. The row is not promoted beyond code readback. It says that a particular labeled portion of the standard table has a compact cubical boundary form.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite sampling.}
For the primary shape comparison, the verified run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled alternatives. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite comparison was strongly constrained by collisions in the sampled space. The exact shape appeared once among the $2000$ sampled alternatives, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ shape\ matches}\}&=1, & p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A looser Jaccard threshold of at least $0.9$ produced $25$ matches, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{J\geq 0.9\}&=25, & p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore a finite-row rarity observation for the code-layer shape. It is not a causal explanation of the genetic code. It is also not a statement that the geometry forces the standard table; the label table remains the biological contact against which the coordinate calculation is read.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null comparison.}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact\ matches}\}&=0, & p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0.

The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, yielding
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{lower\ tail}\leq 20\}&=7, & p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}

This comparison matters because it does not test arbitrary relabeling alone; it asks whether the same kind of compact local shape appears when broad label-class constraints are retained. The answer in the finite run is still rare. The membership list for $M$ is not included in this packet, so the chapter records its verified size and null-model role without inventing its codon contents.
