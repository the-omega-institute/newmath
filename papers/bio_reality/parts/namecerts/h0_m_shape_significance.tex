\subsection{Window-six codon shape significance at the code-read layer}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the standard-code codon table, viewed through a six-coordinate codon display, carries a local shape that is unusual under finite relabelling probes.  The biological object is not translation itself, nor a protein, nor a biochemical mechanism.  The object tested here is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels, read only as a code-level table.  The coordinate carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes available as finite distinctions.  The observed pattern is therefore a statement about local codon-label geometry in $Q_6$, not a statement about folding, physical admissibility, organismal fitness, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite codon residue.}
The residue set singled out by the code-table calculation has cardinality $13$.  In lexicographic codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite shape being tested is not merely a count.  It retains vertices, one-dimensional adjacencies, and two-dimensional faces in the six-bit codon coordinate cube.  The residue contains the stop-related codons $UAA$, $UAG$, and $UGA$, the arginine pair $AGA,AGG$, the isoleucine--methionine switch codon $AUA$, the leucine block $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$, and the two codons $UCA,UUA$ that appear in the module accounting below.

\paragraph{Shape-significance probe.}
For the primary finite null comparison, the experiment used seed $42$ and requested $N=2000$ distinct samples.  Producing those distinct samples required $57377$ attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ distinct sampled tables, exactly one reproduced the observed shape exactly, and $25$ reached Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$.  The empirical probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both numbers are small on this finite test.  The exact-shape statistic says that the observed codon-label tile is rare among the sampled alternatives.  The high-Jaccard statistic says that near copies are also uncommon, though less rare than exact copies.  The result supports a local code-read regularity: the standard label table occupies a particular corner-and-face arrangement in $Q_6$ more sharply than a random finite relabelling would normally do under this probe.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A stricter control preserved amino-acid class information while testing the same kind of shape event.  In this run, again with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the observed size was $20$.  The exact-shape match count was $0$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second null is important because it does not treat the code table as an unconstrained bag of labels.  Even after preserving amino-acid class structure, the observed local shape remains in a small tail of the sampled finite distribution.  The explicit member list for the $M$-set is not part of the supplied row, so this paragraph uses only the recorded size and finite test counts.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The same finite object has a recorded spectral profile.  The residue-side and $M$-side parameters were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check returned
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is numerically at zero at the recorded tolerance scale.  This does not turn the codon table into a dynamical system or a biochemical law.  It supplies a compact numerical invariant of the finite local geometry: the $M$-side spectral radius is larger than the residue-side value, and the stored cubic residual is negligible relative to the displayed precision.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The code-level prediction probes were also finite.  In the leave-one-code-out check, $23$ code tables were evaluated over $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  These values say that the local tile is useful for recovering held-out code-label entries under the finite protocol.  They do not say that the tile causes translation or that it determines a physical molecule.

In the leave-one-codon-out check, the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the predictable count was $8$, and the high-coverage closure count was $8$.  The recorded recovery rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This supports the same narrow conclusion: within the finite code-read experiment, the local boundary information is sufficient to recover the tested held-out codon cases.

\paragraph{Family and module accounting.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out summary grouped the evaluated alternatives into $5$ families.  The recorded hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  Thus the signal is not concentrated in a single named family in this finite survey, although the alternative-yeast group is the least saturated of the five.

The module classification accounts for all $13$ residue codons without extras or omissions.  Its complete assignment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing residue codons, and no unassigned codons.  Every module has a predictable or boundary role in the finite classification.  This is the strongest internal bookkeeping result in the row: the shape residue, the module partition, and the boundary-prediction check close on the same thirteen codons.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The proper reading is a code-layer one.  The curated standard-code table supplies the label contact, and the six-coordinate codon geometry supplies the local finite comparison space.  In that space, the observed standard-code tile is rare under the sampled nulls, spectrally structured, recoverable in leave-one probes, and exactly partitioned by the recorded modules.  The evidence therefore supports the claim that window-six codon coordinates expose a significant local boundary-tile pattern in the standard code table.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
Nothing in this row observes ribosomal translation, tRNA charging, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, function, evolutionary necessity, or a universal biological mechanism.  Amino-acid label readback is still label readback.  A separate contact would be required before any statement about translated molecules, folded proteins, organism-matched usage, biochemical realization, or biological function could be made.  The present conclusion is deliberately narrower: the standard-code label table contains a finite local codon-tile structure whose exact and near-exact occurrence rates are small in the recorded null experiments.
