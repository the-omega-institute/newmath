\subsection{Window-six codon shape significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record treats the standard genetic-code table only as a codon-to-label contact.  The coordinate object is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with rows read as codons and labels read as amino-acid or stop symbols.  The observed local tile $M$ has size $20$ in the supplied finite rows.  Its boundary remainder $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same list is reported by the punctured-cube check, with face-vector data $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite row is not merely a count: it fixes a concrete thirteen-codon boundary set and a four-face local surface pattern inside the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The primary significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled alternatives.  Because duplicate draws were rejected, the run records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact observed shape occurred once among the $2000$ alternatives, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A relaxed comparison at Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$ found $25$ matches, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These two numbers support a code-layer statement: the observed $20$-row tile is rare under the sampled comparison class, and near-matches are still uncommon.  They do not say that the geometry is a biological cause, a translation rule, or a structural law.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null.}
A second null preserves amino-acid class information.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving test therefore strengthens the same local conclusion: even after preserving the relevant label-class background, the observed finite shape remains a small-tail event in the sampled population.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The curated standard-code table supplies codon-label readback and local split tests.  From these rows one may say that window-six codon coordinates expose a statistically rare local boundary-tile pattern in the standard-code label table.  One may not infer translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a law binding all biological coding systems.  Any such higher-layer statement would require a separate contact that directly observes the corresponding layer.
