\subsection{Window-six shape significance for the standard-code boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studies the standard genetic-code label table through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read as the finite set of sixty-four codons equipped with the bit-level distinctions induced by the three nucleotide positions.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: the curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments, including amino-acid labels and stop labels.  It does not supply a translation event, a folded molecular state, a physical-admissibility test, a functional assay, or a universal mechanism.  The mathematical question is therefore local.  It asks whether the codons singled out by the observed label split form an unusually shaped boundary tile inside $Q_6$, not whether that tile explains biology beyond the label table.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified codon list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate punctured-cube check returns the same thirteen codons and records the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a cardinality observation: it fixes the actual codon support and the finite face profile of the local tile.  The module partition is complete on this same support.  The modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The classification has $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The significance run for the local shape used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported tail counts should be read as a finite Monte Carlo audit over distinct sampled shapes rather than as a symbolic enumeration of all possible alternatives.  The observed exact shape appeared once among the $2000$ comparisons, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap event with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both finite tail values are small in this sampled model.  The claim supported here is correspondingly modest: the observed standard-code boundary tile is rare relative to the sampled shape comparison, and near-copies are also uncommon at the stated Jaccard threshold.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information.  In that row the observed $M$-size is $20$.  With seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the exact-shape count is $0$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second test is important because it weakens the possibility that the tile is only a trivial consequence of preserving coarse label classes.  The row still remains a code-layer test: it compares finite codon-label configurations, not realized proteins or cellular mechanisms.

The cubic relation is satisfied to numerical residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, and the radial-polynomial residual is the same value.  The spectral-radius entry in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers give an internal numerical coordinate for the tile.  They are not used here as a physical spectrum of a molecule; they are finite graph data extracted from the codon-window construction.

\paragraph{Readback checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, $1.0$ for candidate-other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate, across $5$ families.  The leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, recovery rate $1.0$, and recovery rate above threshold $1.0$.  Together these rows say that the local tile is not only rare under the shape tests but also useful for finite code-label recovery probes.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The ready claim is the following.  In the window-six codon coordinate system, the standard-code label table determines a thirteen-codon boundary set $R$ with face vector $(13,16,4)$, complete module assignment, small sampled exact-shape tail $0.0005$, small high-Jaccard tail $0.0125$, a stronger amino-acid-class preserving lower-tail value $0.0035$, and consistent finite recovery behavior in leave-one-code and leave-one-codon tests.  These data support a code-read statement: the curated standard-code labels expose a statistically sharp local tile in $Q_6$.  They do not support promotion to translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Any claim at one of those higher layers requires a separate observation designed for that layer.
