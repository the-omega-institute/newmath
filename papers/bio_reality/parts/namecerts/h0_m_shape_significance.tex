\subsection{Window-six codon shape and local label boundary}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label contact placed on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The coordinate object is the binary display of a codon position pattern, while the biological labels are taken from the curated standard genetic-code table.  Thus the finite object being tested is not a translation mechanism and not a molecular dynamics model.  It is a code-layer readback: codons have amino-acid or stop labels, and the window-six geometry asks whether a local tile boundary is visible inside those labels.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its observed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This means that the finite boundary is not merely a list of exceptional labels: it has a local face structure in the cube, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces.  The row therefore supports a precise local shape claim at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape-significance experiment used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ null samples.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ sampled alternatives, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The resulting finite empirical probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape value and the high-Jaccard value are both small in this finite test, so the observed local boundary is not behaving like a typical sampled row under this null procedure.  The conclusion is limited to the row geometry and its label table contact: it says that the displayed standard-code row has a rare local shape under the stated sampling experiment.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null keeps amino-acid-class information fixed and again uses $N=2000$ with seed $42$.  In that class-preserving experiment the observed size was $|M|=20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and only $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size.  The recorded probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\

\end{aligned}
$$
This separate null strengthens the code-layer observation: preserving coarse amino-acid class does not make the same local codon shape common.  It still does not turn the row into a statement about protein folding, biological function, or physical realization.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The supported statement is narrow.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a rare local boundary-tile pattern when read against the curated standard-code label table.  The contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and permits local split checks.  It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Any claim at those higher layers requires an additional contact of the appropriate kind.  In particular, amino-acid label readback is not protein structure, and cross-organism wording about codon families or tRNA labels does not become structure evidence without an organism-matched structural or mechanistic observation.
