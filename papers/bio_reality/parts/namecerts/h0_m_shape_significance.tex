\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The primary shape experiment used seed $42$ and evaluated $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes.  The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, which is a useful warning that the finite shape space has strong collision pressure under the chosen draw procedure.  Within these $2000$ comparisons, the exact observed shape occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A relaxed comparison by high overlap found $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the local claim that the observed codon tile is not a typical draw under this finite comparison protocol.  They do not identify a biological mechanism.  They only say that, when the standard-code labels are read at this coordinate level, the selected window-six shape is rare relative to the stated finite null draw.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The chapter's claim is that window-six codon coordinates expose a statistically unusual local boundary tile when checked against the curated standard-code label table.  The contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and supports local split tests.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, molecular folding, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, causal mechanism, or universality. In the present record, amino-acid label readback remains label readback; it is not folded protein structure and it is not biological function.
