\subsection{Window-six codon shape significance}\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with coordinates supplied by a binary window encoding of the three nucleotide positions. The observed object is not a mechanism of translation but a code-layer table: each codon is read with its curated amino-acid or stop label. In this finite setting, the question is whether the label table exposes a local boundary-tile shape that is unusually concentrated relative to finite null samples. The record is therefore a shape-significance claim at the code-read layer, not a claim about folded proteins, biochemical admissibility, organismal function, or universal biological law.

\paragraph{Observed row.}
The finite row for the main statistic used $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes with seed $42$. The distinct-sample procedure made $57377$ attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported tail counts are tied to a deliberately deduplicated finite sample. The exact observed shape occurred once among the $2000$ comparison rows, while $25$ rows had Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$. Thus the two reported finite p-values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
  p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
  p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both numbers are code-layer statistics. They say that the observed local tile is rare under the recorded comparison protocol; they do not by themselves identify a causal biochemical principle.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$. The codons in the row are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. The chapter uses this as the finite geometric support for the local tile: $13$ codon vertices, $16$ adjacency edges, and $4$ square faces in the displayed subcomplex. The observed $M$-row size supplied for the amino-acid-class preserving null is $20$, and the lower-tail test found $7$ rows at or below that size among $2000$ trials.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral coordinates distinguish the boundary component from the ambient code readback. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
  \lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
  \mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual was $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same value was recorded for the cubic-polynomial check. This near-zero residual is used only as a numerical consistency check for the finite spectral row. It is not promoted to an exact biological identity.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null.}
A second null row preserved amino-acid classes. In $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$, the exact shape count was $0$, giving the recorded exact-shape p-value $0.0$ at the finite resolution of the experiment. The lower-tail count at the observed $M$ size was $7$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
  p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
  p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite shape reading because the rarity persists after preserving a biologically meaningful label-class constraint. It still remains a table-level fact: the null is about codon labels and local shape, not about molecular dynamics or selection.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code readback.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ target events. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate-other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, across $5$ recorded families. These rows support the claim that the local codon-tile coordinates retain predictive information under finite code-table leave-out tests.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon row.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ predictable cases, and $8$ high-coverage closure cases. The recovery rate was $1.0$, and the above-threshold recovery rate was also $1.0$. The module classification accounts for all $13$ boundary codons with no missing codons, no extras, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
  \mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
  \mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
  \mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
  \mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
  \mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This finite partition is the strongest local readback component of the packet: each boundary codon is assigned to a named module, and every module has either predictable or boundary support.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table. It supplies codon-to-label assignments at codon-label resolution. This contact is sufficient to test local label splits and code-read recovery, but it does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Consequently, an amino-acid label sequence read from the table must not be described as a folded protein structure. Any later claim about structure, function, or physical realization would require a separate contact that actually measures or models that layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a ready code-layer statement: the window-six display of the standard-code table contains a rare local boundary tile, with exact-shape p-value $0.0005$, high-Jaccard p-value $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail p-value $0.0035$, boundary size $13$, punctured-cube $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, and complete module coverage of the $13$ boundary codons. The result is deliberately narrow. It records a significant finite shape in the curated codon-label table and blocks promotion beyond that layer unless an independent higher-layer contact is supplied.
