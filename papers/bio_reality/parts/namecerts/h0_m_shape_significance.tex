\subsection{Window-Six Local Tile Significance for the Standard Code}
\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The packet \texttt{h0.M.shape.significance} concerns a window-six codon coordinate system over the codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is treated as a six-bit display point, and the only biological readout used here is the curated standard-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The claim is therefore confined to the code-read layer: the question is whether the observed label table has a finite local tile shape that is unusually well matched by the internal window-six geometry.  The coordinate side distinguishes the six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes; the readout side is the standard codon-label table.  These are matched only at the level of finite label readback.

\paragraph{Observed boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is verified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite object is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it carries a local cubical boundary profile in which $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ square faces remain visible after the relevant puncture.  The module accounting is complete: the $13$ codons are partitioned without extras or omissions into the modules $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The classification reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no extra module codons, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The finite randomization test for the $M$-shape used $N=2000$ distinct sampled comparisons, with seed $42$.  Because distinct samples were enforced, the run made $57377$ sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicates.  The exact observed shape occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the high-overlap count was $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  These values are small enough to treat the observed local codon tile as a significant finite-row event under this sampling rule.  A second null model preserving amino-acid classes also rejects the shape as common: with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$-support size was $20$, exact-shape count was $0$, and only $7$ samples lay in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The corresponding values are $p=0.0$ for exact class-preserving shape match and $p=0.0035$ for the lower-tail event.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}

\mu&=3.051487585298128.

$$
The radial cubic check returns $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is at numerical zero for the recorded tolerance.  This spectral row does not add a biological mechanism.  It says that the same finite codon-label support that gives the local boundary set also carries a stable numerical signature under the chosen coordinate operators.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the five reported groups all remained above the baseline row: \texttt{alternative\_yeast} gave $0.8333333333333334$, while \texttt{bacterial\_plastid}, \texttt{candidate\_other}, \texttt{mitochondrial}, and \texttt{nuclear\_ciliate} each gave $1.0$.  The leave-one-codon-out row is also finite and bounded: anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These checks support a local code-read regularity rather than a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table.  It observes codon-to-label assignments and permits local codon-tile label-split tests.  It does not observe translation in a cell, folded structure, structural order, thermodynamic or kinetic admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label readback must not be promoted into a protein-structure claim.  The rows above justify the narrower statement that, in the standard-code label table, the window-six codon coordinates expose a significant local boundary-tile pattern with a $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary, a $20$-point observed $M$ support under the class-preserving null row, significant exact and high-Jaccard sampling values, coherent module coverage, and strong leave-one recovery statistics.  Any claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires a separate contact that measures that higher layer directly.
