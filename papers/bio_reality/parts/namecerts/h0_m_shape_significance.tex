\subsection{Window-six local tile significance in the standard code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The finite display has four relevant distinctions: a six-bit codon coordinate, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and a three-point label class.  Its internal reading uses coordinate placement, closure under the local adjacency rule, a spectral summary, and the induced relation between the tile and the curated codon labels.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: the observed table assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, folded protein structure, physical feasibility, biological role, or a universal generative mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The relation $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The rows are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite set is certified as a punctured cube with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it has a small cubical incidence profile inside the window-six coordinate system.  The module partition also accounts for every member of $R$: $\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$ in the $\mathrm{agr}$ module, $\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$ in $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$, $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ in $\mathrm{cun}$, $\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$ in $\mathrm{ile\_met}$, $\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$ in $\mathrm{ssn}$, and $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$ in $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$.  The classification reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $13$ assigned codons, no extra module codons, no missing rows, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
For the main shape test, the finite run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ null samples.  Producing those distinct samples required $57377$ attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The observed local shape appeared exactly once among the $2000$ samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The relaxed comparison counted $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both reported significance fields agree with these values: the exact-shape significance value is $0.0005$, and the high-Jaccard significance value is $0.0125$.  This supports the finite statement that the window-six local tile is unusually shaped relative to the sampled null population.  It does not by itself identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null comparison.}
A second null test preserved amino-acid class information.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$.  The exact class-preserving match count was $0$, so the reported exact-shape value was

$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving

The test therefore says that the observed size-and-shape behavior remains small under this class-aware comparison.  The conclusion is still at the label-table layer: the data distinguish code labels and local codon geometry, not protein realization.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The finite rows justify a constrained conclusion.  Window-six coordinates expose a local tile whose exact shape is rare in the sampled null, whose high-Jaccard neighborhood is also uncommon, whose boundary set has a compact punctured-cube profile, and whose row modules exactly cover the $13$ named boundary codons.  The curated standard-code table supplies the codon-to-label contact needed to read this as a code-layer pattern.  The result must not be promoted beyond that layer.  No statement about translation machinery, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows unless another observation supplies that higher-layer contact.
