\subsection{Window-Six Codon Shape Significance}
\label{sec:bior-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record uses the six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$ as its finite carrier. A codon is treated only as a point in this six-bit window together with a curated standard-code label. The observed biological object is therefore the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer, not translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, organismal function, mechanism, or universality. The finite row under inspection separates a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes inside the codon cube, while the amino-acid or stop labels are supplied by the curated code table rather than by the coordinate system itself.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports that this is a punctured-cube boundary with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a count of codons. It records a finite shape: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the local codon tile. The row also classifies all thirteen $R$ codons into named modules. The $AGR$ module is $\{AGA,AGG\}$, the $ar\_lys$ module is $\{AAA\}$, the $CUN$ module is $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, the $ile\_met$ module is $\{AUA\}$, the $SSN$ module is $\{UCA,UUA\}$, and the $stop\_trp$ module is $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$. The module audit reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite sampling.}
The main significance run sampled $N=2000$ distinct randomized comparison shapes using seed $42$. Because duplicate proposals were rejected, the run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Against this sample, the exact observed shape appeared once, while the high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared twenty-five times. The reported empirical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite conclusion is therefore a local significance statement: the observed window-six shape is rare in the sampled null population, and near matches are still uncommon. This does not license a biological necessity claim. It says that the code-read table, when read through the six-coordinate codon window, produces a boundary-tile pattern with low empirical frequency among the sampled alternatives.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving control.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class structure. In that run $N=2000$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-shape count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This control matters because it keeps one biologically meaningful label partition in place while still testing whether the local shape is routine. The result again supports a finite code-layer asymmetry: the observed geometry is not recovered as an ordinary consequence of the sampled label-preserving alternatives. The data row gives the size of $M$ but not a codon-by-codon listing of $M$, so the present statement uses the supplied cardinal statistic and does not infer an omitted membership table.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The same packet gives a spectral summary for the codon tile. The reported parameters are

\begin{aligned}

\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\

The cubic or radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at tolerance for the recorded polynomial relation. This is used only as a finite internal diagnostic of the tile computation. It is not a molecular-energy statement, not a folding spectrum, and not a physical model of translation.

\subsection{Leave-One-Out Checks}
\label{sec:bior-h0-m-shape-significance-leave-one-out}

\paragraph{Code-table deletion.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is recorded as the hit rate above baseline. These numbers say that the local coordinate-and-boundary rule recovers nearly all held-out code-table targets in this finite test. They do not say that the coordinate rule performs translation or supplies a universal genetic-code law.

\paragraph{Codon deletion.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. Since the recovery-rate threshold flag is also $1.0$, every target in the tested deletion row is recovered by the finite rule used in the packet. The interpretation remains deliberately narrow: recovery is a code-label recovery test inside the curated table interface, not a statement about ribosomal realization, protein shape, biophysical viability, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Family split.}
The leave-one-code-out family audit contains five families. The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate. This family row is evidence that the finite recovery behavior is not concentrated in only one named family of table variants. It still remains a label-table result: family robustness at the code-read layer is not a mechanism for translation and cannot be promoted into a claim about folding, structural order, admissibility, biological function, or universality without another observation source.

\subsection{Local Boundary of the Claim}
\label{sec:bior-h0-m-shape-significance-boundary}

\paragraph{What is supported.}
The supported claim is that window-six codon coordinates expose a statistically uncommon local boundary-tile pattern in the curated standard-code label table. The data include a thirteen-codon boundary set, a punctured-cube face vector, a complete module assignment for the boundary codons, two finite null-model significance rows, spectral diagnostics at numerical tolerance, and leave-one-out recovery rows with high hit rates. These are all finite, table-level observations. They support code-read structure and local prediction inside the supplied codon-label contact.

\paragraph{What is not supported.}
No higher biological layer follows from this packet alone. In particular, the row does not establish translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a global law for DNA. Amino-acid label readback is not folded structure. Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, or module names do not change the layer of the evidence. A claim at any of those higher layers would require an additional observation contact designed for that layer, such as a translation assay, structural measurement, physical admissibility model tied to experiment, or functional phenotype record.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is ready at the code-read layer. The strongest local sentence is: the curated standard-code table, read through the six-coordinate codon window, yields a rare and recoverable boundary-tile pattern with $|R|=13$, $f(R)=(13,16,4)$, $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$, amino-acid-class lower-tail value $0.0035$, and leave-one-code-out recovery $0.9841269841269841$ above baseline. The sentence must stop there unless a separate contact supplies evidence for a different biological layer.
