\subsection{Window-Six Codon Shape Significance}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
This record studies the standard genetic-code table through a six-coordinate codon display.  The finite object is the codon cube $Q_6$, with codons represented as six-bit words obtained from the three nucleotide positions.  The biological contact used here is deliberately narrow: it is a curated standard-code label table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop.  The claim is therefore a code-layer claim.  It asks whether the window-six coordinate presentation exposes a local boundary-tile pattern whose shape is unusually specific relative to finite null models.  It does not assert translation, folded structure, physical realizability, biological function, a mechanism of code formation, or a universal law.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the code-table contact is not being read as an open-ended biological principle; it is being used to test a precise finite subset of the codon cube.  The complementary shape statistic reports an observed $M$-size of $20$ in the amino-acid-class preserving null experiment.  The supplied row does not include a member-by-member $M$ list, so the present chapter uses the recorded cardinal and shape statistics rather than inventing codon membership for $M$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The main Monte Carlo row used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, which is important because the null space is finite and collisions are not negligible.  Exactly one sampled alternative matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-Jaccard row counted $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$.  In the amino-acid-class preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at the observed $M$-size was $7$.  This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These figures support a finite shape anomaly at the code-label level.  They do not identify a biochemical cause; they only say that the local tile seen in the curated standard-code labels is rare under the recorded finite comparison procedures.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The same packet records a three-parameter spectral summary.  The numerical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial value is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same number reported for the cubic-polynomial residual.  At the finite precision of the run, this is a near-zero residual rather than an additional biological observation.  It gives a numerical consistency check tying the $M$ and $R$ summaries to the recorded local geometry.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
A separate finite check evaluated $23$ alternative genetic-code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the recorded rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, across $5$ families.  These rows indicate that the local code-label pattern has predictive reach across curated code variants, but only for the finite code-read task being tested.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure coverage cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ with no extras, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification completeness row reports $R$-size $13$ and assigned count $13$.  This is a strong internal accounting check for the local boundary packet: every listed residual codon is placed in a named finite module, and every module has either predictable or boundary status.

\paragraph{Scope and exclusion.}
The contact is a code-label table, not an observation of ribosomal translation, molecular dynamics, folding, admissible chemistry, phenotype, or organism-level function.  Amino-acid label readback is not protein structure.  CUN, UUR, stop, tryptophan, lysine, arginine, isoleucine, and methionine labels in this chapter are labels in the curated table, not claims about physical realization.  To cross into translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality, a different contact would have to be supplied and tested on its own terms.  The present result is therefore ready as a finite code-read record: the window-six codon geometry exposes a statistically sharp local tile in the standard-code labels, while the biological interpretation remains confined to the table layer that was actually observed.
