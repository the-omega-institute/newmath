\subsection{Window-six codon shape and its code-level significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-reading layer.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is displayed as a six-bit word after choosing a fixed two-bit representation for each nucleotide.  This chapter does not treat the cube as a biochemical mechanism.  It treats it as a finite coordinate chart on which local tiles, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes can be compared against the curated codon-to-label table.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite rows isolate a boundary set $R$ of size $13$.  Its codon members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records that this set has the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed rows are not merely a list of exceptional labels.  They form a small finite subcomplex with $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ two-faces inside the six-coordinate display.  The role of this set is local: it marks where the standard-code labels sit on a particular codon-tile boundary, not where biology must place translation events.

\paragraph{Shape test for $M$.}
The shape statistic for $M$ was tested against $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported tail counts are attached to a nontrivial finite sampling process rather than to a single hand-picked comparison.  Only $1$ sampled alternative exactly matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the relaxed high-overlap statistic, $25$ sampled alternatives had Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row supports a code-layer shape signal: the observed local codon tile is rare under the recorded sampling comparison both exactly and at high Jaccard overlap.  The same numerical result must not be read as a statement about protein realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, translation machinery, or a universal law of genomes.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information and reports observed $|M|=20$.  In this null, $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$ produced $0$ exact shape matches, so the empirical exact-shape count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ matches}\}=0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail comparison counted $7$ samples at or below the observed $M$ size, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the local reading: even after preserving the amino-acid class constraint encoded by the finite comparison, the observed tile remains atypical in the recorded sample.  The conclusion is still about code-label geometry, not about the physical route by which a cell realizes any label.

$$
The corresponding cubic or radial check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically zero at the recorded tolerance.  The spectral radius entry repeats $0.6752479308830213$ for $M$.  These numbers provide a compact finite invariant for the observed tile and boundary pair.  They do not supply dynamics, kinetics, molecular structure, or an evolutionary explanation.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The recorded hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adding the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is recorded for the rate above baseline.  These rows show that the local tile and its boundary recover most held-out code-label targets in the finite comparison.  The result is a prediction-style readback at the codon-label layer; it is not evidence that the window-six chart implements translation or determines downstream biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records no anchors, $0$, but records $5$ boundary cases, $8$ predictable cases, and $8$ high-coverage closure cases.  Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{recovery\ rate}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within the finite packet, every held-out codon that is counted by the row is recovered through the local predictable-or-boundary mechanism.  This supports the claim that the coordinate display is useful for code-layer interpolation.  It still leaves translation realization, folding, admissible chemistry, and function outside the tested contact.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The boundary set $R$ is completely assigned to six modules.  The recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\

\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Therefore the module partition exactly accounts for the observed boundary set.  This exact accounting is one of the strongest finite checks in the packet: it connects the punctured-cube boundary to recognizable code-label modules without requiring a structural or mechanistic interpretation.

\paragraph{Reality contact and limitation.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, which supplies codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop labels.  It can test code readback and local codon-tile label splitting.  It cannot test translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or universal mechanism.  Any claim crossing those layers requires a separate contact that actually observes that layer.  In this chapter, the supported statement is narrower: the window-six coordinate chart exposes a statistically rare and internally organized local boundary tile in the standard-code label table, with $R$ of size $13$, observed $|M|=20$ in the class-preserving null row, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, exact-shape empirical probability $0.0005$, high-Jaccard empirical probability $0.0125$, and complete assignment of all $13$ boundary codons to the six recorded modules.
