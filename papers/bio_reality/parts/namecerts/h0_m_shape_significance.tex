\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile Shape Significance}
\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite object and readout level.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table only as a finite codon-to-label table.  The working carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon treated as a six-bit display point after choosing the usual four-symbol nucleotide alphabet presentation.  The relevant distinctions are coordinate position, local closure, spectral response, and adjacency relation inside this finite cube.  The biological readout attached to the carrier is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Thus the observed object is a code-layer label table, not a claim about translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The boundary subset detected by the finite readback has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that $R$ agrees with a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite local shape being tested: a codon-label boundary tile inside $Q_6$, with singleton-corner and three-point label-class features visible at the code-label layer.  No global biological necessity is asserted from this geometry.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo significance test.}
The primary shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes.  Because distinct samples were enforced, the run made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the accepted comparisons, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The resulting empirical probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape statistic is therefore a rare finite-row event under this sampling model, and the high-Jaccard statistic remains small but not zero.  The claim supported here is correspondingly modest: the observed standard-code label table places the $R$ tile in an unusually specific local shape class under the reported null comparison.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid label classes.  In that run, again with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the observed $M$-side size was $20$.  The exact-match count was $0$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite code-layer reading: even after preserving amino-acid classes, the observed local shape is not typical in the reported sample.  The numerical result still remains a comparison of finite codon-label arrangements, not a physical or functional conclusion.

\paragraph{Leave-one stability.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  The leave-one-codon-out row reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchors, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These rows indicate that the local tile is not merely a one-shot fit to the full table; it has finite deletion stability at the code-label layer.

The module audit reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Every module has a predictable or boundary status.  This supports the interpretation that the detected tile is organized by finite codon-label modules rather than by an unstructured list of exceptional codons.

\paragraph{Local verdict and boundary.}
The finite evidence supports the following code-layer statement: in the six-coordinate codon cube, the standard-code label table determines a $13$-codon boundary subset with punctured-cube face-vector $(13,16,4)$, rare exact-shape behavior under the reported Monte Carlo nulls, compatible spectral numerics, and stable leave-one/module behavior.  The claim stops there.  The curated table supplies codon-to-label readback only.  It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal mechanism, or a universal law.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact that directly measures the higher layer being claimed.
