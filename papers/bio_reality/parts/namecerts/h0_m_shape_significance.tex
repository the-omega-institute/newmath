\subsection{Window-six codon tile shape significance}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object and finite display.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label readback on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The biological object under inspection is not translation as a cellular process, but the curated standard-code table that assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The finite distinctions used here are the six-coordinate codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The internal structure consists of coordinates, closure, spectrum, and relation data, while the readback is only the standard-code label table.  Thus the claim lives at the code-read layer: a window-six coordinate system may expose a local boundary-tile pattern, but the labels themselves remain supplied by the curated code table.

\paragraph{Observed relation set.}
The finite relation set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run verifies that this is a punctured-cube shaped relation with face-vector data $(13,16,4)$.  This is a small local shape inside the full codon table, not a statement that the whole genetic code is forced by cube geometry.  The row is therefore interpreted as a finite tile boundary in the codon-label table: $R$ is large enough to have internal incidence structure, but still small enough that every biological reading must pass through the external label table rather than through geometry alone.

\paragraph{Shape significance under randomization.}
The primary shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparison rows.  Because distinct samples were required, the run made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ sampled rows, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ rows with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The reported finite probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape and high-Jaccard significance entries repeat these same values, $0.0005$ and $0.0125$.  The empirical result supports the narrow statement that the observed local codon-tile shape is uncommon under the sampled null comparison.  It does not identify a mechanism of codon assignment and does not promote a codon-window pattern into a law of biology.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information.  In that run, again with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the observed $M$ size was $20$.  The run found $0$ exact matches and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  Its exact-shape probability was $0.0$, while the lower-tail probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite-code reading: even under a null that respects amino-acid class, the observed arrangement remains unusual.  The available row data give the size of $M$ and its null-model behavior, but not a codon-by-codon listing of $M$ in this packet.  The claim is therefore phrased through the supplied $M$ size, the $R$ list, and the verified comparison statistics.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral probe separates the relation from a purely combinatorial count by recording numerical eigenvalue data.  The values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check produced $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same residual was reported for the cubic-polynomial check.  The spectral radius entry in tolerance was $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers give a stable finite numerical fingerprint for the local tile, with near-zero residual at the stated precision.  They do not by themselves say that codons are physically constrained by this spectrum; they only record that the finite code-read shape has a reproducible spectral signature.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables with $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the five reported groups had the following rates above baseline:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite rows are evidence for predictive stability at the code-table layer.  They say that when known code-table variants are tested against the local tile and its boundary, the omitted targets are usually recovered or boundary-accounted.  They still do not assert that the table is generated by the tile.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior and module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reported $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The recovery-rate-above-threshold entry was also $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and there are no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has either predictable or boundary behavior in the finite row.  This gives a concrete module-level decomposition of the local codon relation: the $13$ codons are not merely counted, but partitioned into named finite modules that exhaust the relation.

\paragraph{Reality contact and claim boundary.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code bridge: it supplies codon-to-label assignments at codon-label resolution.  That contact can test code readback and local codon-tile label splitting.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the admissible conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite rows support a statistically uncommon and spectrally reproducible local codon-tile pattern in the standard-code label table, together with strong leave-one-out behavior and complete module accounting for the listed $R$ codons.  No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows unless an independent higher-layer observation is attached.  In particular, amino-acid label readback is still label readback; it is not protein structure, and it is not evidence that a folded object has been realized.
