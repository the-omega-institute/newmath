\subsection{Window-six codon-tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim tested here is that the standard-code codon table, when displayed as a six-coordinate codon cube, contains a local boundary-tile pattern whose shape is unusually specific under the finite null ensembles that were run.  The object under inspection is not translation itself and not a molecular mechanism.  It is the code-read layer: a curated table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label is placed on the finite codon cube, and the resulting local split is tested as a combinatorial shape.  The relevant carrier is the window-six codon display $Q_6$, with distinctions for a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  Its internal bookkeeping uses coordinates, closure, spectrum, and relations, while its biological readback remains the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Finite codon residue.}
The verified residue $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The finite row gives the complete list
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports that $R$ matches a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite shape assertion about the displayed codon labels.  It says that the selected codons occupy a boundary-like local geometry inside the six-bit codon presentation.  It does not say that the geometry causes the standard code, nor that every organism must realize the same pattern as a law of biology.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The main shape-significance experiment used $N=2000$ sampled comparators with seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ sampled comparators there was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
At the high-overlap threshold, $25$ sampled comparators had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These two numbers are the quantitative core of the finite row.  Exact recovery of the observed shape is rare in this sample, and near recovery is also uncommon, though not absent.  The result therefore supports a local codon-table shape signal at the code-read level, not a global explanatory principle for translation or protein realization.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null ensemble preserved amino-acid class information.  It again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that ensemble the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  Thus the finite values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row does not supply an explicit codon enumeration for $M$, so the present record treats $M$ through its reported size and null behavior.  Within that limitation, the class-preserving null strengthens the conclusion that the observed local shape is not a routine consequence of label-class preservation alone.

The spectral row records three scalar values associated with the local codon geometry:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic or radial polynomial residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is within numerical tolerance for the recorded spectral relation.  This supplies an internal numerical consistency check for the displayed shape.  It is not a physical-energy calculation, a folding prediction, or a claim about biochemical admissibility.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the finite row reports $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$.  These figures show that the local codon-tile construction has predictive stability across the tested code-table deletions, but the prediction is still about code labels in finite tables.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The recovery-rate threshold check is also $1.0$.  The complete module classification assigns all $13$ residue codons with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The assigned count is $13$, matching $|R|=13$.  Every listed module contains either predictable or boundary-supported codons, so the finite partition is closed over the reported residue set.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact observes that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  It can test code-read behavior and a local codon-tile label split.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, folded protein structure, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the chapter records a significant finite codon-label shape in the standard-code table, together with stability checks under leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out probes.  No conclusion about folding, function, molecular implementation, or biological necessity follows from these rows unless an independent higher-layer contact is supplied.
