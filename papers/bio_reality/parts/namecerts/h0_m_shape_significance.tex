\subsection{Window-six codon boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Coordinate claim.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table seen through a six-coordinate codon display. Each codon is treated as a point of the codon cube $Q_6$, while the curated standard-code table supplies only the amino-acid or stop label attached to that point. The packet does not treat that table as an explanation of translation. It uses the table as a label contact and asks whether the window-six coordinates expose a local boundary tile around a finite family of codons whose code labels are unstable across nearby code variants.

The finite distinguished set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is reported as a punctured-cube shape with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the local shape is not merely a list of exceptional labels: it carries a finite cubical incidence profile inside $Q_6$. The row also records an observed $M$-region of size $20$ under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model. The available finite data give the size and significance behavior of $M$, but not an explicit enumerated list of its $20$ codons.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. Exact recovery of the observed shape occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9} &= 0.0125,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $25$ high-Jaccard matches. This is the finite-row basis for treating the tile as a significant local pattern rather than a freely recurring random artifact. The test is still a code-layer test: it compares coordinate-and-label shape under finite randomization, not biochemical realization.

A second null model preserves amino-acid class. In that row, $N=2000$, the exact-shape count is $0$, and the lower-tail count for sets no larger than the observed $M$-size is $7$. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035,\\
|M|_{\mathrm{obs}} &= 20.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the local reading: even after respecting amino-acid-class structure, the observed window-six tile is not reproduced in the finite sample. The conclusion remains about the labeled codon table and its coordinate geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The same packet records a spectral profile for the local relation. The numerical row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic and radial polynomial residuals are both reported as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical witness is stable to the stated tolerance. The value $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ is also recorded as the spectral radius in tolerance. These scalars give a second finite check on the same local object: the tile is not only a set-theoretic boundary but also has a reproducible relational spectrum in the row data.

\paragraph{Leave-one recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. Against that baseline, the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, no anchors, closure coverage high at $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. These recovery rows support the local-boundary interpretation: removing one table or one codon still leaves enough coordinate-and-label information to recover the finite boundary behavior with high success.

The family split has $5$ families. The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate. The module classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and the rows list no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has either predictable or boundary status in the finite row. This is a precise code-read statement about the curated label table and the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Biological boundary.}
The contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table. It observes that codons carry amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code. It does not observe ribosomal translation, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, cellular function, or a universal mechanism. Consequently, the chapter may say that window-six codon coordinates expose a significant local boundary tile for code-label readback. It may not say that DNA is forced by this geometry, that a label sequence is a folded protein, that CUN or UUR wording is structure evidence, or that the tile is a global biological law. Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact at the translation, structure, physics, or function layer.
