\subsection{Window-six codon boundary shape significance}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier and observed contact.}
The packet for claim $\mathrm{h0.M.shape.significance}$ is a finite record over the codon cube $Q_6$. Its coordinate display treats each codon as a six-bit word, while the biological readback is restricted to the curated standard-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The internal distinctions used here are the coordinate positions of the cube, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The reality contact is therefore only a code-table contact: it says which codon label appears in the standard table, and it does not by itself observe translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Boundary set $R$.}
The boundary row $R$ is finite and explicit. The verified cardinality row gives $|R|=13$, and the listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check returns the same roster and the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a count: it is a concrete subset of the sixty-four codon coordinates, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the local tile complex. This is the geometric object whose code-label readback is being tested.

\paragraph{Shape significance row.}
The finite randomization row for the $M$-shape comparison used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled tables. The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported sample is a finite distinct-sample experiment rather than a raw independent-draw count. Among the $2000$ accepted samples, exactly one sample matched the observed shape exactly, and twenty-five samples reached Jaccard score at least $0.9$. The resulting finite tail estimates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row stores these as $p\_value\_exact\_shape\_significant=0.0005$ and $p\_value\_high\_jaccard\_significant=0.0125$. The reading is local: the standard-code labels give a rare window-six shape under this finite null procedure, but the result is a code-layer shape statement, not a biological law.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null row.}
A second finite null keeps amino-acid class structure fixed while testing the observed $M$ size. This row also uses seed $42$ and $N=2000$. It reports observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$ for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape statistic is therefore absent in the sampled class-preserving null, while the lower-tail event appears seven times in two thousand accepted samples. This strengthens the finite code-table anomaly reading, but it still remains attached to the codon-label layer.

Since the residual is at numerical roundoff scale for the recorded computation, this row supplies a reproducible numerical signature for the local tile shape. It does not supply a mechanism for why a cellular translation system realizes the table.

These numbers show that the local coordinate packet has predictive reach over code-table label variation in the tested finite collection. They do not show that organisms must obey this geometry, and they do not convert label variation into protein structure or biological function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row and module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure coverage high $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The recovery-rate-above-threshold row is also $1.0$. The module classification is complete: $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}

Every module has either a predictable or boundary status, and the six displayed modules partition the thirteen-codon boundary set. This gives a finite module-level explanation of the local label split, but only at the level of codon-table readback.

\paragraph{Local scientific verdict.}
The claim is ready as a code-read statement. The finite evidence says that the standard-code table, when read through window-six coordinates, contains a rare local boundary-tile shape: $R$ has thirteen explicitly listed codons, the punctured-cube vector is $(13,16,4)$, the exact-shape sample probability is $0.0005$, the high-Jaccard sample probability is $0.0125$, the amino-acid-class preserving lower-tail probability is $0.0035$, and the leave-one tests recover the boundary/module structure at rates near one in the reported finite rows. The permissible conclusion is that window-six codon coordinates expose a statistically sharp local organization in the curated code labels. The impermissible conclusion would be to promote that local organization into translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a global law. Any such promotion requires a separate observation route whose contact actually measures the higher-layer object.
