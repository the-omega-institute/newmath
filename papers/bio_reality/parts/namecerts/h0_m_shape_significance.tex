\subsection{Window-Six Local Tile Shape in the Standard Code}
\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate packet.}
The packet records a code-layer observation on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is treated as a six-bit display point, with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes available as finite distinctions.  The observation is not a claim about translation dynamics.  It is a finite comparison between the standard-code label table and the local combinatorics exposed by the window-six display.  The curated standard-code table is used only as the contact assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label; it supplies the readback labels and does not supply a fold, a biochemical pathway, a physical admissibility test, a function, or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The verified boundary subset $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this is the expected codon set for the packet.  A separate finite-topological check identifies the same $R$ as the punctured-cube boundary pattern and reports the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed shape is not merely a count: the row records $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the local cell complex determined by the codon subset.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparators.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries, so the null comparison was visibly constrained by repeated finite draws.  The exact observed shape appeared once among the $2000$ samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A relaxed comparison at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ produced $25$ matches among $2000$ samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row therefore supports a narrow statement: the window-six codon-coordinate packet isolates a local tile shape whose exact finite geometry is rare under the sampled comparison, while high-overlap shapes remain uncommon but not absent.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The conclusion is deliberately local.  Window-six geometry may expose a rare and recoverable tile boundary in the standard-code label table.  The table contact permits codon-label readback and local split checks only.  No claim about protein realization, folded structure, structural order, translation realization, physical admissibility, biological function, global mechanism, or universality follows from these rows.  Any such claim would require a separate reality contact with its own observed facts.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded protein structures, and cross-organism wording or tRNA-family notation cannot promote this code-layer packet into a structural or functional result.
