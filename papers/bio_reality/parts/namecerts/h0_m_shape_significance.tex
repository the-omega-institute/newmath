\subsection{Window-Six Codon Shape Significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim tested here is a code-layer statement about the standard genetic-code table.  Codons are displayed as six-bit window coordinates in the codon cube $Q_6$, and the question is whether the standard-code label table leaves a small local boundary pattern that is unusually shaped relative to finite reshufflings.  The biological object under inspection is only the curated codon-to-label table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label.  The finite carrier therefore has a coordinate side, where codons occupy cube vertices, and a label-read side, where the observed table supplies the labels.  The contact does not observe ribosomal translation, folded protein geometry, chemical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the verified row it is exactly
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row records the punctured-cube face data
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}$$
so the local object is not merely a count of exceptional codons.  It has a finite adjacency-and-face profile inside the six-coordinate display.  The codons split naturally into the recorded modules
$$\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{agr}=\{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\},\quad
\texttt{ar\_lys}=\{\texttt{AAA}\},\quad
\texttt{cun}=\{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\},\\
&\texttt{ile\_met}=\{\texttt{AUA}\},\quad
\texttt{ssn}=\{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\},\quad
\texttt{stop\_trp}=\{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The module audit is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ elements are assigned, and the recorded lists of extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite sampling row used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled tables.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the experiment is explicitly a finite-row computation rather than an asymptotic claim.  The exact local shape appeared once among the $2000$ samples, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}$$
A looser high-overlap test found $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
 p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving comparison was also run at $N=2000$ with seed $42$.  In that row the observed $M$-size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$.  Thus the recorded class-preserving probabilities are
$$\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{class,tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
These numbers support a narrow conclusion: the standard-code labels create a rare local shape under the reported finite null models.  They do not by themselves identify the biological cause of the shape.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum row gives a second finite diagnostic.  The recorded values are
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row reports a radial cubic residual of $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$ and the identical value for the cubic-polynomial check.  Within the stated computation, this is numerical agreement at near-zero residual scale.  The spectral row is therefore consistent with the same local codon-tile object detected by the shape and punctured-cube rows, but it remains a coordinate diagnostic of the code table rather than a physical law of translation.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the recorded hit rate in $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$ and the hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  The leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These rows show that the local code-layer pattern has finite predictive stability under the reported leave-one tests.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The contact used here is the curated standard-code label table.  It is a legitimate source for codon-label readback and for local label-split checks, but it is not a measurement of molecular realization.  Therefore the chapter licenses the following statement and no stronger one: window-six codon coordinates expose a rare and stable local boundary pattern in the standard-code label table.  No conclusion about translation dynamics, folded structure, structural ordering, chemical possibility, biological function, global necessity, or universality follows from these rows unless an additional contact directly measures that higher layer.  Amino-acid label sequences remain labels in this chapter; they are not protein folds.  The mixed or informal naming of a cross-organism probe likewise supplies no structural evidence.  Any future promotion beyond the code-read layer must be backed by a separate organism-matched or structure-level observation.
