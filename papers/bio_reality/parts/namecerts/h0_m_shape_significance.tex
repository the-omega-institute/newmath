\subsection{Window-Six Codon Shape Significance}
\label{sec:bior-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard genetic-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and the mathematical object tested here is the local label geometry induced by that assignment. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with coordinates supplied by the six-bit codon display. The relevant distinctions are the local tile face, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class. The internal organization used by the row is coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation; the readback is only the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The relation row isolates a thirteen-codon set $R$. In lexicographic codon notation the observed set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit records $|R|=13$. The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube-shaped boundary object with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed object is not merely a list of reassignment-sensitive codons; it has a finite cubical incidence profile inside the window-six coordinate system.

\paragraph{Shape-significance test.}
The primary significance row compares the observed $M$-shape against a sampled null over distinct random attempts. The run used seed $42$, drew $N=2000$ accepted samples, and required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts because $55377$ attempts repeated an already-seen sample. The exact observed shape appeared once among the $2000$ accepted samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold also remained sparse: $25$ sampled shapes had Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
This supports the chapter claim at the code-read layer: the window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern that is unusual under the sampled shape null. The conclusion is a finite statistical statement about the curated label table, not a statement about biochemical necessity.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
A second null keeps amino-acid class information constrained. Under that comparison, again with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the exact shape count is $0$. The observed $M$ size is $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below that size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving run therefore does not explain away the local shape as a generic consequence of coarse amino-acid class structure. It strengthens the finite-code reading while still leaving the mechanism question untouched.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral row gives a compact numerical invariant for the same finite object. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated cubic relation is numerically satisfied to tolerance, with polynomial value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$. The spectral-radius value reported in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$. This row is useful because it compresses the local shape into a reproducible scalar profile, while the codon-list row keeps the biological readback explicit.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
Across $23$ evaluated code tables and $63$ total targets, the baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The local model reaches a hit rate of $0.9365079365079365$ inside $M\setminus T$ and $0.9841269841269841$ after adding the boundary, with the above-baseline hit rate also $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the reported hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast. These finite rows say that the local codon geometry has predictive contact with code-table variation, but only as codon-label readback.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, and $8$ high-coverage closure cases. The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the thresholded recovery value is also $1.0$. The module classification accounts for every codon in $R$: $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The audit gives $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, and empty extra, missing, and unassigned lists. Hence the module split is complete for the observed boundary set.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The chapter records a statistically sharp local geometry in the standard-code label table: a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary, an unusual $M$-shape under two finite null models, a reproducible spectral profile, and strong leave-one-out behavior over codon-label targets. The contact does not observe translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or any law saying that biology must instantiate the coordinate geometry. Those stronger claims require separate contacts of the appropriate kind. In this chapter the admissible conclusion is only that window-six codon coordinates reveal a local boundary-tile structure in curated code labels, with the table itself supplying the reality contact for label readback.
