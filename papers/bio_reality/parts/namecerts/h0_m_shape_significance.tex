\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, whose points are the finite codon labels represented by their window-six coordinates.  The relevant observation is not a claim about the whole biology of translation.  It is a code-layer claim: a curated standard genetic-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and the induced label split can be inspected as a local tile pattern inside $Q_6$.  The contact therefore supplies codon-to-label readback and local split tests only.

The finite exceptional boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the run used here it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row verifies the punctured-cube shape by the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary object is not merely described by its size: the row fixes the actual codons in $R$ and the combinatorial face counts of the induced tile.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under label-respecting randomization.}
The primary significance probe used $N=2000$ randomized samples with seed $42$.  Because the sampling required distinct draws, the run made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ accepted samples, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$.  Consequently the empirical exact-shape probability and high-overlap probability were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers make the local tile an unusually specific code-layer pattern relative to the sampled null family.  They do not by themselves turn the tile into a biological law; they say that the observed standard-code split has a rare finite shape under the stated shuffle experiment.

A second null row preserved amino-acid class structure.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed size of the companion set $M$ was $20$.  The exact-shape count was $0$, so the empirical exact-shape value was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second row is important because it keeps the comparison closer to the amino-acid-label organization while still finding the observed local size and shape to be rare.

\paragraph{Spectral readout.}
The same finite packet records a spectral summary.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial check is numerically closed at
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same value reported for the cubic polynomial residual.  Within the recorded tolerance, the spectral radius attached to $M$ is therefore $0.6752479308830213$.  This is a numerical invariant of the finite codon tile construction, not a claim that a molecule realizes this spectrum as a physical operator.

\paragraph{Recovery probes.}
Leave-one-code-out testing evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is recorded for hit rate above baseline.  Family-level rows separate the result across five families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.

The leave-one-codon-out row records no anchors, five boundary cases, eight high-coverage closure cases, and eight predictable cases.  Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same value reported above the recovery threshold.  The module classification is complete on the $13$ codons of $R$: the assigned count is $13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable or boundary status.  This gives the local tile a finite module-level accounting rather than only a global score.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The validated conclusion is deliberately narrow.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern in the standard-code label table, and the finite rows above show rarity, spectral structure, recovery behavior, and complete module assignment for that code-layer pattern.  The curated table, however, contains codon-to-label assignments only.  It does not observe translation as a biochemical process, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code formation, or a universal law across life.  Any statement crossing from code labels to those higher layers would require a separate contact that directly measures the relevant layer.  Without such a contact, amino-acid label-sequence readback remains a code-level object and cannot be promoted to folded protein structure or functional biology.
