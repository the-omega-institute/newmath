\subsection{Window-six codon tiles and the standard-code label split}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The object under study is the standard-code codon table, read only at the level of codon labels.  Each codon is displayed as a six-coordinate binary window, so that a codon table becomes a labelled finite subset of the codon cube $Q_6$.  The claim is not that the six-coordinate display causes translation or determines protein structure.  The claim is narrower: after the curated standard-code labels are placed on $Q_6$, the local boundary between a singleton label corner and a three-point label class has an unusually rigid finite shape.  The observed boundary set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified row records $|R|=13$.  The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face data $(13,16,4)$, so the witness is not merely a count: it has vertices, adjacency, and square-face incidence recorded as a finite combinatorial shape.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate content.}
The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with the curated standard genetic-code table supplying the amino-acid or stop label at each of the $64$ codon positions.  The distinction being tested is local: it compares a singleton label corner against a neighboring three-point label class inside the six-coordinate display.  The finite rows give the observed marker set $R$ explicitly and give an observed companion tile size $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null calculation.  The supplied rows do not list the elements of $M$, so this chapter uses only the verified size and the verified shape tests for $M$, while spelling out the full verified membership of $R$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Under the reported sampling experiment with seed $42$, the null run used $N=2000$ randomized tables.  Because the sampler required distinct samples, it made $57377$ sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  The observed exact shape reappeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local shape is a rare event in the finite comparison class: exact recovery occurs in $0.05\%$ of the sampled alternatives, while a high-overlap recovery at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurs in $1.25\%$ of them.  A second null that preserves amino-acid class information reports $N=2000$, $0$ exact matches, and only $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size.  In the recorded finite arithmetic this gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore not only a raw-coordinate accident: it remains small when the comparison is constrained by amino-acid class.

The marker-side value is $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, the boundary-side value is $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and the radial parameter is $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, reported equivalently as the radial polynomial value.  At the precision of the finite run, this means the spectral datum satisfies its cubic check to numerical tolerance.  The spectral radius row also records $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$ in the reported tolerance field.

\paragraph{Prediction checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ alternative genetic-code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the boundary-augmented prediction reaches $0.9841269841269841$.  The restricted rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary again reaches $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  These five family rows are finite readbacks of the same local codon geometry; they are not independent evidence for a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Codon-removal module row.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor cases, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no extras, no omissions, and no unassigned codons.  The verified modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This decomposition is the strongest finite organization recorded here: it shows that the same $13$-codon boundary can be partitioned into biologically named codon modules while preserving complete assignment.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which says which amino-acid or stop label is assigned to each codon.  That contact is enough to test code-layer label readback and local codon-tile splitting.  It is not an observation of ribosomal translation, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, evolutionary mechanism, or universality across life.  Consequently, this chapter supports a finite code-layer statement: the standard-code labels, when placed on $Q_6$, expose a rare local boundary shape with explicit codon membership, significant null-model behavior, a numerical spectral check, and high recovery in leave-one-out probes.  Any claim about translation, folding, physical realization, function, mechanism, or universal law would require an additional contact designed for that higher layer.
