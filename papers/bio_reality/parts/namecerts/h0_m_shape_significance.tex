\subsection{Window-six codon geometry and local label boundary}
\label{sec:bireality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The claim identified by \texttt{h0.M.shape.significance} concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube. The carrier is the finite cube $Q_6$, whose displayed points are codons written in the usual alphabet $\{\mathrm{A},\mathrm{C},\mathrm{G},\mathrm{U}\}^3$. The chapter records a local geometric observation: a window-six coordinate presentation exposes a boundary-tile pattern in the codon label table, while the amino-acid and stop labels themselves remain supplied by the curated standard-code table. The layer asserted here is only code readback. It is not a claim about translation execution, folded protein structure, physical realizability, biological function, a causal mechanism, or a universal rule across life.

Thus the observation is not merely that a chosen codon list has thirteen entries. The finite check reports a specific local cell count: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the displayed residual tile. This is the concrete object whose shape is tested against randomized alternatives.

\paragraph{Shape significance under the main sampling row.}
For the main significance check, the verified experiment used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparison rows. The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported probability is tied to a finite deduplicated sampling process rather than an informal visual comparison. Among the $2000$ sampled alternatives, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ cases with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$. The finite-row estimates are therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are recorded as significant in the supplied row: $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$ for exact shape agreement and $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$ for the high-overlap relaxation. The claim supported by these numbers is narrow: the observed local tile is rare under the stated finite comparison procedure. The numbers do not establish why the biological table has that tile, and they do not license promotion from code labels to physical or functional biology.

\paragraph{Reality contact and blocked promotions.}
The empirical contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table. Its observed content is a codon-to-label assignment: each codon is read as an amino-acid label or stop label. That contact can test code-layer label readback and local tile splits. It cannot by itself test translation execution, structural order, physical admissibility, folded form, biological function, or universality. Consequently, amino-acid label sequences produced from this table must not be treated as protein structures. Cross-organism wording, CUN/UUR naming, or tRNA-associated notation likewise remains outside this chapter unless matched to a separate organism-specific or structure-specific contact. The proper conclusion is therefore bounded: the six-coordinate codon display gives a statistically rare and reproducibly structured local boundary tile in the standard-code label table, with residual set $R$, module partition, spectral fingerprint, and leave-one-out behavior as listed above. No higher biological layer follows from these rows alone.
