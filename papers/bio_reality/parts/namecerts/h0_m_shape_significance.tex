\subsection{Window-six codon shape significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table viewed at the code-read layer through a six-bit codon display. Each codon is treated as a vertex of the codon cube $Q_6$, with the table supplying only the observed amino-acid or stop label attached to that vertex. The finite packet separates four ingredients: the coordinate cube, a closure operation on local tiles, the spectral readout of the induced relation, and the curated standard-code label table used for readback. The biological object is therefore not translation as a process, but the labeled codon table as a finite contact.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its verified codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a local shape statement about selected codon vertices and tile faces. It does not assert that the cube geometry is a biological law; it records that the standard-code labels, when read through this six-bit coordinate system, isolate this finite boundary pattern.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo shape test.}
The primary shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled comparison shapes. Because distinct comparison rows were required, the sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the $2000$ retained samples, exactly $1$ matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap check counted $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed local tile is rare under the sampled finite-row comparison model, both for exact equality and for near-equality. The result is a code-table regularity, not an explanation of molecular implementation.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid class information and again used seed $42$ with $N=2000$. The observed $M$ size was $20$. In this null, the exact shape count was $0$, so the empirical exact-shape probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite claim that the observed code-read shape is not a typical output of that class-preserving comparison. It still does not convert a label table into a theory of folding, admissible biophysics, or organism-level function.

\paragraph{Spectral readout.}
The finite relation associated with the local codon tile has verified spectral values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the radial polynomial evaluation gives the same residual value. The spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$. These numbers provide a finite algebraic check on the local relation attached to the observed shape; they are not a separate biological contact.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out evaluation used $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.

For leave-one-codon-out recovery, the verified row has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The threshold comparison also reports $1.0$. These checks show that the local boundary-and-closure description has predictive stability inside the finite code-read experiment, while remaining silent about translation realization as a physical process.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The $13$ codons of $R$ are completely assigned to six modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification report has $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from modules, and no unassigned codons. Every module has either predictable or boundary status. This is the finite module-level content of the claim.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code-read label structure and local codon-tile splits. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. In particular, amino-acid label readback is not a protein-structure observation, and cross-organism wording about codon families or tRNA notation does not supply a structure contact. Any claim crossing from this local code-read packet into folding, function, physical mechanism, or universal biological law would require a separate observation designed for that higher layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite rows support a narrow conclusion: in the standard-code codon table, the window-six coordinate representation exposes a statistically uncommon local boundary-tile pattern with $|R|=13$, face-vector $(13,16,4)$, exact-shape probability $0.0005$, high-Jaccard probability $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail probability $0.0035$, stable spectral readout, complete module assignment, and strong leave-one recovery behavior. The conclusion remains confined to code-layer readback from a curated table.
