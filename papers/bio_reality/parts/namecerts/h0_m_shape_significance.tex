\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and readout.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through the finite six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  Each row is a codon display, and the observed labels are the curated amino-acid or stop labels attached to those codons by the standard code.  The mathematical object under inspection is therefore not translation itself, but a finite labelled cube in which local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes can be counted and compared.  The relevant relation row is the set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit reports $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons appear in the punctured-cube row.  The reported face-count vector for that punctured cube is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the record has a concrete local shape: thirteen selected codon vertices, sixteen selected one-dimensional adjacencies, and four selected two-dimensional faces inside the six-coordinate codon display.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The principal finite-row test compares the observed local shape against $N=2000$ random samples generated with seed $42$.  The run needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so the null sampling was operating in a finite space with substantial repeated draws.  Among the $2000$ samples, exactly $1$ matched the observed shape exactly and $25$ achieved Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The resulting reported tail frequencies are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a narrow significance claim: the observed window-six local tile is rare under the stated finite sampler.  They do not by themselves identify a biological cause, a translational mechanism, or a universal law.  The evidence is a coded-label geometry result tied to the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null comparison.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class information and again uses $N=2000$ with seed $42$.  In that test the observed module has supplied size $20$.  The exact-shape count is $0$, giving the reported exact-shape frequency $0.0$.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving comparison is important because it asks whether the same shape can be dismissed as an artifact of label-class preservation alone.  In these finite rows, it cannot: the exact shape is absent from the $2000$ class-preserving samples, and the lower tail remains small.  The conclusion remains local and statistical, not mechanistic.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, molecular structure, folding order, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular phenotype, or function.  The chapter therefore treats the window-six pattern as a significant finite code-layer shape with a concrete relation set, spectral row, null comparisons, and omission tests.  Any statement about translation, folded protein structure, physical feasibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal necessity would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind.  In the present record, the supported conclusion is only that the standard-code labels exhibit a rare and recoverable local tile pattern in the six-coordinate codon display.
