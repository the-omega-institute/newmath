\subsection{Window-six local codon-tile significance}
\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and readout.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is read as a six-bit display, and the finite observation is kept at the level of code labels: a codon has an amino-acid or stop label supplied by a curated standard-code table.  The construction separates four local ingredients: coordinates in the cube, closure under the codon-neighborhood rule, a spectral summary of the resulting finite relation, and a label relation back to the curated table.  The biological object under inspection is therefore not translation as a cellular process, but the finite standard-code label table as a code-read object.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check returns the same thirteen codons and the face-count vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite pattern is not merely a count: it is a small local subcomplex with thirteen codon vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local two-dimensional faces.  The row is organized by six modules.  The module assignment is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and there are no extra module codons, missing codons, or unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},&
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},&
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module contains a codon that is predictable from the local test or is placed on the boundary, so the module-level row does not leave an unexplained residue inside $R$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The primary finite test used $N=2000$ sampled comparators with seed $42$.  Producing these $2000$ distinct samples required $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which records that the comparison was performed over distinct rows rather than over repeated draws.  The observed shape occurred exactly once among the $2000$ comparators, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-Jaccard statistic counted $25$ comparators with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The exact-shape and high-overlap rows are therefore both small-tail observations for this finite null experiment.  A second null model preserving amino-acid classes evaluated $N=2000$ samples, found $0$ exact matches to the observed shape, and had $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size.  The recorded observed value was $|M|=20$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a local significance claim for the window-six tile geometry relative to the finite comparators.  They do not by themselves say why the standard code has this form, nor do they identify a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check returned $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also recorded as the cubic-polynomial residual.  This residual is numerically near zero at the stated precision, so the spectral row is consistent with the finite cubic relation used by the probe.  The value $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ is also the recorded spectral radius within tolerance.  In this chapter the spectral data are used only as finite diagnostics of the codon-tile relation; they are not treated as a law of molecular dynamics.

\paragraph{Predictive checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the same row reports $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$ above baseline, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each record $1.0$.  The leave-one-codon-out row has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These finite values say that the local tile and boundary tests recover many held-out code-layer entries.  They do not promote the code readout to a statement about protein realization.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The supporting contact is a curated standard genetic-code bridge: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  That contact can test code-read claims and local codon-label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or any claim that the geometry is biologically necessary.  Amino-acid label sequences remain label sequences in this record.  A separate structure contact would be required before making a folding claim; a separate translation contact would be required before making a translation-process claim; a separate functional assay would be required before making a function claim.  The finite row is therefore ready as a local codon-table significance result, with the biological boundary explicitly held at the code-label layer.
