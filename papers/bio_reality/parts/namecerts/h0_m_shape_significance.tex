\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record studies whether a six-coordinate codon display can expose a reproducible local tile in the standard genetic-code label table. The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, read as a six-bit presentation of the $64$ codons. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The analysis asks whether the window-six coordinates, the local face structure, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class pick out a statistically unusual boundary pattern at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Finite codon set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation the verified set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run verifies that $R$ is the expected punctured-cube boundary, with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed object is not an arbitrary list of recoded codons: it is a finite subcomplex-like pattern in the six-bit codon cube, with $13$ vertices, $16$ local adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the checked boundary representation.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The main finite-row test sampled $N=2000$ null shapes with seed $42$. The distinct-sample generator needed $57377$ attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the observed comparison is being made against a heavily constrained finite null ensemble rather than against an unconstrained continuum model. The exact observed shape appeared once among the $2000$ samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\text{exact matches}\} &= 1, & p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A high-overlap relaxation using Jaccard threshold $0.9$ produced $25$ matches, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{J\geq 0.9\} &= 25, & p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the limited claim that the observed local tile is rare in this finite coordinate null model. They do not by themselves identify a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null preserves amino-acid class information. In that null, the observed $M$-tile size is $20$. Among $2000$ sampled class-preserving tables, the exact-shape count was $0$, so the empirical exact-shape estimate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa,class,exact}}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa,class,lower}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the strongest finite-row evidence in the packet: even after preserving amino-acid-class structure, the observed local pattern remains in a small empirical tail.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The same packet records a spectral summary for the two finite regions. The verified values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual was $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, equal to the recorded cubic-polynomial residual to the displayed precision. This places the spectral computation within numerical tolerance and gives an independent finite invariant attached to the same local tile. The spectral line is evidence about the coordinate construction and its checked numerical consistency; it is not evidence that a protein fold, interaction, or organism-level function has been observed.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.}
The cross-table code-read probe evaluated $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ target cases. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, while the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is recorded as the rate above baseline. By family, the above-baseline hit rates were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The five-family summary is therefore consistent with a stable code-read boundary signal, with the weakest listed family still far above the baseline recorded by the same finite run.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out module check.}
The codon-level ablation probe records $0$ anchor cases, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module partition is complete over $R$: $13$ assigned codons, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. The verified module classes are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has either a predictable member or a boundary role in the recorded classification. This matters because the local tile is not only a global count anomaly; it is also organized into the known reassignment families captured by the finite module audit.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The result is a code-layer statement. The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label observations, and the coordinate analysis detects a rare local boundary tile in that finite table. The supported conclusion is that the six-bit codon presentation exposes a non-generic local shape around the listed $R$ codons, with exact-shape empirical probability $0.0005$, high-Jaccard empirical probability $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail probability $0.0035$, and complete module coverage over the $13$ boundary codons.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
Nothing in this packet shows translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law. Amino-acid label readback remains a label-table fact. A protein-structure claim would require an independent structure observation; a physical-admissibility claim would require an independent physical or biochemical contact; a function claim would require a functional assay or equivalent organism-level evidence. The present evidence is therefore significant for local codon-table geometry and for code-read prediction only.
