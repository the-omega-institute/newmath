\subsection{Window-six local shape in the standard-code table}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table through the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is treated as a finite word over the usual nucleotide alphabet and displayed as a six-bit local coordinate, so the object under inspection is not translation itself but a finite labeled table.  The observed contact is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is strong enough to test code-layer readback and local label splitting, but it does not observe protein synthesis, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism of code formation, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Boundary subset.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The finite row identifies it as
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this set is the punctured-cube boundary pattern seen by the coordinate test, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the code-layer datum is not merely a count: it is a finite local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces after the puncture implied by the observed label pattern.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ null samples.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries, showing that the null space was sampled under a uniqueness constraint rather than by accepting repeated draws.  The exact observed shape occurred once among the $2000$ trials, while $25$ trials reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The finite estimates are therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a local-shape claim: the standard-code labels induce a rare window-six tile among the tested alternatives.  They do not, by themselves, identify why the biological code has this form.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class constraints.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed size of the comparison set $M$ was $20$.  The exact-shape count was $0$, giving the recorded exact-shape estimate
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the interpretation that the local tile is not a routine artifact of preserving broad amino-acid classes.  The conclusion remains finite and empirical: it concerns the tested table and the stated null ensembles.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ alternative code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was

\end{aligned}
$$
Against this baseline, the recorded hit rate above baseline was
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$ and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  This shows that the local code-read geometry has predictive reach across the evaluated table variants, with the alternative-yeast family being the only recorded family below perfect recovery in this finite summary.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The codon-level deletion test reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, and $0$ anchor cases.  The high-coverage closure count is $8$, and the recovery rate is

\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The row also records that this rate is above the stated threshold.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$: $\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}$ form the $\mathrm{agr}$ module; $\mathrm{AAA}$ forms $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$; $\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}$ form $\mathrm{cun}$; $\mathrm{AUA}$ forms $\mathrm{ile\_met}$; $\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}$ form $\mathrm{ssn}$; and $\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}$ form $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$.  The assigned count is $13$, with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite evidence supports the following bounded claim.  In the standard-code codon table, the window-six codon representation exposes a rare local boundary tile, with a $13$-codon punctured-cube set, face-vector $(13,16,4)$, significant exact and high-Jaccard null behavior, a matching spectral signature, and strong leave-one-out recovery at both code-table and codon levels.  The observed object is nevertheless a code-layer table.  The same evidence must not be promoted to translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality unless another contact directly measures the promoted layer.
