\subsection{Window-six codon tile shape significance}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Coordinate setting.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table after it is placed on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The carrier is the finite set of codon displays, with coordinates supplied by the three nucleotide positions and their binary presentation.  The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Thus the chapter tests a code-read pattern: whether the window-six coordinate display isolates a small local tile whose label boundary is unusually specific relative to shuffled labelings.  It does not test translation in a cell, molecular folding, physical admissibility of a chain, biological function, a mechanism of code formation, or universality across organisms.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is reported as
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is also reported as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the local geometric datum used in the comparison: it is a finite codon subset and its face incidence profile, not a claim about a global biological law.

\paragraph{Shape and null comparison.}
For the primary shape statistic, the run with seed $42$ drew $N=2000$ distinct null samples after $57377$ sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape match count was $1$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap criterion counted $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null gives a second check.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, exact shape matches were $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  Hence the class-preserving exact-shape probability is reported as $0.0$, while the lower-tail probability is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite rows make the local tile statistically sharp under the tested null models, but they do not turn the tile into translation, folding, function, or mechanism.

The role of these numbers is to record a finite coordinate-and-relation signature of the codon tile; no structural order of proteins is observed by this row.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ target events.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These rows show that the coordinate pattern has predictive strength for code-label readback under the stated finite tests.  They still remain code-layer tests: a recovered codon label is not an observed folded molecule, biochemical pathway, or organism-level function.

\paragraph{Family and module rows.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row contains $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  The module classification row partitions all $13$ members of $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The assigned modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.

$$
The complete assignment count is $13$, equal to $|R|$, with $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  This is the strongest finite-row form of the claim: the boundary set is small, fully classified, spectrally nontrivial, and rare under the supplied null comparisons.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is that the standard-code label table, when read through window-six codon coordinates, exposes a statistically unusual local boundary-tile pattern.  The contact is a curated codon-to-label table, so the readback is an amino-acid or stop label readback and nothing higher.  A separate empirical contact would be required before asserting translation realization, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  In the present finite row, the correct scientific interpretation is local code-layer significance: $R$ is a $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, exact-shape probability $0.0005$, high-Jaccard probability $0.0125$, class-preserving lower-tail probability $0.0035$, complete module assignment, and strong leave-one recovery at the code-label level.
