\subsection{Window-Six Codon Tile and Standard-Code Label Readback}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed label contact.}
The claim is confined to the code-reading layer of the standard genetic code.  The finite carrier is the window-six codon cube $Q_6$, viewed as the $64$ codon displays obtained from triples over $\{\mathrm{A},\mathrm{C},\mathrm{G},\mathrm{U}\}$ with a six-bit coordinate presentation.  The reality contact used here is only a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  Thus the observable datum is a finite codon-to-label table, not an account of translation dynamics, folding, chemistry, function, mechanism, or universality.

The row singled out by the coordinate-and-closure computation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit records $|R|=13$.  The same thirteen codons are also the row reported by the punctured-cube check, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This means that the observed row is not being treated merely as a list of exceptional labels; it is being tested as a local tile in the codon cube, with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.  The local tile includes the CUN block $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, the AGR pair $\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, the stop-related triple $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$, and singleton or small boundary entries such as $\mathrm{AAA}$, $\mathrm{AUA}$, $\mathrm{UCA}$, and $\mathrm{UUA}$.

\paragraph{Label-side shape statistic.}
The finite row for $h0.M.shape.significance$ compares the observed standard-code label shape against $N=2000$ distinct randomized samples generated with seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ accepted samples, exactly one matched the observed shape, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the relaxed comparison using Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, the audit found $25$ matches, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model sharpens the same point.  In that audit, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$-side size was $20$.  The exact class-preserving shape count was $0$, hence the recorded exact-shape value is $0.0$.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite data therefore support a code-layer statement: the standard-code label table gives a local shape that is rare under the sampled nulls.  The available rows do not provide an explicit membership list for $M$; what is present is the observed $M$ size $20$ and the sampled shape frequencies attached to that observed label-side object.

with coupling parameter

Thus the numerical certificate is internally tight at the displayed tolerance: the codon-row radius, the label-side radius, and the scalar $\mu$ fit the recorded cubic relation to near machine precision.  This spectral datum is evidence about the finite coordinate object, not evidence that the biological system must realize the coordinate object as a physical law.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out audit evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  These values say that, within the finite code-table task, the boundary-aware local shape recovers withheld code labels far more often than the baseline row frequency.

The leave-one-codon-out audit reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closures, and $8$ predictable codons.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, with the threshold comparison also recorded as $1.0$.  This is again a code-readback result: it concerns whether a withheld codon label is recovered by the local finite structure, not whether a ribosome, protein fold, molecular interaction, or cellular phenotype has been derived.

\paragraph{Local boundary statement.}
The evidence supports the following bounded claim.  In the standard-code table, the window-six codon coordinates expose a rare local boundary-tile pattern: a $13$-codon punctured-cube row with face-vector $(13,16,4)$, a rare observed label-side shape under both unrestricted and amino-acid-class-preserving sampled nulls, a near-zero residual in the recorded spectral relation, and strong leave-one recovery on finite table tasks.  The finite numbers justify treating the pattern as a meaningful code-layer readback phenomenon.

Nothing in this chapter licenses promotion beyond that layer.  The curated table contains codon-to-label assignments only.  It does not observe translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, evolutionary mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Amino-acid label sequences are labels at the code layer; they are not protein structures.  Cross-organism wording, CUN/UUR notation, or tRNA-Leu-related hypotheses require their own organism-matched contacts before they can be used as biological evidence.  Until such separate contacts are supplied, the result remains a finite standard-code shape statement and no more.
