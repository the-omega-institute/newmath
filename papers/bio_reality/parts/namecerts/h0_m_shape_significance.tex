\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and label contact.}
This record works on the codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon is displayed as a six-bit coordinate and the biological readout is supplied by a curated standard-code table. The mathematical object is therefore a finite coordinate experiment on codon labels, not a mechanism of translation. The contact row states only that the standard genetic-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label. The admissible claim is that a window-six representation exposes a local tile and boundary pattern in this label table. The inadmissible promotion is to treat that local geometry as protein folding, physical feasibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a law of translation.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The finite boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube computation returns the same $13$ codons and the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely reporting a chosen codon list; it reports a small cubical boundary profile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces inside the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Shape test.}
The shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled alternatives. To obtain the distinct sample set it made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact observed shape occurred once among the $2000$ samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A high-overlap relaxation using Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurred $25$ times among the same $2000$ samples, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are finite-row Monte Carlo summaries for the codon-label table. They support the claim that the observed local tile is uncommon relative to the sampled null ensemble, while still leaving the interpretation at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class information. In that run, also with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, the observed $M$-set size is $20$. The exact-shape count is $0$, so the reported exact-shape probability is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail event at or below the observed $M$ size occurs $7$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite claim in a label-aware comparison: even when the randomization respects amino-acid class constraints, the observed local geometry remains in a low-count tail. The row does not identify a biochemical cause; it only narrows the chance-model comparison for the displayed codon labels.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
which is also the recorded value for the cubic-polynomial check. Within numerical tolerance, the row therefore supplies a stable spectral readout for the finite tile. The spectral data are evidence about the coordinate construction and its label boundary; they do not supply an independent biological layer beyond the codon table.

\paragraph{Predictive holdout checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The recorded rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level breakdown uses $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$. These rows show that the local construction has strong code-label recovery behavior across the evaluated table families, but the target remains codon-label recovery rather than organismal function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row.}
The codon holdout row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases. Its recovery rate is

\end{aligned}
$$
which is also the recorded above-threshold recovery rate. The module classification covers all $13$ boundary codons with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The assigned modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\

The classification row records $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, an empty extra-codon list, an empty missing-from-modules list, and an empty unassigned-codon list. Every module has either predictable or boundary status, so the finite classification is complete for the supplied boundary set.

\paragraph{Scope of the conclusion.}
The ready conclusion is a local one. The codon cube, the punctured boundary vector $(13,16,4)$, the exact-shape probability $0.0005$, the high-Jaccard probability $0.0125$, the amino-acid-class tail probability $0.0035$, the spectral values, and the holdout recovery rows jointly support a significant window-six tile in the standard-code label table. They do not show that DNA is governed by this geometry as a biological necessity. They do not show translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or protein behavior. Any such stronger statement would require a separate empirical contact at the corresponding biological layer. In the present row, the contact is a curated codon-to-label table, and the mathematically licensed readout is code-layer local structure.
