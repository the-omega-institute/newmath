\subsection{Window-six local shape significance for the standard code}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon placed by its two-bit base coordinates across the three codon positions. The observable label layer is not inferred from geometry: it is supplied by the curated standard-code table, which assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label. The question is therefore narrow. It asks whether the label table, after being read on the fixed $Q_6$ coordinate display, exposes a local boundary-tile shape that is unusually structured under finite reshufflings, while keeping the biological interpretation at the code-label level.

\paragraph{Observed residual set.}
The residual codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified row, the set is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records $R$ as a punctured cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the finite observation is not merely a count of exceptional codons; it includes a local face-and-edge shape inside the codon coordinate cube. The codons also split into six named modules with no missing or extra codons: $\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$ for the $\mathrm{agr}$ module, $\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$ for $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$, $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ for $\mathrm{cun}$, $\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$ for $\mathrm{ile\_met}$, $\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$ for $\mathrm{ssn}$, and $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$ for $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$. The module audit reports $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Shape significance row.}
The finite null comparison used $N=2000$ distinct samples, generated with seed $42$. Because the sampling space produced many duplicate draws, the run made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries. Against this finite ensemble, the exact observed local shape appeared once. The exact-shape estimate is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap comparison counted $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both rows point in the same direction: the exact tile is rare under the finite reshuffling model, and close substitutes are still uncommon. The statement supported here is limited to that finite comparison. It does not assert that the codon cube forces the genetic code, nor that the pattern is an evolutionary mechanism.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving control.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class information and still fails to reproduce the observed shape. In that control, $N=2000$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the exact-shape estimate is recorded as $0.0$ in the finite sample. The same row reports observed $M$ size $20$ and a lower-tail count of $7$ samples at or below that size, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This control is important because it prevents the chapter from claiming only that amino-acid degeneracy classes are being rediscovered. Even after respecting that coarser biological label structure, the observed local shape remains a low-frequency finite outcome.

\paragraph{Spectral finite check.}
The associated spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The recorded cubic residual is numerically close to zero:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,\\
P(\lambda_M,\lambda_R,\mu)&=-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The spectral-radius entry repeats $0.6752479308830213$ as inside tolerance. This gives an independent finite numerical check on the same window-six object: the coordinate shape is accompanied by a stable spectral signature rather than by an isolated set-count anomaly.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The code-table perturbation row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. Written as a compact finite summary,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{tables}&=23,\\
\mathrm{targets}&=63,\\
\mathrm{baseline}&=0.19821739130434785,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{>\mathrm{baseline}}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{M\setminus T}&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{M\setminus T+\partial}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
By family, the run reports five families. The alternative-yeast family has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while the bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families each have hit rate $1.0$. These numbers support a local robustness claim: when a related code table is held out, the window-six module-and-boundary description often still locates the relevant targets far above the baseline rate.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The codon-level deletion row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The same row records the recovery rate above threshold as $1.0$. This means that the finite local description does not depend on a single codon being hard-coded as an anchor. In the verified deletion test, the recoverable signal is carried by predictable and boundary positions rather than by an explicit anchor list.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The result is a code-layer observation about a curated label table on a fixed finite coordinate display. The data support the following restrained reading: the standard-code labels, when placed on $Q_6$, determine a residual set $R$ of size $13$ with a punctured-cube shape, a module decomposition covering all residual codons, low exact-shape frequency under finite null sampling, low lower-tail frequency under an amino-acid-class preserving control, a stable spectral row, and strong leave-one-out recovery behavior. This is enough to record a local window-six boundary-tile phenomenon.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No stronger biological conclusion is licensed by these rows. The curated code table supplies codon-to-label assignments; it does not observe translation in a ribosome, folded protein structure, thermodynamic admissibility, biochemical function, a generative mechanism, or universality across all biological contexts. Amino-acid label readback is also not structural readback: a sequence of labels is not a fold, and a codon-tile split is not a physical model of a protein. Any claim about translation realization, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal law would require a separate biological contact that measures that higher layer directly. This chapter therefore closes only the finite code-read claim: the window-six standard-code table has an unusually specific local shape, and the observed support remains confined to that shape-and-label layer.
