\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance}\label{subsec:h0-m-shape-significance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The packet treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label readback over the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is displayed by its six-bit coordinate, while the observed labels are supplied by the curated standard genetic-code table.  The mathematical object under test is therefore not a chemical mechanism and not a claim about translation.  It is a finite comparison between a local coordinate tile and the externally curated codon-to-label assignment.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The internal organization used by the packet is coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation; the biological readback is only the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Boundary relation.}
The relation $R$ selected by the finite row has cardinality $13$.  Its members are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check reports the same list and gives the face-vector summary
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite pattern is not merely a counted subset: it also carries a small cubical incidence profile, with thirteen selected vertices, sixteen selected one-dimensional incidences, and four selected two-dimensional faces.  The supplied row does not provide an explicit member list for $M$, but it does report the observed size $|M|=20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null experiment.  The text below therefore uses the finite $R$ list and the recorded size of $M$, without pretending that an omitted $M$ membership list has been observed.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The main randomization experiment used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ accepted samples.  Producing those accepted samples required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the accepted samples, the exact local shape appeared once, and the high-overlap condition $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times.  The reported empirical probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same values are recorded as the significance entries for exact shape and high Jaccard overlap.  On this finite row, the exact local tile is rare under the sampled comparison distribution, and near matches remain uncommon.  This supports a code-layer shape observation: the window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern aligned with the curated label table.  It does not by itself promote the pattern into a biological law.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information while resampling the relevant finite structure.  It again uses $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that run the exact-shape count is $0$, so the empirical exact-shape value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class},\mathrm{exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, with observed $|M|=20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class},\mathrm{lower}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This row is useful because it tests whether the pattern can be dismissed as a trivial consequence of class-preserving label information.  The finite result says no: even after that restriction, exact recovery is absent in the sampled run and the lower-tail event is uncommon.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code readback and a local codon-tile label split.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, or biological function.  Consequently the packet supports the statement that window-six codon coordinates expose a rare local boundary-tile pattern against the curated standard-code labels.  It does not establish that the cell implements the coordinate geometry as a necessity, that a codon-label sequence determines a protein fold, that the pattern is physically admissible as a molecular process, that it realizes biological function, or that it is a universal mechanism.  Any such stronger statement would need a different contact: for example a translation experiment for translation, structural evidence for folding, biochemical constraints for physical admissibility, and functional assays for biological function.  Without those additional contacts, the present result remains a finite code-layer observation with explicit higher-layer promotion blocked.
