\subsection{Window-six codon tile significance at the code layer}
\label{sec:h0-m-shape-significance}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed table contact.}
The record studies the standard genetic-code table through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the $Q_6$ codon cube, with vertices named by the sixty-four codons over $\{A,C,G,U\}$.  The observed contact is the curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop.  This contact supplies code-label readback only: it is a finite table of labels, not an observation of translation dynamics, folded molecular structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  The local tile under study is therefore a code-layer object.  Its admissible interpretation is that the window-six coordinates expose a finite boundary pattern in the label table; its inadmissible interpretation is that the geometry by itself explains biological realization.

\paragraph{Boundary set and punctured-cube shape.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is verified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite shape has thirteen vertices, sixteen tile edges, and four two-dimensional faces in the induced local complex.  The data also record the observed module partition of $R$.  The six modules are $\{AGA,AGG\}$ for $agr$, $\{AAA\}$ for $ar\_lys$, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ for $cun$, $\{AUA\}$ for $ile\_met$, $\{UCA,UUA\}$ for $ssn$, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$ for $stop\_trp$.  The classification is complete: $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, with no missing module codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo significance of the $M$ shape.}
The observed $M$-shape test used $N=2000$ distinct sampled comparison rows with seed $42$.  Because duplicate samples were rejected, the run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ retained samples, exactly one sample matched the observed shape exactly, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{exact}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both reported significance slots equal the corresponding empirical values: $p\_value\_exact\_shape\_significant=0.0005$ and $p\_value\_high\_jaccard\_significant=0.0125$.  A second null model preserving amino-acid class used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It observed $M\_size=20$, exact\_match\_count $=0$, lower\_tail\_count\_le\_observed\_M\_size $=7$, and empirical probabilities
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{aa\text{-}class,exact}=0.0,\qquad p_{aa\text{-}class,lower}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite counts support the claim that the local $M$-shape is uncommon under both the unconstrained sampled comparison and the amino-acid-class-preserving comparison.  They do not identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and boundary.}
The finite evidence supports a narrow statement: in the six-bit codon cube, the curated standard-code labels determine a statistically uncommon local tile whose boundary set $R$ has a verified thirteen-codon punctured-cube shape, a complete six-module decomposition, strong leave-one-out recovery, and a stable numerical spectral summary.  The conclusion remains inside the code-read layer.  No statement about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or biological necessity follows from these rows unless an independent contact for that higher layer is supplied.
