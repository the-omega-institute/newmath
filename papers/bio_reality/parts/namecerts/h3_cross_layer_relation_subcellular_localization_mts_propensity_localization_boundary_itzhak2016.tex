\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization boundary for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite localization surface.}
The localization contact considered here is deliberately narrower than a protein-output or trafficking-mechanism claim.  Its row object is a finite joined table whose rows are coding sequences or protein identifiers for which three pieces of information are simultaneously present: a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate readout computed from the coding sequence, the covariate surface used to remove amino-acid composition and elementary sequence composition effects, and a binary mitochondrial-localization readback derived from the Itzhak-style subcellular-localization boundary.  The verified row count for this contact is $n=8696$, with $657$ rows falling on the mitochondrial side of the localization boundary.  Thus the observed mitochondrial fraction on the joined surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \widehat{\pi}_{\mathrm{mito}}
    &= \frac{657}{8696}
     \approx 0.07555.
\end{aligned}
$$
The permutation budget recorded for the finite computation is $2000$ permutations, and the recorded runtime is $11.034382$ seconds.  These numbers certify that the packet reached an explicit finite-row localization contact, not merely a named route to a localization database.

\paragraph{What is tested.}
The object tested by this boundary is not whether a synonymous residual coordinate causes mitochondrial targeting.  It is only whether the residual-coordinate surface has a detectable finite-row contact with a mitochondrial-localization label after the ordinary sequence controls have been placed in the background.  In the notation used locally in this chapter, let $Q_i$ range over the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates used elsewhere in the same finite program,
$$
\begin{aligned}
  Q_i \in \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
            &Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each joined row, the localization response is a finite label $L\in\{0,1\}$, where $L=1$ denotes membership in the mitochondrial-localization side of the Itzhak boundary.  The local question is whether any residual coordinate retains information about $L$ after the sequence-background controls are accounted for.  Equivalently, the chapter asks whether the localization readback is wholly compressed by the ordinary controls, or whether some residual coordinate remains visible on the localization surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation and feature contacts.}
The same residual-coordinate family has already been evaluated against several neighboring finite contacts, and those contacts fix the scale of the present boundary.  In the measured translation-efficiency contact, the per-organism joined row counts were $13140$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $3415$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $5128$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, each with rank $23$ in the control matrix.  The measured-TE absorption values were small: $0.01759429001506974$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  The localization surface is therefore not being promoted from a translation-efficiency result; it is a separate readout layer.

The reviewed-feature contact gives a second scale check.  On that surface, the joined feature counts were $2242$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $18335$ for human, and $5682$ for yeast.  Several protein-feature absorptions exceeded their degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni nulls in some organisms, for example human complex membership with absorbed value $0.0075271999877804495$ against a Bonferroni threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, human PTM density with $0.014884825920540636$ against $0.00042094892238120357$, and human transmembrane count with $0.0015569064882873212$ against $0.0002697448894057371$.  Those finite feature contacts remain descriptive projections.  They do not make the present mitochondrial-localization label a mechanism, a trafficking model, or a protein-function result.

\paragraph{Finite null boundary.}
The localization computation uses $2000$ permutations as its finite null reference.  The natural null class is the row-preserving randomization in which the mitochondrial label is detached from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates while keeping the joined row surface fixed.  Because the joined surface contains $8696$ rows and only $657$ positives, the class imbalance is part of the contact and must remain visible in the interpretation.  A positive localization readback would mean only this: within this finite joined table, at least one residual coordinate has more localization information than the permutation null assigns to it under the declared row boundary.  A null or weak readback would mean only that this localization contact does not currently expose the residual coordinate after the selected controls and row join.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The broader organism-specific meta contact remains exploratory and does not supply a universal localization law.  Its complete-feature meta table had $12$ organisms, with a domain-by-dominant-coordinate permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ from $1000$ trials, a GC3 correlation for $M\_QTP$ fraction of $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $p=0.35264735264735264$, and a domain variance-ratio test with right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$.  Those finite values argue for caution: the localization boundary should be read as a row-level contact in the Itzhak-style surface, not as a phylogenetically corrected rule about synonymous residual geometry across organisms.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The contact licenses only a bounded statement about the finite localization table.  It does not by itself establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, trafficking mechanism, compartment-identity mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  A separate translation contact is required before any $Q_i\to T_j$ interpretation is made.  A separate protein-abundance contact is required before any abundance interpretation is made.  A separate perturbation, imaging, fractionation, rescue, or mechanistic contact is required before any claim about targeting, import, localization mechanism, or biological function is made.  The present finite result is therefore a localization-boundary readback only: $8696$ joined rows, $657$ mitochondrial positives, and a $2000$-permutation null surface.
