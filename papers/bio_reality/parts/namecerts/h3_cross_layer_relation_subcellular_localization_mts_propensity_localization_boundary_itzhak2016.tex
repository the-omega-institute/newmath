\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization boundary for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The localization contact considered here is deliberately narrow.  It asks whether the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinate surface can be placed next to a protein-localization readout without promoting the coordinate itself into a trafficking, compartment-identity, folding, functional, mechanistic, or universal biological claim.  The computed localization panel contains $8696$ joined protein rows, of which $657$ are mitochondrial-localization rows under the mitochondrial propensity boundary.  The permutation budget used for the finite check is $2000$, and the recorded run time is $11.034382$ seconds.  These numbers define the empirical size of the row contact; they do not by themselves identify a causal route from codon residuals to organelle targeting.

\paragraph{What is being tested.}
For each joined coding-sequence/protein row, the relevant object is a residual coordinate value from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ basis together with a binary or propensity-style mitochondrial-localization readout.  The admissible question is whether the residual coordinate family leaves a detectable controlled trace on the localization label after the usual coding-sequence covariates have been accounted for.  In schematic form the local statistical target is a controlled survival entry of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QL}_{i,\mathrm{mito}} &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Q_i$ ranges over the residual coordinates and $L_{\mathrm{mito}}$ is the mitochondrial-localization readout.  The inequality is only a finite-row readback criterion.  It is not a statement that the codon coordinate causes localization, encodes a targeting peptide, changes import, or determines compartment identity.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation rows.}
The localization boundary is downstream of a stricter translation-readout boundary.  In the measured translation-efficiency panel, the same $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate family was evaluated on four organism-specific joins: $13140$ zebrafish rows, $3415$ \textit{E. coli} rows, $11554$ human rows, and $5128$ yeast rows, with rank $23$ for the control matrix in each organism and $24$ control columns reported.  The measured translation-efficiency absorption values were small: $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  Thus the present localization contact is not allowed to inherit a translation mechanism from those rows.  It must be read as a separate localization-side surface unless a same-row translation step is explicitly joined and shown to survive under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Protein-feature comparison.}
The neighboring protein-feature contact shows why this separation is necessary.  Feature joins were uneven across organisms: $2242$ zebrafish rows, $18335$ human rows, and $5682$ yeast rows.  The modeled-complement self-check failed the $0.02$ stability threshold for zebrafish, with absolute difference $0.1384608720516286$, while it remained within threshold for human at $0.002065597989181489$ and yeast at $0.0006197817630094193$.  Some feature candidates exceeded their degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds, such as human complex membership with threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, human PTM density with threshold $0.00042094892238120357$, human transmembrane count with threshold $0.0002697448894057371$, and zebrafish PTM density with threshold $0.003775603188484381$.  Even there, the computed quantities were descriptive projection values.  They did not license a stable protein-state mechanism.  The localization panel is therefore treated with the same discipline: a localization row count and permutation run can define a contact, but it cannot substitute for a translation-conditioned mediation matrix.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The broader organism-specific summary also warns against over-reading a single biological layer.  In the cross-organism meta table, $12$ complete-feature organisms were used for several permutation checks.  The dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency had Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer value $0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation probability $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous tests were similarly exploratory: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 had $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation probability $0.35264735264735264$; versus log protein count had $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with probability $0.913086913086913$; and versus log tRNA gene count had $r=0.06029405206037823$ with probability $0.8621378621378621$.  These finite-row summaries make the localization boundary more conservative, not less: organismal heterogeneity remains a measured feature of the contact landscape.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The Itzhak-style mitochondrial-localization boundary is accepted as a finite localization contact with $8696$ joined rows, $657$ mitochondrial rows, and $2000$ permutations.  Its role is to delimit the next possible readout layer for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis.  A positive localization statistic, if reported from the same row surface, would mean only that the residual coordinate helped describe the localization label under the declared finite controls.  It would not imply translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, trafficking mechanism, compartment mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Each of those stronger interpretations requires its own matched contact and its own finite statistic.
