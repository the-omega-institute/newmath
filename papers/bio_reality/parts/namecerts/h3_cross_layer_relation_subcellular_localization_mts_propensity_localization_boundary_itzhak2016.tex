\subsection{Mitochondrial localization as a bounded post-translation contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-propensity-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record treats the Itzhak-2016 subcellular-localization table as a finite protein-level readout placed downstream of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates. The surface is not a translation-efficiency surface and is not a protein-abundance surface. It is a joined localization contact whose current finite row count is $n=8696$, with $657$ proteins marked in the mitochondrial class. The computation used $2000$ deterministic permutation draws and completed in $11.034382$ seconds. Thus the empirical object available here is only a finite localization boundary: a list of joined proteins, a binary mitochondrial membership readback, and a permutation design over that joined table.

\paragraph{Readout geometry.}
Let $Q$ denote the nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual block used throughout this BioReality packet, and let $L_{\mathrm{mito}}$ denote the mitochondrial-localization indicator after joining to the Itzhak-2016 surface. The observed finite counts are recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 8696,\\
 n_{\mathrm{mito}} &= 657,\\
 \widehat{\pi}_{\mathrm{mito}} &= 657/8696 \approx 0.07555,\\
 B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 2000.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers license a bounded localization question: whether a $Q_i$ residual coordinate has a reproducible finite-row association with the mitochondrial class under the declared controls and permutation boundary. They do not by themselves identify an import peptide, a trafficking pathway, a folded-state requirement, a causal compartment mechanism, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The same packet also contains measured translation-efficiency calculations on organism-matched ribosome-profiling panels. Those panels are separate from the localization surface. Their joined row counts were $n=13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for budding yeast, each with rank $23$ for the control matrix $Z$ and $24$ control columns. The measured-TE absorption fractions were small on the recorded panels: $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for budding yeast. The localization contact is therefore not allowed to inherit a translation claim from notation alone. A $Q_i\to L_{\mathrm{mito}}$ association, even if positive on the Itzhak-2016 surface, is not a $Q_i\to \mathrm{TE}$ survival result unless the same row system also supplies the translation-efficiency statistic with matched mRNA control.

\paragraph{Protein-feature comparison.}
The nearest protein-level comparison already computed in this packet used sparse reviewed-feature records rather than the Itzhak localization table. Its joined sizes were $2242$ for zebrafish, $18335$ for human, and $5682$ for budding yeast. On that feature surface, the strongest recorded descriptive absorptions were PTM density in zebrafish, with absorbed fraction $0.014211240113472575$, PTM density in human, with absorbed fraction $0.014884825920540636$, and complex membership in human, with absorbed fraction $0.0075271999877804495$. Some feature candidates exceeded their Bonferroni thresholds on their own finite joins: zebrafish PTM density exceeded its threshold $0.003775603188484381$, while human complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceeded thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, respectively. These feature calculations are useful as a caution: protein-level annotation surfaces can give finite-row signals while still failing to define a stable cross-organism mechanism. The Itzhak-2016 mitochondrial localization row set must therefore be reported as its own localization boundary, not as a borrowed consequence of the reviewed-feature panel.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The organism-specific meta table was also computed separately. It contained $16$ organisms overall, but only $12$ organisms with complete feature rows for the reported cross-organism tests. The domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency test had $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail value $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. Continuous tests for the $M^{QTP}$ fraction gave weak or non-resolved permutation results: versus GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with value $0.913086913086913$; and versus log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with value $0.8621378621378621$. These numbers matter for the localization contact because they prevent a domain-level or organism-universal reading from being smuggled into a single protein-localization table.

\paragraph{Local claim.}
The admissible claim is narrow. The Itzhak-2016 contact supplies a finite mitochondrial-localization readout with $8696$ joined proteins, $657$ mitochondrial positives, and a $2000$-draw permutation boundary. It can support a bounded statement about whether $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against mitochondrial class on that row set once the declared covariates and null design are applied. If the controlled survival entries are non-positive, the $Q6$ residual basis remains outside this measured localization realization. If an entry is positive, it is only a localization-surface association on this finite contact.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule.}
Nothing in this contact licenses a claim about translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, trafficking mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Translation requires a separate ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact with matched mRNA controls. Folding or physical admissibility requires a structure or physical-assay contact. Function, mechanism, phenotype, and global biological law each require their own layer-matched data and statistics. The mitochondrial-localization table is therefore a downstream boundary surface for finite-row testing, not a mechanism certificate.
