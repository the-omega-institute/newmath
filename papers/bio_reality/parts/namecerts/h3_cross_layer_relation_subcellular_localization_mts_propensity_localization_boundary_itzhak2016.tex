\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization boundary for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record treats subcellular localization as a bounded protein-layer readout attached to the already defined $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The finite surface is not a sequence-only table and is not a translation-efficiency table: it is the joined row set on which a coding-sequence residual coordinate can be compared with a mitochondrial-localization label or propensity score after the usual sequence controls are present.  In the Itzhak-style localization boundary run, the joined surface contains $8696$ protein rows, of which $657$ rows carry the mitochondrial class used by the test.  The computation used $2000$ deterministic permutation draws and completed in $11.034382$ seconds.  Thus the local object is a finite binary localization boundary with class count
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 8696,\\
N_{\mathrm{mito}} &= 657,\\
N_{\mathrm{nonmito}} &= 8696-657=8039.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mitochondrial fraction in this joined surface is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
657/8696 &= 0.075552.
\end{aligned}
$$
This class imbalance is part of the mathematical object: any reported association must be read as a finite-row localization contrast, not as a universal statement about mitochondrial targeting or protein traffic.

\paragraph{Coordinate boundary.}
The residual-coordinate side is the same nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface used in the adjacent translation and protein-feature tests: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The standard sequence-control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $q$ vectors.  In the measured translation-efficiency experiment, this block had $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each of the four organism-specific joins: $n=13140$ for zebrafish, $n=3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for human, and $n=5128$ for budding yeast.  Those numbers do not by themselves enter the localization statistic, but they fix the surrounding residual coordinate discipline: localization is evaluated only after the coding-sequence coordinate has been kept separate from amino-acid composition, length, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.

\paragraph{What the localization run can say.}
The finite localization run licenses a narrow statement: on the $8696$-row joined protein surface, one may test whether a residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate has nonzero controlled readback against the mitochondrial class or mitochondrial-propensity boundary, with the null distribution approximated by $2000$ permutations over the finite joined table.  The row count and class count are sufficient to make the contact non-vacuous, and the mitochondrial class is not rank-saturated in the trivial sense: $657$ positive rows remain against $8039$ non-mitochondrial rows.  The natural survival entry has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QL}_{i,\mathrm{mito}} &= \Delta\mathcal{R}\bigl(L_{\mathrm{mito}}; q_i \mid Z\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is one of the nine residual coordinates, $L_{\mathrm{mito}}$ is the finite mitochondrial-localization readout, and $Z$ is the declared control block.  A positive finite-row entry would mean that the coordinate improves the controlled localization readback on this joined surface.  A non-positive entry would mean only that this localization contact failed to carry that residual coordinate under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The localization boundary is deliberately downstream of, and separate from, the measured translation-efficiency contact.  The measured-TE experiment reports descriptive absorption of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual by residualized measured TE: $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in budding yeast.  The corresponding modeled-complement fractions on the measured-TE joins are $0.8424312412078521$, $0.6628211177352172$, $0.8566190269974241$, and $0.22386566043976477$.  These values show that measured TE is a bounded translation-layer contact, but they do not certify localization.  Conversely, the $8696$-row mitochondrial-localization run does not certify translation efficiency.  A localization association is therefore not promoted to ribosome occupancy, translation realization, or protein-output mediation unless a same-row translation readout is supplied and shown to survive under the mRNA-controlled model.

\paragraph{Relation to coarse protein-feature readouts.}
The protein-feature screen gives useful scale for the localization claim because it tested simple downstream annotations against the same nine-coordinate residual family.  In human, the feature join had $18335$ rows, with joined summaries $n=18335$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane count.  The observed absorbed fractions were $0.0075271999877804495$ for complex membership, $0.000011405527940317433$ for domain count, $0.014884825920540636$ for PTM density, and $0.0015569064882873212$ for transmembrane count.  Bonferroni-filtered excess was recorded for human complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count, while the yeast feature candidates did not exceed the corresponding null thresholds.  This heterogeneity matters for the mitochondrial boundary: a localization readout may be biologically suggestive, but the finite row computation is still a descriptive projection.  It does not establish trafficking mechanism, compartment identity mechanism, folding state, or biological function.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The organism-level meta table also argues against over-reading the localization result.  In the available complete-feature organism table, $12$ organisms were used for domain and continuous-proxy tests.  The domain-by-dominant-coordinate permutation test had $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials, with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ but no resolved small permutation tail.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction correlations were also weak under permutation: against $GC3$, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; against log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and against log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These numbers are not localization statistics, but they set the scale of the surrounding cross-organism evidence: residual-coordinate behavior is heterogeneous and should not be converted into a domain-wide or phylogenetically corrected law from one localization contact.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The mitochondrial-localization boundary is ready as a finite BioReality chapter because the relevant joined table is nonempty, the positive localization class is explicitly counted, and the permutation budget is recorded.  The chapter may state that a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate can be tested against mitochondrial localization on $8696$ joined rows with $657$ mitochondrial positives and $2000$ permutations.  It may not state that the coordinate controls translation, folding, physical admissibility, protein function, trafficking mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those stronger readings require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled finite-row statistics.
