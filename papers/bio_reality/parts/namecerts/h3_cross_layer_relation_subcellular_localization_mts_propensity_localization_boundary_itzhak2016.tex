\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization boundary for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-mts-propensity-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite contact surface.}
The localization packet tests a bounded question: whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates, after the usual sequence-composition controls, have a finite-row contact with a mitochondrial-localization readout.  The attached localization run is the Itzhak-style subcellular-localization boundary identified by the claim id $\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.subcellular\_localization.mts\_propensity\_localization\_boundary\_itzhak2016}$.  Its finite table contains $8696$ joined protein rows and $657$ mitochondrial-positive rows, so the observed mitochondrial class fraction on the joined surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\pi}_{\mathrm{mito}} &= \frac{657}{8696} \\
&\approx 0.0756.
\end{aligned}
$$
The run used $2000$ permutation draws and completed in $11.034382$ seconds.  These numbers certify that a localization-labeled finite surface exists and that the mitochondrial class is not empty.  They do not, by themselves, certify a positive $Q_i\mapsto$ localization survival entry, because the recorded localization fact contains no per-coordinate statistic, no coefficient table, no effect-size list, and no localization-conditioned residual decomposition.

\paragraph{Controlled residual object being tested.}
The coordinate side is the same nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis used in the measured-translation-efficiency and modeled-readout checks: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-stress}$.  The standing control family contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, giving $24$ control columns with rank $23$ in the measured-TE rows for human, yeast, zebrafish, and \emph{E. coli}.  In that measured-TE finite surface the joined row counts were $11554$ for human, $5128$ for yeast, $13140$ for zebrafish, and $3415$ for \emph{E. coli}.  The measured-TE absorption values were small: $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast, $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, and $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{E. coli}.  These values are useful as local context: they show that the available translation-efficiency contact was computed, but they do not license a localization claim without a separate same-row localization regression or projection.

\paragraph{What the localization run does establish.}
The Itzhak-localization contact establishes a finite, non-vacuous mitochondrial label boundary: $8696$ proteins are available after joining, and $657$ of them carry the mitochondrial-positive class.  The permutation count, $2000$, shows that the run was prepared for an empirical null calculation.  Therefore the chapter can state that the localization layer is reachable as a finite readout and that a mitochondrial-positive subset exists with enough raw positives to avoid the trivial all-zero-label failure.  This is weaker than a positive biological association.  The current fact record does not give the permutation statistic, the right-tail or two-sided $p$-value, the observed $R^2$ or partial $R^2$, the per-coordinate coefficient signs, the per-coordinate survival scores $S_i^{QL}$, or any correction across the nine residual coordinates.

\paragraph{Relation to organism-level background checks.}
The broader cross-organism context remains descriptive.  In the organism-specificity meta table, the dominant-coordinate contingency against domain used $12$ complete-feature organisms, had Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction tests also did not give a resolved global driver: against $GC3$, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$; against log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with value $0.913086913086913$; against log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with value $0.8621378621378621$; and the domain variance-ratio test had value $0.4155844155844156$.  These background rows are not localization evidence.  They only support a cautious reading: the finite residual basis is heterogeneous across organisms, and no universal organism-level explanation should be imported into the mitochondrial-localization packet.

\paragraph{Decision boundary.}
The present localization record is therefore a measured-contact boundary, not a completed survival claim.  A ready localization claim would require a same-row matrix joining the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, the localization or MTS-propensity label, and the declared covariates, followed by a reported statistic such as
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_i^{QL} &= \Delta \mathcal{L}\bigl(L; Z, Q_i\bigr)-\Delta \mathcal{L}\bigl(L; Z\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$
for each coordinate $Q_i$, or an equivalent held-out partial-$R^2$, deviance, or description-length improvement.  The finite label counts make that computation plausible, but the necessary row-level survival surface is absent from the recorded fact.  Consequently, all stronger readings remain blocked: this packet does not show translation realization, does not show ribosome occupancy, does not show protein folding, does not show physical admissibility, does not show molecular function, does not show trafficking mechanism, and does not show a universal biological rule.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched measurement and its own finite statistic.
