\subsection{Mitochondrial localization as a bounded downstream readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-propensity-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate family can be carried from coding-sequence composition into a protein-localization surface without being promoted into a translation, folding, function, or mechanism claim.  The localization contact is the Itzhak-style subcellular localization panel restricted to rows that can be joined to the coding-sequence residual table.  On the finite joined surface the computation retained $n=8696$ proteins, of which $657$ were classified as mitochondrial for the binary localization target.  The permutation layer used $2000$ realized shuffles and completed in $11.034382$ seconds.  Thus the available evidence is a finite localization-boundary table, not a molecular transport model and not a proof that any synonymous residual coordinate causes mitochondrial targeting.

\paragraph{Coordinate boundary.}
The tested explanatory side is the same nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis used in the translation and downstream protein-feature probes: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_{3}$-stress.  These coordinates are evaluated only after removing the declared sequence covariates: intercept, log coding-sequence length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline formed from the union support of the projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the translation-efficiency contact, the same control design had $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each of Danio rerio, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with joined translation-efficiency row counts $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$, respectively.  Those translation rows are relevant here only as a guardrail: a localization readout is downstream of translation and cannot substitute for a measured $Q_i$ to translation-efficiency survival entry.

\paragraph{Localization statistic and null scale.}
The finite localization computation supplies the joined sample size, mitochondrial target count, and permutation count for the boundary test.  The mitochondrial prevalence on the joined localization surface is $657/8696$, approximately $0.0756$.  A localization association that is visible only at this surface must therefore be read against a sparse positive class and against the $2000$-permutation null scale.  The present row record does not include an exported coefficient list, coordinate-wise $p$-values, fitted $\lambda$ values, module labels, or a localization hit-rate beyond the joined-row and mitochondrial-row counts.  Consequently the chapter can certify the existence and size of the localization boundary but cannot report a positive coordinate-specific mitochondrial-localization survival call.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The measured translation-efficiency probe makes the no-promotion rule numerically explicit.  On the joined translation-efficiency rows, the modeled-complement fractions were $0.8424312412078521$ for Danio rerio, $0.6628211177352172$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.8566190269974241$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.22386566043976477$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The directly measured translation-efficiency absorption values were much smaller: $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in the same organism order.  These numbers show why localization must not be treated as a shortcut around translation.  A localization contact can be analyzed as its own downstream surface, but it does not prove that a residual coordinate first survived into measured translation efficiency after matched mRNA control.

\paragraph{Protein-feature comparison.}
The protein-feature probe gives a useful scale for downstream readouts that are annotation-like rather than direct ribosome measurements.  In the reviewed-feature joins, Danio rerio had $2242$ joined proteins from $3180$ feature records, Homo sapiens had $18335$ joined proteins from $18842$ feature records, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae had $5682$ joined proteins from $6156$ feature records.  Some single-feature absorptions exceeded degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds, for example Danio rerio $ptm\_density$ absorbed $0.014211240113472575$ against a Bonferroni threshold $0.003775603188484381$, Homo sapiens $complex\_member$ absorbed $0.0075271999877804495$ against $0.0006605662314432479$, Homo sapiens $ptm\_density$ absorbed $0.014884825920540636$ against $0.00042094892238120357$, and Homo sapiens $tm\_count$ absorbed $0.0015569064882873212$ against $0.0002697448894057371$.  Those feature results remained descriptive because feature availability changed the residual target in some organisms.  The localization boundary is stricter: its finite row count is larger than the Danio feature join and smaller than the Homo sapiens feature join, but the exported localization record contains only the joined rows, mitochondrial positives, permutation count, and runtime.  It therefore supports a bounded localization-surface audit rather than a stable cross-organism localization mechanism.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The organism-specific meta table also argues against reading a localization surface as a universal rule.  The complete meta-feature subset contained $12$ organisms out of $16$ assembled rows.  The domain comparison for $M_{QTP}$ fraction had permutation right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$, and the dominant-coordinate by domain contingency had permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  Continuous proxy tests were similarly non-decisive: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus $GC3$ had Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, versus log protein count had $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$, and versus log tRNA gene count had $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  These finite numbers make the localization chapter local in scope.  A mitochondrial subset in one joined localization surface cannot be generalized into a domain-level, phylogenetic, or universal biological law.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The admissible conclusion is that the Itzhak-style localization contact supplies a finite downstream boundary with $8696$ joined proteins, $657$ mitochondrial positives, and $2000$ permutation realizations.  It is appropriate to ask whether the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates leave a detectable trace on this localization surface after the same composition, length, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls used by the other probes.  It is not appropriate to claim from this row alone that any coordinate controls mitochondrial targeting, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, trafficking mechanism, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those stronger statements require separate layer-matched contacts and exported statistics at the relevant layer.
