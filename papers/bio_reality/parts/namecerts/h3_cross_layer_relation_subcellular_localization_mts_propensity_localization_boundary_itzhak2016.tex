\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization Boundary for the $B^*_{Q6}$ Residual Surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-mts-propensity-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
The localization contact considered here is a finite human protein panel in which coding-sequence residual coordinates are joined to a binary mitochondrial-localization readout derived from the Itzhak-style subcellular-localization surface. The computed join contains $8696$ proteins, of which $657$ are marked mitochondrial. Thus the empirical positive fraction on the joined surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \frac{657}{8696} \approx 0.0756.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded permutation budget is $2000$ permutations, and the completed run time is $11.034382$ seconds. These numbers make the record a finite localization-boundary computation rather than a general statement about organelle targeting. The surface is large enough to state a bounded contact between coding-sequence residual coordinates and a measured compartment label, but the available row summary does not expose per-coordinate coefficients, module labels, permutation tail probabilities, or fitted effect sizes for the localization statistic itself.

\paragraph{Coordinate interface.}
The coding side is the same nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis used in the matched translation-efficiency and protein-feature panels: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. In the measured translation-efficiency panel, the same residual-coordinate design is joined to four organisms with $n=13140$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; in each case the control matrix has $24$ declared columns and rank $23$. The human localization join of $8696$ proteins is therefore smaller than the human measured-TE join of $11554$ proteins and smaller than the human protein-feature join of $18335$ proteins. That difference is part of the claim: localization is a separate readout surface with its own missingness pattern, not a relabeling of translation efficiency or of generic protein annotations.

\paragraph{Control interpretation.}
The residual-coordinate interpretation is bounded by the controls used in the neighboring finite panels: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The measured-TE human panel reports $d_{modeled}=0.8566190269974241$ on its all-readout join and only $0.0028947149257963897$ absorbed by measured TE, while the protein-feature human panel reports per-candidate absorbed fractions $0.0075271999877804495$ for complex membership, $1.1405527940317433\times 10^{-5}$ for domain count, $0.014884825920540636$ for PTM density, and $0.0015569064882873212$ for transmembrane count. These values are not localization statistics, but they fix the scale of nearby cross-layer contacts: the residual basis can be carried across finite biological readouts, yet each readout must be evaluated on its own joined rows.

\paragraph{Localization boundary.}
The present contact asks only whether the human joined localization panel can support a mitochondrial-targeting or mitochondrial-localization propensity readback for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface. With $657$ mitochondrial positives among $8696$ joined proteins, any positive claim must overcome both class imbalance and the usual coding-sequence covariates. The run record confirms that a finite permutation computation was executed with $2000$ permutations, but it does not publish the resulting coordinate-level permutation tail, any Bonferroni decision, or a surviving coordinate list. For that reason the chapter records a boundary result: the surface is ready as a measured localization contact, while positive localization survival is not promoted beyond the finite row facts shown here.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specific summary remains exploratory. Its complete-feature table contains $12$ organisms, while four organisms remain outside the complete M-QTP feature surface. The domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency has $n=12$, chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and a right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. Continuous proxy checks are likewise nondecisive: M-QTP fraction versus GC3 gives $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$, versus log protein count gives $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with value $0.913086913086913$, and versus log tRNA-gene count gives $r=0.06029405206037823$ with value $0.8621378621378621$. These figures caution against reading the human mitochondrial panel as a universal organism-level rule.

\paragraph{No-promotion clause.}
This chapter licenses only the finite localization-contact statement: $8696$ joined human proteins, $657$ mitochondrial positives, and a completed $2000$-permutation computation for the Itzhak-style boundary run. It does not establish translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance mediation, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, trafficking mechanism, compartment-identity mechanism, phenotype, causality, or universality. Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own statistic, with the relevant controls declared on the same joined rows.
