\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization boundary for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This chapter records a bounded localization contact for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate surface.  The object under test is not a protein-structure or functional mechanism, but a finite joined table in which coding-sequence residual coordinates can be compared with a mitochondrial-localization readout.  In the available row audit, the Itzhak-style localization join contains $8696$ matched protein rows, of which $657$ are assigned to the mitochondrial class used by the test.  The permutation budget actually executed for the finite null comparison is $2000$, and the recorded runtime is $11.034382$ seconds.  Thus the finite surface has the following observed size profile:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 8696,\\
N_{\mathrm{mito}} &= 657,\\
N_{\mathrm{nonmito}} &= 8696 - 657 = 8039,\\
\widehat{\pi}_{\mathrm{mito}} &= 657/8696 \approx 0.07555,\\
B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 2000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mitochondrial rows therefore form a finite minority stratum, about $7.56\%$ of the joined localization panel.  This is enough to state a concrete localization boundary, but it is not by itself a positive $B^{*}_{Q6}$ localization-survival result.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates and controls.}
The surrounding $Q6$ residual program uses nine named residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The standard control surface contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; the recorded control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in the matched translation-efficiency panels for zebrafish, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast.  In the measured translation-efficiency contact, the matched row counts are $13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast.  Those translation-efficiency rows are important background because localization cannot be read as a downstream consequence of $Q_i$ unless a translation-layer step has first been separated from transcription and sequence-composition effects.

\paragraph{Localization readout as a boundary test.}
The localization contact asks whether the residual-coordinate surface can be attached to a finite mitochondrial-propensity or compartment-membership readout without promoting the result to protein mechanism.  The finite data supplied here certify only the existence and size of the localization join: $8696$ total matched rows, $657$ mitochondrial positives, and a $2000$-permutation null budget.  No finite-row effect size, coordinate ranking, $p$-value, survival score, or translation-conditioned attenuation statistic is present in this record.  Consequently the chapter records the localization surface as a measured boundary rather than as a positive cross-layer conclusion.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-state contacts.}
The adjacent finite contacts show why this boundary must remain narrow.  In the measured translation-efficiency contact, the descriptive absorption by measured TE is small: $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast.  In the measured tRNA-copy tAI contact, the corresponding descriptive absorption values are $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast.  In the yeast turnover contact, the recorded absorption by half-life is $0.0009086031889966327$ on $3640$ joined rows.  These finite numbers show that downstream readouts can be attached to the residual surface, but they do not license a shortcut from codon residuals to localization, trafficking, folded structure, function, or mechanism.  Localization must be tested on its own joined row surface and, if a translation-conditioned claim is desired, after an explicit same-row translation step.

\paragraph{Protein-feature comparison.}
The broader protein-feature contact gives a useful scale for the localization boundary.  In the feature panel, joined rows are $2242$ for zebrafish, $18335$ for human, and $5682$ for yeast.  The strongest listed descriptive feature absorptions remain small: zebrafish PTM density has $0.014211240113472575$, human PTM density has $0.014884825920540636$, human complex membership has $0.0075271999877804495$, and human transmembrane count has $0.0015569064882873212$.  Several feature candidates exceed their local Bonferroni-calibrated null thresholds, but sparse coverage and organism-dependent joins prevent a stable cross-organism mechanism claim.  The mitochondrial-localization surface is even more constrained in the present record, because its supplied facts stop at row counts and the permutation budget.

\paragraph{Mathematical readback.}
Let $Q$ denote the finite matrix of nine residual coordinates after the declared sequence-composition controls, and let $L$ denote the binary mitochondrial-localization indicator on the $8696$ joined rows.  The observed record supports the existence of a finite pair $(Q,L)$ with a nonempty positive class:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &\in \mathbb{R}^{8696 \times 9},\\
L &\in \{0,1\}^{8696},\\
\sum_{r=1}^{8696} L_r &= 657.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive localization-survival statement would require an additional statistic, for example a controlled partial-$R^2$, logistic deviance improvement, description-length improvement, or permutation-calibrated score for at least one coordinate after the declared covariates and after the translation boundary is handled.  The finite record supplied here does not include such a score.  The correct conclusion is therefore a row-certified localization boundary: the surface is large enough to pose the test, but not yet sufficient to assert coordinate survival into localization.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation realization follows from this localization join alone.  No ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, compartment-trafficking mechanism, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from the $8696$-row localization panel or from the $657$ mitochondrial positives.  A later positive claim would need a separate finite statistic on the same intended layer: a translation statistic for translation, a localization statistic for localization, a structure or physical assay for structure or admissibility, and functional or perturbation evidence for function or mechanism.  The present chapter only records that a mitochondrial-localization boundary has been reached with concrete finite-row support and a declared permutation budget.
