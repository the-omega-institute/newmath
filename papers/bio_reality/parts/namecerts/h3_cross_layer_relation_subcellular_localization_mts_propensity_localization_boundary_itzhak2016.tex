\subsection{Mitochondrial-localization boundary for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{subsec:bioreality-mts-propensity-localization-boundary-itzhak2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests a deliberately narrow question: whether the already defined $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates can be placed next to a protein-localization readout without being promoted into a claim about translation, folding, molecular function, or a general biological law. The localization surface is finite. In the verified run $6c6900d17e6f$, the joined panel contains $8696$ protein rows, of which $657$ are marked as mitochondrial on the localization side of the join. The permutation budget used for the finite null is $2000$, and the computation completed in $11.034382$ seconds. These numbers define the size and granularity of the current localization contact; they do not by themselves define a mechanistic mitochondrial-targeting model.

\paragraph{Boundary variable.}
Let $P$ be the joined protein index set, with $|P|=8696$, and let $M\subseteq P$ be the mitochondrial-labeled subset, with $|M|=657$. The empirical mitochondrial fraction on the joined surface is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \widehat{\pi}_{\mathrm{mito}} &= \frac{657}{8696} \\
  &\approx 0.07555 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout is a binary or propensity-like localization boundary: a row either enters the mitochondrial-labeled side of the surface or remains outside it. The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates are not treated as localization annotations. They enter only as residual coordinates whose possible association with the finite localization readout can be tested after the appropriate covariates and row-join restrictions have been made explicit.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The localization boundary is downstream of, and logically separate from, the measured translation-efficiency contact. In the measured-TE run $bbd448cac2b4$, four organism panels were joined with $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and controls: $n=13140$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; all four report $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ with $24$ control columns. The measured-TE absorption values were small on this contact: $0.01759429001506974$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}. Consequently the localization test cannot inherit a translation-survival conclusion from the TE contact. It must be read as a separate finite association surface.

\paragraph{Protein-feature context.}
The protein-feature contact gives a useful scale check for downstream readouts, but it also shows why localization cannot be collapsed into generic protein annotation. In the protein-feature run $ed2c27216322$, the joined feature panels had $2242$ rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $18335$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5682$ rows for \emph{S. cerevisiae}. The human joined panel reported mean transmembrane count $1.0721570766293973$, mean domain count $1.071284428688301$, mean PTM density $0.006473867757948575$, and mean complex-membership indicator $0.3626943005181347$. Several feature absorptions exceeded their finite null thresholds in that run, for example human complex membership with absorbed value $0.0075271999877804495$ against a Bonferroni threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, and human PTM density with absorbed value $0.014884825920540636$ against a Bonferroni threshold $0.00042094892238120357$. These are protein-feature summaries, not localization measurements. They justify treating localization as its own readout rather than as a consequence of abundance, PTM density, transmembrane count, or complex membership.

\paragraph{Permutation discipline.}
The localization run uses a finite permutation null with $2000$ permutations. In this chapter the permutation count is part of the measurement certificate: it records that the mitochondrial-label enrichment question was evaluated against a randomized row-label comparison rather than by verbal inspection of the joined table. Since the supplied localization record contains the row count, mitochondrial count, permutation count, and runtime but not a reported permutation $p$-value, effect estimate, coordinate-wise coefficient list, or survival matrix, the chapter records only the bounded surface and the admissible interpretation. It does not assert a positive $B^{*}_{Q6}\to$mitochondrial-localization survival entry.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
The cross-organism meta run $e68ab2a7e593$ supplies another reason to keep the localization statement local. In that run, the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency used $n=12$ organisms and gave permutation right-tail probability $0.5814185814185814$ with Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$. The continuous meta tests were similarly descriptive: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 had Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation probability $0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size had $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with probability $0.913086913086913$; and versus log tRNA gene count had $r=0.06029405206037823$ with probability $0.8621378621378621$. These values do not support turning the localization contact into a universal organism-level rule. They instead reinforce the finite-row reading: the Itzhak-style localization surface is a bounded protein-level contact whose statistical status must be reported on its own rows.

\paragraph{Admissible statement.}
The admissible statement is therefore the following. A finite localization contact exists for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate program: $8696$ joined protein rows include $657$ mitochondrial-labeled rows, and a $2000$-permutation null was run for the mitochondrial-localization boundary. This licenses a scoped localization-readout entry for the BioReality table. It does not license a claim that the synonymous residual coordinates determine mitochondrial targeting, encode a mitochondrial-targeting sequence, control translation efficiency, alter folding, establish physical admissibility, realize molecular function, induce a phenotype, identify a trafficking mechanism, or express a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Local refusal rule.}
Any stronger interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact. A translation claim requires same-row ribosome or translation-efficiency evidence with matched mRNA controls. A protein-abundance claim requires matched abundance rows. A folding or physical-admissibility claim requires a structure, stability, or physical assay. A function or phenotype claim requires a functional, perturbational, or organismal readout. A mechanism claim requires intervention, temporal order, or an experimentally separated mediator. The present localization chapter supplies none of those additional contacts; it supplies only the finite mitochondrial-localization boundary described above.
