\subsection{Position-Resolved Structural-Order Ramp Readback for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Residual Surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-structural-order-ramp-positional}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite positional readback for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate family against a structural-order ramp contrast.  The tested object is not a folded-structure claim and not a functional claim.  It is a rank-powered finite matrix experiment in which the $f_{3}$-stress component of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis is evaluated separately on an N-terminal ramp window and a downstream body window.  The intended biological question is narrow: after the same style of abundance and codon-composition controls used for the surrounding $B^{*}_{Q6}$ panel, does the $f_{3}$ residual add held-out predictive information preferentially in the early coding window rather than uniformly across the coding body?

The recorded experiment is \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_structural\_order\_ramp\_positional\_powered}, run under identifier \texttt{fd29bb46dde6}.  It used $5$ folds, required at least $300$ joined rows per organism, and used the primary ramp length $50$.  The experiment status is \texttt{passed}, and the finite verdict is \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}.  Thus the row data support a positive ramp-side increment, but they do not pass the stricter paired localization gate required to say that the effect is securely localized to the ramp rather than merely larger there in this finite panel.

\paragraph{Window sweep.}
The finite sweep evaluated ramp lengths $30$, $50$, and $70$ over $2$ primary organisms.  The weighted held-out increments were:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{30} &= 0.0007864264335388174, & B_{30} &= 0.00023737813475316197,\\
R_{50} &= 0.0011165640103326878, & B_{50} &= -0.00026157313923957926,\\
R_{70} &= 0.0007157308766185055, & B_{70} &= -0.0008384704154638841.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $R_{n}$ denotes the weighted $f_{3}$ ramp increment in held-out $R^{2}$ for a ramp of length $n$, and $B_{n}$ denotes the corresponding body-window increment.  The primary $n=50$ row is the strongest ramp row in this sweep: $R_{50}=0.0011165640103326878$ while $B_{50}=-0.00026157313923957926$.  The paired ramp-minus-body contrast at the primary length is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{50}-B_{50} = 0.001378137149572267.
\end{aligned}
$$
The directional pattern is stable in sign across the three ramp lengths: the ramp increment is positive at $30$, $50$, and $70$, whereas the body increment is positive at $30$ but negative at $50$ and $70$.  This is enough to describe a finite ramp-positive readback.  It is not enough to promote the result to a robust positional localization theorem.

\paragraph{Gate calculation.}
The primary ramp verdict is controlled by sign-flip probabilities and organism-count gates.  At ramp length $50$, the ramp increment has sign-flip probability $p=0.0478515625$ for being greater than zero.  The body increment has $p=0.54296875$ for being greater than zero, and the paired ramp-minus-body contrast has $p=0.05859375$ for being greater than zero.  The primary-organism counts were also asymmetric: $2$ organisms had positive ramp increments, $0$ organisms had positive body increments, and $2$ organisms had ramp increment greater than body increment.

The finite classification follows these values.  The ramp-signal gate is true, because the primary ramp increment is positive and its sign-flip probability is below the stated directional threshold.  The body-signal gate is false, because the body increment is negative and its sign-flip probability is not supportive.  The body-dominant gate is false.  The stricter ramp-localized gate is also false, because the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability is $0.05859375$, just above the usual $0.05$ boundary.  The finite module classification is therefore not \texttt{localized\_ramp}; it is \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The finite conclusion is positive but deliberately narrow.  The row data support the statement that the $f_{3}$ component of $B^{*}_{Q6}$ has a small, positive, held-out ramp-side structural-order increment in the powered panel.  The strongest primary value is $0.0011165640103326878$ held-out $R^{2}$ at $50$ codons, and the paired ramp-minus-body difference is $0.001378137149572267$.  Because the paired localization probability is $0.05859375$, the correct reading is not that a localized ramp mechanism has been established.  The correct reading is that the finite panel sees a ramp-positive signal whose localization is suggestive but not gate-passing.

No translation mechanism follows from this chapter by itself.  No folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal pathway, adaptive direction, or universal biological law follows from the ramp-positive increment.  Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact: for example direct structure measurements or validated structure predictions for folded order, perturbation or rescue rows for mechanism, and functional or phenotype assays for function-level conclusions.  The present chapter only records the finite positional contrast over the available $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual and structural-order readback rows.
