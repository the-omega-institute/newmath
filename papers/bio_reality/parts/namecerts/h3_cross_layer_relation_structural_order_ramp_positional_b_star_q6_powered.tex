\subsection{Rank-powered positional ramp signal for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$ coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-structural-order-ramp-positional}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $f_3$ stress coordinate inside the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual basis has a position-specific association with a structural-order-style readout after the usual residual controls have already been applied. The finite surface is not an abstract structural claim: it is a held-out regression comparison over joined protein rows, with the readout split into an N-terminal ramp window and a downstream body window. The registered run is \texttt{fd29bb46dde6} for experiment \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_structural\_order\_ramp\_positional\_powered}; it used five folds, required at least $300$ joined rows per eligible organism, and set the primary ramp length to $50$ codons. The primary comparison retained $2$ primary organisms. The outcome class was \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}, which means that the ramp increment was positive and sign-flip significant, while the stronger paired ramp-versus-body localization gate did not pass.

\paragraph{Controlled ramp statistic.}
Let $P_{f_3}$ denote the component of the controlled protein-abundance target aligned with the residualized $f_3$ coordinate on the active joined surface. For each ramp length $n \in \{30,50,70\}$, the analysis computes a held-out increment for the ramp window and a held-out increment for the body window. The finite table is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 30: &\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{ramp}} &= 0.0007864264335388174, &\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{body}} &= 0.00023737813475316197, &N_{\mathrm{org}} &= 2,\\
 n &= 50: &\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{ramp}} &= 0.0011165640103326878, &\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{body}} &= -0.00026157313923957926, &N_{\mathrm{org}} &= 2,\\
 n &= 70: &\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{ramp}} &= 0.0007157308766185055, &\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{body}} &= -0.0008384704154638841, &N_{\mathrm{org}} &= 2.
\end{aligned}
$$
At the registered primary ramp length $n=50$, both primary organisms had positive ramp increments and zero primary organisms had positive body increments. The paired ramp-minus-body increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{ramp-body}} &= 0.001378137149572267.
\end{aligned}
$$
The one-sided sign-flip values were $p_{\mathrm{ramp}>0}=0.0478515625$, $p_{\mathrm{body}>0}=0.54296875$, and $p_{\mathrm{ramp-body}>0}=0.05859375$. Thus the ramp signal itself passed the positive sign-flip test, but the paired localization test stayed just outside the registered gate. The correct finite verdict is therefore not structural localization. It is a weaker positional readback: the $f_3$ coordinate carries a small, reproducible ramp-positive increment on this panel, but the body contrast does not certify localization to the ramp as a separated structural module.

\paragraph{Relation to dwell and abundance contacts.}
The positional record is attached to the same $f_3$ abundance component used in the yeast dwell analyses. In the dwell mediation run \texttt{946f33083caf}, the active yeast join had $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1257$ rows, base size $1800$, five folds, and $\operatorname{rank}(f_3)=1$ with control-basis rank $24$. The base model had $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, the dwell-only readout had $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, and the full model had $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, giving a held-out dwell increment of $0.01856473944057712$. The fixed row-label permutation null had $120$ permutations, mean $-0.0032911642620130563$, $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$, minimum $-0.013799622026666476$, and maximum $0.005001350039772926$. The $f_3$--dwell correlation was $0.1615441450901653$ with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. The associated positional dwell run \texttt{6db78e1bf746} classified the leading segment by held-out increment as \texttt{5prime}; its five-prime gate passed, its three-prime body gate did not pass, and the same target component had $\|P_{f_3}\|^2=44.20095750389323$ with fraction $0.1999063916718794$ of the controlled target.

These dwell facts matter only as neighboring finite contacts: they show that the $f_3$ component is not an isolated algebraic decoration and that an N-terminal readout can carry signal in a matched yeast surface. They do not by themselves turn the structural-order ramp statistic into a folding, stability, localization, or function claim. The present run keeps the classification local to the held-out ramp/body comparison.

\paragraph{Translation and protein-abundance boundary.}
The wider $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ panel contains positive translation and protein-abundance readouts, but those readouts have their own scopes. In the measured ribosome-TE survival run \texttt{58611670ec19}, all nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates survived against measured TE in four organisms after residual controls, with $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.009394266754822262$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish. The corresponding joined TE row counts were $5128$, $3415$, $11554$, and $13140$, respectively. In the measured translation-conditioned abundance run \texttt{d71384d16e71}, the measured mediated fractions were $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0$ in zebrafish after truncating a negative raw fraction of $-0.04801486048721136$. Those facts support a bounded translation-readout and abundance-decomposition context; they do not license a direct inference from the ramp statistic to translation mechanism.

The direct protein-omics run \texttt{104db736a756} also showed positive controlled $Q \to P$ association across a larger organism panel, with all nine residual coordinates surviving under the positive threshold. Representative controlled $R^2_{QP}$ values were $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, and $0.06397514251295629$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. The joined protein-abundance row counts included $5815$ for yeast, $3739$ for \textit{E. coli}, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, and $1656$ for \textit{Sulfolobus}. These rows show that the residual coordinates can predict measured protein abundance after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and M-density controls. They still remain direct omics associations unless a measured translation readout absorbs the corresponding direct signal.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite result is positive but narrow. The primary $50$-codon ramp window has a positive weighted held-out increment, $0.0011165640103326878$, with sign-flip $p=0.0478515625$, while the body increment is negative, $-0.00026157313923957926$, with sign-flip $p=0.54296875$. The paired ramp-minus-body contrast is positive, $0.001378137149572267$, but its sign-flip value $0.05859375$ does not pass the registered localization gate. The module label \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized} is therefore the exact conclusion: this is evidence for a small N-terminal ramp-associated $f_3$ readout, not evidence that structural order has been localized as a biological mechanism.

No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this chapter. Such claims require separate layer-matched contacts: measured translation rows for translation, structural or physical assays for folding or admissibility, functional or perturbation assays for function, and appropriate multi-condition evidence for mechanism. The present record is a finite held-out positional association with a specified non-promotion boundary.
