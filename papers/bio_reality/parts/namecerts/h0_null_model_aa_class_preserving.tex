\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{subsec:bioreality-aa-class-preserving-null}

\paragraph{Record scope.}
This record concerns a finite code-read phenomenon in the standard genetic-code table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube obtained by displaying a codon as a word over four bases with two binary coordinates per position.  The object tested here is not translation as a biochemical event and not protein structure.  It is the following narrower question: after the standard table assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label, does the distinguished local tile remain exceptional when the null model is allowed to preserve amino-acid label classes?  The answer recorded by the finite run is affirmative at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Distinguished row set.}
The finite boundary set has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the verified set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records the face data of this punctured-cube tile as
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the code-read object being tested is a finite local subcomplex of the codon cube, not a genome-wide or organism-wide assertion.  The row set contains stop-related codons, arginine-related codons, leucine-family codons, an isoleucine/methionine-adjacent codon, and singleton or small-class boundary codons; those descriptions are label readbacks from the curated table rather than mechanistic claims.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null test.}
The null experiment sampled $N=2000$ class-preserving alternatives with seed $42$.  The observed local object has size $20$ on the measured $M$ side of the run.  Among the $2000$ class-preserving samples, the exact tile shape occurred $0$ times, while only $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The recorded probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{exact}} &=0.0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035,\\
 \#\{\mathrm{exact\ matches}\}&=0,\\
 \#\{\mathrm{lower\ tail\ samples}\}&=7.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central finite-row conclusion for this chapter: even when the null model respects amino-acid class information, the observed local codon tile is not reproduced by the sampled alternatives, and its lower-tail behavior is rare at the recorded resolution.

\paragraph{Comparison with the unrestricted shape run.}
A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$ and made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, including $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The corresponding recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}&=0.0005,\\
 p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving null is therefore not merely repeating the unrestricted comparison.  It asks whether preserving label classes erases the local geometric signal; in the finite sample reported here, it does not.

These numbers provide an internal coordinate check for the finite tile: the recorded spectrum is numerically coherent with the polynomial constraint used by the run.  The spectral datum is not treated as a physical energy, folding score, or dynamical rate.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out checks.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  A separate leave-one-codon-out run recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These tests support the claim that the local tile is stable as a code-table readback pattern under these finite probes.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out summary has $5$ families.  The recorded hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  The module classification assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This decomposition is useful because it makes the local boundary legible without treating a codon-label table as a structural biology observation.  The complete assignment count is $13$, equal to $|R|$.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments.  It permits checks at the code-read layer, including the local split, the row set $R$, the module partition, and the sampled null comparisons above.  It does not by itself observe translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal law across organisms.  Any statement crossing one of those boundaries requires an additional contact whose measured object is actually at that higher layer.  Consequently, the result here is a finite and table-grounded code-read result: the amino-acid-class-preserving null did not reproduce the observed local tile in $2000$ samples, and the related finite probes agree that the boundary pattern is unusually organized within the sampled code-label setting.
