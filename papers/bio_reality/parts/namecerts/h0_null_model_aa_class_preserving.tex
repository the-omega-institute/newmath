\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bior-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving} tests whether the observed window-six codon tile can be dismissed as an artifact of preserving only amino-acid or stop-label classes in the standard code. The object under inspection is the standard-code codon table, read only at the code-label level. The coordinate carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with the finite distinctions needed to talk about a local tile face, singleton label corners, three-point label classes, coordinate closure, spectra, and relations among displayed codons. The biological contact is a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for local split tests, but it is not a contact with translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Observed residual set.}
The finite residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the run used here, the residual codons are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row records $|R|=13$ and identifies this set with the punctured-cube residual, with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a scalar count: it fixes the actual codon names and the small cubical boundary profile seen by the window-six display.

\paragraph{Spectral fingerprint.}
The spectral row reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic residual is numerically negligible at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, recorded both as the cubic-polynomial value and as the radial-polynomial value. In compact form, the finite computation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\

P_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mu)&=-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}.

$$
This is a numerical fingerprint of the local code-layer geometry. It does not by itself explain why a cell translates, whether a peptide folds, or whether a molecule is admissible in a physical environment.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The decisive null row uses $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$ while preserving amino-acid or stop-label classes. The observed tile size is $20$. The exact-shape count is $0$, so the exact-shape empirical value is recorded as $p=0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving a lower-tail empirical value of $7/2000=0.0035$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
|M|_{\mathrm{obs}}&=20,\\
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ shape\ matches}\}&=0,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
\#\{\mathrm{lower\ tail}\leq |M|_{\mathrm{obs}}\}&=7,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row therefore supports the narrower conclusion that the observed local tile is not reproduced by this finite class-preserving null experiment. The conclusion remains bounded by the sampling design and by the fact that the input labels come from the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Comparison with unrestricted shape sampling.}
A related shape-significance row also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match, hence $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, hence $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. These numbers provide a second finite check that the displayed shape is not common under the reported sampling procedures:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{attempts}&=57377,\\
\mathrm{duplicate\ retries}&=55377,\\
\mathrm{exact\ matches}&=1,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9\ \mathrm{matches}&=25,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
This comparison is still a code-table comparison, not a biological mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate in $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate in $M-T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. These rows say that the finite coordinate-and-boundary pattern is useful for code-label recovery tests in the recorded design. They do not supply a contact with ribosomal realization, tRNA charging, kinetic selection, folding thermodynamics, or organism-level fitness.

\paragraph{Family and module rows.}
The family row groups the leave-one-code-out behavior into $5$ families. The reported hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. The module-classification row assigns all $13$ residual codons with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The modules are

\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\

The assignment count is $13$, equal to the residual size, so the finite module row is complete for this residual set.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The supported statement is intentionally local. Window-six coordinates expose a finite codon-tile boundary whose residual set, face-vector, spectral values, null-model behavior, leave-one-out behavior, family rates, and module partition can all be stated with concrete finite rows. The curated standard-code table supplies the codon-to-label contact needed for those rows. Nothing in this chapter licenses promotion from code-label geometry to translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or a global biological law. Any such higher-layer statement would require a separate observation route with its own finite rows and its own failure conditions.
