\paragraph{Finite carrier and readback contact.}
This record studies a window-six presentation of the standard-code codon table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is read as a finite coordinate word and then compared with the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The only biological contact used here is that curated label table.  It supports codon-to-label readback and local label-split tests, but it does not itself observe translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, organism-level function, mechanism, or universality.  Those higher layers require separate observations before they can be asserted.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are

&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.

The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector

Thus the finite observation is not merely a count: it specifies the thirteen codon vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional tile faces detected in the local window.  The row therefore separates singleton corner behavior from three-point label-class behavior inside a finite cubical display, while keeping the curated labels as the external readback contact.

\paragraph{Shape significance against an unconstrained row sample.}
A shape-significance row used $N=2000$ sampled rows with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled row matched the observed shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  Twenty-five sampled rows reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  These values say that the observed local shape is rare in that finite sampling experiment.  They do not say that the geometry is a universal law of genetic coding, nor that the local tile is sufficient to reconstruct any biological process beyond code-label comparison.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model.}
The claim tested in this row is the null model preserving amino-acid label classes.  The experiment used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed $M$-size was $20$.  Among the sampled class-preserving rows, the exact shape match count was $0$, so the recorded exact-shape probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row verdict is therefore that the observed window-six pattern is not explained by this amino-acid-class-preserving null sample at the recorded resolution: no exact class-preserving sampled row reproduced the shape, and only seven sampled rows landed at or below the observed $M$-size.  This is a code-layer rejection of that finite null model, not a claim about translation realization, folding, physical feasibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}

The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$.  Broken out by family, the five recorded family rates above baseline were: alternative yeast $0.8333333333333334$, bacterial plastid $1.0$, candidate other $1.0$, mitochondrial $1.0$, and nuclear ciliate $1.0$.  These values show that the finite code-label pattern has strong leave-one-code-out recovery within the row family that was tested.  They remain label-table statistics; they do not observe ribosomal execution, tRNA availability, protein fold, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite evidence supports a narrow statement: in the window-six codon display, the standard-code label table exposes a rare local boundary-tile pattern, and the amino-acid-class-preserving null model did not reproduce that pattern in $2000$ sampled rows.  The exact class-preserving match probability was recorded as $0.0$, the lower-tail probability as $0.0035$, the observed $R$ set has $13$ explicitly listed codons, and the module classification assigns all thirteen of them.  The supported layer is code readback.  No statement about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless a separate contact supplies observations at that higher layer.
