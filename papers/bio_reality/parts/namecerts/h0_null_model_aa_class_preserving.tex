\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null for the window-six codon tile}
\label{subsec:biorality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The packet records a finite code-layer test on the standard-code codon table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the four-letter codon alphabet, and the only biological contact used here is a curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The claim is not that the coordinate system explains translation.  It is that, after the standard table is used as a label contact, the observed local tile has a shape that is difficult to reproduce under an amino-acid-class-preserving randomization of the label data.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports that this set is the punctured-cube boundary object used by the test, with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely counting labels; it fixes a concrete subcomplex inside the six-bit display.  The module partition is complete on these $13$ codons: $\{AGA,AGG\}$ form the $agr$ module, $\{AAA\}$ the $ar\_lys$ module, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ the $cun$ module, $\{AUA\}$ the $ile\_met$ module, $\{UCA,UUA\}$ the $ssn$ module, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$ the $stop\_trp$ module.  The module audit reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The amino-acid-class-preserving null keeps the amino-acid class structure fixed while randomizing the specific code-layer placement.  With seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled tables, the observed tile has $|M|=20$.  The exact observed shape occurs $0$ times in the sampled null ensemble, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0/2000=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=7/2000=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both reported significance fields equal these same values: the exact-shape significance is $0.0$, and the lower-tail significance is $0.0035$.  A separate shape-randomization row with the same seed and $N=2000$ records one exact shape match, $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$, $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Its exact and high-Jaccard rates are therefore $0.0005$ and $0.0125$, respectively.  These numbers support the narrower statement that the observed code-label tile is uncommon under the recorded finite nulls.

\paragraph{Scientific interpretation.}
The empirical content is confined to codon labels.  The curated standard-code table supplies the contact that turns a six-bit codon coordinate into an amino-acid or stop label, and the null model tests how unusual the resulting local tile is when amino-acid classes are preserved.  The observed values support a code-layer claim: the standard table induces a locally structured boundary set whose exact shape is rare in the sampled nulls and whose module assignment is complete for the listed $13$ codons.

\paragraph{Scope limit.}
Nothing in this row establishes translation as a physical process, folded protein structure, structural order, molecular admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a law applying across all genetic systems.  Amino-acid label readback is a symbolic code observation, not a fold observation.  CUN/UUR wording, stop/tryptophan reassignment, and tRNA-related hypotheses would need organism-matched contacts and separate measurements before they could bear any structural or functional conclusion.  The present packet therefore remains a local standard-code readback result with finite null evidence, not a theory of protein realization.
