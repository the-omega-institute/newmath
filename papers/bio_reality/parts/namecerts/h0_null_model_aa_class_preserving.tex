\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{subsec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim site.}
The claim tested here is the amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon display of the standard genetic-code table.  The biological object is not protein synthesis as a process, and not a folded protein; it is the curated codon-to-label table at the code-read layer.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with codon labels supplied by the standard-code table.  The test asks whether the observed local tile and its boundary set are reproduced by relabelings that preserve amino-acid class while otherwise randomizing the finite row structure.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the boundary object is not a verbal grouping of codons but a finite combinatorial subshape: it has $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the induced local display.  The module partition accounts for all $13$ boundary codons with no extras and no omissions: $\{AGA,AGG\}$ in the $agr$ module, $\{AAA\}$ in $ar\_lys$, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ in $cun$, $\{AUA\}$ in $ile\_met$, $\{UCA,UUA\}$ in $ssn$, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$ in $stop\_trp$.

\paragraph{Null experiment.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed $M$-tile size was $20$.  Among the $2000$ null samples there were no exact matches to the observed shape.  Only $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\text{exact null matches}\}&=0,\\
\#\{\text{null samples with } |M_{null}|\leq 20\}&=7,\\
p_{exact}&=0.0,\\
p_{lower\ tail}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
On this finite row, preserving amino-acid class does not make the observed tile ordinary.  The exact-shape statistic is absent from the sample, and the lower-tail statistic is small enough to mark the observed size as exceptional under the stated null model.

\paragraph{Companion finite checks.}
The broader shape randomization run used the same seed and $N=2000$ samples.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and therefore incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  In that run there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  These companion counts are consistent with the amino-acid-class preserving result: the observed row is not merely a large arbitrary subset of the codon cube, but a sharply constrained local pattern whose near matches remain scarce.

\paragraph{Spectral readout.}
The finite adjacency readout also records a nontrivial spectrum for the same local construction.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check returned $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the cubic relation, so the numerical residual is at tolerance scale rather than a visible violation.  This is a finite spectral certificate for the row geometry; it is not a biological dynamics model.

\paragraph{Prediction checks.}
The leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the recorded rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast.  The leave-one-codon-out probe recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ predictable cases, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers support a local code-read regularity: missing labels are often recovered by the finite tile-and-boundary organization.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for testing the local label split inside $Q_6$.  It is not a contact with translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Consequently, the evidence supports the following limited conclusion: under an amino-acid-class preserving null model, the observed window-six standard-code tile is statistically exceptional as a finite code-layer pattern.  No claim about protein folding, biological function, organism-level mechanism, or universal necessity follows unless a separate empirical contact supplies that higher-layer observation.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The null-model verdict is therefore local and affirmative.  The finite table, the $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary, the $(13,16,4)$ face-vector, the $20$-site observed $M$ size, the exact-match count $0$ under class-preserving randomization, and the lower-tail value $0.0035$ jointly identify a non-generic code-read geometry.  The same data also impose a strict stopping rule: codon labels may be discussed as labels in a curated table, and the tile may be discussed as a local finite pattern, but the chapter does not promote that pattern into translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanistic account, or a universal law.
