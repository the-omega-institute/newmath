\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Record and scope.}
This record concerns the claim $\mathrm{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving}$ for the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon coordinate display. The biological input is not a claimed mechanism: it is the curated assignment of each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The mathematical object under inspection is the local window-six tile obtained after that label table has been read as finite codon data. The claim is therefore a code-layer statement about a finite labeled cube, not a statement about translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal law of coding systems.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its reported members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed object is not merely a list of labels: it also carries finite adjacency and face information inside the codon cube. The amino-acid-class preserving null keeps the class profile available to the randomization while asking whether the same local tile shape is reproduced. Under this null, $N=2000$ samples were evaluated with seed $42$. The observed set size for $M$ is $20$, the exact tile shape appeared $0$ times, and the reported empirical exact-shape value is $0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row evidence says that the observed code-layer tile is unusually small or tight within the amino-acid-class preserving comparison ensemble, while exact shape recurrence was not seen in the sampled rows.

\paragraph{Comparison with the unconstrained shape probe.}
A separate shape-significance row, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, recorded $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. In that run, the exact shape matched once, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and high-overlap matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
This comparison is useful because the amino-acid-class preserving null is stricter about label-class composition, whereas the shape-significance row records recurrence of the geometric pattern itself. The two finite rows point in the same direction: the local codon tile is not a typical sample artifact under these probes, yet the evidence remains confined to the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Prediction-style readback.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$; the same $0.9841269841269841$ is reported for the hit rate above baseline. These numbers show that the finite window-six tile retains strong code-label recovery behavior across the evaluated table rows. They do not show that the tile causes those tables, implements translation, or constrains organismal evolution.

\paragraph{Single-codon removal and module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports recovery rate $1.0$, with $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, and $0$ anchors. The module classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and there are no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The modules are

This module partition is the strongest finite bookkeeping part of the record: it states exactly where every boundary codon is placed. It still remains a partition of codon labels inside the code table. It is not a protein-structure observation and it is not evidence that a CUN, UUR, stop, tryptophan, arginine, lysine, isoleucine, or methionine reassignment has a shared physical explanation.

\paragraph{Null-model verdict.}
For the amino-acid-class preserving null, the finite-row outcome is ready as a code-layer finding. The sampled null produced no exact copy of the observed local tile among $2000$ rows, and only $7$ rows fell at or below the observed $M$ size. Together with the punctured-cube data for $R$, the spectral fingerprint, the leave-one-code-out hit rates, and the complete module classification, this supports the statement that window-six codon coordinates expose a nontrivial local boundary pattern in the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Local restriction.}
The permitted conclusion stops at codon-label readback and local tile structure. Nothing in these rows supplies a translation apparatus, a folded protein contact, a physical admissibility assay, a function assay, or a mechanism valid across all biological codes. Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate observed contact at that layer. In the absence of such a contact, amino-acid label sequences remain label sequences, and the local codon tile remains a finite code-layer pattern.
