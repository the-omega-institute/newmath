\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{subsec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The finite claim tested here is that the standard-code codon table, when read through the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, has a local tile-and-boundary profile that is not reproduced by amino-acid-class preserving relabelings. The relevant observation is deliberately narrow. The codon table supplies codon-to-label assignments; the coordinate construction supplies a finite cube, local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes; the comparison asks whether the observed local code-read shape is typical under a null ensemble that preserves amino-acid class information. The outcome is a code-layer statement only. It does not assert translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or any law of organismal necessity.

\paragraph{Finite codon boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified finite row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object under discussion is not a free verbal pattern over codon names. It is a fixed $13$-vertex subcomplex of the codon-coordinate cube, with $16$ one-dimensional incidences and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the reported combinatorial summary. The amino-acid labels remain contacts supplied by the curated standard-code table, not consequences of the coordinate system alone.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectral computation reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the cubic-polynomial residual. This is numerically at tolerance scale for the finite computation and is used here only as a consistency check on the coordinate-spectrum row. It is not a biochemical energy, folding score, fitness estimate, or physical rate.

\paragraph{Shape significance against unrestricted sampled shapes.}
A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled distinct shapes with seed $42$. The sampling required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. The observed exact-shape match count was $1$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the looser high-overlap criterion, $25$ samples had Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the finite claim that the observed local code-read shape is uncommon in the sampled coordinate ensemble. They do not by themselves identify a mechanism that generated the code, nor do they license any inference about protein structure or organismal function.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null model.}
The claim-specific null model preserves amino-acid class information while varying the finite code-label arrangement. In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed marked set had size
$$
\begin{aligned}
|M|=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact observed shape appeared $0$ times under the class-preserving null, so the finite-row estimate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail event at or below the observed marked-set size occurred $7$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central finite-row evidence for the present claim. Under the reported amino-acid-class preserving randomization, the exact local tile shape was not recovered in the $2000$ sampled rows, and the lower-tail size statistic was rare at the reported resolution.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, while the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. These figures say that the finite local construction recovers most held-out code-read targets in the tested table family, relative to the baseline supplied in the row.

The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, and $8$ codons at high closure coverage. Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{recovery}}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Again this is a code-read statistic. It means that the finite coordinate-and-boundary rule recovers the tested codon-label targets within the reported evaluation frame; it does not observe ribosomal decoding, molecular dynamics, protein folding, selection, or cellular function.

\paragraph{Family and module classification.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row has $5$ families. The hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate tables, $1.0$ for the candidate-other group, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast. The module-classification row assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons: $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module is reported as having predictable or boundary status. This makes the finite partition complete for the $13$-codon boundary set and keeps the statement at the level of codon labels and local code-read recovery.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local boundary.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test codon-label readback and local label splits. It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function, or a universal biological mechanism. Consequently, amino-acid label sequences produced by this readback are not treated as folded protein structures, and codon-tile geometry is not promoted to a global biological law.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row record is ready for the local claim. The observed $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary, the $20$-element marked-set size statistic, the spectral row, the exact-shape and high-Jaccard sampling rates, the amino-acid-class preserving null estimates, and the leave-one-out recovery rows jointly support a narrow code-read conclusion: the standard-code table exhibits an unusual local window-six tile profile under the reported finite null models. The result remains bounded by its contact. It is evidence about the geometry of codon-label readback, not about translation machinery, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.
