\subsection{Amino-Acid-Class-Preserving Null Model for the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate frame.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon displayed as a point in a window-six coordinate system.  The distinctions used in this packet are the codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The biological contact is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is strong enough to test label readback and local tile splitting, but it is not an observation of translation dynamics, molecular structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite boundary subset $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In the verified row, the set is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\

The punctured-cube check reports the same codon list and the face-vector signature
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the coordinate claim is not merely that a selected family of codons is present in the table.  It is that the selected codons occupy a finite local boundary shape with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces in the window-six cube.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null test.}
The null model preserves amino-acid label classes and resamples finite code-label configurations under that constraint.  In the verified run $\mathrm{e8d5de747a0c}$, the sample size was $N=2000$ with seed $42$.  The observed selected-module size was
$$
\begin{aligned}
|M|=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
Across the $2000$ amino-acid-class-preserving null draws, the exact-shape match count was $0$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed module size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row verdict is therefore that the observed local tile is not reproduced by the amino-acid-class-preserving null sample in exact shape, and that the observed size sits in a small lower tail under the same null.  This is a code-layer statistical statement about the curated standard-code labels, not a statement about biochemical realization.

$$
These values support the same local conclusion from a different resampling angle: the observed tile is a rare finite shape among the sampled alternatives, and high-overlap imitations are also uncommon at the reported sample size.

\paragraph{Scope of the conclusion.}
The ready conclusion is deliberately narrow.  Window-six coordinates, the $13$-codon boundary set, the $(13,16,4)$ face vector, the spectral values, the null-model $p$-values, and the leave-one-out recovery rows jointly support a local code-read pattern in the curated standard-code table.  They do not show that DNA or translation is governed by the coordinate geometry.  They do not identify a folded protein structure, a physically admissible molecule, a biological function, a universal mechanism, or a cross-organism law.  Any such higher-layer statement would require a separate contact that directly observes that layer.  In this packet, amino-acid labels remain labels supplied by the curated code table, and label sequences are not treated as folded structures.
