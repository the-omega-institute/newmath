\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the codon tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object in this record is the standard-code codon label table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, with local information read through coordinate faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: the curated standard-code table assigns each codon a label, either an amino-acid name or a stop label.  That table is enough to test code-layer readback and local label splits.  It is not, by itself, an observation of translation dynamics, molecular structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its listed codons are
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row verifies the punctured-cube incidence profile with $f$-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the finite boundary is not merely a count; it is a thirteen-codon face complex with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces.  The row therefore supports a local geometric claim about the codon display, not a global biological law.

\paragraph{Observed window and class-preserving null.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null experiment keeps the label-class structure fixed and asks whether the observed local shape is typical under that constraint.  The observed window size is $|M|=20$.  Across $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving null tables with seed $42$, the exact shape count is $0$, so the recorded exact-shape probability is
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}$$
The lower-tail event, comparing samples with size at most the observed $M$ size, occurs $7$ times in $2000$ samples, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
Both numbers are code-layer finite-row measurements.  They say that the observed twenty-codon local window is atypical under this amino-acid-class preserving null model.  They do not say that the geometry causes the code, realizes translation, or determines protein behavior.

\paragraph{Shape significance check.}
A separate shape-significance row sampled $N=2000$ finite alternatives with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exact shape agreement occurred once, so
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}$$
High-overlap agreement, measured by Jaccard score at least $0.9$, occurred $25$ times, so
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
This gives a second finite indication that the observed local tile is not a common random shape under the tested sampling scheme.  The conclusion remains statistical and local: it concerns the codon-label window supplied by the curated table, not a higher biological layer.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null row is rejected for exact shape at the recorded sampling resolution: no exact match appears in $2000$ trials.  The lower-tail probability $0.0035$, the independent exact-shape probability $0.0005$, the high-Jaccard probability $0.0125$, the spectral values, and the leave-one-code-out rates all point to a stable code-layer pattern in the standard table.  The admissible conclusion is therefore restricted: the six-coordinate codon window exposes a finite local boundary tile whose label behavior is unusually structured under the tested null models.  No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal law follows from this chapter unless another empirical contact is supplied for that higher layer.
