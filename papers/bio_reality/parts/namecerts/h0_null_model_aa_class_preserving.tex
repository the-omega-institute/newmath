\paragraph{Claim under test.}

The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read at the level of codon labels in the standard genetic code.  The distinctions used in this record are the six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The relevant observed object is not a translation process or a protein object; it is the finite standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.

\paragraph{Unrestricted shape comparison.}
A broader random comparison with $N=2000$ trials and seed $42$ found one exact shape match.  The exact-shape probability estimate is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\frac{1}{2000}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run recorded $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=\frac{25}{2000}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries.  This supports the finite observation that the tile is scarce in the sampled comparison space, but it remains a statement about codon-label geometry, not about folded structure, organismal function, or physical admissibility.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
The stricter null model keeps amino-acid classes fixed and asks whether the observed local shape is still typical.  In $N=2000$ class-preserving samples with seed $42$, the exact match count was $0$.  The exact-shape estimate is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed $M$-size is $20$, and the lower-tail count satisfying the observed-size bound is $7$.  Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central finite-row result for the claim: preserving amino-acid classes does not make the observed local tile shape common in the recorded sample.  The result is still not a universality assertion.  It says that, under this finite null design, the specific local shape is rare relative to the class-preserving sample, with zero exact matches and a lower-tail rate of $0.0035$.
