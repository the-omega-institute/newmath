\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite contact.}
This record concerns a code-layer object: the curated standard-code assignment from each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The coordinate object is the six-bit codon cube, read only as a finite display of codon labels. The relevant distinctions are the six coordinate bits, local cube faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The table contact is therefore a label contact, not a mechanism contact. It can test codon-to-label readback and local label splitting, but it does not observe translation dynamics, folded molecular structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Observed residual set.}
The finite residual row has cardinality $13$. The observed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. This says that the residual codons form a concrete local tile in the window-six display, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces. The row is finite and inspectable: no asymptotic or population-level biological assumption is being used at this stage.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral computation attached to the tile gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same value is recorded for the radial polynomial residual. Thus the numerical row is internally coherent to the stated tolerance: the spectral radius entry $0.6752479308830213$ is the operative value for the measured tile, while the residual is at floating-point scale rather than biological scale.

\paragraph{Shape significance against unrestricted relabeling.}
A separate shape-significance row used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. The sampling required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$. These numbers support the narrower claim that the observed local shape is uncommon under the sampled comparison procedure. They do not, by themselves, identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The present null model preserves amino-acid class while testing whether the observed local shape is recovered by such class-respecting randomization. In $N=2000$ sampled rows with seed $42$, the exact-match count was $0$, so the empirical exact-shape value is $p=0.0$ at this sample size. The observed measured set has size $20$. The lower-tail count for sampled rows with size at most the observed size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=7/2000=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the strongest finite-row evidence in this packet: even after preserving amino-acid class, the observed tile shape is not recovered exactly in the sampled null ensemble, and the lower-tail event is rare at $0.0035$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The code-table transfer row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. This row is useful because it tests whether the same local codon-window information remains predictive when one code table is withheld. The table-level result is high relative to the baseline, but it remains a code-label result.

\paragraph{Family-level transfer.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row has $5$ families. The reported family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes. It still does not turn the codon tile into a statement about organismal universality or molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. The thresholded recovery indicator is also $1.0$. This row says that the observed local boundary and closure structure is sufficient to recover the tested held-out codon labels in the recorded finite trial. The lack of anchors is important: the support is coming from boundary and closure behavior in the code display, not from a separate physical anchor measurement.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The ready claim is therefore modest and precise. In the standard-code label table, the window-six codon coordinate exposes a local boundary-tile pattern whose residual set has cardinality $13$, punctured-cube shape $(13,16,4)$, coherent spectral values, rare exact recovery under the amino-acid-class preserving null, and high finite recovery in leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out tests. The claim stays at the code-read layer. Nothing here establishes that DNA must obey this geometry as biological necessity, that translation is realized by the tile, that an amino-acid label sequence has a folded structure, that the pattern is physically admissible as a molecule, that it has biological function, or that it is a universal mechanism. Those stronger claims would require separate contacts observing those layers directly.
