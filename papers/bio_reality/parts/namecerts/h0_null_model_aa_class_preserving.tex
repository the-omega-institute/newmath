\subsection{Amino-Acid-Class-Preserving Null Test for the Codon Window}
\label{sec:bioreality-aa-class-preserving-null}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object in this record is the standard-code codon table read only as a finite label assignment on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$. Each codon is treated as a six-bit display point together with its curated amino-acid or stop label. The local geometry is not asked to explain translation, folding, biochemical feasibility, organismal regulation, or functional selection. It is asked a narrower question: after the standard labels are attached, does the window-six coordinate system isolate a small boundary tile whose shape is difficult to reproduce under amino-acid-class-preserving randomization?

The observed boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite set is recorded as a punctured-cube tile with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. The record therefore has two independent finite readings of the same row data: one as an explicit codon list and one as a local cubical shape summary. No higher biological layer is contained in that reading; the only observed contact is the curated table that assigns codons to labels.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape signatures.}
The window record also carries a numerical spectrum for the local split. The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same numerical residual reported for the cubic relation. This is a tolerance-level residual rather than a new biological observable. It says that the finite matrix calculation is internally coherent for the displayed row data.

The broader shape randomization run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. Among those samples there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite rarity statement for the observed local shape under that sampling rule. They do not by themselves identify a causal mechanism in biology.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
The claim-specific null model keeps amino-acid class information fixed while randomizing the remaining label placement allowed by the test. In this run $N=2000$ and the seed was again $42$. The observed $M$-side size was $20$. The exact-match count was $0$, so the empirical exact-shape probability was reported as $0.0$ at the available sample resolution. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite conclusion is that the observed window-six local tile is not reproduced in $2000$ amino-acid-class-preserving trials, and its lower-tail event occurs in $7$ of those trials. The result is a code-read statistic: it concerns the relation between the six-coordinate codon display, the standard label table, and the induced local boundary tile.

\paragraph{Leave-one-row checks.}
Two finite recovery checks constrain how brittle the local tile is. In the leave-one-code-out test, $23$ tables were evaluated and $63$ total targets were counted. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, while the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid tables, $1.0$ for the candidate-other family, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate tables, across $5$ named families.

In the leave-one-codon-out check, the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the predictable count was $8$, and the high closure-coverage count was $8$. The recovery rate was $1.0$, matching the stated threshold comparison. These recovery numbers again remain at the code layer: they are finite readback tests on codon labels and local membership, not observations of translated molecules.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen codons in $R$ are completely assigned to named modules. The classification reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons. The module rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has a predictable-or-boundary witness in the finite record. This is useful because it prevents the $13$-point tile from being only a cardinality artifact: the actual codon names, module membership, and boundary status agree on the same finite support.

\paragraph{Scope of the result.}
The supported statement is deliberately local. The standard-code table gives codon-to-label assignments; the six-coordinate display and derived probes then expose a low-probability local tile under the stated null models. This supports a finite code-read claim about boundary shape and label split. It does not show that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological law, and it does not establish translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism of code origin, or universality across organisms. Any such assertion would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind, for example a translation assay, a structure observation, a physical admissibility test, or a function experiment. Until such a contact is supplied, amino-acid label readback remains a code-layer observation and the folded-structure and function interpretations are blocked.
