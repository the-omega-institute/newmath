\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record tests whether the local window-six codon geometry still looks exceptional after the null model is allowed to preserve amino-acid class labels.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read through the standard-code table as a finite codon-to-label display.  The object under inspection is not translation itself, but the code-layer pattern formed by codons whose local window-six relation places them in the boundary region $R$ and in the observed module size $M$.  The amino-acid-class preserving shuffle asks a stricter question than an unconstrained relabeling: after holding the coarse label classes fixed, how often does the same boundary shape reappear?

\paragraph{Observed boundary row.}
The standard-code contact gives a finite row of thirteen codons in the boundary set:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count is $|R|=13$.  The same finite computation identifies $R$ with a punctured cube shape having face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has a local cubical incidence profile with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the chosen window-six display.

\paragraph{Spectral finite row.}
The spectral summary separates the measured module part from the boundary part.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the displayed finite calculation satisfies its cubic relation to numerical tolerance.  This number is used only as a finite diagnostic of the codon-tile computation; it is not a biological force, a folding score, or a law of translation.

\paragraph{Shape significance before class preservation.}
The broader shape comparison used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  Out of those samples there was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampling process made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so these empirical probabilities should be read as finite-row Monte Carlo measurements under the recorded sampler, not as closed-form distributional facts.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The class-preserving null model is the central test for this claim.  It again used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed module size was
$$
\begin{aligned}
|M|=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
No sampled class-preserving table produced the exact observed shape, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ matches}\}&=0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed module size, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite run, the boundary pattern therefore remains rare even when amino-acid class preservation is built into the null model.  The appropriate conclusion is local and statistical: the standard-code label table supports a nonrandom-looking code-layer tile under this null, while the evidence still remains confined to codon labels and their window-six coordinates.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family split kept the same pattern at the recorded family level: alternative yeast had rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each had rate $1.0$.

The leave-one-codon-out row recorded no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases.  Its recovery rate was $1.0$, matching the recorded threshold comparison.  These checks say that the codon-tile row has finite predictive stability under held-out code and held-out codon probes.  They do not say that the row implements translation or explains a protein phenotype.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The thirteen boundary codons are completely assigned to six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification has $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Hence the finite module row closes exactly over the observed boundary list.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop labels.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organism-level mechanism, or universality across biology.  Consequently, the conclusion of this section is deliberately narrow: window-six coordinates expose a rare and stable local code-layer tile under the amino-acid-class preserving null model, with $|R|=13$, $|M|=20$, $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$, and $p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  Any promotion from this code-read layer to translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological principle requires a separate contact that directly measures that higher layer.
