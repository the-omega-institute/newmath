\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record evaluates the claim \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving} for the standard-code codon table.  The biological contact is a curated codon-to-label table: each of the $64$ codons is read only as an amino-acid or stop label.  The internal object is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, equipped with local tile faces, singleton label corners, three-point label classes, closure, spectrum, and relations.  The claim is deliberately placed at the code-reading layer.  It asks whether the observed local window-six tile and its boundary row remain exceptional when the null model preserves amino-acid label classes rather than freely reshuffling all labels.

\paragraph{Observed boundary row.}
The finite row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified codons in the row are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check returns the same $13$ codons and the face-vector data $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row is not merely a list of exceptional labels; it is a finite cubical boundary pattern inside $Q_6$ with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.

\paragraph{Null-model result.}
Against the amino-acid-class preserving null model, the observed tile size is $|M|=20$.  The simulation used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  No sampled table reproduced the exact observed shape, so the exact-shape count was $0$ and the reported exact-shape p-value was $0.0$ at the finite sampling resolution.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
|M|&=20,\\
\#\{\hbox{exact matches}\}&=0,\\
\#\{\hbox{samples with size}\leq 20\}&=7,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The numerical reading is narrow: preserving amino-acid classes does not make the observed window-six shape typical in this experiment.  The lower-tail value $0.0035$ says that only $7$ of $2000$ sampled class-preserving tables landed at size $20$ or below, while the exact observed shape was not seen in the sample.

\paragraph{Companion shape and spectrum checks.}

These companion values give a second finite-row view of the same phenomenon: exact agreement is rare, and even high-overlap agreement remains scarce in the sampled ensemble.

The small residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$ is treated as numerical satisfaction of the recorded cubic relation within tolerance, not as an independent biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The curated standard-code table observes codon-label assignment only.  It does not observe a translation process, a folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.  The local window-six geometry may identify a sharply constrained boundary-tile pattern in the label table, and the class-preserving null experiment may show that the observed shape is rare under its sampled null ensemble.  Neither fact promotes the record beyond code-layer readback.  Any claim about protein realization, folding, structural order, physical viability, organismal function, or universal necessity requires a separate empirical contact that directly measures that layer.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
For the claim \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving}, the finite data support a ready code-layer statement: the standard-code label table contains a $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary row, a $20$-codon observed tile, rare sampled agreement under amino-acid-class preservation, coherent spectral values, and complete module assignment of the row.  The correct interpretation is a local codon-label phenomenon in $Q_6$, not a statement about translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
