\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.} The record \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving} tests whether the observed window-six codon tile can be recovered after randomization that preserves amino-acid label classes. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of the standard code. The only biological contact used here is the curated codon-to-label table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label, and the computation asks whether the induced local boundary pattern is exceptional under a label-class-preserving null. This is a code-layer test. It does not observe translation machinery, folded structure, molecular admissibility, organismal function, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.} The verified boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row was independently checked as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the finite phenomenon is not merely that thirteen codons were named; it is that these thirteen codons form a specific local tile with sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies and four two-dimensional faces in the chosen cube display.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null result.} The amino-acid-class-preserving null was run with $N=2000$ sampled tables and seed $42$. The observed module size was $20$. Among the sampled tables, the exact window-six shape appeared $0$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class},\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ matches}\}&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail statistic counted $7$ samples with size at most the observed $M$ size, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class},\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=7/2000=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This says that the observed code-layer tile is unusually tight even after the null is constrained to respect amino-acid label classes. The statement is finite and conditional on the curated standard-code table: the sample does not license a claim that codon geometry by itself causes translation or determines protein-level behavior.

\paragraph{Comparison with the unconstrained shape test.} A related finite-row shape test sampled $N=2000$ distinct randomized displays under seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, including $55377$ duplicate retries, and found one exact shape match. The exact-shape value was therefore $p=0.0005$. A high-overlap threshold using Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$ found $25$ matches, giving $p=0.0125$. These values agree with the class-preserving null in direction: the observed tile is rare as an exact finite shape and remains rare when the null keeps amino-acid classes intact.

The radial polynomial evaluation was $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the cubic-polynomial residual, which is numerically zero at the stated tolerance. These spectral numbers are used here only as coordinates of the finite tile. They do not turn the code table into a dynamical model of ribosomal decoding or a physical model of a protein.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.} The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, was recorded as the hit rate above baseline. By family, the above-baseline hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, $1.0$ for candidate other, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, across $5$ families. These finite rows support a code-readback claim: when codon-label assignments vary across curated code tables, the local tile and its boundary account for most held-out targets. They still do not supply evidence for folding, admissibility, or function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.} The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$. This is a stronger local interpolation check: withheld codons can be recovered within the finite code-layer geometry when the test is restricted to the same kind of codon-label information. The result is not a statement about an amino-acid sequence folding into a structure, because no structural measurement is present in the contact.

\paragraph{Module-level classification.} The thirteen $R$ codons are completely assigned to six modules. The recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completion audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. Every module has either a predictable or boundary status. The module table is therefore a closed finite account of the observed boundary split within the standard-code readback.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.} The finite rows support the following narrow conclusion. In the six-bit codon display, the standard-code labels determine a rare local boundary tile: $R$ has size $13$, the full module support has observed size $20$, exact amino-acid-class-preserving null recovery is absent in $2000$ trials, and the lower-tail value is $0.0035$. The leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out checks show that this tile is not an isolated coincidence of a single statistic; it remains informative under held-out table and held-out codon probes. The contact, however, is deliberately small. It is a curated codon-label table, not an experiment on translation kinetics, protein conformation, energetic feasibility, biological role, or universal law. Any step from this code-layer tile to those higher biological layers requires an additional contact that directly observes that layer.
