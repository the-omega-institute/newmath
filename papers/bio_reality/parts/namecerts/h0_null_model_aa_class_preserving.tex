\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}

\paragraph{Finite codon object.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table through a finite window-six display.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read only at the code-label layer: a codon is assigned its curated amino-acid or stop label, and no further biological layer is imported into the finite object.  The displayed distinctions are the six-bit codon coordinate, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The row therefore tests whether a local codon-tile boundary survives a null model that preserves amino-acid classes, rather than whether the coordinate system explains translation or protein realization.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has size $13$.  In the supplied standard-code row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathtt{AAA},\mathtt{AGA},\mathtt{AGG},\mathtt{AUA},\mathtt{CUA},\mathtt{CUC},\mathtt{CUG},\mathtt{CUU},\\
&\mathtt{UAA},\mathtt{UAG},\mathtt{UCA},\mathtt{UGA},\mathtt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same set is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row is not merely a count: it gives a concrete codon list and a small cubical incidence profile.

\paragraph{Shape significance before class preservation.}
A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among those samples, exactly one matched the observed shape exactly, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
This preliminary null row says that the observed tile shape is rare under the sampled coordinate-respecting comparison, but it does not yet separate label-class preservation from unrestricted reshuffling.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null row.}
The present claim is the stricter null row.  It preserves amino-acid classes and asks whether the observed local tile reappears when the table is randomized under that constraint.  The run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed $M$ size is $20$.  The exact observed shape appeared $0$ times, so the recorded exact-shape probability is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= \frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row is therefore stronger than a bare coordinate coincidence: even after preserving amino-acid label classes, the observed local boundary has no exact match in the sampled null ensemble and lies in a small lower tail for $M$ size.

\paragraph{Leave-one checks.}

The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  These figures indicate that the local coordinate pattern has predictive stability across held-out code-table perturbations, measured at the label-readback layer.

The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, with the threshold comparison also recorded as $1.0$.  The finite read is that every tested held-out codon in this row is recovered by the local closure or boundary mechanism available to the finite code table.  This is a code-layer recovery statement, not a claim that a translation apparatus, folded structure, or functional phenotype has been reconstructed.

\paragraph{Local verdict and boundary.}
The finite record supports a narrow conclusion.  In the standard-code table, the window-six codon coordinate exposes a local boundary-tile pattern whose $R$ set has size $13$, whose punctured-cube profile is $(13,16,4)$, whose spectral values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, and whose amino-acid-class preserving null run gives no exact matches in $2000$ samples with lower-tail probability $0.0035$.  The leave-one rows and module decomposition are consistent with the same code-layer pattern.

The conclusion stops there.  The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments; it does not observe ribosomal translation, folded protein geometry, energetic admissibility, organismal function, causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Amino-acid label readback is not folded structure.  CUN, UUR, AGR, stop, tryptophan, lysine, isoleucine, and methionine modules are being used here as finite code-table labels and boundary modules only.  Any statement about translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality would require an additional empirical contact of the appropriate kind.
