\subsection{Amino-Acid-Class Preserving Null Test for the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Object and finite display.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, presented as a six-bit codon display with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The row does not treat codons as a complete biological mechanism.  It treats them as finite coordinate data equipped with a curated label readback: each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label by the standard-code table.  The internal analysis uses coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data, while the label names themselves enter through the curated code table rather than through a derivation from cube geometry.

\paragraph{Distinguished relation.}
The relation row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified codon set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check gives the same thirteen codons and records the face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is not merely a list of exceptional labels: it is a finite subconfiguration with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional tile faces in the window-six display.  This is the exact finite region whose amino-acid-class preserving null model is tested below.

\paragraph{Observed size and class-preserving null model.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null experiment used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed tile size was
$$
\begin{aligned}
|M_{\mathrm{obs}}|=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
Among the $2000$ amino-acid-class preserving samples, the exact-shape count was $0$, so the reported exact-shape probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite conclusion is therefore narrow but strong: preserving amino-acid label classes does not, in this sample, reproduce the observed local tile shape, and only $7$ of $2000$ sampled class-preserving rows fall at or below the observed tile size.  The conclusion is about the displayed code-layer tile, not about translation, folding, molecular feasibility, phenotype, or an organism-level law.

These scalar checks support the claim that the row is seeing a stable finite pattern, while still leaving all biochemical interpretation outside the finite codon-label contact.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out behavior.}

The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out run reported recovery rate $1.0$, recovery rate above threshold $1.0$, $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $0$ anchors.  These rows say that the finite local display has strong code-layer recovery behavior under deletion tests.  They do not say that the deleted codon is biologically forced by the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The evidence supports a code-read statement: window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern whose observed shape is rare under the supplied amino-acid-class preserving null model, and the curated standard-code table supplies the label contact needed to read the codons as amino-acid or stop labels.  Nothing in this record promotes that local pattern into translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or a global law of DNA.  Any such assertion would require a separate contact that observes the relevant layer directly.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded proteins, CUN/UUR wording is not structure evidence by itself, and a codon-to-label table is not a measurement of physical or functional realization.
