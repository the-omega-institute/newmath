\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
The record tests a window-six codon display on the codon cube $Q_6$, with the observed biological contact restricted to standard-code label readback. The carrier is finite: the rows are codons, the displayed coordinates are six binary positions, and the only admitted biological labels are the curated amino-acid or stop assignments supplied by the standard genetic-code table. The local exceptional region $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly the codon set
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
As a finite cubical shape, the same row reports the face-vector data $(13,16,4)$ for the punctured-cube description of $R$. Thus the observation is not a verbal association between codon names and a favored drawing; it is a finite subset with recorded vertex, edge, and square counts inside the six-bit display.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The null model keeps amino-acid classes fixed while randomizing the finite arrangement tested by the local tile. In $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving trials with seed $42$, the observed local shape had exact-match count $0$. The reported exact-shape probability is therefore $p=0.0$ on this sample, while the lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$-size is $7$ out of $2000$, giving $p=0.0035$. The observed $M$-size is $20$. These data make the finite local arrangement unusually small or sharp relative to this class-preserving null, but the conclusion remains a code-layer conclusion: the row tests label-class geometry, not protein realization.

\paragraph{Shape significance against the broader finite sample.}
A separate shape-significance row used $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts because $55377$ duplicate retries occurred. The exact local shape appeared once, yielding $p=0.0005$, and high-overlap matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, yielding $p=0.0125$. These scalar values support the claim that the observed local tile is not a common artifact of the sampled finite rearrangements. They still do not promote the tile into a law of biology. The data are finite, coordinate-level evidence attached to a curated code table.

Every listed module has a predictable-or-boundary status in the finite row. This is the strongest local reading of the packet: the exceptional codons are not merely counted, but partitioned into named finite modules compatible with the code-label contact.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code readback and local label splits. It cannot by itself test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or any necessity claim about DNA obeying the six-bit geometry. In particular, an amino-acid label sequence is not a folded protein structure, and a local codon tile is not a global biological law. Any later claim about translation, folding, physical constraint, function, or universality must bring a separate contact that directly observes that higher layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite record is ready as a code-read claim. The $13$-codon region $R$, the face vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, $\mu=3.051487585298128$, the class-preserving null probabilities $0.0$ and $0.0035$, the broader shape probabilities $0.0005$ and $0.0125$, and the leave-one recovery and module-completion rows all point to a nontrivial local boundary-tile structure in the standard-code readback. The proper conclusion is narrow: window-six codon coordinates expose a finite local arrangement around the listed codons, while the curated label table remains the only biological contact used by this chapter.
