\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model for the local codon tile}
\label{sec:bior-aa-class-preserving-codon-tile}

\paragraph{Object and coordinate layer.}
The object considered here is the standard genetic-code label table restricted to a six-bit codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read through the usual three nucleotide positions and then through the curated standard-code assignment of each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The finite observation is not a claim about translation dynamics.  It is a code-layer observation: local coordinate, closure, spectral, and relation data are compared with the curated codon labels.  The distinguished boundary set is
$$\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The verified cardinality is $|R|=13$.  The same run identifies this set as a punctured-cube boundary with face data
$$\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the local codon tile has thirteen selected vertices, sixteen selected one-dimensional adjacencies, and four selected two-dimensional faces.  The row data used in this chapter are finite and explicit: no hidden biological mechanism is introduced between the cube coordinates and the label table.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null experiment preserves amino-acid classes while randomizing the detailed local placement.  Against this class-preserving background, the observed local shape has size $20$.  In $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving trials with seed $42$, the exact local shape appeared zero times, and only $7$ samples had size at most the observed value.  The reported finite-sample probabilities are
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
The first number records that no exact copy occurred in the sampled null ensemble; it is not a mathematical assertion that the true null probability is literally zero.  The second number is the lower-tail comparison for the observed $M$-size statistic.  This is the main finite-row reason to keep the boundary tile as a real code-layer signal rather than treating it as an arbitrary class-preserving artifact.

\paragraph{Shape comparison and spectrum.}
A separate shape-significance run with $N=2000$ and seed $42$ observed one exact shape match after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  It reported $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{shape}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
The spectral row gives the local numerical profile
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The same computation reports the cubic residual as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, equivalently the radial-polynomial residual within numerical tolerance.  These numbers do not create a biological law.  They say that the observed codon-label boundary has a stable finite coordinate signature under the tested probes.

\paragraph{Predictive readback checks.}
The leave-one-code-out check evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out check found $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$.  These checks support the narrower statement that the local tile and its boundary coordinates organize code-label recovery.  They do not show that a ribosome realizes the pattern, that a protein folds because of it, or that a cellular function follows from it.

\paragraph{Family and module stratification.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row records $5$ families.  The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  The module classification covers all thirteen codons in $R$ with no extra module codons, no missing module members, and no unassigned codons.  The verified modules are
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR/Lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile/Met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop/Trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The classification completeness row gives $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$.  This makes the boundary tile inspectable at the module level: every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the finite run.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model is rejected at the level tested by the finite rows: the observed local code-label tile is not reproduced by the sampled class-preserving null ensemble, and its boundary modules are completely accounted for inside the thirteen-codon set $R$.  The admissible conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The curated code table supplies codon-to-label readback, and the cube probes expose a local boundary arrangement in that readback.  Nothing in these rows establishes translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or universality across biology.  Any such stronger claim requires a separate observation at the corresponding biological layer.
