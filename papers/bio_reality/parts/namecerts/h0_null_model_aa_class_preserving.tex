\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:bioreality-aa-class-preserving-null}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether the observed local codon tile remains unusual after the null model is required to preserve amino-acid label classes.  The object under inspection is the standard-code codon table read as a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon carries its curated amino-acid or stop label.  The claim is deliberately confined to code-layer readback: the data may support a local split in the labelled codon cube, but it does not by itself assert translation dynamics, folded structure, chemical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological rule.  Any such higher-layer assertion would need an independent contact outside the curated label table.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same thirteen-codon set is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row does not merely report a large exceptional collection; it records a specific local face structure in the six-bit codon display.  The singleton-corner and three-point-label distinctions are read through the standard code labels, while the carrier geometry supplies the coordinate, closure, spectral, and relation data used to interrogate the table.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null result.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null model used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  In this model the exact observed shape was never reproduced: $0$ exact matches were found among $2000$ trials, so the reported exact-shape estimate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class, exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed $M$-size was $20$.  Only $7$ of the $2000$ class-preserving samples landed in the lower tail at or below that observed size, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class, lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the strongest finite-row reason for treating the codon tile as non-generic relative to a label-class-preserving background.  The row does not supply a membership list for $M$, so the chapter uses the recorded $M$ cardinality and null-tail counts rather than inventing unobserved members.

\paragraph{Comparison with the broader shape null.}
A separate shape-significance run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving null is therefore not an isolated anomaly report.  It is compatible with the broader finite sampling signal: exact shape agreement is rare, and even high-overlap agreement remains uncommon in the sampled background.

The radial polynomial value, equivalently the cubic-polynomial residual in the row, is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$.  This is effectively zero at the reported precision and keeps the spectral coordinate tied to the same finite codon-tile computation.  The spectral radius recorded in tolerance is again $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$ at the stated precision.

\paragraph{Leave-one readback tests.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  Against that baseline, the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  These figures support the code-read interpretation: the local tile is useful for recovering label-table variation within the finite experiment, while remaining a statement about codon labels rather than molecular realization.

\paragraph{Family and codon deletion structure.}
The leave-one-code-out family classification covered $5$ families.  The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. This complete module accounting is important because it prevents the local tile from being explained away as a bookkeeping fragment with unmatched residues.

\paragraph{Local biological interpretation.}
The contact used here is the curated standard-code table: it supplies codon-to-label assignments at codon-label resolution.  That contact is sufficient for asking whether a finite codon-coordinate pattern aligns with the standard code labels.  It is not sufficient for claiming that the pattern is realized as translation machinery, a protein fold, a physical feasibility constraint, a biological function, or a universal rule across organisms.  The correct conclusion is narrower and stronger: under the reported finite null models, the window-six codon coordinates expose a statistically unusual local boundary tile in the standard-code label table, and this tile remains non-generic even when amino-acid label classes are preserved.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a ready code-layer claim and a blocked promotion path.  The ready part is the finite table result: $R$ is the thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary above; the class-preserving exact-shape count is $0/2000$; the class-preserving lower-tail count is $7/2000$; the spectral coordinates are numerically recorded; and the leave-one and module rows support local label recovery.  The blocked part is any assertion beyond code readback.  No translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless a separate empirical contact supplies that layer.
