\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:bior-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
The record tests whether the local window-six codon geometry remains visible after the null model is forced to preserve amino-acid label classes.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read at the code-label layer through a curated standard-code table.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, local tile faces in $Q_6$, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The internal data used here are coordinate position, closure membership, spectral response, and finite relations among codons.  The biological contact is deliberately narrower: it supplies standard-code codon-to-label assignments and does not supply a translation apparatus, a folded molecule, a physical admissibility assay, a function measurement, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite relation $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the relation is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it is a finite subshape in the codon cube, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces.  The module classification accounts for all thirteen codons and leaves no extras or omissions: $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, assigned count $13$, no unassigned codons, no missing module codons, and no extra module codons.  The classified modules are

Every listed module has either a predictable member or a boundary member in the leave-one-codon test, so the finite relation is locally organized at the code-read layer rather than being an uninterpreted residue.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The null model fixes amino-acid class structure and asks whether the observed local shape remains exceptional under that restriction.  In the recorded experiment, the seed was $42$ and the sample size was $N=2000$.  The observed $M$-set size was $20$.  Under the amino-acid-class preserving randomization there were no exact shape matches, and only seven samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
|M|_{\mathrm{obs}}&=20,\\
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ shape\ matches}\}&=0,\\
\#\{|M|\leq 20\}&=7,\\
\widehat p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
\widehat p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact-shape value is an empirical Monte Carlo estimate at this finite resolution: it records zero hits among the $2000$ sampled class-preserving alternatives, not an analytic proof that the probability is literally zero.  The lower-tail estimate $0.0035$ supplies the more conservative finite-row reading: even after preserving amino-acid class, the observed size level is rare in the sampled null ensemble.

\paragraph{Comparison with the broader shape test.}
The companion shape-significance row used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$ and required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, including $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found one exact shape match and twenty-five samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ matches}\}&=1,\\
\#\{J\geq 0.9\}&=25,\\
\widehat p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}&=0.0005,\\
\widehat p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving null is therefore not the sole source of rarity.  The local tile persists as a finite signal across two tests: a broad shape comparison and a stricter amino-acid-class preserving comparison.  The two rows are not interchangeable, because the stricter null keeps label-class information fixed while the broader row measures geometric resemblance in the sampled ensemble.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  It observes that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code.  It can test code readback and local codon-tile label splitting.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or function realization.  Consequently, amino-acid label-sequence readback remains a code-layer observation.  A fold, an admissible molecule, a biological function, a mechanism, or a universal rule would require a separate observation channel aimed at that layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite rows support the following bounded conclusion.  In the six-coordinate codon cube, the standard-code label table exposes a thirteen-codon relation $R$ with punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, complete module assignment, stable spectral coordinates, and rarity under both broad and amino-acid-class preserving null models.  The amino-acid-class preserving row is especially important because it blocks the simpler explanation that the pattern is only an artifact of preserving amino-acid classes: among $2000$ such samples, the exact observed shape appeared zero times, and only seven samples had $M$ size at most $20$.  The claim is therefore ready as a finite code-read statement about local codon-window structure.  It remains explicitly bounded away from translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, and universality unless later records attach independent contacts for those layers.
