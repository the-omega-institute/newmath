\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model for the codon tile}
\label{subsec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim and finite display.}
The packet records a code-layer claim about the standard genetic-code table under a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, read through codon labels supplied by the curated standard-code table.  The tested object is not a translation apparatus and not a protein structure; it is the finite assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Within that assignment, the distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that this set has the punctured-cube face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite evidence is a local codon-label pattern in $Q_6$: thirteen listed codons, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional tile faces.  Its biological reading is deliberately narrow.  It says that standard-code labels expose a particular local split around the listed codons; it does not say that the split is a law of biology.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The active null model preserves amino-acid label classes while randomizing the finite code-layer geometry.  In the run with seed $42$ and $N=2000$ samples, the observed marked set size was $20$.  The exact-shape match count was $0$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed marked size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the main finite-row result for the claim: even after amino-acid classes are held fixed, the observed local shape is not recovered by the exact-shape statistic in the sampled null population, and the lower-tail statistic is small.  The result is a code-read regularity against this particular finite null, not a mechanistic derivation of translation or folding.

\paragraph{Companion shape and spectrum checks.}
A second shape-significance run, also with seed $42$ and $N=2000$, recorded one exact shape match and twenty-five high-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$.  The corresponding probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite experiment is visibly concentrated in a constrained combinatorial region rather than in a large unconstrained cloud.

The spectral row gives three scalar checks for the same local pattern.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial check.  This residual is numerical evidence that the recorded spectral scalar is internally consistent with the finite matrix calculation used in the packet.  It is not evidence for biochemical necessity; it only stabilizes the code-layer computation attached to the listed codons.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level row separates these results into five families.  The reported above-baseline family hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These rows say that the local code-layer tile remains predictive in the finite leave-out tests; they do not license a statement about all organisms, all code histories, or all translation contexts.

The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Its module-classification row assigns all thirteen boundary codons with no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This exhausts $R$: the assigned count is $13$, matching the recorded $R$ size.  The module row therefore supplies a finite partition of the boundary pattern, not an assertion about protein order or cellular realization.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local boundary.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, folded conformation, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently, the proper conclusion is local and conditional: the six-coordinate codon display exposes a statistically sharp boundary tile in the standard-code label table, including the thirteen-codon set $R$, the $20$-point observed marked set, the small amino-acid-class-preserving null probabilities, and the module partition above.  No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows unless a separate contact measures that higher layer directly.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite row is ready as a code-read result.  The amino-acid-class-preserving null model reports $0$ exact matches among $2000$ samples and lower-tail probability $0.0035$; companion rows record $R$ as a thirteen-codon punctured cube with face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, leave-one-code-out hit rate $0.9841269841269841$ above baseline, leave-one-codon-out recovery rate $1.0$, and a complete six-module assignment of all codons in $R$.  These numbers support a finite local boundary statement for standard-code labels and block promotion to any higher biological layer without new measurements.
