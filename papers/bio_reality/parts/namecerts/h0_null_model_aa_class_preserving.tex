\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null test for the local codon tile}
\label{sec:bior-reality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}
This record concerns the standard genetic-code table only at the codon-label readout layer.  The coordinate carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with codons read as finite words over the usual nucleotide alphabet after the table contact assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label.  The local object under test is not the whole code and not a biochemical mechanism; it is the label-sensitive boundary pattern selected by the window-six codon coordinates.  The finite row identifies a distinguished boundary set $R$ of size $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that this set is a punctured-cube face pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the observed object is a finite local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.  This is the finite combinatorial signal being compared against null labelings.

\paragraph{Observed shape and spectrum.}
The observed $M$-side tile has size $20$ in the amino-acid-class-preserving null test.  The row does not supply the full codon membership list for $M$, so the present chapter uses its reported cardinality and shape-comparison statistics rather than inventing a codon list.  The spectral row gives the numerical profile
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so within the reported tolerance the same finite construction supports both the boundary cardinality statement and the spectral-radius statement.  These values are not interpreted as dynamics of translation.  They are internal coordinates of the finite codon-label pattern after the curated table has supplied the labels.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model.}
The claim tested here is the null model that preserves amino-acid label classes while randomizing the local shape.  In $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving trials, the exact observed shape occurs $0$ times.  The finite-row probability is therefore recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0,\\
\#\{\text{exact matches}\} &= 0,\\
N &= 2000.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the lower-tail statistic at the observed $M$-size threshold, $7$ sampled trials are at or below the observed size $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The random seed is $42$ and the run identifier is $\mathrm{e8d5de747a0c}$.  Under this finite null, the boundary-tile shape is therefore not reproduced by amino-acid-class preservation alone in the sampled ensemble.  A related unconstrained shape-significance row, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, records one exact shape match, $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$, $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Its probabilities are $0.0005$ for exact shape and $0.0125$ for Jaccard at least $0.9$.  The two rows agree in direction: the local codon tile is a specific finite object, not a generic consequence of keeping label classes fixed.

The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the reported rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  The by-family split reports $5$ families: bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, nuclear ciliate, and alternative yeast.  Four families have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline; alternative yeast has $0.8333333333333334$.  The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchors, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers make the local pattern robust as a code-layer readout probe, but they still do not make it a biochemical explanation.

The module classification row accounts for all $13$ members of $R$ with no extras, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.  The six reported modules are

Each module has either predictable or boundary support in the finite row.  This matters because the signal is not merely a total cardinality statement: the $13$ codons are organized into named finite blocks, and the accounting check confirms that the partition covers exactly the reported boundary set.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local boundary.}
The only reality contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is sufficient for code-layer readout and for testing local codon-tile label splits.  It is not an observation of ribosomal realization, molecular folding, thermodynamic admissibility, biological activity, evolutionary universality, or a general law of genetic systems.  The correct conclusion is therefore narrow: window-six coordinates, after contact with the curated standard-code labels, expose a statistically sharp local boundary tile whose amino-acid-class-preserving null probability is small in the reported finite experiment.  Nothing in this row upgrades that tile into translation, protein structure, physical feasibility, organismal function, mechanism, or universality.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate empirical contact designed for that higher layer.
