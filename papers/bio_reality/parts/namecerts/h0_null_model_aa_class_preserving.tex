\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model for the codon boundary packet}\label{sec:bior-codon-aa-class-preserving-null}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The packet \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving} concerns the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  The displayed carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with the biological labels supplied by a curated standard-code table.  The finite distinctions used here are the six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  These distinctions are enough to ask whether a local boundary tile is unusually placed inside the code table, but they do not by themselves describe translation chemistry, folded protein geometry, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The verified boundary set $R$ has thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its cardinality and punctured-cube face data are recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R|&=13,\\
f(R)&=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row-level object is not merely a count.  It includes a particular thirteen-codon subset and a local cubical incidence profile with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-faces.  The observed module decomposition assigns all thirteen codons and leaves no extras: the modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$.  The classification row reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing module members, no unassigned codons, and no extra module codons.

\paragraph{Unrestricted shape comparison.}
Before imposing amino-acid-class preservation, the shape-significance row used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
This row says that the observed local shape is rare under that finite sampling rule.  It does not say that the codon table was generated by the geometry.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null row.}
The claim row itself tests a stricter null model that preserves amino-acid class information.  The observed size of the comparison object $M$ is $20$.  Across $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving trials with seed $42$, the exact-match count was zero:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ matches}\}&=0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both recorded significance fields equal the corresponding finite-sample values: $0.0$ for exact shape and $0.0035$ for the lower tail.  The interpretation is narrow: after preserving amino-acid class, the observed local codon-window boundary remains rare in the sampled comparison population.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon readback.}
The code-level prediction rows are compatible with the same boundary interpretation.  In the leave-one-code-out table, $23$ code tables were evaluated and $63$ targets were counted.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the high-closure-coverage count was $8$, the predictable count was $8$, and the recovery rate was $1.0$.

\paragraph{Reality contact and stopping rule.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or universality.  Therefore the result is a finite code-layer statement: the standard-code labels, when placed on the six-coordinate codon cube and compared against the amino-acid-class-preserving null row, exhibit a rare local boundary pattern with the numerical statistics above.  Any claim about protein structure, function, mechanism, or organism-wide necessity would require a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.
