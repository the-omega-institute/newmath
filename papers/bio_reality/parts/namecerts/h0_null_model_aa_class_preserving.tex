\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the codon tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon window.  The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, with codons read as six-bit displays over the usual nucleotide positions.  The observed object is not a mechanism of translation, but a code-layer table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The local question is whether the window-six coordinate presentation isolates a small boundary tile in the standard-code labels, and whether that tile remains exceptional after preserving amino-acid label classes in the null sampling scheme.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The finite boundary row is the following set of thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count check reports $|R|=13$.  The punctured-cube check reports the same codon list and the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object used here is a thirteen-vertex local tile with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces.  This is the complete codon list used by the boundary claim in this row; no larger biological law is being inferred from it.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectral computation records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the cubic-polynomial residual.  The spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers give a finite numerical check on the local shape and its comparison row; they do not identify a physical energy, a folding order, or a biochemical rate.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null row.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null model used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed comparison size was $|M|=20$.  Among the sampled class-preserving tables, the exact-shape count was $0$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, so the lower-tail probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=\frac{7}{2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both reported significance fields repeat these same values: $0.0$ for exact shape and $0.0035$ for the lower tail.  Within this finite null model, preserving amino-acid classes does not make the observed local tile look typical.

\paragraph{Unrestricted shape comparison.}
A separate shape-significance row also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, giving $p=0.0005$, while $25$ samples reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  These values are weaker than the exact zero in the amino-acid-class preserving exact-shape row, but they point in the same direction: the finite codon-tile shape is sparse under the sampled comparison regimes.

The leave-one-codon-out recovery row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, and $8$ predictable cases. \paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The data justify a finite code-layer statement: in the standard-code label table, the six-bit codon window isolates a thirteen-codon local tile whose shape is rare under the sampled null comparisons, including the amino-acid-class preserving null model.  The strongest finite-row statement is the amino-acid-class preserving result: $0$ exact matches in $2000$ samples and a lower-tail probability of $0.0035$ at observed $|M|=20$.  The supporting rows add the punctured-cube face-vector, the spectral residual check, the unrestricted shape comparison, the leave-one-code-out hit rates, and the complete leave-one-codon-out module assignment.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This chapter does not claim that the local codon tile is translation itself.  It does not claim folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a global biological necessity.  The only contact used here is the curated standard-code label table, whose observed fact is a codon-to-amino-acid-or-stop assignment.  Any statement about translation realization, folding, physical chemistry, function, or universality would require an additional contact of the appropriate kind.  Amino-acid label readback remains a code-layer observation, not a protein-structure observation.
