\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim and finite setting.}
The record \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving} tests whether the local window-six codon tile survives a null model that preserves amino-acid label classes.  The finite arena is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as the $64$ codons of the standard code after a fixed binary display of the three nucleotide positions.  The distinctions used here are a six-coordinate codon display, local tile faces in that cube, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The biological contact is deliberately narrow: the curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and this chapter uses that table only for code-layer label readback.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
As a cubical subcomplex this set has recorded face-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the observed object is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it also carries a small local cubical geometry with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ square faces.  The module classification accounts for every member of $R$ with no extra or missing codons: $\texttt{agr}=\{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\}$, $\texttt{ar\_lys}=\{\texttt{AAA}\}$, $\texttt{cun}=\{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\}$, $\texttt{ile\_met}=\{\texttt{AUA}\}$, $\texttt{ssn}=\{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\}$, and $\texttt{stop\_trp}=\{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}$.  The recorded totals are $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The null experiment keeps amino-acid class information fixed while randomizing the code-layer arrangement.  In $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving trials, the observed local shape was reproduced exactly $0$ times.  The observed $M$-size was $20$, and only $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below that observed size.  The finite-row statistics are therefore
$$\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
\#\{\text{exact shape matches}\}&=0,\\
\#\{\text{samples with } |M'|\leq 20\}&=7,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
The exact-shape row says that the amino-acid-class preserving null did not recreate the observed tile in the sampled population.  The lower-tail row says that the observed $M$-size lies in a rare part of this null distribution, with empirical mass $7/2000=0.0035$ at or below the observed value.  These are finite sampling statements, not asymptotic biological laws.

\paragraph{Shape comparison against broader randomization.}
A companion shape-significance run used $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  In that run there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The corresponding empirical values were
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
This second row is not identical to the amino-acid-class preserving null, but it provides a finite comparison scale: exact reproduction of the observed local tile is rare in the sampled random background, and near-reproduction remains uncommon at the $0.9$ Jaccard threshold.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  Broken out by family, the hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchors, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These rows show that the local tile is useful for code-layer recovery tasks under the tested finite deletions.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The claim is ready as a finite BioReality record: window-six coordinates expose a reproducible local codon-tile pattern whose boundary set $R$ has explicit codons, cubical face data, module coverage, spectral values, null-model rarity, and leave-one recovery behavior.  The class-preserving null is the most restrictive row in this record because it tests whether the pattern remains rare even when amino-acid class structure is retained.  In that sampled null, exact reproduction occurs $0$ times out of $2000$, and the lower-tail mass at the observed $M$-size is $0.0035$.

\paragraph{Local scope limit.}
The result remains at the code-read layer.  From these rows alone one may say that the curated standard-code labels align with a finite local tile in $Q_6$, and that the observed tile is rare under the recorded null experiments.  One may not infer translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a biochemical mechanism, or universality across life.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate observation source targeted at that layer.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded structures, CUN/UUR wording is not structural evidence, and the standard-code table is not a mechanism for translation or function.
