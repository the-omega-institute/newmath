\subsection{Amino-Acid-Class-Preserving Null Test for the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record tests the claim that the standard-code codon table, viewed only through a six-coordinate codon display and through curated amino-acid or stop labels, has a local tile shape that is not explained by random reassignment inside amino-acid label classes. The carrier is the finite codon cube $Q_6$, with codons read as six-bit window coordinates after the ordinary four-letter codon alphabet has been fixed. The relevant observed boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports that this set is a punctured-cube pattern with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tested object is not an unrestricted biological mechanism. It is a finite code-layer shape: a local boundary tile inside $Q_6$, labelled by the curated standard genetic-code table.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving comparison.}
The null model preserves amino-acid label classes while randomizing the geometry that is visible to the tile probe. In the reported run there were $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. The observed tile has $|M|=20$. Among the $2000$ amino-acid-class-preserving samples, the exact observed shape occurred $0$ times, so the exact-shape Monte Carlo value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count for samples with size at most the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{tail}}=7/2000=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both reported significance fields therefore agree with the numerical readout: the exact class-preserving match is absent in the sample, and the observed size lies in a small lower tail of this finite null ensemble.

\paragraph{Comparison with the broader shape null.}
A second shape probe, also run at $N=2000$ with seed $42$, gives useful scale. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. In that broader null ensemble, there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{shape}}&=1/2000=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jac}\ge 0.9}&=25/2000=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null is therefore not an isolated numerical curiosity. It sits beside a separate finite shape test in which exact agreement is rare and high-overlap agreement is still uncommon.

\paragraph{Spectral finite signature.}
The same local tile record includes a spectral row. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check returns $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, numerically indistinguishable from zero at the reported tolerance. The spectral-radius field repeats $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$. These numbers give the tile a finite numerical fingerprint: the codon boundary is not only a list of $13$ codons, but also a reproducible coordinate-and-spectrum package attached to that list.

\paragraph{Code-table recovery rows.}
Leave-one-code-out evaluation used $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$. Leave-one-codon-out evaluation reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. These rows say that, at the code-label level, the local tile supplies a strong finite recovery pattern. They do not say that translation has been physically realized or that any protein structure has been observed.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row groups $5$ families. The reported hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast. The codon-module classification is complete: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and there are no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has a predictable-or-boundary status in the finite row. This makes the claim local and auditable: each codon in $R$ is accounted for by a named finite module, and the module partition has no leftover codons.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is adequate for code-layer readback and local label-split checks. It is not an observation of ribosomal translation, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law. In particular, reading an amino-acid label sequence from codons does not observe a folded protein. Any statement about translation realization, structural order, physical possibility, function, or universality would require a distinct contact supplying that higher-layer evidence. The present result is therefore a finite code-read claim: within the given curated labels and six-coordinate codon window, the amino-acid-class-preserving null sample did not reproduce the observed tile shape, while related finite recovery, module, and spectral rows remain numerically coherent.
