\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null for the window-six codon tile}\label{subsec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim being tested.}
The finite object in this record is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  Each codon is treated as a point of the codon cube $Q_6$, and the only external contact used here is the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The question is not whether the coordinate geometry causes the genetic code.  The question is narrower: after the standard labels are read as data, does the observed local tile survive a null model that preserves amino-acid label classes?  The tested claim is the null-model row $h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving$.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The finite readback is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
    &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is also recorded as a punctured-cube shape with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local codon tile is not being inferred from an unlimited biological principle; it is a finite combinatorial trace attached to thirteen named codons and sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies inside the six-bit display.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving comparison.}
The amino-acid-class-preserving null run used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$.  The observed local object had $|M|=20$.  No sampled table produced the exact observed shape:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
\operatorname{exact\_match\_count}&=0,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail statistic counted sampled tables at or below the observed $M$-size.  Seven of the $2000$ samples lay in that tail, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{lower\_tail\_count}_{\le |M|}&=7,\\
p_{\le |M|}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central finite-row result for the present claim: when amino-acid classes are preserved, the exact local tile was not recovered in the sampled null ensemble, and the observed size lay in a small lower tail of the sampled distribution.

\paragraph{Shape comparison against unconstrained samples.}
A companion shape-significance row used the same sample size and seed, $N=2000$ and $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  In that run there was one exact shape match, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{exact\_shape\_matches}&=1,\\
p_{\mathrm{shape}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Jaccard}_{\ge 0.9}\operatorname{\ matches}&=25,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers agree with the amino-acid-class-preserving row in the following limited sense: the local tile is rare under the finite comparisons that were actually run.  They do not convert the coordinate tile into an organism-level mechanism.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The admissible reading is therefore precise.  The standard-code label table supplies a finite external contact for codon-to-label assignments.  The six-coordinate display exposes a rare local boundary tile under the sampled null comparisons, and the supporting rows record its cardinality, punctured-cube shape, spectrum, recovery behavior, family-level rates, and module assignment.  The result remains at the code-read layer.  It does not assert translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code formation, or universality across life.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind, not merely the curated codon-label table used here.
