\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null test for the local codon tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
This record tests whether the window-six codon coordinate system produces a local boundary pattern that is more rigid than an amino-acid-class preserving randomization would normally produce. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as the standard set of $64$ codons with a curated standard-code label attached to each codon. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: the table gives codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop readback, and nothing in this row observes translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal mechanism, or universal biological necessity.

\paragraph{Finite tile data.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube boundary with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is not a vague visual cluster: it is a finite subset of the codon cube with a fixed row count, fixed listed codons, and a fixed elementary face count. The observed ambient module size used by the amino-acid-class preserving null is $|M|=20$.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving randomization.}
The null model keeps the amino-acid label classes intact and samples class-preserving alternatives. With seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled tables, the observed local shape had no exact match under the class-preserving null:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\hbox{exact matches}\}=0,
\qquad
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the lower-tail statistic based on the observed module size $|M|=20$, the finite row records $7$ sampled cases at or below the observed size, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}={7\over 2000}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the chapter's central finite-row result: preserving amino-acid classes does not, in this sample, ordinarily recreate the same local codon-tile shape, and only a small lower-tail fraction reaches the observed module-size threshold.

\paragraph{Shape comparison against a broader null.}
A separate shape-significance row gives a broader comparison at the same sample count $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It reports $57{,}377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55{,}377$ duplicate retries, one exact-shape match, and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$. The corresponding finite probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}=0.0005,
\qquad
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second row supports the same reading from a different finite summary: the local shape is not merely a high-frequency accident of the sampling frame, although the high-Jaccard count also shows that near-neighbor shapes exist and should not be erased from the interpretation.

Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the finite row. This is the strongest local reading licensed here: the codon cube, standard-code labels, and sampled null comparisons isolate a reproducible code-layer boundary tile.

\paragraph{Scope of the conclusion.}
The verified row supports the following bounded statement. In the six-bit codon cube with curated standard-code labels, the listed $13$-codon boundary set has a punctured-cube face profile, a stable module partition, nontrivial leave-one recovery behavior, and a low-frequency match profile under amino-acid-class preserving randomization. The contact remains a codon-label table. Therefore the result is a finite code-readback observation about local tile structure, not an observation of translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, global mechanism, or universality. Any future claim across one of those boundaries must bring its own independent empirical contact.
