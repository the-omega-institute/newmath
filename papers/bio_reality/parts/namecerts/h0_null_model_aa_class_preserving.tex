\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null for the codon window}

\paragraph{Scope of the record.}
This BioReality record concerns the claim \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving}.  The object under study is the standard-code codon table, read only as a finite codon-to-label table.  The coordinate display is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes used as the finite distinctions.  The biological contact is the curated standard genetic-code table: it supplies the codon label readback, meaning that each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label.  It does not supply a translation process, a folding experiment, a physical-admissibility test, a function assay, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.  All numerical claims below are therefore claims about code-layer label geometry and its finite null comparisons.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
The null experiment preserves amino-acid class information while randomizing against the exact observed shape.  With seed $42$ and $N=2000$ sampled null tables, the observed comparison row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{observed\_M\_size}&=20,\\
\operatorname{exact\_match\_count}&=0,\\
\operatorname{lower\_tail\_count\_le\_observed\_M\_size}&=7.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding empirical probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
In this finite run, no amino-acid-class-preserving null sample reproduced the exact shape, and only seven of the $2000$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  This supports the narrower statement that the standard-code labels exhibit a rare local tile boundary under this null model.  It does not support any inference about protein realization, folded structure, biochemical feasibility, biological function, or universality.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The curated code table is a valid contact for codon-label readback and local split checks.  It is not a contact for translation realization, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Amino-acid label sequences read from the table are therefore not folded protein structures, and a local codon tile is not a global biological law.  Any later claim crossing from label geometry into translation, molecular structure, or organismal function must attach a separate reality contact suited to that layer.  Without such a contact, the present record remains a finite, code-level account of a rare local boundary pattern in the standard genetic-code table.
