\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The standard-code codon table is treated here only as a curated codon-to-label contact.  The finite object under test is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ equipped with a local tile, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The claim is not that the genetic code is forced by this coordinate system.  It is the narrower statement that the observed local tile remains unusually shaped even after a null model is constrained to preserve amino-acid classes.  In the run recorded for this row, the observed tile size was
$$
\begin{aligned}
|M| &= 20.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against $N=2000$ amino-acid-class preserving samples with seed $42$, the exact observed shape appeared $0$ times.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed tile size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0/2000 = 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}} &= 7/2000 = 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact value $0.0$ is a finite-sampling report: it means that no exact match occurred in the $2000$ sampled trials, not that a mathematical probability has been proven to be zero.

\paragraph{Finite codon rows.}
The boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified codons in $R$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check returns the same thirteen codons and has face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row contains a concrete local combinatorial shape rather than merely a label-frequency statistic.  The spectral check places the corresponding row in a stable numerical regime: the recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with cubic residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$.  This residual is numerically negligible at the precision of the run and serves as a consistency check on the spectral calculation for the finite row.

\paragraph{Comparison with unconstrained shape sampling.}
A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, of which $55377$ were duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The reported probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{shape}} &= 1/2000 = 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 25/2000 = 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class preserving null model is therefore the stricter comparison for this row: it keeps label-class information in the randomization and still gives no exact match in the $2000$ sampled trials, with only seven samples in the lower tail for $|M|$.

\paragraph{Predictive stress tests.}
The same local tile has finite leave-out behavior at the code-read layer.  In the leave-one-code-out experiment, $23$ tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  Restricting to $M-T$ gave hit rate $0.9365079365079365$, and allowing the boundary gave $0.9841269841269841$.  In the leave-one-codon-out experiment, the row had $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.

\paragraph{Family and module resolution.}
The family-resolved leave-one-code-out row contains $5$ families.  The rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, $1.0$ for candidate-other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast.  The thirteen boundary codons are also fully assigned to six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module-classification audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  This makes the row suitable for local readback: every boundary codon belongs to a named finite module, and every module has either predictable or boundary behavior.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for code-layer readback and for testing whether a local codon-tile split is compatible with the recorded label table.  It is not a translation experiment, not a folding observation, not a measurement of physical admissibility, not a biological-function assay, not a mechanism, and not evidence of universality across life.  Any claim at those higher layers would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind.  The present row therefore supports a bounded conclusion: within the standard-code label table, the window-six local tile has a finite boundary set of thirteen codons, a punctured-cube face profile $(13,16,4)$, a stable spectral row, and a statistically uncommon shape under both unconstrained and amino-acid-class preserving finite null comparisons.
