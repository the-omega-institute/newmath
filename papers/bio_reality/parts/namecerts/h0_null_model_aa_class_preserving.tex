\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the local codon tile}
\label{sec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  A codon is represented as a six-coordinate display, and the finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$ with its codon labels read from a curated standard-code table.  The tested distinction is local: a boundary tile is allowed to separate codon positions and amino-acid or stop labels, but the table contact itself supplies only codon-to-label assignments.  It does not observe translation in a cell, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism of origin, or a universal law.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and consists exactly of
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a cubical subobject it has the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a label count: it fixes a concrete thirteen-codon boundary pattern together with a finite local face profile.

\paragraph{Null model and observed count.}
The null model preserves amino-acid label classes while randomizing the codon placement used to test the local shape.  In the verified run with seed $42$, the sample size was $N=2000$.  The observed local tile size was $|M|=20$.  Among the $2000$ amino-acid-class preserving samples, no sample matched the exact observed shape, and only $7$ samples landed in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  Equivalently, the finite-row statistics are
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
\#\{\text{exact shape matches}\}&=0,\\
\#\{\text{samples with size}\leq 20\}&=7,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The conclusion is therefore a code-layer rejection of the corresponding amino-acid-class preserving null at the recorded scale.  It says that the observed local codon tile is unusually placed relative to that constrained null ensemble.  It does not say that the geometry explains translation, folding, or function.

\paragraph{Relation to the unconstrained shape survey.}
The same finite object also appears in a broader shape-significance survey.  That run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$, required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$.  The resulting finite probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{shape}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class preserving row is stricter in one respect: the exact-match count drops to $0$ under the label-class preserving constraint, while the lower-tail statistic records $7$ samples out of $2000$.  These two rows are compatible local tests of the same boundary phenomenon, not evidence for a higher biological layer.

This supplies a numerical consistency check for the local finite construction: the boundary set, the tile count, and the spectral calculation all point to a stable finite code-read pattern.  The residual is a computation about the codon-coordinate object, not an observation of molecular realization.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.}
The code-read boundary also has predictive behavior under finite deletion tests.  In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$ and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The recorded hit rate above baseline was likewise $0.9841269841269841$.

In the leave-one-codon-out row, there were $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers show that the local tile is not a one-row accident of the full table: the finite deletion probes still recover the code-layer pattern at high rate.  They remain deletion probes over codon labels, so their scope is still restricted to readback of the standard-code table.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The leave-one-code-out family summary separates $5$ families.  The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial and plastid codes, $1.0$ for candidate other codes, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate codes.  This family split is useful because it keeps the finite statement from depending on a single aggregate average.

The thirteen boundary codons are also completely assigned to named modules.  The recorded module decomposition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\

The module audit records $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from modules, and no unassigned codons.  Every module has a predictable-or-boundary status, so the module accounting closes exactly over the finite boundary set.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The amino-acid-class preserving null row is accepted as a finite code-read result: the exact-shape null probability is $0.0$ in $2000$ samples, the lower-tail probability is $0.0035$, and the same boundary set is supported by the punctured-cube face vector, spectral check, deletion probes, family rates, and complete module classification.  The scientifically permitted statement is local and conditional on the curated standard-code contact: window-six codon coordinates expose a structured boundary tile in codon-label space.  No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless a separate observation is supplied at that higher layer.
