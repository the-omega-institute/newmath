\subsection{Amino-acid-class preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bior-aa-class-preserving-null-model}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The claim identified by \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving} concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The coordinate object is the $Q_6$ codon cube, with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes as the operative finite distinctions.  The biological readout used here is only the curated codon-to-label table for the standard code: each of the $64$ codons is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label.  The experiment asks whether the observed local tile can be explained by random relabelings that preserve amino-acid class sizes.  In this restricted null model the observed tile remains rare: among $N=2000$ sampled class-preserving tables with seed $42$, the exact observed shape appeared $0$ times, while only $7$ samples had tile size at most the observed size $20$.  Thus the finite Monte Carlo readout is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N &= 2000,\\
|M_{\mathrm{obs}}| &= 20,\\
\#\{\hbox{exact matches}\} &= 0,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0,\\
\#\{|M_{\mathrm{sample}}|\leq 20\} &= 7,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is evidence against the particular amino-acid-class preserving null ensemble, not evidence that the coordinate geometry by itself generates biology.

\paragraph{Boundary set and punctured cube shape.}
The recovered boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are exactly \texttt{AAA}, \texttt{AGA}, \texttt{AGG}, \texttt{AUA}, \texttt{CUA}, \texttt{CUC}, \texttt{CUG}, \texttt{CUU}, \texttt{UAA}, \texttt{UAG}, \texttt{UCA}, \texttt{UGA}, and \texttt{UUA}.  The same row is recorded as a punctured cube with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R) &= (13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
The important point is that this is a finite local shape inside the codon coordinate display.  It may organize label changes near the standard table, but it is not a global law of translation, molecular folding, physical viability, cellular function, evolutionary mechanism, or universality.  Those stronger readings would require separate contacts that directly measure the relevant layer.

These numbers support the claim that the tile has a stable finite numerical fingerprint under the present construction.  They do not identify a protein structure, an energetic constraint, a translational apparatus, or a functional phenotype.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under broader sampling.}
A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives and required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries, at seed $42$.  The exact observed shape appeared once, so the exact-shape frequency was $p=0.0005$.  A high-overlap criterion with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ accepted $25$ samples, giving $p=0.0125$.  In symbols,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\hbox{exact shape matches}\} &= 1,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}} &= 0.0005,\\
\#\{J\geq 0.9\} &= 25,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Together with the amino-acid-class preserving run, this says that the finite shape is not a common byproduct of the tested randomizations.  The evidence remains finite-row evidence: it is a table-level regularity, checked against declared finite null ensembles.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  These figures show that the local tile is useful for recovering code-table label changes in the tested family of finite tables.  They should be read as code-layer prediction numbers, not as evidence that the label sequence is realized as a folded protein or as a physical admissibility certificate.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closures, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The same $1.0$ value is recorded for recovery above the declared threshold.  The row therefore supports the local-readback interpretation: when a codon is withheld, the finite coordinate relations can still recover the relevant code-layer placement in the tested cases.  This is still only a statement about codon-label assignment.  No folding, function, translation realization, physical admissibility, mechanism, or universal biological necessity follows from this recovery statistic.

This module accounting is the finite row that prevents the $13$-codon boundary from being an unnamed residue set: each codon is assigned to a declared local module.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local verdict.}
The only biological contact used by this chapter is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact supports codon-label readback and local split checks.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, does not observe a folded protein, does not measure structural order, does not test physical admissibility, does not assay function, and does not establish a universal biological mechanism.  The ready verdict is therefore narrow: the standard-code table contains a finite window-six boundary tile whose $13$-codon punctured-cube row, spectral fingerprint, module accounting, and null-model rarity are all recorded by finite experiments.  The amino-acid-class preserving null model gives $0/2000$ exact matches and a lower-tail rate $7/2000=0.0035$ at observed size $20$, so the observed local tile is exceptional inside that tested ensemble.  The chapter claims exactly that code-layer regularity and nothing above it.
