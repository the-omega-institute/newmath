\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null model for the window-six codon tile}
\label{subsec:h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The finite object in this record is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The coordinate carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the readout is restricted to codon labels: amino-acid names and stop labels as assigned by the curated standard-code table.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  This subsection concerns the null model in which amino-acid classes are preserved while the local window-six geometry is sampled.  The question is therefore not whether the code is generated by the coordinate system.  The question is whether the observed local tile and its complement behave like a typical amino-acid-class-preserving relabelling sample.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The observed boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check gives the same thirteen codons and has face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary object is not merely a count: it is a specific finite subset of the codon cube with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the recorded local geometry.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null sample.}
For claim $\mathrm{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving}$, the experiment run $\mathrm{e8d5de747a0c}$ sampled $N=2000$ amino-acid-class-preserving alternatives with seed $42$.  The observed size of the companion set $M$ is $20$.  Among the $2000$ samples, the exact observed shape was never reproduced:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\text{exact matches}\}=0,\qquad
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\text{samples with } |M'|\leq 20\}=7,\qquad
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
In this finite sample, the amino-acid-class-preserving null therefore does not explain the observed window-six tile as a common outcome.  The exact-shape statistic is absent from the sample, and the lower-tail statistic places the observed $M$ size in a small tail of the sampled class-preserving alternatives.

\paragraph{Comparison with the unconstrained shape sample.}
The separate shape-significance run $\mathrm{0b267c88f8e4}$ used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, which is a useful warning that the finite sampling space has heavy collision pressure under this sampler.  In that run there was $1$ exact shape match, and $25$ samples had Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$ with the observed shape:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact\ shape}}=0.0005,\qquad
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving null is therefore not weaker than the unconstrained shape comparison in the exact-match direction; in the class-preserving run the exact count is $0$ rather than $1$ out of $2000$.  The two finite rows jointly support the narrower statement that the observed tile is atypical under the recorded null families, while leaving all mechanistic interpretation outside the result.

\paragraph{Scope of the result.}
The evidence supports a local statement about the standard-code label table: the observed window-six tile, its thirteen-codon boundary, the twenty-element $M$ size, the spectral row, and the leave-one-out rows are mutually coherent at the code-read layer and are atypical under the recorded amino-acid-class-preserving null sample.  The contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is not an observation of translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  No such higher-layer claim follows from this subsection.  Any future promotion beyond code-label readout requires a separate empirical contact whose measured object is already at that higher layer.
