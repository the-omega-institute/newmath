\subsection{Amino-acid-class-preserving null test for the codon boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-null-model-aa-class-preserving}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table as a finite labelled object on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The internal display has coordinate positions, a local closure operation, a spectral measurement, and a relation extracted from the labelled corners of the cube.  The biological contact is narrower: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  Thus the finite datum tested here is not translation as a cellular process, and it is not protein structure.  It is a code-layer readback in which codon labels can be compared with local cube geometry.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished relation $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The finite row identifies it as
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that this is a punctured-cube shaped set with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ elements of $R$: $\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$ in the $\mathrm{agr}$ module, $\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$ in $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$, $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ in $\mathrm{cun}$, $\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$ in $\mathrm{ile\_met}$, $\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$ in $\mathrm{ssn}$, and $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$ in $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$.  The module audit records $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
The null model keeps amino-acid class information fixed while sampling alternative labelled arrangements.  In $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$, the observed local object has $|M|=20$.  The exact-shape match count is $0$, giving reported exact-shape probability $0.0$.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving lower-tail probability $7/2000=0.0035$.  In finite-row form the test summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
|M|&=20,\\
\#\{\text{exact matches}\}&=0,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0,\\
\#\{\text{samples with } |M'|\leq 20\}&=7,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is therefore not an artefact of merely preserving amino-acid classes in the sampled tables: under this finite null, no sampled table reproduced the exact observed shape, and only seven sampled tables landed in the lower tail at the observed size threshold.

\paragraph{Shape and spectrum checks.}
A separate shape-significance row, also using $N=2000$ and seed $42$, needed $57{,}377$ distinct-sample attempts because $55{,}377$ attempts were duplicate retries.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  Its radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic polynomial check, so the spectral calculation is internally consistent at the recorded numerical tolerance.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The code-table leave-one-out row evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$; the same $0.9841269841269841$ is reported as the rate above baseline.  By family, the hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast, across $5$ families.  The codon leave-one-out row reports recovery rate $1.0$, recovery rate above threshold $1.0$, predictable count $8$, closure coverage high $8$, boundary count $5$, and anchor count $0$.  These rows support the narrow statement that the boundary tile is predictive for code-layer label recovery under these finite probes.

\paragraph{Scope of the record.}
The chapter’s empirical contact is the curated codon-to-label table.  That contact can test codon-label readback and local label splitting, but it does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal necessity.  Amino-acid label sequences are still labels at the code layer; they are not three-dimensional proteins.  The local tile can therefore be stated as a finite and reproducible code-read pattern on $Q_6$, with strong finite null-model separation and high leave-one recovery, but no claim crosses into translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality unless another contact independently measures that higher layer.
