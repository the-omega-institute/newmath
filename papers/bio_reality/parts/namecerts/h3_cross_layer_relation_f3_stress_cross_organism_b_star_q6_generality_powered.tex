\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This record concerns the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate called $f_3$-stress. The coordinate is not an amino-acid composition claim: it is read after quotienting by the amino-acid projection, so that the relevant signal has the form $q-P_{\tau}q$ rather than a raw codon-usage count. The finite biological question is whether this residual coordinate keeps explanatory power across organisms only when the paired codon readout has a wobble-weight asymmetry between a codon $c$ and its $f_3$ partner $c^{f_3}$. In symbols, the admissible route has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{f_3} &= q-P_{\tau}q,\\
w_c &\neq w_{c^{f_3}},\\
S^{QT}_{f_3,j} &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
after the declared controls and rank checks. If the wobble weights remain symmetric on the paired fiber, or if the controlled panel is rank-saturated, the same coordinate remains a bounded codon-residual readout and is not promoted to a translation-layer distinction.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No stronger biological conclusion follows from these rows alone. The chapter does not claim folded protein structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, protein localization, post-translational state, or a universal law. It also does not treat modeled tAI or stAI weights as observed ribosome occupancy, nor does it treat the $3$-organism bounded coupling row as powered survival. Translation-layer language here means only the finite controlled readout surfaces recorded above: modeled codon-adaptation readbacks, powered $S^{QT}$ context, and the bounded position-resolved occupancy contact. Any claim about protein output, folding, physical feasibility, mechanism, function, phenotype, or universality would require a separate layer-matched contact and a same-row statistic for that layer.
