\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record treats m6A site load as a finite RNA-coordinate label only after it is joined to a same-gene half-life readout and to the explicit coverage rule used by the boundary statistic. \paragraph{Observed boundary statistic.}
The finite statistic crosses the declared boundary in the sense that the static DMSO association and the perturbation-associated half-life change are both numerically separated from the null after the stated filtering. In the static condition, the Spearman association between m6A load and $\log_2$ half-life in DMSO is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929 .
\end{aligned}
$$
For the perturbation-associated readout, the association between m6A load and the change in $\log_2$ half-life is positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.245804,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL}\mid \mathrm{length}) &= 0.227683,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}} &= 0.000999001 .
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers license a finite RNA half-life relation: under the registered coverage rule, m6A site load is not exchangeable with the half-life-change readout in the joined rows. The sign contrast is also interpretable at the boundary level: higher static load is associated with shorter DMSO half-life, while the perturbation-associated change is positively ordered with site load under the reported statistic. This is a statement about the measured RNA half-life surface, not about translation output or a downstream biological role.

The permitted conclusion is narrow: in the filtered finite panel, m6A site load remains associated with mRNA half-life and with the perturbation-associated half-life change after the declared row-selection and length-partial checks.
