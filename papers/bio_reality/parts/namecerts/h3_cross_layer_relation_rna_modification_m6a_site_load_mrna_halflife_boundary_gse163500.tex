\subsection{m6A Site Load and the RNA Half-Life Boundary in a Joined Human Panel}\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite human transcript panel in which an RNA-modification coordinate is joined to an mRNA half-life readout under a perturbation-sensitive comparison. The operative row is a gene-level joined tuple containing an m6A site-load coordinate, a DMSO half-life value, a perturbation-linked change in log half-life, transcript length information for a partial-rank control, and the coverage rule used to retain rows. The retained panel contains $5837$ genes. The admissibility rule is explicit: a row is kept only when the smaller of the DMSO and STM2457 mean transcript coverages is at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes. This makes the statistic a finite readout over covered, jointly observed rows rather than an extrapolation over all human transcripts.

\paragraph{Declared statistic.}
The finite readout separates a static stability association from a perturbation-linked boundary statistic. In the DMSO condition, the rank association between m6A load and log half-life is negative, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929 .
\end{aligned}
$$
For the perturbation-linked readout, the rank association between m6A load and the change in log half-life is positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.245804 .
\end{aligned}
$$
After partialing transcript length, the corresponding Spearman statistic remains positive at
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}\mid \mathrm{length})=0.227683 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$. The finite-row verdict attached to this statistic is that the boundary is crossed: the external m6A and half-life data support the predeclared RNA-stability boundary statistic.

\paragraph{Layer reading.}
The chapter reads the statistic as a bounded cross-layer relation between an RNA-modification coordinate and an RNA half-life response. The negative static DMSO association and the positive perturbation-linked change are not interchangeable claims. The static value records how m6A load is ranked against baseline half-life in the retained rows. The perturbation-linked value records that the same coordinate is associated with a change in RNA stability after the STM2457 comparison, and that this association is not erased by the length partial statistic. The local conclusion is therefore narrow: in this finite panel, m6A site load has a measured relation to RNA half-life behavior under the declared coverage rule and the declared permutation test.

\paragraph{Boundary against stronger mechanisms.}
This record does not by itself establish translation realization, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation in the unrestricted sense, or a universal law. It also does not establish a decay-factor RBP mechanism. The finite rows used here do not contain same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target control arms. A positive half-life statistic may motivate a mechanistic panel, but it is not already that panel. In particular, no m6A-to-RBP bridge is licensed unless the same finite rows carry measured local RBP occupancy and perturbation or rescue behavior that separates the proposed RBP route from transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation-readout boundary.

\paragraph{Relation to adjacent RNA-stability records.}
The m6A boundary is not isolated inside the finite evidence table. A broader human m6A load and half-life record retains $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, uses $1000$ permutations, and reports $3$ sign-concordant comparisons. That companion statistic supports the existence of a wider RNA-modification-to-half-life surface, while the present GSE163500 record supplies the sharper perturbation-linked readout with $5837$ retained genes and a two-sided partial permutation value below $0.001$. A distinct endogenous 3UTR ARE half-life transfer record retains $14933$ genes, reports a negative ARE coefficient $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, improves test $R^2$ from $0.0657771$ to $0.0827561$, and has $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$ over $2000$ permutations. These neighboring records are useful because they show that RNA-stability boundaries can be finite, signed, and separately controlled without being promoted to protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter licenses exactly the following finite statement. On the retained $5837$-gene joined panel, m6A site load is associated with RNA half-life behavior under the stated coverage rule: the baseline DMSO association is $-0.388929$, the perturbation-linked association with $\Delta\log_2$ half-life is $0.245804$, the length-partial statistic is $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$. This is enough for a bounded RNA-modification-to-RNA-stability boundary claim. It is not enough for a translation claim, an RBP-mechanism claim, a protein-layer claim, a structural claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a biological-function claim, or a universal biological law.
