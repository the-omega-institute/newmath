\subsection{m6A Site Load and the RNA Half-Life Boundary in GSE163500}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-halflife-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined transcript panel in which an m6A site-load coordinate is compared with an RNA half-life readout under a perturbation contrast.  The row unit is a gene-level joined observation retained after the declared coverage rule: $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ STM2457})$ must be at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  After that filter the evaluated panel contains $5837$ genes.  Each retained row carries an m6A-load quantity, a DMSO half-life readout, a perturbation-associated half-life shift, gene length for partial control, and the declared pass or refusal condition for the finite statistic.  The relevant BioReality object is therefore not a universal law about modified RNA, but a bounded finite relation between a measured m6A coordinate and an RNA half-life surface in the GSE163500-derived joined table.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
The static comparison in the DMSO state is negative: the Spearman association between m6A load and $\log_2$ half-life is $-0.388929$.  The perturbation-associated comparison has the opposite direction for the half-life shift: the Spearman association between m6A load and $\Delta \log_2$ half-life is $0.245804$.  After controlling for length, the partial Spearman value remains $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$.  The local numerical certificate can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 5837,\\
 \rho(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 H_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 H) &= 0.245804,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{partial\ length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 H) &= 0.227683,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,2sided}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
On these rows the predeclared verdict is that the boundary is crossed: the m6A-load coordinate has a reproducible finite relation to the RNA half-life readout under the stated coverage and length-control rules.

\paragraph{Layer placement.}
The result licenses a restricted cross-layer relation from an RNA-modification coordinate to an RNA-stability readout.  It does not by itself identify translation output, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological rule.  The translation readout mentioned in the governing finite-row design is a boundary condition for avoiding promotion from RNA half-life to translation.  It is not supplied by this statistic as a positive same-row translation mechanism.  Likewise, matched mRNA abundance and transcription controls are needed to separate half-life behavior from abundance or production effects, but the half-life statistic is not itself an mRNA-production claim.

\paragraph{Mechanism refusal.}
This finite result must also be kept below the decay-factor RBP mechanism level.  A mechanism bridge would require the same finite rows to include a prespecified decay-factor RBP, measured local RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and controls for local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  Those rows are not present in the verified GSE163500 boundary statistic.  Consequently, the supported statement is that m6A site load separates RNA half-life behavior on the retained finite panel; it is not that a particular RBP decay mechanism has been established.

\paragraph{Companion calibration.}
A broader human m6A half-life calibration record is available for comparison, but it is not used to enlarge the claim.  That companion record contains $135300$ sites over $15845$ genes, uses $1000$ permutations, and reports $3$ sign-concordant components.  Its role here is only to show that the GSE163500 boundary sits inside a larger family of m6A and half-life contacts.  It does not supply the missing RBP occupancy, rescue, or translation rows for the present finite panel.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter therefore records a positive RNA-stability boundary and a negative promotion boundary.  The positive part is the finite, length-controlled, permutation-supported relation between m6A load and RNA half-life shift on $5837$ retained genes.  The negative part is equally important: no translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, RBP mechanism, causation, adaptation, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurements and finite statistic.
