\subsection{m6A Site Load and the RNA Half-Life Boundary in a Finite Human Panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The object of this record is a finite joined human gene panel in which an m6A site-load coordinate is read in the same row as an RNA half-life response. The evaluated table contains $5837$ joined genes after the declared coverage filter
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage}_{\mathrm{DMSO}},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage}_{\mathrm{STM2457}})
\geq \mathrm{20th\ percentile}
\end{aligned}
$$
among valid joined genes. Each retained row is used only as a row of measured RNA-layer evidence: m6A load, DMSO half-life, STM2457-associated half-life displacement, gene length, and the filtering information needed to keep the comparison finite. The biological content is therefore not an unrestricted statement about m6A, translation, or decay machinery; it is a bounded statement about whether the measured coordinate crosses an RNA half-life boundary in this dataset.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $q_i$ denote the finite m6A load coordinate for gene $i$, let $h_i^{\mathrm{DMSO}}$ denote the DMSO log half-life readout, and let $\Delta h_i$ denote the perturbation-associated log half-life displacement. The registered readback is summarized by the three scalar checks
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(q,h^{\mathrm{DMSO}})&=-0.388929,\\
\rho(q,\Delta h)&=0.245804,\\
\rho(q,\Delta h\mid \mathrm{length})&=0.227683.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite permutation check reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{two\ sided}}=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row panel is not being used to infer a universal m6A law. It licenses a narrower conclusion: in these $5837$ joined rows, the m6A load coordinate remains associated with the RNA half-life displacement after the stated length control, and the finite permutation statistic is strong enough to mark the RNA half-life boundary as crossed.

\paragraph{Direction and interpretation.}
The static DMSO association is negative, while the perturbation-associated displacement is positive. This sign pattern matters because it prevents the chapter from collapsing all m6A-half-life statements into a single verbal rule. The record instead keeps two measurements separate. The static baseline statistic says that larger m6A load is associated with lower DMSO half-life in the joined table. The perturbation statistic says that larger m6A load is associated with a positive half-life displacement under the STM2457 comparison used by the finite analysis. The result is therefore a signed, condition-indexed row statistic, not a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Boundary classification.}
The verdict attached to the finite run is $\mathrm{crosses\_boundary}$. The status semantics are local: passing means that the external m6A and half-life dataset supports the pre-registered RNA half-life boundary statistic. It does not by itself supply measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate-occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target rescue, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, causation, adaptation, or a general biological law. In particular, an RNA half-life difference is not promoted to translation realization merely because a same-row translation boundary is required for stronger cross-layer interpretations. Translation remains a separate readout requirement, and protein output remains another layer again.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite check.}
A second m6A-half-life record gives a broader but still bounded comparison surface. In the human m6AConquer-linked cross-organism check, the finite payload contains $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, with $1000$ actual permutations and $3$ sign-concordant cases. This neighboring fact is not substituted for the GSE163500 statistic. It is cited only to show that the BioReality record is not relying on a single verbal m6A motif story: the local GSE163500 result supplies the signed half-life boundary statistic, while the larger m6AConquer-linked record supplies an adjacent finite-row consistency check over a larger site table.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The chapter refuses every stronger reading unless the relevant rows are actually present. If high- and low-coordinate labels become exchangeable after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch controls, then the coordinate is only a descriptor. If RNA half-life changes but no measured decay-factor RBP occupancy changes, the result remains an RNA-stability association. If target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective control, and non-target control are absent, the RBP bridge is not established. If RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, or batch reproduces the observed pattern, the proposed bridge is rejected. No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, universality, or protein-output statement follows without a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
