\subsection{m6A Site Load and mRNA Half-Life as a Translation-Bounded RNA-Stability Contact}\label{subsec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite joined panel of coding-RNA rows in which an m6A site-load coordinate is read together with an mRNA half-life response.  The admissible carrier is not an unrestricted sequence law: it is a matched RNA-stability panel whose rows must preserve gene or transcript identity, a site-load coordinate, the half-life readout, coverage eligibility, and the declared covariates used to separate length, local composition, local structure, isoform state, transcriptional or abundance effects, and batch.  In the verified GSE163500 row set, the retained table has $5837$ genes after the coverage rule
$$\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})\geq \text{20th percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}$$
The reported finite outcome is a positive boundary decision, recorded as \texttt{crosses\_boundary}, for the predeclared RNA-stability statistic.  The same row explicitly states that this decision is not a mechanism claim and is not a function claim.

\paragraph{Observed scalar readout.}
The static untreated association between m6A load and log half-life in DMSO is negative, with
$$\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929.
\end{aligned}$$
The perturbation-oriented statistic has the opposite sign for the finite response contrast, with
$$\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2\mathrm{HL})=0.245804,
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}}=0.227683.
\end{aligned}$$
The permutation calculation gives a two-sided partial-permutation value
$$\begin{aligned}
p=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the chapter licenses only this finite statement: within the retained coverage-filtered gene panel, m6A site load is separated enough from the length baseline to pass the declared RNA half-life boundary test.  The numbers are row-level audit values, not symbols for a universal biological constant.

\paragraph{Cross-row calibration.}
The same audit collection contains a broader human m6A half-life contact with $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, using $1000$ permutations and reporting $3$ sign-concordant checks.  That neighboring finite result supports the decision to treat m6A burden as a repeatable RNA-stability coordinate rather than as a single-table artifact.  It does not enlarge the present claim into a mechanism.  A separate miRNA target-repression contact in the same collection has $55170$ rows, median aggregate per-miRNA partial Spearman value $-0.224966$, and $7$ replicated gradients; this is useful only as a sanity comparison showing that transcript-layer readouts can carry finite signed gradients under declared controls.  It is not evidence for the m6A claim itself and is not imported as a causal explanation.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The permitted conclusion is a bounded cross-layer relation between an RNA modification coordinate and an RNA half-life readout.  The finite row supplies a measured mRNA-stability boundary, and the positive decision is driven by the retained $5837$-gene panel, the stated coverage rule, the static association $-0.388929$, the perturbation association $0.245804$, the length-partial association $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial-permutation value $0.000999001$.  The relation is still local to the data design: it is a claim about the observed joined rows and their declared controls, not about all m6A sites, all cell types, all organisms, or all RNA-decay regimes.

\paragraph{Boundary against over-reading.}
No translation realization follows from this RNA half-life row.  No ribosome occupancy, protein output, folding state, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal law is licensed by the half-life statistic.  A translation readout must be present on the same rows before any translation-conditioned statement is considered, and even then the statement would remain separated from protein abundance and downstream protein state.  A decay-factor RBP mechanism would require same-window measured RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and a finite statistic showing that the half-life effect follows that RBP route.  Because those RBP occupancy and perturbation rows are not present here, the mechanism reading is refused.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The chapter stops being valid if the finite statistic is promoted beyond its readout surface.  In particular, the result breaks if m6A site load is treated as a direct observation of translation, if RNA half-life is treated as protein output, if the GSE163500 association is used as proof of an RBP-decay mechanism without measured occupancy and rescue controls, or if the finite sign and permutation values are described as a general law of coding sequence biology.  The correct status is narrower: a finite RNA-modification-to-RNA-stability boundary is crossed in the retained joined panel, while translation, RBP mechanism, protein state, function, phenotype, and universality remain outside the claim.
