\subsection{A finite m6A-load boundary for mRNA half-life}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite panel of coding-RNA or transcript-indexed rows in which an m6A site-load coordinate is compared with an RNA half-life readout on matched genes.  Each retained row carries a gene identifier, an m6A load statistic, a half-life statistic in the DMSO condition, a perturbation-side half-life contrast under STM2457, transcript length information used for partialing, and the coverage flag required by the declared inclusion rule.  The coverage rule is not an interpretive afterthought: a row enters the statistic only when the smaller of the DMSO and STM2457 mean transcript coverage values lies at or above the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  After this rule is applied, the finite panel contains $5837$ joined genes.

\paragraph{Observed statistic.}
On the static DMSO surface, the m6A-load coordinate is negatively ordered with the log half-life readout.  The observed Spearman coefficient is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
The perturbation-side statistic compares m6A load with the half-life contrast induced by STM2457.  On the same finite joined table, the causal-direction rank statistic is positive,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.245804,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the length-partial rank statistic remains positive,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial\ length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.227683.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two-sided partial permutation value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{perm,2s}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered verdict for this finite statistic is therefore that the m6A-load to RNA-half-life boundary is crossed.  This wording is deliberately narrow: the observed rows support a predeclared RNA-stability boundary statistic, not a general claim about translation, protein output, function, adaptation, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the sign pattern.}
The static surface says that higher m6A load is associated with shorter half-life in the DMSO table, because the rank coefficient is negative.  The STM2457 contrast surface says that the perturbation-side half-life change is ordered positively by m6A load, even after transcript length is partialed out.  The finite-row statement is thus not merely that modified transcripts differ from unmodified transcripts.  It is that, inside the coverage-filtered joined table, the m6A-load coordinate orders both a baseline half-life surface and a perturbation contrast surface in directions consistent with an RNA-stability boundary.

\paragraph{Same-row boundary conditions.}
The chapter does not promote half-life association into translation realization.  A same-row translation readout is part of the intended boundary discipline: without a matched ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency measurement, an RNA half-life contrast remains an RNA-layer observation.  Likewise, mRNA abundance and transcription-rate controls are needed before a finite row can separate decay from altered production or normalization artifacts.  The finite result reported here is therefore a bounded cross-layer contact between an RNA modification coordinate and an RNA half-life readout, with the translation surface held as a boundary condition rather than as a conclusion.

\paragraph{RBP mechanism refusal.}
The data summarized by the verified statistic do not include measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein on the same windows, coordinate--occupancy decoupling rows, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target controls.  Consequently, the chapter does not assert that a decay-factor RBP mediates the observed half-life statistic.  A positive RBP-mediated claim would require a finite table in which each row carries the coordinate label, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation state, rescue state, binding-defective or non-target control state, local RNA structure, motif density, transcription or abundance control, isoform state, batch, and a declared failure condition.  The present evidence reaches the m6A-load/RNA-half-life boundary and stops there.

\paragraph{Local controls and possible break conditions.}
The finite statistic would be downgraded if high- and low-load rows became exchangeable after the declared controls, or if the effect were reproduced by transcription rate, isoform choice, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, or batch.  It would also be downgraded if a translation readout were absent where downstream interpretation depends on it.  Most importantly, even a robust RNA half-life contrast does not by itself identify protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.  Each of those layers would need its own matched readout and its own finite statistic.

\paragraph{Comparison with adjacent finite records.}
The same audit bundle contains an independent m6A-load and half-life record over human m6Aconquer-derived rows, with $15845$ genes, $135300$ sites, $1000$ permutations, and sign concordance in $3$ evaluated settings.  That comparison is useful because it shows that the m6A/half-life boundary is not being introduced as a one-row anecdote, but it still does not change the claim type: the comparison remains a finite RNA-stability readout and does not add RBP perturbation or rescue evidence.  A separate endogenous ARE half-life transfer record gives another RNA-stability benchmark: $14933$ genes, $2000$ permutations, a baseline $R^2$ of $0.0657771$, a test $R^2$ of $0.0827561$, a negative ARE coefficient $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, an improvement $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$.  These adjacent records support the practical choice to treat RNA half-life as a finite measurable layer, while preserving the rule that each molecular mechanism requires its own contact.

\paragraph{Chapter-level conclusion.}
The finite GSE163500-derived panel licenses a specific statement: among $5837$ coverage-qualified joined genes, m6A load is associated with baseline mRNA half-life and with the STM2457 half-life contrast, including a length-partial signal with permutation support.  The numerical certificate is the triple
$$
\begin{aligned}
(n,\rho_{\mathrm{partial\ length}},p_{\mathrm{perm,2s}}) &= (5837,0.227683,0.000999001),
\end{aligned}
$$
together with the static and perturbation rank coefficients $-0.388929$ and $0.245804$.  The result is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because the row count, coverage rule, signed statistics, and permutation value are all finite and explicitly declared.  The endpoint is an RNA-modification to RNA-half-life boundary, not a claim of translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.
