\subsection{A finite m6A-load boundary for mRNA half-life}
\label{subsec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record treats synonymously comparable coding-RNA rows as a finite panel whose declared observable surface is RNA stability, not protein realization.  A row is admissible only when the coordinate label, local m6A-site load, RNA half-life readout, transcript-length information, coverage status, and matched gene identifier are present in the same joined table.  The active finite audit is the GSE163500 m6A/half-life join with $5837$ retained genes after applying the declared coverage condition: the smaller of the DMSO and STM2457 mean T coverages must be at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  Thus the row set is not a symbolic codon-space assertion; it is a concrete finite table with a stated inclusion rule and a named perturbation contrast.

\paragraph{Statistic recorded by the table.}
Let $L_i$ denote the m6A-load statistic for retained gene $i$, let $H_i^{\mathrm{DMSO}}$ denote the DMSO log-half-life readout, and let $\Delta H_i$ denote the STM2457-versus-DMSO log-half-life contrast.  The finite report records three scalar checks:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 5837,\\
 \rho(L,H^{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho(L,\Delta H) &= 0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
It also records a length-controlled partial Spearman value
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(L,\Delta H\mid \mathrm{length}) &= 0.227683,
\end{aligned}
$$
and a two-sided partial-permutation value
$$
\begin{aligned}
 p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The local meaning is therefore narrow but real: in this finite joined dataset, m6A load is associated with the measured half-life surface, and the perturbation contrast remains nonzero after the declared length control.  The audit label is \emph{crosses boundary}: the external table supports the predeclared RNA-half-life statistic.  It does not by itself supply an RNA-binding-protein mechanism, a translation claim, or a protein-layer conclusion.

\paragraph{Direction of the readout.}
The static DMSO association is negative, while the perturbation contrast is positive.  This sign pattern is not interpreted as a universal biological law.  It says that, inside the retained finite rows, higher m6A load is paired with shorter DMSO half-life in the baseline readout, whereas the STM2457 contrast shifts in the positive direction with m6A load.  The boundary claim is exactly the compatibility of those finite statistics with an RNA-stability readout.  It is not a claim that a codon coordinate has become a translation output, a folded protein state, a physical admissibility certificate, a function, a phenotype, or an adaptation.

\paragraph{Control surface and refusal condition.}
The table licenses only a bounded cross-layer relation from RNA modification load to RNA half-life.  A stronger decay-factor RBP bridge would require same-row measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target controls, transcription-rate control, mRNA-abundance control, local RNA-structure control, motif-density control, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch handling, and a translation readout attached before downstream interpretation.  If high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable for half-life after these controls, the coordinate remains only a descriptor.  If half-life changes without the measured RBP-occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern, the observation remains an RNA-stability association rather than a mechanism-level bridge.

\paragraph{Comparison row.}
A separate human m6A-load and half-life audit supplies a consistency check for the same biological surface without being merged into the GSE163500 statistic.  That comparison row contains $135300$ m6A sites over $15845$ genes, uses $1000$ permutations, and records $3$ sign-concordant components.  Its role here is limited: it shows that the m6A-load/half-life surface is not represented by a single-row anecdote, while the GSE163500 boundary remains the chapter's operative finite statistic.  The comparison row does not add RBP occupancy, rescue, translation efficiency, protein abundance, structure, function, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite evidence is sufficient for a standalone BioReality statement at RNA-stability scope.  The chapter may say that the joined GSE163500 rows support a bounded relation between m6A-site load and mRNA half-life under the stated coverage rule, with $n=5837$, $\rho(L,H^{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929$, $\rho(L,\Delta H)=0.245804$, $\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}=0.227683$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.000999001$.  The chapter may not say that translation has been realized, that an RBP decay mechanism has been established, that protein output or folding follows, that physical admissibility or biological function follows, or that a universal rule has been obtained.  Those claims require their own layer-matched rows and their own finite statistics.
