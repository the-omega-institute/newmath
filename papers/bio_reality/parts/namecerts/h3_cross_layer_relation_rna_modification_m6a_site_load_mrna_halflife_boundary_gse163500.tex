\subsection{m6A site load and the RNA half-life boundary in GSE163500}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined gene panel in which an m6A site-load coordinate is read beside an RNA half-life quantity.  The positive object is not an RNA-decay mechanism and not a translation claim.  It is the narrower assertion that, after a coverage rule and a declared length control, the same finite rows show a stable relation between m6A load and half-life.  The evaluated panel contains $5837$ joined genes.  The coverage rule retained genes for which
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min\{\text{Mean T coverage in DMSO},\text{Mean T coverage in STM2457}\}
\end{aligned}
$$
was at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  On these rows the static DMSO association between m6A load and log half-life is negative, with Spearman coefficient $-0.388929$.  The perturbation-oriented statistic has the opposite sign: the Spearman coefficient between m6A-load change and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life is $0.245804$.  After controlling for length, the corresponding partial Spearman statistic is $0.227683$, with two-sided permutation value $0.000999001$.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
The finite claim is therefore a signed readout statement over the retained rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 5837,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\Delta \mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.245804,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{partial,length}}(\Delta \mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.227683,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module classification is consequently a crossed RNA-readout boundary: the statistic crosses the predeclared m6A-load/RNA-half-life boundary, but it does not by itself identify a decay-factor RBP bridge.  The word ``crosses'' here means only that the finite statistic survives the declared numerical test on the joined empirical rows.  It does not promote the result to protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general biological law.

\paragraph{Same-row requirements.}
The admissible carrier is a matched coding-RNA window or gene-row panel with synonymous coordinate labels, RNA half-life, translation readout when a translation-conditioned interpretation is attempted, mRNA abundance or transcription controls, local RNA-structure and sequence-composition covariates, isoform state, batch, and perturbation state.  The GSE163500 statistic supplies the RNA half-life side of that carrier and a perturbation-associated m6A-load contrast.  It does not supply, in the same finite rows, measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target control behavior.  For that reason the accepted interpretation is deliberately smaller than an RBP-decay mechanism.  A mechanism-level bridge would require the half-life effect to track measured local RBP occupancy, collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail under binding-defective or non-target controls while remaining separated from transcription rate, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.

\paragraph{Comparison with a broader m6A load screen.}
A companion finite m6A-load screen in the same BioReality bundle uses $135300$ sites over $15845$ genes and records $3$ sign-concordant summaries under $1000$ permutations.  That broader row count supports the choice to treat m6A load as a measurable RNA-layer coordinate, but it does not add the missing RBP perturbation and rescue rows to the GSE163500 panel.  Thus the present claim remains a bounded RNA half-life result even though it is numerically strong.  The supporting scalar evidence is the retained-gene count, the signed correlations, and the permutation value; no unobserved RBP occupancy, ribosome-occupancy, protein-abundance, structural, physical, functional, phenotypic, adaptive, causal, or universal conclusion is licensed from those scalars.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The boundary breaks if high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for half-life after transcription, local-structure, motif-density, nucleotide-composition, codon-context, isoform, abundance, and batch controls.  It also breaks if a half-life difference appears without a matching RBP-occupancy change when an RBP mechanism is claimed, if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect, or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, or batch can reproduce the pattern.  Under the available finite rows none of those stronger bridge conditions is met, so the ready verdict is confined to the RNA half-life boundary statistic and not to an RNA-decay mechanism.
