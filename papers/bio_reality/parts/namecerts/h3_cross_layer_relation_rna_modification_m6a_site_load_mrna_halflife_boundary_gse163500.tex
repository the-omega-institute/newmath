\subsection{m6A Site Load and the RNA Half-Life Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite joined table in which coding or transcript rows carry an m6A site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout.  The biological object is not a protein, a folded state, or a phenotype.  It is a bounded RNA-layer panel: each retained row has an m6A-load statistic, a DMSO half-life readout, an STM2457 perturbation half-life readout where available, transcript-length information for residualization, and coverage sufficient to avoid treating weakly observed rows as decisive.  The finite coverage rule used by the run was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage}_{\mathrm{DMSO}},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage}_{\mathrm{STM2457}})
  \geq \text{20th percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
After this rule was applied, the retained panel contained $5837$ genes.  The declared statistic is therefore a same-table RNA-stability boundary statistic, not an unconstrained biological law.

\paragraph{Observed boundary statistic.}
The run identifier for this row set is $\mathrm{7cd189f15c4b}$.  In the static DMSO comparison, m6A site load was negatively associated with log half-life, with Spearman value
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) = -0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
The perturbation-oriented statistic compared m6A load with the change in log half-life and gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL}) = 0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
After controlling transcript length in the finite residual calculation, the partial Spearman value remained positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL}\mid \mathrm{length}) = 0.227683.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two-sided partial-permutation value was $0.000999001$.  On the predeclared boundary criterion, the verdict for the row set is therefore $\mathrm{crosses\_boundary}$: the external m6A and half-life data support a bounded RNA half-life association under the stated coverage and residualization rules.

\paragraph{Interpretation allowed by the finite evidence.}
The positive result licenses only the following statement.  In this finite joined panel, m6A site load is not exchangeable with the RNA half-life perturbation readout after the specified filtering and length control.  The sign pattern also separates the static DMSO half-life association from the perturbation-oriented half-life change: the static association is negative, while the perturbation statistic is positive.  This is enough to mark a cross-layer boundary between an RNA-modification coordinate and an RNA-stability readout, because the coordinate and readout live in distinct measured columns and the association survives the stated finite test.

\paragraph{Local comparison rows.}
Two neighboring finite assays in the same BioReality ledger show that this record is part of a larger bounded-readout regime rather than a one-off unrestricted promotion.  The transcript-surveillance position-rule assay retained $38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations, with runtime $55.602$ seconds.  The ARE-deletion RNA-stability assay used $1000$ actual permutations, had a grading target of $2000$ permutations, retained $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, and reported sign concordance under a pooled label permutation over deletions.  These comparison rows do not prove the m6A result; they only show that the present row belongs to a family of finite RNA-readout boundary tests where row count, permutation count, and the declared null construction are explicit parts of the evidence.

\paragraph{Boundary of refusal.}
No translation claim follows from this row set.  The half-life statistic does not measure ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  A protein-layer or translation-layer conclusion would require a separate row-matched contact carrying the relevant measurement.  Likewise, the present data do not by themselves establish a decay-factor RBP mechanism.  A mechanism-level RBP bridge would require same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density controls, transcription-rate controls, isoform-state controls, transcript-abundance controls, batch controls, and a declared finite statistic tying those columns to the half-life response.  If any of those columns are absent, or if the pattern is reproduced by transcription, RNA structure, motif density, isoform composition, abundance, batch, or non-target controls, the correct conclusion remains a bounded RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The chapter is ready as a BioReality record because the finite evidence contains a declared carrier, a row-retention rule, a row count, signed rank statistics, a residual statistic, a permutation value, and an explicit non-promotion boundary.  The result is not a mechanism theorem and not a universal biology claim.  It is a finite, externally read RNA-layer boundary: m6A site load crosses into the RNA half-life readout under the predeclared GSE163500-derived statistic, with $n=5837$, $\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}=0.245804$, $\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}=0.227683$, and $p=0.000999001$.
