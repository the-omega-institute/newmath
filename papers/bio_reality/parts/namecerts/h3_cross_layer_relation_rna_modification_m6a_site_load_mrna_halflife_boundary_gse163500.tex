\subsection{m6A site load as an RNA half-life boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite joined panel in which coding or transcript-level RNA rows carry an m6A site-load coordinate and an mRNA half-life readout in the same organismal setting. The evaluated GSE163500-derived statistic keeps the claim at the RNA-stability layer: each retained row contributes an m6A-load value, a DMSO half-life value, a perturbation-associated half-life contrast, transcript-length information, and the coverage gate used to decide whether the row is admissible. The retained panel has $n=5837$ genes after the declared coverage filter
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\operatorname{MeanT}_{\mathrm{DMSO}},\operatorname{MeanT}_{\mathrm{STM2457}}) \geq \text{20th percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This finite filter matters because the chapter does not license a sequence-wide law about m6A. It records a bounded RNA half-life contact: rows that pass the coverage rule can be used to ask whether the m6A-load coordinate crosses an RNA-stability readout boundary under the declared statistic.

\paragraph{Observed boundary statistic.}
The static DMSO association between m6A load and log half-life is negative, with Spearman correlation $-0.388929$. The perturbation-oriented statistic relates m6A load to the half-life contrast, giving Spearman correlation $0.245804$ for m6A load versus $\Delta \log_2$ half-life. After controlling transcript length, the corresponding partial Spearman value is $0.227683$. The reported two-sided partial-permutation value is $0.000999001$, so the finite-row statistic is not a null readback under the pre-registered permutation test. In compact form, the row-level conclusion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 5837,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 H_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 H) &= 0.245804,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{partial\ length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 H) &= 0.227683,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,two-sided}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module classification for this record is therefore an RNA half-life boundary crossing, not an unrestricted mechanism claim. The sign pattern is also layer-specific: the static DMSO association is negative, while the perturbation-associated half-life contrast is positive. That is enough to mark a finite relation between m6A site load and RNA stability, but it is not enough to infer translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Same-row interpretation.}
The admissible carrier for the chapter is a matched RNA panel whose rows preserve the identity of the measured transcript or gene through the m6A-load coordinate and the half-life readout. The coordinate is not treated as biological evidence by itself. It becomes informative only through the finite statistic above and only at the RNA half-life layer. A stronger coding-window bridge would require additional same-row fields: a translation readout, matched mRNA abundance or transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. Those fields are named here as requirements for a later mechanism test, not as facts already supplied by the GSE163500 boundary statistic.

\paragraph{Mechanism boundary.}
The observed values support the following narrow statement: in the retained finite panel, m6A site load crosses a pre-registered mRNA half-life readout boundary after the declared coverage gate, and the length-adjusted association remains nonzero under permutation testing. They do not identify the decay-factor RBP that carries the effect, do not prove occupancy at the relevant windows, and do not show that loss of a target RBP collapses the effect or that wild-type rescue restores it. The required mechanism pattern would have to satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{coordinate contrast} \rightarrow \text{measured local RBP occupancy contrast},\\
&\text{target-RBP loss} \rightarrow \text{collapse of the half-life effect},\\
&\text{wild-type rescue} \rightarrow \text{return of the effect},\\
&\text{binding-defective or non-target control} \rightarrow \text{no restoration of the same effect}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No part of the verified GSE163500 scalar set supplies that full chain. For this reason the chapter records an RNA-stability boundary and refuses the stronger RBP-mediated decay bridge unless the finite rows are enlarged with occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and control arms.

\paragraph{Cross-panel calibration.}
A separate human m6A-load and half-life contact provides a useful scale check without changing the local claim. In that independent m6A/half-life survey, the finite payload contains $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, with $1000$ permutations and $3$ sign-concordant organism or comparison units. That auxiliary record shows that m6A-load-to-half-life signals can be organized as finite RNA-stability contacts beyond the GSE163500 panel, but it does not import its rows into this chapter and does not supply the missing RBP-occupancy or rescue evidence. Its role is only to confirm that the RNA-stability layer has recurring finite-row structure; it is not used to assert a shared mechanism.

\paragraph{Local refusal line.}
The chapter therefore permits one positive sentence and blocks several tempting extensions. The positive sentence is that the GSE163500-derived finite panel crosses an RNA half-life boundary for m6A site load, with $n=5837$, static Spearman $-0.388929$, perturbation-associated Spearman $0.245804$, length-adjusted partial Spearman $0.227683$, and two-sided partial-permutation value $0.000999001$. The blocked extensions are equally part of the result: RNA half-life is not translation realization; m6A load is not protein output; a stability association is not a folding, physical-admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality claim. Any such statement requires a separate layer-matched contact and a declared finite statistic at that layer.
