\subsection{m6A Site Load And The RNA Half-Life Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite joined panel in which coding or transcript-associated RNA rows carry a measured m6A site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout. The biological object is not a free-standing claim about translation, protein production, or cell behavior. It is a bounded RNA-layer comparison: for each retained gene row, the analysis asks whether the m6A load readback has a reproducible relation to measured RNA half-life, including the perturbation contrast between the DMSO state and the STM2457 state. The retained panel contains $5837$ joined genes after the coverage filter. The declared filter is that $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})$ must be at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes. Thus the statistic is not being read from all annotated transcripts, but from a finite matched table after an explicit coverage rule.

\paragraph{Observed statistic.}
The main same-dataset statistic crosses the RNA half-life boundary. In the DMSO state the static association between m6A load and log half-life is negative, with Spearman coefficient $-0.388929$. Under the perturbation contrast, the relation between m6A load and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life is positive, with Spearman coefficient $0.245804$. After controlling for length, the partial Spearman coefficient remains $0.227683$. The corresponding two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$. In compact form, the finite readout is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 5837,\\
 \rho(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log\mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2\mathrm{HL}) &= 0.245804,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{partial,length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2\mathrm{HL}) &= 0.227683,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The local verdict for this row family is therefore that the finite m6A-load table reaches the predeclared RNA half-life boundary. The statement is deliberately narrower than a mechanism claim: the table supports a measured association between m6A burden and RNA half-life behavior under the specified coverage and perturbation join, but it does not by itself identify the decay-factor RBP, explain binding, or assign a causal molecular route.

\paragraph{Relation to a broader m6A half-life panel.}
A second finite m6A half-life record supplies background scale without replacing the site-load statistic above. In that companion row family, $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites were retained, $1000$ permutations were run, and the sign-concordant count was $3$. Those numbers show that the m6A-to-half-life boundary is not being introduced as an isolated symbolic label; it belongs to a finite-data family in which site counts, gene counts, and permutation depth are declared. However, the cross-record comparison is only a scope check. The $5837$-gene GSE163500 statistic remains the local object of this subsection, and the larger $15845$-gene, $135300$-site panel is not imported as proof of the same perturbation response.

\paragraph{What is licensed.}
The licensed conclusion is a bounded cross-layer RNA statement: in the finite retained rows, m6A load is associated with RNA half-life readouts in the declared direction and at the declared permutation level. The row object may be used as evidence that an RNA modification coordinate can survive contact with an RNA stability measurement, provided the statement stays at the level of the finite table, its coverage rule, its perturbation contrast, and its scalar statistics. The permissible readback is therefore a tuple containing gene or transcript identity, m6A load, coverage state, DMSO half-life, STM2457 contrast, length control, and the reported permutation statistic.

\paragraph{What is not licensed.}
No translation claim follows from this table. The presence of an RNA half-life statistic does not imply ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, cellular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation in the broad biological sense, or a universal sequence law. In particular, the result cannot be promoted to a decay-factor RBP bridge unless additional same-row data are present: measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, mRNA abundance controls, isoform-state controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, batch diagnostics, and a matched translation readout used only as a boundary condition. If RNA half-life differs while RBP occupancy is unmeasured, if target-RBP loss and rescue are absent, or if structure, motif density, transcription, isoform state, abundance, or batch can reproduce the pattern, the record remains an RNA-stability association rather than a mechanism bridge.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The finite evidence is strong enough for the standalone BioReality entry to report a positive RNA half-life boundary crossing for the GSE163500 m6A site-load statistic. Its exact status is narrower than mechanism and narrower than function: $5837$ joined genes pass the coverage rule, the static DMSO association is $-0.388929$, the perturbation contrast is $0.245804$, the length-partial statistic is $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$. These numbers justify retaining the entry as a finite RNA-layer result while keeping all translation, protein, structure, physical-admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, and universality claims outside the chapter unless separately measured contacts are supplied.
