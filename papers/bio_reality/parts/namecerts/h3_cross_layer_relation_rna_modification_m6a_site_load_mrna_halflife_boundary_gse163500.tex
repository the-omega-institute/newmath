\subsection{A finite m6A-load half-life boundary for coding-RNA rows}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-half-life-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined panel in which each retained gene row carries an m6A load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout under the same external experiment family. The positive statement is deliberately narrow: it is a boundary statement about whether measured m6A load is separated from RNA half-life after the declared finite controls are applied. The audited GSE163500-derived panel retains $5837$ joined genes after the coverage rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min\{\operatorname{coverage}_{\mathrm{DMSO}},\operatorname{coverage}_{\mathrm{STM2457}}\}
&\geq \text{the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row object therefore has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i = (&\text{gene id}_i,\; \text{m6A load}_i,\; \Delta\log_2 \operatorname{HL}_i,\; \log_2 \operatorname{HL}_{i,\mathrm{DMSO}},\\
&\text{length}_i,\; \text{coverage diagnostics}_i,\; \text{treatment state}_i,\; \text{declared inclusion status}_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
with no promotion of the row into a protein, structure, phenotype, or mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistics.}
The static half-life relation in the retained panel is negative: the Spearman coefficient between m6A load and DMSO log half-life is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\text{m6A load},\log_2 \operatorname{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
The perturbation-oriented statistic reads the m6A load against the treatment half-life displacement and is positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\text{m6A load},\Delta\log_2 \operatorname{HL})=0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
After partialing the length coordinate, the corresponding rank statistic remains
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(\text{m6A load},\Delta\log_2 \operatorname{HL}\mid \text{length})=0.227683,
\end{aligned}
$$
with a two-sided partial-permutation value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the finite audit semantics, this is a crossing of the predeclared RNA half-life boundary: the measured m6A-load coordinate is not exchangeable with respect to the half-life displacement in the retained rows under the stated finite statistic.

\paragraph{Boundary interpretation.}
The result licenses only the following bounded reading. In the retained gene panel, the m6A-load coordinate carries a reproducible RNA half-life association, including a perturbation-facing half-life displacement statistic that survives the length partial. This is not a statement that m6A load realizes translation, changes protein abundance, determines folded structure, supplies physical admissibility, realizes function, creates a system phenotype, proves a decay mechanism, or states a universal law. Those layers require separate row contacts at the relevant level. In particular, an RNA half-life row is not an RBP-occupancy row, and a half-life displacement is not by itself a decay-factor mechanism.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The conjectural cross-layer route mentions same-row translation readout as a boundary condition, not as a consequence of the GSE163500 statistic. The present finite panel supports an RNA half-life relation, while the translation layer remains unpromoted unless the same rows also carry a matched translation-efficiency or ribosome-occupancy readout with matched mRNA abundance controls. Thus the allowed implication is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{m6A load row} + \text{RNA half-life row}
&\Longrightarrow \text{bounded RNA half-life boundary},\\
\text{m6A load row} + \text{RNA half-life row}
&
Rightarrow \text{translation realization}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The second line is part of the scientific content of the record: the row does not carry enough coordinates to cross into translation or protein-output interpretation.

\paragraph{Mechanism refusal.}
The stronger decay-factor RBP bridge is not accepted from these rows alone. To claim that the half-life boundary is mediated through a prespecified decay-factor RBP, the same finite panel would need measured local RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or a matched non-target control, plus transcription-rate, local-structure, motif-density, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, isoform, abundance, and batch controls. The current finite statistic does not contain those perturbation and rescue coordinates. Consequently the chapter records a positive RNA half-life boundary and a local refusal of the RBP-mechanism bridge.

\paragraph{Companion finite check.}
A second audited m6A half-life contact supplies a useful scope check. In the human m6Aconquer cross-organism-style aggregation, the finite table contains $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, with $3$ sign-concordant entries in the declared comparison and $1000$ permutations. That companion result is consistent with treating m6A load as a repeatable RNA half-life boundary surface, but it still does not add measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, translation readout, protein output, structure, function, or mechanism to the GSE163500 rows. It therefore reinforces the same bounded classification rather than enlarging the claim.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite GSE163500 row set crosses the m6A-load RNA half-life boundary with $n=5837$, static $\rho=-0.388929$, perturbation-facing $\rho=0.245804$, length-partial $\rho=0.227683$, and two-sided partial-permutation $p=0.000999001$. The chapter accepts that bounded RNA half-life readout and rejects every stronger interpretation unless an additional layer-matched contact supplies the missing finite rows. No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, decay-factor RBP mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality follows from this statistic alone.
