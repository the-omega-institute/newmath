\subsection{m6A site load and the RNA half-life boundary in GSE163500}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record is a finite joined gene panel for an RNA-modification boundary test. Each retained row carries an m6A site-load coordinate, a DMSO half-life readout, an STM2457-associated half-life contrast, transcript-length information, and the coverage guard used before the statistic is computed. The row filter is explicit: the minimum of mean transcript coverage in DMSO and STM2457 must be at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes. After this rule, the tested panel contains $5837$ genes. The module is classified as $\mathrm{cross\_layer\_relation}$ because the coordinate is not being read as a protein, structure, phenotype, or mechanism; it is being compared with a same-gene RNA half-life surface under a declared finite statistic.

\paragraph{Observed statistic.}
The primary same-study boundary readout is non-null. In DMSO, the static association between m6A load and log half-life is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the perturbation contrast, the causal-direction summary reported for m6A load against the change in log half-life is positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
After length partialing, the association remains positive, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial,length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.227683,
\qquad
p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}}=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The declared verdict for this finite computation is $\mathrm{crosses\_boundary}$: the external m6A and half-life rows support the pre-registered RNA half-life boundary statistic. This is a statement about a measured transcript-layer readout, not an interpretation of translation output or downstream biological function.

\paragraph{Cross-record consistency.}
A separate RNA-modification boundary record gives a compatible but weaker background constraint. The human m6AConquer cross-organism half-life panel reports $15845$ genes, $135300$ m6A sites, $1000$ permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant components. That auxiliary record does not replace the GSE163500 joined statistic, because it does not supply the same perturbation contrast and coverage rule. Its role here is narrower: it shows that the m6A-load to RNA-half-life direction is not isolated to a single bookkeeping convention, while leaving the GSE163500 finite rows as the local object that carries the boundary verdict.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The licensed statement is therefore modest. A coding-RNA coordinate decorated by m6A site load can be connected to an RNA half-life readout in this finite GSE163500 panel, with the length-controlled statistic still separated from zero by the reported permutation test. The chapter does not infer that m6A load determines translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, cellular function, adaptation, causation, or a general biological law. Those claims would require their own row-matched contacts and statistics at the corresponding layer.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
The stronger decay-factor RBP bridge is not established by these rows. The present contact supplies RNA half-life and m6A-load information, but it does not by itself include same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target controls. Consequently, the half-life boundary may be reported, while any mechanistic RBP-decay explanation remains blocked until the same finite rows contain occupancy, perturbation, rescue, local-structure, motif-density, transcription-rate, isoform, abundance, batch, and translation-readout controls sufficient to test that stronger bridge directly.
