\subsection{m6A site load and the finite RNA half-life boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite joined panel of human gene rows in which an m6A-site-load coordinate is compared with an RNA half-life readout under a matched perturbation contrast.  The admissible row is not a free sequence interpretation: it is a tuple carrying an m6A load measurement, an RNA half-life value, a joined coverage filter, transcript-length information, and the perturbation label separating DMSO from STM2457.  After applying the declared coverage rule,
$\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ STM2457})$ at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes, the retained panel contains $5837$ genes.  The row count is part of the claim: the chapter does not assert a genome-wide law outside this finite joined matrix.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $q_i$ denote the m6A-site-load coordinate for retained row $i$, let $h_i^{\mathrm{DMSO}}$ be the DMSO half-life readout, and let $\Delta h_i$ be the perturbation-associated log-two half-life change.  The reported finite statistics are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 5837,\\
 \rho(q,h^{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho(q,\Delta h)_{\mathrm{causal}} &= 0.245804,\\
 \rho(q,\Delta h\mid \mathrm{length}) &= 0.227683,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The negative static association says that, in the DMSO half-life surface, larger m6A load is paired with shorter RNA half-life in the retained finite panel.  The positive perturbation statistic says that the STM2457 contrast changes the half-life surface in a direction that remains visible after transcript-length partialing.  The two-sided permutation value, $0.000999001$, is the finite-row support for crossing the RNA-half-life boundary; it is not a certificate for translation, protein output, function, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The chapter licenses only the following bounded statement.  In the retained GSE163500-derived matrix, m6A-site load is non-exchangeable with respect to the measured mRNA half-life surface under the declared coverage rule and length-controlled statistic.  The same record explicitly classifies the status as a boundary result: a passed result means that the m6A/half-life dataset supports the pre-registered statistic, not that it supplies a biochemical mechanism or a functional biological conclusion.  Thus the usable claim is a finite cross-layer relation from an RNA-modification coordinate to an RNA-stability readout.

\paragraph{Refusals.}
No translation conclusion follows from the RNA half-life statistic.  Ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, and universality remain outside the licensed statement unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies those measurements on the same finite rows.  Likewise, this record does not by itself establish a decay-factor RBP mechanism.  Measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target controls are not present in the cited finite statistics.  If those rows are absent, the result remains an RNA-stability boundary rather than an RBP-bridge result.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The claim fails upward promotion if the statistic is read as translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, or a general biological law.  It also fails as a mechanism claim if an alternative explanation through transcription rate, transcript abundance, isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, nucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, or missing RBP perturbation controls can reproduce the observed pattern.  The finite positive content is therefore narrow: m6A load crosses into a measured RNA half-life surface in the declared GSE163500 joined panel, with $n=5837$, $\rho(q,\Delta h\mid \mathrm{length})=0.227683$, and $p=0.000999001$.
