\subsection{m6A site load and the RNA half-life boundary in GSE163500}\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-gse163500}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined panel in which coding or transcript-associated rows carry an m6A site-load coordinate, an RNA half-life readout, a coverage rule, and the covariates needed to keep the statement at the RNA-stability layer.  The realized GSE163500 join retained $5837$ genes after the declared coverage filter, namely $\min(\text{Mean T coverage DMSO},\text{Mean T coverage STM2457})$ at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  The row is therefore not a generic m6A claim: it is a bounded finite statistic over joined genes with half-life measurements under DMSO and STM2457, length control, and a declared permutation test.

\paragraph{Observed boundary statistic.}
The primary finite-row fact is the signed relation between m6A load and RNA half-life.  In the DMSO condition, the static Spearman association between m6A load and $\log_2$ half-life is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})&=-0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the perturbation contrast, the observed association between m6A load and the half-life change is positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})&=0.245804,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{partial,length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})&=0.227683.
\end{aligned}
$$
The length-controlled permutation calculation reports a two-sided value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{perm}}&=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
With these numbers, the finite panel crosses the predeclared RNA half-life boundary: m6A site load remains associated with the half-life change after the stated length adjustment and permutation audit.  The associated run id is $7\mathrm{cd}189\mathrm{f}15\mathrm{c}4\mathrm{b}$, and the row semantics explicitly restrict the result to a boundary statistic rather than a mechanism or function claim.

\paragraph{Same-layer comparison records.}
The interpretation is strengthened only in the narrow sense that other RNA-stability boundary records in the same finite audit family also stay within transcript-layer readouts.  A human cross-organism m6A load and half-life record used $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, with $1000$ permutations and $3$ sign-concordant comparisons in run $\mathrm{d}7949\mathrm{df}4\mathrm{a}751$.  An ARE deletion RNA-stability record used $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, reported sign concordance, and used $2000$ grading permutations in run $\mathrm{a}41440\mathrm{ab}2792$.  An endogenous $3'$ UTR half-life transfer record used $14933$ genes, with baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$ over $2000$ permutations in run $\mathrm{e}96\mathrm{bf}26\mathrm{e}626\mathrm{b}$.  These neighboring records do not certify the present m6A mechanism; they show that the paper treats RNA-stability evidence as finite transcript-layer statistics rather than as automatic promotion to protein, phenotype, or universal law.

\paragraph{Local licensing rule.}
The GSE163500 result licenses only the following local sentence: within the finite joined panel, m6A site load is a bounded coordinate whose association with RNA half-life change is non-null under the stated coverage, length-control, and permutation procedure.  The statistic may be written as a finite-row readback
$$
\begin{aligned}
(g_i, L_i, H_i^{\mathrm{DMSO}}, H_i^{\mathrm{STM2457}}, C_i)&\longmapsto (L_i,\Delta\log_2 H_i,C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g_i$ is the joined gene row, $L_i$ is the m6A load, $H_i$ is the half-life readout, and $C_i$ denotes the declared coverage and length-control information.  The accepted statement is not that m6A load determines decay in general, but that this finite readout survives the named boundary test in this dataset.

\paragraph{Refusal of overreach.}
No translation conclusion follows from the RNA half-life statistic alone.  The row does not by itself measure ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.  The same-row translation readout named in the broader design is a boundary requirement for any later cross-layer interpretation; it is not supplied by this half-life statistic.  Likewise, the present finite rows do not contain measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target controls.  Therefore an RBP-mediated decay mechanism remains unlicensed here, even though the m6A load and half-life boundary itself is passed.

\paragraph{Break conditions.}
The chapter refuses promotion if the observed half-life association is treated as translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical feasibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or a global sequence law.  It also refuses promotion if a reader replaces the missing RBP occupancy and perturbation rows by the mere presence of an m6A coordinate.  A positive mechanism bridge would require a finite matched panel in which the same rows carry the m6A or synonymous coordinate, RNA half-life, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate and abundance controls, isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and an explicit same-row translation-readout boundary.  Until those rows exist and pass their own statistic, the present result remains a transcript-layer half-life boundary with no claim above that layer.
