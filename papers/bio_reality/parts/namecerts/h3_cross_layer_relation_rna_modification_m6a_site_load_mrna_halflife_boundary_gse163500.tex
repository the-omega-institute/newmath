\subsection{m6A Site Load And The RNA Half-Life Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite joined panel in which transcript-level m6A site load is read against an RNA half-life measurement under a perturbation contrast.  The biological object is not a free sequence law and not a claim about protein production.  It is a finite collection of gene rows whose fields include an m6A load coordinate, a DMSO half-life readout, an STM2457 contrast, transcript length, coverage eligibility, and a declared scalar test for whether m6A load remains associated with the RNA half-life shift after the named controls are imposed.  The operative coverage rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})
  \geq \text{20th percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
After that rule is applied, the finite row count is $5837$ genes.  This is the population on which the m6A-site-load boundary statistic is interpreted.

\paragraph{Observed scalar surface.}
The static DMSO association between m6A load and log RNA half-life is negative, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
The perturbation-facing association between m6A load and the half-life change is positive, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
After partialing transcript length, the corresponding partial Spearman statistic remains positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial,length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta \log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.227683.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two-sided partial permutation value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}}=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The declared finite verdict for this table is therefore \emph{crosses\_boundary}: the data support the predeclared RNA half-life boundary statistic for m6A load in the joined rows.

\paragraph{What the boundary licenses.}
The result licenses only a bounded cross-layer relation from the m6A-load coordinate to an RNA half-life response surface in this finite dataset.  The sign pattern is biologically coherent at the level of RNA stability: higher m6A load is associated with shorter baseline half-life in the DMSO surface, while the perturbation contrast carries a positive association between site load and the change in half-life.  The length-adjusted statistic remains in the same direction, and the permutation value is below $0.001$ at the stated resolution.  Thus the finite rows contain enough contact with measured RNA half-life to treat m6A site load as more than a syntactic coordinate for this RNA-stability question.

\paragraph{Local refusal of overreach.}
No translation conclusion follows from the RNA half-life statistic alone.  The same finite table does not by itself measure ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, causation, or a universal biological law.  A translation claim would require a same-row translation readout and matched mRNA controls.  An RBP-mediated decay mechanism would require measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  A protein or phenotype claim would require a separate layer-matched readout at that layer.  The present chapter therefore reports an RNA half-life boundary, not a promotion into protein output or mechanism.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
Two neighboring finite surfaces help calibrate the scope without changing the local claim.  A human m6A cross-organism-style load and half-life check contains $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, with $1000$ permutations and $3$ sign-concordant components.  Separately, an endogenous 3-prime UTR ARE half-life transfer check contains $14933$ genes, uses $2000$ permutations, and reports $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$.  These companion row systems show that RNA-stability contacts can be finite, signed, and permutation-graded across different RNA features.  They do not supply the missing RBP occupancy, rescue, translation, protein, structure, physical, function, phenotype, or universality contacts for the m6A site-load panel.

\paragraph{Declared interpretation.}
The chapter-level reading is narrow.  The $5837$-gene table supports the statement that m6A site load crosses an RNA half-life boundary under the named coverage rule and scalar tests.  The evidence is finite, dataset-bound, and readout-specific.  It remains separated from transcriptional abundance, translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, and general sequence law unless those layers are supplied by additional matched finite rows and their own declared statistics.
