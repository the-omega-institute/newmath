\subsection{m6A site load and mRNA half-life as a finite RNA-stability boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite gene-level panel in which an m6A site-load coordinate is joined to an mRNA half-life readout under a coverage rule and then tested as an RNA-stability boundary. The panel keeps the coordinate, the decay readout, and the control variables in the same finite row space before any biological interpretation is made. The usable join contains $5837$ genes after applying the rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ under\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ under\ STM2457})
  \geq \text{20th percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The declared statistic is not a statement about protein output or cellular function. It is a bounded RNA-layer comparison asking whether the m6A-load coordinate remains associated with half-life after the finite controls available in the joined table are imposed.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}
In the DMSO state, the static association between m6A load and log half-life is negative, with Spearman value
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the perturbation-oriented readout, the joined rows give a positive association between m6A load and the half-life change, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
After length partialing, the corresponding finite statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}\mid \mathrm{length})=0.227683,
\end{aligned}
$$
with two-sided permutation value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered verdict for this finite table is therefore that the RNA-stability boundary is crossed: the external m6A and half-life rows support the predeclared boundary statistic. That verdict is deliberately narrower than an m6A mechanism claim. It says that the coordinate survives to an RNA half-life readout in this finite setting; it does not identify the molecular executor of decay.

\paragraph{Position among nearby RNA-layer records.}
The same evidence ledger contains two useful scale checks. A human m6A load and half-life record over $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites reports $1000$ permutations and $3$ sign-concordant organism-level outcomes. A separate miRNA target-repression record over $55170$ rows reports median aggregate per-miRNA partial Spearman value $-0.224966$ and $7$ replicated gradient cases. These neighboring RNA-layer entries do not prove the present claim. They show that the present $5837$-gene result is being treated with the same finite-row discipline: a coordinate may be compared with a transcript-layer readout only through an explicitly joined table, a declared statistic, and a local refusal to promote the result beyond the measured layer.

\paragraph{Controls and declared break conditions.}
The intended full biological panel for a stronger decay-factor RBP bridge would include synonymous coding-window labels, amino-acid composition control, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, transcription-rate or mRNA abundance controls, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective control, and non-target control. The verified GSE163500 boundary statistic supplies the m6A-to-half-life RNA-layer crossing, but it does not by itself supply all of those mechanism rows. The bridge breaks if high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after the declared controls, if the half-life change appears without measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, if target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect, or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, local RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform state, abundance, or batch reproduce the same pattern.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The result is an RNA-stability boundary result, not a translation, folding, physical-admissibility, function, adaptation, causation, mechanism, phenotype, or universality result. The half-life statistic may coexist with a mandatory translation-readout boundary in the proposed finite panel, but RNA half-life alone is not translation realization and is not protein abundance. A separate ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact would be required for translation. A separate proteomics contact would be required for protein output. Separate structure, physical assay, functional assay, phenotype, perturbation, localization, or conservation contacts would be required for those layers. Thus the positive finite statistic licenses only this statement: within the covered joined rows, m6A site load crosses into an mRNA half-life readout under the declared boundary statistic, while all higher-layer and mechanism interpretations remain blocked until their own same-row contacts and controls are supplied.
