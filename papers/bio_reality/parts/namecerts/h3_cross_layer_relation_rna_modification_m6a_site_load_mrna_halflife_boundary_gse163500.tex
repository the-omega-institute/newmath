\subsection{m6A Site Load And RNA Half-Life Boundary In A Finite Coding-RNA Panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA rows in which each row carries a synonymous-coordinate label, an m6A site-load measurement, a same-gene RNA half-life readout, and enough joined covariates to keep the RNA-stability statement separated from translation, protein output, folding, function, and organism-level generality. The intended row is not merely a transcript identifier. It is a matched tuple containing an m6A load coordinate, a half-life value, coverage information, transcript-length information, condition labels for DMSO and STM2457 where present, and the declared filtering rule used before the statistic is evaluated. The concrete run retained $5837$ joined genes after imposing the coverage rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})
\geq \mathrm{20th\ percentile\ among\ valid\ joined\ genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite carrier, the observed static association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was negative, with Spearman coefficient $-0.388929$. The perturbation-oriented readout compared m6A load with the change in log half-life and gave Spearman coefficient $0.245804$. After controlling for transcript length, the partial Spearman coefficient remained $0.227683$, with two-sided permutation value $0.000999001$ under $1000$ effective permutations. The recorded verdict for this row set is therefore that the predeclared RNA half-life boundary is crossed.

\paragraph{Statistic licensed by the rows.}
The statistic licensed here is a bounded RNA-stability relation: m6A site load is associated with an RNA half-life readout in the filtered joined panel, and the association persists after a length adjustment. A compact way to state the admitted claim is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}})=-0.388929,\\
&\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.245804,\\
&\rho_{\mathrm{partial,length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL})=0.227683,\\
&p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}}=0.000999001,\qquad n=5837.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values are read as finite evidence that an m6A-load coordinate can cross into an RNA half-life readout under the declared coverage and length-control regime. The numbers do not by themselves identify the biochemical route by which decay is mediated. In particular, this chapter does not infer the identity of a decay-factor RNA-binding protein, the occupancy of that factor at each local window, or the result of an RBP perturbation and rescue experiment.

\paragraph{Boundary against mechanism promotion.}
The biological wording must stay narrower than an RNA-decay mechanism claim. A positive RBP bridge would require same-window measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a separation between synonymous-coordinate labels and occupancy, loss of the target RBP, restoration by wild-type rescue, failure of restoration by binding-defective or non-target controls, and controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch. Those rows are not supplied by the present finite statistic. The present run supplies an m6A-load to RNA-half-life boundary, not an RBP occupancy experiment and not a perturbation-rescue mechanism. Therefore a half-life shift is allowed to count only as an RNA-stability readout. It is not evidence, on its own, for translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence law.

\paragraph{Role of the translation boundary.}
The same-row translation readout is named in the conjectural design because RNA half-life can be misread as downstream protein behavior if the layer boundary is not explicit. The concrete GSE163500-derived statistic does not license that promotion. Even when an mRNA half-life difference is strong, it remains an RNA-layer observation unless ribosome profiling or another matched translation-efficiency contact is joined row by row and evaluated with its own statistic. A matched mRNA-abundance control can help separate transcriptional or abundance effects from RNA half-life effects, but it is not a translation readout. Conversely, a ribosome-profiling contact would not itself become RNA half-life or RBP evidence unless the same finite rows also carry the half-life, occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and control fields required for the stronger bridge.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite evidence and scope calibration.}
A separate human m6A load and half-life check provides useful scale calibration but is not imported as a proof of the present mechanism. That neighboring finite run contained $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, used $1000$ permutations, and reported $3$ sign-concordant strata. Its role here is only to show that m6A-load and half-life surfaces can be evaluated as finite joined RNA-stability panels at a larger scale. It does not add measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective control, or same-window coordinate/occupancy decoupling to the GSE163500 boundary statistic. The present chapter therefore keeps the support local to the verified row set: $5837$ joined genes, the stated coverage threshold, the three reported Spearman coefficients, and the permutation value $0.000999001$.

\paragraph{Admitted conclusion.}
The finite conclusion is that the m6A site-load coordinate crosses a declared RNA half-life boundary in the joined GSE163500-style panel. The evidence is strong enough for a bounded cross-layer RNA-stability statement because the retained rows support a nonzero association after the declared filtering and length adjustment. It is not strong enough for a decay-factor RBP mechanism bridge, and it is not a route to translation, protein state, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. Any future strengthening must add the missing row fields rather than reinterpret the present half-life association.
