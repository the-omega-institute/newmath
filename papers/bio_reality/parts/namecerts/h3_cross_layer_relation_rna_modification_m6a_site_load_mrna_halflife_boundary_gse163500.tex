\subsection{m6A site load and RNA half-life as a bounded same-row relation}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite joined panel in which each retained gene row carries an m6A site-load coordinate and an RNA half-life readout under matched experimental coverage.  The operative row is not a free biological story: it is a declared tuple consisting of the m6A-load coordinate, the DMSO half-life readout, the STM2457 perturbation comparison, transcript length information, coverage eligibility, and the finite statistic used to decide whether the RNA-stability boundary is crossed.  The joined panel contains $5837$ genes after the coverage rule
$\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ STM2457})$ is required to lie at or above the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  Thus the claim is evaluated only on rows that have enough paired coverage to support the stated half-life comparison.

\paragraph{Observed boundary statistic.}
The static DMSO association between m6A site load and log half-life is negative, with Spearman value $-0.388929$.  The perturbation-facing statistic has the opposite sign: the Spearman association between m6A-load and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life under the causal comparison is $0.245804$.  After controlling for transcript length, the partial Spearman statistic is $0.227683$, with a two-sided permutation value $0.000999001$.  The finite calculation therefore crosses the predeclared RNA half-life boundary for this joined panel.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 5837,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 H_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 H) &= 0.245804,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{partial\ length}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 H) &= 0.227683,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm,two\ sided}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $H$ denotes the measured RNA half-life readout on the retained row.  The sign pattern is chapter-specific: high m6A site load is associated with shorter static half-life in DMSO, while the perturbation comparison reports a positive association with the half-life change.  The length-adjusted statistic and the permutation value are the finite evidence used by this record; no unobserved continuous law is inserted between the rows and the conclusion.

\paragraph{Scope of the positive statement.}
The positive statement is deliberately narrow.  The row panel supports a bounded relation between m6A site load and RNA half-life in the stated GSE163500-derived comparison.  It does not by itself identify a decay-factor RBP mechanism, a translation realization, a protein-output effect, a folding consequence, a phenotype, an adaptive explanation, or a universal sequence law.  A mechanism-level statement would require same-row measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and transcription-rate or abundance controls on the same finite subject.  Those mechanism rows are not supplied by the statistic above.

\paragraph{Translation and abundance boundary.}
The translation boundary is also local.  The present row may be carried beside a translation-readout requirement in a broader cross-layer design, but the m6A/half-life statistic itself is an RNA-stability readout.  Ribosome profiling, translation efficiency, mature protein abundance, protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, and organismal phenotype remain separate measurement layers.  A same-row translation readout would be allowed to condition or stratify the RNA half-life relation; it would not convert the RNA half-life association into protein output without an additional protein-layer contact.

\paragraph{Companion RNA-stability scale check.}
The same BioReality packet family contains a cross-organism m6A-load and half-life finite record with $15845$ genes, $135300$ m6A sites, $1000$ actual permutations, and $3$ sign-concordant organism-level comparisons.  That companion record is not used to enlarge the present conclusion; it only shows that the local GSE163500 result sits inside an RNA-stability measurement regime where finite row counts, site counts, and permutation counts are explicitly declared.  The present chapter remains anchored to the $5837$ retained GSE163500 genes and the length-adjusted partial statistic reported above.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim is refused if the high- and low-m6A-load rows become exchangeable for RNA half-life after the declared controls, if the joined coverage rule removes the apparent effect, if a batch or isoform explanation reproduces the signal, or if the result is used as a substitute for measured RBP occupancy or perturbation rescue.  It is also refused for any conclusion that treats codon-window geometry, m6A site load, or RNA half-life alone as evidence for translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, causation, adaptation, or universality.  The accepted conclusion is therefore only this: in the finite eligible panel, m6A site load crosses a bounded RNA half-life readout boundary with the stated effect sizes and permutation support.
