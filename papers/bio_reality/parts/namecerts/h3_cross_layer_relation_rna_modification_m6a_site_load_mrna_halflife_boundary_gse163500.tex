\subsection{Finite m6A-load half-life boundary for GSE163500}
\label{subsec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-boundary-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite joined panel of coding-RNA rows in which an m6A-site-load coordinate is compared with an mRNA half-life readout.  The relevant verified record is \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500}.  Its experiment identifier is \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, and the retained panel contains $5837$ genes after the declared coverage rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage}_{\mathrm{DMSO}},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage}_{\mathrm{STM2457}})
&\geq \text{the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each retained row is read as a bounded tuple containing an m6A-load coordinate, a DMSO half-life value, an STM2457-responsive half-life contrast, transcript-length information, and the identifier structure needed to keep the half-life statistic attached to the same gene row.  The statistic is therefore a finite RNA-stability readout, not a free-standing statement about translation, protein state, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Observed boundary statistic.}
The static association between m6A load and DMSO half-life is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
The perturbation-sensitive statistic has the opposite sign and remains positive after the declared length adjustment:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.245804,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{length\ partial}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.227683.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two-sided partial permutation value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.000999001.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the paper's local scale this is enough to mark the RNA-half-life boundary as crossed: the finite rows support a predeclared relation between m6A-site load and an mRNA half-life contrast.  The word ``crossed'' is used only for this bounded statistic.  It does not certify an RBP mechanism, a translation consequence, a protein-output consequence, or a universal rule for synonymous coding sequence.

\paragraph{Neighboring m6A context.}
A second verified record, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_load\_halflife\_crossorganism\_human\_m6aconquer}, supplies a useful finite-row comparison without enlarging the claim.  That record used experiment \texttt{d7949df4a751}, retained $15845$ genes and $135300$ sites, ran $1000$ permutations, and reported sign concordance in $3$ tested settings.  This neighboring result shows that m6A-load and half-life readouts can be organized as finite gene-site panels in more than one data surface.  It does not import mechanism into the GSE163500 row set, and it does not replace the missing same-window RBP occupancy, perturbation, or rescue arms required for a decay-factor bridge.

\paragraph{Permitted interpretation.}
The permitted statement is narrow.  In the GSE163500 finite panel, m6A site load is statistically coupled to mRNA half-life, with a static DMSO association of $-0.388929$, a perturbation-sensitive association of $0.245804$, a length-partial association of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value of $0.000999001$ over $5837$ retained genes.  The result licenses a bounded cross-layer RNA-stability relation because the coordinate and the half-life readout are present in the same finite joined table and because the declared permutation statistic is non-null at the recorded resolution.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
The same numbers do not license stronger biology.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or general sequence law follows from the half-life statistic.  A decay-factor RBP mechanism would require additional same-window evidence: measured local RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, local-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, abundance controls, batch controls, and the mandatory same-row translation-readout boundary.  If those rows are absent, the present record remains an RNA half-life boundary even when the permutation statistic is strong.

\paragraph{Finite conclusion.}
The finite evidence therefore has the following form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{m6A-load coordinate} + \text{matched half-life row} + \text{declared coverage rule}\\
&\quad \Longrightarrow \text{bounded RNA-stability relation},\\
&\text{bounded RNA-stability relation} \not\Longrightarrow \text{RBP mechanism, translation, protein state, function, or universality}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the complete local content of the GSE163500 boundary record.  The positive verdict belongs to the finite RNA-half-life statistic; every downstream interpretation remains blocked until a separate layer-matched contact supplies the missing measurements and controls.
