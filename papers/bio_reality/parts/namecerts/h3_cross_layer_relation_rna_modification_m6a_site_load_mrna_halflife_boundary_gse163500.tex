\subsection{Finite m6A load and mRNA half-life boundary in GSE163500}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6a-site-load-mrna-halflife-gse163500}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite gene-level panel in which an m6A site-load coordinate is joined to an mRNA half-life readout under matched treatment rows.  The retained panel contains $5837$ joined genes after applying the declared coverage rule: $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})$ must be at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  The row object is therefore not a free statement about RNA modification in general.  It is a bounded table whose visible columns include m6A load, DMSO half-life, treatment-response half-life shift, gene-length control, and the finite inclusion rule that decides which rows are present.

\paragraph{Observed statistic.}
On this finite table, the static association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life is negative, with Spearman correlation
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\log_2 \mathrm{HL}_{\mathrm{DMSO}}) &= -0.388929.
\end{aligned}
$$
The perturbation-facing statistic is positive: the causal-rank readout relating m6A load to the treatment-associated change in log half-life is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}) &= 0.245804.
\end{aligned}
$$
After controlling for length in the recorded partial-rank calculation, the corresponding value remains positive,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(\mathrm{m6A\ load},\Delta\log_2 \mathrm{HL}\mid\mathrm{length}) &= 0.227683,
\end{aligned}
$$
with a two-sided partial-permutation value $p=0.000999001$.  The recorded verdict for this finite statistic is $\mathrm{crosses\_boundary}$: the joined data support a predeclared m6A-load/mRNA-half-life boundary statistic on the retained rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The sign pattern is biologically specific enough to be worth recording.  In the same finite object, higher m6A load is associated with shorter baseline half-life, while the treatment-linked half-life shift moves in the positive direction with m6A load.  The point is not merely that a correlation exists; it is that the same retained-row panel carries the coverage rule, row count, static statistic, treatment-shift statistic, length-partial statistic, and permutation calibration.  This licenses a bounded cross-layer statement from an RNA-modification coordinate to an RNA-stability readout, but only for the declared finite surface.

\paragraph{Neighboring check.}
A separate human m6A-load/half-life finite check gives scale for the boundary without replacing the GSE163500 statistic.  That comparison used $15845$ genes and $135300$ m6A sites, ran $1000$ permutations, and recorded $3$ sign-concordant outcomes.  It is useful here only as a nearby consistency pressure: it shows that m6A-load and half-life can be put into finite counted panels at a larger scale, while the present GSE163500 claim remains tied to its own $5837$-gene joined table, its own coverage filter, and its own partial-permutation value.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The chapter does not infer translation realization from RNA half-life.  It also does not infer protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, causation, or a universal biological law.  Such claims would require separate rows that directly measure the relevant layer.  In particular, a decay-factor RBP bridge would require same-window measured RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, isoform-state controls, abundance controls, batch controls, and a declared finite RBP-decay statistic.  Those rows are not supplied by the GSE163500 boundary statistic itself.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The accepted content is therefore narrow and finite.  For the retained GSE163500 joined rows, m6A load is associated with mRNA half-life structure strongly enough to cross the declared RNA-stability boundary: $n=5837$, $\rho_{\mathrm{static}}=-0.388929$, $\rho_{\mathrm{causal}}=0.245804$, $\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}=0.227683$, and $p=0.000999001$.  The result is an RNA-modification to RNA-half-life readout boundary.  It remains silent about translation, protein state, molecular mechanism, organismal function, and general biological law unless those claims are supplied by additional layer-matched finite contacts.
