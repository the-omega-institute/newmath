\subsection{Escherichia coli mRNA-stability readout for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ codon-residual panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-ecoli-mrna-stability-bstarq6}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous codon-residual panel retains measurable predictive content for an $E.\ coli$ mRNA-stability readout after the amino-acid quotient and ordinary sequence-composition controls have already been imposed.  The readout is $\log_{10}$ mRNA half-life in minutes from the Esquerr\'e et al. 2015 table at growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$.  The coordinate panel is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_{3}$-stress.  The scientific claim is deliberately narrow: it is a controlled association between residualized codon-choice coordinates and a measured transcript-stability scalar, not a claim about translation output, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, protein function, or organism-wide law.

\paragraph{Finite row and join.}
The computed row joins $2720$ $E.\ coli$ coding sequences to positive half-life records.  The CDS-side join hit rate reported by the loader is $0.9978649586335735$, using the key in which the Esquerr\'e GeneId b-number is matched to the CDS locus tag.  The source table contains $2947$ valid half-life records by gene id, while $3739$ CDS records are present on the codon-count side.  The skipped CDS records are concentrated in the missing-half-life class, with $1019$ no-half-life matches and zero duplicate locus tags, invalid lengths, empty sense-codon counts, missing locus tags, non-object records, or nonpositive abundance records.  On the half-life side there are zero duplicate gene ids, missing gene ids, missing growth-rate entries, non-object records, or nonpositive half-life entries.  These diagnostics make the row a real finite measurement join rather than a symbolic construction.

\paragraph{Control model.}
The control design contains $24$ columns and has rank $23$ on the joined data.  It includes an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 computed from real CDS codon counts, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  After this residualization the reported residual degrees of freedom are $2697$.  Thus the test is not merely detecting amino-acid composition, length, GC3, or the simpler support-density baseline.

\paragraph{Survival statistic.}
Let $X$ denote the residualized $9$-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ design and let $m$ denote the residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA half-life response.  The experiment reports a nondegenerate coordinate design with $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$ and the following scalar summary:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{QM} &= 0.02298556640056076,\\
\mathrm{explained\ energy} &= 4.951800341206348,\\
\lVert m\rVert^{2}_{Z\perp} &= 215.43086017169205,\\
\mathrm{permutation\ }p &= 0.000999000999000999.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dominant coordinate in the $E.\ coli$ mRNA-stability vector is $Arg_{AGR}$.  The matched protein-abundance comparison on the same join gives $R^{2}_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, and its dominant coordinate is also $Arg_{AGR}$.  However, the cosine between the mRNA-stability vector $S^{QM}$ and the protein-abundance vector $S^{QP}$ is $-0.7784923375569599$, so shared dominance by the $Arg_{AGR}$ coordinate does not mean that the two readout directions agree.  In this row the mRNA-stability and protein-abundance residual summaries are directionally opposed at the vector level.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The $E.\ coli$ result is not interpreted as a universal codon-stability law.  It is useful precisely because it differs from the corresponding yeast and human rows.  In the yeast mRNA-stability row, $R^{2}_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, the dominant coordinate is $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, the same-join protein-abundance value is $R^{2}_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, and the cosine between $S^{QM}$ and $S^{QP}$ is $0.8040935468384264$.  In the human consensus relative-half-life row, $R^{2}_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, the dominant coordinate is again $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, the same-join protein-abundance value is $R^{2}_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, and the mRNA-versus-protein cosine is positive at $0.6351183883924848$.  Against those two rows, the $E.\ coli$ row is the sharper counter-orientation: it has a positive controlled mRNA-stability association, but its $S^{QM}$ direction points against the same-join protein-abundance direction.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The appropriate module classification for this chapter is cross-layer relation.  The measured layer is mRNA stability, while the source coordinates are synonymous codon residuals after amino-acid composition and sequence-composition controls.  The row therefore supports a transcript-stability association for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate panel in $E.\ coli$ K-12 MG1655, not a translation-realization statement.  The available robustness pointer records an additional growth-rate half-life table at $0.10\,h^{-1}$, but the finite statistic stated here uses the selected $0.40\,h^{-1}$ growth-rate readout only.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The accepted content of this record is that the $E.\ coli$ finite join gives a positive, permutation-supported $S^{QM}$ signal after the declared controls: $R^{2}_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  No translation-efficiency conclusion follows from this mRNA-stability row.  No ribosome occupancy, tRNA charging, protein abundance mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, causal decay mechanism, or universal biological rule follows unless a separate measurement contact supplies the relevant layer and the corresponding controlled statistic is computed.  The half-life measurement is growth-rate-specific and observational; correlation with the codon-residual panel is not by itself evidence that codon optimality causes transcript stabilization.
