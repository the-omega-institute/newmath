\subsection{Escherichia coli mRNA-stability projection of the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-ecoli-mrna-stability-bstar-q6}

\paragraph{Claim under test.}
This record evaluates whether the nine-coordinate B$^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual panel carries a measurable association with mRNA stability in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 after removing the sequence-level background used throughout the protein-abundance and codon-optimality analyses.  The readout is $\log_{10}$ mRNA half-life in minutes from the Esquerr\'{e} et al. 2015 table at growth rate $0.40\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$.  The tested statement is deliberately narrow: B$^*_{Q6}$ codon residuals are statistical predictors of residualized bacterial mRNA half-life under the same CDS-derived controls used for the corresponding protein-abundance surface.  It is not a statement about translation realization, decay mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a cross-species law.

\paragraph{Finite row set and controls.}
The joined bacterial table contains $2720$ usable CDS--half-life rows.  The half-life source contains $2947$ valid records keyed by GeneId, while $3739$ CDS records were available for the organism-level coding-sequence side; the reported CDS join hit rate is $0.9978649586335735$.  The join key identifies the Esquerr\'{e} GeneId b-number with the E. coli CDS match identifier or locus tag.  The skipped CDS rows are accounted for explicitly: $1019$ have no half-life match, while duplicate locus tags, empty sense-codon counts, invalid lengths, missing locus tags, non-object records, and nonpositive abundance records are all reported as $0$.  The skipped half-life side is likewise clean for duplicate GeneId, missing GeneId, missing growth rate, non-object records, and nonpositive half-life, each with count $0$.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate design.}
The source coordinate block has nine B$^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$, leaving $2697$ residual degrees of freedom after control removal.  The controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 computed from the real CDS codon counts, and the M-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected B$^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the claim is tested only after amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, and the simpler support-density baseline have been absorbed.

\paragraph{Observed survival statistic.}
For the E. coli half-life readout, the residualized nine-coordinate regression gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QM} &= 0.02298556640056076,\\
\operatorname{rank}(X_{\perp Z}) &= 9,\\
\|\widehat{y}_{Q}\|^2 &= 4.951800341206348,\\
\|y_{\perp Z}\|^2 &= 215.43086017169205.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dominant coordinate in this bacterial mRNA-stability surface is $Arg_{AGR}$.  A permutation audit with $1000$ permutations reports a right-tail value $p = 0.000999000999000999$ for $R^2_{QM}$, so the finite row is not being read from an untested fitted coefficient alone.  On the same E. coli join, the corresponding protein-abundance residual surface has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.11632311854858134,\\
\cos(S^{QM}_{\mathrm{E.coli}},S^{QP}_{\mathrm{E.coli}}) &= -0.7784923375569599.
\end{aligned}
$$
The protein-abundance surface on that same join is also dominated by $Arg_{AGR}$, but the negative cosine shows that the mRNA-stability and protein-abundance projection directions are not aligned as a single positive propagation direction.

\paragraph{Comparison anchor.}
A yeast half-life row gives a useful descriptive contrast without turning the bacterial result into a phylogenetic claim.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the analogous mRNA-stability statistic is $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$ on $5085$ joined rows, with dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$ and same-join protein-abundance statistic $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$.  The yeast comparison reports $\cos(S^{QM},S^{QP})=0.8040935468384264$, whereas the E. coli comparison reports $-0.7784923375569599$.  The contrast is therefore qualitative and organism-specific: yeast shows a positive alignment between the mRNA-stability and protein-abundance projection surfaces, while the bacterial row shows a strong negative alignment, even though both rows pass the same nominal permutation resolution $p=0.000999000999000999$ for the mRNA-stability statistic.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite E. coli result supports a bounded cross-layer association between B$^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals and residualized mRNA half-life.  The supported numerical content is the controlled $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, the $Arg_{AGR}$ dominant coordinate, the $2720$-row joined table, the $24$-column rank-$23$ control design, the $1000$-permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$, and the negative same-join alignment with the protein-abundance projection.  This does not identify a codon-optimality cause of half-life, does not prove an mRNA decay mechanism, and does not say that codon residuals realize translation.  The half-life measurement is growth-rate specific, the organism is a single bacterial strain, and the cross-organism comparison with yeast is descriptive rather than phylogenetically controlled.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation, ribosome occupancy, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this row.  A separate layer-matched contact would be needed for any of those claims.  In particular, the E. coli $S^{QM}$ row is an mRNA-stability projection under explicit sequence controls; it cannot be promoted into a translation-readout entry, a protein-output mediation result, or a functional interpretation without additional joined measurements and their own controlled statistics.
