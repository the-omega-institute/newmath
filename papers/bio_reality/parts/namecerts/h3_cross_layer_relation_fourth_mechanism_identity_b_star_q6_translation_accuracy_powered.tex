\subsection{Fourth-mechanism identity test for translation accuracy}\label{sec:biorality-q6-translation-accuracy-fourth-mechanism}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The object tested here is the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting, restricted to a finite coding-sequence panel with organism labels, synonymous-design rows, tRNA or wobble-weight annotations, and matched translation-readout candidates.  The row is not treated as a protein-output or phenotype row.  Its admissible readback is only a controlled translation-layer survival entry of the form $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a tAI, stAI, ribosome-occupancy, or translation-efficiency readout.  Any claim above that layer must be supplied by a separate measurement class.

\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The finite $f_3$ coordinate has a concrete codon boundary.  The verified parent set $M=\mathrm{Med}_R\cup\mathrm{ACA}$ contains the 20 codons
$$\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The $f_3$-saturation of $R$ removes precisely the ACA/ACG pair and leaves the 18-codon set
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,\\
&CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty, so the tested distinction is not a boundary-leak artifact inside this finite coordinate surface.  It is a residual synonymous-codon asymmetry that can become biologically readable only if the empirical translation contact breaks the $c\leftrightarrow c^{f_3}$ symmetry through context tRNA supply, wobble weights, or an observed translation-efficiency row.

\paragraph{Powered translation-accuracy assay.}
The finite assay for this chapter used the claim identifier $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.fourth\_mechanism\_identity.b\_star\_q6\_translation\_accuracy\_powered$ and experiment identifier $b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_translation\_accuracy\_powered$.  It was run with 41 synonymous-design columns, 5 folds, 200 permutations, and 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested organisms, under the deterministic seed $\mathrm{sha256}:97e0f836e1a0e4fb71a81bc6201b6e67d532adce9d7fa9c348b5df160e3ae2e1$.  The run status was passed, and its finite conclusion was
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{FourthMechanismIdentity}(B^{\ast}_{Q6},\mathrm{accuracy})=\mathrm{not\_accuracy}.
\end{aligned}$$
Thus, within this panel and this controlled design, translation accuracy is not identified as the dominant fourth mechanism behind the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual axis.  The result is negative for the accuracy identity, not negative for the existence of a residual fourth axis.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring finite rows.}
The negative accuracy call sits inside a larger finite pattern.  A three-axis sufficiency test on the same $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ family reported $\mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present}$ using 41 synonymous-design columns, 5 folds, and 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum was led by $d_{resid4}$ in all 11 computed dominant-count entries, with $d_{tRNA}$ next on average; the domain means were explicitly descriptive because the domain counts were imbalanced, with $n_{archaea}=1$, $n_{bacteria}=2$, and $n_{eukaryote}=8$.  The largest GC3 rank effect in that spectrum was small, $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{usage}$.

The third-axis identity row gave a separate positive finite signal: $d_{\perp}$ aligned with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  Its mechanism row reported selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, and a CSC/positional identity requiring further contact.  Those rows keep the accuracy conclusion from being overread: the fourth mechanism exists in the finite residual geometry, but the accuracy assay does not identify it as translation accuracy.

\paragraph{Competing fourth-mechanism exclusions.}
Other fourth-mechanism identity probes constrain the same neighborhood.  The mRNA-stability assay concluded $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because the yeast $d_{opt\_halflife}$ direction had low alignment with the abundance $d_{resid4}$ direction under the fixed gates.  The codon-pair assay concluded $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ with 5 folds, 50 shuffle-null draws, and 41 synonymous-design columns.  The elongation-dwell assay in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} returned $\mathrm{partial}$ rather than a clean identity.  Together with the present $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ call, these rows say that the fourth residual axis is not explained, in this finite evidence packet, by translation accuracy, mRNA stability, or codon-pair structure as a dominant identity; elongation dwell remains only a partial contact.

\paragraph{Local falsification rule.}
The controlled survival statement remains narrow.  For each organism and each candidate translation readout $T_j$, the relevant quantity is a row-level statistic $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and mRNA where the readout is translation efficiency.  Support would require a non-vacuous positive entry after simpler-codon-baseline ablation.  A rank-degenerate matrix, including an all-$1.0$ three-organism surface, is a null boundary rather than evidence.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
This chapter establishes only a finite negative identity result for translation accuracy as the fourth mechanism in the tested $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual setting.  It does not establish observed ribosome occupancy, mature protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, a mechanistic law, or universality.  Those conclusions require their own layer-matched empirical contacts and their own controlled statistics.  The codon coordinate, the saturation sets, modeled tAI or stAI weights, and the passed accuracy-identity assay cannot by themselves promote the residual geometry into any higher biological claim.
