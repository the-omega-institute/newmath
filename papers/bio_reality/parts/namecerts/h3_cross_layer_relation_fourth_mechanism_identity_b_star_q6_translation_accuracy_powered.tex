\subsection{A fourth-axis rejection for translation accuracy}
\label{sec:b/q6-translation-accuracy-rejection}

\paragraph{Finite row under review.}
The record considered here is the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate restricted to the $f_3$-stress direction after amino-acid projection.  The biological question is deliberately narrow: whether the residual fourth axis, already detected as a finite cross-organism signal, is explained by translation accuracy.  The powered translation-accuracy assay was run with experiment id \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_translation\_accuracy\_powered}.  Its finite row has status \texttt{passed}, but its conclusion is \texttt{not\_accuracy}.  The run used $5$ folds, $200$ permutations, $41$ synonymous design columns, and computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms.  Thus the chapter records a negative identity result: within this assay, the fourth residual component is not identified with translation accuracy.

\paragraph{Coordinate surface.}
The underlying $f_3$ row is not an unrestricted codon-composition statement.  The saturating finite sets fix the available coordinate support.  The set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ is

\begin{aligned}

The saturated $f_3$ image removes exactly the two codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from that displayed list:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\

The reported $f_3$ boundary set is empty, so the finite codon row does not supply an extra boundary exception that could be used to rescue an accuracy interpretation.

\paragraph{Relation to the residual-axis spectrum.}
The negative translation-accuracy call is not a denial that the residual basis carries cross-organism structure.  In the axis-strength spectrum, the powered panel again computed $11$ organisms out of $16$, used $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous design columns, and found $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ leading on average; the per-organism dominant-count summary was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  The same finite summary reports imbalanced domain counts $\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$ and the largest GC3 rank effect as $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.  Separately, the three-axis sufficiency test concluded \texttt{fourth\_mechanism\_present} on the same $11$ of $16$ organism scale with $5$ folds and $41$ synonymous columns.  Therefore the evidence pattern is not ``no fourth axis''; it is ``fourth axis present, but not translation accuracy in this assay.''

\paragraph{Neighboring exclusions and partial positives.}
The surrounding finite rows sharpen the interpretation.  The non-tRNA residual optimality row identifies $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant residual axis in an $11$-organism, $5$-fold, $41$-column design.  The two-axis closure row concluded \texttt{third\_axis\_present}; its reason was that $d_{\perp}$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms.  The third-axis identity row aligned $d_{\perp}$ to usage frequency within family with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The third-axis mechanism row reported \texttt{selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs\_external}, because GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ retained significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.  Against that background, the fourth-axis identity tests exclude several simple candidates: mRNA stability was rejected as the dominant identity in the yeast half-life assay, codon-pair identity was rejected, and translation accuracy was rejected here.  Elongation dwell remains only \texttt{partial} in the yeast row, not a universal identity assignment.

\paragraph{Accuracy verdict.}
Let $Q$ denote the controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate and let $A$ denote the translation-accuracy candidate readout used by the assay.  The finite decision recorded by the powered row is not a positive survival statement of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{Q,A}>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Rather, under the declared $5$-fold design, $200$-permutation comparison, and $41$ synonymous design columns, the identity classifier returns \texttt{not\_accuracy}.  The scientifically conservative reading is that translation accuracy should be removed from the current list of accepted identities for the fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ residual component.  This does not erase the residual component, and it does not select a replacement identity by elimination alone.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
This chapter only licenses the finite negative statement just described.  The data here do not establish observed translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, a causal pathway, or a universal biological rule.  Any such stronger assertion would require its own layer-matched contact: for example, same-organism and same-CDS translation-efficiency rows for translation, separate protein-abundance rows for protein output, and separate structure, localization, stability, function, perturbation, or phenotype assays for those higher layers.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights, codon usage rows, tRNA-copy summaries, and the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual geometry may bound candidate readouts, but they do not by themselves promote the negative accuracy result into a positive biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Falsifiability.}
The rejection is finite rather than metaphysical.  It would be overturned only by a new matched panel in which the same $f_3$-stress coordinate, after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls, shows a non-vacuous positive translation-accuracy survival entry.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface or a modeled wobble-weight asymmetry without joined readout rows would not suffice.  Until such a row exists, the BioReality status of this packet is: powered residual fourth-axis evidence is present, but translation accuracy is not its identified finite mechanism in the current assay.
