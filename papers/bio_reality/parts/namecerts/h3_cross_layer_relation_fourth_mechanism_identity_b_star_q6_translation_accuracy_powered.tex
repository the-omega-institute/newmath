\subsection{The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation-accuracy non-identity row}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-accuracy-nonidentity}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record concerns the $f_3$-stress residual coordinate on a finite CDS-by-organism surface after synonymous codons have been projected through amino-acid identity.  The row is not a general statement about translation; it is a bounded test of whether the fourth residual mechanism in the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ panel is explained by translation accuracy.  The relevant finite saturation datum fixes the ambient codon support.  The enlarged set $M=\operatorname{Med}_{R}\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ is
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$,
whereas the $f_3$-saturation of $R$ removes precisely $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, leaving
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The recorded boundary $\partial_{f_3}R$ is empty.  This matters because the accuracy test is not allowed to borrow evidence from an unresolved codon-boundary residue; it must live on the stated finite residual coordinate surface.

\paragraph{Accuracy test and numerical outcome.}
The powered accuracy assay used $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and $200$ permutations.  Its deterministic seed was $\mathrm{sha256}:97e0f836e1a0e4fb71a81bc6201b6e67d532adce9d7fa9c348b5df160e3ae2e1$.  The status was passed, and the finite conclusion was $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$.  In the notation of this paper, the empirical statement is therefore not
``the fourth $B^{*}_{Q6}$ mechanism is translation accuracy,''
but rather the opposite bounded identification:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{fourth residual mechanism} &\not\equiv \text{translation-accuracy axis}
\end{aligned}
$$
within the tested organism panel, fold design, synonymous-coordinate basis, and permutation protocol.  The conclusion is a mechanism-exclusion row, not a biological impossibility theorem: it says that this finite assay did not identify the fourth residual coordinate with the translation-accuracy candidate.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding $Q6$ mechanism rows.}
The exclusion is consistent with the rest of the finite mechanism table.  The three-axis sufficiency assay passed with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, with conclusion $\mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present}$; thus the fourth coordinate is not an artifact of pretending that three axes suffice.  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum, on the same $11$ of $16$ organism panel and $41$ design columns, reported that spectra are led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, with all $11$ per-organism dominant counts assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ and with the largest GC3 rank effect only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  The residual optimality assay separately identified the non-tRNA residual optimality direction with $f_3$ translation selection, again on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ and $41$ synonymous design columns.  These rows leave room for a real fourth residual component while ruling out the narrower statement that the component is captured by translation accuracy in the present assay.

\paragraph{Other rejected or partial fourth-mechanism candidates.}
The same finite table also keeps the accuracy result from being overread.  The mRNA-stability assay passed but concluded $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$, because yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  The codon-pair assay passed with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, $50$ shuffle-null replicates, and conclusion $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$.  The elongation-dwell assay passed on \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} with $5$ folds and conclusion $\mathrm{partial}$.  Consequently the finite readout is not a simple replacement of translation accuracy by mRNA stability or codon-pair structure; the only positive wording available here is that elongation dwell remains partial while accuracy is rejected as the fourth-mechanism identity in this panel.

\paragraph{Readout rule.}
The only admissible translation-layer readback for this row is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ for a declared translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA controls kept in the design.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface on three organisms is not survival evidence; it is a null boundary for the readout.  Likewise, a wobble or tRNA asymmetry can make the $f_3$ coordinate testable, but it does not by itself create a measured translation-survival statistic.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
This row gives a finite negative identity result for translation accuracy.  It does not establish measured translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universality.  Any one of those stronger claims requires its own layer-matched contact and its own recorded statistic.  In particular, codon geometry, modeled tAI or stAI weights, review eligibility, and $S^{QT}$ notation do not substitute for same-organism and same-CDS joined codon, wobble-weight, ribosome or translation-efficiency, and mRNA-control rows.  The correct interpretation is therefore narrow: within the finite powered $B^{*}_{Q6}$ fourth-mechanism screen, translation accuracy is not the identified mechanism, while downstream biological interpretations remain blocked until separately measured.
