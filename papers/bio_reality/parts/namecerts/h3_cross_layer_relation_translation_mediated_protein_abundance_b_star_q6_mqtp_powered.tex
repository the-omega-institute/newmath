\subsection{Translation-mediated protein-abundance readback for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{subsec:biores-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
This record tests a cross-layer relation in which a finite family of synonymous residual coordinates is allowed to approach protein abundance only through an intervening translation readout.  The residual side consists of nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates computed on real coding sequences:
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation coordinate $T$ is per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as a codon-count-weighted average of codon tAI weights derived from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein coordinate $Y$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  Thus the finite surface consists of joined rows of the form $(q_i,T,Y)$, with controls held outside the promoted coordinates.

\paragraph{Rows and controls.}
The joined data contain $3739$ proteins for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $4129$ proteins for \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ proteins for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The tRNA totals used in the modeled tAI readout are $90$, $49$, and $275$, respectively; the zero raw-weight entries filled by a geometric mean are $1$, $1$, and $0$.  Each organism uses $24$ control columns, with residualized control rank $23$, and the residual degrees of freedom after control projection are $3716$, $4106$, and $5792$.  The residualized design has $\mathrm{rank}(X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(T)=1$, and $\mathrm{rank}(X,T)=10$ in all three organisms, so the mediation decomposition is rank-sufficient rather than a degenerate fit.

\paragraph{Modeled translation contact.}
The translation readout is not a ribosome-profiling measurement.  It is a bounded modeled-tAI contact built from organism-scoped tRNA gene-copy data and wobble weights.  A separate modeled translation-survival run confirms that the standard tRNA-family payload is present for the organisms in that contact: $20$ standard amino-acid families are represented for \textit{E. coli} K-12, human, and yeast, with matched FASTA-header counts $87$, $432$, and $275$.  The same modeled-tAI run reports $61$ sense codons for each organism, zero raw-weight fallback count $0$ at the codon-weight stage, $81$ bounded-coupling entries above threshold $0.01$, and maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  Its own scope is still underpowered for population survival because it has only $3$ organisms, below the stated $40$-species requirement for powered survival.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
Let $X_Q$ denote the nine-dimensional residual matrix, let $T$ denote residualized per-protein modeled tAI, let $Y$ denote residualized log protein abundance, and let $Z$ contain amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched abundance controls.  The reported scalar is a statistical mediation fraction, not a causal effect:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{R^2(Q \to P)-R^2(Q \to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q \to P)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, the total residual-to-protein association is $c=0.10767526677985896$, the direct association after conditioning on modeled tAI is $c'=0.04167869969498872$, the indirect difference is $0.06599656708487024$, and $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$.  For \textit{M. smegmatis}, $c=0.03209009170649934$, $c'=0.029395293228368185$, the indirect difference is $0.0026947984781311544$, and $M^{QTP}=0.08397602919861291$.  For yeast, $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, the indirect difference is $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  All three mediation fractions lie in the unit interval.

\paragraph{Energy audit.}
The residual-energy audit separates the translation step from the protein-abundance step.  In \textit{E. coli}, $X_Q$ explains $0.256128714706293$ units of translation-readout energy and $432.89050065533684$ units of protein-abundance energy; $T$ explains $582.5912438239341$ units of protein-abundance energy, with $317.26302294928666$ units added after $X_Q$, while the direct $X_Q$ improvement after $T$ is $167.56227978068944$.  In \textit{M. smegmatis}, the corresponding values are $0.026187928294862578$, $183.85037813566998$, $15.813547327443837$, $0.37452260494680445$, and $168.41135341317295$.  In yeast, they are $5.671703404506426$, $882.6526210803958$, $941.8858618613751$, $104.11162139652299$, and $44.87838061554362$.  These numbers justify a finite cross-layer readback only in the restricted sense that conditioning on modeled tAI absorbs a nonzero part of the residual-to-protein abundance association.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  The record supports a controlled statistical decomposition $Q_i \to T \to P$ for matched CDS/protein rows, where $T$ is modeled per-protein tAI and $P$ is measured steady-state protein abundance.  It does not establish a perturbational translation mechanism, does not show that ribosomes were directly observed, and does not isolate protein abundance from mRNA abundance, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, or pathway context.  No claim about translation realization in the measured ribosome-profiling sense, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, selection, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that evidence and the same residual coordinate survives the required controlled step.
