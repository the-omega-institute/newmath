\subsection{Translation-mediated protein abundance for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tested here is a finite mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,T,P)$: a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate $q_i$, a translation readout $T$, and a matched protein-abundance readout $P$.  The residual coordinates are the nine computed axes
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation coordinate is per-protein modeled tAI, computed as the codon-count-weighted mean of codon tAI weights from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein coordinate is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The readout is therefore not a bare association $q_i\to P$: it is the controlled decomposition of the joint nine-dimensional residual block $X_Q$ against protein abundance after conditioning on the modeled translation coordinate.

\paragraph{Controls and row support.}
Every organism-specific fit residualizes against the same control family: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the M-density baseline induced by the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ support.  The rank audit reports $24$ control columns, residualized control rank $23$, residualized $X_Q$ rank $9$, residualized $T$ rank $1$, and residualized joint rank $10$ in every organism.  The data rows are finite and organism keyed: $4042$ proteins in \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ in \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ in \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ in \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ in \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ in \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ in \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $13322$ in \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The tRNA-copy totals used for the modeled tAI contact range from $47$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $8874$ in \emph{D. rerio}; zero raw codon-weight entries required geometric-mean fill only in \emph{B. subtilis} ($3$), \emph{E. coli} ($1$), \emph{M. smegmatis} ($1$), and \emph{S. solfataricus} ($1$).

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
For each organism the finite statistic is the scalar mediation fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{R^2(Q\to P)-R^2(Q\to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to P)},\\
R^2(Q\to P) &= c,\\
R^2(Q\to P\mid T) &= c'.
\end{aligned}
$$
All twelve organism rows have $0\leq M^{QTP}\leq 1$.  The largest mediated fraction is in \emph{S. cerevisiae}, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  Large finite fractions also appear in \emph{D. melanogaster} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, \emph{E. coli} with $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$, and \emph{B. subtilis} with $M^{QTP}=0.6084594553474505$.  Midrange rows are \emph{C. elegans} with $M^{QTP}=0.4872268357227724$, \emph{D. rerio} with $0.3157053953614641$, and \emph{H. sapiens} with $0.27206289160119157$.  Smaller but positive rows are \emph{G. gallus} with $0.1976903964724546$, \emph{R. norvegicus} with $0.17489681798543982$, \emph{M. musculus} with $0.15488799907323988$, \emph{M. smegmatis} with $0.08397602919861291$, and \emph{S. solfataricus} with $0.01850668564589326$.

\paragraph{Energy audit.}
The same table shows that the residual block is not being read through a rank-degenerate shortcut.  In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $X_Q$ explains $882.6526210803958$ units of protein-abundance residual energy and $5.671703404506426$ units of translation residual energy, while $T$ explains $941.8858618613751$ units of protein-abundance residual energy.  In \emph{H. sapiens}, the corresponding energies are $1370.6490494847067$, $0.3099459586758378$, and $850.7864327764183$.  In \emph{M. musculus}, they are $2431.9284360407714$, $0.08224668862908166$, and $923.4025869376133$.  The organism-specific residual degrees of freedom remain large after controls, from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in \emph{M. musculus}.  The finite calculation is therefore a controlled variance decomposition over matched CDS/protein rows, not an extrapolation from codon vocabulary alone.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring finite facts.}
The translation contact is bounded by the earlier modeled-tAI survival audit: only $3$ organisms were present there, enough to compute $81$ modeled coupling entries above the $0.01$ threshold and a maximum absolute modeled coupling of $3801961.031607896$, but not enough for powered survival under the declared $\geq 40$ matched-species condition.  The present mediation table is stronger in row support for protein abundance because it uses $12$ organism-matched proteome joins, but its translation coordinate is still modeled tAI rather than ribosome profiling.  A separate yeast structure partition also shows why this chapter must not over-read protein abundance: in yeast, $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$, with structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$ and permutation $p=0.027972027972027972$ for $S_Q$ structure association but $p=0.1258741258741259$ for the incremental structure fraction.  A distinct structural-order audit assigns the dominant powered axis to $f3\_stress$, with sign-flip $p=0.03125$ and weighted held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0031744502294756994$.  These neighboring results are consistent with a finite residual signal, but they do not convert this protein-abundance mediation table into a folding or structure claim.

\paragraph{Readback and boundary.}
The supported readback is narrow: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block has a positive, controlled, modeled-tAI-conditioned mediation decomposition against matched protein abundance in every organism row listed above.  It may be summarized as a finite $Q\to T\to P$ statistical surface where the direct $Q\to P$ component is reduced after conditioning on $T$.  The phase-one background confirms that locked codon-geometry contacts can be externally discriminating, with $40/40$ successes in both the boundary and independent-replication binomial checks and one-sided $p=9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$, while $20$ negative-control and $20$ critical-ablation rows show zero unexpected successes.  That background supports disciplined finite testing, not promotion across biological layers.  No translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, perturbation response, selection law, or universality follows from this chapter.  Those claims require their own matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.  In particular, modeled tAI is not ribo-seq, protein abundance is not protein state or function, and a positive $M^{QTP}$ fraction is not a causal effect.
