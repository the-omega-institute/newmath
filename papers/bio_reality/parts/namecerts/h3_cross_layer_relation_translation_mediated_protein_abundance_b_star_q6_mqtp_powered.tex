\subsection{Translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
This record treats the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis as a finite cross-layer mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,T,P)$ rather than direct residual-to-protein assertions.  The residual side has nine computed coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The middle coordinate $T$ is not an observed ribosome-profiling trace in this finite run.  It is the per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as codon-count-weighted tAI using GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The downstream readout $P$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The permitted readback is therefore a controlled statistical decomposition $Q \to T \to P$, not a direct claim that synonymous residual geometry is already a protein-level mechanism.

\paragraph{Joined rows and residualized controls.}
The finite joined tables contain $3739$ proteins for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $4129$ proteins for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ proteins for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The tRNA-copy totals used by the modeled tAI construction are $90$, $49$, and $275$, respectively, with geometric-mean filling of zero raw weights in one protein set for \emph{E. coli}, one protein set for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and none for yeast.  After residualizing by the declared control block, the design has $24$ control columns with control rank $23$ in each organism.  The residual degrees of freedom after the controls are $3716$, $4106$, and $5792$.  The rank checks are finite and nondegenerate in the fitted surface: $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{XT})=10$ for all three organisms, under a degenerate-$R^2$ cutoff of $0.999999$.

\paragraph{Mediation scalars.}
The decomposition uses the joint nine-dimensional $X_Q$ block as the residual-coordinate side.  For \emph{E. coli}, the total controlled residual-to-protein coefficient of determination is $c=0.10767526677985896$, while the direct value after conditioning on modeled tAI is $c'=0.04167869969498872$.  The indirect difference is $0.06599656708487024$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$.  For \emph{M. smegmatis}, $c=0.03209009170649934$, $c'=0.029395293228368185$, the indirect difference is $0.0026947984781311544$, and $M^{QTP}=0.08397602919861291$.  For yeast, $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, the indirect difference is $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  All three mediation fractions lie in the unit interval, so the finite run supports a bounded statistical attenuation statement: modeled tAI absorbs a substantial part of the joint $B^*_{Q6}$ association with protein abundance in \emph{E. coli} and yeast, and a small part in \emph{M. smegmatis}.

\paragraph{Energy accounting.}
The residualized energy checks keep the fitted relation anchored to finite rows.  In \emph{E. coli}, $X$ explains $0.256128714706293$ energy on $T$ and $432.89050065533684$ energy on $Y$; $T$ explains $582.5912438239341$ energy on $Y$; the incremental $T$ improvement given $X$ is $317.26302294928666$; the direct $X$ improvement given $T$ is $167.56227978068944$; and the residual energies are $0.4728468725814029$ for $T$ and $4020.333671802061$ for $Y$.  In \emph{M. smegmatis}, the corresponding values are $0.026187928294862578$, $183.85037813566998$, $15.813547327443837$, $0.37452260494680445$, $168.41135341317295$, $0.041191576348982066$, and $5729.194538214237$.  In yeast, they are $5.671703404506426$, $882.6526210803958$, $941.8858618613751$, $104.11162139652299$, $44.87838061554362$, $6.90537888244409$, and $2958.1865890925337$.  These numbers show that the fitted mediation surface is not a shape-only construction: each organism contributes a finite residualized matrix with explicit rank, energy, and abundance rows.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The positive mediated fractions do not establish causality, ribosome occupancy, translation mechanism, synonymous-edit response, perturbation rescue, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  The $T$ coordinate in this run is modeled per-protein tAI, not ribo-seq translation efficiency, and conditioning on modeled tAI in cross-sectional CDS/protein-abundance rows is not a demonstrated biochemical mechanism.  Matched protein abundance remains affected by mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, and pathway context.  A residual-to-protein association is admissible here only as a controlled decomposition through the modeled translation readout; any stronger biological layer requires its own separately matched contact and statistic.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
This finite run is ready as a BioReality vision-level record for translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition.  It does not say that $B^*_{Q6}$ creates protein abundance.  It says that, across three organism-matched finite tables with $13683$ total joined proteins, the nine-coordinate residual block has a controlled protein-abundance association whose direct component is reduced after introducing modeled per-protein tAI.  The result is strongest in yeast, substantial in \emph{E. coli}, and weak but positive in \emph{M. smegmatis}; it remains bounded to statistical mediation through the available translation proxy.
