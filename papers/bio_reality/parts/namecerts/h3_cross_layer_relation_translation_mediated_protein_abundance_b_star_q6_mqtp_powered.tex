\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation for the $B^*_Q6$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record considered here is a finite mediation surface whose rows join a $B^*_Q6$ synonymous residual coordinate, a bounded translation readout, and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The residual block has nine coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each joined protein-coding row, $T$ is the per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as the codon-count-weighted mean of codon tAI weights derived from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein readout $Y$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real coding-sequence codon counts.  The control block $Z$ contains an intercept, coding-sequence length, twenty amino-acid composition coordinates, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  In every organism-specific fit, $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(T)=1$, $\operatorname{rank}(X,T)=10$, and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, with a degenerate-$R^2$ threshold of $0.999999$.

\paragraph{Joined organisms and rank sufficiency.}
The finite surface covers twelve organism-matched joins: $4042$ Bacillus subtilis proteins, $11661$ Caenorhabditis elegans proteins, $14410$ Danio rerio proteins, $13295$ Drosophila melanogaster proteins, $3739$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 proteins, $12418$ Gallus gallus proteins, $19053$ Homo sapiens proteins, $19395$ Mus musculus proteins, $4129$ Mycobacterium smegmatis proteins, $13322$ Rattus norvegicus proteins, $5815$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins, and $1656$ Sulfolobus solfataricus proteins.  All twelve organism fits are rank-sufficient under the declared finite design.  The tRNA-copy totals used in the modeled tAI readout range from $47$ in Sulfolobus solfataricus to $8874$ in Danio rerio; the geometric-mean fill count for zero raw wobble weights is $3$ in Bacillus subtilis, $1$ in Escherichia coli, $1$ in Mycobacterium smegmatis, $1$ in Sulfolobus solfataricus, and $0$ in the other eight organisms.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
The scalar mediation audit uses the joint nine-dimensional residual block $X_Q$ and asks whether conditioning on $T$ reduces the residual-to-protein association.  With $c=R^2(Y\sim X_Q\mid Z)$ and $c'=R^2(Y\sim X_Q\mid Z,T)$, the finite mediated fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{c-c'}{c}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed fractions are all in $[0,1]$: Bacillus subtilis $0.6084594553$, Caenorhabditis elegans $0.4872268357$, Danio rerio $0.3157053954$, Drosophila melanogaster $0.6165167749$, Escherichia coli $0.6129222528$, Gallus gallus $0.1976903965$, Homo sapiens $0.2720628916$, Mus musculus $0.1548879991$, Mycobacterium smegmatis $0.0839760292$, Rattus norvegicus $0.1748968180$, Saccharomyces cerevisiae $0.9491551041$, and Sulfolobus solfataricus $0.0185066856$.  The corresponding total and direct residual-to-protein $R^2$ pairs include $c=0.2983762499$, $c'=0.0151709094$ in yeast; $c=0.1076752668$, $c'=0.0416786997$ in Escherichia coli; $c=0.1021790676$, $c'=0.0523946838$ in Caenorhabditis elegans; and $c=0.0445456512$, $c'=0.0324264325$ in Homo sapiens.  Thus the finite row surface supports a statistical statement: the joint $B^*_Q6$ residual block has a protein-abundance association whose fitted component is partially absorbed by modeled per-protein tAI in every organism-specific table.

\paragraph{Energy diagnostics.}
The residual-energy ledger gives the same conclusion without relying only on the fraction.  In yeast, $X_Q$ explains $882.6526211$ units of protein-abundance residual energy, while $T$ explains $941.8858619$ units of the same residual target and $X_Q$ explains $5.6717034$ units of the residual tAI target; the mediated fraction is therefore large, $0.9491551041$.  In mouse, the same fields are $2431.9284360$, $923.4025869$, and $0.0822467$, giving a smaller but positive mediated fraction of $0.1548879991$.  In Sulfolobus solfataricus, the protein association remains mostly direct after conditioning on modeled tAI: $X_Q$ explains $62.7443618$ units of protein residual energy, $T$ explains $1.7564662$, and the mediated fraction is only $0.0185066856$.  These contrasts matter: the claim is not a universal sign law, but a finite table of organism-scoped decompositions.

The surrounding ledger prevents over-reading the mediated fractions.  A separate modeled-tAI survival matrix used only three organisms and explicitly reported that $3$ organisms are insufficient for a powered survival claim, despite $81$ bounded-coupling entries above threshold and maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  A yeast structure-mediation audit joined $4759$ genes and found $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.1377396770$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.1375901121$, with a structure-only fraction $0.0032845645$ and a structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507779$; its structure-incremental permutation $p$-value was $0.1258741259$.  A structural-order audit identified $f3\_stress$ as the only fold-level sign-flip passing coordinate, with weighted held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0031744502$ and $p=0.03125$.  In contrast, the cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit did not support the strong selection-imprint claim: $\Delta R^2=0.0325051502$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477730$, and null $95\%$ maximum $\Delta R^2=0.0542715707$ on $80$ orthogroups and $240$ rows.  The dynamic RPF-protein mass-balance run on $120$ genes reported $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628202$, $R^2_{L1}=0.9964106289$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443378$, $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568$, with $\Delta R^2=-0.0758443809$ and time-shuffle $p=0.9306930693$.  The turnover audit similarly gave no positive residual extension beyond its controls: $R^2_{L2}=0.0557408516$, $R^2_{L3}=0.0502788722$, $\Delta R^2=-0.0054619794$, matched-bin $p=0.8855721393$, and recoded $p=1.0$ on $3290$ genes.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite rows show a controlled statistical decomposition from $Q$ to modeled $T$ to matched protein abundance $Y$; they do not establish a causal translation mechanism.  The $T$ coordinate is modeled tAI, not ribosome profiling, not position-resolved occupancy, and not direct translation efficiency from matched RPF and mRNA rows.  The protein readout is steady-state abundance, not isolated synthesis rate, turnover, localization, post-translational state, folding, or molecular activity.  Therefore a direct $Q_i\to Y$ association is not promoted unless the same finite surface also passes through the declared translation-readout coordinate.  No observed translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanistic intervention, organismal phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that measurement and a new controlled statistic is computed.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
Within the stated finite design, the claim is ready as a bounded cross-layer relation: the $B^*_Q6$ residual block, after amino-acid composition, organism-specific coding controls, GC3, length, and $M$-density baseline residualization, has a protein-abundance association that is partly mediated by modeled per-protein tAI in all twelve organism-specific joins.  The strongest finite fraction is yeast, $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041$, and the weakest is Sulfolobus solfataricus, $M^{QTP}=0.0185066856$.  This range is the result, not a defect: it records a finite mediation matrix rather than a population law.
