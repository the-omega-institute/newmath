\subsection{Translation-Mediated Protein-Abundance Readback for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface can be read through a translation-layer quantity before it is compared with measured protein abundance. The finite carrier is the table of triples $(q_i,T,P)$, where $q_i$ ranges over
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},
\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},
\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation coordinate $T$ is the per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T=\frac{\sum_c n_c w_c}{\sum_c n_c},
\end{aligned}
$$
with codon counts $n_c$ from the coding sequence and $w_c$ from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights. The protein readout $P$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts. The experiment therefore does not ask whether $q_i$ directly predicts a protein-level quantity. It asks whether the joint nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block has a nonzero part of its protein-abundance association that is removed when the modeled translation readout is placed between $Q$ and $P$.

\paragraph{Rows and rank.}
The joined panel contains twelve organism-specific finite tables. The usable protein counts are $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. In each organism the residualized design has $9$ $Q$ columns, rank $9$ for $\widetilde X$, rank $1$ for $\widetilde T$, and rank $10$ for $(X,T)$ after control residualization. The control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every organism-specific fit. The residual degrees of freedom after controls range from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in \emph{M. musculus}. Thus the reported mediation scores are not produced by an empty or rank-collapsed regression surface.

\paragraph{Controls and computed readout.}
The controls keep amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline outside the promoted coordinate set. Per-protein modeled tAI was computed for every joined organism. The tRNA all-copy totals were $86$, $581$, $8874$, $290$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $407$, $49$, $417$, $275$, and $47$ in the organism order listed above. The zero raw-$W$ fallback count was $3$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $1$ for \emph{E. coli}, $1$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $1$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}, and $0$ for the remaining eight organisms. This matters because the translation coordinate is a bounded modeled contact, not an observed ribosome-footprint matrix. A separate smaller modeled-tAI survival audit had only $3$ organisms, although it already showed $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$ and a maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$; that audit remains only a bounded modeled readout and not ribo-seq validation.

\paragraph{Mediation scalar.}
For each organism the finite statistic compares the total residualized $Q$--protein association
$$
\begin{aligned}
c=R^2(P\sim Q\mid Z)
\end{aligned}
$$
with the remaining direct term after conditioning on the modeled translation readout,
$$
\begin{aligned}
c'=R^2(P\sim Q\mid Z,T),
\end{aligned}
$$
and records
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{c-c'}{c}
\end{aligned}
$$
when the fraction is in the unit interval. All twelve organism tables satisfy this interval condition. The strongest fraction is in yeast, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$. The next largest fractions are \emph{D. melanogaster} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, \emph{E. coli} with $0.612922252793667$, \emph{B. subtilis} with $0.6084594553474505$, and \emph{C. elegans} with $0.4872268357227724$. Intermediate or smaller but still positive mediated fractions occur in \emph{D. rerio} with $0.3157053953614641$, human with $0.27206289160119157$, chicken with $0.1976903964724546$, rat with $0.17489681798543982$, mouse with $0.15488799907323988$, \emph{M. smegmatis} with $0.08397602919861291$, and \emph{S. solfataricus} with $0.01850668564589326$.

\paragraph{Organism-level residual magnitudes.}
The same calculation can be read through the residual energies. In yeast, $X$ explains $882.6526210803958$ units of residual protein-abundance energy and $5.671703404506426$ units of residual modeled-tAI energy; the direct $Q$ improvement after conditioning on $T$ is only $44.87838061554362$. In \emph{E. coli}, $X$ explains $432.89050065533684$ units on $P$ and $0.256128714706293$ on $T$, while the direct improvement after $T$ is $167.56227978068944$. In human, the corresponding values are $1370.6490494847067$, $0.3099459586758378$, and $997.7463057114725$. These numbers show a finite and reproducible attenuation pattern, but they also show why the claim must remain a statistical decomposition: the amount of $Q$ energy visible in the modeled tAI coordinate is small in several organisms even when the protein-abundance association is large.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The supported readback is therefore narrow. The data support a cross-layer statistical statement: in matched CDS/protein-abundance rows, after the declared sequence-composition and codon-baseline controls, the joint nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has a positive modeled-tAI-mediated component of its protein-abundance association in every organism tested. The positive component is not uniform in size; it ranges from $0.0011839678516385038$ indirect $R^2$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $0.28320534058057895$ in yeast. It is also not a statement about one universal sign for CUN/UUR, one taxon-specific mechanism, or one coordinate acting alone. The finite object is a controlled matrix-level attenuation pattern, not a population law.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No stronger biological conclusion follows from this table alone. Modeled tAI from tRNA gene-copy counts and wobble weights is not ribosome profiling. Conditioning on modeled tAI in cross-sectional CDS/protein-abundance rows is not a causal translation mechanism. Protein abundance is not isolated here from mRNA abundance, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, pathway context, or experimental abundance bias. Consequently the chapter does not claim translation realization in the measured-ribosome sense, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, causation, selection, or universality. Those claims require their own layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics. The admissible conclusion is only that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface has a positive, rank-sufficient, modeled-translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition on the reported finite rows.
