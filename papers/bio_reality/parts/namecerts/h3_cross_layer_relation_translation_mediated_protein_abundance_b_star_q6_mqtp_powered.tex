\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record studies a finite mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,T,P)$, where $q_i$ ranges over the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, $T$ is a per-protein modeled translation-adaptation readout, and $P$ is a matched protein-abundance readout. The coordinates used in the run were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation covariate was the per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g)=\frac{\sum_c n_{g,c}w_c}{\sum_c n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $w_c$ computed from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights. The protein readout was $P(g)=\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance after joining to real coding-sequence codon counts. The control surface contained amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline, with organism handled by separate organism-specific fits rather than by merging rows into a single unstratified law.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
For each organism the run decomposed the residual association between $X_Q$ and $P$ by conditioning on $T$. The scalar readback is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{R^2(P\sim X_Q\mid Z)-R^2(P\sim X_Q\mid Z,T)}{R^2(P\sim X_Q\mid Z)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The corresponding total residual $R^2(P\sim X_Q\mid Z)$ values were $0.061906245886660105$, $0.10217906758013379$, $0.04613529807085213$, $0.0770952887136557$, $0.10767526677985896$, $0.03980793596647631$, $0.04454565118019448$, $0.049827984062539195$, $0.03209009170649934$, $0.02763998603083845$, $0.29837624994140827$, and $0.06397514251295629$, respectively. \paragraph{Organism-level readback.}
The strongest mediation fraction was yeast, but it should not be read as a species-general law. Other organisms have lower mediated fractions and different total residual association magnitudes: mouse has a total residual $R^2$ of $0.049827984062539195$ with $M^{QTP}=0.15488799907323988$, human has $0.04454565118019448$ with $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$, and \emph{S. solfataricus} has $0.06397514251295629$ with $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$. The finite conclusion is therefore not that a particular codon-family direction, organism sign, or single residual coordinate has universal force. It is that, on this finite joined surface, conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI absorbs a positive portion of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual-to-protein-abundance association in every organism-specific panel tested.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible claim is bounded. A direct $q_i\to P$ association is not treated as protein realization unless the same analysis includes a surviving $q_i\to T$ step or an explicitly modeled translation-adaptation conditioning surface. The $T$ used here is modeled tAI from tRNA gene-copy and wobble-weight data, not ribosome profiling. The protein readout is steady-state abundance, not folding, localization, turnover, post-translational state, molecular function, or organismal phenotype. Consequently this chapter does not establish observed translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, selection, or universality. Each of those would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic. The finite result is a ranked mediation matrix over matched coding sequences, modeled tAI, and PAXdb abundance; its positive entries remain statistical cross-layer associations under controls.
