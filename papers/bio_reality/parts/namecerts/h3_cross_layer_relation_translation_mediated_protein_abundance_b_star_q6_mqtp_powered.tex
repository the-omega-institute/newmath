\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{sec:biorality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
The record tested here is a finite cross-layer surface with rows indexed by triples $(q_i,T,Y)$, where $q_i$ ranges over the nine computed $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, $T$ is a per-protein modeled translation-adaptation readout, and $Y$ is a matched protein-abundance readout.  The residual coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{Q}_{Q6} = \{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation coordinate is not a ribosome-profiling measurement: in this finite run it is the per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as $\sum_{c} n_c w_c/\sum_c n_c$ from real CDS codon counts, GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy totals, and dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein coordinate is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb abundance after joining to real CDS codon counts.  The fitted surface therefore asks whether a residual-to-protein association is partly absorbed by the modeled translation-adaptation coordinate after the declared controls have been residualized.

\paragraph{Rows and organism scope.}
Six organism panels were joined.  The finite row counts are $14410$ proteins for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The associated tRNA all-copy totals used in the modeled tAI readout are respectively $8874$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $49$, and $275$.  The zero raw-weight fallback count was $0$ for zebrafish, chicken, human, and yeast, and $1$ for the bacterial and mycobacterial panels.  These values define the finite contact actually used by the chapter: it is a same-panel codon-count, modeled-tAI, and measured-protein-abundance join, not a perturbation experiment and not a direct observation of translation dynamics.

\paragraph{Residualized design.}
For every organism, the control matrix had $24$ columns with residualized rank $23$.  The residualized $B^{*}_{Q6}$ design had rank $9$, the translation readout had rank $1$, and the joint $(Q,T)$ design had rank $10$.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls were $14387$, $3716$, $12395$, $19030$, $4106$, and $5792$ in the same organism order.  Thus the mediation calculation is rank-admissible for this finite data table: each panel keeps the nine-dimensional residual block and the one-dimensional translation coordinate separable after amino-acid composition, organism scope, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched abundance controls are accounted for.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
The scalar reported for each organism is the fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} = \frac{R^2(Q\to P)-R^2(Q\to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to P)},
\end{aligned}
$$
computed only when the numerator and denominator remain in a nondegenerate interval.  The total residual-to-protein $R^2$ values were $0.04613529807085213$ for zebrafish, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.03980793596647631$ for chicken, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.03209009170649934$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast.  After conditioning on modeled tAI, the direct $R^2$ values were $0.031570135553274765$, $0.04167869969498872$, $0.031938289322513524$, $0.03242643251185274$, $0.029395293228368185$, and $0.0151709093608293$.  The corresponding indirect differences were $0.014565162517577362$, $0.06599656708487024$, $0.007869646643962787$, $0.012119218668341744$, $0.0026947984781311544$, and $0.28320534058057895$.

\paragraph{Finite readback.}
The mediated fractions are all positive and lie in the unit interval:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.3157053953614641,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{ecoli}} &= 0.612922252793667,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{gallus}} &= 0.1976903964724546,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.27206289160119157,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{mycobacterium}} &= 0.08397602919861291,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
The strongest finite mediation fraction in this table is yeast, where the direct residual-to-protein association falls from $0.29837624994140827$ to $0.0151709093608293$ after conditioning on modeled tAI.  The weakest positive fraction is \emph{M. smegmatis}, where the indirect difference is $0.0026947984781311544$.  The chapter therefore reads back a bounded statistical pattern: in these six finite panels, a nonzero part of the joint $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-to-protein-abundance association is routed through the modeled translation-adaptation coordinate.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The result is a controlled association statement only.  A direct $q_i\to P$ association is not promoted unless the same finite table also contains the translation coordinate and the conditioning calculation.  Even then, modeled tAI is an adaptation-weight readout derived from tRNA gene-copy and wobble weights; it is not ribosome occupancy, ribosome profiling, translation efficiency measured by RPF over mRNA, or a causal intervention.  Protein abundance is also not isolated from mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, complex membership, or pathway context.  Consequently, this chapter does not claim translation mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, selection, or universality.  Those claims would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.  The finite claim made here is narrower: across six organism panels and nine residual coordinates, modeled translation adaptation absorbs a positive fraction of the residual-to-protein-abundance association under the stated controls.
