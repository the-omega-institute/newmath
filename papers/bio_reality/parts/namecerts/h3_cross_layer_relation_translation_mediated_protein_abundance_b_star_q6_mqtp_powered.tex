\subsection{Translation-mediated protein-abundance readback for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} This record tests a finite cross-layer surface whose rows join three kinds of measured or computed quantities: a $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual vector $Q$, a per-protein translation readout $T$, and a matched protein-abundance readout $P$. The residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The tested translation coordinate is per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$\sum_{c} n_{c}w_{c}/\sum_{c}n_{c}$,
where $n_{c}$ is the CDS codon count and $w_{c}$ is the tAI weight computed from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights. The downstream protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb-style protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts. The tested claim is therefore not a free $Q\to P$ association. It is a controlled finite matrix in which a residual-to-protein signal is allowed to enter this layer only through a declared $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition.

\paragraph{Controls and rank.} In the main finite run, all organism blocks used $24$ control columns before reading the residual and translation variables. The controls include amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline; the residualized control rank is $23$ in every listed organism block. The residual coordinate matrix has rank $9$, the modeled-tAI column has rank $1$, and the joint residual-plus-tAI matrix has rank $10$ in every organism block, so the fitted mediation scalar is not produced by an immediate rank collapse. The finite organism panel contains $12$ joined CDS/protein/tRNA blocks: $4042$ rows in \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, $11661$ in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ in \textit{Danio rerio}, $13295$ in \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ in \textit{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ in human, $19395$ in mouse, $4129$ in \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ in rat, $5815$ in budding yeast, and $1656$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. The tRNA-copy totals for the same blocks range from $47$ in \textit{Sulfolobus} to $8874$ in zebrafish, and the zero raw-weight geometric-mean fallback counts are bounded at $0$, $1$, or $3$ depending on organism.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.} The finite readback compares the total residual-to-protein coefficient of determination $c=R^{2}(P\sim Q\mid Z)$ with the residual direct term after conditioning on modeled translation, $c'=R^{2}(P\sim Q\mid Z,T)$. The reported mediated fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} = \frac{c-c'}{c}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All $12$ organism blocks have $M^{QTP}$ inside $[0,1]$. The strongest mediated fraction is in budding yeast: $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$. Other large finite fractions include \textit{Drosophila melanogaster} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 with $0.612922252793667$, and \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168 with $0.6084594553474505$. Intermediate cases include \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans} at $0.4872268357227724$, zebrafish at $0.3157053953614641$, human at $0.27206289160119157$, chicken at $0.1976903964724546$, rat at $0.17489681798543982$, and mouse at $0.15488799907323988$. The weakest but still positive finite fractions are \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis} at $0.08397602919861291$ and \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus} at $0.01850668564589326$.

\paragraph{Energy-scale diagnostics.} The residualized energy diagnostics are consistent with a bounded statistical decomposition rather than a qualitative biological law. In budding yeast, $X$ explains $882.6526210803958$ units of protein-abundance residual energy and $5.671703404506426$ units of modeled-tAI residual energy, while $T$ explains $941.8858618613751$ units of protein-abundance residual energy and the direct $X$ improvement after $T$ is only $44.87838061554362$. In human, the same finite audit gives $X$-on-$Y$ energy $1370.6490494847067$, $X$-on-$T$ energy $0.3099459586758378$, $T$-on-$Y$ energy $850.7864327764183$, and direct $X$ improvement after $T$ equal to $997.7463057114725$. In mouse, the direct residual term remains large after $T$, $2055.2519066931027$, even though the mediated fraction is positive; this is why the chapter records a mediation scalar, not a claim that modeled translation explains all protein-abundance variation.

\paragraph{Relation to the modeled translation-contact check.} A separate modeled-tAI survival run verifies that the same $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate family can be coupled to organism-scoped modeled tAI weights. That contact used $3$ organisms, a $61$-codon sense space, a coupling threshold of $0.01$, and found $81$ entries above threshold, with maximum absolute score $3801961.031607896$. The run also records the necessary limitation: $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival, and this is modeled-tAI bounded coupling rather than ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation. The present chapter therefore uses the modeled-tAI contact only as a finite translation readout in a cross-sectional decomposition. It does not convert modeled tAI into observed ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite checks.} The yeast structure-mediation audit gives a useful boundary comparison. There, the same nine-coordinate residual family has $R^{2}_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$ for protein abundance after controls, $R^{2}_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$ after a $5'$ CDS MFE structure proxy, $R^{2}_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$ after measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, and $R^{2}_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$ after all three. The structure-only fraction is only $0.003284564452662019$, and the structure-incremental fraction is $0.0005507778820193332$, with right-tail permutation values $0.027972027972027972$ for $S_{Q}$ structure association and $0.1258741258741259$ for the incremental structure term. A turnover audit gives another boundary: with $3290$ genes, adding the residual family beyond expression, TE, pLDDT, and N-end-style controls has $\Delta R^{2}=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$. These adjacent checks show why the protein-abundance mediation row should not be promoted to structure, turnover, function, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.} The finite result is positive for a bounded statistical readback: every joined organism block reports a nonnegative indirect term $c-c'$ and an in-range $M^{QTP}$ fraction, with the largest organism-specific case in yeast and the smallest in \textit{Sulfolobus}. The statement supported here is precisely that the $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual family has a controlled association with matched protein abundance that is partly attenuated by conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI. The statement not supported here is equally important. No translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy, folding pathway, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, perturbation rescue, evolutionary selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and the same controls are recomputed at that layer.
