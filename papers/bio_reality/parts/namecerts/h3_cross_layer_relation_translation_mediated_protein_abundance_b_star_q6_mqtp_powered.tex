\subsection{Modeled-translation mediation of protein-abundance residuals}
\label{subsec:bior-reality-q6-modeled-translation-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,T,P)$: $q_i$ ranges over the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, $T$ is a per-protein modeled translation-adaptation readout, and $P$ is matched steady-state protein abundance.  The nine residual coordinates used in the run are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{Q6}=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout $T$ is the mean modeled tAI per protein, computed as codon-count-weighted tAI over the coding sequence using GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The readout $P$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The fitted object is not a direct $q_i\to P$ promotion: it asks whether the joint nine-dimensional residual block has a positive protein-abundance association that is reduced after conditioning on the modeled translation readout.

\paragraph{Rows and rank.}
The experiment joined twelve organism panels.  The finite row counts were $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} MC2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  In every organism, the residualized design had $9$ residual coordinates, $23$ control rank, $24$ control columns, $\mathrm{rank}(T)=1$, $\mathrm{rank}(X)=9$, and $\mathrm{rank}(X,T)=10$, so the reported mediation scalar is not produced by a missing residual degree of freedom.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls ranged from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in mouse.

\paragraph{Controls.}
The residualization stage controlled amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The reported decomposition therefore reads as a controlled codon-residual, modeled-tAI, protein-abundance calculation on finite matched rows.  It does not contain measured ribosome-footprint rows and it does not isolate protein abundance from matched mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, or pathway context.  Those omissions are part of the finite claim boundary rather than a secondary caveat.

\paragraph{Mediation values.}
For each organism the run reports the total residual-to-protein fit $c=R^2(Q,P)$, the direct fit after modeled-tAI conditioning $c'=R^2(Q,P\mid T)$, and the mediated component $c-c'$.  The scalar mediation fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{c-c'}{c}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All twelve reported fractions lie in $[0,1]$.  The largest value is in yeast, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  The next largest values are \emph{D. melanogaster} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, \emph{E. coli} with $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$, and \emph{B. subtilis} with $M^{QTP}=0.6084594553474505$.  Intermediate values were observed in worm, zebrafish, human, chicken, rat, mouse, and mycobacterium: respectively $0.4872268357227724$, $0.3157053953614641$, $0.27206289160119157$, $0.1976903964724546$, $0.17489681798543982$, $0.15488799907323988$, and $0.08397602919861291$.  The archaeal panel was the weakest positive case, with \emph{S. solfataricus} giving $c=0.06397514251295629$, $c'=0.06279117466131778$, $c-c'=0.0011839678516385038$, and $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$.

\paragraph{Concrete organism readbacks.}
The yeast panel is the strongest finite row in the table: $5815$ proteins were joined, the residualized protein-abundance energy was $2958.1865890925337$, the modeled-tAI residual energy was $6.90537888244409$, the residual block explained $882.6526210803958$ energy on protein abundance, and the modeled-tAI readout explained $941.8858618613751$ energy on the same abundance target.  The mouse panel shows a different shape: $19395$ proteins were joined, $c=0.049827984062539195$, $c'=0.04211022731323921$, and the mediated component was $0.007717756749299982$, even though the residual block explained $2431.9284360407714$ protein-abundance energy.  The \emph{E. coli} panel gives a compact bacterial case with $3739$ joined proteins, $c=0.10767526677985896$, $c'=0.04167869969498872$, and $c-c'=0.06599656708487024$.  These finite rows support a bounded statistical statement: modeled translation adaptation often absorbs a positive part of the joint $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual association with matched protein abundance.

\paragraph{Earlier modeled-tAI contact.}
A separate modeled-tAI survival contact computed the same residual family over three organisms and found nonzero bounded coupling: $81$ entries exceeded the $0.01$ coupling threshold, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}=3801961.031607896$.  That earlier contact used only $3$ organisms, so it explicitly remained underpowered for a species-powered survival law.  The present twelve-organism protein-abundance run should be read as a larger finite mediation audit using modeled tAI and PAXdb abundance, not as a measured ribosome-profiling validation.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following chapter-local claim.  Across twelve organism-matched CDS, tRNA-copy, modeled-tAI, and PAXdb protein-abundance panels, the joint nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has positive protein-abundance association, and a positive portion of that association is absorbed by conditioning on per-protein modeled tAI in every reported organism.  The claim is strongest for yeast and several bacterial or invertebrate panels, weaker for mammalian and archaeal panels, and always remains a statistical decomposition on joined finite rows.  No measured translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the corresponding controlled statistic is computed.
