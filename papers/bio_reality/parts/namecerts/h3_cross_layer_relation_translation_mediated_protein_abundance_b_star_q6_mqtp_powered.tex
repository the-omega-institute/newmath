\subsection{Modeled translation mediation of B$^*_{Q6}$ residuals into protein abundance}\label{sec:bq6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer surface whose rows are matched coding sequences with three measured or computed fields: a B$^*_{Q6}$ residual vector $q$, a translation readout $T$, and a protein-abundance readout $Y$.  The residual vector has nine coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,\mathrm{vs}\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,\mathrm{vs}\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,\mathrm{vs}\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,\mathrm{vs}\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,\mathrm{vs}\,ACY},\ f3_{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation readout is per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as the codon-count weighted average of organism-specific tAI weights derived from GtRNAdb all-tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\ ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The joined panel contains $107{,}957$ protein rows across ten organisms: $4{,}042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. \emph{subtilis} str. 168, $11{,}661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14{,}410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13{,}295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3{,}739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12{,}418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19{,}053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19{,}395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4{,}129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5{,}815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.

\paragraph{Controlled decomposition.}
For each organism, the model first residualizes the nine-dimensional residual block $X_Q$, the scalar translation readout $T$, and the protein-abundance response $Y$ against a control matrix $Z$.  The controls include amino-acid composition, organism-local sequence baselines, GC3, CDS length, and M-density baseline; the finite runs report $24$ control columns, with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in every organism.  After this residualization, the statistical question is whether the direct residual-to-protein association is reduced when the modeled translation readout is inserted between them.  In compact notation, the reported mediated fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} = \frac{R^2(Q\to P)-R^2(Q\to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to P)},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $P$ denotes the matched protein-abundance response and all terms are computed after the stated controls.  This is a finite regression decomposition, not an intervention, perturbation, or mechanistic claim.

\paragraph{Rank and row sufficiency.}
The rank checks are nondegenerate for the finite panel.  In every organism, the residualized residual block has rank $9$, the residualized translation scalar has rank $1$, and the combined residualized block $(X_Q,T)$ has rank $10$.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $4{,}019$, $11{,}638$, $14{,}387$, $13{,}272$, $3{,}716$, $12{,}395$, $19{,}030$, $19{,}372$, $4{,}106$, and $5{,}792$ in the organism order listed above.  Thus the mediation surface is not the earlier three-organism modeled-tAI rank boundary: the earlier modeled translation-survival check used only $3$ organisms and explicitly did not provide powered survival, whereas this protein-abundance decomposition is row-powered at the per-CDS/per-protein level.

\paragraph{Observed finite values.}
All ten organisms have $M^{QTP}$ inside the unit interval.  The mediated fractions are $0.6085$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.4872$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $0.3157$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.6165$ for \emph{D. melanogaster}, $0.6129$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.1977$ for \emph{G. gallus}, $0.2721$ for human, $0.1549$ for mouse, $0.0840$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0.9492$ for yeast.  The corresponding total residual-to-protein $R^2$ values are $0.0619$, $0.1022$, $0.0461$, $0.0771$, $0.1077$, $0.0398$, $0.0445$, $0.0498$, $0.0321$, and $0.2984$.  After conditioning on modeled tAI, the direct $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)$ values are $0.0242$, $0.0524$, $0.0316$, $0.0296$, $0.0417$, $0.0319$, $0.0324$, $0.0421$, $0.0294$, and $0.0152$.  The largest finite mediated fraction is therefore the yeast value $0.9492$, where $R^2(Q\to P)$ falls from $0.2984$ to $0.0152$ after the modeled translation scalar is included.  The smallest finite mediated fraction is the \emph{M. smegmatis} value $0.0840$, where the total $R^2$ is $0.0321$ and the conditioned direct $R^2$ is $0.0294$.

\paragraph{Translation contact used in the test.}
The translation coordinate in this chapter is modeled tAI, not measured ribosome occupancy.  The per-protein tAI computation used organism-specific tRNA-copy totals of $86$, $581$, $8{,}874$, $290$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $407$, $49$, and $275$ in the organism order listed above.  The zero raw-weight fallback counts were $3$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $1$ for \emph{E. coli}, $1$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0$ for the other seven organisms.  The earlier bounded translation-contact run showed nonzero modeled-tAI coupling above threshold, with $81$ entries above $0.01$ and maximum absolute entry $3{,}801{,}961.0316$, but that run was restricted to three organisms and was explicitly not a ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded cross-layer statistical statement: within these joined CDS/protein rows, the nine-coordinate B$^*_{Q6}$ residual block retains a protein-abundance association, and a modeled translation-efficiency scalar explains a positive fraction of that association in every tested organism after the declared controls.  The correct readback is therefore a controlled $Q\to T\to P$ mediation matrix, with $T$ instantiated here by modeled per-protein tAI and $P$ by matched protein abundance.  A direct $Q\to P$ association is not enough for protein realization unless the translation step is present and evaluated; in this run that step is modeled rather than ribosome-measured.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No claim of translation mechanism, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, selection, perturbation response, rescue, or universality follows from these rows alone.  The contact used for $T$ is a codon-adaptation model based on tRNA gene-copy counts and wobble weights, and the response $Y$ is steady-state abundance rather than synthesis rate, turnover, localization, post-translational state, folded structure, or functional activity.  Any statement at those layers requires an additional layer-matched contact and a separate controlled statistic.  The present chapter only licenses the narrower conclusion that the finite row panel contains positive, controlled, modeled-translation mediation of B$^*_{Q6}$ residual association with matched protein abundance.
