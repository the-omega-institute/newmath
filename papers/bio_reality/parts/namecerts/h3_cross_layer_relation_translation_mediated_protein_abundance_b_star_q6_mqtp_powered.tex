\subsection{Translation-Mediated Protein-Abundance Surface for $B^*_{Q6}$}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} This record tests a finite cross-layer surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,T_j,P_k)$. The $q_i$ coordinates are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual axes $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The translation coordinate $T$ is not a ribosome-footprint measurement here. It is the per-protein modeled tAI value
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g)=\frac{\sum_c n_c(g)w_c}{\sum_c n_c(g)},
\end{aligned}
$$
computed from codon counts, GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts, and dos Reis wobble weights. The protein coordinate $P$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts. The finite panel covers twelve organism-specific joins: $4042$ rows in \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $11661$ in \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ in \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ in \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ in \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ in \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ in \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ in \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.

\paragraph{Controlled decomposition.} The row model residualizes against an intercept, amino-acid composition, organism-scoped sequence controls, $GC3$, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline before reading a $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition. The reported design is rank-sufficient in every organism: after the control block, $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(T)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(X,T)=10$ in each organism, with control-column count $24$ and control rank $23$. The scalar reported for the mediated share is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{R^2(Q\to P)-R^2(Q\to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to P)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All twelve organism-specific $M^{QTP}$ values lie in $[0,1]$: $0.608459$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.487227$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $0.315705$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.616517$ for \emph{D. melanogaster}, $0.612922$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.197690$ for chicken, $0.272063$ for human, $0.154888$ for mouse, $0.083976$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.174897$ for rat, $0.949155$ for yeast, and $0.018507$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}. The corresponding total-to-direct decompositions are concrete: in yeast, $R^2(Q\to P)=0.298376$ and $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.015171$, giving an indirect component $0.283205$; in \emph{E. coli}, $R^2(Q\to P)=0.107675$ and $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.041679$, giving $0.065997$; in human, $R^2(Q\to P)=0.044546$ and $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.032426$, giving $0.012119$.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.} The broader modeled-tAI survival contact computed the same nine residual coordinates against a finite modeled tAI matrix in three organisms. It found $81$ entries above the bounded-contact threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}=3801961.031608$, but it also records that $3$ organisms are not enough for a powered survival claim requiring at least $40$ matched species. The present chapter therefore uses modeled tAI only as an explicitly bounded translation-layer covariate. It is not a measured ribosome-occupancy surface, not a TE matrix from ribosome profiling, and not evidence that the codon residuals have entered a physical translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance readback.} The finite result is a statistical attenuation claim, not a causal claim. A direct $Q\to P$ association is not read as protein realization unless the $Q\to T$ step is retained as the intervening readout being conditioned on. The largest attenuation occurs in yeast, where $M^{QTP}=0.949155$ on $5815$ joined proteins and the direct residual signal after modeled tAI drops to $R^2=0.015171$. The smallest attenuation occurs in \emph{S. solfataricus}, where $M^{QTP}=0.018507$ on $1656$ rows and the direct component remains $R^2=0.062791$ against a total $R^2=0.063975$. These two endpoints show why the chapter is stored as a finite organism-indexed matrix rather than a single universal sign. The data support the existence of nonzero mediated statistical components in the registered rows, while preserving organism-level heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Related finite checks.} The yeast structure-mediation audit supplies a local comparison against a separate 5-prime CDS MFE proxy on $4759$ joined genes. There, $R^2(Q\to P)=0.271552$, $R^2(Q\to P\mid S)=0.270660$, $R^2(Q\to P\mid T,M)=0.137740$, and $R^2(Q\to P\mid T,M,S)=0.137590$; the structure-only fraction is $0.003285$, the structure-incremental fraction is $0.000551$, and the permutation $p$-values are $0.027972$ for $S_Q$ structure association and $0.125874$ for the incremental structure fraction. This comparison keeps the present protein-abundance mediation separate from folded-structure interpretation. The cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit is also bounded: with $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species, it reports $\Delta R^2=0.032505$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.002148$, null95 $\Delta R^2=0.054272$, and positive-selection-imprint $=\mathrm{false}$. Thus the protein-abundance matrix is not promoted to an evolutionary selection law.

\paragraph{Local boundary.} The admissible readback is only this: finite $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, after the stated controls, show organism-indexed attenuation of the residual-to-protein-abundance association when modeled per-protein tAI is inserted as the intermediate coordinate. Nothing in this chapter by itself establishes measured translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbation response, causal mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality. Each of those claims would require its own layer-matched measurement surface and its own controlled statistic before it could be stated.
