\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation for $B^*_{Q6}$}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
The finite row for this record evaluates a controlled cross-layer surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,T,P)$: $q_i$ is one of the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, $T$ is a per-protein modeled translation-adaptation readout, and $P$ is matched protein abundance. The nine residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The translation coordinate used here is not a ribosome-footprint measurement: it is the per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as codon-count-weighted tAI weights from GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights. The protein readout is $\log_{10}$ abundance in ppm from PAXdb joined to real CDS codon counts.

\paragraph{Controls and admissible readback.}
The fitted rows residualize against a control block containing an intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; the reported organism-level diagnostics have $24$ control columns, rank $23$ for the control matrix, rank $9$ for the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ block, rank $1$ for the residualized modeled-tAI block, and rank $10$ after adjoining $B^*_{Q6}$ to modeled tAI. Thus the admissible readback is a statistical decomposition of a joint nine-coordinate residual block, not a claim that synonymous coordinates directly realize protein state. In the notation of the row, the total residual-to-protein association $c$ and the direct association $c'$ after conditioning on $T$ define
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{c-c'}{c},\\
 c &= R^2(Q\to P\mid Z),\\
 c' &= R^2(Q\to P\mid Z,T).
\end{aligned}
$$
All twelve reported organism entries have $M^{QTP}$ inside $[0,1]$ and have sufficient residual rank for this finite decomposition.

\paragraph{Organism-level finite rows.}
The joined row counts are substantial but heterogeneous: $4042$ proteins for \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, $11661$ for \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \textit{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for budding yeast, and $1656$ for \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. The strongest mediated fraction appears in budding yeast, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$. Other high fractions are \textit{Drosophila} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, \textit{E. coli} with $0.612922252793667$, \textit{Bacillus} with $0.6084594553474505$, and \textit{C. elegans} with $0.4872268357227724$. The low end is also explicit: \textit{Sulfolobus} has $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$, \textit{Mycobacterium} has $0.08397602919861291$, rat has $0.17489681798543982$, and mouse has $0.15488799907323988$.

\paragraph{Residual-energy audit.}
The organism diagnostics separate modeled-tAI association with protein abundance from the residual block association. In yeast, $X$ explains $882.6526210803958$ energy units on $Y$, while modeled tAI explains $941.8858618613751$ on $Y$ and the direct $X$ improvement after $T$ is $44.87838061554362$. In human, $X$ explains $1370.6490494847067$ on $Y$, modeled tAI explains $850.7864327764183$, and the direct $X$ improvement after $T$ is $997.7463057114725$. In mouse, the corresponding values are $2431.9284360407714$, $923.4025869376133$, and $2055.2519066931027$. These rows show that the mediation scalar is not a universal monotone law across taxa; it is an organism-indexed finite regression decomposition whose magnitude depends on the joined CDS, tRNA-copy, tAI, and abundance surface.

\paragraph{Relation to nearby finite checks.}
The same coordinate family has a bounded modeled-tAI survival contact in a smaller three-organism table: $81$ entries exceed the coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}=3801961.031607896$, but that table explicitly reports only $3$ organisms and refuses powered survival without at least $40$ matched species. A separate yeast structure-mediation row reports $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$, with a structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$ and structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507778820193332$. The structural-order axis audit identifies $f3\_stress$ as the only significant coordinate, with weighted held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip $p=0.03125$. The evolutionary imprint row remains negative for the strong selection-imprint claim: $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$, null95 $\Delta R^2=0.05427157066522958$, and $80$ orthogroups across $240$ rows. The turnover residual row is also negative after added controls, with $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, and $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The ready claim is therefore narrow. The finite rows support a protein-abundance association whose direct residual component is reduced after conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI in all reported organism entries, with the largest reduction in yeast and weaker reductions in several bacteria, archaea, and vertebrates. This is a controlled statistical mediation surface for $B^*_{Q6}$, modeled translation adaptation, and matched protein abundance. It does not by itself establish observed ribosome occupancy, translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality. Any such stronger assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact: measured ribosome profiling or TE for translation dynamics, structural or physical assays for folding and admissibility, perturbation or rescue data for mechanism, and functional or phenotype assays for biological effect.
