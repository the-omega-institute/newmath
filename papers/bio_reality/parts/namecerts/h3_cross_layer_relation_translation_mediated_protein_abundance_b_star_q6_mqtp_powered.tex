\subsection{Translation-Mediated Protein-Abundance Surface for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}\label{sec:biorreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite cross-layer table whose rows join a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ codon-residual vector, a translation-side readout, and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The computed residual block has nine coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The translation readout $T$ is the per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T=\frac{\sum_c n_c w_c}{\sum_c n_c},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_c$ is the observed CDS codon count and $w_c$ is the codon tAI weight computed from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein readout $Y$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  This makes the table a finite statistical readback of $Q\to T\to Y$, not a perturbation experiment and not a direct observation of ribosome motion.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank.}
The powered run joined twelve organism-level CDS/protein/tRNA surfaces.  The row counts were $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  Each organism passed the local rank check: $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X,\widetilde T)=10$, after residualizing the declared control block.  The control rank was $23$ in every organism, with $24$ control columns before rank reduction.  Residual degrees of freedom ranged from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in mouse, so the computed mediation scalar is not the three-organism rank-degenerate surface seen in the smaller modeled-tAI survival contact.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
For each organism the run reports a total controlled $Q\to Y$ fit, a remaining direct fit after conditioning on $T$, and the difference
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{R^2(Q\to Y)-R^2(Q\to Y\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to Y)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All twelve reported fractions lie in the unit interval.  The largest mediated fraction occurs in yeast, where $R^2(Q\to Y)=0.29837624994140827$, $R^2(Q\to Y\mid T)=0.0151709093608293$, the indirect component is $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  The next largest fractions are $0.6165167748739756$ in fly, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{E. coli}, and $0.6084594553474505$ in \emph{B. subtilis}.  The weakest positive fractions are $0.08397602919861291$ in \emph{M. smegmatis} and $0.01850668564589326$ in \emph{S. solfataricus}.  Thus the finite matrix contains positive modeled-tAI-conditioned attenuation in every joined organism, but with large organism-to-organism variation.

\paragraph{Energy diagnostics.}
The same computation records the residualized energy geometry.  In yeast, $X$ explained $882.6526210803958$ units of $Y$ energy, $T$ explained $941.8858618613751$, and the direct $X$ improvement after conditioning on $T$ was $44.87838061554362$.  In human, the corresponding values were $1370.6490494847067$, $850.7864327764183$, and $997.7463057114725$; in mouse they were $2431.9284360407714$, $923.4025869376133$, and $2055.2519066931027$.  The bacterial and archaeal endpoints show a different scale: \emph{E. coli} had $X$-on-$Y$ energy $432.89050065533684$, $T$-on-$Y$ energy $582.5912438239341$, and direct-after-$T$ energy $167.56227978068944$, while \emph{S. solfataricus} had $62.74436180679531$, $1.756466189204982$, and $61.58317162678476$.  These numbers are the reason the readback is kept as a finite controlled decomposition rather than compressed into a universal sign claim.

\paragraph{Translation contact status.}
The same research line contains a smaller modeled-tAI survival matrix over three organisms.  That contact computed all standard amino-acid tRNA families for human, yeast, and \emph{E. coli} K12, used a coupling threshold of $0.01$, and found $81$ entries above threshold with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  It also records that three organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that the readout is modeled tAI from tRNA gene copy and wobble weights, not ribosome profiling.  The present twelve-organism protein-abundance table resolves the rank problem for the modeled-tAI mediation scalar, but it still inherits the translation-contact limitation: $T$ is a modeled adaptation readout, not an observed ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency measurement.

\paragraph{Local comparison with adjacent protein rows.}
The yeast structure-mediation run gives a useful scale check.  On a five-way yeast join of $4759$ genes, the controlled $Q\to$ protein-abundance association had $R^2=0.2715520808117803$, while conditioning on measured translation efficiency plus mRNA stability reduced it to $0.13773967696311287$ and adding the 5-prime CDS MFE proxy changed it only to $0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the structure-incremental fraction was $0.0005507778820193332$, and the right-tail permutation $p$ value for the structure-incremental fraction was $0.1258741258741259$.  A separate dynamic mass-balance run with $120$ genes and $718$ intervals had $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628201518571$, $R^2_{L1}=0.996410628851051$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443377743347$, $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$, and $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$.  A turnover audit over $3290$ genes likewise reported $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  These neighboring rows support a conservative interpretation: the protein-abundance mediation surface is real as a fitted finite table, but it does not by itself settle turnover, structure, dynamic mass balance, or causal execution.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The admissible statement is therefore narrow.  In the joined twelve-organism surface, the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has a positive controlled association with PAXdb protein abundance, and a positive fraction of that association is absorbed by conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI in every organism.  The strongest finite entry is yeast with $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$; the weakest is \emph{S. solfataricus} with $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$.  This supports a bounded cross-layer relation of the form $Q\to T\to Y$ for modeled tAI and matched protein abundance under the recorded controls and rank checks.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No stronger biological reading follows from this table alone.  Modeled tAI is not ribosome profiling; PAXdb abundance is not a matched causal protein-production assay; and conditioning in cross-sectional rows is not a mechanism.  The finite result does not establish observed translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, selection, rescue, perturbation response, turnover control, localization, post-translational state, or universality.  Any such claim requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  A direct $Q\to Y$ association is also not accepted as protein realization unless the corresponding $Q\to T$ step is present and the downstream attenuation is evaluated on the joined surface.
