\subsection{Modeled translation mediation of protein abundance}
\label{subsec:q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record considered here is the finite surface of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ in which $q_i$ ranges over the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, $t_j$ is a translation-layer readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout.  The realized table uses a single translation coordinate per organism, namely per-protein modeled tAI, and a single abundance coordinate, namely $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb rows joined to real CDS codon counts.  The nine residual coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each organism, the design residualizes against $Z$ with $24$ control columns before estimating the joint nine-dimensional residual-to-abundance association and its attenuation after the modeled tAI coordinate is conditioned on.  The controls include amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline; the rank audit reports $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, $\mathrm{rank}(X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(T)=1$, and $\mathrm{rank}(X,T)=10$ in every organism listed below, with powered-rank sufficiency marked true for each joined table.

\paragraph{Translation coordinate.}
The translation coordinate is not a ribosome-profiling observation.  It is the per-protein mean modeled tAI
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_g=\frac{\sum_c n_{g,c}w_c}{\sum_c n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{g,c}$ is the codon count for protein $g$ and $w_c$ is computed from GtRNAdb all-tRNA gene-copy counts using the dos Reis wobble weights.  The finite tRNA-copy contacts cover $12$ organism tables: Bacillus subtilis subsp. subtilis str. 168 with $4042$ joined proteins and $86$ all-tRNA copies, Caenorhabditis elegans with $11661$ and $581$, Danio rerio with $14410$ and $8874$, Drosophila melanogaster with $13295$ and $290$, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 with $3739$ joined proteins and an Escherichia coli tRNA table of $90$ all-tRNA copies, Gallus gallus with $12418$ and $288$, Homo sapiens with $19053$ and $432$, Mus musculus with $19395$ and $407$, Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155 with $4129$ and $49$, Rattus norvegicus with $13322$ and $417$, Saccharomyces cerevisiae with $5815$ and $275$, and Sulfolobus solfataricus with $1656$ and $47$.  Zero raw tAI weights were filled by a geometric-mean fallback in four tables: $3$ codons for Bacillus subtilis, $1$ for Escherichia coli, $1$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, and $1$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus; the other eight tables required no such fallback.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
For each organism, write $X$ for the residualized nine-coordinate $Q$ matrix, $T$ for residualized modeled tAI, and $Y$ for residualized protein abundance.  The recorded scalar compares the total residual association $c=R^2(Y\sim X)$ with the direct residual association after the translation coordinate has been conditioned on, $c'=R^2(Y\sim X\mid T)$.  The mediated fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}=\frac{c-c'}{c},
\end{aligned}
$$
when the denominator is nonzero and the resulting value lies in the unit interval.  In the present run every organism has $0\leq M^{QTP}\leq 1$.  The largest value is in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  The next tier includes Drosophila melanogaster with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 with $0.612922252793667$, Bacillus subtilis with $0.6084594553474505$, and Caenorhabditis elegans with $0.4872268357227724$.  The middle and lower fractions are Danio rerio at $0.3157053953614641$, Homo sapiens at $0.27206289160119157$, Gallus gallus at $0.1976903964724546$, Rattus norvegicus at $0.17489681798543982$, Mus musculus at $0.15488799907323988$, Mycobacterium smegmatis at $0.08397602919861291$, and Sulfolobus solfataricus at $0.01850668564589326$.

\paragraph{Row-level readout.}
The strongest total protein-abundance residual association in this table is the yeast row, where $X$ explains energy $882.6526210803958$ on $Y$, the residual protein-abundance energy is $2958.1865890925337$, $T$ explains $941.8858618613751$ on $Y$, and the incremental improvement of $T$ after $X$ is $104.11162139652299$.  In Mus musculus, the total association is smaller as a fraction even though the direct $X$ energy is large: $X$ explains $2431.9284360407714$ on $Y$, the residual abundance energy is $48806.47856410273$, and $M^{QTP}=0.15488799907323988$.  The bacterial and archaeal small tables remain rank-sufficient but have distinct scales: Mycobacterium smegmatis has $y$ residual energy $5729.194538214237$, $X$ energy on $Y$ equal to $183.85037813566998$, and indirect $R^2$ $0.0026947984781311544$, while Sulfolobus solfataricus has $y$ residual energy $980.7615792975887$, $X$ energy on $Y$ equal to $62.74436180679531$, and indirect $R^2$ $0.0011839678516385038$.  These numbers support a finite statistical attenuation surface, not a universal sign law.

\paragraph{Relation to translation survival.}
A separate modeled-tAI survival table computed the same nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates over $3$ organisms: Escherichia coli K12, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  It used a bounded coupling threshold $0.01$ and found $81$ entries above threshold, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  That table explicitly marked $3$ organisms as insufficient for powered survival and therefore placed the result at modeled-tAI bounded coupling rather than ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.  The present $12$-organism protein-abundance table improves the finite row count and rank situation for the modeled-tAI-to-abundance decomposition, but it still inherits the same distinction: modeled tAI is a codon-adaptation readback, not an observed ribosome occupancy trace.

\paragraph{Adjacent evidence.}
Two nearby finite records help locate this claim without changing its scope.  In the structural-order axis identity run, the same nine axes were tested over $5$ folds with minimum joined count $300$; exactly one coordinate passed the fold-level sign-flip gate, namely $f_3$-stress, with weighted held-out $R^2$ increment $0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip $p=0.03125$.  Other coordinates did not pass that gate, for example Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR had weighted increment $-0.0003205780368125179$ and $p=0.779296875$, while Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY had $-0.00045340330387383447$ and $p=0.529296875$.  In the yeast structure-mediation run, a five-way join gave $4759$ rows, reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, overall join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, and single-and-joint residual values $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the structure-incremental fraction was $0.0005507778820193332$, $S_Q$ structure $R^2$ was $0.003774248455422586$, and the right-tail permutation $p$ values were $0.027972027972027972$ for $S_Q$ structure and $0.1258741258741259$ for the incremental structure term.  These rows are useful boundaries: they show that the $Q$ coordinates can be carried into several finite readouts, but each readout remains separately interpreted.

\paragraph{Decision.}
The finite-row statement accepted here is therefore narrow.  Across $12$ organism-matched CDS/protein-abundance/tRNA-copy tables, the joint nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has a positive residual association with protein abundance, and conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI attenuates that association by a nonnegative unit-interval fraction in every organism.  The evidence is strongest as a statistical modeled-translation attenuation surface, with yeast the most extreme row and Sulfolobus the weakest mediated row.  The result licenses the notation $Q\to T\to P$ only for this controlled decomposition, where $T$ is modeled tAI and $P$ is steady-state protein abundance.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No stronger biological statement follows from these rows alone.  A direct $Q\to P$ association is not counted as protein realization unless an intervening translation readout is explicitly present and survives its own controls.  Modeled tAI is not ribosome profiling, and conditioning on modeled tAI in cross-sectional abundance rows is not a causal mechanism.  Protein abundance is not isolated here from mRNA abundance, protein turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, pathway context, or perturbation response.  Consequently the table does not establish translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, mechanism, or universality.  Each of those layers would require its own matched contact and its own finite statistic before promotion beyond the bounded $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition is allowed.

\paragraph{Confirmatory background.}
The broader locked genetic-code contact remains consistent with this caution.  In the independent confirmatory dataset, all $40$ boundary-probe trials and all $40$ independent-replication trials succeeded, each with exact one-sided binomial upper-tail probability $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$, while $20$ negative controls and $20$ critical ablations produced zero unexpected successes.  Those rows support codon-geometry contact with curated genetic-code tables, but they do not themselves establish translation, protein abundance, structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a global biological law.  The present chapter uses that same discipline at the protein-abundance layer: finite success is reported at the layer actually measured or modeled, and no unmeasured layer is imported by wording.
