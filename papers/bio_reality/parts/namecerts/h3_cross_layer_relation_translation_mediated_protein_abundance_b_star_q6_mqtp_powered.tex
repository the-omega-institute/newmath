\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein abundance from the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests whether the nine-dimensional synonymous residual surface $B^*_{Q6}$ can be read as more than a codon-count residual when it is placed between a translation readout and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The finite rows are triples $(q_i,T,P)$: $q_i$ ranges over the nine residual coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$; $T$ is per-protein modeled tAI computed from real CDS codon counts, GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy totals, and dos Reis wobble weights; $P$ is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance ppm joined to the same CDS-derived protein rows.  The control surface contains an intercept, amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, and an $M$-density baseline for the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ directions.  After residualizing these controls, the test asks whether the association from $Q$ to $P$ is reduced by conditioning on $T$, rather than treating a direct $Q \to P$ regression as translation evidence.

\paragraph{Rank and row support.}
The powered run joined twelve organism panels.  The joined protein-row counts were $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  In every organism the residualized design had nine $Q$ coordinates, one $T$ column, rank $9$ for $\widetilde X$, rank $1$ for $\widetilde T$, and rank $10$ for the joint $[X,T]$ block, while the control rank was $23$ against $24$ declared control columns.  The residual degrees of freedom ranged from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in mouse, so the finite rows are not a saturated three-organism artifact.  The tRNA-copy totals used by the modeled tAI contact were also finite and organism-specific: $86$, $581$, $8874$, $290$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $407$, $49$, $417$, $275$, and $47$, respectively, with geometric-mean filling only in the bacterial, mycobacterial, and archaeal panels where zero raw $W$ entries occurred.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
For each organism the scalar mediation fraction is computed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{R^2(Q\to P)-R^2(Q\to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q\to P)},\\
R^2(Q\to P) &= c,\\
R^2(Q\to P\mid T) &= c'.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting fractions were positive and inside the unit interval for all twelve panels: $0.608459$ in \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.487227$ in worm, $0.315705$ in zebrafish, $0.616517$ in fly, $0.612922$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.197690$ in chicken, $0.272063$ in human, $0.154888$ in mouse, $0.083976$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.174897$ in rat, $0.949155$ in yeast, and $0.018507$ in \emph{S. solfataricus}.  The largest mediated share occurs in yeast, where $c=0.298376$, $c'=0.015171$, and $c-c'=0.283205$.  Large bacterial and animal examples are also nonzero: \emph{E. coli} has $c=0.107675$, $c'=0.041679$, and $c-c'=0.065997$; fly has $c=0.077095$, $c'=0.029565$, and $c-c'=0.047531$; worm has $c=0.102179$, $c'=0.052395$, and $c-c'=0.049784$.  The weakest positive mediated component is the archaeal panel, where $c=0.063975$, $c'=0.062791$, and $c-c'=0.001184$.  Thus the finite result is not that every organism has a large effect, but that the controlled decomposition remains nonnegative and interpretable across the joined panels.

\paragraph{Translation-readout contact.}
The translation coordinate used here is modeled tAI, not measured ribosome occupancy.  A separate modeled-tAI survival run computed all nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates over three organisms and found $81$ entries above the bounded-contact threshold $0.01$, with maximal absolute coupling $3801961.031608$.  That auxiliary result supplies a bounded codon-adaptation readout, but it explicitly reported that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that the run is not ribo-seq or proteomics validation.  The present protein-abundance mediation therefore does not use the earlier modeled-tAI contact as a direct observation of translation dynamics; it uses per-protein tAI as a finite conditioning coordinate in a cross-sectional mediation decomposition.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance interpretation.}
The protein readout is steady-state PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  It is not a folding assay, localization assay, turnover assay, perturbation assay, or function assay.  The finite statement supported by the rows is narrower: after the declared control residualization, the direct $Q\to P$ association is partly absorbed by modeled $T$ in every organism panel.  The claim is strongest as a statistical cross-layer relation and weakest as a mechanistic biological explanation.  Even the yeast panel, where $M^{QTP}=0.949155$, should be read as a fitted attenuation of $Q\to P$ after conditioning on modeled tAI, not as proof that the synonymous residual coordinates cause protein abundance through ribosome kinetics.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
Two neighboring audits help delimit the interpretation.  A yeast structure-mediated abundance decomposition, using measured translation efficiency and mRNA half-life together with a $5'$ coding-region MFE proxy, found $R^2_{QP}=0.271552$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.270660$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.137740$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.137590$ on $4759$ joined genes.  Its structure-only fraction was only $0.003285$, while its incremental structure fraction beyond measured TE and mRNA stability was $0.000551$, with permutation $p=0.125874$ for the incremental structure component.  Separately, a structural-order axis audit over five folds identified $f3\_stress$ as the only coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate, with weighted held-out $\Delta R^2=0.003174$ and sign-flip $p=0.03125$.  These neighboring rows show that the $Q$ surface can contact other biological readouts, but they also show why the present chapter must not collapse protein abundance, structure, and mechanism into one claim.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The result licenses a finite controlled matrix of the form $Q_i \to T \to P$ where $T$ is modeled per-protein tAI and $P$ is matched protein abundance.  It does not license a claim of observed translation, ribosome occupancy, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, adaptation, or universality.  A direct $Q_i\to P$ association is not enough for translation-mediated interpretation unless the same finite row system includes a surviving translation-readout step and the protein-abundance association is evaluated after the declared controls.  A stronger biological claim would need a separate layer-matched contact: measured ribosome profiling for observed translation, perturbation or rescue for mechanism, structural data for folding, physical assays for admissibility, and functional or phenotypic assays for function or organismal consequence.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The chapter-level verdict is ready at the statistical mediation level.  The powered finite rows support a controlled, nonnegative modeled-tAI mediation decomposition from the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates to matched protein abundance across twelve organism panels.  The proper readback is therefore a cross-layer relation constrained by modeled translation coordinates, not a claim that synonymous residual geometry has been promoted to translation realization, protein mechanism, folding, function, or a universal biological law.
