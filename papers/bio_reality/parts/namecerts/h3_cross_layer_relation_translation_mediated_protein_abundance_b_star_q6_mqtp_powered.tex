\subsection{Translation-mediated protein abundance for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a finite panel of synonymous residual coordinates can enter a protein-abundance readout only through an explicitly declared translation readout.  The carrier is the finite table of triples $(q_i,T,P)$, where $q_i$ ranges over the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \in \{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \mathrm{f3\_stress}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
$T$ is per-protein modeled tAI, and $P$ is measured protein abundance as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb rows joined to real CDS codon counts.  The translation readout is not ribosome profiling: it is the per-protein mean of codon weights computed from GtRNAdb all-tRNA gene-copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(g)=\frac{\sum_c n_{g,c}\,w_c}{\sum_c n_{g,c}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{g,c}$ is the CDS count of codon $c$ in protein-coding gene $g$ and $w_c$ is the modeled tAI weight for codon $c$.  The control block $Z$ contains an intercept, CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the support of the nine projected residual vectors.  The residualized design has $23$ effective control rank columns in every organism-specific fit and $9$ residual-coordinate columns after $Z$ is removed.

The powered finite row contains twelve organism-specific joined surfaces.  The joined protein counts are $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The corresponding residual degrees of freedom after the control projection are $4019$, $11638$, $14387$, $13272$, $3716$, $12395$, $19030$, $19372$, $4106$, $13299$, $5792$, and $1633$.  In each case the residualized translation column has rank $1$, the residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$, and the joint $(Q,T)$ block has rank $10$, so the mediation scalar is not a rank-saturated artifact under the declared threshold $R^2<0.999999$.

\paragraph{Modeled tAI contact.}
The tRNA-copy totals used to form the modeled translation weights were $86$, $581$, $8874$, $290$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $407$, $49$, $417$, $275$, and $47$ in the same organism order.  Zero raw codon weights had to be filled by the geometric mean in four cases only: $3$ codons for \emph{B. subtilis}, $1$ for \emph{E. coli}, $1$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $1$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}; the remaining eight organism surfaces had zero such fills.  This makes the translation contact finite and computable, but it remains a model-derived codon-adaptation readout rather than observed ribosome occupancy.  A smaller three-organism modeled-tAI contact also showed nonzero bounded coupling, with $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute coupling $3801961.031607896$, but that earlier surface was explicitly underpowered for a measured survival claim because it used only $3$ organisms.

\paragraph{Mediation statistic.}
For each organism the total residual-coordinate protein-abundance association is
$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= R^2(P_Z \sim Q_Z),\\
c' &= R^2(P_Z \sim Q_Z \mid T_Z),\\
M^{QTP} &= \frac{c-c'}{c}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite row is positive in every organism for the indirect difference $c-c'$, and every reported mediation fraction lies in $[0,1]$.  In the same organism order as above, the total $Q\to P$ values $c$ are $0.061906245886660105$, $0.10217906758013379$, $0.04613529807085213$, $0.0770952887136557$, $0.10767526677985896$, $0.03980793596647631$, $0.04454565118019448$, $0.049827984062539195$, $0.03209009170649934$, $0.02763998603083845$, $0.29837624994140827$, and $0.06397514251295629$.  After conditioning on modeled tAI, the direct residual values $c'$ are $0.02423880523185755$, $0.05239468380596188$, $0.031570135553274765$, $0.02956474995793468$, $0.04167869969498872$, $0.031938289322513524$, $0.03242643251185274$, $0.04211022731323921$, $0.029395293228368185$, $0.0228058404248828$, $0.0151709093608293$, and $0.06279117466131778$.

\paragraph{Observed finite verdict.}
The mediated fractions $M^{QTP}$ are $0.6084594553474505$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.4872268357227724$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $0.3157053953614641$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.6165167748739756$ for \emph{D. melanogaster}, $0.612922252793667$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.1976903964724546$ for \emph{G. gallus}, $0.27206289160119157$ for human, $0.15488799907323988$ for mouse, $0.08397602919861291$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.17489681798543982$ for rat, $0.9491551041216973$ for yeast, and $0.01850668564589326$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}.  The largest finite mediation fraction is yeast, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$.  The smallest is \emph{S. solfataricus}, where $c=0.06397514251295629$, $c'=0.06279117466131778$, and $c-c'=0.0011839678516385038$.  The strongest absolute indirect differences after yeast are $0.06599656708487024$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.04978438377417191$ in \emph{C. elegans}, $0.047530538755721025$ in \emph{D. melanogaster}, and $0.037667440654802556$ in \emph{B. subtilis}.  Thus the finite row supports the bounded statement that the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block has a reproducible statistical attenuation pattern when modeled per-protein tAI is inserted between codon residuals and protein abundance.

\paragraph{Yeast comparison row.}
The same yeast protein-abundance surface has an independent decomposition against a 5-prime CDS folding proxy and measured translation efficiency plus mRNA stability.  In that row, $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction is only $0.003284564452662019$, while the structure-incremental fraction beyond measured TE and mRNA stability is $0.0005507778820193332$.  The $Q$-to-structure score is $S_Q=0.003774248455422586$ with permutation $p=0.027972027972027972$, while the incremental structure test has permutation $p=0.1258741258741259$.  This comparison keeps the current chapter from reading protein abundance as folded-structure evidence: the available finite rows point to translation-conditioned abundance attenuation, not to a structure mechanism.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule.}
The local interpretation is deliberately narrow.  A direct $Q\to P$ association is not counted as protein realization unless a declared $Q\to T$ translation-readout step is present, and here $T$ is modeled tAI rather than a measured ribosome-footprint or same-condition translation-efficiency matrix.  The protein abundance column is steady-state PAXdb abundance and is not isolated from transcript abundance, turnover, localization, post-translational modification, folding, or pathway context.  Consequently this finite row permits a controlled statistical mediation readback of $Q\to T\to P$ under the stated covariates.  It does not establish translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causation, selection, rescue by synonymous editing, or universality.  Any one of those stronger claims would require its own layer-matched contact and a separate finite statistic on joined rows.
