$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding $Q6$ rows.}
The spectrum row is coherent with the broader powered $Q6$ evidence without collapsing those rows into one claim. The tRNA-poverty computation for the $R$ sense-codon boundary requested $46$ organisms, computed $40$, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and called the $R$ sense codons systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN with dos Reis wobble weights. The non-tRNA residual optimality row on the same computed-organism scale identified $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant residual axis. The two-axis closure row, again at $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, concluded that a third axis is present because $d_\perp$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms. The third-axis identity row then identified $d_\perp$ provisionally with usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The corresponding mechanism row says that this third-axis signal is selection-not-GC and not wobble-clustered, while its CSC or positional identity still needs an additional contact.

\paragraph{Fourth-axis separation.}
The spectrum row also gives a disciplined reading of the fourth-axis neighborhood. The three-axis sufficiency row concluded that a fourth mechanism is present on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, again with $5$ folds, $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns. The tested fourth-mechanism rows do not identify that mechanism as mRNA stability, codon-pair structure, or translation accuracy. In particular, the mRNA-stability row concluded $\texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ has low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates. The codon-pair row concluded $\texttt{not\_codon\_pair}$ with a shuffle-null count of $50$. The translation-accuracy row concluded $\texttt{not\_accuracy}$ using $200$ permutations across the $11$ computed organisms. The elongation-dwell row is weaker and organism-specific: in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} it records a partial conclusion, not a complete identification. Consequently the local mathematical reading is a spectrum statement: $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ is the dominant captured per-organism axis in the finite powered surface, while the biological identity of the residual fourth direction remains bounded by the contacts actually measured.

\paragraph{Readout rule.}
The only permitted readback from this chapter is the finite controlled spectrum statement just described. A modelled tAI or stAI row may bound a translation-adjacent codon-adaptation readout, and ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows may test a direct translation readout only when same-organism and same-CDS rows are joined with the stated controls. In the present finite memory, the exact $f_3$-stress translation-readout packet lacks a same-organism and same-CDS joined matrix containing codon counts, wobble-weight rows, measured translation readout, matched mRNA control, and the declared $S^{QT}$ statistic. Therefore this chapter does not assert translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universality. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics. The result here is narrower: after amino-acid projection and finite rank control, the $B^*_{Q6}$ per-organism spectrum is empirically led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on all $11$ computed organisms, with $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$ next and only a small recorded GC3 rank effect for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.
