\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage as a bounded translation-layer statistic}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is an organism-indexed leucine module rather than a free biological law. Its carrier consists of the residual coordinate $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ paired with an organism label, an organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control. The finite codon side used by the packet also records the associated $M$ set as a list of $20$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
&CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the contact does not start from an unconstrained verbal comparison between organisms. It starts from a fixed codon window and asks whether the leucine split between the CUN family and the UUR family changes with an independently supplied tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum. The biological objects named by the row are per-organism codon-usage tables and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations; neither source is treated as a direct observation of ribosome motion, folding, fitness, or biochemical function.

\paragraph{Observed rows.}
The validated finite run loaded three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. For these three rows the recorded leucine log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign is therefore not uniform across the measured organisms: the Boolean field $sign\_uniform$ is $false$. The finite validation run reports experiment id $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$ and run id $675fdddb3187$. Within that run, the ordinary Pearson value for the paired statistic is $1.0$, and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson statistic against the tRNA-Leu ratio is also $1.0$ with $n=3$. The readout is consequently a small finite-row positive pairing between the CUN/UUR leucine usage residual and the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio, not a population-level estimate with broad statistical margin.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The packet is admitted only as a controlled organism-level usage statement. The data say that, in the three loaded organisms, the raw sign of $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ is not constant, while the paired controlled statistic is positive in the available tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate. This supports the narrow claim that the CUN/UUR leucine split can be read as an externally checked organism-indexed translation-layer statistic when it is paired with codon-usage tables, tRNA-Leu copy annotations, and amino-acid-composition control. The negative yeast-side sign is important: it prevents the chapter from stating a single universal direction for the CUN family over the UUR family. The finite contact is therefore context-sensitive at the organism level.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No biological translation event, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility statement, functional realization, or universal cross-organism rule follows from these rows by themselves. Codon-usage tables summarize coding-sequence composition and sampling conventions, while tRNA gene-copy counts summarize genome annotation. Those contacts are sufficient for the bounded paired statistic above, but they are not sufficient for a mechanistic account of elongation, a statement about protein conformation, an organismal function claim, or a global law of leucine coding. Any such stronger conclusion would require a separate contact, such as ribosome-profiling, protein-output, structural, biochemical, or functional data matched to the relevant organisms and controls.

\paragraph{Audit verdict.}
The row passes as a finite, externally paired organism-level statistic because the required contacts are present, the loaded organism count is $3$, the CUN/UUR signs are explicitly non-uniform, and the recorded controlled partial correlation with tRNA-Leu copy ratio is positive. Its evidential weight remains deliberately bounded: exact correlation values at $n=3$ are descriptive for this curated row set and do not license an asymptotic, mechanistic, structural, functional, or universal biological conclusion.
