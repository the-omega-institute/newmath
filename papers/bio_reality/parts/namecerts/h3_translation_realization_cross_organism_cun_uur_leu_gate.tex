\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu Gate}
\label{sec:bior-h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Packet scope.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family as a bounded organism-level statistic. The carrier is the residual coordinate obtained from the per-organism log ratio $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, paired with an organism label and checked against an organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after amino-acid-composition control. The relevant distinctions are $\log(\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR})$, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and the amino-acid-composition control. The readout is therefore not a claim about a particular ribosome, peptide, fold, phenotype, or universal biological rule; it is a finite cross-organism comparison between codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu annotation summaries.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The supporting run is identified as \texttt{675fdddb3187} with experiment id \texttt{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}. It loaded three organisms: \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, and \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12}. The measured CUN/UUR log ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\mathrm{human}} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{ecoli}} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding sign vector was $(1,-1,1)$, so the recorded field \texttt{sign\_uniform} was false. The cross-organism Pearson value reported for the paired statistic was $1.0$. The controlled statistic was recorded as a positive partial correlation with tRNA-Leu ratio, with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$. These numbers support only the finite observation that the sign is not uniform in the three-row table and that the available controlled paired statistic is positive in this dataset.

Within the present record, this codon set supplies a finite coordinate vocabulary for the CUN side of the leucine split and for the surrounding codon-window bookkeeping. It does not by itself measure translation. The translation-layer relevance enters only through the organism-matched usage and tRNA-copy contacts used by the three-row run.

\paragraph{Reality contacts.}
Two contacts are used. The first is the per-organism codon-usage contact, whose observation is that curated sequence resources report codon-count or codon-frequency summaries for coding sequences. Its resolution is a per-organism codon-usage readout, and its known limitation is that such tables summarize sequence composition and sampling choices rather than ribosome dynamics, protein structure, biological function, or a global law. The second is the tRNA gene-copy contact, whose observation is that genome annotation resources report organism-level tRNA-Leu copy counts by anticodon family. Its resolution is an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number readout, with the limitation that gene-copy annotation is not itself a dynamical translation measurement.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow: in the recorded three-organism table, $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ changes sign across organisms, and the paired statistic reports a positive controlled association with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio. This is enough to keep the CUN/UUR leucine split as a translation-realization probe at the level of codon usage and tRNA-copy contacts. It is not enough to infer a ribosome-mediated mechanism, a protein fold, physical admissibility, function, or universality. Raw CUN/UUR sign alone is also insufficient; the record requires the amino-acid-composition control and the paired organism-level tRNA-Leu statistic.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No general translation claim follows from identifier spelling, from the codon list alone, or from a single raw log ratio. No folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal directionality follows from this packet unless a separate contact supplies the relevant measurement. The sign reversal is therefore reported as context-dependent finite evidence in the three loaded organisms, not as a rule applying to all organisms or all leucine coding contexts.
