\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}\label{subsec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.} This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ family and the $UUR$ family as an organism-level usage statistic, not as a direct statement about ribosome motion or protein output. The carrier is the residual coordinate obtained from the signed log-ratio $\log(CUN/UUR)$, paired with organism labels, a tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control. The measured row set contains three organisms: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$. For these rows, the recorded log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(CUN/UUR)_{\mathrm{human}} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\log(CUN/UUR)_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\log(CUN/UUR)_{\mathrm{ecoli}} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding sign vector is $(1,-1,1)$, so the finite table is not sign-uniform. The packet is therefore a cross-organism sign-and-magnitude test: the same leucine split is positive in two of the three recorded organisms and negative in one.

\paragraph{Codon-window coordinate.} The associated code-window contact records the finite set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its recorded cardinality is $|M|=20$. In this chapter the $M$ row is used only as a finite codon-label coordinate containing the $CUN$ entries $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$ and the $UUR$ entries $UUA,UUG$. It does not by itself measure an organism-level abundance, a tRNA-copy spectrum, or a protein-level outcome. The biological test is attached only after the organism-specific codon-usage table and the organism-specific tRNA-Leu copy summary are paired with the same row label.

\paragraph{Controlled readout.} The finite experiment reports status $passed$ for the cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate. Across the three loaded organisms, the raw Pearson coefficient between the recorded CUN/UUR statistic and the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate is $1.0$. After applying the amino-acid-composition control as a per-codon null on synonymous classes, the recorded partial Pearson coefficient remains $1.0$ with $n=3$ rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{Pearson}} &= 1.0,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}\mid \mathrm{AA\ control}} &= 1.0,\\
n&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite-row realization of the intended test: the sign is not constant across organisms, while the signed magnitude is reported as positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the amino-acid-composition control. Because the table has only three rows, the result is a bounded contact rather than a population-level law. No $p$-value is recorded in the supplied row, so the evidence is read here as an exact finite-table statistic, not as a large-sample significance claim.

\paragraph{Local scope.} The admissible conclusion is narrow. The chapter records that organism-matched codon usage and tRNA-Leu copy-number summaries can support a translation-layer usage statistic for the CUN/UUR leucine split under the stated control. Nothing in this packet establishes actual translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal rule across organisms. The sign reversal is explicitly context-dependent. A stronger biological conclusion would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind, such as ribosome-profiling evidence for dynamics or protein-output evidence for structure or function. The codon labels, the packet identifier, and the finite correlation table are therefore kept at their stated resolution: a three-row organism-level codon-usage/tRNA-copy relation for leucine-family coding bias.
