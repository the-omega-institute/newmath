\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine coding split between the CUN family and the UUR family as a bounded organism-level statistic. The carrier is the residual coordinate formed from the signed codon-usage contrast $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$, paired with organism labels and with an external tRNA-Leu gene-copy comparison. The registered organisms are Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12, so the row count for the paired cross-organism statistic is $n=3$. The coordinate is not a protein-output measurement. It is a finite readout asking whether a per-organism leucine codon-usage contrast changes sign and whether its magnitude is aligned with an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after amino-acid-composition control.

\paragraph{Codon support set.}
The associated code-table fact records the finite set $M$ as a twenty-element union of WNR and CUN codons. In this row the recorded cardinality is $|M|=20$, and the listed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This codon set supplies the finite label support used by the packet; it does not by itself supply a cross-organism biological conclusion. Its role here is to make the CUN side of the leucine split explicit while keeping the organism-level comparison tied to separate codon-usage and tRNA-copy contacts.

\paragraph{Observed cross-organism statistic.}
The paired validation row reports that codon-usage data and tRNA-Leu copy-number data were loaded for the three organisms. The measured log-ratio values for $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&Homo\ sapiens: 1.3514980212896148,\\
&S.\ cerevisiae: -0.24869078488829677,\\
&E.\ coli\ K12: 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs are $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not uniform across the three rows. The reported raw Pearson coefficient for the paired statistic is $1.0$. The amino-acid-composition-controlled result is also recorded as positive, with partial Pearson value $1.0$ at $n=3$. Thus the finite row supports the narrow statement that, in this curated three-organism panel, the CUN/UUR leucine usage contrast is sign-changing across organisms and is positively aligned with the available tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate under the recorded control.

\paragraph{Admissible reading.}
The admissible biological reading is deliberately local. The packet is a translation-layer contact only in the weak sense that codon usage and tRNA gene-copy spectra are organism-level summaries attached to coding and tRNA annotation resources. It says that the leucine split is not represented by a single universal sign in the tested organisms, and that the finite paired statistic passes the recorded correlation test. The observation is compatible with context dependence: the yeast row has the opposite sign from the human and bacterial rows, so the row itself rejects a uniform-direction story for the measured panel.

\paragraph{Local exclusions.}
Nothing in this row establishes ribosome-mediated dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal cross-organism law. A raw CUN/UUR sign without the amino-acid-composition control is not accepted here as a translation-layer conclusion. The standard code-table support is also not a repair for missing organism-matched evidence: it can identify codon labels, but it cannot replace paired codon-usage and tRNA-copy measurements. Any future claim about folding, function, physical realization, or mechanistic translation would need its own contact, such as ribosome-profiling, protein-output, structural, or functional evidence. The present record remains a finite three-row organism statistic with a passed status for the stated codon-usage/tRNA-copy comparison only.
