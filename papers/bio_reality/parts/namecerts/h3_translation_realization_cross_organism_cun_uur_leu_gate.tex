\subsection{Cross-Organism CUN/UUR Leu Gate}
\label{subsec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The packet records a Leu-module statistic on organism-indexed codon-usage rows.  Its carrier consists of a residual coordinate for $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, an organism label, an organism-level $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control on the synonymous Leu class.  The finite row set used by the checked run contains three organism labels,
$\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.
The auxiliary codon-window fact attached to the same record identifies the twenty-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},
\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\qquad |M|=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this chapter the relevant Leu pair is the split between the four $\mathrm{CUN}$ codons and the two $\mathrm{UUR}$ codons.  The role of $M$ is only to keep the finite codon naming and membership data explicit; it is not treated as a mechanistic description of translation.

\paragraph{Observed finite readout.}
The validation run $675\mathrm{fdddb}3187$ reports status $\mathrm{passed}$ for the organism-matched statistic.  The three measured residual log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the sampled organisms.  The same finite run reports Pearson correlation $1.0$ between the paired quantities recorded for the row set, and a positive partial correlation after the amino-acid-composition control,
$$
\begin{aligned}
n=3,\qquad r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers make the packet admissible as a bounded organism-level codon-usage and $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-number contact: the sign reversal is present in the three-row table, and the controlled statistic is positive in that same finite table.

\paragraph{Interpretive scope.}
The scientific content here is deliberately narrow.  The chapter does not infer a ribosome-level process from the codon-usage table, and it does not infer folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law from the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ sign pattern.  The recorded contact says only that, in the three-organism finite row set, the Leu codon-usage residual is non-uniform and is positively paired with the available $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy ratio after controlling within the amino-acid composition of the Leu synonymous class.  A broader translation-level, structural, functional, mechanistic, or universal conclusion would require a separate measurement channel supplying that kind of evidence.

\paragraph{Null boundary and failure modes.}
The packet would fail in either of two finite ways.  First, if every measured organism had the same sign for $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, the cross-organism sign-reversal cue would disappear.  Second, if sign reversal remained but the paired statistic lost its positive controlled association with the $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy ratio, the organism-level gate would no longer support the stated contact.  The present row set avoids both failures: $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$, the sign vector is $(+1,-1,+1)$, and the reported controlled correlation is $1.0$ on $3$ rows.  Identifier spelling and codon-label bookkeeping do not repair missing biological data; only organism-matched codon-usage and $\mathrm{tRNA}$-copy rows can support this specific Leu-module statistic.
