\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu-gate readback}\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Packet and observable carrier.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ and $\mathrm{UUR}$ families as an organism-indexed measurement packet, not as a universal biological principle.  The carrier consists of a per-organism residual coordinate for $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, paired with the organism label and the organism-level $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy ratio, with amino-acid composition treated as an explicit control.  Thus the scientific question is only whether the sign and magnitude of the leucine-family codon-usage residual track the corresponding $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-number spectrum across the measured organisms.  The finite check loaded three organism rows, namely $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  These are the rows used in the stated statistic; no additional unlisted organism is silently included.

\paragraph{Observed signs.}
For the three loaded organism rows, the checked log-ratio values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{human}}&=1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}}&=-0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{E.coli\ K12}}&=1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding sign vector is $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not uniform across the finite sample.  This is the first required finite obstruction to a one-directional rule: the packet records a positive human sign, a negative yeast sign, and a positive $\mathrm{E.coli\ K12}$ sign.  The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The sign reversal shows that the raw $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ direction is organism-context dependent in these rows; it does not license any claim about a general mechanism.

Its role is to say that the three-row table is internally consistent with the stated gate condition: non-uniform $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ sign, together with positive association to the corresponding $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu ratio after the declared control.

\paragraph{Layer discipline.}
The admissible layer is the bounded translation-realization readback supplied by codon-usage summaries and organism-level $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy annotations.  The local exclusion rule is part of the claim: this packet does not establish ribosome-mediated dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal cross-organism law.  The recorded finite result also states this negatively: the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ codon-usage and $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy contact does not establish folding; it does not establish function or physical admissibility; it does not promote the translation-layer statistic to a global law; and sign reversal is reported as context dependence rather than universal directionality.  Any later move to mechanism, structure, function, or universality would require a separate measurement channel with its own organism-matched readout.

\paragraph{Verdict for the standalone record.}
The finite data therefore support a restricted organism-level statement: in the three loaded rows, the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ leucine log-ratio has values $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$, has signs $(1,-1,1)$, and is reported with Pearson $1.0$ and controlled partial Pearson $1.0$ against the $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate.  The result is a valid finite cross-organism contact for the specified codon-usage and gene-copy quantities, while remaining silent about all stronger biological readings not directly measured here.
