\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{subsec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This BioReality record treats the leucine codon pair $CUN/UUR$ as an organism-level usage-and-annotation contact, not as a direct observation of ribosomal motion or protein output. The finite carrier is the residual coordinate comparing the four $CUN$ leucine codons against the two $UUR$ leucine codons, paired with an external organism label and a same-amino-acid control. The observed readback is the sign and magnitude of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ for each organism, checked against the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after controlling for amino-acid composition within synonymous classes.

\paragraph{Codon support.}
The supporting code-window fact fixes the finite set used by the packet. The checked $M$ list has cardinality $20$ and is exactly the union of the $WNR$ window with the $CUN$ leucine block:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
     &CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its recorded size is $|M|=20$. This finite list matters because the packet is not allowed to infer biology from a free-form verbal resemblance between codon names. The admissible coordinate is the specific leucine contrast $CUN$ versus $UUR$, read only through organism-indexed codon-usage and tRNA-Leu copy-number contacts.

\paragraph{Finite organism table.}
The validation run loaded three organism rows: Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12. For these rows the recorded $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated sign vector was
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\,-1,\,+1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the sign-uniformity test returned false. The same run recorded Pearson correlation $1.0$ and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$. Thus the finite table supports the narrow statement that the CUN/UUR leucine usage contrast is not sign-uniform across the three measured organisms and that, in this row set, its controlled statistic is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.

\paragraph{Layer discipline.}
The contact is bounded to sequence-derived codon usage and genome-annotation tRNA copy counts. Codon-usage tables report coding-sequence count or frequency summaries; tRNA gene-copy resources report organism-level anticodon-family copy spectra. These contacts can test a cross-organism usage relation at the translation-realization boundary only in the modest sense of an externally checked statistic on codon usage and tRNA-copy annotations. They do not observe ribosome dwell time, aminoacylation, translation efficiency, protein folding, molecular stability, organismal function, or physical admissibility.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The packet therefore records a context-dependent leucine-module signal rather than a universal biological law. The data do not say that $CUN$ is always favored over $UUR$, since one of the three measured rows has negative $\log(CUN/UUR)$. They also do not say that a sign reversal itself is mechanistic evidence. What is retained is the paired finite result: three organism rows were loaded; the sign vector was non-uniform; the controlled tRNA-Leu association was positive with partial Pearson $1.0$ at $n=3$; and the run explicitly declined promotion to protein structure, biological function, physical admissibility, or global rule status.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No independent translation-process claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, functional claim, mechanistic claim, or universality claim follows from this record. Such a claim would require an additional contact of the relevant kind, for example ribosome-profiling data for a ribosome-level assertion or protein-output measurements for structure or function. In the present chapter, the only accepted reality contact is the organism-indexed CUN/UUR codon-usage statistic paired with tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotation under the stated amino-acid-composition control.
