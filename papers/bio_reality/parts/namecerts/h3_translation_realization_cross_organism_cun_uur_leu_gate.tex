\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record isolates the leucine codon pair $CUN/UUR$ as an organism-indexed usage residual, not as a direct assertion about proteins or ribosome physics.  The finite carrier consists of four recorded distinctions: the signed scalar $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the organism-level $tRNA\text{-}Leu$ gene-copy ratio, the external organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The internal shape used by the packet is therefore a coordinate together with a relation: each row names an organism, reads the CUN and UUR leucine usage contrast, and compares that contrast with the matched $tRNA\text{-}Leu$ copy-number spectrum after controlling for amino-acid composition within synonymous classes.

\paragraph{Codon support.}
The auxiliary code-table fact used by the packet is the finite set $M=WNR\cup CUN$, with cardinality $20$.  The recorded list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only the $CUN$ and $UUR$ leucine portion is used for the present organism-level statistic.  The larger $M$ row records the finite codon environment in which $CUN$ appears, but it does not itself supply organism-matched usage, copy-number, ribosome, structure, or function evidence.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The validation row loaded three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  Their observed $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
&1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702,
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
&+1,\quad -1,\quad +1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign was not uniform across the measured organisms: $sign\_uniform=false$.  This is the first finite readout required by the packet, because a uniform sign would have collapsed the proposed cross-organism contrast into a simple direction rather than an organism-conditioned residual.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
For the same three-row organism table, the recorded Pearson statistic between the CUN/UUR contrast and the matched $tRNA\text{-}Leu$ copy-number ratio is $1.0$.  The amino-acid-composition-controlled statistic is also recorded as a positive partial correlation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
partial\_pearson&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite row set, the support condition is therefore satisfied in its narrow form: the CUN/UUR sign reverses across organisms, and the magnitude/sign pattern is positively aligned with the organism-level $tRNA\text{-}Leu$ copy-number ratio after the stated synonymous-class composition control.  The experiment row is marked $status=passed$ under the identifier $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The scientific content of this packet is deliberately small.  It says that, for the three organisms in the loaded row set, the leucine $CUN/UUR$ usage residual is not a universal-sign statistic and is compatible with a matched $tRNA\text{-}Leu$ copy-number explanation at the recorded partial-correlation level.  The row is therefore admissible as a bounded organism-level contact between codon usage and a translation-adjacent genomic annotation.  It is not a direct measurement of ribosome dynamics, elongation rate, codon-pair effects, aminoacylation, protein abundance, folding, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
No independent translation-realization conclusion follows from $\log(CUN/UUR)$ by itself.  No folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, biological-function claim, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal cross-organism law follows from these rows.  The contact remains at the level of organism-indexed codon-usage and $tRNA\text{-}Leu$ gene-copy statistics.  A stronger claim would require a separate matched contact, for example ribosome profiling for mechanism, proteomic output for realized translation products, structural assays for folding, or perturbational biology for function.  The sign reversal is reported as context-dependent evidence in this finite table, not as a global directionality rule.
