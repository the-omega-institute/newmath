\subsection{Cross-organism Leu codon usage and tRNA-Leu copy-number contact}
\label{sec:bior-reality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record concerns a bounded organism-level question about the Leu codon split between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ family and the $\mathrm{UUR}$ family.  The finite carrier is a per-organism residual coordinate: for each organism in the observed row set, the packet records the sign and magnitude of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ and compares it with the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, with amino-acid composition treated as a required control rather than as optional context.  The observed row set contains exactly three organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{homo\_sapiens},\quad \mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae},\quad \mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The companion code-table contact fixes the finite codon module used by this packet.  In the checked row, $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is recorded as the following list:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This finite list is not used as a mechanistic explanation.  It supplies the codon-label coordinate against which the organism-level Leu split is named.

\paragraph{Observed numerical row.}
The cross-organism test reports status $\mathrm{passed}$ for experiment $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$.  The three recorded log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702,
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
&+1,\quad -1,\quad +1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row does not support a uniform sign across the measured organisms: the recorded value of $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}$ is $\mathrm{false}$.  The same row reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the amino-acid composition control, with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$.  The numerical content is therefore a small, explicitly row-bounded association: in these three organisms, the direction of the $\mathrm{CUN}$ versus $\mathrm{UUR}$ Leu usage split changes across organisms, and the controlled paired statistic is positive in the recorded finite table.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The packet may be read as an organism-level codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact for the Leu module: it asks whether the $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ coordinate is aligned with the organism's tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after controlling for amino-acid composition.  The answer in the recorded finite row is affirmative for the available three-organism table, because the sign is non-uniform and the paired controlled statistic is reported as positive.  The result is not a universal directionality statement.  The negative yeast-side sign in the row is part of the observation, not an exception to be erased.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter does not infer a ribosome-level translation event from the codon-usage statistic alone.  It also does not infer folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Codon-usage tables summarize coding-sequence composition, and tRNA gene-copy counts summarize genome annotation.  Those contacts can support a bounded comparison between usage and copy-number spectra, but they do not observe translation kinetics, protein output, structural admissibility, or organismal function.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate contact that directly measures the relevant layer.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
The packet would lose support if the measured organisms all had the same $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ sign independently of tRNA-Leu copy-number spectra, or if a sign reversal remained after measurement but showed no controlled association with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.  It would also remain ungrounded if only separate codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact categories were available with no organism-matched paired statistic.  In the present finite row, the paired statistic is recorded for three organisms, so the packet is usable as a limited translation-layer coordinate, while every stronger biological reading remains outside the recorded contact.
