\subsection{Cross-organism Leu codon usage and tRNA-copy gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record concerns the leucine synonym split between the CUN block and the UUR block, evaluated as an organism-level codon-usage statistic paired with organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotation. The admissible data carrier has four named distinctions: the signed coordinate $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control. The finite test row loaded three organisms: Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K-12. The attached code-window row also records the finite set $M=WNR\cup CUN$ with cardinality $20$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This set is used only as a finite codon-coordinate check. It is not itself an observation of translation, ribosome dynamics, protein output, or organismal phenotype.

\paragraph{Observed signs.}
The measured organism-level log-ratio row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the CUN-over-UUR sign is not uniform across the three organisms in the finite row. Human and E. coli K-12 occupy positive CUN-over-UUR sign positions in this readback, while yeast occupies a negative sign position. The test therefore rejects the simpler finite alternative in which the CUN/UUR direction is constant across all loaded organisms. This is a bounded statement about the loaded codon-usage summaries; it is not a universal rule about all organisms or all growth conditions.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-copy statistic.}
The same run reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu copy ratio after the amino-acid-composition control. The reported controlled row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
On this finite row, the sign and magnitude of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ are therefore aligned with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated control. The support is exact on the three loaded rows, but its size is also exactly three. The mathematical content of the record is consequently a finite paired-contact result, not an asymptotic population claim. No p-value, confidence interval, lambda parameter, hit-rate field, or larger row count is present in the supplied run, so none is asserted here.

\paragraph{Layer assignment.}
The record is admissible at the translation-realization contact layer only in the narrow sense that codon-usage summaries and tRNA-gene-copy annotations are external sequence-database facts connected to translation-associated molecular labels. The usable claim is that, for the loaded organisms, the leucine CUN/UUR usage coordinate is not sign-uniform and is positively paired with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated amino-acid-composition control. The contact does not measure ribosome movement, aminoacylation, elongation rate, protein abundance, folded structure, biological activity, or physical admissibility of a protein product. In particular, the controlled codon/tRNA statistic is not promoted into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Failure conditions.}
The claim would fail on this finite protocol if the loaded organisms had a uniform $\log(CUN/UUR)$ sign independent of the tRNA-Leu copy spectrum, or if a non-uniform sign had no positive controlled association with the tRNA-Leu copy ratio. The supplied run records the opposite finite outcome: $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$, $\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0$, and $\mathrm{status}=\mathrm{passed}$. The result is therefore ready as a bounded three-organism contact statement.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter licenses a broader biological conclusion without another contact of the appropriate kind. No standalone translation claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, function claim, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal cross-organism law follows from the CUN/UUR sign row, the tRNA-Leu copy row, the packet identifier, or the standard codon labels. A separate ribosome, protein-output, structural, physical, functional, or population-scale dataset would be required before any such conclusion could be stated.
