\subsection{Cross-organism Leu-module gate for CUN and UUR}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN block and the UUR block as a bounded organism-level measurement problem.  The carrier is the residual comparison between the two leucine synonym families, indexed by organism labels and paired to a separate tRNA-Leu copy-number readback.  The finite coordinate set used by the companion code-window check is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|M|=20$.  The CUN entries inside this finite list are $CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU$, while the UUR leucine entries are $UUA,UUG$.  In this chapter the list is not interpreted as a mechanism.  It only fixes the finite codon labels on which the cross-organism statistic is allowed to be read.

\paragraph{Measured row set.}
The recorded validation loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  For these rows the tested scalar was the organism-level residual
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_o=\log\left({\#CUN_o\over \#UUR_o}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
after requiring the comparison to be paired with an external tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio and to be interpreted only after amino-acid composition control.  The recorded residual values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the three organisms.  The finite result therefore rejects the overly simple reading that the CUN-over-UUR direction has one fixed organism-independent sign in this row set.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu check.}
The same validation records a Pearson coefficient of $1.0$ for the available paired statistic, and a partial Pearson coefficient of $1.0$ with $n=3$ after the stated amino-acid-composition control.  Written only as the finite-row assertion supplied here, the supported statement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&n=3,\qquad sign\_uniform=false,\qquad r=1.0,\qquad r_{partial}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a narrow translation-layer contact in the sense of codon-usage and tRNA-gene-copy bookkeeping: the CUN/UUR residual changes sign across the measured organisms, and the recorded controlled statistic is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.  The row count is small, so the value $1.0$ should be read as an exact statement about these three recorded rows, not as a population-level law.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The biological object of the packet is the combination of per-organism codon-usage summaries and organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra.  These contacts can test whether a codon-usage residual is organism-dependent and whether that residual is paired with the tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum in the recorded data.  They do not observe ribosome trajectories, elongation kinetics, protein conformations, organismal function, or physical feasibility.  Consequently, the chapter licenses only the bounded statement that the three-row cross-organism CUN/UUR residual is non-uniform and has the recorded positive controlled association with the tRNA-Leu ratio.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
Nothing in this packet, by itself, establishes translation in the stronger mechanistic sense.  No folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal cross-organism rule follows from the numbers above.  Such claims would require an additional contact of the appropriate kind, for example a ribosome-profiling, protein-output, folding, function, or physical-assay measurement.  The present chapter therefore remains at the level of a finite organism-indexed codon-usage and tRNA-copy statistic: it records a real sign reversal and a real paired controlled coefficient for the loaded rows, while refusing to promote that statistic into a general biological mechanism.
