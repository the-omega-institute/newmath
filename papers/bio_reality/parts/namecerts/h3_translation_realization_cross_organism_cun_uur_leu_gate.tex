\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:biorreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record isolates a leucine-codon question at organism scale.  The measured object is not a protein, a ribosome trace, or a biochemical activity assay.  It is a paired finite statistic: for each organism in the available table, compare the codon-usage balance between the leucine families $CUN$ and $UUR$, then compare that balance with the organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy spectrum.  The admissible readout is therefore a controlled codon-usage relation, not a claim about folding, function, physical realizability, or a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate.}
The codon-side coordinate uses the finite set named by the supporting code-table fact.  In this row the recorded set has cardinality $20$ and is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M ={}& \{AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The leucine gate uses the two leucine subfamilies inside this vocabulary: $CUN=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ and $UUR=\{UUA,UUG\}$.  For an organism $o$, the observed codon-usage coordinate is summarized by
$$
\begin{aligned}
L(o)=\log\left(\frac{\#CUN(o)}{\#UUR(o)}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
with the understanding that this scalar is only a sequence-table statistic until paired with the matched $tRNA$-Leu copy-number coordinate and an amino-acid-composition control.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The validation row loaded three organism entries: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  The recorded $L(o)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
and their signs were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is explicitly non-uniform across the three measured organisms.  The row also reports $sign\_uniform=false$, so the observed table does not collapse to a single direction for the $CUN/UUR$ balance.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The finite experiment named $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$ reports a Pearson coefficient of $1.0$ for the paired relation available in the row.  After the amino-acid-composition control described as a per-codon null on synonymous classes, the recorded partial correlation with the $tRNA$-Leu copy-number ratio is also $1.0$ with $n=3$.  In symbols, the finite row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= 1.0,\\
r_{partial} &= 1.0,\\
n &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a small finite check, not an asymptotic law.  Its role is to show that, for the three loaded organism rows, the $CUN/UUR$ balance is not sign-uniform and the available matched copy-number coordinate has the recorded positive controlled association.

\paragraph{Contacts.}
Two observation channels are used.  The first is a per-organism codon-usage summary derived from curated coding-sequence tables.  It can test codon-count or codon-frequency distributions, but it does not observe elongation dynamics, peptide conformation, biochemical activity, or an organism-independent rule.  The second is an organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy annotation by anticodon family.  It can test whether a genome annotation supplies the relevant copy-number spectrum, but copy-number annotation alone is not a ribosome measurement and does not determine the realized translation process.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The row supports a bounded translation-layer statistic in the following narrow sense: among the three loaded organism rows, $L(o)$ changes sign, and the matched $tRNA$-Leu copy-number coordinate has a recorded positive controlled correlation with that codon-usage scalar.  The evidence is finite and organism-indexed.  It does not say that the sign reversal is universal, and it does not turn codon usage into a mechanistic model of translation.  No enacted translation event, folded structure, physical admissibility claim, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or cross-organism law follows from this row unless a separate contact supplies that additional measurement.
