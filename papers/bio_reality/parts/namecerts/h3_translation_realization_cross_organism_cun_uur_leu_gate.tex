\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family as an organism-indexed measurement problem. The carrier is not a protein, a ribosome trajectory, or a cellular mechanism. It is the finite row consisting of an organism label, the signed coordinate $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$, a matched tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio coordinate, and an amino-acid-composition control condition. The biological question is therefore deliberately narrow: after restricting attention to leucine usage and controlling against amino-acid composition, does the CUN-versus-UUR coordinate vary with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy spectrum?

The linked codon-window fact fixes the ambient codon set used by the record. The finite set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this ambient finite set, the leucine coordinate used here singles out the CUN block $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ and the UUR block $\{UUA,UUG\}$. The record does not infer a biological law from codon spelling. It only records whether the measured organism rows make the CUN/UUR statistic nonconstant and whether the matched tRNA-Leu coordinate points in the same direction after the stated control.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The verified run loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. The corresponding CUN/UUR log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign vector was
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,-1,+1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the sign is not uniform across the three rows. This is the first finite obstruction to treating the CUN/UUR split as a direction-free universal preference: yeast carries the negative sign in this sample, while the human and bacterial rows carry positive signs.

The matched statistic also records a Pearson coefficient of $1.0$ for the available paired coordinate, and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson entry is $1.0$ with $n=3$. These numbers are finite-row evidence that, in this small checked table, the CUN/UUR log-ratio and the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate move together after the declared control. The value $n=3$ is part of the result, not a hidden population claim. It means the contact is a three-organism statistic over the loaded rows, not a survey of all organisms.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The admissible conclusion is a bounded translation-layer contact: the CUN/UUR leucine usage coordinate is externally paired with a tRNA-Leu gene-copy coordinate, and in the loaded three-row table the sign is nonuniform while the controlled paired statistic is positive. The result is therefore a realization of a measured codon-usage/tRNA-copy relation, not an explanation of how translation occurs inside a ribosome.

The positive paired coefficient is reported together with the sign reversal. This matters because a raw positive or negative CUN/UUR sign by itself is not sufficient evidence for a translation-layer statement. A sign can be produced by sequence composition, sampling, annotation practice, or organism-specific coding bias. The packet becomes biologically interpretable only because the codon-usage coordinate is paired with an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate and because the record explicitly invokes the synonymous-class amino-acid-composition control.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No full translation event, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal cross-organism rule follows from this record. The table does not observe ribosome motion, protein output, folding, activity, fitness, or biochemical feasibility. It also does not say that the direction of the CUN/UUR effect is fixed across life; the loaded rows already show that the sign is not uniform. Any claim about protein structure, function, physical admissibility, mechanistic translation dynamics, or universality would require a separate contact designed for that layer.

Within that boundary, the finite result is ready to state: for the three loaded organisms, the CUN/UUR leucine log-ratio has signs $(+1,-1,+1)$, the sign-uniformity test returns false, and the matched tRNA-Leu relation records Pearson $1.0$ and controlled partial Pearson $1.0$ at $n=3$. The packet therefore supports a narrow organism-indexed codon-usage/tRNA-copy contact and rejects only the stronger reading that a CUN/UUR sign alone determines a universal biological direction.
