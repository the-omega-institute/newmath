\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu usage gate}
\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Question isolated by the row.}
This record concerns one bounded organism-level question about the leucine codon families $\mathrm{CUN}$ and $\mathrm{UUR}$.  The admissible object is not a protein, a ribosomal trajectory, or a functional phenotype.  It is the finite pairing of a codon-usage residual with organism labels and a matched $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ gene-copy readout.  The packet asks whether the signed quantity $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ changes across organisms and whether that change is aligned with the organism-level $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ copy-number ratio after controlling for amino-acid composition inside synonymous codon classes.

The finite code-window support used by the linked code-table check is the set $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ with cardinality $20$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This list is used only as a finite codon-label support for the Leu-module readout.  It does not itself provide a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Finite organism rows.}
The checked run loaded exactly three organism rows: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  In that order, the observed log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{human}}&=1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}}&=-0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}}&=1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs were $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  The finite row therefore passes the first break condition: the packet is not seeing a single organism-independent direction for $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$.

The same run reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic, and a positive partial correlation with the $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ ratio after amino-acid composition control:
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The status of the experiment $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$ is recorded as passed.  The result is therefore a finite three-row success for the specific cross-organism statistic: the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ sign is context-dependent in the rows at hand, and the measured direction is compatible with the matched $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ copy-number ratio under the stated control.

\paragraph{Interpretation allowed by the contacts.}
Two contacts are being used.  The codon-usage contact supplies per-organism codon-count or codon-frequency summaries for coding sequences.  The $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ contact supplies organism-level gene-copy counts by anticodon family.  Their joint contribution is a controlled organism-level association between synonymous Leu codon usage and a matched tRNA-copy spectrum.  That is enough to treat the row as a translation-layer statistical contact in the narrow sense of codon usage being compared with a translation-related genomic annotation.

The experiment also records the negative boundary explicitly.  The CUN/UUR codon-usage and $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ copy-number contact does not establish protein folding, biological function, physical admissibility, ribosome dynamics, or a global biological law.  The observed sign reversal is context-dependent rather than a universal directionality statement.  A raw log-ratio without amino-acid composition control is not accepted as a translation-layer statistic here, and this row does not promote the Leu-module association to structure, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within this finite table, the claim is ready in a narrow statistical sense.  The measured organisms give non-uniform signs for $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, with values $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$.  The controlled paired statistic reports $r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0$ over $n=3$ rows, and the ordinary Pearson value is also $1.0$.  The chapter therefore records a bounded cross-organism Leu codon-usage gate: CUN/UUR usage is permitted as an externally checked organism-level statistic paired with $\mathrm{tRNA}^{\mathrm{Leu}}$ copy-number data, but it remains silent about ribosome-mediated translation events, protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanistic causation, and universal law unless separate evidence is supplied for those stronger readings.
