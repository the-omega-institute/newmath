\subsection{Cross-organism leucine codon gate}
\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This record isolates the leucine codon pair formed by the CUN block and the UUR block as an organism-level translation-realization statistic.  The measured item is not a protein output and not a ribosome trace.  It is the paired finite datum consisting of a per-organism codon-usage residual for leucine and an organism-matched tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, with the organism label retained as part of the row.  The local coordinate is the signed quantity $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, interpreted only after amino-acid composition has been controlled by keeping the comparison inside the synonymous leucine class.

\paragraph{Code-window anchor.}
The finite coding window used by the packet has cardinality $20$.  The verified list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The CUN members visible in this list are $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, and $\mathrm{CUU}$, while the UUR members are $\mathrm{UUA}$ and $\mathrm{UUG}$.  Thus the local readout is not a free comparison across unrelated amino acids: it is a leucine-internal contrast whose sign can be asked separately in each organism.

\paragraph{Finite organism rows.}
The checked finite table contains three organism labels: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  For these three rows the recorded logarithmic CUN-over-UUR values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702).
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding sign vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  This is the central finite contrast: the yeast row carries the opposite sign from the human and bacterial rows in the supplied table.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu contact.}
The same run reports a positive organism-level association with the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio.  The ordinary Pearson value recorded for the paired statistic is $1.0$.  The amino-acid-composition-controlled statistic is a partial Pearson value of $1.0$ on $n=3$ rows.  In formula form, the finite result recorded here is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Pearson} &= 1.0,\\
\operatorname{partialPearson}_{\mathrm{AA\ control}} &= 1.0,\\
n&=3,\\
\mathrm{signUniform} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This supports only the narrow statement that, in the supplied three-row table, the CUN/UUR leucine residual is not sign-constant and is positively aligned with the organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated control.

\paragraph{Layer discipline.}
The classification of the record is $\mathrm{translation\_realization}$ only in the restricted sense of a codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact.  The row values do not observe ribosomal motion, do not measure translation speed, and do not identify a biochemical mechanism.  They also do not establish folded structure, physical admissibility, protein function, or any cross-organism law.  The sign reversal is therefore a context-sensitive finite observation, not a universal direction assigned to CUN or UUR.  Any stronger statement would require a separate measurement channel, such as direct ribosome profiling, protein-output data, structural evidence, or functional assays.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within the supplied finite rows, the packet passes its bounded test: the CUN/UUR leucine log-ratio has signs $+,-,+$ across the three named organisms, and the controlled tRNA-Leu association is recorded as positive with partial Pearson $1.0$.  The accepted content is exactly this organism-level paired statistic.  The record remains silent about mechanism, structure, function, physical feasibility, and universality.
