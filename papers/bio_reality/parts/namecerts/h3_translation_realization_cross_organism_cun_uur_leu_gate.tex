\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu Gate}\label{sec:bior-bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record concerns the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family, read at organism scale rather than at the level of an individual transcript or protein. The finite carrier is the residual statistic formed from $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, an organism label, a tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control. The biological readout is therefore narrow: per-organism codon-usage summaries are paired with per-organism tRNA-Leu annotation summaries, and the question is whether the sign and magnitude of the CUN/UUR residual move with the available tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after synonymous-class control.

The finite code-side support also records the associated codon set $M$ as a 20-element list,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|M|=20$. This list is not itself a translation measurement; it fixes the finite codon window in which the CUN component is explicitly present and can be compared with the UUR leucine component in the organism-level table.

\paragraph{Organism rows and measured signs.}
The recorded experiment loaded three organism rows: \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, and \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12}. The observed log-ratio vector for $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
and the corresponding sign vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the three loaded organisms. The finite result records \texttt{sign\_uniform = false}, so the packet does not collapse to a single cross-organism direction for CUN preference over UUR preference.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu statistic.}
The same finite row reports a positive paired statistic between the CUN/UUR log-ratio and the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio. The ordinary Pearson value is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
r=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the amino-acid-composition-controlled statistic is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0,\qquad n=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite table, the intended test therefore passes in the narrow sense that the non-uniform CUN/UUR sign pattern is accompanied by a positive partial correlation with the organism-level tRNA-Leu ratio after the stated control. Because $n=3$, this is a small-row contact rather than a population-level biological law. The result is best read as a bounded organism-table association: the CUN/UUR residual is not sign-invariant, and in the recorded rows it moves monotonically with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio under the available control.

\paragraph{Scientific scope.}
The admissible conclusion is limited to the tabulated codon-usage and tRNA-gene-copy summaries. These contacts report sequence-composition summaries and genome-annotation summaries; they do not observe ribosome traffic, translation rates, protein folding, protein activity, cellular fitness, or physical admissibility of a molecular state. In particular, the sign reversal across organisms is a context-dependent table fact, not a universal directionality principle. A raw CUN/UUR ratio by itself is also insufficient: without the paired tRNA-Leu statistic and the amino-acid-composition control, the ratio remains a sequence-composition coordinate and not evidence for a translation-layer event.

No realized translation event, folding state, physical admissibility claim, biological function, mechanistic ribosome claim, or universal cross-organism rule follows from this row alone. Any such stronger statement would require an additional contact of the appropriate kind, such as ribosome-profiling, protein-output, structural, functional, or physical measurements matched to the organism and condition. The present chapter records only that, in the three loaded organism rows, $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ changes sign across organisms and has recorded Pearson and controlled partial-correlation values of $1.0$ with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.
