\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu Gate}\label{sec:biorality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ family and the $UUR$ family as a bounded organism-level statistic, not as a direct molecular mechanism.  The carrier consists of four named coordinates: the signed usage coordinate $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control coordinate.  The finite organism list loaded for the checked row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
O = \{&\mathrm{homo\_sapiens},\ \mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae},\ \mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For these three rows the measured $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\ -0.24869078488829677,\ 1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding sign vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\ -1,\ +1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not constant across the finite organism set.  The recorded gate status for this statistic is $\mathrm{passed}$, with experiment identifier $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$ and run identifier $675\mathrm{fdddb}3187$.

\paragraph{Codon-window support.}
The linked codon-window fact supplies a finite codon set $M$ of cardinality $20$.  Its recorded contents are

\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA, AAG, ACA, ACG, AGA, AGG, AUA, AUG, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UCG, UGA, UGG, UUA, UUG\}.

The same row records $|M|=20$ and identifies this set as the union $WNR \cup CUN$.  In the present chapter this finite list is used only as a codon-level coordinate for naming the CUN side of the leucine split and its neighboring code-window context.  It is not used as evidence for folded structure, organismal function, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The operative relation is a cross-organism comparison between the signed usage coordinate and the tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate after amino-acid-composition control.  In the finite checked row, the raw Pearson entry is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r = 1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the recorded controlled statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{partial}} = 1.0, \qquad n = 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The interpretation is deliberately narrow.  The data support only the statement that, within this three-organism table and under the recorded control, the CUN/UUR log-ratio sign is non-uniform and its magnitude is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate.  The negative yeast-side sign prevents a one-directional rule, while the two positive signs show that reversal is context-sensitive rather than globally forced.  Because $n=3$, the row is best read as a finite contact establishing admissibility of this organism-level comparison, not as a population-level law.  No $p$-value is recorded in the finite row, so the statistic is not presented as a hypothesis-test result.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
Two external contact types are attached.  The first is a per-organism codon-usage table contact, whose resolution is codon-count or codon-frequency readback for coding sequences.  The second is an organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy contact, whose resolution is copy-number readback by anticodon family.  These contacts can test a bounded translation-layer statistic in the weak sense of comparing sequence usage and genome-annotation counts.  They do not observe ribosome dynamics, aminoacylation kinetics, protein folding, protein function, or physical realizability.  Consequently the row is admitted only as a paired codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
No translation process, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, functional claim, mechanistic claim, or universal biological law follows from this packet unless another contact separately supplies that information.  In particular, a raw $\log(CUN/UUR)$ value without amino-acid-composition control is not enough for the present claim, and a codon-label table alone cannot repair a missing organism-matched statistic.  Identifier spelling also carries no biological weight: the normalized probe name is merely a routing key for the finite record.  The only local conclusion is that the loaded three-row organism table has a non-uniform CUN/UUR sign pattern and a recorded positive controlled association with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate.
