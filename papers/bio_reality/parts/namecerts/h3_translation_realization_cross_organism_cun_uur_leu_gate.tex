\subsection{Cross-organism Leu codon-usage gate}
\label{subsec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record concerns the organism-indexed Leu coding pair $({\rm CUN},{\rm UUR})$.  The finite carrier is not a protein-level object and not a ribosome trace.  It is the residual comparison between two synonymous Leu codon families, together with an organism label, an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The intended readout is the sign and size of $\log({\rm CUN}/{\rm UUR})$ after the comparison has been paired with the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectrum.  Thus the chapter treats the Leu pair as a measured organism-level statistic, not as an autonomous biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Finite codon window.}
The auxiliary codon window used by the packet has cardinality $20$.  In the recorded run, the set $M$ is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&{\rm AAA},{\rm AAG},{\rm ACA},{\rm ACG},{\rm AGA},{\rm AGG},{\rm AUA},{\rm AUG},\\
&{\rm CUA},{\rm CUC},{\rm CUG},{\rm CUU},{\rm UAA},{\rm UAG},{\rm UCA},{\rm UCG},\\
&{\rm UGA},{\rm UGG},{\rm UUA},{\rm UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cardinality is $|M|=20$, matching the listed elements.  This codon window is only a coordinate device for the recorded Leu-family comparison.  It does not, by itself, assert a codon-usage law across organisms.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The controlled cross-organism run loaded three organism rows: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  The recorded Leu-family log ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\rm human}&=1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\rm yeast}&=-0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\rm ecoli}&=1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
Their signs were $(1,-1,1)$, so the finite table explicitly reports $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$.  The sign reversal is the main local observation: the Leu-family statistic is not constant across the three recorded organisms.  The run also reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio after amino-acid-composition control, with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The evidence supports only the following bounded statement.  In the three-row table, the organism-indexed value $\log({\rm CUN}/{\rm UUR})$ is non-uniform in sign, and the controlled paired statistic is recorded as positively aligned with the organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio.  The admissible mathematical form is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
&n=3,\qquad (\operatorname{sgn}\ell_i)_{i=1}^{3}=(1,-1,1),\\
&\operatorname{Pearson}=1.0,\qquad \operatorname{partial\ Pearson}_{\rm AA\ control}=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because the table has only three rows, the statement is a finite contact claim about these recorded organisms, not a population-level theorem.  The amino-acid-composition control is part of the claim; the raw log-ratio alone is not enough to carry the biological reading.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No realized translation event, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or universal cross-organism rule follows from this record alone.  The codon-usage table contact summarizes coding-sequence composition, and the tRNA-copy contact summarizes genome-annotation counts.  Those contacts are sufficient for a bounded codon-usage/tRNA-copy comparison, but they do not observe ribosome dynamics or protein output.  A stronger claim would require a separate matched contact, such as ribosome-profiling evidence for translation dynamics or a protein-level assay for structure or function.  The recorded sign reversal is therefore treated as context-dependent variation in an organism-level statistic, not as a general directionality principle.
