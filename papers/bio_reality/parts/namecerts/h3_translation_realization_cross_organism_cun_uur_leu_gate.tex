\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage as an organism-level translation contact}
\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The packet treats the leucine codon split between the CUN block and the UUR block as a finite organism-indexed statistic, not as a molecular mechanism. The carrier has four recorded coordinates: the signed value of $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, an external organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control. The relevant codon support also records the finite set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|M|=20$. Within that support, the leucine comparison uses the two synonymous blocks $CUN=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ and $UUR=\{UUA,UUG\}$. The coordinate is therefore not the assertion that either block is biologically prior; it is the finite signed readout obtained after grouping the observed codon-usage rows by organism and comparing the two leucine subfamilies.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The recorded validation run loaded three organism rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&homo\_sapiens,\quad saccharomyces\_cerevisiae,\quad escherichia\_coli\_k12.
\end{aligned}
$$
For these rows the observed $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs were
$$
\begin{aligned}
+1,\quad -1,\quad +1,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the finite row set explicitly rejects sign uniformity: $sign\_uniform=false$. This is the main finite obstruction to treating the CUN/UUR sign as a fixed organism-independent direction. Human and the bacterial row are positive in the recorded order, while the yeast row is negative in that same finite table. The statistic is therefore a context-sensitive organism-level readout.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu contact.}
The validation also records a positive paired association between the CUN/UUR log ratio and the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio. The reported Pearson coefficient is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{partial}=1.0,\qquad n=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the packet is admitted only in the narrow sense that the three-row organism table supplies a paired codon-usage and tRNA-copy-number contact whose finite statistic passes the stated test. The amino-acid-composition control matters: a raw leucine-block log ratio alone would only be a sequence-composition observation, whereas the accepted row is the controlled pairing of codon usage with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy spectrum.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The outcome supports a bounded translation-layer statistic: the sign of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ is not uniform across the measured organisms, and in the available three-row table its magnitude and sign align positively with the recorded tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated control. This is a contact between codon-usage summaries and genome-annotation summaries. It does not observe ribosomes in motion, does not measure translation speed, and does not determine which proteins are made under a cellular condition. The reported value $r_{partial}=1.0$ is therefore best read as an exact statistic for the provided finite rows, not as a population law.

\paragraph{Local exclusion.}
No process-level translation conclusion, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal cross-organism principle follows from this packet unless a separate contact supplies that additional measurement. In particular, the sign reversal is not a rule about all organisms, and the positive paired statistic is not evidence about protein folding or organismal function. The chapter records only the finite organism-matched codon-usage and tRNA-Leu gene-copy relation described above.
