\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record isolates one organism-level leucine-codon statistic and asks only for the part of it that can be checked against outside biological tables.  The carrier is the residual comparison between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ and $\mathrm{UUR}$ leucine codon families, paired with organism labels and with a control against amino-acid composition.  The readable coordinate is the signed quantity $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, and the paired biological coordinate is the organism-level ratio of annotated $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy support for the corresponding anticodon families.  The finite row therefore has four named distinctions: the log-ratio $\log(\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR})$, the $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and the amino-acid composition control.

\paragraph{Code-window support.}
The lower coding packet used by this record contains exactly twenty codons in the finite set $M$.  The verified enumeration is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $|M|=20$, and in this row $M$ is the union of the $\mathrm{WNR}$ codon block with the $\mathrm{CUN}$ leucine block.  This enumeration is not used as a mechanism for translation.  It only fixes the finite codon window from which the $\mathrm{CUN}$ side of the leucine comparison is named.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The checked cross-organism table contains three organism labels: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  For these three rows the recorded $\log(\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR})$ values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{human}}&=1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}}&=-0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}}&=1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{\mathrm{human}})&=1,\\
\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}})&=-1,\\
\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}})&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently the sign is not uniform across the three checked organisms.  The row therefore rejects the simpler reading in which $\mathrm{CUN}$ dominance or $\mathrm{UUR}$ dominance is treated as having a fixed direction independent of organism context.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The finite check reports a Pearson correlation of $1.0$ for the paired comparison in the loaded three-organism table.  After the amino-acid composition control, the partial correlation between the $\log(\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR})$ coordinate and the $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate is also recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite row, the admissible conclusion is therefore narrow: the organism-level leucine codon-usage residual varies in sign, and the controlled paired statistic is positive in the three loaded organisms.  The row is a contact between codon-usage summaries and genome-annotation summaries; it is not a direct measurement of ribosome motion, elongation speed, protein output, folding, or organismal phenotype.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The support condition is satisfied only at the level of a curated organism-matched statistic.  Codon-usage resources contribute per-organism coding-sequence count or frequency summaries, while $\mathrm{tRNA}$ annotation resources contribute organism-level $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy spectra.  The biological reading is that the $\mathrm{CUN}$ versus $\mathrm{UUR}$ leucine split is suitable here as a bounded cross-organism usage gate: its sign is context-dependent in the observed rows, and its magnitude is aligned with the recorded $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated control.  This is stronger than an isolated codon-label observation, because it uses paired organism rows, but it remains weaker than a translation-dynamics claim.

\paragraph{Scope limits.}
No in-vivo translation process follows from these numbers alone.  No ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, or universal biological law is inferred from the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ sign pattern or from the reported correlation.  The sign reversal is read as context dependence in this finite table, not as a global rule.  A later claim about translation dynamics would require a separate contact such as ribosome profiling, direct aminoacylation or decoding measurements, or another organism-matched assay that observes the relevant process rather than only codon counts and gene-copy annotations.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For the standalone BioReality paper, the row is ready as a bounded translation-adjacent realization record.  The accepted content is precisely the three-organism controlled statistic: loaded organisms $=3$, $\log(\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR})$ values $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$, non-uniform signs $1,-1,1$, Pearson correlation $1.0$, and controlled partial correlation $1.0$ with $n=3$.  The record should remain at this statistical contact level unless additional rows or direct process measurements are supplied.
