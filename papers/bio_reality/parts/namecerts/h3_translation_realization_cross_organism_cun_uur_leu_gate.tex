\subsection{Cross-organism Leu codon-usage gate}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the Leu synonymous split between the CUN and UUR families as an organism-indexed usage statistic, not as a direct statement about a molecular translation event.  The finite carrier consists of four recorded distinctions: the residual coordinate $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, an organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The admissible readback is therefore the sign and magnitude of the CUN-over-UUR residual, paired with an organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy spectrum after the synonymous-class control has been applied.

The same row reports $|M|=20$ and identifies this set with $WNR\cup CUN$.  Inside this chapter, that list is used only to delimit the codon families entering the Leu-gate statistic.  It does not by itself provide a ribosome measurement, a protein-output measurement, or a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The scientific content of the packet is narrow.  It says that, in the recorded finite sample, the CUN/UUR Leu usage residual changes sign across organisms and is positively aligned with the organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated control.  This is enough to keep the CUN/UUR split as a bounded translation-layer usage contact: the statistic is organism-indexed, paired to an annotation-derived tRNA-Leu coordinate, and not reducible to one raw codon-frequency sign.  The reversal in Saccharomyces cerevisiae relative to the two positive-sign rows is part of the evidence, because it blocks any reading in which one global direction is simply assumed.

\paragraph{Local limits.}
No stronger biological conclusion is licensed here.  The codon-usage table contact summarizes sequence composition, and the tRNA-Leu contact summarizes genome annotation.  From these rows alone one may not infer an actual translation event, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, protein function, a mechanistic law, or universal cross-organism directionality.  Any such claim would require a further organism-matched measurement at the relevant layer.  In particular, raw $\log(CUN/UUR)$ without amino-acid-composition control is not counted as the translation-layer statistic used in this chapter, and the observed sign reversal is reported as context-dependent rather than as a universal rule.
