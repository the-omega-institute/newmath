\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}\label{subsec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record concerns a three-organism Leu-module statistic rather than a protein-level or ribosome-level event.  The measured coordinate is the organism-indexed residual comparing the CUN Leu block with the UUR Leu block, paired with an organism label and a tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio.  The finite codon support used by the linked code-table check has cardinality $20$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The CUN Leu part visible inside this support is $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, while the UUR Leu part visible inside the same support is $\{UUA,UUG\}$.  The biological question is therefore not whether Leu exists in the genetic code, but whether the sign and magnitude of the organism-level statistic $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ are aligned with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after amino-acid composition has been controlled.

\paragraph{Observed rows.}
The finite run loaded exactly three organism rows: Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12.  The recorded log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{human}&=1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{yeast}&=-0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{ecoli}&=1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated sign vector was $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not constant across the three rows.  The run explicitly records $sign\_uniform=false$.  This matters because the packet is meant to separate an organism-indexed usage relation from a one-direction slogan: the same Leu module does not keep a single sign across these measured organisms.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The attached validation run reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired organism-level statistic available in the run.  It also reports a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio after the stated composition control:
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
r_{partial}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite table, the packet is therefore admissible as a bounded translation-layer readout: the CUN/UUR Leu codon-usage coordinate is not merely an isolated code-table label, since it is paired with an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate and the controlled statistic is recorded as positive.  The result is small-row and exact as reported; it should be read as a finite contact result, not as an asymptotic biological law.

\paragraph{Controls and exclusions.}
The amino-acid composition control is part of the claim.  A raw CUN-over-UUR log-ratio alone is not enough for this packet, because raw synonymous usage can reflect sequence composition, sampling, annotation conventions, and organism-specific coding-content biases.  The claim becomes admissible only at the level where codon-usage tables and tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra are paired per organism and then evaluated after the composition control.  The run also records that no promotion is made to protein folding, biological function, physical admissibility, or a global rule.  In particular, the sign reversal is context-dependent: it is not reported as universal directionality.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter establishes only the following finite statement: in the three-row table consisting of Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12, the CUN/UUR Leu log-ratio signs are non-uniform, with values $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$, and the paired controlled statistic with the tRNA-Leu copy ratio is recorded with partial Pearson correlation $1.0$ on $n=3$ rows.  No broader translation process, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility condition, biological function, or universal cross-organism principle follows from this packet unless a separate measurement supplies that layer.  Codon-usage summaries and tRNA gene-copy annotations are sufficient for this bounded organism-level statistic; they are not sufficient for claims about dynamics, structure, function, or universality.
