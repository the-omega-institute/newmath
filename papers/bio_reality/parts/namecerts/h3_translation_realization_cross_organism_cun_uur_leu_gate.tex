\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate}
\label{subsec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite claim surface.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ family and the $\mathrm{UUR}$ family as a bounded organism-level measurement problem. The carrier is the residual comparison of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ against an organism label and a matching $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy ratio, with amino-acid composition treated as a required control rather than as optional background. The biological object is therefore not a protein, a folding pathway, or a ribosome model. It is a finite cross-organism table whose admissible readout is the sign and magnitude of the leucine-family usage contrast after comparison with the available $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-number spectrum.

\paragraph{Codon-window support.}
The finite codon-side support used by the companion readback contains exactly $20$ codons. It is the union recorded for the $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ window:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality claim records both $|M|=20$ and the displayed member list. This support is used only as a finite coordinate frame for the leucine usage question. It does not by itself establish an organism-level biological process.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The verified run loaded three organism rows: human, budding yeast, and \emph{E. coli} K-12. Their recorded $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{human} &: 1.3514980212896148,\\
\mathrm{budding\ yeast} &: -0.24869078488829677,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli\ K\text{-}12} &: 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs were $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign was not uniform across the measured organisms. This is the first finite contact demanded by the packet: a single cross-organism direction for the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ contrast is not what the rows report.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The organism-matched statistic then compares the leucine codon-usage contrast with the available $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy ratio after the stated amino-acid composition control. In the recorded run, the ordinary Pearson value was $1.0$, and the controlled statistic was reported as a positive partial correlation with
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite table, the packet therefore passes its stated support test: the sign is not constant across organisms, and the magnitude/sign relation is positive against the paired $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate under the declared control. The claim is intentionally narrow. It says that these rows supply a bounded translation-layer usage contact, not that codon usage alone reveals a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Empirical contacts.}
Two observation classes are sufficient for the present record. Per-organism codon-usage summaries provide the $\mathrm{CUN}$ and $\mathrm{UUR}$ counts or frequencies in coding sequences. Organism-level genome annotations provide $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family. Both contacts are sequence-database or annotation summaries, so their noise profile is inherited from sampling, coding-sequence curation, genome annotation, and the choice of organisms included in the finite table. The claim remains tied to those summaries and to the paired statistic constructed from them.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
No realized translation event, ribosome dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this record unless an additional contact supplies that information directly. The non-uniform sign pattern is reported as context-dependent rather than as a law across all organisms. The $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-number contact is a genome-annotation readout, not a live ribosome readout. Likewise, the codon-usage contact is a sequence-composition summary, not a measurement of protein folding or downstream function. A raw $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ without the amino-acid composition control would not count here as the same translation-layer statistic.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite table is sufficient for a ready, bounded chapter claim: across the three loaded organisms, the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ leucine split is not sign-uniform, and the paired controlled statistic against the $\mathrm{tRNA}$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate is positive with $r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0$ at $n=3$. The result is a compact organism-level codon-usage and gene-copy contact. Its force ends at that contact.
