\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ family and the $UUR$ family as an organism-indexed usage statistic, not as a free-standing biological mechanism.  The carrier is the Leu-module codon-usage residual paired with external organism labels.  Its recorded distinctions are $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The internal data shape has only coordinate and relation components: it can compare signs and magnitudes across rows, and it can ask whether the comparison changes with an independently recorded tRNA-Leu copy spectrum.

\paragraph{Codon support.}
The linked code-table check fixes the finite codon window used by the packet.  The verified support set has cardinality $20$ and is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this support, the leucine comparison uses the two synonymous blocks $CUN=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ and $UUR=\{UUA,UUG\}$.  The code-table fact is only a label and membership check.  It does not by itself measure organism-level expression, tRNA abundance, ribosome traffic, protein output, folding, fitness, or function.

\paragraph{External rows.}
The finite validation row set contains exactly three organism labels: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  For these rows the recorded $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  The stored boolean value for uniformity is $false$, and the experiment status is $passed$.  The row count is small, so the packet is best read as a finite cross-organism contact: one yeast row reverses sign relative to the human and bacterial rows, and the reversal is part of the observed table rather than a universal directional law.

\paragraph{Controlled relation.}
The paired statistic compares the codon-usage residual with the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after the amino-acid-composition control required by the packet.  On the three organism rows, the recorded Pearson value is $1.0$.  The recorded partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio is also $1.0$ with $n=3$.  In formula form, the only checked relation asserted here is the finite-row association
$$
\begin{aligned}
partial\_pearson(\log(CUN/UUR),\;tRNA\text{-}Leu\ ratio\mid AA\ composition)=1.0,
\quad n=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a bounded translation-layer statistic in the narrow sense that codon usage and tRNA-gene-copy annotations are both translation-adjacent genomic summaries.  It is not a direct observation of translation kinetics, ribosome occupancy, peptide production, or codon-by-codon decoding rate.

\paragraph{Admissible reading.}
The packet supports the following local reading.  In the three recorded organisms, the $CUN/UUR$ leucine usage balance is context-dependent rather than sign-uniform, and its controlled relation to an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy ratio is positive in the stored finite table.  The scientific content is therefore a cross-organism paired-summary claim: codon usage and tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra can be placed in the same organism-indexed table, and the recorded rows show a perfect positive partial correlation after the stated composition control.

\paragraph{Local limits.}
No broader biological conclusion follows from this packet alone.  A raw $CUN/UUR$ sign, an identifier spelling, or a code-table membership check does not establish translation in the operational ribosome-mediated sense.  The present data also do not establish folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, or a universal cross-organism rule.  Such conclusions would require separate contacts of the appropriate kind, for example ribosome-profiling, proteomic, structural, biochemical, or perturbational evidence.  The sign reversal is consequently recorded as context dependence in a finite organism table, not as a general law of leucine decoding.
