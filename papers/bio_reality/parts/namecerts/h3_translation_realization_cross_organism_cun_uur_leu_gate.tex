\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:biorality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record isolates a narrow organism-level statistic for the leucine codon families $CUN$ and $UUR$.  The carrier is the residual coordinate formed from $\log(CUN/UUR)$ together with organism labels, tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratios, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The admissible reading is therefore not a molecular mechanism.  It is a finite comparison between codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu copy-number summaries across named organisms.  The verified row for $h3.translation\_realization.cross\_organism.cun\_uur\_leu\_gate$ loaded three organisms: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  The experiment identifier is $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$, and the row status is recorded as passed.

\paragraph{Finite codon coordinate.}
The companion finite fact $h0.M.equals.WNR.CUN$ fixes the codon window used by this packet.  Its recorded cardinality is $20$, and the finite set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The list contains the four $CUN$ leucine codons $CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU$ and the two $UUR$ leucine codons $UUA, UUG$.  In this chapter the set $M$ is used only to name the finite codon-side coordinate; it is not treated as a protein-output, ribosome, or phenotype measurement.

\paragraph{Measured row.}
For the three loaded organisms, the recorded $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702).
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row explicitly records $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$.  The finite table therefore rejects the simplest constant-sign reading for this three-organism sample.  It also records a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic available in the row and a partial Pearson value of $1.0$ after the amino-acid-composition control, with $n=3$.  No $p$-value is present in the row, and no larger-population inference is made from these three entries.

\paragraph{Controlled interpretation.}
The statistic is allowed to say only this: in the supplied finite sample, the sign of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ is not constant, and the recorded paired statistic is positive with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the amino-acid-composition control.  The control is essential because a raw codon-ratio sign by itself can reflect amino-acid composition and sampling choices rather than a translation-layer contact.  Thus the row supports a bounded codon-usage/tRNA-copy association for these organism labels, not a universal direction of leucine codon preference.

\paragraph{Contacts.}
The codon-usage contact is a per-organism sequence-summary contact: it reports codon counts or frequencies for coding sequences.  The tRNA contact is a genome-annotation summary: it reports organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family.  These two contacts are sufficient for the finite statistic above only when they are paired by organism and evaluated with the stated amino-acid-composition control.  They do not observe ribosome movement, translation speed, protein abundance, folding, biochemical activity, cellular function, or organismal fitness.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter makes no direct translation claim beyond the codon-usage and tRNA-copy statistic just described.  No folding, physical admissibility, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal cross-organism law follows from the three-row table.  A future claim at any of those levels would need its own contact, such as ribosome profiling for a translation-process claim, protein structural evidence for folding, biochemical measurement for function, or a broader independently sampled organism panel for population-level regularity.  The sign reversal is therefore reported as context-dependent finite evidence, not as a rule that must hold across life.
