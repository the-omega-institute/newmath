\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{subsec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record isolates one bounded organism-level statistic inside the leucine codon module.  The carrier is the residual pair formed from the per-organism value $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ together with the organism label and the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, with amino-acid-composition effects held at the synonymous-class level.  The finite contact therefore concerns a coordinate relation, not a direct ribosome movie and not a protein-output assay.  The coding window used by the adjacent codon-label check contains exactly $20$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The leucine part of this window separates the CUN family $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ from the UUR family $\{\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.  The biological question is whether this split behaves as a cross-organism usage residual that tracks an independently supplied tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after the synonymous-composition control is imposed.

\paragraph{Finite organism rows.}
The finite run loads three organism rows: $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  On these rows the recorded log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{human}} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{E.coli\ K12}} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated sign vector is $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  The run reports $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$ and records the non-uniformity explicitly rather than replacing it with a single universal direction.  The same finite check reports a Pearson value of $1.0$ for the paired statistic made available to the packet, and a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu ratio after the amino-acid-composition control:
$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= 3,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row set supports only the narrow statement that, in this curated three-organism table, the CUN/UUR log-ratio changes sign and the available controlled statistic is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The contact is a translation-layer usage contact in the restricted sense that codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations are both external organism-level sequence resources.  It is not a claim that the attached organisms share a conserved mechanistic law.  It is also not a claim that CUN or UUR codons realize a protein fold, a cellular function, a physical admissibility constraint, or a ribosome-mediated dynamical mechanism.  The finite statistic can be read as a controlled cross-organism alignment test: when CUN usage is compared against UUR usage inside the leucine synonymous class, the observed signs are positive for human, negative for yeast, and positive for $\mathrm{E.coli\ K12}$, while the recorded partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio is positive on the three rows.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No broader translation event, folding conclusion, physical admissibility conclusion, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this record unless an additional contact supplies that specific evidence.  Codon-usage tables summarize coding-sequence composition and sampling decisions; tRNA gene-copy tables summarize genome annotations.  These two resources are sufficient for the finite organism-level coordinate test stated above, but they do not observe ribosome dwell times, aminoacylation kinetics, protein structure, protein activity, organismal fitness, or a law valid outside the measured rows.  The correct scientific status is therefore a passed finite-row usage-and-copy-number alignment, with the context-dependent sign reversal kept visible rather than erased.
