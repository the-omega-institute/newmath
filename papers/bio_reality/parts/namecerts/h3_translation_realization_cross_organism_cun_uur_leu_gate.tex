\paragraph{Question.}
This record isolates a Leu-module question at organism scale.  The carrier is the residual comparison between the synonymous Leu codon families $CUN$ and $UUR$, paired with an organism label and with a separate control for amino-acid composition.  The biological readout is not a protein object and not a ribosome trace.  It is the finite statistic asking whether the per-organism sign and magnitude of $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ vary with the organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after the synonymous-class control has been applied.

The admissible probe name is the normalized packet identifier $\texttt{cross-organism.cun-uur-sign-correlates-with-trna-leu}$; mixed-case biological wording in an identifier is treated only as spelling metadata, not as biological evidence.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The result is a cross-organism usage-and-copy-number contact.  It supports the claim that the Leu-module coordinate is not merely an internal codon-window cue: on the loaded rows, it reaches an organism-indexed sequence-database statistic and a matched tRNA-gene-copy statistic.  The context dependence matters.  A uniform-sign rule is not what is observed; one of the three rows has the opposite sign.  The packet therefore records a conditional organism-level relation rather than a universal direction for CUN versus UUR usage.

\paragraph{Local limits.}
The finite row does not observe ribosome dynamics, translation events at a molecule, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a general law across all organisms.  The codon-usage tables summarize coding-sequence composition, and the tRNA-Leu counts summarize genome annotations by anticodon family.  Those contacts are enough for the bounded statistic above, but they do not by themselves establish a mechanism.  No stronger translation claim, folding claim, physical-realizability claim, function claim, mechanistic claim, or universality claim follows without an additional contact designed for that target.  In particular, raw $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ without the amino-acid-composition control is not treated as a translation-layer result.
