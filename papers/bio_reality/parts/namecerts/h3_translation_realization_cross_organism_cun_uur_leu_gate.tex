\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record isolates one organism-level leucine statistic rather than a molecular mechanism. The finite carrier is the residual pairing of a CUN-versus-UUR leucine codon-usage coordinate with organism labels and an independently recorded tRNA-Leu gene-copy coordinate. The claim id is \texttt{h3.translation\_realization.cross\_organism.cun\_uur\_leu\_gate}; the validation run is \texttt{675fdddb3187}, reported with status \texttt{passed} at \texttt{2026-06-01T08:06:33+00:00}. The measured organism list is exactly three rows: \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, and \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12}. The tested observable is not the amino-acid label ``leucine'' alone. It is the signed and scaled contrast between the CUN leucine block and the UUR leucine block, checked against an organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after an amino-acid-composition control.

\paragraph{Finite code window.}
The accompanying code-window check \texttt{a53ef0f36703} records that the finite set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is equal to the union of the WNR window with the CUN block. In the recorded order, the set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AAG},\texttt{ACA},\texttt{ACG},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{AUG},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\\
&\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UCG},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UGG},\texttt{UUA},\texttt{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality $|M|=20$ is used only to anchor the local codon vocabulary. It does not by itself say that the CUN/UUR contrast has biological force. The reality-facing statistic is built only after organism labels, per-organism codon-usage summaries, and tRNA-Leu copy-number summaries are paired in the same row.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The finite-row outcome records non-uniform CUN/UUR signs across the three organisms. The logged CUN-over-UUR ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{homo\_sapiens}: 1.3514980212896148,\\
&\texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}: -0.24869078488829677,\\
&\texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12}: 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign vector is $(1,-1,1)$ and the Boolean uniform-sign flag is false. The row-level reading is therefore not that CUN always dominates UUR, nor that UUR always dominates CUN. The yeast row reverses the sign relative to the human and \texttt{E. coli} K-12 rows in this finite record. That reversal is the central reality contact: the statistic behaves as a context-indexed organism coordinate, not as a universal direction attached to the codon pair.

\paragraph{tRNA-Leu pairing.}
The same run records a positive association with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio. The ordinary Pearson value is $1.0$, and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson value is also $1.0$ on $n=3$ rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{Pearson}} &= 1.0,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}\mid \mathrm{AA\ composition}} &= 1.0,\\
n &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $n=3$ is very small, this is a finite-row acceptance of the packet's narrow test condition, not a population-level biological law. The support is that, within the three recorded organism rows, the signed CUN/UUR residual varies with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate after the amino-acid-composition control recorded by the experiment. No p-value is present in the supplied finite row, so no significance threshold is claimed here.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The admissible reading is an organism-level contact between codon usage and tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra. Per-organism codon-usage resources supply coding-sequence count or frequency summaries, while genome-annotation resources supply tRNA-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family. These are both sequence-derived summaries. They are enough to test the bounded relation between $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ and a tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated control, but they do not observe ribosomes translating transcripts in real time and do not measure protein products. The finite evidence therefore supports only a checked cross-organism usage/copy-number statistic at the translation-facing layer.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No direct translation event, folding result, physical admissibility result, biological function, mechanistic ribosome account, or universal rule follows from these rows unless another contact separately supplies that evidence. In particular, the sign reversal is not promoted into a global rule, and the positive partial correlation is not treated as a proof of ribosome dynamics. A raw CUN/UUR log-ratio without amino-acid-composition control is also outside the accepted reading. The result remains a three-row, externally paired statistic: human and \texttt{E. coli} K-12 are positive on this coordinate, yeast is negative, and the recorded controlled association with tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio is positive in the supplied finite table.
