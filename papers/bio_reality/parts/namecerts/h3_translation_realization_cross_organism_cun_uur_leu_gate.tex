\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ and $UUR$ families as an organism-level statistic, not as a direct molecular mechanism.  The finite carrier is the paired residual consisting of a signed codon-usage coordinate, an organism label, and a matched tRNA-Leu gene-copy coordinate, with amino-acid composition held fixed at the synonymous-class level.  The row asks whether the sign and size of $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ vary with the external tRNA-Leu copy ratio across organisms.  The available organisms in the checked row are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{homo\_sapiens},\quad
\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae},\quad
\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the statistical contact has $n=3$ organism rows.  The recorded log-ratio values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\ -0.24869078488829677,\ 1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\ -1,\ +1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign is therefore not uniform across the finite table.  The paired statistic reports Pearson correlation $1.0$, and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson statistic against the tRNA-Leu copy ratio is also $1.0$ on $n=3$ rows.

\paragraph{Codon-window coordinate.}
The lower-level codon certificate used by the same packet fixes a twenty-element codon set $M$ as the union of the $WNR$ window with the $CUN$ family.  Its recorded cardinality is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|M|=20,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the explicit finite list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
&CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This list is used only as a discrete coordinate for naming the codon families that enter the organism-level comparison.  It does not by itself measure expression, ribosome motion, peptide output, folding, organismal phenotype, or biochemical feasibility.  In particular, the appearance of $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$ in $M$ is a codon-family registration fact; the translation-layer contact in this row comes only from the organism-matched usage and tRNA-copy table.

\paragraph{Readout.}
The empirical readout is the following bounded statement.  Within the three-organism table, $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ changes sign, and the finite paired statistic is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after the specified synonymous-class control.  Written as the local tested condition, the row supports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{sign}\bigl(\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)\bigr)\ \text{is not constant across organisms},
\end{aligned}
$$
and records the controlled association
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0\quad (n=3).
\end{aligned}
$$
The point of the row is the organism dependence: human and $E.\ coli$ K12 lie on the positive side of the finite sign readout, while budding yeast lies on the negative side in the recorded ordering.  The chapter therefore treats the CUN/UUR leucine split as a controlled cross-organism usage-and-copy-number contact, rather than as a universal codon rule.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No broader biological conclusion is licensed by this row alone.  The finite data do not establish a general translation process, ribosome-mediated dynamics, peptide folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Those claims would require separate contacts with the relevant measurement type, such as ribosome profiling for translation dynamics, structural assays for folding, biochemical or fitness assays for function, or a larger independently curated multi-organism panel for a law-like statement.  The present packet is therefore confined to the checked relation among codon-usage summaries, tRNA-Leu gene-copy summaries, organism labels, and the amino-acid-composition control.  Its positive result is a bounded finite-row observation: in the recorded three-organism table, the CUN/UUR leucine usage residual is not sign-uniform and is paired with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate by a reported controlled correlation of $1.0$.
