\subsection{Cross-Organism CUN/UUR Leucine Gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This BioReality record concerns the leucine synonym split between the CUN family and the UUR family at organism scale.  The measured carrier is not a protein object and not a ribosome trace.  It is a paired organism-level statistic whose visible coordinates are the signed residual $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The readout asks whether the CUN/UUR residual changes sign across organisms and whether its magnitude is aligned with the corresponding tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after the synonymous-class control has been applied.

\paragraph{Codon set used by the packet.}
The supporting code-table fact records a finite set $M$ of cardinality $20$.  In the run, this set is the union of the WNR codons with the CUN leucine family:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
     &CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The only role of this set here is to pin down the finite codon vocabulary being read by the packet.  It does not, by itself, supply an organism-matched usage table, a tRNA annotation, or any protein-output observation.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The finite cross-organism run loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  The recorded CUN/UUR log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the three organisms.  Human and $E. coli$ K-12 occupy the positive side of the measured residual, while budding yeast occupies the negative side.  This is the finite feature that makes the packet informative: the CUN/UUR comparison is not being treated as a single universal direction, but as an organism-indexed readout.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu statistic.}
The paired validation run reports $pearson = 1.0$ for the available three-row dataset.  It also records a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu copy ratio after the amino-acid-composition control, with $n=3$ and
$$
\begin{aligned}
partial\_pearson = 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite packet, the admissible claim is therefore narrow: among the three loaded organism rows, the sign and magnitude of the CUN/UUR leucine residual are aligned with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated control.  The validation status for this packet is recorded as $passed$ in experiment $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$ under run $675fdddb3187$.

\paragraph{Interpretive scope.}
The contact is a translation-layer statistical contact only in the limited sense that codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra are both externally indexed to organisms and both concern the coding apparatus.  The result does not observe translation itself, does not measure ribosome dynamics, and does not identify a biochemical mechanism.  It also does not establish folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal cross-organism law.  Any such stronger conclusion would require another contact with the corresponding biological object, such as ribosome profiling, protein-output measurements, structural assays, functional assays, or a broader organism panel with a separate test of generality.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The packet is accepted as a finite organism-level leucine-gate statistic.  Its positive content is the three-row, amino-acid-composition-controlled association between $\log(CUN/UUR)$ and the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate, together with the observed non-uniform sign pattern $(+1,-1,+1)$.  Its negative content is equally important: raw CUN/UUR sign alone is not a translation claim, and the paired statistic remains confined to the measured rows and contacts.  The record is therefore a bounded BioReality entry for cross-organism codon-usage realization, not a statement about protein structure, cellular function, physical viability, mechanism, or global biological necessity.
