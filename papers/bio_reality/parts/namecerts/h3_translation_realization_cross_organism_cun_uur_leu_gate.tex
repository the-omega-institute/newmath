\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}\label{sec:bior-h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite record.}
This chapter records a bounded cross-organism test for the leucine codon pair $CUN$ and $UUR$. The object under inspection is not a protein, a ribosome trajectory, or a universal biological mechanism. It is the organism-indexed statistic obtained by pairing the signed residual $\log(CUN/UUR)$ with an organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy ratio, while retaining an amino-acid-composition control so that a raw codon-count imbalance is not mistaken for a translation-level conclusion. The finite carrier has four named distinctions: the coordinate $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the coordinate for the $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and the control against amino-acid composition. Its internal shape is therefore a coordinate--relation packet: it asks whether the external per-organism sign and magnitude of the leucine codon-usage split co-vary with an independently recorded leucine $tRNA$ copy-number split.

\paragraph{Codon window used by the test.}
The linked finite codon fact fixes the ambient codon set $M$ as a set of $20$ codons. In the recorded run, $M$ is exactly the union of the $WNR$ block with the $CUN$ block, namely
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},\\
|M|&=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
The present chapter uses that finite codon window only as a coordinate constraint. It does not infer a biological process from the spelling of the codons. The biological contact is supplied only by organism-level codon-usage tables and organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy annotations.

\paragraph{Rows loaded in the organism-level check.}
The verification run loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. The observed $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{human}&=1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{yeast}&=-0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{ecoli}&=1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{human})&=1,\\
\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{yeast})&=-1,\\
\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{ecoli})&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the three measured organisms. The finite result records $sign\_uniform=false$, with the sign vector $(1,-1,1)$.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The test did not treat non-uniform sign alone as sufficient. The recorded statistic pairs the codon-usage residual with the $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy ratio and then reports the controlled association. For the three loaded organism rows, the ordinary Pearson value recorded for the paired statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial correlation is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{partial}&=1.0,\\
n&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite table, the packet therefore passes the intended gate: the $CUN/UUR$ sign is context-dependent rather than uniform, and the signed magnitude is positively associated with the matched $tRNA$-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated control. The experimental identifier attached to this row is $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$, and the recorded status is $passed$.

\paragraph{Interpretation allowed by the contacts.}
The result is a translation-layer statistic only in the narrow sense that it compares codon-usage summaries against a separate $tRNA$-Leu copy-number annotation at organism resolution. The codon-usage contact reports per-organism codon-count or frequency summaries over coding sequences. The $tRNA$ contact reports genome-annotation summaries for leucine $tRNA$ gene-copy families. These contacts are sufficient for the organism-level paired statistic above, but they are not direct observations of ribosome dynamics.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
No stronger translation claim follows from this row unless a separate contact supplies it. In particular, the data here do not establish an actual ribosome-mediated mechanism, do not establish protein folding, do not establish physical admissibility, do not establish biological function, and do not establish a universal law across organisms. The sign reversal is reported as context-dependent evidence inside the measured organism table, not as universal directionality. A raw $\log(CUN/UUR)$ value without the matched $tRNA$-Leu statistic and the amino-acid-composition control is outside the claim recorded here.

\paragraph{Failure conditions retained by the packet.}
The test would be broken by either of two finite outcomes: all measured organisms could have shown the same $\log(CUN/UUR)$ sign independently of the $tRNA$-Leu copy spectrum, or the sign could have varied while showing no controlled association with the $tRNA$-Leu copy ratio. The recorded table has neither failure mode: the signs are non-uniform, and the controlled partial correlation is positive with value $1.0$ on $3$ rows. The conclusion is therefore limited but ready: the finite organism table supports a cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate as an externally paired codon-usage and $tRNA$-copy statistic, with no promotion to structure, function, mechanism, physical admissibility, or universality.
