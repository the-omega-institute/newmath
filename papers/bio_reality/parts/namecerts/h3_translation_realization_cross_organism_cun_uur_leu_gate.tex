\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}
\label{subsec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the leucine codon pair $CUN/UUR$ as a bounded organism-level statistic.  The carrier consists of four recorded distinctions: the coordinate $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  The finite code-side support also records the set $M=WNR\cup CUN$ with cardinality $20$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not a free claim about leucine biology.  It is a paired coordinate test whose readout is the sign and magnitude of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ across named organisms, compared with an independently recorded $tRNA$-Leu copy-number spectrum under a synonymous-class composition control.

\paragraph{Organism rows and numerical readout.}
The finite organism list contains three rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  The recorded log-ratio values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(CUN/UUR)_{homo\_sapiens} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\log(CUN/UUR)_{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\log(CUN/UUR)_{escherichia\_coli\_k12} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding sign vector is $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not uniform across the three organisms.  The paired statistic reports Pearson correlation $1.0$ and an amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson value $1.0$ with $n=3$ organism rows.  The local numerical statement is therefore only this: in the available three-row table, the $CUN/UUR$ log-ratio changes sign across organisms, and the recorded controlled relation with the $tRNA$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate is positive in the reported finite table.

\paragraph{Interpretation at the translation layer.}
The admissible biological reading is narrow.  Codon-usage tables summarize per-organism coding-sequence composition, while $tRNA$ gene-copy data summarize genome annotations by anticodon family.  Their pairing can test whether a codon-usage residual is organized with a matching $tRNA$-Leu copy-number coordinate.  In this record, the observed non-uniform sign prevents a universal same-direction rule, while the reported positive partial correlation keeps the finite packet alive as a translation-realization contact at the level of codon use and gene-copy annotation.  The contact is not a measurement of ribosome motion, peptide elongation rate, protein abundance, folding, molecular function, or physical viability.

\paragraph{Local null boundary.}
The row explicitly blocks every stronger inference.  A raw $\log(CUN/UUR)$ value without the amino-acid-composition control is not enough for a translation-layer reading.  A sign reversal is not evidence for a universal organism law.  The paired codon-usage and $tRNA$-copy statistic does not establish ribosome-mediated mechanism, protein structure, folding, biological function, or physical admissibility.  Even the word translation is used here only for the bounded codon-usage/$tRNA$-copy contact; no broader translation-realization conclusion follows unless a separate contact supplies the missing ribosome or protein-output measurement.

\paragraph{Break and support conditions.}
The finite support condition is satisfied only by a multi-organism table containing both codon-usage summaries and organism-matched $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy spectra, evaluated with a synonymous-class control for amino-acid composition.  The claim would break if every measured organism had the same $\log(CUN/UUR)$ sign regardless of the $tRNA$-Leu spectrum, or if sign reversal appeared but the controlled relation with the $tRNA$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate vanished.  With the recorded rows, neither break condition is triggered: the sign vector is $(1,-1,1)$ rather than uniform, and the reported controlled partial Pearson statistic is $1.0$ for $n=3$.

\paragraph{Finite conclusion.}
This chapter records a small, organism-indexed leucine codon-use gate.  Its content is the finite fact that the three named organisms have non-uniform $CUN/UUR$ log-ratio signs and a reported positive controlled relation to the $tRNA$-Leu copy-ratio coordinate.  The scientific force is correspondingly limited: the row supports a bounded codon-usage/$tRNA$-copy association test and leaves mechanism, structure, function, physical admissibility, and universality outside the claim.
