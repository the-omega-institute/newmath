\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}\label{subsec:bior-h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite packet.} The record isolates an organism-level Leu codon-usage statistic rather than a claim about a completed biological mechanism. Its carrier is the residual pair consisting of the CUN-over-UUR logarithmic usage coordinate and an external organism label, with the readout checked against organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra after amino-acid composition control. The finite codon set attached to the packet has cardinality $20$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
     &CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The Leu module used by the probe is the CUN/UUR split, so the measured coordinate is $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ for each organism, not a protein-output observable. The three loaded organism rows are $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.} The run named $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$ reports three log-ratio values:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{human} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{yeast} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{ecoli} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
Their signs are $(1,-1,1)$, so the sign is not uniform across the finite organism table. The same run records $sign\_uniform=false$, Pearson correlation $1.0$, and a positive partial Pearson correlation $1.0$ with $n=3$ after the amino-acid composition control encoded by the per-codon synonymous-class null. Thus the packet is accepted only in the narrow sense that the organism-indexed CUN/UUR residual varies with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate on these three rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The finite result is a contact between two sequence-annotation summaries: codon-usage tables and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations. The sign reversal matters because it prevents the packet from being reduced to a single organism-independent direction. The positive controlled correlation matters because it keeps the row-wise variation tied to an external Leu-copy coordinate rather than to the raw CUN/UUR sign alone. On the available table, the evidence is therefore a bounded translation-layer statistic: human and $E.\ coli$ rows have positive CUN/UUR log-ratios, the yeast row has a negative log-ratio, and the controlled tRNA-Leu association remains positive across the three rows.

\paragraph{Local scope.} This chapter does not promote the statistic beyond the measured contacts. A CUN/UUR usage sign, even when paired with a tRNA-Leu copy-ratio correlation, does not establish an actual translation event, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, or a universal biological rule. Any such stronger statement would need an additional organism-matched contact, for example ribosome profiling for dynamics, protein-output evidence for folding or function, or a larger independently sampled cross-organism table for a universality claim. The present record is only the finite organism-level usage-and-copy-number gate described above.
