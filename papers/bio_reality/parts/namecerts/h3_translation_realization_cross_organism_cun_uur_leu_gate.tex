\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bior-h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record isolates an organism-level leucine coding statistic.  The carrier is not a protein, a ribosome trajectory, or a biochemical mechanism.  It is the finite table obtained by pairing, for each organism in the measured row set, the sign and magnitude of the leucine codon-usage contrast $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ with the corresponding organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, while treating amino-acid composition as a control variable.  The measured organism rows are $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$, so the present statistic has row count $n=3$.

The associated code-window fact records the finite codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|M|=20$.  In this chapter the relevant leucine subfamilies are the $CUN$ block and the $UUR$ block, where the measured readout is the per-organism logarithmic contrast between the two blocks rather than a claim about the meaning, folding, or activity of any translated product.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The validation row reports status $\mathrm{passed}$ for the cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate.  The three observed logarithmic contrasts are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{human}} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed sign vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{sgn}\ell_{\mathrm{human}},\operatorname{sgn}\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}},\operatorname{sgn}\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}})=(1,-1,1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  This is the finite obstruction to treating a single direction of the CUN/UUR contrast as an organism-independent rule.

\paragraph{Controlled contact.}
The recorded paired statistic gives Pearson correlation $1.0$ for the available three-row table and a positive partial Pearson correlation, after the amino-acid-composition control, of $1.0$ with $n=3$.  In notation, the observed controlled row can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{Pearson}} &= 1.0,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}\mid \mathrm{AA\ composition}} &= 1.0,\\
n&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a small finite check, not an asymptotic population theorem.  Its role is to make the cross-organism leucine contrast externally inspectable: the CUN/UUR usage contrast is compared with the organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum, and the raw codon sign alone is not promoted into a biological conclusion without the paired tRNA-copy contact and the amino-acid-composition control.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The accepted content is deliberately narrow.  The packet supports the statement that, in the three recorded organisms, the CUN/UUR leucine contrast changes sign across organisms and that the paired controlled statistic is positive in the supplied row set.  The human row is positive, the yeast row is negative, and the \emph{E. coli} K12 row is positive.  This context dependence is the important biological readback: the contrast is organism-indexed and cannot be collapsed into a universal direction.

The statistic may be discussed at the translation-realization interface only in this limited sense: it concerns codon usage and tRNA-Leu gene-copy summaries attached to named organisms.  It does not by itself observe ribosome dynamics, prove that translation has occurred in a particular cellular condition, identify a molecular mechanism, establish protein folding, establish physical admissibility, establish biological function, or state a universal law.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate contact with the corresponding type of observation.

\paragraph{Null boundaries.}
The same finite data also fixes the blocked directions.  A CUN/UUR sign by itself is insufficient; without the organism-matched tRNA-Leu copy-number statistic and the amino-acid-composition control, the row is only a codon-usage contrast.  A mixed-case or otherwise malformed packet identifier is likewise not biological evidence.  A standard genetic-code lookup can confirm codon labels, but it cannot repair a missing cross-organism paired statistic, cannot supply ribosome or protein-output data, and cannot turn this leucine contrast into a structure, function, mechanism, physical-admissibility, or universality claim.

\paragraph{Finite conclusion.}
Within the supplied finite table, the gate is ready as a bounded organism-level record.  The concrete row content is $|M|=20$, three measured organisms, non-uniform CUN/UUR signs $(1,-1,1)$, logarithmic contrasts $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$, Pearson correlation $1.0$, and controlled partial Pearson correlation $1.0$ at $n=3$.  The conclusion is therefore not that leucine coding has a universal direction, but that this three-row cross-organism contact records a context-dependent CUN/UUR contrast whose controlled association with tRNA-Leu copy ratio is positive in the supplied data.
