\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate}
\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Packet scope.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ block and the $\mathrm{UUR}$ pair as an organism-level usage statistic tied to a separately recorded tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum. The admissible object is not an isolated codon label and not a protein-output assertion. It is the paired finite row consisting of an organism label, a controlled value of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, and the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio. The recorded organisms are $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$. The finite test is therefore a three-row cross-organism readout, not a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The companion codon carrier check records a twenty-element set $M$ assembled as $\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$. Its enumerated content is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|M|=20$. Within this carrier, the leucine comparison isolates the four $\mathrm{CUN}$ codons $\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}$ against the two $\mathrm{UUR}$ codons $\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}$. The purpose of the carrier is only to keep the codon-window bookkeeping finite and inspectable before the organism-level statistic is evaluated.

\paragraph{Measured rows.}
The completed run reports that the cross-organism data were loaded for the three named organisms and that the sign of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ is not uniform. The recorded log-ratio row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the packet passes the first finite obstruction: the observed direction does not collapse to a single sign across the measured organism list. This is important because the claim being tested is context-sensitive. A uniform sign would have made the proposed organism-gated leucine split uninformative for this table.

\paragraph{Controlled association.}
The same run records a positive association between the CUN/UUR statistic and the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated amino-acid composition control. The reported Pearson value is $1.0$, and the reported partial Pearson value is $1.0$ on $n=3$ organism rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=1.0,\\
r_{\mathrm{partial}}&=1.0,\\
n&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
No $p$-value, confidence interval, bootstrap interval, or independent held-out organism row is present in the finite record. For that reason the result should be read as a passed finite gate over the available rows, not as a population-level statistical law. The numerical perfect correlation is carried exactly as reported, but its strength is bounded by the row count and by the curated nature of the paired organism table.

\paragraph{Reality contacts used by the row.}
Two kinds of biological contact are used. First, per-organism codon-usage tables provide sequence-composition summaries for coding regions. Second, genome-annotation summaries provide organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family. These contacts are adequate for the narrow question asked here: whether the organism-level CUN/UUR usage statistic varies with the recorded tRNA-Leu copy spectrum after the amino-acid composition control. They are not ribosome-dynamics measurements, protein-structure measurements, functional assays, or physical-admissibility assays.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The packet establishes only the finite, organism-matched statistic just described. The successful row does not by itself assert a ribosome-mediated mechanism, does not infer folding, does not establish protein function, does not certify physical admissibility, and does not turn the sign reversal into a universal rule. Even the translation-layer wording is confined to the attached codon-usage and tRNA-copy contacts: without a separate experimental contact, no additional translation event, mechanism, structure, function, or global organism law follows. The run explicitly records that the CUN/UUR and tRNA-Leu contact does not establish protein folding, does not establish biological function or physical admissibility, does not promote the statistic to a global biological law, and treats sign reversal as context-dependent rather than universally directed.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For the finite table supplied here, the cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate is ready at the bounded statistic level. The carrier check supplies $|M|=20$ and an explicit codon list; the organism table supplies three rows; the signs are non-uniform; and the reported controlled association with tRNA-Leu copy ratio is positive with $r_{\mathrm{partial}}=1.0$ on $n=3$. The remaining uncertainty is not a contradiction in the packet. It is the expected boundary of a small curated organism table: stronger biological interpretation would require additional organism rows and independent contacts aimed at ribosome behavior, protein output, structure, function, or physical admissibility.
