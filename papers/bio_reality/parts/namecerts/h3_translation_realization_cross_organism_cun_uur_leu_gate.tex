\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage as a bounded organism-level contact}
\label{subsec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the leucine codon pair $CUN/UUR$ as a finite organism-indexed statistic, not as a direct account of ribosome behavior.  The carrier consists of four recorded distinctions: the coordinate $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control.  Its internal data are only coordinate and relation data.  The readback question is therefore narrow: for each organism in the finite panel, compare the sign and magnitude of the $CUN/UUR$ log-ratio with the corresponding $tRNA$-Leu copy-number ratio after controlling for amino-acid composition through the synonymous-codon background.

\paragraph{Code-window anchor.}
The auxiliary finite code-window fact fixes the $M$ carrier as the union of the $WNR$ family with the $CUN$ family and gives cardinality $20$.  The recorded list is
$$\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}$$
This $20$-element list supplies only a finite codon-coordinate background.  It does not by itself measure organismal usage, $tRNA$ availability, translation kinetics, protein output, or biological function.

\paragraph{Observed finite run.}
The validation run for claim $h3.translation\_realization.cross\_organism.cun\_uur\_leu\_gate$ loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  The recorded experiment id is $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$, with run id $675fdddb3187$ and status $passed$.  The three observed values of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ were
$$\begin{aligned}
1.3514980212896148,\quad -0.24869078488829677,\quad 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}$$
Their signs were $+1,-1,+1$, so the sign was explicitly not uniform across the measured organisms.  The finite panel also records a Pearson value of $1.0$ and a positive partial correlation with the $tRNA$-Leu ratio, with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$ after the stated amino-acid-composition control.  Within this small row set, the packet therefore realizes a bounded organism-level contact: the leucine codon-usage residual reverses sign across organisms and aligns positively with the recorded $tRNA$-Leu copy-ratio statistic under the recorded control.

\paragraph{Reality contacts.}
Two contact types are used.  The codon-usage contact reads per-organism codon-count or codon-frequency summaries from coding-sequence resources.  Its resolution is per-organism codon-usage readback.  The $tRNA$ contact reads organism-level $tRNA$-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family from genome annotation resources.  Its resolution is organism-level $tRNA$-Leu copy-number readback.  These contacts are sufficient for the finite statistical comparison just stated: organism label, $CUN/UUR$ usage residual, $tRNA$-Leu copy-ratio, and the amino-acid-composition control all live at the same organism-indexed level.

\paragraph{Null edges and scope.}
The same record also preserves blocked edges that should not be confused with the passed finite statistic.  A raw $CUN/UUR$ sign alone is not a translation-realization claim.  Identifier spelling, including mixed-case spellings of $tRNA$-Leu in packet ids, is not biological evidence.  A curated standard-code table can confirm codon-label readback, but it cannot repair the absence of an organism-matched usage and $tRNA$ statistic.  Here that paired statistic is present only in the finite run described above; outside that run, the older null boundary remains active.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The result is a constrained translation-layer usage contact, not a mechanism.  It does not show ribosome-mediated translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal cross-organism law.  The sign reversal is context-dependent in the three-row panel, not a universal direction of effect.  No conclusion about structure, function, mechanism, or global biological necessity follows unless a separate contact supplies the relevant measurement.  The admissible conclusion is only this: in the recorded three-organism dataset, the $CUN/UUR$ leucine usage residual is non-uniform and has a positive recorded partial correlation with the organism-level $tRNA$-Leu copy-ratio after amino-acid-composition control.
