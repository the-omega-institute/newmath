\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bior reality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record isolates a small organism-indexed leucine statistic.  The biological object is not a ribosome trace or a protein-output measurement; it is a paired finite table whose rows are organism labels equipped with codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu copy-number summaries.  The relevant coordinate is the leucine split between CUN codons and UUR codons, read through the signed quantity $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ and compared with an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.  The claim is therefore deliberately narrow: the packet asks whether the sign and magnitude of the CUN/UUR leucine residual vary with the organism's tRNA-Leu copy spectrum after amino-acid-composition control.  The controlled statistic is allowed to count as bounded evidence at the translation-realization layer only at this codon-usage and tRNA-copy contact surface.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The checked organism rows are $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  Thus the paired cross-organism statistic has $n=3$ rows.  The observed CUN/UUR log-ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with sign vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1,\;-1,\;1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  In the same finite table, the reported Pearson contact with the tRNA-Leu ratio is $1.0$, and the amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson value is also $1.0$ on $n=3$ rows.  These are finite-row values, not asymptotic estimates: the result records an exact pass condition for this three-organism table rather than a population-level biological law.

\paragraph{Codon-module support.}
The associated code-table check fixes the relevant CUN-containing module as a finite set of $20$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\cr
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded size is $|M|=20$, matching the row value $M\_cardinality\_equals\_20=20$.  This module check does not by itself measure organism-specific expression or tRNA abundance.  Its role here is to keep the CUN/UUR coordinate attached to a concrete codon list before the organism-level statistic is evaluated.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the pass.}
The finite result supports a specific statement: in the three loaded organisms, the CUN/UUR leucine split is context-sensitive, and the observed direction and magnitude agree with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number statistic after the stated amino-acid-composition control.  The non-uniform signs block any reading in which CUN dominance or UUR dominance is treated as a universal direction.  Human and \emph{E. coli} rows have positive log-ratio signs in the recorded ordering, while the yeast row has a negative sign.  The reported controlled partial correlation of $1.0$ says that, for this finite table, the paired tRNA-Leu ratio accounts for the observed ordering under the control model used by the validation run.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter does not infer a ribosome-mediated mechanism from the statistic.  It also does not infer protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or universality.  A codon-usage table summarizes sequence composition and sampling decisions; a tRNA gene-copy table summarizes genome annotation.  Neither contact observes translation kinetics directly.  Therefore no broader translation process, folded structure, admissible physical state, functional phenotype, mechanistic pathway, or global biological rule follows from these rows unless a separate measurement supplies that contact.  In particular, raw $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ without amino-acid-composition control is not treated as a translation-layer result here.

\paragraph{Failure modes retained.}
The packet remains sensitive to two explicit breaks.  First, if future organism rows made the CUN/UUR sign uniform independently of the tRNA-Leu spectrum, the present context-sensitive reading would fail.  Second, if sign reversal remained but lost the controlled association with tRNA-Leu copy ratio, the statistic would reduce to an unpaired codon-usage contrast rather than a translation-realization contact.  Identifier spelling and codon-label lookup cannot repair either failure: a normalized probe id is only a routing constraint, and a standard code table can confirm codon labels without supplying organism-matched usage or tRNA-copy statistics.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For the finite table actually recorded, the claim is ready in its bounded form.  The validation run loaded three organism rows, found non-uniform signs, reported Pearson $1.0$, reported controlled partial Pearson $1.0$ with $n=3$, and preserved the rule that no structure, function, mechanism, physical admissibility, or universal law is promoted from the statistic.  The chapter therefore records a narrow cross-organism leucine codon-usage/tRNA-copy contact, not a general biological theory of leucine translation.
