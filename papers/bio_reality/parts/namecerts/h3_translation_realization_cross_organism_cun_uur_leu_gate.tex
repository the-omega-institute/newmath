\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Packet and measurement frame.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ block and the $UUR$ block as an organism-level measurement problem. The carrier is a finite table whose rows pair an organism label with a controlled codon-usage residual for leucine and an organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio. The coordinate read from the codon-usage side is $\log(CUN/UUR)$, and the external comparison coordinate is the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio grouped by anticodon family. The finite contact recorded here is therefore not a claim about a ribosome trajectory, a folded protein, or a downstream function. It is a bounded question about whether the sign and magnitude of the leucine split vary across organisms in the same direction as the available tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after amino-acid composition control.

\paragraph{Finite codon support.}
The associated code-table check fixes the internal codon support used by the packet. In run $a53ef0f36703$, the recorded set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$\{AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}$.
The four $CUN$ leucine codons present in this list are $CUA$, $CUC$, $CUG$, and $CUU$, while the $UUR$ leucine codons present are $UUA$ and $UUG$. This finite support is used only to locate the leucine module inside a codon table. It does not by itself supply organism-level usage, tRNA copy-number evidence, translation dynamics, structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Observed cross-organism rows.}
The paired organism-level experiment $675fdddb3187$ loaded three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. The recorded log-ratio vector for $\log(CUN/UUR)$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
and the corresponding sign vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row set does not support a uniform-sign reading: the recorded value of $sign\_uniform$ is false. The yeast row is negative in this table, while the human and $E.\ coli$ K-12 rows are positive. The packet therefore records a context-dependent reversal, not a global direction assigned to the $CUN/UUR$ split.

\paragraph{Controlled tRNA-Leu association.}
For the same three-row table, the recorded Pearson statistic is $1.0$. The amino-acid-composition-controlled entry records a positive partial correlation with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio, with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$. In the limited finite table, this passes the stated probe condition: the sign is not uniform across organisms, and the controlled association with the tRNA-Leu ratio is positive. The result is deliberately narrow. The row count is only three, the statistic is a paired organism-level summary, and no p-value, confidence interval, lambda parameter, regression coefficient beyond the reported Pearson quantities, or independent replication row is recorded in the supplied data. The correct reading is therefore a small finite contact between codon usage and tRNA-Leu gene-copy summaries, not a general law of leucine encoding.

\paragraph{Local admissible conclusion.}
Within this packet, the admissible conclusion is that the cross-organism $CUN/UUR$ leucine split is ready as a bounded translation-layer statistic: the finite data contain three organism rows, a non-uniform sign vector $(+1,-1,+1)$, and a reported positive amino-acid-composition-controlled partial correlation of $1.0$ with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio. This is enough to keep the organism-level codon-usage/tRNA-copy relation as an externally checked measurement contact for the named packet.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
Nothing stronger follows from these rows. The codon-usage table contact summarizes sequence composition, and the tRNA contact summarizes genome annotation. Those contacts do not observe ribosome dynamics, do not establish an actual translation process, do not show protein folding, do not certify physical admissibility, do not establish biological function, and do not justify a universal cross-organism rule. A stronger claim would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind, such as ribosome-profiling evidence for dynamics or protein-level evidence for structure or function. In the present record, sign reversal is reported only as organism-dependent behavior in a three-row statistic, and raw $\log(CUN/UUR)$ without the amino-acid-composition control is not treated as a biological realization claim.
