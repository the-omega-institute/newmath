\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate}\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record treats the leucine coding split between the CUN block and the UUR block as a bounded organism-level statistic.  The carrier is not a protein, a ribosome state, or a kinetic model.  It is the paired datum consisting of the signed codon-usage residual $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, the organism label, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and the amino-acid-composition control that keeps the comparison inside synonymous leucine usage.  The attached finite run loaded exactly three organism rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O &= \{\mathrm{homo\_sapiens},\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae},\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}\},\\
|\mathcal O|&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
For these rows the measured CUN-over-UUR logarithmic residuals were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with sign vector $(1,-1,1)$.  Thus the sign is not constant across the three organisms.  The finite run also reports $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}=\mathrm{false}$ and status $\mathrm{passed}$ for the experiment named $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$.

\paragraph{Codon-window coordinate.}
The companion codon-window fact fixes the finite set $M$ as a twenty-codon set, namely
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cardinality is $|M|=20$.  Within this finite coordinate, the leucine pair is the comparison between the four CUN codons $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ and the two UUR codons $\{\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.  The role of this coordinate is to define the count ratio that is later compared with organism-level tRNA-Leu copy spectra.  It does not, by itself, supply any cellular mechanism or protein-level conclusion.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The test asks whether variation in $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ tracks the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio once amino-acid composition has been controlled by staying within the leucine synonymous class.  On the three recorded organism rows, the reported Pearson correlation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the reported partial Pearson correlation after the composition control is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{partial}}&=1.0, & n&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite table therefore supports only the narrow statement that, in these three rows, the CUN/UUR residual changes sign across organisms and the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number statistic is positively aligned with that residual after the stated control.  The support is intentionally row-bound: a perfect coefficient on $n=3$ is a sharp finite observation, not a population theorem.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The admissible readback is an organism-level relation among codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations.  Human, budding yeast, and \textit{E. coli} K-12 do not show one universal CUN/UUR sign in the recorded table: the middle row is negative while the other two rows are positive.  The packet therefore rejects the raw universal-sign reading and keeps the interpretation at the level of a controlled cross-organism usage statistic.  The statistic is allowed to say that the sign and magnitude of the leucine CUN/UUR residual are paired with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio contact in the finite run.  It is not allowed to say that the codons are thereby realized by ribosome dynamics, that a folded structure is obtained, that a protein is physically admissible, that any biological function follows, or that a general law holds across organisms.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The contact with codon-usage tables is a sequence-composition contact, and the contact with tRNA-Leu gene-copy numbers is a genome-annotation contact.  Neither contact observes translation kinetics, ribosome occupancy, protein folding, biochemical activity, or organism-wide necessity.  Consequently, no translation event, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, functional claim, mechanistic claim, or universal biological claim follows from this chapter unless another independently specified contact supplies that missing evidence.  The present chapter closes only the finite paired-statistic reading: three organism rows, a non-uniform sign vector $(1,-1,1)$, a reported Pearson value $1.0$, and a reported controlled partial Pearson value $1.0$ on $n=3$.
