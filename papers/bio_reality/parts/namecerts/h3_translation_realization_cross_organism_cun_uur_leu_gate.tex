\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the CUN block and the UUR block as a bounded organism-level translation statistic.  The carrier is the residual coordinate obtained from a per-organism codon-usage table, paired with organism labels and a tRNA-Leu copy-number readback.  The finite claim is not that a ribosome mechanism has been reconstructed.  It is that, in the attached finite row, the sign and magnitude of the CUN-over-UUR leucine usage residual vary with an organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio after the amino-acid composition control named in the packet.

\paragraph{Finite alphabet contact.}
The linked code-table fact fixes the relevant codon window before the cross-organism statistic is read.  The finite list $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The CUN leucine block contributes $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$, while the UUR leucine block contributes $UUA,UUG$.  The point of the construction is not the spelling of those codons as labels, but the organism-indexed comparison between the two synonymous leucine routes after the control for amino-acid composition has been imposed.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The validated row loads three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  For these three rows, the recorded logarithmic CUN/UUR residuals are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\ -0.24869078488829677,\ 1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\ -1,\ +1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row explicitly rejects sign uniformity: $sign\_uniform=false$.  The sign reversal is a context-sensitive observation inside this three-organism table; it is not a universal direction assigned to CUN versus UUR.

\paragraph{Controlled statistic.}
The attached validation run is $675fdddb3187$, under experiment id $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$.  Its reported Pearson scalar is $1.0$.  The amino-acid-composition-controlled partial-correlation entry is
$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= 3,\\
partial\_pearson &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite table, the statistic therefore supports the intended bounded readout: the CUN/UUR leucine residual is non-uniform across the measured organisms and is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate after the stated control.  The value $1.0$ is reported only for the three loaded organism rows, so the chapter records a perfect finite-row association rather than an inferred population law.

\paragraph{Admissible interpretation.}
The evidence basis is a pair of external contact types: per-organism codon-usage summaries and organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations.  These contacts are appropriate for testing an organism-level codon-usage distribution and a tRNA-copy spectrum.  They are not direct observations of ribosome kinetics, translation dynamics at a locus, protein folding, protein function, or physical admissibility of a molecular structure.  The statistic may therefore be read as a translation-layer usage contact only in the narrow sense that codon usage and tRNA gene-copy summaries are compared at matched organism resolution.

\paragraph{Local exclusion.}
No stronger biological claim follows from this row.  The result does not establish folded structure, biological function, physical realizability, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or a global rule about leucine codon choice.  It also does not allow the raw sign of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ to stand alone as a translation claim, because the amino-acid composition control is part of the admissible statistic.  Identifier shape, mixed-case biological notation, and codon-label readback are likewise insufficient as evidence; the biological contact is supplied only by the paired codon-usage and tRNA-copy rows recorded above.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For claim $h3.translation\_realization.cross\_organism.cun\_uur\_leu\_gate$, the finite row is ready as a bounded cross-organism leucine usage record.  It reports three organism rows, a non-uniform sign vector, a Pearson value of $1.0$, and a controlled partial Pearson value of $1.0$ at $n=3$.  The result is scientifically usable only at this bounded level: an organism-indexed codon-usage and tRNA-copy association, not a mechanism, structure, function, physical-admissibility result, or universal biological law.
