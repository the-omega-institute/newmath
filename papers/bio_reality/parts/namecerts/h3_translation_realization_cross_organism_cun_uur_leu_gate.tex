\subsection{Cross-Organism CUN/UUR Leucine Gate}
\label{subsec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ family and the $UUR$ family as a finite organism-indexed statistic.  The carrier is the paired datum consisting of an organism label, the controlled residual direction of $\log(CUN/UUR)$, and the corresponding organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio.  The tested distinction is not a codon-label identity alone; it is the relation between a per-organism synonymous leucine usage imbalance and a separately curated tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum, with amino-acid composition treated as a control rather than as a hidden source of signal.

The finite codon module used by the linked code-table contact is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified cardinality is $|M|=20$, and the row reports that this set is exactly the union $WNR \cup CUN$ in the code-window bookkeeping used by the packet.  For the present chapter, that identity only fixes the local codon-coordinate context.  It does not by itself test cross-organism translation behavior.

The paired statistic against the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio reports Pearson correlation $1.0$.  After the amino-acid composition control specified for the packet, the partial Pearson correlation is also $1.0$ on $n=3$ organism rows.  The experiment identifier is $\mathrm{cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation}$, the run identifier is $675fdddb3187$, and the finite-row status is $\mathrm{passed}$.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite result supports a narrow organism-level statement: within the three loaded rows, the $CUN/UUR$ leucine usage imbalance changes sign across organisms, and the signed magnitude is positively aligned with the recorded tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated amino-acid composition control.  The yeast row carries the negative sign, while the human and bacterial rows carry positive signs.  The chapter therefore records context dependence of the leucine codon split rather than a single direction shared by all organisms in the sample.

This is a translation-layer contact only in the restricted statistical sense just described.  The evidence is made of codon-usage summaries and tRNA gene-copy annotations.  Those contacts can test whether an organism-indexed codon-usage residual is paired with an organism-indexed tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum.  They do not observe ribosome motion, elongation kinetics, protein abundance, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, or a universal biological rule.  In particular, the sign reversal is not promoted to a general law of leucine translation, and the positive correlation reported on three rows is not a mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The admissible conclusion is the following finite claim.  There are three loaded organism rows; their $\log(CUN/UUR)$ signs are $+,-,+$; the sign is therefore non-uniform; and the paired tRNA-Leu statistic records Pearson $1.0$ and amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson $1.0$ on $n=3$.  This supports the use of the $CUN/UUR$ leucine split as a bounded cross-organism probe at the codon-usage and tRNA-copy level.

No broader biological assertion follows from this chapter alone.  No full translation-process realization, no ribosome-mediated mechanism, no folding claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no protein-function claim, and no universality claim is licensed unless another contact supplies the relevant measurement.  The code-table identity $M=WNR\cup CUN$ supplies a finite codon-coordinate frame; the organism rows supply the paired usage-and-copy statistic; neither component turns the result into a structural, functional, kinetic, or global biological theorem.
