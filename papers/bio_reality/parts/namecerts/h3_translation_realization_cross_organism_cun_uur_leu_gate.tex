\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Packet.}
This chapter records a bounded organism-level contact for the leucine codon pair $CUN/UUR$. The carrier is the residual coordinate obtained from the sign and magnitude of $\log(CUN/UUR)$, paired with an organism label, an amino-acid-composition control, and an independently supplied tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio. The intended readback is therefore not a protein claim and not a ribosome-dynamics claim. It is a small cross-organism statistic: after controlling for the fact that the compared codons belong to the same amino-acid class, the packet asks whether the organism-level CUN-over-UUR residual agrees in direction with an independently tabulated tRNA-Leu copy-number contrast.

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The associated code-window certificate uses a finite set $M$ of size $20$. In the recorded row, $M$ is exactly the union of the $WNR$ window and the $CUN$ leucine block, listed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
     &CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The scalar certificate reports $|M|=20$. This finite codon carrier is used only to locate the CUN side of the leucine comparison inside a named coordinate system. It does not, by itself, measure translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or cross-organism universality.

\paragraph{Observed rows.}
The paired organism table loaded three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. For these three rows, the measured log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1,\;-1,\;1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the sampled organisms. The recorded value of $sign\_uniform$ is $false$, so the finite row passes the first check in the packet: the CUN/UUR contrast is not being treated as a fixed direction shared by every organism in the table.

\paragraph{Controlled tRNA contact.}
The same run reports a positive organism-level association between the CUN/UUR residual and the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio. The ordinary Pearson value is $1.0$. The controlled statistic is recorded as a positive partial correlation with tRNA-Leu ratio, with $n=3$ and $partial\_pearson=1.0$. These values are read only at the resolution of the finite table. They support the local statement that, in this three-organism packet, the non-uniform CUN/UUR sign and magnitude are aligned with the independently supplied tRNA-Leu copy-number contrast after the synonymous-class control used by the row.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The admissible biological content is narrow. The codon-usage table contact says that curated per-organism resources report codon-count or codon-frequency summaries for coding sequences. The tRNA contact says that genome annotation resources report organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy counts by anticodon family. These two contacts can test a cross-layer relation between codon-usage distribution and tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectrum at organism resolution. They do not observe a translation event, ribosome kinetics, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law. The recorded run also keeps this boundary explicit: the CUN/UUR and tRNA-Leu contact does not establish protein folding; it does not establish function or physical admissibility; it does not promote the translation-realization layer to a global law; and the sign reversal is context-dependent rather than universal directionality.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The packet is accepted as a bounded translation-layer statistic, not as a mechanistic or structural conclusion. Its finite support consists of the three loaded organisms, the log-ratio vector $(1.3514980212896148,-0.24869078488829677,1.0086640520051702)$, the sign vector $(1,-1,1)$, the non-uniform-sign flag $false$, the Pearson value $1.0$, and the controlled partial-correlation row $n=3$, $partial\_pearson=1.0$. The associated finite codon carrier has $20$ entries and identifies the CUN block inside the named codon window. Within that limited evidence envelope, the result is ready: CUN/UUR is an admissible organism-level coordinate for a controlled codon-usage versus tRNA-Leu copy-number comparison. No translation process, folding behavior, physical admissibility, biological function, ribosome-mediated mechanism, or universal rule follows without a separate measurement that directly supplies that higher claim.
