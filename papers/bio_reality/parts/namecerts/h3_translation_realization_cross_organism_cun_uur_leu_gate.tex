\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bior reality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record isolates a leucine-codon statistic at organism resolution.  The measured coordinate is the signed residual $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$, paired with an organism label and with a tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, while amino-acid-composition effects are held outside the raw synonymous count comparison.  The packet is therefore not a claim about a protein product.  It is a claim about whether the organism-level usage of the two leucine codon families has a non-uniform sign and whether the signed magnitude tracks an independently curated tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after the relevant compositional control is imposed.

\paragraph{Codon universe used by the row.}
The companion finite code-read check fixes the internal codon set $M$ as the union of $WNR$ with $CUN$.  In this row, the recorded size is $20$, with $M$ listed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The separate cardinality field also reports $M\_cardinality\_equals\_20=20$, matching the listed set size.  The CUN members appearing in this finite row are $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$, and the UUR leucine members appearing in the same listed universe are $UUA,UUG$.  This is the finite codon-label substrate on which the leucine-family comparison is attached; it is not, by itself, a ribosomal observation.

\paragraph{Organism rows and signed residuals.}
The cross-organism run reports data loaded for three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  The recorded values of $\log(CUN/UUR)$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs $(1,-1,1)$.  Thus the sign field is explicitly non-uniform: $sign\_uniform=false$.  The statistic separates the yeast row from the human and bacterial rows at the sign level, so the packet cannot be collapsed to a universal direction of CUN preference or UUR preference across the observed organisms.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-Leu contact.}
The same validation row records a positive association with the external tRNA-Leu copy-ratio contact.  The ordinary Pearson entry is $1.0$, and the controlled entry is reported as a positive partial correlation with $n=3$ and $partial\_pearson=1.0$.  In the finite-row language of this chapter, that means the available organism-matched table supports a bounded statement: within these three loaded organisms, the sign and magnitude of the CUN/UUR leucine statistic are consistent with the recorded tRNA-Leu gene-copy-ratio ordering after the stated amino-acid-composition control.  The status field for the cross-organism validation is $passed$, with experiment id $cross\_organism\_cun\_uur\_leu\_gate\_validation$ and run id $675fdddb3187$.

\paragraph{What is admitted.}
The admitted content is a cross-organism coordinate relation: codon-usage tables and tRNA-Leu copy-number summaries jointly support a finite statistic about leucine codon-family usage.  The empirical contacts have the correct resolution for this claim because they operate at organism-level codon usage and organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra.  The observation is also intentionally sign-sensitive: the row records reversal rather than uniformity, and the reversal is interpreted as context-dependent variation, not as an invariant biological law.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
No stronger biological conclusion is licensed by this row.  The codon-usage contact is a sequence-summary contact, and the tRNA copy-number contact is a genome-annotation summary.  Consequently this packet does not establish translation as an observed ribosome-mediated process, does not establish folding, does not establish physical admissibility of a protein state, does not establish biological function, does not supply a molecular mechanism, and does not state a universal rule.  Any one of those stronger conclusions would require a separate contact at the appropriate resolution, such as a direct ribosome, protein-output, structural, physical, functional, or broader comparative measurement.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The row is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because it contains two finite checks that meet at the same leucine gate: the $20$-element codon universe check for $M=WNR\cup CUN$, and the three-organism CUN/UUR versus tRNA-Leu paired statistic.  Its positive result is narrow: a passed organism-level translation-realization statistic for the CUN/UUR leucine gate, with non-uniform sign and positive controlled association in the recorded three-row table.
