\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage gate}
\label{subsec:bior-h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This packet concerns the leucine synonymous split between the CUN codon block and the UUR codon pair, read organism by organism through codon-usage summaries and matched tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations. The finite carrier is the residual coordinate whose visible coordinates are $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$, the organism label, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid-composition control. The admissible biological reading is therefore narrow: the packet tests whether the sign and magnitude of the CUN-over-UUR log ratio vary with the organism-level tRNA-Leu copy spectrum after controlling for amino-acid composition. It is not a direct observation of ribosome motion, peptide production, folding, cellular role, or molecular feasibility.

Its role in this chapter is to keep the CUN and UUR symbols attached to an explicit finite codon vocabulary before any organism-level statistic is evaluated.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The validation run loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. For these rows, the recorded CUN-over-UUR log ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702).
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding signs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the uniform-sign hypothesis is false in the finite table: $sign\_uniform=false$. The yeast row is negative while the human and bacterial rows are positive. This is the first reality contact carried by the packet: the leucine codon split is not represented here as a single direction that survives unchanged across the three organism labels.

\paragraph{Paired tRNA-copy statistic.}
The same validation run reports a positive association between the CUN/UUR coordinate and the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio. The ordinary Pearson statistic recorded for the three loaded rows is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
After the amino-acid-composition control named by the packet, the recorded partial statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{partial}=1.0,\qquad n=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite result is not merely that the signs differ; it is that, in this three-row table, the controlled CUN/UUR coordinate is perfectly positively ordered with the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate. Because $n=3$, the statistical content is small and should be treated as a bounded organism-table contact, not as a population-level law. No $p$-value, confidence interval, permutation score, likelihood ratio, or independent replication row is recorded in the finite data supplied here.

\paragraph{What the row establishes.}
The chapter records a constrained cross-organism translation-layer statistic: in the available three-organism table, the CUN/UUR leucine-usage log ratio changes sign across organisms and has a recorded positive controlled correlation with the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio. The contacts used are per-organism codon-usage summaries and organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations. These contacts are suitable for testing a codon-usage distribution paired with a tRNA-copy spectrum. They are not suitable for testing folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a global rule about all organisms.
