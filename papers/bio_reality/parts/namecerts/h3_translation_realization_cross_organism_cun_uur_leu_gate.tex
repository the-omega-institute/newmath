\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine gate}
\label{sec:bior reality-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the leucine split between the $\mathrm{CUN}$ and $\mathrm{UUR}$ codon families as an organism-indexed finite table, not as a direct assertion about a translating ribosome. The measured carrier has one row for each loaded organism, with the three organism labels $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$. For each row the observed coordinate is the signed log-ratio $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, paired with an organism-level tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio and interpreted only after controlling for amino-acid composition inside synonymous classes. The finite codon universe attached to the companion code-read fact has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Inside this set, the leucine gate is localized to the contrast between the four $\mathrm{CUN}$ codons $\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}$ and the two $\mathrm{UUR}$ codons $\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}$. The remaining members of $M$ provide the surrounding code-read context and do not by themselves supply organism-level translation evidence.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The loaded cross-organism run reports three log-ratio values:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{human}} &= 1.3514980212896148,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= -0.24869078488829677,\\
\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}} &= 1.0086640520051702.
\end{aligned}
$$
Their signs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\operatorname{sgn}\ell_{\mathrm{human}},\operatorname{sgn}\ell_{\mathrm{yeast}},\operatorname{sgn}\ell_{\mathrm{ecoli}})=(1,-1,1),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms. This is the first finite check: the packet is not describing a constant preference for $\mathrm{CUN}$ over $\mathrm{UUR}$ across the loaded rows. The paired statistic then reports Pearson correlation $1.0$ and amino-acid-composition-controlled partial Pearson correlation $1.0$ with $n=3$. These values are read as a finite-row positive contact between the CUN/UUR usage coordinate and the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio coordinate in this small organism panel. The result is therefore a bounded organism-level statistic: it supports the presence of a non-uniform leucine codon-usage sign whose magnitude is aligned with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate in the recorded rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation scope.}
The admissible scientific content is narrow. The result can be cited as a finite cross-organism codon-usage/tRNA-copy association for the leucine module, after the stated amino-acid composition control. It does not say that a ribosome event has been observed, and it does not identify a molecular mechanism. It also does not establish protein folding, physical admissibility of any molecule, biological function, or a universal biological law. In particular, the sign reversal is context dependent: the negative yeast row prevents any reading in which the CUN/UUR direction is treated as globally fixed. A later claim about translation in the stronger biochemical sense would need its own contact, such as ribosome profiling, direct tRNA charging or decoding measurements, or another organism-matched measurement that observes the relevant translation process rather than only sequence composition and gene-copy summaries.

\paragraph{Null edges and blocked promotions.}
The available contacts are sufficient for codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra, but they remain summaries of sequence databases and genome annotation. They do not observe folding, function, physical admissibility, or a universal rule. The packet therefore stays at the organism-level translation-realization statistic only in the weak sense that codon usage and tRNA-copy coordinates are externally paired for the measured organisms. A raw $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$ value without the composition control is not enough for this claim. Likewise, the normalized probe identifier is only a routing device for the record; spelling such as $\mathrm{tRNA}$ in prose is not evidence, and an identifier shape cannot repair a missing organism-matched statistic. The finite data here pass the stated small-panel check, while all stronger biological readings remain unavailable until a separate measurement supplies them.
