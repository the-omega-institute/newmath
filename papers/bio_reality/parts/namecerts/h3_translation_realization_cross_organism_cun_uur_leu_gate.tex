\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR leucine usage and tRNA-Leu copy ratios}
\label{subsec:h3-translation-realization-cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Record.}
This record isolates a finite organism-level test for the leucine codon split between the CUN family and the UUR family.  The carrier is the residual statistic obtained from per-organism codon usage after retaining the organism label, the sign and magnitude of $\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR})$, the corresponding tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, and an amino-acid composition control.  The biological object is therefore not a protein, a ribosome trajectory, or a cellular phenotype.  It is the paired table entry in which codon-count summaries and tRNA-Leu annotation summaries are read at the same organism index.  The test asks whether the CUN/UUR leucine split changes sign across organisms and whether the controlled statistic is positively aligned with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.

\paragraph{Finite codon window.}
The associated code-window check records the finite set $M$ as the union of WNR and CUN codons, with cardinality $20$.  In the recorded order this set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The leucine component used by the present test is the contrast between the four CUN codons $\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}$ and the two UUR codons $\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}$.  This finite window supplies the codon-coordinate vocabulary for the statistic, but the coordinate by itself is not a biological conclusion.  The biological content enters only when each organism row carries both codon-usage data and a tRNA-Leu copy-number readback.

\paragraph{Observed rows.}
The validation run loaded three organism rows: $\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}$, $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{escherichia\_coli\_k12}$.  For these rows the recorded CUN/UUR log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with sign vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\;-1,\;+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms.  The recorded field $\mathrm{sign\_uniform}$ is $\mathrm{false}$, so the finite table rejects the simplest uniform-sign reading of the CUN/UUR leucine contrast.  The cross-organism statistic is therefore context-dependent at the level of these three organism labels.

\paragraph{Controlled association.}
The paired statistic records Pearson correlation $1.0$ for the organism-level CUN/UUR signal against the tRNA-Leu ratio, and it records a positive partial correlation after amino-acid composition control with $n=3$ and partial Pearson value $1.0$.  In the notation of this subsection, the reported finite-row relation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{partial}}(\log(\#\mathrm{CUN}/\#\mathrm{UUR}),\;\mathrm{tRNA\mbox{-}Leu\ ratio}\mid \mathrm{AA\ composition})=1.0,
\qquad n=3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a sharply bounded table result: it says that, in the recorded three-row contact, the controlled leucine codon-usage residual is positively aligned with the corresponding tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio.  The result is not interpreted as an asymptotic estimate, and no p-value is recorded in the row data supplied here.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The contact is admissible only as an organism-level usage-and-annotation statistic.  Codon-usage tables summarize coding-sequence composition, and tRNA gene-copy counts summarize genome annotations by anticodon family.  Those contacts can test a cross-layer relation between codon usage and a tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum, but they do not observe translation dynamics directly.  In particular, no realized translation process, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or universal biological law follows from this finite table unless another independent contact supplies that missing layer.  The non-uniform sign is therefore reported as a local organism-indexed reversal, not as a general rule for leucine coding in all organisms.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Within the supplied finite rows, the packet is supported as a bounded translation-layer statistic: the CUN/UUR leucine log-ratio is non-uniform across human, yeast, and $E.\ coli$ K-12, and its controlled association with tRNA-Leu copy ratio is recorded as positive with partial Pearson value $1.0$ over $3$ rows.  The admissible conclusion is exactly this paired codon-usage/tRNA-copy relation.  The record deliberately stops before claims about protein output, folding, function, physical realization, ribosome dynamics, or organism-independent lawhood.
