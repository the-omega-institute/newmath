\subsection{Cross-organism leucine codon usage and tRNA-Leu copy-number contact}
\label{subsec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
This record treats the leucine codon pair $CUN/UUR$ as a bounded organism-level statistic, not as a direct observation of ribosome kinetics or protein output. The carrier is the residual comparison between the $CUN$ leucine block and the $UUR$ leucine block, paired with organism labels and with an external tRNA-Leu gene-copy readout. The distinguished coordinates are $\log(CUN/UUR)$, the tRNA-Leu gene-copy ratio, the organism label, and an amino-acid-composition control. The finite computation is therefore about whether a per-organism sign and magnitude in codon usage can be checked against an independently recorded tRNA-Leu copy-number spectrum after the synonymous-class control has been imposed.

\paragraph{Codon window.}
The associated code-window fact fixes the finite set used by the companion codon-label check. The verified set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
     &CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The leucine block used in the present organism-level test is the split between $CUN=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ and $UUR=\{UUA,UUG\}$. This codon set is a finite coordinate system for the measurement; it is not itself a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed rows.}
The recorded cross-organism run loaded three organism rows: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. For these rows the computed $\log(CUN/UUR)$ values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\ -0.24869078488829677,\ 1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\ -1,\ +1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms. The finite run reports $sign\_uniform=false$ and $status=passed$ for the bounded statistic.

\paragraph{Paired statistic.}
The same run records a positive association with the tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio after the stated amino-acid-composition control. The reported Pearson value is $1.0$, and the reported partial Pearson value is also $1.0$ on $n=3$ organism rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= 1.0,\\
r_{partial} &= 1.0,\\
n &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within this finite table, the admissible conclusion is narrow: the non-uniform $CUN/UUR$ sign and magnitude are consistent with the paired tRNA-Leu copy-number coordinate under the recorded control. The row count is small, so the statistic should be read as a contact-bearing finite check rather than as a population-level law. No p-value, confidence interval, lambda parameter, hit-rate field, or broader module classification is recorded in this row, so none is asserted here.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result does not certify an actual translation event, a ribosome-mediated mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or organism-independent universality. It also does not license the raw $\log(CUN/UUR)$ ratio without the amino-acid-composition control as a translation-layer conclusion. The only positive content is the finite organism-matched contact: codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations jointly support the bounded comparison reported above. Any claim about translation dynamics, protein folding, physical realization, function, or a universal cross-organism rule would require a separate contact specifically measuring that target.
