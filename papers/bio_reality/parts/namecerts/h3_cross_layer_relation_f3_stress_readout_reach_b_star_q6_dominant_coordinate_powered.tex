\subsection{The reach of independent readouts for the $f3\_stress$ coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-readout-reach}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The claim concerns a single coordinate-level readout problem inside the nine-coordinate $B^\ast_{Q6}$ panel.  For Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the target vector is the abundance component carried by the $f3\_stress$ coordinate after residualizing log-protein abundance against the declared control basis $Z$.  The finite ledger records the base readout dictionary, the larger independent readout panel, the selected best subset, and a selection-aware permutation null.  The experiment used $512$ candidate subsets, exactly all subsets of the nine requested independent readouts including the empty subset, with best-subset selection replayed inside every permutation trial.  The row counts are not decorative: the base dictionary used $n=1800$ joined rows, while the full and best-subset panels used $n=1056$ joined rows.  Thus the conclusion is about the finite common-row projection problem actually computed, not about all yeast proteins or all possible stress-associated biology.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The reported coverage is the orthogonal projection coverage of
$P_{f3}=\operatorname{Proj}_{x_{f3\_stress}}(Z\hbox{-residualized }\log_{10}\hbox{ protein abundance})$
by $Z$-residualized independent readout subsets.  The numerical comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{coverage}_{\mathrm{base}} &= 0.1897720314893696,\\
\operatorname{coverage}_{\mathrm{best}} &= 0.22618445168998694,\\
\operatorname{coverage}_{\mathrm{full}} &= 0.22618445168998694,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{best-base}} &= 0.03641242020061733.
\end{aligned}
$$
The squared target norm changes with the row set: $\|P_{f3}\|^2=43.831314029512704$ on the base join and $\|P_{f3}\|^2=38.18587535550906$ on the best-subset join.  The coordinate fraction of the target is therefore $0.19788872448081418$ on the base rows and $0.20738990828488693$ on the best rows.  The unified residualized design rank was $24$; the base readout dictionary had rank $3$, while the best and full independent readout panels had rank $19$.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The finite null is selection-aware.  It permutes row labels of $P_{f3}$, reruns the same best-subset search, and then compares the observed selected coverage against that replayed null distribution.  With $120$ permutations, the naive null had mean $0.017376213916576056$ and $95\%$ cutoff $0.028169169068843265$, while the selection-aware null had mean $0.018736269159555625$, $95\%$ cutoff $0.02697141300639046$, and maximum $0.03878006220013416$.  The observed gain over the base dictionary, $0.03641242020061733$, is above the selection-aware $95\%$ cutoff but below the maximum null realization.  The finite verdict is therefore a bounded positive reach statement: the expanded independent readout panel partially reaches the $f3\_stress$ abundance component beyond the base dictionary, but the effect remains small on the total target scale.

\paragraph{Residual magnitude.}
Even after the best subset is admitted, the unexplained fraction is $0.7738155483100131$.  In the language of the finite projection ledger, the strongest admissible statement is that roughly $22.6\%$ of this coordinate target is reachable under the selected independent readout panel, leaving roughly $77.4\%$ outside the tested dictionary.  This is consistent with the broader residual-compressibility ledger: across danio, Escherichia coli, human, and yeast, the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary did not compress the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ abundance component strongly, with a reported median compressibility of $0.026$ and organism-level unexplained fractions $0.9756319042010854$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9736442326306781$, and $0.8676586345294415$.  The yeast value is the least unexplained among those four, but it is still dominated by residual mass.

\paragraph{Relation to direction-aware $H$ ranking.}
The same finite program also ranks candidate readouts for the modeled-translation-complement residual using absorbed energy $A_H$, coordinate-support direction cosine $C_H$, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Score}(H) = A_H C_H - \lambda\operatorname{DL}(H),
\qquad \lambda=0.01,
\qquad \operatorname{DL}(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
This score is a regularized descriptive ranking rule.  It is not a mechanism score.  In yeast, the top raw direction-aware entry was mRNA with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and Score $=-0.008853737772559931$.  Localization had larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.011225105608516642$, but with $12$ columns it carried $\operatorname{DL}=3.700439718141092$ and Score $=-0.03223607310225219$.  In human, complex membership led the one-column ranking with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, and Score $=-0.0034339698846599055$, while localization again had substantial raw alignment, $A_HC_H=0.006894914063528885$, but Score $=-0.030109483117882037$ after the multi-column description-length penalty.  In danio, measured TE and mRNA were the two strongest one-column entries, with $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$ and $0.01073458857797759$.  No shared universal readout $H$ is produced by this table.

\paragraph{Translation-side context.}
The measured translation-efficiency mediation ledger shows strong organism dependence rather than a uniform translation channel.  The measured mediated fractions were $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ for Escherichia coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.0$ for danio after clipping a raw negative value.  The translation-complement ledger gives modeled-complement residual fractions $d=0.21173219572706703$ for yeast, $0.6629960038578532$ for Escherichia coli, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.874628721049266$ for danio, $0.9400341806302775$ for chicken, and $0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis.  These values explain why the yeast $f3\_stress$ reach result must be read locally: yeast has a smaller modeled-translation-complement residual than the vertebrate rows, and the independent readout panel partly reaches only a yeast coordinate target.

\paragraph{Boundary-set comparison.}
A separate boundary-set test supplies a useful contrast.  The fixed Window6 $R$ set showed an avoided abundance-preference signature in all $15$ computed organisms: $15$ avoided, $0$ preferred, and $0$ zero among directional non-ties.  The one-sided avoided sign-test value was $3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and the two-sided value was $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  Among individually significant organisms, the direction was again $6$ avoided, $0$ preferred, and $0$ zero.  That boundary result is a cross-organism sign regularity for the fixed $R$ set.  It does not convert the $f3\_stress$ reach statistic into a universal biological rule; it only shows that some codon-window geometry can carry reproducible abundance-side association under its own finite test.

\paragraph{Additional reality checks.}
The optimal-codon conservation test classified the cross-organism direction pattern as clustered, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ over $15$ computed organisms and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The N-end turnover negative-control-style ledger joined $3898$ Mathieson human rows and reported a consistent-sign fraction of $0.5$ with $12000$ actual permutation computations.  Yeast turnover had a reported join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, joining $3640$ turnover-abundance-CDS rows; its turnover absorption was only $0.0009086031889966327$.  Human turnover used $8057$ joined rows from $8164$ mapped turnover proteins and had absorbed energy $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, below its dof-matched null $95\%$ value $0.0004342285066808464$.  These checks support the same local reading: available fetchable readouts can be attached and scored, but they do not close the residual as a single mechanism.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The ready statement is narrow.  For yeast, a larger independent readout dictionary reaches the $f3\_stress$ abundance component better than the base dictionary by $0.03641242020061733$ coverage units, and this improvement exceeds the selection-aware $95\%$ null cutoff of $0.02697141300639046$.  The result is still partial, since the best dictionary leaves $0.7738155483100131$ unexplained.  The result is also local, since the surrounding $H$-ranking table is organism-specific and does not identify a common absorber across organisms.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
Nothing in this chapter asserts translation realization beyond the attached translation readouts, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, phenotype, pathway sufficiency, or universality.  Direction cosines, absorbed energies, null cutoffs, ranks, and description-length-penalized scores are finite statistical readbacks.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own same-row statistic and its own null model.
