\subsection{Modeled-Translation Complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ Protein-Abundance Component}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-complement-residual}

\paragraph{Object.}
This record studies the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association that remains after removing the component aligned with a modeled translation-adaptation readout.  The coordinate system is the nine-dimensional synonymous residual basis
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  For each organism row, codon counts were residualized against the same control family: intercept, CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The fitted protein-abundance component explained by the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ design is denoted $P_Q$.  The modeled-translation direction is $T$, obtained from organism-matched tRNA gene-copy and wobble-weight adaptation data rather than measured ribosome profiling.  The statistic reported here is the orthogonal complement $P_{Q\perp T}$ and its readback $a_\perp$ in the nine-coordinate basis.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
Six organism rows were evaluated.  The control matrix had $24$ columns in every row and rank $23$ after residualization.  The joined row counts were $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding residual protein-abundance fits were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.0461352981, & m &= 0.125371279, & d &= 0.874628721,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.107675267, & m &= 0.337003996, & d &= 0.662996004,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{Gallus}) &= 0.0398079360, & m &= 0.0599658194, & d &= 0.940034181,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{H.sapiens}) &= 0.0445456512, & m &= 0.0791050644, & d &= 0.920894936,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{M.smegmatis}) &= 0.0320900916, & m &= 0.102732798, & d &= 0.897267202,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{S.cerevisiae}) &= 0.298376250, & m &= 0.788267804, & d &= 0.211732196.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $m$ is the fraction of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component carried by the modeled-translation direction, while $d$ is the complementary fraction retained after that direction is removed.  The complement norms $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2$ were $1005.361410$ in \emph{Danio}, $287.004672$ in \emph{E. coli}, $695.532471$ in \emph{Gallus}, $1262.223768$ in human, $164.962914$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $186.885977$ in yeast.  Thus the complement is not a small numerical artifact in any of the six rows; it is largest in absolute norm for human and \emph{Danio}, and largest as a fraction of $P_Q$ in \emph{Gallus}, human, and \emph{M. smegmatis}.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The readback vector $a_\perp$ is nine-dimensional in every organism and has normalized absolute support sum $1$ up to floating-point roundoff.  Its dominant coordinates are organism-specific.  In \emph{Danio rerio} the leading support is $f_3$-stress, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$.  In \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 the leading support is $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, and $f_3$-stress.  In \emph{Gallus gallus} it is $Arg_{AGR}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $K_{AAA}$.  In human it is $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $f_3$-stress.  In \emph{M. smegmatis} it is $K_{AAA}$, $f_3$-stress, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, and $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$.  In yeast it is $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The finite table therefore supports a coordinate-level statement: the modeled-translation complement of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component is not carried by a single universal codon family, and its support changes across organism context.

\paragraph{Comparison with mediation fractions.}
The direct complement scalar $m$ was compared with a separate joint-decomposition reference on the same residualized $Q_e,T_e,P_e,Z$ rows.  Only the \emph{M. smegmatis} row matched within $0.02$: its complement value was $0.102732798$ versus a reference value $0.0839760292$, an absolute difference of $0.0187567689$.  The other absolute differences were $0.190334116$ in \emph{Danio}, $0.275918257$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.137724577$ in \emph{Gallus}, $0.192957827$ in human, and $0.160887300$ in yeast.  This disagreement is informative rather than disqualifying.  The orthogonal complement measures how much of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component is not aligned with modeled adaptation, while the joint mediation reference uses a different decomposition of the same residualized row space.  The row-wise failures therefore prevent promotion of $m$ into a causal mediation parameter.

\paragraph{Supplementary readout ranking.}
A related readout search ranked candidate explanations for the modeled-translation complement using the score $A_H C_H - \lambda DL_H$ with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{col})$.  The selected top readout was measured translation efficiency for \emph{Danio rerio}, complex membership for \emph{Gallus gallus} and human, mRNA for yeast, and no selected readout for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 or \emph{M. smegmatis}.  The positive Bonferroni outcomes in that ranking were readout-specific rather than universal: for \emph{Danio}, measured translation efficiency had threshold $0.000719995$ and null $p95=0.000329746$, while mRNA had threshold $0.000587830$ and null $p95=0.000235601$; for human, complex membership, mRNA, PTM density, transmembrane count, measured translation efficiency, and localization exceeded their listed Bonferroni gates, although measured tRNA-tAI did not.  These rows are useful as search directions for missing covariates, not as mechanisms.

\paragraph{Adjacent stability and abundance checks.}
The same nine-coordinate design also appears in independent mRNA-stability and abundance decompositions.  In yeast, the protein-abundance association had $R^2_{QP}=0.276114037$, and conditioning on residualized log half-life reduced it to $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.248517995$, giving absorbed variance $0.0275960421$ and mediation fraction $0.099944365$ with permutation right-tail $p=0.000999001$.  The yeast half-life join used $5085$ rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.915603842$.  The same yeast half-life readout gave $R^2_{QM}=0.0517942904$, dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, and cosine $0.804093547$ against the same-join abundance vector.  In \emph{E. coli}, the half-life readout gave $R^2_{QM}=0.0229855664$, dominant coordinate $Arg_{AGR}$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.116323119$, cosine $-0.778492338$, permutation $p=0.000999001$, and CDS join hit rate $0.997864959$.  In human, consensus relative half-life gave $R^2_{QM}=0.0105036704$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.0409521556$, cosine $0.635118388$, permutation $p=0.000999001$, and CDS join hit rate $0.977929477$ across $16165$ joined rows.  These stability rows show that the nine-coordinate design repeatedly leaves measurable residual projections, but they do not convert the modeled-translation complement into a biochemical pathway.

\paragraph{Coordinate reducibility and local ribosome contact.}
A single-coordinate decomposition in yeast used $1800$ base rows, $1258$ joined rows, five folds, $120$ permutations, and rank $24$ for the sibling control basis.  Eight of the nine named coordinates had a real-source flag under the held-out and permutation gate; $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$ was the only coordinate absent from that flagged set.  The $f_3$-stress family decomposition used $21$ synonymous-family subcoordinates, $1800$ joined rows, five folds, and $120$ permutations; $19$ families had a real-source flag, with $P_{f3}$ norm squared $52.7691171$ and target fraction $0.122597120$.  A separate local ribosome-occupancy analysis used $5754$ joined yeast genes after length checking, requested $200$ within-position-bin permutations, and found $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, and $f_3$-stress passing the $C_H>0$ and $\lambda DL$ gates with $DL=2$ and $\lambda=0.01$.  This last contact is position-controlled and descriptive; it does not identify a causal pausing mechanism.

\paragraph{Condition and tissue variation.}
The protein-abundance projection is also context dependent.  In yeast, matched YEPD and SD condition rows shared $2209$ proteins.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance fit was $R^2_{QP}=0.328082560$ in YEPD and $0.305038554$ in SD, with $f_3$-stress dominant in both and a condition-vector cosine $0.999377594$.  In human tissue proteomics, Adult B cells used $9244$ joined rows with $R^2_{QP}=0.0362611743$ and dominant $Arg_{AGR}$; Adult heart used $6468$ rows with $R^2_{QP}=0.0232089632$ and dominant $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$; Adult liver used $8664$ rows with $R^2_{QP}=0.0261334562$ and dominant $Ile_{AUA}$.  The pairwise tissue cosines were $0.688365523$, $0.899009345$, and $0.917679337$.  These values reinforce the main complement table: the residual support is structured, but the dominant coordinate is context-sensitive.

\paragraph{Modeled-translation scope.}
The translation-adaptation side of this record is explicitly modeled.  A rank-powered modeled-tAI matrix across $40$ organisms had organism count $40$, residual degrees of freedom $17$, rank $9$ for the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ design, rank $23$ for the control matrix, and $R^2_{QT}=0.900384900$ on the modeled per-codon tAI readout.  A broader organism sweep reached $41$ organisms and reported an $R^2_{QT}$ distribution with minimum $0.157927184$, first quartile $0.263539547$, median $0.339348458$, mean $0.384665554$, third quartile $0.514395155$, and maximum $0.821345722$.  These numbers show that the modeled adaptation layer is not rank-empty, but they remain modeled tAI or stAI readbacks rather than measured ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row result is ready as a bounded cross-layer statement.  After amino-acid composition, length, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls, the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component has a finite modeled-translation complement in every evaluated organism, and that complement reads back into explicit nine-coordinate support.  The strongest fractional modeled-translation component is yeast, $m=0.788267804$, while the strongest complementary fractions are \emph{Gallus}, human, and \emph{M. smegmatis}, with $d=0.940034181$, $0.920894936$, and $0.897267202$.  The row-level support is therefore a coordinate-support and residual-decomposition fact, not a claim that translation has been measured in all rows.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome-mediated realization, protein folding, structural admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal pathway, or universal biological law follows from this table alone.  The modeled $T$ direction uses tRNA-copy and adaptation weights, not direct ribosome-footprint measurements.  Protein abundance is a downstream steady-state readout, not a folding or function assay.  The complement vector $a_\perp$ identifies which $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates carry the abundance component left outside the modeled-adaptation direction; it does not by itself explain why those coordinates matter biologically.  Any stronger statement requires its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic on the corresponding layer.
