\subsection{Modeled-translation complement of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-translation-complement-residual}

\paragraph{Object of the finite computation.}
The record considered here is a coordinate-support computation for the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual family.  The nine coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.
For each organism, coding-sequence codon counts are residualized against the control system $Z$ consisting of an intercept, log CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The resulting $Z$ design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every organism reported here.  The tested rows are organism-specific: $14410$ proteins for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.

\paragraph{Projection being measured.}
Let $Q$ denote the residualized $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design, let $P$ denote the residualized protein-abundance readout, and let $T$ denote the modeled-tAI readout obtained from organism-matched tRNA gene-copy data.  The finite computation first forms the $Q$-explained abundance component $P_Q$, then removes its modeled-tAI component, producing $P_{Q\perp T}$.  The reported scalar
$m$ is the modeled-translation share of the $Q$-explained abundance component, while
$d=1-m$ is the remaining complement share.  The complement vector is also read back into the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates as $a_{\perp}$, so that the support pattern of the unexplained part is visible coordinate by coordinate.

The organism-level complement values are

\begin{aligned}

These numbers show two distinct regimes.  In yeast, most of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component lies in the modeled-tAI-aligned part, with $m=0.7882678043$ and $d=0.2117321957$.  In the other five organisms, the modeled-tAI share is smaller, and the complement dominates: $d=0.8746287210$ in zebrafish, $d=0.6629960039$ in \emph{E. coli}, $d=0.9400341806$ in chicken, $d=0.9208949356$ in human, and $d=0.8972672019$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}.  Thus the finite table does not support a single organism-independent decomposition pattern; it supports a measured split between modeled-tAI-aligned abundance variation and a remaining $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate component.

\paragraph{Coordinate support of the complement.}
The $a_{\perp}$ readback is nine-dimensional in every organism, and its absolute support fractions sum to $1.0$ up to numerical roundoff.  The dominant coordinates are organism-dependent.  In \emph{D. rerio}, the leading support is carried by $f3\_stress$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $K\_AAA$, and $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the leading support is $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, and $f3\_stress$.  In chicken it is $Arg\_AGR$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and $K\_AAA$.  In human it is $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $K\_AAA$, and $f3\_stress$.  In \emph{M. smegmatis} it is $K\_AAA$, $f3\_stress$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, and $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$.  In yeast it is $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.

This support list is the main chapter-specific observation.  The modeled-tAI complement is not concentrated on a single universal codon family.  Rather, each organism has a finite coordinate signature after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, and $M$-density have been removed.  The complement is therefore a residual coordinate object: it identifies where the $Q$-explained abundance component remains after subtracting the modeled-tAI direction, but it does not by itself identify the biological process responsible for that remainder.

\paragraph{Comparison to the mediation scalar.}
A separate joint-decomposition calculation supplies a mediation reference value for the same residualized $Q$, $T$, $P$, and $Z$ rows.  The complement share $m$ agrees with that reference within $0.02$ only for \emph{M. smegmatis}: $m=0.1027327981$ versus reference $0.0839760292$, absolute difference $0.0187567689$.  The other organisms do not meet that tolerance: zebrafish differs by $0.1903341164$, \emph{E. coli} by $0.2759182567$, chicken by $0.1377245771$, human by $0.1929578272$, and yeast by $0.1608872998$.  The complement projection and the joint mediation scalar are therefore not interchangeable summaries.  The projection statistic asks how much of $P_Q$ remains outside the modeled-tAI direction, while the joint decomposition asks a conditioning question in the combined residual system.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite computation supports the following bounded statement.  After the declared controls are applied, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component can be decomposed relative to modeled tAI in six organisms, and the modeled-tAI complement has finite nonzero support in the nine residual coordinates.  Yeast is the strongest modeled-tAI-aligned case in this table, with $m=0.7882678043$, while the other organisms retain large complement shares ranging from $d=0.6629960039$ to $d=0.9400341806$.  The coordinate readback shows organism-specific support rather than a single universal coordinate.  No translation mechanism, ribosome-mediated realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal pathway, or universal biological law follows from this computation alone.  Such conclusions would require a separate contact supplying the relevant measured layer and a statistic evaluated under the corresponding controls.
