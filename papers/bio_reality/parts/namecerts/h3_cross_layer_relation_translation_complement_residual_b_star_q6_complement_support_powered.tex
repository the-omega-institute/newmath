\subsection{Modeled-translation complement support in the B*\_Q6 abundance component}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-translation-complement-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record fixes a coordinate-level readback of the part of protein-abundance variation that is explained by the nine residual B*\_Q6 codon coordinates but is not aligned with the modeled tAI direction used in the same rows.  For each organism, the computation first residualizes the codon-coordinate matrix $Q$, the modeled translation vector $T$, and the abundance readout $P$ against the same control block $Z$: an intercept, $\log$ CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline.  The nine coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The module is classified as $cross\_layer\_relation$, because it compares a codon-residual coordinate system, a modeled translation-weight direction, and measured protein abundance without promoting the comparison into a causal biological layer.

\paragraph{Projection definition.}
Let $P\_Q$ denote the component of residualized abundance explained by the nine-dimensional residualized B*\_Q6 design, and let $P\_{Q\perp T}$ denote the part of $P\_Q$ orthogonal to the modeled tAI direction within the same residualized row space.  The reported complement fraction is $d$, and the modeled-translation-aligned fraction is $m=1-d$.  The coordinate vector $a\_\perp$ is then obtained by reading $P\_{Q\perp T}$ back through the nine B*\_Q6 directions.  In this finite computation the relationship is summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
P\_Q &= \operatorname{proj}\_{Q\mid Z}(P),\\
P\_{Q\perp T} &= P\_Q-\operatorname{proj}\_{T\mid Z}(P\_Q),\\
d &= \frac{\|P\_{Q\perp T}\|^2}{\|P\_Q\|^2},\qquad m=1-d.
\end{aligned}
$$
The test does not ask whether modeled tAI alone explains abundance.  It asks which B*\_Q6 coordinates remain visible after the modeled-tAI-aligned part of the B*\_Q6 abundance component has been removed.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
Six organism rows were computed with finite values and with the control design of rank $23$ in all cases.  The joined row counts were $14410$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $3739$ for $Escherichia\ coli$ K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for $Gallus\ gallus$, $19053$ for $Homo\ sapiens$, $4129$ for $Mycobacterium\ smegmatis$ str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$.  The corresponding $R^2\_{QP}$ values for the B*\_Q6 abundance component were $0.04613529805760884$, $0.10767526667768725$, $0.03980793595345331$, $0.04454565117192484$, $0.032090091625816136$, and $0.29837624987287115$.  The complement fractions $d$ were respectively $0.874628721049266$, $0.6629960038578532$, $0.9400341806302775$, $0.9208949355679455$, $0.8972672018746489$, and $0.21173219572706703$.  Thus the modeled-tAI-aligned fractions $m$ were $0.12537127895073397$, $0.3370039961421468$, $0.05996581936972245$, $0.07910506443205445$, $0.10273279812535108$, and $0.788267804272933$.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The complement readback is not sparse in a universal way; its support changes by organism.  In $Danio\ rerio$, the dominant coordinates were $f3\_stress$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $K\_AAA$, and $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$.  In $Escherichia\ coli$ K-12 MG1655, the dominant coordinates were $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, and $f3\_stress$.  In $Gallus\ gallus$, they were $Arg\_AGR$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and $K\_AAA$.  In $Homo\ sapiens$, they were $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $K\_AAA$, and $f3\_stress$.  In $Mycobacterium\ smegmatis$ str. MC2 155, they were $K\_AAA$, $f3\_stress$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, and $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$.  In $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, they were $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The absolute support fractions sum to $1.0$ in every row up to rounding, including $1.0000000000000002$ in the $E.\ coli$ row.

\paragraph{Relation to modeled translation survival.}
The companion modeled-tAI survival computation used $40$ organisms, residualized a $24$-column control block of rank $23$, retained residual degrees of freedom $17$, and obtained a rank-$9$ B*\_Q6 residual design with $R^2\_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$.  That result confirms that modeled tAI weights have strong coordinate-level contact with B*\_Q6 after the same style of amino-acid, GC3, length, organism, and M-density controls.  The present complement computation asks a narrower question: after such a modeled-translation direction is supplied, how much of the B*\_Q6 abundance component remains outside it?  The answer is organism-dependent.  Yeast has the largest modeled-aligned fraction, $m=0.788267804272933$, while gallus and human have large complements, $d=0.9400341806302775$ and $d=0.9208949355679455$.

\paragraph{Mediation comparison.}
The complement fraction $m$ is not identical to the separately computed joint mediation scalar.  Against the joint-decomposition reference, the absolute differences were $0.19033411641073011$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $0.27591825665152014$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.13772457710273214$ for $Gallus\ gallus$, $0.19295782716913712$ for $Homo\ sapiens$, $0.018756768926738165$ for $M.\ smegmatis$, and $0.16088729984876438$ for $S.\ cerevisiae$.  Only the $M.\ smegmatis$ row matched within the $0.02$ tolerance.  This failure of general agreement is informative: an orthogonal complement within the B*\_Q6 abundance component and a joint mediation decomposition answer related but distinct finite-row questions.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row result is positive for the stated complement-support claim: every organism row yields a finite $P\_{Q\perp T}$ norm, a finite $R^2\_{QP}$, and a nine-coordinate $a\_\perp$ readback.  The numerical pattern does not support a single organism-independent direction, but it does support the more modest conclusion that B*\_Q6 abundance-associated residual structure is not exhausted by the modeled tAI direction in these rows.  The strongest complement fractions occur in $Gallus\ gallus$, $Homo\ sapiens$, $Mycobacterium\ smegmatis$, and $Danio\ rerio$, while $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$ is the row with the strongest modeled-tAI-aligned fraction.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter does not establish measured ribosome-mediated translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Modeled tAI is a bounded codon-adaptation readout, not a ribosome-profiling measurement.  Protein abundance is a downstream measurement, not a folding or function assay.  The complement vector $a\_\perp$ identifies residual coordinate support for an abundance component after modeled-tAI removal; it is not, by itself, a biochemical mechanism.  Any promotion to measured translation, protein structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or universality requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
