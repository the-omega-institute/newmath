\subsection{Modeled-tAI complement support of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-complement-support}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the part of protein abundance that is captured by the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates is already exhausted by a modeled tAI direction, or whether a nontrivial complement remains when the modeled-tAI component is removed.  For each organism, the computation starts with residualized codon-coordinate rows $Q_e$, a modeled tAI readout $T_e$, and a protein-abundance readout $P_e$, all evaluated after the same control family $Z$: amino-acid composition, organism-specific context when present, GC3, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline.  The object reported here is not a direct ribosome measurement.  It is the support pattern of the modeled-tAI-complement component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection.

\paragraph{Coordinate system and controls.}
The finite computation uses the nine coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each of the six organism rows.  The joined row counts are $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  These rows pair CDS-derived codon counts with organism-matched tRNA-copy inputs for the modeled tAI surface and with protein-abundance rows for the downstream abundance projection.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the part of the abundance readout explained by the residualized nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ design, and let $P_{Q\perp T}$ denote the component of $P_Q$ orthogonal to the modeled-tAI direction.  The reported complement fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2},\\
 m &= 1-d.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite rows give the following $(R^2_{QP},d,m,\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2)$ values.  For \emph{D. rerio}, $(0.0461353,0.874629,0.125371,1005.3614)$.  For \emph{E. coli}, $(0.107675,0.662996,0.337004,287.0047)$.  For \emph{G. gallus}, $(0.0398079,0.940034,0.0599658,695.5325)$.  For \emph{H. sapiens}, $(0.0445457,0.920895,0.0791051,1262.2238)$.  For \emph{M. smegmatis}, $(0.0320901,0.897267,0.102733,164.9629)$.  For \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $(0.298376,0.211732,0.788268,186.8860)$.  Thus the complement is not numerically empty in any row, but its size is strongly organism-dependent: yeast is dominated by the modeled-tAI-aligned fraction $m=0.788268$, whereas the vertebrate rows and \emph{M. smegmatis} leave most of $P_Q$ in the modeled-tAI complement.

\paragraph{Coordinate readback of the complement.}
The complement vector $a_\perp$ is read back in the same nine coordinates.  Its absolute support sums to $1$ in every row up to floating-point tolerance.  The dominant coordinates are not constant across organisms.  \emph{D. rerio} is led by $f_3$-stress, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$.  \emph{E. coli} is led by $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, and $f_3$-stress.  \emph{G. gallus} is led by $Arg_{AGR}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $K_{AAA}$.  \emph{H. sapiens} is led by $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $f_3$-stress.  \emph{M. smegmatis} is led by $K_{AAA}$, $f_3$-stress, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, and $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$.  \emph{S. cerevisiae} is led by $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The support pattern is therefore a finite organism-indexed profile, not a single universal direction.

\paragraph{Comparison with mediation and auxiliary readouts.}
The complement fraction $m$ was compared with a separate joint-decomposition reference computed on the same residualized $Q_e,T_e,P_e,Z$ rows.  The differences are $0.190334$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.275918$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.137725$ for \emph{G. gallus}, $0.192958$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, $0.0187568$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0.160887$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}; only the \emph{M. smegmatis} row matches within the $0.02$ tolerance.  This is an important negative feature of the table: the simple orthogonal complement is not interchangeable with the joint mediation statistic in five of the six rows.  A related direction-aware search over candidate readouts used the regularizer $\lambda=0.01$ with $DL=\log_2(1+n_{col})$.  In that search, the selected $H_o^*$ rows were measured TE for \emph{D. rerio}, complex membership for \emph{G. gallus} and \emph{H. sapiens}, mRNA for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, and no selected row for \emph{E. coli} or \emph{M. smegmatis}.  The same evidence packet also contains an mRNA-half-life mediation check in yeast with $R^2_{QP}=0.276114$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.248518$, absorbed fraction $0.0275960$, mediation fraction $0.0999444$, permutation null mean $0.000196745$, null $95\%$ value $0.00145710$, and right-tail permutation probability $0.000999001$ on $5085$ joined rows.  These auxiliary rows support the view that the complement needs readout-specific follow-up rather than a single promoted interpretation.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite computation supports a bounded cross-layer statement: after the declared controls, the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance projection has a modeled-tAI-complement component in all six organism rows, and that component has a concrete nine-coordinate support profile.  The result is strongest as a descriptive decomposition of residual codon-coordinate support.  It does not establish measured translation efficiency, ribosome-mediated causation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a biological law across organisms.  Modeled tAI is a bounded codon-adaptation readout rather than ribosome profiling.  Protein abundance is a downstream measurement rather than proof of mechanism.  Any claim about measured translation, structural order, biochemical function, or universality would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
