\subsection{Modeled-tAI Complement Support for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Abundance Component}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-modeled-tai-complement-support}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the part of the protein-abundance signal captured by the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates is exhausted by the modeled tAI direction, or whether a residual component remains after the modeled-tAI projection is removed. The tested object is not a direct ribosome-occupancy measurement. It is a coordinate-level decomposition of the residualized abundance component $P_Q$ into a modeled-tAI-aligned part and a complement $P_{Q\perp T}$, followed by a readback of $P_{Q\perp T}$ in the same nine-coordinate basis. The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.

\paragraph{Finite design.}
For each organism, codon-count residuals were projected after controlling for amino-acid composition, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline. The control design had $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organism-specific fit. The joined row counts were $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} mc$^2$155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The modeled translation coordinate $T$ was formed from tRNA-copy based modeled tAI rather than from ribosome profiling. Thus the tested statistic is a modeled-readout complement inside a controlled abundance projection, not a measured elongation or occupancy claim.

The other five organisms retain smaller but finite complement components, with $m$ ranging from $0.0599658194$ in chicken to $0.337003996$ in \emph{E. coli}. The squared complement norms were $1005.361410$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $287.004672$ for \emph{E. coli}, $695.532471$ for \emph{G. gallus}, $1262.223768$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, $164.962914$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $186.885977$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The complement readback $a_{\perp}$ is nine-dimensional in every organism and has normalized absolute support sum $1.0$ up to floating-point roundoff. The dominant support coordinates are organism-specific. In \emph{D. rerio} they are $f_3$-stress, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$. In \emph{E. coli} they are $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, and $f_3$-stress. In chicken they are $Arg_{AGR}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $K_{AAA}$. In human they are $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $f_3$-stress. In \emph{M. smegmatis} they are $K_{AAA}$, $f_3$-stress, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, and $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$. In yeast they are $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. This pattern supports the weaker, coordinate-specific conclusion: the modeled-tAI complement is not concentrated in a single universal codon family, and the residual support pattern changes by organism.

\paragraph{Relation to modeled translation survival.}
A separate modeled-tAI survival computation used $40$ organisms, $9$ residual coordinates, $9$ modeled readout columns, rank $23$ controls, residual degrees of freedom $17$, and achieved $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000$ with rank $9$ residual coordinate design. That result shows that the tRNA-copy based modeled readout is strongly coupled to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate block after the same kind of amino-acid, organism, $GC3$, length, and $M$-density controls. It does not turn the complement statistic above into a measured ribosome-profiling claim; it only supplies the modeled $T$ direction whose complement is removed from $P_Q$.

\paragraph{Supplementary readout screen.}
The direction-aware supplementary-readout screen used a regularization choice $\lambda=0.01$ with $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{col})$. Its best-scoring candidate was measured translation efficiency in \emph{D. rerio}, with $A_H=0.0175942900$, $C_H=0.657375310$, $A_HC_H=0.0115660518$, score $0.00156605185$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000719995$, and null $p95=0.000329746$. The yeast top candidate was mRNA, with $A_H=0.00189760547$, $C_H=0.604057189$, $A_HC_H=0.00114626223$, score $-0.00885373777$, Bonferroni threshold $0.001301475$, and null $p95=0.000474582$. Human and chicken selected complex membership as the highest-ranked candidate under that screen, with human $A_H=0.00752719999$, $C_H=0.872307116$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000264617$, null $p95=0.000233442$, and chicken $A_H=0.00744247430$, $C_H=0.569906540$, Bonferroni threshold $0.005541658$, null $p95=0.002708712$. \emph{E. coli} and \emph{M. smegmatis} had no accepted $H_o^\ast$ in that ranking. These rows indicate where the residual complement may be worth interrogating next, but they are not a causal assignment.

\paragraph{Condition check.}
In yeast GFP abundance rows, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance fit was stable across two reported growth conditions. The shared protein count was $2209$, with $R^2_{QP}=0.305038554$ in SD and $R^2_{QP}=0.328082560$ in YEPD. The dominant coordinate was $f_3$-stress in both conditions, and the two condition-specific $S^{QP}$ profiles had Pearson similarity $0.998388026$ and cosine similarity $0.999377594$ over $1000$ permutations. This supports a finite yeast condition-stability statement for the abundance projection, while leaving the modeled-tAI complement and direct translation-readout questions separate.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter supports a bounded finite-row statement: after the declared controls, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component has a computable modeled-tAI complement in all six organisms, and the complement reads back to nonempty, organism-dependent coordinate support. It does not establish measured translation realization, ribosome-mediated mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law. A direct translation claim requires an organism- and CDS-matched ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact with the same controls. A protein-output or functional claim requires its own layer-matched measurement and a separately computed statistic.
