\subsection{Modeled-translation complement of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-complement-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
The record tests a specific residual question rather than a broad biological claim.  For each organism, let $Q$ denote the nine residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ codon coordinates after amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline have been partialled out.  Let $T$ denote a modeled tAI readout built from organism-matched tRNA gene-copy and wobble weights, and let $P$ denote protein abundance where the organism has a matched proteomics table.  The computed object is the part of the $Q$-explained abundance component that remains after projecting away the modeled-translation direction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_Q &= \operatorname{Proj}_{Q_{\perp Z}}(P_{\perp Z}),\\
P_{Q\perp T} &= P_Q - \operatorname{Proj}_{T_{\perp Z}}(P_Q),\\
d &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2}, \qquad m = 1-d.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readback vector $a_{\perp}$ is then expressed in the same nine-coordinate basis, so the calculation says which residual codon coordinates carry the modeled-tAI-complement part of the abundance association.

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The computation used six organism rows with rank-$23$ control matrices and $24$ declared control columns in every row.  The joined protein counts were $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The nine coordinates were $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The underlying modeled-tAI matrix had organism count $40$, rank $9$ after residualization, rank $23$ for $Z$, residual degrees of freedom $17$, and total $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$ for the modeled readout surface.

\paragraph{Complement strengths.}
The complement fractions are strongly organism-dependent.  Yeast is the exceptional row in which modeled tAI absorbs most of the $Q$-abundance component: $d=0.21173219572706703$ and $m=0.788267804272933$, with $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624987287115$ and $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2=186.88597748267532$.  In contrast, the modeled-tAI-complement dominates in human, chicken, zebrafish, and mycobacterium: human has $d=0.9208949355679455$, $m=0.07910506443205445$, $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565117192484$, and $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2=1262.22376787716$; chicken has $d=0.9400341806302775$, $m=0.05996581936972245$, $R^2_{QP}=0.03980793595345331$, and $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2=695.5324705841906$; zebrafish has $d=0.874628721049266$, $m=0.12537127895073397$, $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529805760884$, and $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2=1005.3614101653925$; mycobacterium has $d=0.8972672018746489$, $m=0.10273279812535108$, $R^2_{QP}=0.032090091625816136$, and $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2=164.9629139386272$.  The \emph{E. coli} row is intermediate, with $d=0.6629960038578532$, $m=0.3370039961421468$, $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526667768725$, and $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|^2=287.00467177017856$.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The $a_{\perp}$ readback is finite in all six organisms and has dimension $9$ in every row.  Its dominant supports are not a single universal coordinate.  Zebrafish is carried by $f3\_stress$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $K\_AAA$, and $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$.  \emph{E. coli} is carried by $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, and $f3\_stress$.  Chicken is carried by $Arg\_AGR$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and $K\_AAA$.  Human is carried by $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $K\_AAA$, and $f3\_stress$.  Mycobacterium is carried by $K\_AAA$, $f3\_stress$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, and $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$.  Yeast is carried by $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The absolute support fractions sum to $1.0$ in all rows up to rounding, with the \emph{E. coli} row reported as $1.0000000000000002$.

\paragraph{Relation to the mediation reference.}
The complement statistic $m$ is deliberately not the same scalar as the joint mediation calculation.  Against the stated mediation reference, only mycobacterium agrees within $0.02$: $m=0.10273279812535108$ versus reference $0.08397602919861291$, absolute difference $0.018756768926738165$.  The other rows disagree at larger scale: zebrafish differs by $0.19033411641073011$, \emph{E. coli} by $0.27591825665152014$, chicken by $0.13772457710273214$, human by $0.19295782716913712$, and yeast by $0.16088729984876438$.  This is informative rather than contradictory: the present statistic removes the modeled-tAI direction from the $Q$-explained abundance component, whereas the reference uses a joint decomposition on residualized $Q$, $T$, $P$, and $Z$ rows.

\paragraph{Supplementary readout comparison.}
The associated non-translation residual ranking used the score $A_H C_H - \lambda DL_H$ with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{col})$.  In zebrafish, measured TE had $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, score $0.001566051846174291$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$, null $p95=0.0003297458343745959$, and $n=13140$, while mRNA had score $0.000734588577977589$ and also exceeded its Bonferroni gate.  In human, complex membership, mRNA, PTM density, transmembrane count, measured TE, and localization exceeded their respective Bonferroni gates, but the penalized scores remained negative; complex membership was the top ranked $H$ with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, score $-0.0034339698846599055$, threshold $0.0002646171322365322$, and null $p95=0.0002334419901244703$.  In yeast, mRNA was top ranked with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, score $-0.008853737772559931$, threshold $0.0013014749238591248$, null $p95=0.0004745820831535835$, and $n=5128$.  This supports the reading that the modeled-tAI-complement component is real as a finite statistical residual, but its explanatory label is not resolved by a single universal downstream module.

\paragraph{mRNA-stability check.}
A separate yeast abundance-residual mediation row gives a scale for one candidate downstream explanation.  On $5085$ joined yeast rows, residualized log half-life absorbed $R^2_{abs}=0.027596042074056304$ out of $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, leaving $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$ and a mediation fraction $0.09994436477872035$.  Its permutation right-tail value was $0.000999000999000999$, with null mean $0.00019674487584783138$ and null $p95=0.0014571003414947088$.  The join used $5379$ valid half-life records, $5815$ CDS rows, $5085$ joined rows, and a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$.  Thus mRNA half-life can account for a small but nonzero part of the yeast $Q$-abundance association, but it does not collapse the direct residual association.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite rows justify the following bounded statement.  The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component has a computable modeled-tAI-complement residual in six organisms, and the residual can be read back into the same nine codon coordinates with finite support.  The organism pattern separates yeast, where modeled tAI accounts for most of the $Q$-abundance component, from several metazoan and bacterial rows where most of that component lies outside the modeled-tAI direction.  The result is a controlled projection fact about matched codon counts, modeled tAI, and protein abundance.  It does not by itself establish measured translation, ribosome-mediated dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Any such stronger conclusion would require a distinct layer-matched contact and a separately computed controlled statistic.
