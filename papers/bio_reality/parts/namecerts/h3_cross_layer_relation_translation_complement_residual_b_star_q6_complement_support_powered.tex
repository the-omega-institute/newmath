\subsection{Modeled-tAI complement support of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{subsec:bioreality-bstar-q6-complement-support}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the portion of protein-abundance variation carried by the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates is mostly aligned with the modeled tAI direction, or whether a substantial residual component remains after that modeled translation-weight axis is removed.  For each organism, the computation first forms the residualized nine-dimensional $Q$ design after the declared controls $Z$, projects the protein-abundance readout $P$ onto that $Q$ design, and then subtracts the modeled-tAI component $T$ from this $Q$-explained abundance component.  The resulting vector is denoted $P_{Q\perp T}$.  It is then read back into the same nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates as a support vector $a_{\perp}$.

\paragraph{Coordinate system and controls.}
The coordinate family is the fixed nine-row panel
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3$-stress.  The residualization control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organism reported here: an intercept, log CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition coordinates, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline over the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The joined row counts are $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The tRNA organism labels match these CDS organisms except that the \emph{E. coli} CDS row uses the \emph{escherichia\_coli} tRNA contact.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The finite-row statistic is the pair
$$
\begin{aligned}
  d &= \frac{\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2}{\|P_Q\|_2^2}, \\
  m &= 1-d,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $d$ is the modeled-tAI-complement fraction of the abundance component already explained by $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, and $m$ is the modeled-tAI-aligned fraction in this projection geometry.  The organism-level values are concrete and non-vacuous: for \emph{D. rerio}, $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529805760884$, $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2=1005.3614101653925$, $d=0.874628721049266$, and $m=0.12537127895073397$; for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526667768725$, $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2=287.00467177017856$, $d=0.6629960038578532$, and $m=0.3370039961421468$; for \emph{G. gallus}, $R^2_{QP}=0.03980793595345331$, $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2=695.5324705841906$, $d=0.9400341806302775$, and $m=0.05996581936972245$; for \emph{H. sapiens}, $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565117192484$, $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2=1262.22376787716$, $d=0.9208949355679455$, and $m=0.07910506443205445$; for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $R^2_{QP}=0.032090091625816136$, $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2=164.9629139386272$, $d=0.8972672018746489$, and $m=0.10273279812535108$; and for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624987287115$, $\|P_{Q\perp T}\|_2^2=186.88597748267532$, $d=0.21173219572706703$, and $m=0.788267804272933$.

\paragraph{Residual support pattern.}
The $a_{\perp}$ support is not concentrated on a universal coordinate.  In \emph{D. rerio}, the dominant readback coordinates are $f3$-stress, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$.  In \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, the dominant list is $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, and $f3$-stress.  In \emph{G. gallus}, it is $Arg_{AGR}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, and $K_{AAA}$.  In \emph{H. sapiens}, it is $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $f3$-stress.  In \emph{M. smegmatis}, it is $K_{AAA}$, $f3$-stress, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, and $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$.  In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, it is $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3$-stress.  Each organism has $a_{\perp}$ dimension $9$, and the absolute support fractions sum to $1.0$ up to floating-point roundoff; the \emph{E. coli} value is $1.0000000000000002$.

\paragraph{Comparison with mediation-style summaries.}
The same row set also records whether the complement-projection value $m$ agrees within $0.02$ with the separately computed joint-decomposition reference from the modeled translation mediation calculation.  Agreement is not generally present.  The absolute differences are $0.19033411641073011$ in \emph{D. rerio}, $0.27591825665152014$ in \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.13772457710273214$ in \emph{G. gallus}, $0.19295782716913712$ in \emph{H. sapiens}, $0.018756768926738165$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0.16088729984876438$ in \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  Only \emph{M. smegmatis} passes the $0.02$ agreement check.  Thus the complement statistic is not being used as a silent replacement for the mediation statistic; it is a separate projection diagnostic, with its own finite-row outcome.

\paragraph{Related finite-row context.}
The modeled-tAI survival computation over a $40$ organism panel reports a $9\times 9$ $S^{QT}$ matrix with $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$, rank $23$ for $Z$, rank $9$ for the residualized $Q$ design, and residual degrees of freedom $17$.  In the organism sweep, $41$ organisms are computable and the $R^2_{QT}$ distribution has minimum $0.1579271837024072$, first quartile $0.26353954700074694$, median $0.33934845811598124$, mean $0.38466555440501393$, third quartile $0.5143951545826744$, and maximum $0.8213457220900503$.  These values justify treating modeled tAI as a substantial bounded readout in the dataset, but they do not turn the complement residual into a measured ribosome-occupancy claim.  A separate ribosome-occupancy finite row, using exact position and amino-acid bins with $200$ requested within-bin permutations, reports nine computable families and three families passing the $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $DL=2$, and $C_H>0$ gates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, and $f3$-stress.  That record is local codon-window evidence, not the present cross-organism complement statistic.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row outcome is ready as a bounded cross-layer relation: after amino-acid composition, organism-specific context, GC3, length, and $M$-density controls, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component has a computable modeled-tAI complement in every listed organism, and the complement has an explicit nine-coordinate readback.  The complement fraction is small in yeast, where $m=0.788267804272933$ and $d=0.21173219572706703$, but large in the other five organisms, with $d$ ranging from $0.6629960038578532$ to $0.9400341806302775$.  The record therefore supports a chapter-level claim that modeled tAI does not exhaust the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance-associated signal outside the yeast row, while yeast remains the strongest modeled-tAI-aligned case in this panel.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter does not claim a universal biological direction, a ribosome mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or causal mediation.  Modeled tAI is a bounded codon-adaptation readout derived from tRNA-copy and wobble assumptions; it is not measured ribosome occupancy, measured translation efficiency, protein output, or a structure/function assay.  The $a_{\perp}$ vector identifies which residual coordinates carry the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component in the fitted projection, and nothing stronger follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant measurement and statistic.
