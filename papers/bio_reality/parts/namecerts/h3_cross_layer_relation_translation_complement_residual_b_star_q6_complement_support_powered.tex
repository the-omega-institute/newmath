\subsection{Modeled-tAI Complement Support in the B*\_Q6 Residual Basis}\label{sec:bior-b-star-q6-complement-support}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the part of a protein-abundance projection left outside a modeled-tAI direction still has readable support in the nine B*\_Q6 synonymous residual coordinates. The computation is intentionally narrow. For each organism, the codon residual matrix $Q$ is first residualized against the declared control family $Z$: amino-acid composition, $\log$ CDS length, GC3, and the M-density baseline. The modeled tAI readout $T$ and the protein-abundance readout $P$ are placed on the same residualized rows. The object reported here is not a direct biological pathway. It is the coordinate readback of the component of the $Q$-explained abundance vector that is orthogonal to the modeled-tAI direction.

\paragraph{Coordinate system and residual projection.}
The nine residual coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each organism $o$, let $P\_Q(o)$ be the abundance component explained by the residualized B*\_Q6 coordinates, and let $P\_{Q\perp T}(o)$ be its component outside the modeled-tAI direction. The reported complement fraction is $d(o)$, and the modeled-tAI-aligned remainder is $m(o)=1-d(o)$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
d(o) &= \frac{\lVert P\_{Q\perp T}(o)\rVert^2}{\lVert P\_Q(o)\rVert^2},
& m(o) &= 1-d(o).
\end{aligned}
$$
The vector $a\_\perp(o)$ is then the nine-coordinate readback of $P\_{Q\perp T}(o)$. Its support is finite and organism-specific: it says which codon residual coordinates carry the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component in the fitted table.

\paragraph{Joined rows and ranks.}
Six organism panels were computed. The residualized design used 24 control columns with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in every panel. The joined row counts were $14410$ for Danio rerio, $3739$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $12418$ for Gallus gallus, $19053$ for Homo sapiens, $4129$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The tRNA organism labels matched the CDS organism labels except for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, whose tRNA label was Escherichia coli. These row counts are part of the claim: the result is a finite multi-organism coordinate calculation, not an asymptotic biological law.

\paragraph{Complement fractions.}
The complement is large in five of the six organism panels and smaller in yeast. The finite list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.8746287210, & m(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.1253712790,\\
 d(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.6629960039, & m(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.3370039961,\\
 d(\mathrm{Gallus}) &= 0.9400341806, & m(\mathrm{Gallus}) &= 0.0599658194,\\
 d(\mathrm{Human}) &= 0.9208949356, & m(\mathrm{Human}) &= 0.0791050644,\\
 d(\mathrm{M.smegmatis}) &= 0.8972672019, & m(\mathrm{M.smegmatis}) &= 0.1027327981,\\
 d(\mathrm{Yeast}) &= 0.2117321957, & m(\mathrm{Yeast}) &= 0.7882678043.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding $R^2\_{QP}$ abundance-survival values were $0.0461352981$, $0.1076752667$, $0.0398079360$, $0.0445456512$, $0.0320900916$, and $0.2983762499$ in the same organism order. Thus the largest overall residual abundance fit is in yeast, but the largest modeled-tAI-complement fraction is in chicken, followed by human, mycobacterium, and zebrafish.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
The $a\_\perp$ readbacks have dimension $9$ in every organism, and the absolute support fractions sum to $1.0$ up to floating-point precision. The dominant coordinate lists are organism-dependent. Danio rerio is led by $f3\_stress$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $K\_AAA$, and $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$. Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 is led by $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, and $f3\_stress$. Gallus gallus is led by $Arg\_AGR$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and $K\_AAA$. Homo sapiens is led by $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $K\_AAA$, and $f3\_stress$. Mycobacterium smegmatis is led by $K\_AAA$, $f3\_stress$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, and $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$. Saccharomyces cerevisiae is led by $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.

\paragraph{Relation to mediation and neighboring readouts.}
The complement fraction $m$ is not identical to the joint-decomposition mediation number from the separate mediation computation. Agreement within $0.02$ occurs only for Mycobacterium smegmatis, where $m=0.1027327981$ and the reference mediation value is $0.0839760292$, with absolute difference $0.0187567689$. The other differences are larger: $0.1903341164$ for Danio rerio, $0.2759182567$ for Escherichia coli, $0.1377245771$ for Gallus gallus, $0.1929578272$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.1608872998$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae. This is a useful negative constraint: the complement readback is a coordinate-support decomposition, not a replacement for a mediation matrix.

The neighboring non-translation ranking calculation used a stated regularizer $\lambda=0.01$ with $DL=\log\_2(1+n\_{col})$ and also reported raw $A\_H C\_H$. Its top selected modules were measured TE for Danio rerio, complex membership for Gallus gallus and Homo sapiens, mRNA for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and no selected $H\_o^\star$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 or Mycobacterium smegmatis. Bonferroni-supported examples included Danio measured TE with threshold $0.000719995$ and null $p95=0.000329746$, human complex membership with threshold $0.000264617$ and null $p95=0.000233442$, and yeast mRNA with threshold $0.001301475$ and null $p95=0.000474582$. These auxiliary facts support the interpretation that the complement points to additional readout families, but they do not identify a single universal module.

\paragraph{Cross-checks from mRNA, condition, tissue, and modeled-tAI panels.}
A yeast mRNA-stability panel joined $5085$ rows with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419$. It found $R^2\_{QM}=0.0517942904$, $R^2\_{QP}=0.2761140374$ on the same join, cosine $0.8040935468$ between $S^{QM}$ and $S^{QP}$, and dominant mRNA-stability coordinate $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$. A yeast growth-condition panel joined $2209$ shared proteins and gave $R^2\_{QP}=0.3050385538$ in SD and $0.3280825603$ in YEPD, both dominated by $f3\_stress$, with cross-condition cosine $0.9993775935$ and Pearson $0.9983880256$. A human tissue panel gave $R^2\_{QP}=0.0362611743$ in adult B cells, $0.0232089632$ in adult heart, and $0.0261334562$ in adult liver, with dominant coordinates $Arg\_AGR$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, and $Ile\_AUA$, respectively. The modeled per-codon tAI survival panel across $40$ organisms had $\operatorname{rank}(X\_{\sim})=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $17$, and $R^2\_{QT}=0.9003849000$. These checks place the complement-support result in a broader finite-row pattern: B*\_Q6 residuals survive in several bounded statistical readouts, but the dominant coordinate and readout layer vary.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This section asserts only the computed finite-row support of $P\_{Q\perp T}$ in the nine B*\_Q6 residual coordinates and the associated scalar diagnostics. Modeled tAI is not measured ribosome occupancy or elongation speed. The $a\_\perp$ support lists do not identify a biochemical mechanism. Protein abundance, mRNA half-life, condition dependence, tissue dependence, complex membership, localization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, and cross-organism universality require their own matched contacts and statistics. In particular, a positive coordinate in $a\_\perp$ is not by itself a translation-realization claim, and a positive $R^2\_{QP}$ is not by itself evidence for structure, function, or a general biological rule.
