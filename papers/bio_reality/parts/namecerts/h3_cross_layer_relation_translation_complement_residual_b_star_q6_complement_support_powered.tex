\subsection{Translation-Complement Residual Support for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bior-bstarq6-translation-complement-residual-support}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This section isolates the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that is not accounted for by the modeled translation-adaptation direction.  For each organism $o$, the finite calculation begins with the nine synonymous residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The response $P$ is protein abundance after the same amino-acid composition, length, GC3, and $M$-density controls used throughout the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance panel.  The translation axis $T$ is the modeled tAI direction derived from codon-specific tRNA-copy adaptation weights, not a measured ribosome-footprint experiment.  The object tested here is therefore not total $B^*_{Q6}$ survival, but the residual support left after projecting the $Q$-explained abundance component away from modeled tAI.

\paragraph{Finite construction.}
Let $Q_o$ be the residualized $n_o \times 9$ matrix of $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates for organism $o$, let $P_o$ be the residualized abundance vector, and let $T_o$ be the residualized modeled-tAI readout on the same rows.  The calculation first forms the abundance component explained by the nine-coordinate design, then removes the part aligned with $T_o$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{Q,o} &= \operatorname{Proj}_{\operatorname{span}(Q_o)}(P_o),\\
P_{Q,o}^{\perp T} &= P_{Q,o}-\operatorname{Proj}_{\operatorname{span}(T_o)}(P_{Q,o}),\\
d_o &= \frac{\|P_{Q,o}^{\perp T}\|^2}{\|P_{Q,o}\|^2},
\qquad
m_o = 1-d_o .
\end{aligned}
$$
The vector $a_{\perp,o}$ is then obtained by reading $P_{Q,o}^{\perp T}$ back against the same nine residual coordinates.  Its support profile answers a narrower question: which $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates carry the abundance component that is not explained by the modeled translation-adaptation axis?

\paragraph{Rows and controls.}
Six organism panels were evaluated.  The joined row counts were $n=19053$ for Homo sapiens, $n=14410$ for Danio rerio, $n=12418$ for Gallus gallus, $n=5815$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $n=4129$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155, and $n=3739$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655.  In every panel the control design had $24$ columns and rank $23$.  The E. coli codon table used the E. coli CDS organism label with the Escherichia coli tRNA organism label; the other five panels used matching CDS and tRNA organism labels.  Thus the reported numbers are not single-row signs or unadjusted codon-frequency contrasts: they are finite residual projections after the declared $Z$ controls.

\paragraph{Complement magnitudes.}
The modeled-tAI complement is substantial in most panels but not uniformly so.  The largest complement fraction is in Gallus gallus, where $R^2_{QP}=0.03980793595345331$, $\|P_Q^{\perp T}\|^2=695.5324705841906$, $d=0.9400341806302775$, and $m=0.05996581936972245$.  Homo sapiens is similar, with $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565117192484$, $\|P_Q^{\perp T}\|^2=1262.22376787716$, $d=0.9208949355679455$, and $m=0.07910506443205445$.  Mycobacterium smegmatis gives $R^2_{QP}=0.032090091625816136$, $\|P_Q^{\perp T}\|^2=164.9629139386272$, $d=0.8972672018746489$, and $m=0.10273279812535108$.  Danio rerio gives $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529805760884$, $\|P_Q^{\perp T}\|^2=1005.3614101653925$, $d=0.874628721049266$, and $m=0.12537127895073397$.  E. coli gives $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526667768725$, $\|P_Q^{\perp T}\|^2=287.00467177017856$, $d=0.6629960038578532$, and $m=0.3370039961421468$.  Yeast is the exceptional panel: $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624987287115$, $\|P_Q^{\perp T}\|^2=186.88597748267532$, $d=0.21173219572706703$, and $m=0.788267804272933$, so the modeled-tAI direction absorbs most of the $Q$-explained abundance component there.

\paragraph{Coordinate support.}
Each organism yields a nine-dimensional $a_\perp$ readback with absolute support fractions summing to $1$ up to rounding.  The dominant coordinates are not the same across organisms.  Danio rerio is led by $f_3$-stress, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$.  E. coli is led by $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Gallus gallus is led by $Arg_{AGR}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $K_{AAA}$.  Homo sapiens is led by $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $K_{AAA}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Mycobacterium smegmatis is led by $K_{AAA}$, $f_3$-stress, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, and $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae is led by $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  This heterogeneity is itself part of the result: the complement is not a single universal coordinate, but a finite organism-indexed residual profile.

\paragraph{Comparison with mediation fractions.}
The complement fraction $m_o$ was checked against a separate joint-decomposition reference computed on the same residualized $Q_e,T_e,P_e,Z$ rows.  Only Mycobacterium smegmatis agreed within $0.02$: its complement value $m=0.10273279812535108$ differed from the mediation reference $0.08397602919861291$ by $0.018756768926738165$.  The other five panels did not agree at that tolerance.  Danio rerio had $m=0.12537127895073397$ versus $0.3157053953614641$, absolute difference $0.19033411641073011$.  E. coli had $m=0.3370039961421468$ versus $0.612922252793667$, absolute difference $0.27591825665152014$.  Gallus gallus had $m=0.05996581936972245$ versus $0.1976903964724546$, absolute difference $0.13772457710273214$.  Homo sapiens had $m=0.07910506443205445$ versus $0.27206289160119157$, absolute difference $0.19295782716913712$.  Yeast had $m=0.788267804272933$ versus $0.9491551041216973$, absolute difference $0.16088729984876438$.  The complement projection should therefore be read as a geometrically defined residual support statistic, not as an interchangeable estimate of causal mediation.

\paragraph{Relation to candidate readouts.}
A related ranking table evaluated possible supplementary readouts for the modeled-tAI complement.  With penalty parameter $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL=\log_2(1+n_{col})$, the top readout in Danio rerio was measured translation efficiency, with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_H C_H=0.011566051846174291$, score $0.001566051846174291$, $n=13140$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$, and null $95$th percentile $0.0003297458343745959$.  In Homo sapiens the top ranked readout was complex membership, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_H C_H=0.006566030115340095$, score $-0.0034339698846599055$, $n=18335$, Bonferroni threshold $0.0002646171322365322$, and null $95$th percentile $0.0002334419901244703$.  In yeast the top ranked readout was mRNA, with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_H C_H=0.0011462622274400686$, score $-0.008853737772559931$, $n=5128$, Bonferroni threshold $0.0013014749238591248$, and null $95$th percentile $0.0004745820831535835$.  E. coli and Mycobacterium had no positive selected $H_o^*$ in that ranking.  These rows support the same local interpretation: the complement may point toward translation, transcript, complex, or other readout families depending on organism, but the ranking is descriptive and penalized rather than mechanistic.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
Several independent finite panels constrain the same nine-coordinate system.  In yeast mRNA-half-life mediation, the direct abundance association dropped from $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ to $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$ after conditioning on mRNA half-life, so the absorbed part was $0.027596042074056304$ and the mediation fraction was $0.09994436477872035$; the right-tail permutation probability was $0.000999000999000999$ against a null mean $0.00019674487584783138$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0014571003414947088$.  A per-coordinate reducibility panel on yeast used $n=1258$, five folds, $120$ permutations, rank $D_e=3$, and found real-source evidence for eight of the nine named coordinates, excluding only $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$ from that hit list.  The $f_3$-stress family decomposition used $n=1800$, $21$ synonymous family subcoordinates, five folds, and $120$ permutations; $P_{f3}$ had squared norm $52.76911713166682$ and accounted for $0.12259712041843235$ of the target energy.  A position-controlled ribosome-occupancy panel requested $200$ within-bin permutations and reported nine computed families with three families passing the $\lambda DL$ and positive-$C_H$ gate: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, and $f_3$-stress, with $DL=2$ and $\lambda=0.01$.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row result is positive for the narrow complement-support claim.  The projection $P_Q^{\perp T}$ was computed with finite values in all six organisms, the nine-coordinate readback exists in each case, and the control ranks and row counts are reported.  The evidence is strongest as a bounded residual decomposition: modeled tAI explains much of the yeast $Q$-abundance component but leaves large complements in the other five organisms, and those complements have explicit coordinate support.  The chapter therefore supports the statement that $B^*_{Q6}$ has an organism-indexed abundance residual not exhausted by the modeled tAI axis.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No stronger biological conclusion follows from this calculation alone.  The translation axis used here is modeled tAI, not measured ribosome occupancy or measured translation efficiency.  The statistic is a projection decomposition, not a causal experiment.  It does not establish translation realization beyond the readouts directly supplied, and it does not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universal law.  Any such claim requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic on joined rows.
