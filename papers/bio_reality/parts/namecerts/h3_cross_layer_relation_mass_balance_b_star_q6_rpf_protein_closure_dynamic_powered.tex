\subsection{Dynamic mass-balance closure for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ ribosome-footprint and protein-abundance surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite mediation surface whose rows join three kinds of coordinates: $q_i$ in the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis, $t_j$ in translation-layer readouts, and $p_k$ in matched protein-abundance measurements.  The intended readback is not a direct residual-to-protein arrow.  A residual coordinate may enter the protein-abundance part of the analysis only after the same finite row surface has exposed a translation readout slot and after amino-acid composition, organism identity, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance have remained controls.  In this chapter the tested surface is narrower still: it asks whether a dynamic mass-balance model that already contains a strong baseline can be improved by the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$-attached ribosome-footprint and protein-abundance closure terms.

The concrete run used $120$ genes, sampled from $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed cap, and produced $718$ time or interval rows.  The fixed cap is part of the finite-row definition: it prevents the fit from being read as an unconstrained population law and keeps the reported comparison tied to a bounded, reproducible matrix.

\paragraph{Model ladder and numerical verdict.}
The measured ladder begins with an $M$-only baseline and then adds successive dynamic and residual layers.  The registered values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final comparison for the tested closure term was negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two effective-null checks also failed to support the promoted reading.  The translation-efficiency-bin null gave $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p=0.9306930693069307$.  These are not marginal positive tests; they are large-tail probabilities under the declared null comparisons.  The finite-row conclusion is therefore that the dynamic mass-balance closure did not find a positive $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ increment after the stronger baseline and null controls were applied.

\paragraph{Relation to the wider translation and protein-abundance matrix.}
The negative dynamic mass-balance verdict should be separated from other finite surfaces in the same biological region.  A separate observable-execution decomposition in yeast reported $3289$ genes and concluded that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  Another cross-organism modeled-tAI and protein-abundance decomposition used twelve organism-scoped joined surfaces, with examples including $5815$ yeast proteins, $19053$ human proteins, $19395$ mouse proteins, and $3739$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 proteins.  In that modeled-tAI surface, the yeast mediation fraction was $0.9491551041216973$, while human and mouse had fractions $0.27206289160119157$ and $0.15488799907323988$, respectively.  Those numbers show that some bounded translation-readout or modeled-mediation surfaces can be nonzero, but they do not overturn the present dynamic closure result.  The present claim is about the finite mass-balance ladder above, where the added closure layer degraded the registered fit and failed both null checks.

\paragraph{Permitted readback.}
The allowed statement is a closure refusal for this finite surface.  The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can remain in the codon-count or translation-readout audit ledger, and they can be compared with ribosome-footprint or protein-abundance rows when those rows are explicitly joined and controlled.  For this run, however, the dynamic mass-balance protein-closure claim is not supported: $L_3$ did not improve on $L_2$, the description-length increment was negative, and both null probes returned high $p$-values.  Thus the packet remains bounded at a controlled audit status rather than entering a positive protein-realization fit.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
No stronger biological interpretation follows from this table.  The run does not by itself establish translation realization, ribosome mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality.  A direct $q_i \to p_k$ association is not admissible as protein realization unless a separately measured or explicitly bounded $q_i \to t_j$ translation step survives the declared controls, and even such a step would still not prove folding, function, mechanism, or a general biological law.  The present evidence is only a finite negative result for a dynamic ribosome-footprint and protein-abundance mass-balance closure test.
