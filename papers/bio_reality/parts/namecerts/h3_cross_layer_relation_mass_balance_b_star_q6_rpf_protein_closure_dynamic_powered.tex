\subsection{Dynamic mass-balance closure for the B*\_Q6 residual surface}
\label{subsec:b-star-q6-rpf-protein-closure-dynamic-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the B*\_Q6 residual coordinates can be promoted from a bounded translation/protein association surface to a dynamic mass-balance closure surface.  The finite rows are genes and ribosome-footprint intervals, not abstract sequence possibilities.  Each eligible row carries codon-count residual coordinates, a measured or derived translation slot, and a matched protein-abundance or protein-state target.  The intended closure question is narrow: after a mass term $M$ and an estimated translation term $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$ are already present, does the B*\_Q6 residual block still improve the dynamic readout in held finite data?

\paragraph{Controls and ladder.}
The run used $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample and then imposed a fixed cap of $120$ genes.  The resulting finite panel contained $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=120$ and $n_{\mathrm{intervals}}=718$.  The fitted ladder was recorded as four nested levels.  Level $L_0$ used the mass baseline alone, level $L_1$ added the estimated translation statistic, level $L_2$ entered the next controlled block, and level $L_3$ added the B*\_Q6 residual closure block being tested.  The observed scores were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0\!:\!M) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1\!:\!M,E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the registered final contrast is negative rather than positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2) = -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null probes agree with that direction.  The translation-efficiency-bin null gave $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  These values do not support a positive dynamic closure effect for the B*\_Q6 block on this finite mass-balance panel.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader protein-abundance mediation surface.}
The negative dynamic result is not a contradiction of the static translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition.  In the separate B*\_Q6 mediated protein-abundance run, the finite organism panel was much larger and explicitly cross-organism: for example, the joined protein counts were $3739$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $5815$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $19053$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $19395$ for \textit{Mus musculus}.  That run computed a joint $9$-coordinate residual block and obtained in-unit-interval mediation fractions for all listed organisms, including
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.612922252793667,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.27206289160119157,\\
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{mouse}} &= 0.15488799907323988.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those values describe a controlled cross-sectional mediation decomposition using modeled per-protein tAI and matched protein abundance.  The present chapter asks a stricter dynamic question: whether a mass-balance/RPF/protein closure still gains predictive description once the mass and translation-statistic terms have already been installed.  The answer recorded by the finite rows is no.

\paragraph{Observable execution comparison.}
A second neighboring contact also helps delimit the conclusion.  The observable execution decomposition run in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} used $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=3289$ and ended with the verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That result shows that the B*\_Q6 family can touch a measured execution-rate surface under its own design.  It does not imply that the present mass-balance closure surface must pass.  Here, the fixed subsample of $120$ genes and $718$ intervals is the governing finite object, and its own ladder reports a loss when the final residual block is added.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The chapter therefore reads the dynamic mass-balance experiment as a bounded negative closure test.  The permitted statement is that, on the registered finite panel, the B*\_Q6 residual block did not improve the RPF/protein dynamic closure beyond the installed mass and translation-statistic ladder; the recorded contrast is $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ with high null-tail probabilities.  The result keeps B*\_Q6 at translation-readout, rate-proxy, or static mediation status where those separate finite tests support such language, but it does not allow protein-realization closure in the dynamic mass-balance sense.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No claim of translation mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, evolutionary law, or universal rule follows from this chapter.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not enough for protein realization unless the same finite design also supplies a surviving $Q_i\to T_j$ translation step and then shows a controlled $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ mediation or attenuation pattern.  In the present dynamic closure run, the final residual addition is negative, so the packet remains a bounded audit result rather than a promoted biological mechanism.
