\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for the B$^*_{Q6}$ RPF--Protein Surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance-closure}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record considered here is a finite cross-layer table whose rows join a B$^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, a translation-side readout derived from ribosome-protected fragment information, and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The intended question is not whether synonymous residual coordinates have some cross-sectional association with protein abundance in a broad proteomics table.  That weaker question is already separable from dynamics.  The sharper question is whether the residual coordinates improve a dynamic mass-balance closure once the dominant mass term, the estimated expression or translation state, and the finite interval structure are already present.

The powered run used $4434$ eligible genes before fixing the computational subsample, then analyzed $120$ genes and $718$ gene-interval rows.  The fixed subsample is part of the finite design: it prevents the chapter from reading a partially explored surface as if it were an unrestricted population theorem.  The measured row set therefore has the following local interpretation.  Each retained interval carries a mass-like baseline slot $M$, an estimated state slot $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$, a translation or RPF-conditioned layer, and a candidate B$^*_{Q6}$ residual contribution.  The candidate residuals are judged only by their incremental contribution after the earlier slots have already been fitted.

\paragraph{Nested finite fits.}
The registered fit ladder reports four coefficient-of-determination values.  The mass-only baseline is already nearly closed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0}(M\text{ only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1}(M,E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the next layer gives a lower held-out value rather than an improvement:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the registered contrast from $L2$ to $L3$ is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two effective null checks agree with the negative direction.  The translation-efficiency-bin null gives $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gives $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  These are not borderline positive values.  On this table, the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual block does not supply a dynamic mass-balance closure improvement after the earlier terms are present.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader protein-abundance surface.}
This negative dynamic result must be kept distinct from a broader cross-sectional mediation surface.  In the multi-organism protein-abundance table, the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual block was evaluated against modeled per-protein tAI and PAXdb abundance across twelve organisms.  The same nine coordinates were used there: $K_{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{AUA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  That table reported positive statistical mediation fractions for organism-level rows, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K--12 MG1655, $0.6084594553474505$ in \emph{Bacillus subtilis} strain 168, and $0.27206289160119157$ in human.  In the yeast row of that table the total residual-to-protein association was $c=0.29837624994140827$, the direct association after modeled tAI was $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and the indirect difference was $0.28320534058057895$.

Those numbers show that a static protein-abundance mediation statistic can be nonzero under modeled tAI conditioning.  They do not force the dynamic mass-balance surface to close.  The present finite-row run asks whether the same residual family improves a stricter RPF--protein mass-balance ladder.  Its answer is negative: the fitted $L3$ layer is worse than $L2$ on the registered contrast, and both null p-values are large.

\paragraph{Comparison with neighboring finite audits.}
The neighboring observable-execution decomposition supplies a useful boundary marker.  In the rate-slot bridge run, $3289$ yeast genes were analyzed and the final verdict was an observable-execution subblock: at least one observed rate proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That result says that some rate-proxy contact exists at the execution layer.  It does not say that every downstream residual block improves every mass-balance equation.  The present run is exactly the stricter check: after $M$, $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$, and the preceding fitted layer are in the model, the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual contribution has $\Delta R^2<0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}<0$.

The turnover residual audit points in the same conservative direction for a different downstream layer.  There, $3290$ genes were analyzed and the B$^*_{Q6}$ block beyond expression, translation efficiency, pLDDT, and N-end controls had $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-13.68819707902732$.  Its matched-bin and recoded null p-values were $0.8855721393034826$ and $1.0$.  The mass-balance result is therefore not an isolated refusal artifact; it belongs to a local pattern in which some translation-adjacent contacts can be real while certain downstream dynamic closures remain unearned.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a bounded negative for dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure.  The run completed, the table was nonempty, the tested row count was explicit, and the null checks were evaluated.  The admissible statement is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{on }120\text{ genes and }718\text{ intervals,}\quad
\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836<0,\\
&\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-668.8046070381928<0,\quad
p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832,\quad
p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently, this chapter does not promote B$^*_{Q6}$ from a translation-readout or protein-abundance association surface into a dynamic protein-realization law.  The correct local readback is that the candidate residual family fails this powered mass-balance closure test under the registered finite design.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality follows from this chapter.  A positive static mediation fraction, a modeled tAI contact, an RPF-derived rate proxy, or a direct residual-to-protein association is insufficient for those higher claims.  Such a claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new controlled statistic on rows suited to that layer.  In this chapter the observed evidence instead fixes a narrower conclusion: the dynamic mass-balance closure attempt is tested and does not improve when the B$^*_{Q6}$ block is added after the preceding fitted layers.
