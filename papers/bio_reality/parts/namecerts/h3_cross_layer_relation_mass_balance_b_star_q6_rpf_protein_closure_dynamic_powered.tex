\paragraph{Finite-row surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates improve a dynamic mass-balance account after a translation-side readout and protein-abundance side are already present in the same finite fitting surface. The evaluated table contains $120$ fixed-subsample genes drawn from $4434$ eligible genes and $718$ gene--time or gene--interval rows. The tested residual coordinates are the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ panel used throughout this subject: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The row geometry is therefore not a direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association. It is a controlled comparison between a simpler dynamic mass surface and a larger surface in which $B^*_{Q6}$ terms are allowed to enter only after the translation and abundance slots have been separated.

\paragraph{Nested fits.}
The finite result is most compactly read as a nested model ledger. The baseline mass-only layer already explains nearly all measured variance, with $R^2=0.9960628201518571$. Adding the estimated dynamic translation-side term gives $R^2=0.996410628851051$. The next controlled layer has $R^2=0.9712443377743347$, and the full $B^*_{Q6}$-augmented layer has $R^2=0.8953999568421264$. Thus the registered contrast for the final augmentation is negative rather than positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M+E} &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264,\\
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The effective-null checks agree with this refusal. The translation-efficiency-bin null gives $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gives $p=0.9306930693069307$. These values do not support a positive dynamic propagation claim. They say that, on this table, letting the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block enter the dynamic RPF--protein closure makes the registered fit worse rather than better.

They do not override the dynamic mass-balance result above, because the present chapter asks the harder question of whether the residual block improves a time-aware RPF--protein closure after the simpler mass and translation terms are in place.

Other finite checks locate where the same coordinate family can and cannot be promoted. \paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite verdict is a bounded negative for dynamic RPF--protein closure. The registered run completed in $441.440662$ seconds and produced a nonpositive improvement under the declared contrast. Since $\Delta R^2<0$, $\Delta DL_{bits}<0$, $p_{TE\,bin}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{time\,shuffle}=0.9306930693069307$, the $B^*_{Q6}$ block is not promoted to a dynamic mass-balance protein-realization layer on this surface. The appropriate readback is that $B^*_{Q6}$ remains bounded to the already separated translation-readout or codon-residual contexts unless another finite contact supplies a positive, controlled, same-row statistic.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter does not infer translation mechanism from codon geometry, modeled tAI, RPF counts, protein abundance, or residual notation alone.
