\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for the $B^*_{Q6}$ RPF--Protein Surface}
\label{subsec:b-star-q6-rpf-protein-closure-dynamic-powered}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates add a dynamic mass-balance closure term beyond a high-performing measured baseline. The finite surface contains genes with matched coding-sequence coordinates, ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency information, and downstream protein-abundance readouts. The powered run used $120$ genes after a fixed subsample cap, selected from $4434$ eligible genes, and evaluated $718$ interval rows. The measured baseline was deliberately strong: an $M$-only layer already reached $R^2=0.9960628201518571$, and adding the estimated expression-rate slot gave $R^2=0.996410628851051$. The later dynamic layers did not improve that surface. The reported sequence of fits is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M+E_{\mathrm{stat}}} &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final contrast was negative, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits. The two null checks also gave no support for a positive dynamic closure term: the TE-bin null had $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null had $p=0.9306930693069307$.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The tested object is not a free association from a synonymous residual coordinate to a protein-output claim. It is a controlled finite-row question: after a measured or explicitly bounded translation readout is present, does the $B^*_{Q6}$ surface improve a dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance fit beyond simpler measured quantities? In this run the answer is negative. The sign and size of the fitted contrast say that the additional dynamic $B^*_{Q6}$ closure layer reduced held-out explanatory performance rather than providing a powered improvement. The numerical statement is therefore a bounded nonpromotion result, not a weak positive result.

\paragraph{Relation to the surviving rate slot.}
A separate observable-execution decomposition run found a real rate-proxy subblock in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with $3289$ genes and the verdict that at least one observed rate proxy slot passed both effective null classes. That positive rate-slot result is important because it prevents the present chapter from claiming that translation-layer observables are absent in general. The present finite-row test asks a narrower question: whether the specific $B^*_{Q6}$ dynamic mass-balance closure improves the matched RPF--protein surface. The answer remains no, because the mass-balance run reports $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$ below $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443377743347$ and below the already strong $M$-only layer.

\paragraph{Relation to modeled translation-mediated abundance.}
The broader translation-mediated protein-abundance surface already has a finite controlled decomposition over twelve organism-level joins, including $4042$ joined proteins in \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $3739$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $5815$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $19053$ in human, and $19395$ in mouse. In that modeled $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition, the yeast mediated fraction was $0.9491551041216973$, with $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect component $0.28320534058057895$. Those numbers justify treating translation-conditioned protein abundance as an admissible finite statistical surface. They do not rescue the present dynamic closure test: the present run replaces the broader cross-sectional mediation question with a stricter interval-level mass-balance question, and its own powered contrast is negative.

\paragraph{Proteostasis boundary.}
The turnover audit gives the same local caution from another downstream direction. In $3290$ genes, after expression, TE, pLDDT, N-end, and related controls, the $B^*_{Q6}$ turnover residual contrast was $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$ and $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, with $p_{\mathrm{matched\ bin}}=0.8855721393034826$ and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$. Thus the mass-balance result is not an isolated refusal caused by one unlucky null design. Across the downstream protein-state checks cited here, the finite evidence supports a bounded translation/protein-abundance interface but not a promoted dynamic protein-realization mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For this chapter the finite-row verdict is a closed negative for the proposed dynamic mass-balance promotion. The available numbers support the sentence: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates may be carried into controlled translation and protein-abundance matrices, but the tested RPF--protein dynamic closure layer is not supported by the powered mass-balance run. No translation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from these rows. Such claims would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic; they are not licensed by the negative dynamic closure contrast reported here.
