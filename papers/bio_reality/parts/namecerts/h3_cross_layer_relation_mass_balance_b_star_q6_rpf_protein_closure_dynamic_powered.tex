\subsection{Dynamic mass-balance test for a translation-conditioned protein closure}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-dynamic-mass-balance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a finite mediation surface whose rows are coding-sequence genes and whose columns separate three kinds of observable quantities: a residual codon-coordinate block $Q$, a translation-readout block $T$, and a matched protein-abundance block $P$. The target is not a direct synonymy-to-protein claim. The admissible readback is the controlled path $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$, with amino-acid composition, organism or gene context, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, the $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance held outside the promoted coordinates. In this dynamic mass-balance run, the finite panel contained $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample, then used $n=120$ genes and $718$ time or interval rows. The fixed cap is therefore part of the claim: it is a powered finite-row audit of the specified panel, not an asymptotic statement about all coding sequences.

\paragraph{Model ladder.}
The fitted ladder compares a mass-only baseline, a mass plus estimated execution term, a controlled intermediate model, and the full residual-augmented dynamic model. The recorded coefficients of determination were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M+E} &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The proposed dynamic residual closure therefore did not improve the controlled fit. Its registered increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two effective null checks were also non-supportive: the translation-efficiency-bin null gave $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p_{\mathrm{time}}=0.9306930693069307$. Thus the dynamic closure attempt is bounded on this panel. The correct readback is that the extra $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block fails to buy description-length economy after the mass-balance and translation-conditioned controls are present.

\paragraph{Relation to the positive translation-mediated surface.}
This negative dynamic verdict does not erase the separate cross-sectional mediation signal. In the multi-organism $Q \to T \to P$ protein-abundance audit, the joint nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ block had positive bounded mediation fractions in all twelve organism panels, with examples including $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.4872268357227724$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $0.27206289160119157$ for human, and $0.01850668564589326$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. The corresponding finite joined row counts included $5815$ yeast proteins, $3739$ \emph{E. coli} proteins, $11661$ worm proteins, $19053$ human proteins, and $1656$ \emph{Sulfolobus} proteins. Those fractions are statistical decompositions using modeled per-protein tAI, not direct ribosome-motion measurements. The present mass-balance run asks a stricter dynamic question: after the mass and execution terms are already in the ladder, does the residual block close additional dynamic protein variation? The answer on the registered finite panel is no.

\paragraph{Rate-slot context.}
A different execution audit found an observable execution subblock in yeast, with $3289$ genes and the verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes. This matters only as local context for the present test. It says that a rate-like observable can exist in a finite yeast panel, but it does not force every downstream protein-abundance or dynamic closure to become positive. The mass-balance result is more restrictive: even when a translation-conditioned route is scientifically meaningful, the registered dynamic residual term lost fit relative to its controlled comparison on the $120$-gene, $718$-interval panel.

\paragraph{Turnover and structure checks.}
The turnover audit gives the same caution in another layer. With $3290$ genes, the turnover residual test recorded $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, and $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, with null probabilities $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$ and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$. The yeast structure-mediation audit is more nuanced: the five-way join used $4759$ rows, reported a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and an overall join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, and obtained $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$ with $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$. Yet the structure-specific incremental fraction was only $0.0005507778820193332$, with a right-tail permutation value $0.1258741258741259$. These nearby finite-row facts support the same discipline: a bounded association, a mediation fraction, or a rate-slot signal is not automatically a dynamic protein-closure claim.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The dynamic mass-balance packet is ready as a finite negative closure result. The admissible statement is that, on the registered finite panel, adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual component after the mass-balance and execution terms produced $\Delta R^2<0$ and $\Delta DL<0$, with both null comparisons far from significance. Therefore this subject remains outside protein-realization fitting for the dynamic mass-balance layer. A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association must not be read as protein realization unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled translation-readout step and the downstream protein readout is evaluated after matched mRNA and simpler-baseline controls.
