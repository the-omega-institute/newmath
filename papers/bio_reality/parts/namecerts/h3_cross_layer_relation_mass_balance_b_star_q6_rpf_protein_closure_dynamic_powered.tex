\subsection{Mass-Balance Closure for the B-star-Q6 RPF--Protein Dynamic Surface}\label{subsec:bioreality-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface can improve a dynamic mass-balance closure after the expression and translation slots have already been attached.  The finite rows are not read as a direct synonymous-coordinate-to-protein claim.  They are read as a joined panel in which coding-sequence residual coordinates are kept separate from measured or bounded translation readouts and downstream protein abundance.  The active dynamic audit contains $120$ genes selected from $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample cap, with $718$ time or interval rows.  Its role is to ask whether an added $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block improves a protein-side dynamic closure once the mass and translation terms are already in the model.

\paragraph{Model ladder.}
Let $M$ denote the mass-only baseline, let $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$ denote the attached expression or translation-state statistic used by the finite audit, and let $B$ denote the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block.  The fitted ladder reported by the run is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0=M) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1=M+E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3=L_2+B) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tested residual addition is not a positive closure term on this finite panel.  The registered increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2)\\
&= -0.07584438093220836,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the description-length comparison is also adverse:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= -668.8046070381928\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null checks agree with the negative readback: the translation-efficiency-bin test gives $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle test gives $p=0.9306930693069307$.  These values make the dynamic mass-balance packet a bounded negative result rather than a protein-realization success.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated abundance surface.}
The negative dynamic audit should be separated from the broader static mediation surface.  A separate twelve-organism modeled-tAI/protein-abundance run did compute a finite $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition with organism-specific joined protein counts: $4042$ in \textit{Bacillus subtilis}, $11661$ in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ in \textit{Danio rerio}, $13295$ in \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ in \textit{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ in human, $19395$ in mouse, $4129$ in \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ in rat, $5815$ in yeast, and $1656$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  In that static surface, mediated fractions such as $0.9491551041216973$ for yeast, $0.612922252793667$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.6084594553474505$ for \textit{B. subtilis}, and $0.01850668564589326$ for \textit{S. solfataricus} are statistical decompositions through modeled per-protein tAI.  They do not override the dynamic mass-balance result above.  The present chapter asks the narrower question: after a dynamic RPF--protein closure has already absorbed the available mass and translation-state information, does adding the residual block improve held-out closure?  On the registered rows, it does not.

\paragraph{Turnover-side consistency check.}
A distinct turnover audit gives the same directional warning against over-reading residual coordinates as protein-state mechanisms.  In the turnover residual panel, $n=3290$ genes were analyzed with $200$ null draws, and the fitted comparison moved from $R^2(L_2)=0.055740851626933985$ to $R^2(L_3)=0.050278872193227464$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= -13.68819707902732\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched-bin and recoded null checks were non-supportive, with $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$ and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  This is not the same experiment as the RPF--protein dynamic closure, but it supports the same local discipline: residual codon coordinates may remain useful as finite coordinates, yet they are not promoted to protein turnover, degradation, or proteostasis mechanisms without a layer-matched statistic.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a closed negative for the stated dynamic mass-balance question.  The tested $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block does not improve the registered RPF--protein closure after the mass-only and expression/translation-state baselines are present.  The strongest local statement is therefore:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{support}_{\mathrm{dynamic\ mass\ closure}}(B^{\ast}_{Q6}) &= 0\\
\text{on the registered }(120\ \mathrm{genes},718\ \mathrm{intervals})\text{ panel.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the packet remains bounded at controlled codon-residual, translation-readout, or static mediation status; it does not enter dynamic protein-realization fitting on this audit.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this chapter.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not enough for protein realization, and a modeled or measured translation contact is not enough for folding or function.  Any later positive claim at those layers would need its own finite contact: a surviving $Q_i\to T_j$ translation step under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, and matched mRNA controls; a downstream protein or protein-state measurement matched to the same rows; and a nonnegative held-out or description-length improvement after simpler baselines have been ablated.  The present mass-balance audit supplies the opposite local evidence: the added residual block makes the dynamic closure worse on the registered finite panel.
