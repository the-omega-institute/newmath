\subsection{Dynamic mass-balance closure for B$^*_{Q6}$ RPF--protein rows}\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.} This record concerns a finite joined surface in which each eligible coding sequence carries a B$^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate vector, a ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency readout, and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The intended biological question is not whether a synonymous residual can correlate with protein abundance in isolation.  The sharper question is whether that residual still improves a dynamic mass-balance closure after the dominant translation and expression slots have already been accounted for.  The powered audit used $120$ genes after a fixed subsample cap from $4434$ eligible genes and produced $718$ time or interval rows.  The row count is therefore large enough to test the declared finite regression surface, but the gene count remains deliberately bounded rather than used as a population-wide biological law.

\paragraph{Model ladder.} The audit compares four nested readout layers.  The first layer, $L_0$, uses the mass or mRNA-scale baseline alone.  The second layer, $L_1$, adds the estimated expression or translation state term.  The third layer, $L_2$, adds the ordinary controlled readout block before the B$^*_{Q6}$ increment is interpreted.  The fourth layer, $L_3$, is the candidate closure layer after the residual coordinate contribution is admitted.  The measured coefficients of determination were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The candidate B$^*_{Q6}$ closure step is therefore not a positive mass-balance improvement in this run.  The registered increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2)\\
&= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both signs point in the same direction: adding the tested residual block worsened the held-out dynamic closure rather than improving it.

\paragraph{Null tests.} Two finite null comparisons keep the result from being promoted by inspection of a single fitted ladder.  The TE-bin null gave $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p=0.9306930693069307$.  These values do not support a positive B$^*_{Q6}$ mass-balance residual after the declared controls.  The appropriate readback is a bounded negative result: the powered run completed, the data were joined, the dynamic rows were evaluated, and the candidate residual did not survive the finite null comparisons.

\paragraph{Relation to protein-abundance mediation.} This negative mass-balance result is compatible with, but narrower than, the separate cross-organism protein-abundance mediation audit.  In that audit the B$^*_{Q6}$ coordinate vector had $9$ coordinates: K\_AAA, Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and f3\_stress.  The protein-abundance surface joined $12$ organisms, including $5815$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins, $3739$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 proteins, $19053$ Homo sapiens proteins, and $19395$ Mus musculus proteins.  Several organism-level statistical mediation fractions were positive in that separate matrix, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.612922252793667$ in Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.15488799907323988$ in Mus musculus.  Those values justify retaining the mediated protein-abundance surface as a statistical object, but they do not overturn the present dynamic closure result.  A cross-sectional mediation fraction is not the same quantity as a time- or interval-indexed mass-balance improvement.

\paragraph{Relation to observable execution.} The observable-execution audit supplies another boundary check.  It ran on $3289$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae genes and returned the conclusion that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That outcome says that some rate proxy slots can be empirically visible.  It does not say that every downstream dynamic closure built from B$^*_{Q6}$ residuals must improve held-out mass balance.  The present chapter therefore separates the existence of a rate-slot readout from the stronger claim that the residual coordinates close the RPF--protein dynamic surface.

\paragraph{Relation to turnover residuals.} The turnover audit is directionally consistent with the present refusal to promote the residual.  With $3290$ genes, the turnover run reported $R^2(L_2)=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2(L_3)=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-13.68819707902732$, with $p=0.8855721393034826$ for the matched-bin null and $p=1.0$ for the recoded null.  Thus, in both the dynamic mass-balance surface and the turnover surface, the relevant B$^*_{Q6}$ residual increment is not a supported positive predictor once the stronger baseline terms are present.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.} The local conclusion is finite and deliberately modest.  The powered mass-balance run is a completed negative readout for the tested B$^*_{Q6}$ RPF--protein closure surface: $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=120$, $n_{\mathrm{intervals}}=718$, $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-668.8046070381928$, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  It supports the statement that this particular residual block remains bounded at translation-readout, codon-count residual, or cross-sectional mediation status rather than becoming a dynamic protein-realization closure.  No translation mechanism, folding pathway, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, selection law, causation claim, or universal biological rule follows from these rows.  Any such higher-layer assertion would require a separate matched contact at that layer and a fresh finite statistic showing survival under the relevant controls.
