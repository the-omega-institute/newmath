\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, Footprint Intervals, and Protein Abundance}\label{sec:bior-h3-cross-layer-mass-balance-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-closure-dynamic-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates remain visible after a dynamic mass-balance surface has already accounted for expression scale, estimated translation flow, ribosome-footprint interval structure, and matched protein abundance.  The finite table contains $120$ genes selected from $4434$ eligible genes under a fixed subsample cap of $120$, and it expands those genes into $718$ footprint intervals.  The tested object is not a direct map from a synonymous residual coordinate to a protein claim.  It is a controlled finite relation among codon-residual coordinates $q_i$, translation or footprint readouts $t_j$, and matched protein abundance readouts $p_k$, with amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline kept as controls rather than promoted to biological coordinates.

\paragraph{Nested fits.}
Let $M$ denote the mass or expression-scale baseline, let $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$ denote the static expression-side augmentation, let $R$ denote the footprint-interval readout block, and let $Q$ denote the projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block.  The finite experiment reports the following nested predictive surface:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0=M\text{ only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1=M+E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered increment from $L_2$ to $L_3$ is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two finite null checks also fail to support a positive dynamic closure: the translation-efficiency-bin null gives $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gives $p=0.9306930693069307$.  Thus the observed table does not license a claim that adding the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual layer improves dynamic RPF-to-protein closure once the preceding mass-balance and translation-side blocks are already present.

\paragraph{Relation to the modeled mediation surface.}
A separate modeled mediation table supplies useful context but does not override the dynamic closure result.  In that table the same nine projected coordinates are used: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The modeled $Q \to T \to P$ surface joins twelve organisms and reports, for example, $5815$ joined yeast proteins, $19053$ joined human proteins, and $3739$ joined \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655 proteins.  Its modeled mediation fractions include $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  These numbers show that a modeled codon-adaptation readout can organize a bounded statistical mediation matrix.  They do not show that the dynamic footprint interval table closes positively: in the dynamic mass-balance experiment the incremental fit is negative and the null probabilities are large.

\paragraph{Execution-side comparison.}
The broader execution audit also separates a local rate-slot signal from this stronger closure claim.  One finite execution decomposition in yeast used $3289$ genes and returned the verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That positive rate-slot statement is narrower than dynamic protein-abundance closure.  It says that some observed execution subblock can carry a residual signal under its own controls; it does not imply that the $120$-gene, $718$-interval mass-balance surface gains predictive power after the dynamic blocks are included.

\paragraph{Turnover comparison.}
A turnover audit gives a parallel negative boundary.  With $3290$ genes and $200$ null draws, the turnover residual surface reports $R^2(L_2)=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2(L_3)=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-13.68819707902732$, with $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$ and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  This independent finite table is consistent with the mass-balance verdict: after expression, translation-side, and protein-side controls are present, the current rows do not support promotion of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual geometry into a general protein-state prediction.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For this chapter the admissible conclusion is a bounded negative closure.  The finite dynamic table ran to completion, but the strongest registered increment is not positive: $\Delta R^2<0$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}<0$, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  Therefore the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates remain at controlled translation-readout, modeled mediation, or codon-count residual scope for this test.  A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not admissible here unless the same finite rows first show a surviving $Q_i \to T_j$ translation-readout step and then show downstream attenuation or improvement in the matched protein readout.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection principle, or universal biological rule follows from this mass-balance run.  Modeled tAI, ribosome-footprint intervals, matched mRNA controls, and matched protein abundance are separate measurement routes with their own noise and identification limits.  The present finite result only says that, under the registered controls and null checks, the dynamic RPF-protein closure surface does not gain support from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block.
