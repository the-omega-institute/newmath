\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for the B*Q6 RPF--Protein Interface}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-dynamic-mass-balance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates gain a downstream protein-abundance role after a translation-attached mass-balance model has already accounted for coarse abundance flow.  The finite panel contains $120$ genes selected from $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample cap, with $718$ time or interval rows.  The row surface is deliberately small and explicit: each admissible entry is a matched gene--interval observation carrying a mass baseline, an estimated translation term, finite $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency binning information, and matched protein readout information where the dataset supplies it.  The computation therefore addresses a narrow question: after the mass and translation slots are present, do the residual coordinates add positive predictive closure for the protein-side dynamic readout?

\paragraph{Model ladder.}
The registered ladder has four numerical levels.  The mass-only baseline gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0} &= 0.9960628201518571.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the estimated translation term gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L1} &= 0.996410628851051.
\end{aligned}
$$
The next finite slot, $L2$, evaluates the controlled translation-attached surface and returns
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347.
\end{aligned}
$$
The residual-augmented $B^*_{Q6}$ level returns
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the registered residual increment is not positive:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2_{L3}-R^2_{L2} \\
&= -0.07584438093220836,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the description-length comparison is likewise adverse,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite computation therefore does not support promotion of the residual coordinates into a protein-realization fit for this dynamic mass-balance surface.

\paragraph{Null checks.}
Two effective null routes were evaluated.  The translation-efficiency bin null gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{TEbin}} &= 0.6831683168316832,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the time-shuffle null gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are far from a positive tail claim.  In this chapter the p-values are read only as finite-row guards against accidental ordering or bin artifacts; they are not used as evidence for a mechanism.  The run time was $441.440662$ seconds, which records that the registered offline job completed rather than timing out or returning a partial surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation and protein contacts.}
Other finite rows in the same research packet show why this negative mass-balance result is informative without being globally destructive.  A separate observable-execution decomposition in yeast used $3289$ genes and reported the verdict \emph{observable execution subblock}: at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  A broad modeled translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition across $12$ organisms found positive statistical mediation fractions, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse.  Those rows show that bounded translation or abundance associations can exist on other finite surfaces.  They do not override the present dynamic mass-balance test, because the present ladder asks whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates improve the already strong mass and translation dynamic closure on the $120$-gene, $718$-interval panel.  On that question, the observed increment is negative.

\paragraph{Mass-balance verdict.}
The local conclusion is a bounded refusal rather than a universal negation.  The mass-only and mass-plus-estimated-translation levels already explain nearly all variance on the selected dynamic panel, with $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628201518571$ and $R^2_{L1}=0.996410628851051$.  Once the controlled ladder reaches the residual-augmented level, the held comparison worsens rather than improves.  Consequently this chapter records the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates as bounded at the translation-readout or codon-count residual interface for this mass-balance experiment.  The coordinates are not admitted here as a protein-side dynamic closure term.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No claim about translation realization, ribosome mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality follows from this row.  A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not admissible in this chapter unless the same finite surface first supplies a surviving $Q_i \to T_j$ translation step and then shows a controlled downstream mediation or attenuation pattern.  Here the relevant registered comparison goes the other way: $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-668.8046070381928$.  Matched mRNA abundance, modeled or observed translation readouts, and protein abundance remain measurement contacts or controls; they are not promoted into folding, function, physical feasibility, or a general biological law.

\paragraph{Turnover-side consistency check.}
A related turnover audit also returned a bounded negative result after expression, translation-efficiency, structural-confidence, and N-end controls: $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-13.68819707902732$ over $3290$ genes.  That separate row is not merged with the dynamic mass-balance surface, but it supports the same local discipline: when the controlled finite panel does not improve after adding $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals, the result is recorded as a boundary on the current data rather than converted into a higher-layer biological interpretation.
