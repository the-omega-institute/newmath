\subsection{Dynamic RPF--Protein Closure of the $B^*_{Q6}$ Mass-Balance Route}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-closure-dynamic}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates remain useful after a dynamic mass-balance layer has already accounted for the measured translation and protein-abundance relation.  The finite rows are not abstract sequence examples: the run first found $4434$ eligible genes, then used a fixed subsample of $120$ genes and $718$ time or interval rows for the powered closure audit.  The tested carrier is a matched row table in which a coding-sequence residual coordinate is allowed to reach protein abundance only through the observed or estimated translation layer and the mass-balance state variables.  The nine residual coordinates inherited by the surrounding $Q6$ program are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The local question is therefore not whether these coordinates exist, nor whether codon counts can be projected onto them.  The question is narrower: after the mass-balance variable $M$ and the translation estimate $E_{stat}$ are present, does adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block improve dynamic protein closure?

\paragraph{Nested finite fits.}
The fitted hierarchy reported four nested coefficients of determination.  The $M$-only baseline already explained almost all of the tested variation, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0}(M\ \mathrm{only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1}(M,E_{stat}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the registered comparison scale, the additional dynamic block moved the held-out fit in the wrong direction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL &= -668.8046070381928\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two effective null checks also failed to support a positive closure claim.  The TE-bin null gave $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p=0.9306930693069307$.  Thus the finite table does not show that $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals improve dynamic RPF--protein mass balance once the dominant mass-balance and translation terms are already in the model.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader protein-abundance mediation matrix.}
This negative dynamic result is not a denial that $B^*_{Q6}$ can have bounded statistical contacts with protein abundance in other finite panels.  In the cross-organism mediation audit, the same nine-dimensional residual block was evaluated against per-protein modeled tAI and PAXdb abundance over $12$ organisms.  The joined row counts were substantial: $4042$ proteins in \textit{Bacillus subtilis}, $3739$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $5815$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $19053$ in human, $19395$ in mouse, and $1656$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, among the reported organism panels.  That audit estimated positive statistical mediation fractions such as $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.6084594553474505$ in \textit{B. subtilis}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{S. solfataricus}.  Those numbers belong to a modeled tAI--protein-abundance decomposition, not to the present dynamic RPF--protein closure.  They show why the dynamic closure question is meaningful: a residual-to-protein association can exist on a cross-sectional surface while still failing to add predictive closure on a stricter dynamic mass-balance surface.

\paragraph{Execution and turnover checks.}
The surrounding finite ledger also separates the present negative result from two adjacent claims.  A rate-slot decomposition in yeast used $3289$ genes and returned the module classification \emph{observable\_execution\_subblock}, meaning that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed its effective null classes.  Conversely, a turnover audit on $3290$ genes reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta DL &= -13.68819707902732,
\end{aligned}
$$
with matched-bin $p=0.8855721393034826$ and recoded $p=1.0$.  Its allowed finite reading was that no evidence was found for $B^*_{Q6}$ predicting measured turnover beyond expression, TE, pLDDT, N-end, and related controls.  The mass-balance audit aligns with that stricter interpretation: some execution slots may be observable, but the specific dynamic RPF--protein closure tested here is not promoted.

\paragraph{Matched-row and control discipline.}
The yeast structure-mediation panel gives a useful calibration for how strongly this program depends on row matching.  Its five-way join used $4759$ rows, with a reported join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$.  In that panel the direct residual-to-protein association after controls was $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, while conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability reduced it to $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$; adding the structure proxy produced $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, and the structure-incremental fraction was $0.0005507778820193332$.  These finite values reinforce the same rule used in the present subsection: codon residuals are not allowed to jump directly to protein realization merely because a regression coefficient or residual association is nonzero.  The intervening readout and its controls have to survive on the same finite surface.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance test is a ready negative result.  The data are finite, the row counts and null checks are explicit, and the conclusion is bounded: under this registered finite design, adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ block after $M$ and $E_{stat}$ worsened the dynamic closure score rather than improving it.  The admissible statement is therefore that this claim remains closed at the level of a tested non-promotion boundary for dynamic RPF--protein mass balance.  No translation mechanism, folding route, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, organism-level phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from these rows.  Each of those stronger readings would require its own layer-matched measurement surface and a separate statistic; none is supplied by the negative mass-balance closure audit.
