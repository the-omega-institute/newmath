\subsection{Dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record treats the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates as a finite cross-layer mediation object rather than as a direct biological cause.  The admissible rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$, where $q_i$ is one of the nine synonymous residual coordinates, $t_j$ is a translation-layer readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance or protein-mass-balance readout.  The relevant coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The readout discipline is sequential: a residual coordinate is not allowed to enter the protein layer unless it first has a translation-layer survival entry, with amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, an $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance kept as controls.

\paragraph{Dynamic mass-balance fit.}
The mass-balance experiment used $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample rule, then evaluated $120$ genes across $718$ intervals.  The baseline mass-only regression was already almost saturated, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M\,only} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M+E_{stat}} &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the tested $B^*_{Q6}$-conditioned dynamic layer did not improve the target closure.  The registered comparison gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -668.8046070381928,\\
p_{TEbin} &= 0.6831683168316832,\\
p_{timeshuffle} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row verdict is therefore not a positive protein-realization call.  The observed surface says that, under this dynamic RPF--protein closure design, the residual layer fails to buy description-length economy once the near-complete mass baseline and the declared translation controls are present.  The negative $\Delta R^2$, negative description-length gain, and high effective-null probabilities all point in the same direction: the tested dynamic closure is bounded as a failed improvement, not as a hidden protein-output law.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated abundance.}
A separate cross-organism mediation surface did compute a statistical $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition over twelve organism-level joins.  In that matrix, all organisms had rank-sufficient residualized designs, and the mediation fractions stayed inside the unit interval.  The largest reported mediated fraction was in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect $R^2=0.28320534058057895$ over $5815$ joined proteins.  Other examples included \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 with $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$ over $3739$ joined proteins, \emph{Homo sapiens} with $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$ over $19053$ joined proteins, and \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} with $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$ over $1656$ joined proteins.  Those rows justify keeping the mediation matrix as a legitimate bounded statistical surface, but they do not rescue the dynamic mass-balance experiment above: the mass-balance comparison has its own finite row set, its own null tests, and its own negative improvement.

\paragraph{Execution and turnover context.}
The broader rate-slot audit found an observable execution subblock in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with $3289$ genes and a verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That result permits a local rate-proxy reading, not a direct protein-closure claim.  Conversely, the turnover audit stayed negative after expression, translation-efficiency, predicted-order, and N-end controls: it used $3290$ genes, found $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL_{bits}=-13.68819707902732$, $p_{matchedbin}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{recoded}=1.0$.  The mass-balance and turnover results therefore agree on the conservative reading: available protein-layer rows do not warrant upgrading the residual coordinates into a general protein-realization mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter records a finite negative or bounded outcome, not a biological demotion of the residual coordinates themselves.  A direct $q_i\to p_k$ association is inadmissible unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled $q_i\to t_j$ translation readout, and even then the result remains a statistical mediation statement unless a separate assay supplies the next layer.  No claim of translation mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, selection, or universality follows from the mass-balance table.  The allowed statement is narrower: in the registered dynamic RPF--protein closure run, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual information did not improve the mass-balance target beyond the declared baselines and effective nulls, while other bounded readout surfaces remain separate finite contacts rather than proof of mechanism.
