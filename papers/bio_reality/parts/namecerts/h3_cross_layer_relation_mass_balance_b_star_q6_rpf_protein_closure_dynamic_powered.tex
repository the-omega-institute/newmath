\subsection{Dynamic RPF--protein closure for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-closure-dynamic-powered}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite mediation surface whose rows join a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, a translation-side readout, and a matched protein-abundance readout.  The tested object is not a direct residual-to-protein assertion.  A row is admissible for protein-level interpretation only after the same residual coordinate has passed through a translation readout such as ribosome footprint signal, translation efficiency, modeled tAI, stAI, or another explicitly named translation proxy.  The present powered mass-balance run used $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample, then evaluated $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=120$ genes across $n_{\mathrm{intervals}}=718$ dynamic intervals.  The finite design therefore tests whether adding residual information closes a measured RPF-to-protein accounting surface beyond mass and translation terms, rather than whether a synonymously encoded coordinate directly realizes a protein state.

\paragraph{Layered regression ladder.}
Let $M$ denote the mass-balance baseline, let $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$ denote the static expression or translation-attached term used at the next level, and let $L_2,L_3$ denote the larger residual-augmented stages of the fitted dynamic closure ladder.  The verified finite-row scores were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0=M\ \mathrm{only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1=M+E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The residual-augmented closure did not improve the dynamic accounting target.  The registered contrast gave
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both signs point in the same direction: after the mass and translation-side accounting already present in the lower levels, the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface did not supply a positive dynamic closure increment for the RPF--protein balance endpoint.

\paragraph{Null calibration.}
The finite null checks also failed to support promotion.  The translation-efficiency-bin null returned $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null returned $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  These values are not near a positive discovery boundary.  They say that the observed degradation of the larger fitted surface is compatible with a non-specific or overfit residual insertion under the registered finite-row controls.  The runtime for the evaluated surface was $441.440662$ seconds, so the negative outcome is a completed bounded computation, not an unrun or shape-only record.

\paragraph{Relation to the wider mediated-protein surface.}
The negative dynamic closure result is sharper when placed beside the independent $Q\to T\to P$ mediation surface.  In that surface the residual coordinate set had $9$ coordinates, and all listed organisms had rank-sufficient residualized designs.  The mediated fractions were finite and organism-specific: $0.6084594553474505$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.4872268357227724$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $0.3157053953614641$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.6165167748739756$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $0.612922252793667$ for \emph{Escherichia coli}, $0.1976903964724546$ for chicken, $0.27206289160119157$ for human, $0.15488799907323988$ for mouse, $0.08397602919861291$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $0.17489681798543982$ for rat, $0.9491551041216973$ for yeast, and $0.01850668564589326$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  Those values justify keeping a bounded statistical mediation matrix in the paper, but they do not overturn the dynamic closure verdict.  A positive cross-sectional mediated fraction and a negative dynamic mass-balance increment are different finite surfaces.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The observable-execution run supplies one further boundary condition: in yeast, a rate-proxy decomposition over $3289$ genes reached the verdict that at least one observed rate proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That result permits a bounded rate-slot reading, while the present dynamic mass-balance run says that the stronger RPF--protein closure target did not gain from adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual layer.  The finite conclusion is therefore asymmetric: the residual coordinates may remain useful as translation-attached or rate-slot descriptors, and they may appear in a controlled mediation matrix, but this run does not support a protein-realization closure claim.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
This chapter records a completed negative powered test for the dynamic mass-balance endpoint.  It does not establish translation realization from codon geometry alone, does not infer folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality, and does not treat a direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association as protein realization without a surviving $Q_i\to T_j$ step.  Amino-acid sequence, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance remain controls.  Any future claim above this layer requires its own matched contact and its own finite statistic rather than inheritance from this residual coordinate surface.
