\subsection{Dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}\label{subsec:biorg-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance-closure}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates can enter a dynamic mass-balance relation between ribosome-footprint intervals and matched downstream protein abundance.  The finite surface is not a direct residual-to-protein table.  Its admissible rows pass through a translation readout: a residual coordinate $q_i$ may be compared with protein abundance only after the same row has a translation-facing quantity, here summarized through RPF/translation-efficiency strata and a protein-output closure statistic.  The tested dataset contained $4434$ eligible genes before the deterministic size cap, from which the run used a fixed subsample of $120$ genes and $718$ interval observations.  Thus the experiment is deliberately a finite-row closure audit rather than a population-level biological law.

\paragraph{Model ladder.}
The evaluated ladder separates a mass-only explanation from successively richer readout surfaces.  The four reported coefficients of determination were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0\;\mathrm{M\ only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1\;\mathrm{M+E_{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row comparison therefore does not show an improving closure when the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ dynamic layer is promoted to the last model in the ladder.  The registered change from $L_2$ to $L_3$ was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both signs point in the same direction: the proposed dynamic residual closure loses explanatory fit and description-length efficiency on this finite surface.  The result is not a weak positive; it is a bounded negative for this particular mass-balance promotion.

\paragraph{Null checks.}
Two null comparisons support the same conservative reading.  The translation-efficiency-bin null returned $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null returned $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  These values do not isolate a privileged dynamic ordering for the residual coordinates.  They are also consistent with the separate turnover audit, where adding the residual layer beyond expression, translation efficiency, predicted-order, and N-end style controls decreased the fit from $R^2(L_2)=0.055740851626933985$ to $R^2(L_3)=0.050278872193227464$, with $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=-13.68819707902732$, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  The neighboring finite-row evidence therefore favors a boundary reading: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ may participate in measured translation-facing summaries, but this run does not justify a dynamic protein mass-balance closure.

\paragraph{Relation to positive lower-layer contacts.}
The negative closure result is not a claim that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates are empty.  A separate observable-execution run in yeast used $3289$ genes and reported an observable execution subblock, meaning that at least one rate-proxy slot passed the effective null classes.  Another matched protein-abundance mediation run computed the nine residual coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$ across $12$ organisms, with positive statistical mediation fractions such as $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, and $0.27206289160119157$ in \textit{Homo sapiens}.  Those contacts show that translation-conditioned protein-abundance decompositions can be computed on finite joined surfaces.  They do not override the present dynamic audit, where the mass-balance model comparison itself is negative.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The admissible conclusion is therefore local and finite.  On the $120$-gene, $718$-interval surface, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ dynamic closure layer did not improve the RPF--protein mass-balance ladder; the strongest reported comparison was a loss of $0.07584438093220836$ in $R^2$ and $668.8046070381928$ bits in description-length accounting.  The chapter may record a tested failure of dynamic protein-closure promotion while preserving lower-layer translation and protein-abundance readouts as separate bounded contacts.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility statement, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universality follows from these rows.  A direct $q_i\to P_k$ association is not admissible as protein realization unless a matched $q_i\to T_j$ translation step survives under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched mRNA controls.  Even then, a positive translation or abundance statistic would remain a layer-specific observation; structure, function, phenotype, physical feasibility, and mechanism each require their own layer-matched contact and finite statistic.
