\subsection{Dynamic mass-balance closure for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mass-balance-dynamic-power}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface has a downstream dynamic mass-balance signature once ribosome-profiling, protein abundance, and expression-scale controls are placed on the same finite row system. The carrier is the table of eligible yeast genes, interval-indexed ribosome-footprint summaries, and matched protein-abundance readouts after the amino-acid composition, coding-sequence length, GC3, and $M$-density baseline controls have already been admitted as background coordinates rather than promoted to the tested residual surface. The dynamic row system contains $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample rule, then uses a capped finite panel of $n=120$ genes and $718$ intervals. This makes the test a bounded finite-row audit rather than a population theorem.

\paragraph{Layered regression ladder.}
The fitted ladder separates the mass baseline, the measured expression-rate slot, and the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual increment. With $L_0$ denoting the $M$-only baseline, $L_1$ adding the expression statistic, $L_2$ adding the pre-declared intermediate surface, and $L_3$ adding the tested residual extension, the observed held-row coefficients of determination are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered contrast for the dynamic closure step is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2)=-0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= -668.8046070381928\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The effective-null checks also do not support a positive residual closure call: the translation-efficiency-bin null gives $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gives $p=0.9306930693069307$. The computation ran for $441.440662$ seconds on the registered finite panel. Thus the mass-balance packet is closed as a negative dynamic result: adding the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface after the preceding layers does not improve the mass-balance fit.

\paragraph{Relation to the rate-slot evidence.}
The negative dynamic closure result does not erase lower-layer observable rate evidence. A separate rate-slot decomposition on \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} reported $n=3289$ genes, runtime $2667.02$ seconds, and the finite verdict \texttt{observable\_execution\_subblock}: at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes. That earlier finite result says that a rate-proxy subblock exists. The present mass-balance audit asks the stricter downstream question: after the dynamic mass and expression ladder is already in place, does the same residual family improve interval-level RPF/protein closure? On the registered panel the answer is no.

The broader $Q\to T\to P$ mediation surface also supplies context for why the present negative result is informative rather than vacuous. In the multi-organism protein-abundance decomposition, the nine coordinates were

For yeast, that mediation audit used $5815$ joined proteins, rank $9$ for the residual matrix, rank $1$ for the modeled translation readout, and residual degrees of freedom $5792$ after $23$ control columns. Its scalar decomposition in yeast was
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\

Those numbers show a strong cross-sectional statistical mediation by modeled translation weights in the yeast abundance table, but they are not a dynamic mass-balance proof. The current interval-level audit is allowed to return a negative result even when a cross-sectional abundance mediation scalar is large, because the rows, controls, and readout geometry are not the same.

\paragraph{Structure and turnover side checks.}
Two neighboring finite audits further constrain the interpretation. In the yeast structure-mediation table, the five-way join had $4759$ rows, a reported overall join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$, and a CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$. The residualized protein-abundance partition reported $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$, with a structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$ and a structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507778820193332$. In the turnover audit, $n=3290$ genes gave $R^2(L_2)=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2(L_3)=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$. These side checks agree with the conservative reading: the residual family can be visible in bounded readout surfaces, but it does not automatically propagate into structure-like, turnover-like, or dynamic mass-balance closure.

\paragraph{Coordinate identity.}
The structural-order axis audit identifies $f3\_stress$ as the dominant residual coordinate under its own finite sign-flip procedure. It reported fold count $5$, minimum joined-row threshold $300$, weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ for $f3\_stress$, sign-flip right-tail probability $0.03125$, and the conclusion \texttt{structural\_order\_carried\_by\_f3\_stress}. The mass-balance chapter uses that fact only as coordinate identity information. It does not convert the $f3\_stress$ axis into a dynamic protein-mass mechanism, and it does not use the structural-order result to override the negative $\Delta R^2$ and null-class probabilities in the present interval audit.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a bounded refusal of dynamic protein-closure promotion for this exact claim. The registered mass-balance experiment has enough rows to make the contrast meaningful, and the measured contrast is negative under both the $R^2$ and description-length summaries. The appropriate readback is therefore:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{rate-slot evidence} &\not\Rightarrow \text{dynamic mass-balance closure},\\
\text{cross-sectional } Q\to T\to P \text{ mediation} &\not\Rightarrow \text{interval-level RPF/protein propagation},\\
\Delta R^2<0 \text{ and large null } p\text{-values} &\Rightarrow \text{no positive closure call on this finite panel}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that evidence and survives its own controls. The present chapter only records that the dynamic mass-balance extension of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface fails on the registered finite-row test while remaining compatible with weaker bounded rate-slot and cross-sectional mediation observations.
