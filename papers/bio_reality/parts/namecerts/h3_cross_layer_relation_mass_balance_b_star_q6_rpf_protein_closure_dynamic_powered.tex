\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Translation--Protein Surface}
\label{sec:biorality-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate can pass from a translation readout into a matched protein-abundance state once a mass-balance closure is imposed on finite rows.  The surface is not a direct residual-to-protein assertion.  Its admissible readback is a controlled chain
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow P_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is one of the nine residual coordinates, $T_j$ is a translation readout such as ribosome footprint signal, translation efficiency, modeled tAI, or a comparable bounded translation proxy, and $P_k$ is matched protein abundance.  The tested dynamic closure used $120$ genes selected from $4434$ eligible genes and produced $718$ interval rows.  The finite subsample cap was $120$ genes, so the numerical verdict is a bounded finite-panel result rather than a population law.

\paragraph{Controlled coordinate boundary.}
The admissible coordinates are the residual axes $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline remain controls.  Matched mRNA abundance is also a control whenever translation efficiency or protein abundance is interpreted downstream.  Thus the finite matrix is allowed to ask whether translation information absorbs or carries residual protein variation; it is not allowed to reinterpret codon geometry itself as protein realization.

\paragraph{Mass-balance fit.}
The dynamic powered run reported the following nested fit profile:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M\ only} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M+E_{stat}} &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The decisive increment from $L2$ to $L3$ was negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL &= -668.8046070381928\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null checks also do not support promotion: the translation-efficiency-bin permutation gave $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle check gave $p=0.9306930693069307$.  On this finite panel, the stronger model does not improve held-out dynamic closure after the declared controls.  The correct readback is therefore a negative mass-balance closure result: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ is not promoted from translation-readout or codon-count residual status into a dynamic protein-closure claim by this run.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader mediation surface.}
A separate cross-organism protein-abundance mediation computation did find bounded statistical mediation fractions for a modeled tAI route.  For example, the yeast row used $5815$ joined proteins and reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{yeast} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{indirect} &= 0.28320534058057895.
\end{aligned}
$$
Across that same mediation computation, the organism-matched rows ranged from $1656$ proteins in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $19395$ proteins in mouse, and all reported rank sufficiency for the joint nine-dimensional residual block after the stated controls.  Those values show that a controlled $q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ statistic can be computed on finite matched protein rows.  They do not override the dynamic mass-balance result above.  The mass-balance panel asks a stricter time-structured closure question, and its measured increment is negative.

\paragraph{Turnover comparison.}
A proteostasis turnover audit provides a useful boundary check.  On $3290$ yeast genes, that audit reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta DL &= -13.68819707902732\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its matched-bin and recoded null checks gave $p=0.8855721393034826$ and $p=1.0$.  This independent negative downstream audit is consistent with the mass-balance verdict: current finite rows do not justify turning $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals into a protein-state, turnover, or dynamic closure mechanism beyond the explicitly measured translation and abundance surfaces.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row conclusion is bounded and negative.  The dynamic mass-balance run completed, but the controlled increment is nonpositive, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals.  Therefore the chapter records a closed negative test for this finite panel: no $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate is promoted here to dynamic protein closure.  A future positive claim would require a same-row translation step that survives the controls, followed by a downstream protein-abundance or protein-state statistic that remains positive after matched mRNA abundance, amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density baseline, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablation.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization by itself, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  Modeled tAI, ribosome-footprint, matched proteomics, and matched mRNA contacts each test only their own layer-specific surface.  A direct $q_i \to P_k$ association is not admissible as protein realization unless the same residual coordinate first survives a controlled $q_i \to T_j$ translation-readout step, and even a surviving translation step would still require separate layer-matched data before any folding, function, physical-admissibility, mechanistic, or universal claim could be made.
