\subsection{Dynamic RPF--Protein Mass-Balance Closure for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} The mass-balance packet tests whether $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates add a stable dynamic closure term after a ribosome-footprint and protein-abundance row has already been compressed by a strong mass baseline. The finite table is deliberately small and fixed: $4434$ genes were eligible before subsampling, the registered cap was $120$ genes, and the evaluated panel contains $n=120$ genes distributed over $718$ dynamic intervals. The design is therefore not a genome-wide law claim; it is a bounded row-level audit of whether the residual coordinates improve a dynamic RPF--protein closure once the dominant mass term and the registered dynamic summaries are already present.

\paragraph{Nested fits.} The observed fits are ordered as a sequence of increasingly specific surfaces. The mass-only level already explains nearly all available variance, and the next registered dynamic level gives only a small increase:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M+E_{\mathrm{stat}}} &= 0.996410628851051.
\end{aligned}
$$
The later levels do not behave as a positive closure path for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264,\\
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the registered higher-level residual addition reduces held-out explanatory performance rather than closing a protein-output layer. In the language of the finite matrix, the observed row is a negative dynamic closure result: the dominant mass coordinate is sufficient for this panel, and the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual terms do not earn promotion to a dynamic RPF--protein closure coordinate here.

\paragraph{Null comparison.} The two effective null checks agree with the negative interpretation. The TE-bin null gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{TEbin}} &= 0.6831683168316832,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the time-shuffle null gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{time\ shuffle}} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are far from a positive finite-row gate. They say that the observed dynamic residual does not separate itself from the registered exchangeable alternatives. Since the run completed in $441.440662$ seconds and returned a bounded verdict rather than a missing-data error, the correct BioReality status is not an untested conjecture but an evaluated refusal of the stronger dynamic closure claim on this contact surface.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein mediation.} This result should be read beside, but not merged with, the broader translation-mediated protein-abundance matrix. In the separate $Q\to T\to P$ mediation audit, $12$ organism-scoped protein panels were evaluated with a $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis, and all listed organism rows were rank-sufficient for that statistical decomposition. For example, the yeast row in that audit reported $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, total $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect $R^2=0.28320534058057895$. Those finite mediation numbers show that a modeled translation readout can absorb part of a protein-abundance association in some organism-scoped cross-sectional rows. The present dynamic mass-balance result is stricter and different: after a mass-heavy dynamic baseline, the residual coordinates do not add a positive RPF--protein closure term in the fixed $120$-gene, $718$-interval panel.

\paragraph{Execution boundary.} A related observable-execution audit in yeast reported $3289$ genes and concluded that at least one observed rate proxy slot passed its effective null classes. That supports the existence of a bounded observed rate surface, not the stronger claim that every downstream protein or dynamic mass-balance layer closes. The present chapter therefore separates three statements. First, observed or modeled translation readouts may define a tested translation-layer surface. Second, cross-sectional protein abundance may show statistical mediation in a matched $Q\to T\to P$ table. Third, the dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure tested here is negative, because $R^2$ drops from $0.9712443377743347$ to $0.8953999568421264$ at the registered residual level and both null $p$-values are large.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.} No direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is admissible here unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled translation-readout step. A mass-balance regression, even one containing ribosome-footprint and protein-abundance rows, does not by itself establish translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, selection, or universality. Those claims require their own layer-matched measurements and their own finite statistics. On this finite surface, the allowed statement is narrower: the registered dynamic RPF--protein closure attempt ran to completion, used $120$ genes and $718$ intervals, and returned a negative residual improvement after the dominant mass and dynamic baselines were already accounted for.
