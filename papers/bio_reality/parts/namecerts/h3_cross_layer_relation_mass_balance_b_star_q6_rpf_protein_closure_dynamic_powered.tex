\subsection{Dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-rpf-protein-mass-balance-closure}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates improve a finite dynamic mass-balance surface after the surface has already been given a strong measured-abundance baseline. The observed rows are not read as isolated codon counts. They are used only after joining genes to ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency information, matched protein abundance, and the ordinary sequence controls that keep amino-acid identity, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline outside the promoted coordinate set. The finite run used $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsampling rule and then evaluated $120$ genes across $718$ time or interval rows. The fixed subsample cap was therefore active at $120$ genes, so the conclusion is a bounded finite-row statement, not a population law.

\paragraph{Layered models.}
The audit compares a nested sequence of predictive surfaces. The $L_0$ surface contains the measured-abundance or mass baseline alone. The $L_1$ surface adds the estimated dynamic term. The $L_2$ surface adds the controlled translation/protein readout layer before the final $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block is tested. The $L_3$ surface is the candidate surface after adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ block to the already controlled dynamic closure. The realized scores were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0;M\text{ only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1;M,E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tested residual block did not close the dynamic mass-balance surface. Its finite-row increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2)\\
&= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both signs point in the same direction: after the stronger baseline is present, adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ block worsens the held-out dynamic fit on this panel rather than improving it.

\paragraph{Null checks.}
The finite null comparisons are also unfavorable to promotion. The translation-efficiency-bin null gave $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$. These values do not support a positive residual contribution of the $B^*_{Q6}$ block to dynamic RPF--protein closure. They also do not say that the residual coordinates are biologically meaningless. They say only that this particular mass-balance surface, with this finite row set and this control sequence, does not admit a positive dynamic closure claim.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein readouts.}
Other finite panels keep the same separation discipline. A rate-slot execution audit reported $3289$ yeast genes and concluded that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed the effective null classes. A broader protein-abundance mediation panel used $12$ organisms and computed positive statistical mediated fractions in the modeled-tAI route, including $0.9491551041216973$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, and $0.27206289160119157$ for human. Those records justify keeping translation and protein-abundance readouts as legitimate bounded surfaces, but they do not rescue the dynamic mass-balance test reported here. A separate turnover audit likewise stayed negative after expression, TE, pLDDT, N-end, and related controls, with $R^2(L_2)=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2(L_3)=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, and $\Delta DL_{\mathrm{bits}}=-13.68819707902732$ on $3290$ genes. The mass-balance result is therefore read as part of a controlled boundary: some rate or mediation surfaces can be measurable, while this dynamic closure surface remains unpromoted.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is negative and narrow. On the verified finite rows, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates do not improve dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure after the stated controls and baselines are present. No direct residual-to-protein association is accepted unless the residual first survives a controlled translation-readout step. No translation mechanism, folding pathway, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this run. Any such assertion would require its own matched contact and its own finite statistic at the appropriate layer.
