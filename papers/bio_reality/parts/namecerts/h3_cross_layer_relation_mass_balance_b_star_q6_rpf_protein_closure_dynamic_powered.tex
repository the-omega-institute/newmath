\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for the $B^*_{Q6}$ RPF--Protein Surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-dynamic-mass-balance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The dynamic closure test asks whether the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface contributes a stable improvement once the observable mass-balance route from message abundance through ribosome-footprint pressure to protein abundance has already been represented.  The finite rows are coding-sequence matched rows with residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, together with matched translation and protein readouts where available.  The dynamic mass-balance run used $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample cap, then evaluated $120$ genes across $718$ time intervals.  The cap is part of the finite experimental surface, not a biological population assertion.

\paragraph{Nested readout ladder.}
The fitted ladder separates a mass-only baseline, an expression-state extension, an intermediate residualized surface, and the full $B^*_{Q6}$ dynamic surface.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M} &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1,M,E_{stat}} &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The decisive comparison is the transition from $L2$ to $L3$, where the registered increment is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the dynamic residual addition does not improve the finite-row mass-balance description.  It worsens the held surface relative to the preceding layer, so this chapter records a bounded non-promotion result: the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates do not enter dynamic RPF--protein closure on this panel.

\paragraph{Null calibration.}
Two registered null checks agree with the non-promotion reading.  The translation-efficiency-bin comparison gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{TEbin} &= 0.6831683168316832,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the time-shuffle comparison gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{timeshuffle} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both values are far from a positive finite-row discovery threshold.  The result is therefore not a weak positive that fails only by effect-size convention; it is a directionally negative dynamic fit with null checks that do not support a special residual contribution.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader protein-abundance surface.}
The same family of coordinates can still have bounded cross-sectional contacts in other panels.  In the multi-organism protein-abundance mediation surface, the per-protein modeled-tAI decomposition used twelve organisms and found positive statistical mediation fractions such as $0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $0.15488799907323988$ in \textit{Mus musculus}.  Those are cross-sectional $Q \to T \to P$ decompositions using modeled adaptation weights and protein abundance; they are not dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure.  A separate observable-execution run on \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} reported $3289$ genes and the verdict that at least one rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  That establishes a bounded execution subblock, not the stronger dynamic closure tested here.

\paragraph{Turnover comparison.}
A related turnover audit gives the same cautionary shape at the post-translational boundary.  With $3290$ genes and $200$ null draws, the turnover panel recorded
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -13.68819707902732,\\
p_{matchedbin} &= 0.8855721393034826,\\
p_{recoded} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
That independent negative result does not prove that the residual coordinates are biologically irrelevant.  It shows that, after expression, translation-efficiency, structural-confidence, and terminal-rule controls in that panel, the residual surface should not be promoted to a measured turnover predictor.  The dynamic mass-balance result in this chapter has the same logical form: a finite contact was evaluated and the stronger closure claim failed.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The admissible statement is narrow.  On the registered $120$-gene, $718$-interval dynamic RPF--protein surface, adding the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block after the preceding mass-balance layers decreases the fitted explanatory score and is not rescued by either null calibration.  The chapter therefore closes this subject as a current-data negative for dynamic mass-balance protein closure.  It does not deny all translation-layer or protein-abundance contacts for $B^*_{Q6}$; it says that this particular dynamic closure gate is not passed.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
No claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality follows from this record.  A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not enough for protein realization unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled $Q_i \to T_j$ translation-readout step, and a dynamic mass-balance model is not a folding assay, a functional assay, or a causal perturbation.  Any stronger layer would need its own matched contact and its own finite statistic under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, and simpler-baseline controls.
