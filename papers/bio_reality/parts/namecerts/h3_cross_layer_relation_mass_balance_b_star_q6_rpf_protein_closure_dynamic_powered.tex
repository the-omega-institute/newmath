\subsection{Dynamic Mass-Balance Closure for $B^*_{Q6}$, Ribosome Footprints, and Protein Abundance}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rpf-protein-dynamic-mass-balance}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface can enter a dynamic mass-balance readout after the translation layer has been separated from transcript abundance and protein abundance. The finite carrier is a table of gene--interval rows with a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate block, an mRNA or expression mass term $M$, a ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency statistic, and a matched protein-abundance endpoint. The admissible readback is therefore not a direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association. It is a controlled comparison of nested finite designs in which $M$ is first allowed to explain the abundance trajectory, then a translation statistic is attached, and only then may the residual coordinate block be tested for remaining dynamic closure.

\paragraph{Observed finite row counts.}
The registered dynamic run used $4434$ eligible genes before fixed subsampling, with a declared cap of $120$ genes. The evaluated table therefore contains $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=120$ genes and $n_{\mathrm{intervals}}=718$ gene--time or gene--condition intervals. The runtime was $441.440662$ seconds. These numbers matter because the claim is not a population law: it is a finite-surface audit over a fixed subsample with explicit interval count and null comparisons.

\paragraph{Nested mass-balance fits.}
The measured nested coefficients of determination were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_0;M\ \mathrm{only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2(L_1;M,E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The residual block did not improve the relevant dynamic closure surface. The registered increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row verdict is negative for the strong mass-balance closure claim. The $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates are not promoted from translation-bounded or codon-count residual status into a dynamic protein-realization model on this table. The result is stronger than a mere absence of a small positive gain: the signed change is substantially negative in both the explained-variance and description-length summaries.

\paragraph{Null calibration.}
Two null comparisons were recorded. The translation-efficiency-bin null gave $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gave $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$. These values place the observed dynamic increment well inside the null region rather than near a positive tail. The audit therefore supports a bounded statement: in this finite experiment, adding the residual block after the mass and translation summaries does not produce a reliable dynamic protein-abundance closure.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader mediation surface.}
A separate cross-organism mediation run had already computed a $9$-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ block with coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{\mathrm{stress}}$. That run joined matched CDS and protein-abundance rows across $12$ organisms, including $5815$ joined proteins for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $19053$ for human, and $19395$ for mouse. It reported positive statistical mediation fractions for a modeled-tAI readout, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse. Those mediated fractions are useful as bounded cross-sectional summaries, but they do not force a dynamic mass-balance result. The present run asks a different question: whether the residual block closes remaining dynamic variation after the mass and translation slots are already present. On the measured finite surface, it does not.

\paragraph{Observable execution context.}
Another registered execution-decomposition audit on yeast used $3289$ genes and concluded that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed the effective null classes. That result licenses the local use of observed rate-proxy language as a finite execution subblock, not a mechanism claim. The dynamic mass-balance audit is stricter: it asks whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ block carries additional explanatory power after mass and translation summaries are included. The negative $\Delta R^2$ and negative description-length increment keep the present chapter at a no-promotion boundary.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
This chapter does not claim translation realization from codon geometry, ribosome dynamics from modeled adaptation weights, protein folding from abundance, physical admissibility from a residual score, molecular function from a mass-balance fit, a causal mechanism from mediation notation, or a universal biological rule from one finite table. Such claims would require their own layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics. The only supported conclusion here is the finite negative one: under the registered $120$-gene and $718$-interval dynamic design, the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block fails to improve the mass-balance closure surface after the declared controls and translation summaries are already present.
