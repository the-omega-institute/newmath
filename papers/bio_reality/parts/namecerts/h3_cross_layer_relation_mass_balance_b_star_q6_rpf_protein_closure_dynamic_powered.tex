\subsection{Dynamic mass-balance refusal for the B$^*_Q6$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-dynamic-mass-balance-refusal}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-layer audit for the residual coordinates $q_i$ in the B$^*_Q6$ basis when they are asked to enter a dynamic mass-balance relation between ribosome-footprint-like execution, protein abundance, and coarse temporal closure. The rows are not an unconstrained biological universe: they are a fixed finite panel with $n=120$ genes selected from $4434$ eligible genes before the fixed subsample cap, and the time-indexed design contains $718$ intervals. The admissible readback is therefore a controlled finite matrix of residual coordinates, translation-side readouts, and protein-side abundance or closure quantities, not a statement about arbitrary organisms, arbitrary proteins, or a universal codon law.

\paragraph{Model ladder.}
The mass-balance audit compares four nested readout levels. The first level uses only the mass proxy $M$ and gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0,M} &= 0.9960628201518571.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the estimated execution statistic gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L1,M,E} &= 0.996410628851051.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two downstream fits then fall rather than improve:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered finite verdict is thus not a positive dynamic closure. The final increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= -668.8046070381928\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both signs point away from promoting the B$^*_Q6$ residual surface into a dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance closure. The test completed in $441.440662$ seconds, so the refusal is not a missing-run status; it is the finite-row outcome of the registered computation.

\paragraph{Null tests.}
Two effective null classes were attached to the same finite surface. The translation-efficiency-bin null reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{TEbin}} &= 0.6831683168316832,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the temporal shuffle null reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values are too large to support a positive dynamic closure. They also prevent the negative $\Delta R^2$ from being reinterpreted as a hidden timing law: under the recorded finite controls, the proposed dynamic powered closure does not separate itself from the declared null surfaces.

\paragraph{Relation to the controlled translation--protein mediation surface.}
The broader B$^*_Q6$ translation-mediated protein-abundance experiment is positive only in the narrower statistical sense that a modeled per-protein tAI surface can decompose B$^*_Q6$ residual association with measured PAXdb protein abundance across a multi-organism finite panel. That experiment used $9$ residual coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. For example, the mediated fraction was $0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse, with all recorded fractions lying in the unit interval. The yeast row had $5815$ joined proteins; human had $19053$; mouse had $19395$; and the smallest listed organism, \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, had $1656$.

These numbers justify only a controlled mediation decomposition with modeled tAI as the translation-side readout. They do not override the mass-balance result above. A finite organism-level mediation fraction can coexist with failure of a dynamic closure test, because the latter asks whether the residual surface improves a stronger interval-indexed RPF--protein balance after the declared controls and nulls. On the present rows, it does not.

\paragraph{Turnover and execution context.}
Two neighboring audits sharpen the interpretation. The observable execution decomposition ran on $3289$ yeast genes and ended with the finite verdict that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes. That supports an execution subblock, not the stronger mass-balance closure tested here. Separately, the turnover residual audit used $3290$ genes and obtained
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2,\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2_{L3,\mathrm{turnover}} &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{turnover}} &= -13.68819707902732\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its matched-bin and recoded null values were $p=0.8855721393034826$ and $p=1.0$, respectively. The turnover audit therefore agrees with the dynamic mass-balance result in refusing promotion beyond bounded expression, translation-readout, or abundance-residual status.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter allows the following statement and no stronger one: in the registered finite dynamic mass-balance audit, the B$^*_Q6$ residual coordinates fail to improve the interval-indexed RPF--protein closure surface after the declared controls, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ and high null probabilities. A direct residual-to-protein association is not admissible here unless the same residual first survives an explicit translation-readout step and the downstream protein readout remains controlled by matched mRNA abundance, amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the M-density baseline. This chapter by itself establishes no translation realization, no folding result, no physical admissibility result, no molecular function, no mechanism, no organismal phenotype, and no universal biological law. Each of those claims would need its own layer-matched measurement surface and its own finite statistic.
