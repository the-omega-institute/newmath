\subsection{Mass-balance closure for B$^*_{Q6}$ residuals across RPF and protein abundance}
\label{sec:bioreality-mass-balance-bstar-q6-rpf-protein-closure}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record is a finite cross-layer audit over $120$ genes selected from $4434$ eligible joined coding-sequence rows, with $718$ gene-interval observations after the fixed subsample cap.  The measured surface has three roles: a mass coordinate $M$, a translation-associated estimate $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$, and a downstream protein-abundance response.  The question is not whether B$^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates exist as codon-composition contrasts; they do.  The question is whether adding the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual block after the mass and translation slots improves the held finite-row accounting of protein abundance.  The carrier is therefore the controlled table of tuples
$$
\begin{aligned}
(g, I_g, M_g, E_{\mathrm{stat},g}, Q_g, P_g),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g$ ranges over the retained genes, $I_g$ records the interval membership, $Q_g$ is the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual vector, and $P_g$ is the matched protein-abundance readout.  Amino-acid composition, sequence length, GC3, organism identity, M-density, and matched transcriptional information remain control directions; they are not upgraded into B$^*_{Q6}$ coordinates.

\paragraph{Observed nested fits.}
The finite-row computation reports the following nested explanatory values:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L0}(M\ \mathrm{only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
R^2_{L1}(M,E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
R^2_{L2} &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.8953999568421264.
\end{aligned}
$$
The signed increment attached to the final B$^*_{Q6}$-conditioned layer is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
The effective-null checks agree with this refusal of promotion.  The TE-bin null gives $p=0.6831683168316832$, and the time-shuffle null gives $p=0.9306930693069307$.  Thus the extra residual block does not beat the finite mass-balance and translation-conditioned accounting; it worsens the registered fit on this surface.

\paragraph{Readback of the negative increment.}
The correct readback is a bounded closure statement, not a discovery of a protein-realization mechanism.  On this mass-balance surface, the large baseline value $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628201518571$ shows that the mass coordinate already captures nearly all of the row-level abundance variance available to this design.  Adding $E_{\mathrm{stat}}$ changes the fit only slightly, from $0.9960628201518571$ to $0.996410628851051$.  Once the downstream layers are evaluated under the declared finite-row design, the B$^*_{Q6}$ contribution is not a positive residual explanation: the recorded movement to $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$ and the negative description-length increment give an explicit finite reason to keep the coordinate at bounded residual status for this assay.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated protein-abundance packet.}
A broader matched protein-abundance audit over organism-scoped rows reported positive statistical mediation fractions for modeled tAI readouts in several organisms, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.612922252793667$ in Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, and $0.27206289160119157$ in Homo sapiens.  Those numbers are useful as bounded statistical decompositions of $Q\to T\to P$ surfaces, but they do not override the present mass-balance closure.  The present finite run asks a sharper local question: after mass and the registered translation-associated slot are already in the accounting, does the B$^*_{Q6}$ block add a positive protein-abundance gain?  The answer on the recorded rows is no.

\paragraph{Translation-contact limitation.}
The modeled-tAI survival packet computed $9$ B$^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, namely K\_AAA, Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and $f_3$-stress.  It also found $81$ modeled-tAI coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ across $3$ organisms, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  This is a bounded modeled-readout contact, not a ribosome-footprint or protein-output measurement.  The same distinction applies here: a residual coordinate may enter protein-abundance fitting only through a controlled translation readout, and a direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not treated as protein realization when the $Q_i\to T_j$ step is absent, null, or merely modeled without the required measured contact.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite result closes this chapter negatively for the proposed dynamic mass-balance protein-closure claim.  With $n=120$ genes, $718$ intervals, $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$, B$^*_{Q6}$ is not promoted from residual or translation-readout status to a protein-abundance closure on this assay.  The allowed statement is that the mass-balance design ran to a bounded negative verdict: the residual block does not improve the registered downstream protein-abundance accounting beyond the mass and translation-associated slots.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or a universal biological rule.  Such claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled finite-row statistics.  Protein abundance here is a steady-state downstream readout under a restricted design; it is not a folding assay, not a functional assay, not a perturbation rescue, and not a proof that a synonymous residual coordinate is biologically causal.
