\subsection{Endogenous ARE Transfer to RNA Half-Life With a Translation Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-utr3-are-endogenous-halflife-transfer-rnadecaycafe}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel in which coding-RNA windows are indexed by synonymous coordinate labels and then joined, row by row, to RNA half-life readouts, transcript-abundance controls, isoform state, local sequence covariates, and an explicit translation-readout boundary. The biological object is not a free sequence principle. It is a table of rows whose fields include a coordinate label, a half-life measurement, transcription or abundance controls, local RNA-structure and composition covariates, motif density, codon-pair context, batch, and the declared perturbation or rescue state when such rows exist. The finite test currently available for this chapter is an endogenous 3UTR ARE transfer statistic over $14933$ genes. In that run, the baseline model had $R^2 = 0.0657771$, the model with the ARE term had $R^2 = 0.0827561$, and the finite gain was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.0827561 - 0.0657771 \\
           &= 0.016979 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The fitted ARE coefficient was negative, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, and the permutation readout used $2000$ permutations with $p=0.00049975$. Thus the observed finite relation is that the endogenous ARE feature carries additional negative half-life information beyond the baseline covariates in this joined data surface.

\paragraph{What the statistic licenses.}
The row-level conclusion is deliberately narrow. The run identified by \texttt{e96bf26e626b} supports a bounded RNA-stability relation for endogenous 3UTR ARE load in the tested half-life panel. It does not by itself identify a decay-factor RBP, a binding site occupancy mechanism, or a perturbation-rescue chain. The scalar evidence is nevertheless nontrivial: the sign is concordant with destabilizing ARE behavior, the test set gain is positive, and the permutation probability is below $10^{-3}$. A related 3UTR ARE deletion boundary, run \texttt{a41440ab2792}, used $1000$ actual permutations and $2000$ grading permutations, with $226$ BEAS2B deletion rows and $2242$ Jurkat deletion rows, and reported sign concordance under a corrected pooled label-permutation procedure. These two ARE-facing facts are consistent at the level of RNA stability, but they remain distinct finite surfaces: one is endogenous transfer over gene rows, and the other is deletion behavior over reporter or deletion rows.

\paragraph{Boundary equation.}
For this chapter the finite readout can be summarized as a controlled half-life comparison rather than as a mechanism claim. Let $Y_i$ denote the RNA half-life response for row $i$, let $A_i$ denote the ARE feature, and let $Z_i$ collect the baseline controls available in the row family: transcript abundance or transcription rate when present, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, batch, and matched translation boundary fields. The recorded test has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
Y_i &= \alpha + Z_i\gamma + A_i\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} + \varepsilon_i,\\
\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &< 0,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.016979,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.00049975 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough to record ARE-associated RNA half-life transfer in the finite panel. It is not enough to assert that the effect is mediated by a prespecified decay-factor RBP. That stronger statement would require same-window measured RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls on the same finite row system.

\paragraph{Comparison with another RNA half-life boundary.}
The m6A half-life boundary gives a useful calibration for the kind of claim being made here. In run \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, $5837$ genes passed the coverage rule, the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman statistic for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman statistic was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$. That record was explicitly a boundary statistic for an RNA modification and half-life relation, not a general mechanism or function theorem. The ARE record is treated the same way: finite rows can support a controlled RNA-stability readout while still refusing promotion to translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Failure conditions.}
The chapter refuses the stronger cross-layer bridge if high- and low-coordinate labels become exchangeable after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch. It also refuses the bridge if RNA half-life differs while measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent or unchanged, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue does not return it, or if binding-defective rescue and non-target controls reproduce the same pattern. A same-row translation readout is required only as a boundary against misreading RNA stability as translation output; its presence is not evidence of translation realization unless an independent translation contact supplies that evidence. Consequently, the current finite result is a chapter-specific RNA half-life relation with ARE directionality and permutation support, while the RBP mechanism bridge remains conditional on additional matched occupancy and perturbation rows.
