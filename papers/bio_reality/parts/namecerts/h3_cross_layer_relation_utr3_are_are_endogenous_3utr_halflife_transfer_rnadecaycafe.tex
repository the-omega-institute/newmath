\subsection{Endogenous ARE Load as a Bounded RNA-Half-Life Transfer Record}\label{sec:bioreality-endogenous-are-halflife-transfer}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.} The record concerns a finite, organism-scoped transcript panel in which an AU-rich element coordinate is read against an RNA half-life response. The relevant carrier is not a free sequence law: each row is a joined transcript or gene entry with an ARE-derived coordinate, a half-life readout, baseline covariates, and a declared regression comparison. The endogenous transfer run kept $n=14933$ genes and evaluated whether an ARE term improved prediction of RNA half-life beyond the baseline surface. The finite statistic was not a symbolic promotion from codon geometry, and it was not a direct RBP perturbation experiment. It was a bounded RNA-stability contact: an ARE coordinate was allowed to meet a measured half-life response only through the finite rows that survived identifier joining, covariate construction, and held-out scoring.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.} The run identified a negative ARE coefficient and a nonzero held-out gain. In the recorded fit,
$$\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 14933,\\
 R^2_{\mathrm{baseline}} &= 0.0657771,\\
 R^2_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= 0.0827561,\\
 \Delta R^2 &= 0.016979,\\
 \beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= -0.0607953,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.00049975,\\
 N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 2000.
\end{aligned}$$
The sign is interpreted only at the level of the encoded response: in these rows, larger ARE load is associated with shorter RNA half-life after the baseline comparison represented by the run. The improvement from $0.0657771$ to $0.0827561$ is small in absolute explained variance, but it is finite, reproducible under the stated permutation count, and sufficient for a bounded endogenous RNA-half-life transfer statement. The runtime, $256.449$ seconds, is recorded as execution provenance rather than biological evidence.

\paragraph{Relation to the paired ARE deletion boundary.} The endogenous transfer statistic is consistent with a separate ARE deletion boundary record rather than replacing it. That related deletion run reported $n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}}=226$ and $n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}=2242$ deletion rows, used $1000$ actual permutations with $2000$ grading permutations, and recorded sign concordance. The two records therefore touch the same stability theme in different finite geometries: the deletion record asks whether removing ARE-bearing sequence regions shifts stability in a perturbational reporter setting, while the endogenous transfer record asks whether ARE load carries predictive information for curated half-life among joined endogenous genes. Their agreement licenses a local RNA-stability boundary, not a universal ARE law and not a full decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Comparison contacts.} The same paper-level discipline is visible in other RNA half-life contacts. A methylation-site half-life run kept $5837$ genes and reported a static Spearman value $-0.388929$ for m6A load against DMSO log-half-life, a causal Spearman value $0.245804$ for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life, a length-partial Spearman value $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$. A transcript-surveillance boundary kept $38576$ rows with $1000$ permutations. These comparison records show that the ARE transfer result is one member of a broader finite-row RNA-stability practice: each statistic is admitted only at the layer actually measured by its rows and controls.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.} The mandatory translation readout remains a boundary condition, not a conclusion. The present endogenous ARE run does not contain same-row ribosome profiling, translation efficiency, or mature protein abundance. Therefore the half-life association cannot be read as translation realization, protein output, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a global biological principle. A future translation-conditioned claim would need a same-row panel containing the ARE coordinate, RNA half-life, matched mRNA abundance, ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, transcript abundance, isoform state, batch, local structure, composition, codon-pair context, and the declared break condition.

\paragraph{RBP-mechanism boundary.} The strongest RBP bridge is not licensed by this finite statistic. The recorded run depends on the original synthetic ARE reporter claim, but it does not itself carry measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target controls. Consequently the chapter may state an endogenous ARE-to-half-life transfer association with a negative fitted coefficient and a permutation-supported held-out gain. It may not state that a prespecified decay-factor RBP mediates the effect. Such a bridge would require finite rows in which high- and low-coordinate labels can be separated from local RBP occupancy, the effect collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, fails under binding-defective or non-target control, and remains separated from transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the translation readout.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.} The accepted claim is therefore narrow: in $14933$ endogenous gene rows, ARE load contributes a negative RNA-half-life term with $\Delta R^2=0.016979$ and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$ under $2000$ permutations. This is enough to keep an ARE-linked RNA-stability contact in the BioReality record. It is not enough to infer translation, decay-factor RBP mechanism, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, universality, or any sequence-wide rule beyond the finite matched rows and the stated statistic.
