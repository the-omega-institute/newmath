\subsection{Joint TE--mRNA-Stability Mediation of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Abundance Residual}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel and coordinate surface.}
This record tests whether the observed $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ association with protein abundance is partly accounted for by two measured middle-layer readouts: ribosome-derived translation efficiency and mRNA stability. The finite surface has nine synonymous residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. Each coordinate is evaluated after residualizing against the same control block: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates. The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each computed organism, while the residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$. The rank diagnostic is therefore nondegenerate for the three four-way joins used here.

\paragraph{Rows and readouts.}
The computation uses three organism-matched joins containing CDS codon counts, measured protein abundance, ribosome-derived translation efficiency $T$, and mRNA-stability readout $M$. The yeast join has $4759$ rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$. The human join has $11322$ rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and TE join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$. The E. coli join has $2517$ rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and TE join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$. Yeast TE is computed from Ingolia et al. rich-medium footprint and mRNA normalized read-density files; E. coli TE is computed from Li, Burkhardt, Gross, and Weissman random-fragment-control footprint and mRNA wiggle coverage; human TE uses the RPFdb processed GSE56887 CDS translation-efficiency table. The mRNA-stability side uses the corresponding organism-specific half-life or consensus-stability source named in the finite rows, so the fitted middle layer is explicitly readout-bound and not inferred from codon geometry alone.

\paragraph{Variance decomposition.}
Let $P_e$ denote protein abundance after residualization by the control block, let $Q_e$ denote the nine-dimensional residual coordinate design, and let $T_e$ and $M_e$ denote residualized measured TE and mRNA stability. The fitted quantities are the direct $Q$--protein statistic and the remaining incremental $Q$ statistic after conditioning on one or both middle-layer readouts:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= R^2(P_e \sim Q_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= \Delta R^2(P_e \sim Q_e\mid T_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= \Delta R^2(P_e \sim Q_e\mid M_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= \Delta R^2(P_e \sim Q_e\mid T_e,M_e),\\
\mu_{T,M} &= \frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For yeast the values are $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, giving joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$. For human the corresponding values are $0.04274178488511215$, $0.04250692268197657$, $0.036196017359154814$, and $0.0355223930537993$, giving joint fraction $0.16890712099923358$. For E. coli they are $0.1181743523064932$, $0.11583979275713331$, $0.09093273405121653$, and $0.08975732521542036$, giving joint fraction $0.2404669586626661$.

\paragraph{Single-readout split.}
The joint fraction separates differently across organisms. In yeast, measured TE accounts for the larger share: the TE-only fraction is $0.4697188319693239$, while the mRNA-stability-only fraction is $0.0626618595344251$. In human, the split is reversed: the TE-only fraction is $0.00549490864190343$, while stability-only is $0.15314679870183337$. E. coli is also stability-dominant, with TE-only fraction $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only fraction $0.23052056324898387$. In all three cases the joint fraction is close to the sum of the two single-readout fractions under the declared descriptive tolerance $0.05$: yeast has joint-minus-single-sum $-0.012862468941589822$, human has $0.010265413655496769$, and E. coli has $-0.009808817995950458$. The right-tail paired-permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$ in each organism, using deterministic row-label permutations of the paired middle-layer readouts.

\paragraph{Energy and rank checks.}
The residual degrees of freedom after the control block are $4736$ in yeast, $11299$ in human, and $2494$ in E. coli. The protein residual energies are respectively $1724.6664873071313$, $12615.307912483846$, and $2323.813305213946$. The TE residual energies are $645.3764418081507$, $1866.9940287020145$, and $672.36799431777$; the mRNA-stability residual energies are $194.99141167236996$, $647.920297431553$, and $198.6753916649337$. The combined $T,M$ block has rank $2$, and the augmented $Q,T,M$ block has rank $11$ in each organism. Thus the attenuation of the direct $Q$--protein association is not produced by an all-saturated fit or by a missing residual rank in the tested designs.

\paragraph{Relation to direct and measured-TE contacts.}
The same coordinate family has direct protein-abundance survival in broader measured-proteomics panels: for example, $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in E. coli, and $0.04454565118019448$ in human under the direct PAXdb abundance test. It also has measured-TE survival in the ribosome-derived TE test: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.009394266754822262$ in E. coli, and $0.018837911344127926$ in human. A separate measured-TE mediation run reports measured mediated fractions $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, and $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, with the danio fraction clipped to $0$ from a raw suppressive value. The present chapter therefore refines the protein-abundance association by adding mRNA stability as a second measured middle-layer readout rather than treating the direct $Q\to P$ association as self-explanatory.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
The finite readback is organism-specific. Yeast is a TE-dominant partial-mediation case: the $Q$--protein association is strong, the measured TE step is nonzero, and conditioning on TE plus stability removes about $51.95\%$ of the direct association. Human and E. coli are stability-dominant partial-mediation cases: the measured TE step is positive but small, while mRNA stability accounts for most of the observed attenuation. None of the three organisms is a null direct-protein case, and none is a complete absorption case, because $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}$ remains positive in each organism. The appropriate finite label is therefore partial, readout-conditioned attenuation of a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual, with organism-specific allocation between TE and mRNA stability.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This computation is observational and cross-sectional. It is a variance partition on finite joined rows, not a causal pathway proof. The abundance, TE, and mRNA-stability measurements are drawn from organism-specific studies whose conditions and platforms are not identical, and the comparison is descriptive rather than phylogenetically controlled. A positive $Q\to P$ association does not by itself establish translation mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality. Even the attenuation by $T$ and $M$ remains a statistical readout relation unless a separate perturbational or layer-specific contact supplies the corresponding mechanism. The supported statement is narrower: in the three rank-sufficient four-way joins, conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability reduces the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual by $16.89\%$ to $51.95\%$, with yeast TE-dominant and human/E. coli stability-dominant partial mediation.
