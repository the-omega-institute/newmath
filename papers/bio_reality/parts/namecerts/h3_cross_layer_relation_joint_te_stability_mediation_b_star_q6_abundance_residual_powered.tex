\subsection{Joint measured-translation and mRNA-stability decomposition of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record is a finite cross-layer matrix experiment over nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K_{AAA},\operatorname{Arg}_{AGR},\operatorname{Ile}_{AUA},\operatorname{Leu}_{CUN/UUR},\operatorname{Leu}_{UUA/UUG},\\
&\operatorname{Ser}_{UCR/AGY},\operatorname{Ser}_{UCA/UCG},\operatorname{Thr}_{ACR/ACY},f_3\text{-stress}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
matched to protein abundance $P$, measured translation efficiency $T$, and mRNA stability $M$ on the same coding-sequence rows whenever all readouts are available.  The nuisance design $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine residual coordinates.  The experiment therefore asks a finite variance-partition question: after residualizing $Q$, $P$, $T$, and $M$ against $Z$, how much of the $Q\to P$ association remains after conditioning on $T$, on $M$, and on the pair $(T,M)$?

\paragraph{Joined panels and rank.}
Three organisms have a four-way joined panel.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the joined surface contains $2517$ rows, with reported translation-readout hit rate $0.9133458143888741$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the joined surface contains $11322$ rows, with reported translation-readout hit rate $0.6064136881331024$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined surface contains $4759$ rows, with reported translation-readout hit rate $0.8818572656921754$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$.  The fitted ranks are nondegenerate in all three cases: $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)=1$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde M)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T,\widetilde M)=2$.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $2494$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11299$ for human, and $4736$ for yeast.  Thus the all-ones, rank-saturated failure mode is not the operative explanation for this record.

\paragraph{Definition of the reported fractions.}
Let $R^2_{QP}$ denote the post-$Z$ coefficient of determination for $P$ on $Q$.  Let $R^2_{QP\mid T}$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}$ denote the incremental contribution of $Q$ to $P$ after the residualized translation readout, the residualized mRNA-stability readout, or both readouts have already been included.  The joint absorbed fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{T,M}(Q,P)=\frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The single-readout summaries are computed analogously as $\mu_T=(R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T})/R^2_{QP}$ and $\mu_M=(R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid M})/R^2_{QP}$.  These are descriptive variance partitions on observational rows, not causal effects.

\paragraph{Per-organism decomposition.}
For \emph{E. coli}, the direct post-control association is $R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932$.  Conditioning on measured translation gives $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331$, conditioning on mRNA stability gives $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653$, and conditioning on both gives $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036$.  The corresponding fractions are $\mu_T=0.01975521340963268$, $\mu_M=0.23052056324898387$, and $\mu_{T,M}=0.2404669586626661$, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The joint fraction is close to the sum of the two single-readout fractions: the difference is $-0.009808817995950458$ under the fixed tolerance $0.05$.

For human, $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$.  The measured-translation-only fraction is $0.00549490864190343$, the mRNA-stability-only fraction is $0.15314679870183337$, and the joint fraction is $0.16890712099923358$, again with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The joint-minus-single-sum diagnostic is $0.010265413655496769$, also within the fixed descriptive tolerance.

For yeast, the same finite calculation gives the largest mediation surface: $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$.  The measured-translation-only fraction is $0.4697188319693239$, the mRNA-stability-only fraction is $0.0626618595344251$, and the joint fraction is $0.5195182225621592$, with the same right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  The joint-minus-single-sum value is $-0.012862468941589822$, so the two readouts behave approximately additively at the stated tolerance.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation survival.}
The separate measured-TE survival experiment confirms that the translation readout is not merely a named contact.  After the same class of controls, measured TE has positive $Q$-survival in the relevant organisms: $R^2_{QT}=0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, with all nine residual coordinates present.  A broader measured-TE/protein-abundance decomposition over four organisms also reports measured mediated fractions $0.04091158340950673$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, while danio has a clipped measured fraction $0.0$ from a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$.  These numbers are compatible with the joint $T,M$ result: yeast shows a large measured-translation component, whereas human and \emph{E. coli} show a stronger role for the stability readout in this particular joined panel.

\paragraph{Direct abundance context.}
The direct protein-omics survival surface is positive and rank-powered across a wider organism set, but that fact alone is not a mediation claim.  On the organisms used here, the direct controlled protein-abundance values include $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human in the broad abundance run, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, each with nine surviving coordinates.  The joint experiment refines this direct signal by asking whether measured TE and mRNA stability absorb it on the same rows.  The answer is organism-specific: measured TE carries much of the yeast direct association, mRNA stability carries a larger share in human and \emph{E. coli}, and the pair $(T,M)$ absorbs $24.04669586626661\%$, $16.890712099923358\%$, and $51.95182225621592\%$ of the direct post-control association in \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast respectively.

\paragraph{Verdict and local boundary.}
This record is ready as a finite, observational, rank-powered variance-partition result.  It supports the statement that the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual is partly reduced by measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability on matched coding-sequence rows, with the strongest translation component in yeast and the strongest stability component in human and \emph{E. coli}.  It does not by itself establish a causal translation pathway, a molecular mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  Those stronger readings would require separate layer-matched contacts, perturbation or rescue designs where appropriate, and finite statistics at the relevant layer.  Within this chapter the admissible conclusion is narrower: the observed $Q\to P$ residual association is not a single undifferentiated protein-omics correlation; on the three computed panels, measured TE and mRNA stability explain a nonzero, permutation-supported portion of it after the declared controls.
