\subsection{Joint measured-translation and mRNA-stability decomposition of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record tests a finite matrix surface whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns are the nine residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each organism the coordinate matrix $Q$ is residualized against the same control block $Z$: intercept, $\log$ CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an M-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The downstream response $P$ is residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance.  The two candidate absorbing readouts are residualized measured translation efficiency $T$ and residualized mRNA stability $M$.  Thus the finite claim is not that $Q$ causes protein output, but that a measured row table can decompose the direct $Q \to P$ association after $Z$ into the portions removed by $T$, by $M$, and by the pair $(T,M)$.

\paragraph{Joined panels and rank.}
The four-way join was computed in three organisms.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 the joined table has $n=2517$ rows, with reported CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and TE join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; the TE side contributes $3415$ valid protein-keyed records and the mRNA half-life side contributes $2947$ valid gene-keyed records.  In \emph{Homo sapiens} the joined table has $n=11322$ rows, with CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and TE join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; the TE side contributes $11554$ gene records and the mRNA consensus table contributes $19480$ valid Ensembl-gene records.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} the joined table has $n=4759$ rows, with CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; the TE side contributes $5128$ valid protein-keyed records and the mRNA half-life side contributes $5379$ valid systematic-name records.  The residualized control rank is $23$ in all three panels, while $rank(Q)=9$, $rank(T)=1$, $rank(M)=1$, and $rank(T,M)=2$ after residualization.  The combined designs have $rank(Q,T)=10$, $rank(Q,M)=10$, and $rank(Q,T,M)=11$, so the reported decomposition is not an all-rank-saturated null artifact.

\paragraph{Definition of the decomposition.}
For each organism, $R^2_{QP}$ is the partial coefficient of determination for residualized $P$ explained by residualized $Q$ after $Z$.  The conditional quantities $R^2_{QP\mid T}$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}$ are the incremental $Q$ contributions after adding measured TE, mRNA stability, or both readouts to the residualized design.  The joint removal fraction is computed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
J_{T,M} &= \frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The single-readout fractions are interpreted descriptively as the portions of the direct $Q \to P$ association absorbed by one measured readout at a time.  They are not causal mediation effects, because the abundance, TE, and half-life rows are observational and come from organism-specific studies that need not share condition, platform, or perturbation design.

\paragraph{Finite numerical outcome.}
The direct and conditional $R^2$ list is concrete.  In \emph{E. coli}, $R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036$.  The resulting fractions are TE-only $0.01975521340963268$, stability-only $0.23052056324898387$, and joint $0.2404669586626661$, with right-tail paired-permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In human, $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$, giving TE-only $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only $0.15314679870183337$, joint $0.16890712099923358$, and the same right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$.  In yeast, $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, giving TE-only $0.4697188319693239$, stability-only $0.0626618595344251$, joint $0.5195182225621592$, and right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Overlap classification.}
The two absorbing readouts behave as approximately additive within the declared descriptive tolerance $0.05$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the sum of the single-readout fractions is $0.25027577665861656$ and the joint-minus-single-sum difference is $-0.009808817995950458$.  In human, the corresponding sum is $0.1586417073437368$ and the difference is $0.010265413655496769$.  In yeast, the sum is $0.532380691503749$ and the difference is $-0.012862468941589822$.  Each panel is therefore classified as approximately independent overlap, not as a strong redundancy or strong synergy regime.  The organism-specific pattern is nevertheless different: yeast is dominated by measured TE absorption, whereas \emph{E. coli} and human are dominated by the mRNA-stability readout.

\paragraph{Relation to measured TE and direct abundance survival.}
The measured-TE survival run supplies the upstream finite contact used here: $R^2_{QT,meas}$ is $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, with nine surviving coordinates in each of those panels.  The direct protein-omics survival run independently reports broad $Q \to P$ residual association after the same style of controls, including $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast in the larger direct-abundance panels.  The measured mediation run further reports measured-TE mediated fractions $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, with danio at $0.0$ after a negative raw fraction was clipped to the unit interval.  These surrounding finite results make the present joint table interpretable as a refinement of a real direct abundance signal, not merely as a standalone regression exercise.

\paragraph{Dictionary comparison.}
A separate yeast readout-dictionary certificate, using $n=1800$ sampled rows from $4758$ available rows, five folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $120$ deterministic permutations, found selection-aware coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ against a selection-aware null95 of $0.029149007242956115$.  Its best raw coverage over the candidate family was $0.2745002077400602$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  This comparison is compatible with the present joint-mediator result: measured TE and mRNA stability remove a substantial, finite part of the abundance residual in yeast, but they do not exhaust the residual coordinate signal.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is a finite variance-partition statement.  In three joined organism panels, residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates retain direct association with residualized protein abundance, and conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability removes a reproducible part of that association, with joint removal fractions $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$.  No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, organismal phenotype, causal direction, or universal biological law follows from this table.  Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact and a separately computed finite statistic.  The present chapter therefore supports only the bounded statement that measured TE and mRNA stability are finite absorbing readouts for part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual, with the strongest TE-conditioned absorption in yeast and stronger stability-conditioned absorption in the \emph{E. coli} and human panels.
