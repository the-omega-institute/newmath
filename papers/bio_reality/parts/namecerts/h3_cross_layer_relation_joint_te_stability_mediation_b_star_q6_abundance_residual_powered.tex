\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The joint TE--stability test attaches the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface to a four-way matched table with codon-derived coordinates $Q$, measured translation efficiency $T$, an mRNA-stability readout $M$, and measured protein abundance $P$.  The active coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  All four readout blocks are residualized against the same control design $Z$: an intercept, $\log$ CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The computation is finite-row and organism-indexed: \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 contributes $2517$ four-way joined rows, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and TE join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; \emph{Homo sapiens} contributes $11322$ rows, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and TE join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} contributes $4759$ rows, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.

\paragraph{Variance partition.}
For each organism the primary statistic is the controlled direct abundance association $R^2_{QP}$ and its remaining increment after conditioning on measured TE, mRNA stability, or both.  The definition used by the finite table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= R^2(P_e \sim Q_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= R^2(P_e \sim Q_e\mid T_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= R^2(P_e \sim Q_e\mid M_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= R^2(P_e \sim Q_e\mid T_e,M_e),\\
J_{T,M} &= \frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $e$ denotes residualization against $Z$.  In \emph{E. coli},
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.1181743523064932,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.11583979275713331,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.09093273405121653,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.08975732521542036,\\
J_{T,M} &= 0.2404669586626661.
\end{aligned}
$$
In human,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.04274178488511215,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04250692268197657,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.036196017359154814,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.0355223930537993,\\
J_{T,M} &= 0.16890712099923358.
\end{aligned}
$$
In yeast,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.1439989545940314,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.25453612246767165,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13047582645538838,\\

\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row conclusion is positive but sharply delimited: the $Q\to P$ abundance residual is not merely present; in all three matched organisms a measurable fraction of that residual is attenuated when both measured TE and mRNA stability are included.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter verdict is ready for a statistical, finite-row BioReality statement.  The supported claim is that the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual admits a measured attenuation decomposition through TE and mRNA stability in \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast, with joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$.
