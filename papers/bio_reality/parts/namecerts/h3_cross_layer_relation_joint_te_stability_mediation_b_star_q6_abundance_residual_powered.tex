\subsection{Joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability mediation of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}\label{sec:bior-sec-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record tests a finite, row-matched cross-layer surface in which the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are read against measured protein abundance after the same control family has first been removed.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The controls are an intercept, $\log$ CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine coordinates.  In every computed organism the residualized coordinate rank is $9$, the control rank is $23$, the measured-TE rank is $1$, the mRNA-stability rank is $1$, and the joint measured-TE plus stability rank is $2$.  Thus the calculation is not a rank-degenerate three-row surface: the active four-way joins contain $2517$ CDS rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $11322$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $4759$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.

\paragraph{Matched readouts and joins.}
The four-way join uses real CDS codon counts, measured ribosome-derived translation efficiency, a matched mRNA abundance or half-life readout, and measured PAXdb protein abundance.  The \emph{E. coli} panel has reported CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and four-way join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; its measured TE source contributes $3415$ genes, with $2720$ primary mRNA half-life joins and $2517$ final rows.  The human panel has reported CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and four-way join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; it uses $11554$ TE genes, $16160$ primary mRNA joins, $17$ symbol fallback joins, and $11322$ final rows.  The yeast panel has reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and four-way join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; it uses $5128$ TE genes, $5085$ primary mRNA half-life joins, and $4759$ final rows.  The row losses are explicit: for example, human excludes $7491$ CDS records with no TE match and $208$ with no mRNA half-life match, while yeast excludes $687$ with no TE match and $369$ with no mRNA half-life match.

\paragraph{Variance partition.}
For each organism the primary statistic is the residualized direct abundance association $R^2_{QP}$ and the remaining incremental $Q$ contribution after conditioning on measured TE, mRNA stability, or both.  The finite values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{E. coli}:\quad &R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331,\\
&R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036;\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:\quad &R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657,\\
&R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993;\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:\quad &R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314,\\
&R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast.  The single-mediator split is sharply organism-specific: measured TE alone accounts for $0.01975521340963268$, $0.00549490864190343$, and $0.4697188319693239$ respectively, whereas mRNA stability alone accounts for $0.23052056324898387$, $0.15314679870183337$, and $0.0626618595344251$.  In all three organisms the right-tail pair-permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$, using the deterministic organism-specific joint mediator permutation seed.

\paragraph{Overlap classification.}
The joint mediator fraction is close to the sum of the two one-mediator fractions under the fixed descriptive tolerance $0.05$.  The signed differences between joint mediation and the single-mediator sum are $-0.009808817995950458$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.010265413655496769$ for human, and $-0.012862468941589822$ for yeast.  The module-level classification is therefore approximately independent in all three organisms: TE and stability do not collapse to one identical explanatory direction, but their combined absorption is also not a super-additive mechanism claim.  The rank diagnostics agree with this reading.  In yeast, measured TE explains $440.4712858729507$ units of residualized protein-abundance energy while stability alone explains $33.14864736606459$, and the joint TE-plus-stability block explains $470.61694807139054$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding energies are $22.699433610195115$, $376.8458226543232$, and $388.6777658316693$.  In human they are $6.781948682039624$, $768.267193808668$, and $798.0659074156708$.

\paragraph{Relation to single-readout controls.}
The same coordinate family also survives direct protein-abundance testing in a broader fifteen-organism PAXdb surface, with all nine coordinates surviving in each organism and with $R^2_{QP}$ values ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in \emph{Halobacterium salinarum} to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast.  The measured-TE survival surface is narrower but directly relevant here: measured $R^2_{QT}$ is $0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, with nine surviving coordinates in each of those panels.  The measured-TE-only abundance decomposition is consistent with the joint result: the measured mediated fraction is $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  A separate yeast dictionary reduction reports $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, selection-aware null95 $0.029149007242956115$, accepted coverage $0.24538211439730545$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$; this supports the reading that the abundance residual is partially compressible by measured readout families while remaining far from exhausted.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite result is a ready positive variance-partition statement: in the three four-way joined organisms, conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability reduces the direct $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-to-protein-abundance association, with strongest TE absorption in yeast and stronger stability absorption in \emph{E. coli} and human.  The result is not a causal pathway proof.  The readouts are observational, condition- and platform-specific, and not phylogenetically controlled; measured TE and half-life are noisy summaries rather than interventions.  No claim about translation as a realized mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality follows from these numbers unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the same finite-row controls are recomputed for that layer.
