\subsection{Joint Translation-Efficiency and mRNA-Stability Mediation of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Abundance Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-joint-te-stability-mediation-b-star-q6-abundance-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The experiment fixes a finite row surface whose rows are coding sequences joined to measured protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, and a transcript-stability readout. The coordinate side is the nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual block
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.
Each organism is residualized against the same control family: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected coordinates. The control design has $24$ columns and residual rank $23$ in every computed organism. After this residualization, the target scalar $P_e$ is residualized protein abundance, $T_e$ is residualized measured translation efficiency, $M_e$ is residualized mRNA stability, and $Q_e$ is the residualized $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate matrix.

\paragraph{Joined panels.}
Three organisms have a full four-way join. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the join contains $4759$ rows, with a reported CDS hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and a translation-readout hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; the translation readout is the mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density from rich-medium replicates. In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, the join contains $2517$ rows, with a reported CDS hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and a translation-readout hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; the translation readout is mean CDS footprint wiggle coverage divided by mean CDS mRNA wiggle coverage. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the join contains $11322$ rows, with a reported CDS hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and a translation-readout hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; the translation-efficiency value is the direct positive RPFdb CDS value averaged across positive TE columns for each Ensembl gene. The row counts are therefore not a symbolic existence claim: they are the finite matched panels on which all listed variance partitions are evaluated.

\paragraph{Decomposition.}
The reported quantities use the following residualized regressions. First $R^2_{QP}$ is the fraction of $P_e$ explained by $Q_e$ after the control projection. Then $R^2_{QP\mid T}$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}$ are the incremental fractions for $Q_e$ after conditioning on $T_e$, on $M_e$, and on both $T_e$ and $M_e$.
The joint mediated fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{T,M}
  &= \frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This number is a finite variance-partition statistic. It is not a causal mediation effect and does not by itself identify a biochemical pathway.

\paragraph{Per-organism readback.}

R^2_{QP\mid M} &= 0.25453612246767165,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13047582645538838.
\end{aligned}
$$

The overlap diagnostic has single-mediator sum $0.532380691503749$ and joint-minus-single-sum $-0.012862468941589822$, inside the descriptive tolerance $0.05$; the right-tail permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Rank and energy diagnostics.}
The residualized coordinate rank is $9$ in each organism; $T_e$ and $M_e$ each have rank $1$, and the joint $T_e,M_e$ block has rank $2$. In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $4736$, with $p$-residual energy $1724.6664873071313$, $t$-residual energy $645.3764418081507$, and $m$-residual energy $194.99141167236996$. In \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $2494$, $2323.813305213946$, $672.36799431777$, and $198.6753916649337$. In \emph{H. sapiens}, they are $11299$, $12615.307912483846$, $1866.9940287020145$, and $647.920297431553$. The direct $Q_e$ energy on $P_e$ is $468.33677333459536$ in yeast, $274.6151322248693$ in \emph{E. coli}, and $539.2007770548378$ in human; after both measured readouts are conditioned, the remaining direct improvement energies are $225.02728529130957$, $208.57926657600905$, and $448.1259261619555$, respectively. Thus the finite readback is not all-or-nothing absorption: a substantial residual remains in every panel.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and direct protein abundance.}
The same coordinate block also survives against measured translation efficiency in separate powered TE rows: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish, with all nine coordinates surviving under the positive threshold. Direct protein-omics survival over measured PAXdb abundance is also positive in a broader fifteen-organism panel, including $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, and $0.04454565118019448$ for human. The joint TE--stability result explains how part of that direct abundance association is partitioned once measured translation efficiency and transcript stability are placed in the same finite row design.

\paragraph{Four-case boundary.}
A separate modeled-translation discriminant classified the yeast modeled-readout surface as nine case-$D$ coordinates and the \emph{E. coli} surface as seven case-$D$ coordinates plus two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop. The present measured-readout joint fit is more restrictive: it requires same-row protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, and mRNA stability. Its readback does not license a folded-structure, physical-admissibility, molecular-function, phenotype, mechanism, or universal-law conclusion. It also does not license a general translation claim merely from a positive $Q_e \to P_e$ association. Such claims require a separate layer-matched measurement and a statistic at that layer.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
On the computed three-organism panel, the claim is supported as a finite variance-partition statement: the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual is partially absorbed by the measured translation-efficiency and mRNA-stability readouts, with joint fractions $0.5195182225621592$, $0.2404669586626661$, and $0.16890712099923358$ for yeast, \emph{E. coli}, and human. The permutation values are positive against the recorded null route, and the overlap mode is approximately independent in all three organisms. A related selection-aware dictionary certificate in yeast reports coverage $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$ for the measured-readout family $[\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{turnover}]$, with unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, and a selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.029149007242956115$. The finite conclusion is therefore partial reduction, not closure: measured TE and transcript stability carry real aligned variation, while most of the residual remains outside the listed readout family.
