\subsection{Joint translation--stability attenuation of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether the abundance residual carried by the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinate surface is partly accounted for by two measured readout directions: ribosome-profiling translation efficiency and mRNA stability.  The surface is built on the coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Each organism row first removes a shared control space consisting of an intercept, log CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The tested response is residualized log protein abundance, the first mediator is residualized measured translation efficiency $T$, and the second mediator is residualized mRNA stability $M$.

\paragraph{Finite rows and joins.}
The computation is rank-powered on three matched organism rows.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, the four-way join contains 2517 CDS units, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and TE join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; the TE source contributes 3415 valid protein-keyed records, and the mRNA half-life source contributes 2947 valid gene-id records at growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$.  In human, the four-way join contains 11322 CDS units, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and TE join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; the TE source contributes 11554 valid protein-keyed records, and the consensus mRNA source contributes 19480 valid Ensembl-gene records.  In budding yeast, the four-way join contains 4759 CDS units, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; the TE source contributes 5128 valid protein-keyed records, and the mRNA half-life source contributes 5379 valid systematic-name records.  These are not organism-level summary rows: they are same-organism, per-CDS matched rows after protein abundance, codon count, measured TE, and mRNA-stability readouts have been joined.

\paragraph{Residual design ranks.}
After residualization against the control matrix, each organism retains full nine-coordinate rank for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design.  In \emph{E. coli}, the design has $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(Q)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(T)=1$, $\operatorname{rank}(M)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(T,M)=2$, with residual degrees of freedom $2494$.  The corresponding protein, TE, and mRNA-stability residual energies are $2323.813305213946$, $672.36799431777$, and $198.6753916649337$.  In human, the same ranks hold with residual degrees of freedom $11299$, and the residual energies are $12615.307912483846$ for protein abundance, $1866.9940287020145$ for TE, and $647.920297431553$ for mRNA stability.  In yeast, the same ranks hold with residual degrees of freedom $4736$, and the residual energies are $1724.6664873071313$, $645.3764418081507$, and $194.99141167236996$, respectively.  The rank diagnostic therefore rules out the degenerate all-fit boundary for this three-row mediation surface.

\paragraph{Variance decomposition.}
For each organism, $R^2_{QP}$ denotes the residual protein-abundance variance explained by the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates after the control space is removed.  The conditioned quantities $R^2_{QP\mid T}$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}$ measure the incremental contribution of the same coordinate surface after conditioning on measured TE, mRNA stability, or both.  The computed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{E. coli:}\quad &R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331,\\
& R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036;\\
\textit{human:}\quad &R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657,\\
& R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993;\\
\textit{yeast:}\quad &R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314,\\
& R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165,\quad R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838.
\end{aligned}
$$
The single-mediator pattern is organism-specific.  In \emph{E. coli}, mRNA stability removes a larger part of the abundance residual than measured TE.  In human, the same qualitative relation holds, though the whole $Q \to P$ surface is smaller.  In yeast, measured TE accounts for a large fraction of the residual association, while mRNA stability contributes a smaller but nonzero attenuation.

\paragraph{Joint mediation fraction.}
The joint attenuation fraction is computed as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting finite-row values are $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast.  The corresponding TE-only and stability-only components are $0.01975521340963268$ and $0.23052056324898387$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.00549490864190343$ and $0.15314679870183337$ in human, and $0.4697188319693239$ and $0.0626618595344251$ in yeast.  In all three organisms, the paired-mediator permutation test reports right-tail probability $0.000999000999000999$, using deterministic row-label permutations with the mediator pair as the object being permuted.

\paragraph{Overlap class.}
The two mediators are classified as approximately independent at the descriptive tolerance $0.05$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the sum of the single-mediator fractions is $0.25027577665861656$, and the joint-minus-single-sum contrast is $-0.009808817995950458$.  In human, the single-mediator sum is $0.1586417073437368$, and the contrast is $0.010265413655496769$.  In yeast, the single-mediator sum is $0.532380691503749$, and the contrast is $-0.012862468941589822$.  Thus the joint readout is not behaving like a single collapsed surrogate.  It separates a TE-dominant yeast row from stability-dominant \emph{E. coli} and human rows, while preserving a common finite calculation over the same nine-coordinate residual basis.

\paragraph{Relation to the readout dictionary.}
A related yeast readout-dictionary check used $n=1800$ joined rows from $4758$ available rows, five folds, permutation count $120$, join threshold $\rho_{join}=0.05$, and description-length penalty $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$.  That run reported selected coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ for the readout family $\{\text{measured TE},\text{mRNA stability},\text{turnover}\}$, with selection-aware null $95\%$ level $0.029149007242956115$ and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The joint TE--stability computation in the present record is therefore not a global explanation of the abundance residual.  It is a measured two-readout attenuation surface embedded in a larger dictionary where most of the yeast residual remains outside the tested readouts.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite rows support a bounded cross-layer statement: the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual is reproducibly attenuated by conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability, with significant paired-mediator permutation evidence in the three computed organism rows.  The strongest TE-linked attenuation is in yeast, while the strongest stability-linked attenuation is in \emph{E. coli} and human.  The result is a statistical variance partition on matched finite rows, not a causal pathway.  It does not by itself establish translation realization, ribosome mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality.  Those stronger claims require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.
