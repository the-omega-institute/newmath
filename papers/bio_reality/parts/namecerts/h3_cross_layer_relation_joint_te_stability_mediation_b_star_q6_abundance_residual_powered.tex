\subsection{Joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability partition for $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests a finite, row-matched cross-layer surface in which the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are first residualized against a shared covariate block $Z$, and the residualized protein-abundance vector $P_e$ is then compared with the residualized coordinate matrix $Q_e$ before and after conditioning on two measured readouts.  The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  In all three organisms, the residualized coordinate rank is $\operatorname{rank}(Q_e)=9$, the control rank is $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, the measured-TE rank is $1$, the mRNA-stability rank is $1$, and the joint measured-TE plus stability rank is $2$.

\paragraph{Joined rows and readouts.}
The computed panel contains three four-way joins.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the join uses $4759$ rows, with reported CDS join rate $0.9156038419146592$ and TE join rate $0.8818572656921754$; the measured TE is the mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across the rich-medium replicates, and the mRNA-stability readout is joined from $5379$ valid half-life records.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, the join uses $2517$ rows, with reported CDS join rate $0.9978649586335735$ and TE join rate $0.9133458143888741$; TE is computed from coding-strand footprint wiggle coverage divided by CDS mRNA wiggle coverage, while mRNA half-life is read at growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$ from $2947$ valid records.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the join uses $11322$ rows, with reported CDS join rate $0.9779294769799313$ and TE join rate $0.6064136881331024$; the TE readout is the positive RPFdb CDS translation-efficiency value averaged over positive TE columns, and the transcript readout is joined from $19480$ valid consensus records.  These row counts are not interchangeable with an organism-level table: the claim is about these exact matched finite matrices.

\paragraph{Residual energy and rank checks.}
The same regression geometry is applied in each organism.  For yeast, the residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $4736$, with $p$-residual energy $1724.6664873071313$, measured-TE residual energy $645.3764418081507$, and mRNA-stability residual energy $194.99141167236996$.  For \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $2494$, $2323.813305213946$, $672.36799431777$, and $198.6753916649337$.  For human, they are $11299$, $12615.307912483846$, $1866.9940287020145$, and $647.920297431553$.  Thus the reported associations are not read from raw codon usage alone; they are computed after the same finite control projection and after a nondegenerate residual-rank check.

The single-readout decompositions differ by organism: yeast is TE-heavy, with TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$ and stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$; \emph{E. coli} is stability-heavy, with TE-only fraction $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only fraction $0.23052056324898387$; human is also stability-heavy, with TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$ and stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$. \paragraph{Relation to measured TE and protein mediation.}
A separate measured-TE survival surface reports positive residual-coordinate survival against log measured TE in four organisms: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, $0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, with all nine coordinates surviving in each matrix.  The measured protein-abundance mediation surface gives measured fractions $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.0$ for zebrafish after truncating a negative raw fraction $-0.04801486048721136$.  These numbers agree with the joint partition in the limited sense that the yeast row has a large TE-conditioned attenuation, while the bacterial and human rows retain larger mRNA-stability components.  They do not turn the partition into causal mediation.

\paragraph{Four-case readback and related compression.}
The modeled-tAI four-case discriminant classifies the yeast modeled-tAI/protein matrix as case $D$ for all $9$ coordinates, while the \emph{E. coli} matrix has $7$ case-$D$ coordinates and $2$ positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop.  The present measured-TE plus stability partition is therefore a stricter finite-row readback: it does not rely on the modeled-tAI label alone, and it separates the TE and mRNA-stability attenuation fractions in the same joined abundance rows.  The related readout-dictionary certificate on yeast used $n_{join}=1800$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, and $120$ deterministic permutations, reporting coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell-abundance test, also on yeast, used $n_{join}=1257$ and found base $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, null $95\%$ level $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $f3$--dwell correlation $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  These auxiliary finite rows support the same practical conclusion: the residual signal is real, partially reducible to measured readouts, and still not exhausted by them.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For the matched rows actually computed, the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual is not a single undifferentiated codon-usage effect.  Conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability reduces the direct coordinate-to-protein-abundance signal in yeast, \emph{E. coli}, and human, with organism-specific allocation between the two readouts.  The strongest TE-mediated attenuation is the yeast row; the bacterial and human rows are dominated by the stability term.  The correct conclusion is a statistical variance partition over finite matched matrices: the coordinates retain direct residual abundance association, part of that association is absorbed by measured translation and transcript-stability readouts, and a substantial remaining component persists after both readouts.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation mechanism, folding pathway, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, organismal phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universal biological rule follows from these rows alone.  Measured TE, mRNA half-life or stability, and PAXdb abundance are noisy observational readouts drawn from specific studies and conditions, often not from a synchronized perturbation experiment.  A stronger biological claim would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as perturbation, rescue, structure, localization, turnover, interaction, or functional-assay data, joined to the same CDS units and tested under its own controls.  Direct $Q_i\to P_k$ survival is therefore reported here only as controlled protein-omics association; mediation language means variance attenuation after conditioning on finite measured readouts, not proof of a causal pathway.
