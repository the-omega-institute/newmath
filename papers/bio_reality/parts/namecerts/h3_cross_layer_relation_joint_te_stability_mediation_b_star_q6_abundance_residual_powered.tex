\subsection{Joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability partition for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}\label{sec:biorReality-q6-joint-te-stability-mediation}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite matrix.}
The finite object in this chapter is a three-organism matched table whose rows join coding-sequence codon counts, the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, measured ribosome-based translation efficiency, mRNA-stability or abundance-derived stability readouts, and measured protein abundance.  The nine residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each organism is residualized against a common control block consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organism, while the residualized $Q$ design has rank $9$.

\paragraph{Joined rows and readout provenance.}
The usable four-way joins are nondegenerate but not uniform across organisms.  In \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $2517$ rows are joined, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9978649586335735$ and TE join hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; $3415$ protein-key TE records and $2947$ valid mRNA half-life records are available.  In human, $11322$ rows are joined, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ and TE join hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; the table uses $11554$ TE genes and $19480$ valid consensus mRNA records, with $17$ mRNA rows supplied through the gene-symbol fallback.  In budding yeast, $4759$ rows are joined, with reported CDS hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$; $5128$ TE genes and $5379$ valid mRNA half-life records are present.  The TE definitions are source-specific: \emph{E. coli} uses coding-strand footprint coverage divided by matched mRNA wiggle coverage, human uses positive RPFdb CDS translation-efficiency values averaged per Ensembl gene, and yeast uses mean footprint normalized read density divided by mean mRNA normalized read density across the rich-medium replicates.

\paragraph{Rank diagnostics.}
After residualization, the residual degrees of freedom are $2494$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11299$ for human, and $4736$ for yeast.  The measured-TE and mRNA-stability residual designs each have rank $1$, their joint mediator design has rank $2$, and the augmented $Q$-plus-mediator designs have ranks $10$, $10$, and $11$ for $Q+T$, $Q+M$, and $Q+T+M$, respectively.  The protein residual energies are $2323.813305213946$ in \emph{E. coli}, $12615.307912483846$ in human, and $1724.6664873071313$ in yeast.  The corresponding measured-TE residual energies are $672.36799431777$, $1866.9940287020145$, and $645.3764418081507$, while the mRNA-stability residual energies are $198.6753916649337$, $647.920297431553$, and $194.99141167236996$.

\paragraph{Variance partition.}
Let $P_e$, $Q_e$, $T_e$, and $M_e$ denote the residualized protein-abundance, $B^{*}_{Q6}$, measured-TE, and mRNA-stability columns after the shared controls.  The fitted decomposition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= R^2(P_e \sim Q_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= \Delta R^2(P_e \sim Q_e \mid T_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid M} &= \Delta R^2(P_e \sim Q_e \mid M_e),\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= \Delta R^2(P_e \sim Q_e \mid T_e,M_e),\\
\mu_{T,M} &= \frac{R^2_{QP}-R^2_{QP\mid T,M}}{R^2_{QP}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \emph{E. coli}, the list is $R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036$.  For human, it is $0.04274178488511215$, $0.04250692268197657$, $0.036196017359154814$, and $0.0355223930537993$.  For yeast, it is $0.2715520808117803$, $0.1439989545940314$, $0.25453612246767165$, and $0.13047582645538838$.

\paragraph{Mediation readback.}
The joint measured-readout attenuation is positive in all three joined panels.  The joint fractions $\mu_{T,M}$ are $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast.  The single-readout pieces are strongly organism-specific: measured TE alone accounts for $0.01975521340963268$, $0.00549490864190343$, and $0.4697188319693239$, while mRNA stability alone accounts for $0.23052056324898387$, $0.15314679870183337$, and $0.0626618595344251$ in the same organism order.  Thus \emph{E. coli} and human are stability-dominant in this finite table, whereas yeast is measured-TE dominant.  The overlap module is classified as approximately independent in all three cases: the joint-minus-single-sum terms are $-0.009808817995950458$, $0.010265413655496769$, and $-0.012862468941589822$, each within the fixed descriptive tolerance $0.05$.  The right-tail permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$ for each organism under the deterministic paired mediator-label permutation.

\paragraph{Consistency with neighboring readouts.}
The measured-TE survival experiment supplies the translation-side coordinate contact used here: $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving in each of these three organisms.  The direct protein-abundance survival experiment gives nonzero $Q \to P$ association on the same residual coordinate family; for the three organisms used here, the direct $R^2_{QP}$ values in the broader protein-only table are $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, again with nine surviving coordinates.  The measured-TE abundance mediation run reports measured fractions $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$ for \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast; the joint TE-plus-stability table therefore refines the same direction of attenuation by adding the mRNA-stability readout.

\paragraph{Compression boundary.}
A yeast readout-dictionary comparison keeps the present chapter from over-reading the attenuation as full explanation.  On $n_{join}=1800$ yeast rows, with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, five folds, and $120$ permutations, the best selected dictionary family covers $C^{*}=0.24538211439730545$ after the selection-aware null threshold $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The irreducibility audit on the same yeast scale uses $n_{join}=1800$, $n_{join,available}=4758$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, five folds, and $120$ permutations, and classifies the registered source signal as real but reducible by at least one listed reduction family.  These finite-row checks agree with the local conclusion: the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual is not erased by the measured readouts, but a substantial, organism-dependent part is carried by measured TE and mRNA stability.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The admissible statement is a statistical variance partition on matched observational rows.  It says that the direct $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual association with measured protein abundance is reduced after conditioning on measured TE and mRNA stability, with the strongest TE-mediated reduction in yeast and the strongest stability-mediated reductions in \emph{E. coli} and human.  It does not establish a causal pathway, a universal translation law, a folding statement, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or evolutionary direction.  Those stronger claims would require separate layer-matched measurements and an additional controlled test at the relevant layer.  The present finite table is therefore ready as a bounded cross-layer readout: it supports organism-specific measured-readout attenuation of the protein-abundance residual, while preserving the distinction between translation readout, mRNA stability, direct protein abundance, and higher biological interpretation.
