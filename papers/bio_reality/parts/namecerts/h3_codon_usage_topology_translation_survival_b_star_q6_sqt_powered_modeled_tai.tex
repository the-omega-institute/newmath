\subsection{Modeled tAI survival of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-modeled-tai-survival}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record tests whether the nine synonymous-residual coordinates in the $B^*_{Q6}$ block retain explanatory mass against a bounded translation-layer readout after amino-acid composition and simple sequence covariates have already been removed.  The coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&\texttt{K\_AAA},\texttt{Arg\_AGR},\texttt{Ile\_AUA},\texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\texttt{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout used here is not a ribosome footprint or a protein assay.  It is a modeled per-codon tAI surface computed from GtRNAdb anticodon copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights, then projected onto the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual readout columns.  The resulting matrix has shape $9\times 9$.

\paragraph{Rows and controls.}
The powered run uses $40$ organism rows: \textit{Homo sapiens}, \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}, \textit{Vitis vinifera}, \textit{Danio rerio}, \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \textit{Escherichia coli}, \textit{Gallus gallus}, and $33$ additional bacterial, fungal, plant, and vertebrate entries.  The control design contains $24$ columns and has rank $23$, leaving residual degrees of freedom $17$ after removing organism-level sequence and context effects.  The reported controls include amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, CDS length, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$, so the statistic is not the earlier three-row saturated surface.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $X$ denote the $40\times 9$ matrix of residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates, let $Y$ denote the $40\times 9$ modeled tAI readout matrix after the same quotienting, and let $Z$ denote the control matrix.  The survival score is read as the controlled improvement of the modeled tAI readout from $X$ after projecting away $Z$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT} &= R^2(Y_\perp;X_\perp),\\
R^2_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579,\\
\operatorname{rank}(X_\perp) &= 9,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23,\qquad df_\perp=17.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold for an entry is $10^{-12}$, and the degenerate-fit guard is $0.999999$.  Since the observed value is below that guard and far above the positive threshold, this finite table supports a modeled translation-readout survival statement: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block is not absorbed by the amino-acid, context, GC3, length, and $M$-density baseline model when the target is the modeled tAI coordinate surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier coupling row.}
A preceding proxy coupling computation on three organisms used codon counts and a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum.  Its break condition was $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ for all nine coordinates, and that condition was not met: all $9$ residual coordinates had nonzero entries, with maximum absolute coupling $1882464.1106377328$.  That row was only a coupling contact, not survival.  The present $40$-organism run supplies the missing powered controlled statistic for a modeled tAI readout, while still remaining below measured ribosome or protein output.

\paragraph{Three-organism Leu warning.}
The CUN/UUR Leu gate on three organisms reported Pearson $1.0$ and partial Pearson $1.0$ for $n=3$, with log-ratio signs $(1,-1,1)$ and values $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$.  That sign reversal is retained as a warning against reading a direction from a tiny organism table.  The $40$-organism modeled tAI result is therefore not an upgraded universal Leu direction; it is a separate powered survival estimate on a modeled translation-weight matrix.

\paragraph{Downstream and adjacent checks.}
Several finite audits bound what the modeled tAI result can and cannot explain.  In the translation-complement readback, the residual abundance component left after modeled tAI differs sharply by organism: $d=0.874628721049266$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $0.6629960038578532$ for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.9400341806302775$ for \textit{G. gallus}, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.8972672018746489$ for \textit{M. smegmatis}, and only $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast.  The corresponding $m=1-d$ values are $0.12537127895073397$, $0.3370039961421468$, $0.05996581936972245$, $0.07910506443205445$, $0.10273279812535108$, and $0.788267804272933$.  A direction-aware ranking used the stated regularizer $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$; it selected measured translation efficiency for \textit{D. rerio}, complex membership for human and chicken, mRNA for yeast, and no selected $H$ for \textit{E. coli} or \textit{M. smegmatis}.  Bonferroni status varied: for example human measured TE exceeded its threshold $0.0004670613303535708$, while human measured tRNA-tAI did not exceed threshold $0.00025456436025110654$ despite $n=19053$ joined rows.  These rows show that the modeled tAI surface is one bounded readout among several competing residual explanations.

\paragraph{mRNA and abundance contacts.}
Yeast mRNA half-life gives an additional non-translation readout: $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$ on $5085$ joined rows, with dominant coordinate \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, cosine $0.8040935468384264$ against the same-join protein-abundance vector, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  The same yeast join has $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, and conditioning on mRNA half-life gives $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, so the absorbed fraction is $0.09994436477872035$ with null mean $0.00019674487584783138$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0014571003414947088$.  The yeast half-life join hit rate is $0.9156038419146592$.  In \textit{E. coli}, the half-life table at growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$ gives $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$ on $2720$ rows, dominant coordinate \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, cosine $-0.7784923375569599$ against the protein-abundance vector, and the same permutation tail $p=0.000999000999000999$, with CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$.  These numbers keep mRNA stability and protein abundance separate from modeled tAI survival.

\paragraph{Condition and tissue specificity.}
Protein-abundance residual checks also remain descriptive.  In yeast, the GFP abundance comparison gives $R^2_{QP}=0.3280825603424665$ in YEPD and $0.3050385538305022$ in SD on $2209$ shared proteins, with \texttt{f3\_stress} dominant in both conditions, cosine $0.9993775935182967$, and Pearson $0.9983880256003688$ between condition profiles.  In human tissue proteomics, the adult B-cell, heart, and liver readouts have $R^2_{QP}=0.03626117434944674$, $0.02320896321347296$, and $0.026133456228248052$ on $9244$, $6468$, and $8664$ joined rows respectively, with dominant coordinates \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, and \texttt{Ile\_AUA}.  Pairwise tissue cosines are $0.6883655232187615$, $0.8990093450963941$, and $0.9176793373754919$.  These rows do not replace the modeled tAI contact; they delimit later protein-layer behavior.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The positive $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$ is evidence for survival against a modeled tAI readout under the declared controls.  It is not measured elongation speed, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency from RPF/mRNA, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Gene-copy based tAI is not tRNA abundance or charging.  A stronger translation statement would require an organism- or CDS-matched measured ribosome or TE contact; a protein statement would require matched protein-abundance statistics; structure, function, mechanism, and universality each require their own layer-matched observations and controlled estimates.
