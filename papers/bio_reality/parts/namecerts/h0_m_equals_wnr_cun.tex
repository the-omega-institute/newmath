&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.

This is the finite alphabetic support available to the packet. This geometric summary is useful for detecting whether the same finite object is being discussed across probes, but it is still geometry of an encoded codon support.  It is not evidence that any interval is transcribed, translated, physically viable, or biologically selected.

\paragraph{Contact-gated ORF interpretation.}
The immediate ORF-eligibility seed is deliberately held at boundary status.  The top-level packet has no attached contact rows, no mismatch rows, and no independent sequence or transcript observation.  Its two probes therefore ask only whether the system preserves the correct gate: a start--stop-window pattern may be treated as a testable eligibility proposal only after an external interval contact is attached.  The linked enumeration row \texttt{h2.orf.start\_stop.window\_eligibility} demonstrates the limited form of such a separate test in a one-window manifest: $1$ external window was loaded, and $1$ of $1$ input windows was classified by start--stop enumeration under the scope \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}.  That row explicitly records the classification as \texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only}, which is the appropriate layer for this chapter.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact as a separate event.}
A stronger linked contact row, \texttt{h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact}, supplies a distinct external annotation experiment rather than deriving biology from the codon coordinate.  It loads $168$ curated rows from an interval dataset sourced from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  The input classes present are \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.  The positive interval test accepts $72$ of $72$ positives, with accept rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched decoy test rejects $72$ of $72$ decoys, also with rate $1.0$ and the same upper-tail value.  The predeclared pairwise comparison records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Three held-out splits are reported, each with $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$: \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold}, and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  The boundary controls list $24$ boundary cases and $0$ promotions.  These figures describe the separate interval-contact experiment; they do not retroactively turn the present seed packet into an observed ORF without that contact.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
The chapter's biological content is therefore intentionally modest.  The finite codon rows identify $M$, its motif compression $R$, its saturation behavior, and its quotient geometry.  The ORF rows show how a start--stop-window classifier behaves when an external interval dataset is supplied.  The self-check row records $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is moved across biological layers.  Consequently, this chapter permits the following local conclusion and no stronger one: the packet is a finite codon-coordinate record that can support a contact-gated ORF-eligibility question.  It does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, organism-wide law, or universality.  Any such claim would require a separate contact whose data type can test that specific layer.
