\paragraph{ORF-eligibility interpretation.}
The biological layer named by the row is $orf\_eligibility$, but the row itself is intentionally weaker than an ORF assertion. The start/stop-window enumeration audit $c26a0d6656f7$ classified one input window and reported one classified window under the scope $external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$. Its own output states that the result remains $orf\_eligibility\_only$. Therefore the admissible reading is: a start/stop-window enumeration can mark what would need to be tested by a separate sequence, transcript, or curated annotation contact; it cannot turn the twenty-codon coordinate into an observed biological interval.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore separates two assertions. First, the finite codon equality $M=WNR\cup CUN$ is verified as a twenty-element enumeration with accompanying motif, saturation, and quotient checks. Second, ORF eligibility is not available from that coordinate alone; it requires an attached interval-level contact. Even when the curated interval contact is present, the conclusion remains interval eligibility under the stated classifier and dataset. No translation event, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biochemical mechanism, organismal role, or universal biological law follows from this row unless a further contact specifically measures that layer. The self-check run $0f47d2f0cf41$ is consistent with this reading: it records a BioReality self-check with three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
