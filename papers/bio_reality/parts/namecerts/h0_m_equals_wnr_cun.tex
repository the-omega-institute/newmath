\subsection{A finite codon-row boundary for ORF eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-m-wnr-cun-orf-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite codon certificate.}
The record considered here is the claim $h0.M.equals.WNR.CUN$, interpreted only as a finite codon-row certificate and not as a biological realization claim.  The verified row reports that the set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly the following union of the $WNR$ and $CUN$ codon rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation records $\lvert M\rvert=20$.  This equality is a finite enumeration fact: every listed codon belongs to the certified row, and no additional codon is included in that row.  In the present BioReality use, this is enough to define a controlled start/stop-window question, but it is not enough to assert that any organism, transcript, ribosome, or protein has realized the window.

\paragraph{Motif compression row.}
A separate finite row verifies the compressed motif set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For this row the missing-list and extra-list audits are both empty, so the finite motif description has neither omitted nor spurious entries relative to its certified enumeration.  The compression row therefore supplies a smaller finite support from which later closure probes can be phrased.  Its role is combinatorial: it records a row identity among codon strings and does not by itself provide a nucleotide interval observed in a genome or transcript.

\paragraph{Closure under the third face probe.}
The saturation audit reports that $Sat_{f3}(R)$ is the $18$-element set

$$

& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.

$$
The recorded $f3$ boundary residual is the empty list.  Thus the finite combinatorial audit has no unresolved row item at that boundary, while still leaving biological contact entirely outside the codon-row calculation.
