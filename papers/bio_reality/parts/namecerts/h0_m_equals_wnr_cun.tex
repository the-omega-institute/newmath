\paragraph{Local ORF boundary rule.}
Open-reading-frame eligibility is therefore treated as a layer that requires independent biological input. The row contains two boundary probes. The first says that a start/stop-window seed must remain below ORF eligibility unless a sequence, transcript, or curated interval supplies the observed biological window. The second says that the packet may not record an ORF eligibility claim while no sequence database, transcript evidence, or curated annotation can test the proposed interval. These probes are not failures of the codon packet; they are the discipline that prevents a finite codon pattern from being mistaken for biological realization. The local rule is: start/stop-window enumeration may define a candidate test, but it cannot certify translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality unless a separate contact supplies the relevant biological fact.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The row is ready as a self-contained BioReality record because it gives both sides of the boundary. On the finite codon side, $M$ has $20$ codons, $R$ has the declared motif union with no missing or extra elements, the $f_3$ saturation has empty boundary, and the quotient geometry has the recorded face vectors and $10$ quotient rows. On the biological side, the row states that ORF eligibility is not available from those data alone. A separate interval contact may test ORF eligibility, as illustrated by the $168$-row curated-contact ledger, but the permitted conclusion remains limited to ORF interval eligibility within that contact. No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, global law, or universal genomic classification follows from the finite codon packet or from the interval-contact result unless another specifically targeted contact supplies that stronger evidence.
