\subsection{Window-six codon spectrum at the standard-code boundary}
\label{subsec:h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed contact.}
The packet treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label readout on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The carrier is not a biological mechanism: it is a coordinate display in which codons occupy cube vertices and curated amino-acid or stop labels are read as observed labels.  The verified contact is the standard genetic-code table, used only at the code-read layer.  Its role is to say which codon has which label, not to supply translation chemistry, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal rule for organisms.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation verifies that this row is the punctured-cube boundary instance with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite object is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it has a local tile shape with $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ two-faces inside the codon-coordinate display.  The singleton and three-point label phenomena are therefore recorded as local code-table features of this tile, while the surrounding codon cube remains larger than the tile itself.

This is evidence for a coordinate-and-label regularity inside the finite code table; it is not evidence that the geometry itself causes translation or protein behavior.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The direct shape test used $N=2000$ sampled trials with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact tile shape appeared once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  High-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  A second amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, found zero exact matches, with $p=0.0$ for exact shape in the finite sample.  Its lower-tail count at or below the observed module size $20$ was $7$, giving $p=0.0035$.  These numbers make the observed window-six tile uncommon under the stated finite nulls, including a null that preserves amino-acid class information.  They do not identify an evolutionary cause, a physical law, or a functional optimum.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level split used $5$ families: alternative yeast scored $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each scored $1.0$.  The leave-one-codon-out row reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, no anchor cases, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These values show that the local tile is stable under finite deletion probes at the code-label level.  They do not show that the deleted codon would be biologically restored by a cell or that the same pattern governs all genetic-code variants.

Every module has either predictable status or boundary status in the finite audit.  This is the packet's strongest row-level conclusion: the spectral tile, the punctured-cube boundary, and the module partition describe the same finite code-read object.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and scope.}
The ready claim is that window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile spectrum in the curated standard-code table.  The claim is deliberately narrow.  The contact supplies codon-to-label assignments, and the computations supply finite coordinate, closure, spectrum, relation, null-model, deletion, and module rows.  Nothing in this packet observes translation realization, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Crossing any of those boundaries would require an additional contact that directly measures the higher-layer phenomenon.
