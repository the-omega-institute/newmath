\subsection{Window-Six Codon Spectrum and Local Boundary Readback}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record treated here is a window-six presentation of the standard-code codon table.  Each codon is read as a six-bit coordinate in the codon cube, while the biological labels are supplied by a curated standard-code table.  The construction therefore has two different kinds of information in the same finite row: the coordinate geometry is internal to the window-six display, and the amino-acid or stop labels are imported as observed code labels.  The relevant boundary set is the thirteen-codon row
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count is not inferred qualitatively: the measured cardinality is $|R|=13$.  The same row is also checked as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, giving thirteen vertices, sixteen local one-dimensional incidences, and four square faces in the observed local tile.

\paragraph{Spectral finite row.}
The spectral row attached to this tile records three numerical quantities.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same residual is reported for the cubic check.  In this record the tolerance-level spectral radius is therefore the $M$-side value $0.6752479308830213$.  The near-zero residual is used only as a finite numerical consistency check for the local window-six construction.  It is not a biological mechanism, a translation model, a folding rule, or a law about all genetic systems.

\paragraph{Shape comparison under finite nulls.}
The local shape is rare under the recorded randomized comparison.  In the unconstrained shape run, $N=2000$ samples were evaluated with seed $42$; producing those distinct samples required $57377$ attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, so the exact-shape frequency was $p=0.0005$.  The high-overlap condition at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p=0.0125$.  A second null that preserves amino-acid class evaluated $N=2000$ samples with the same seed.  It recorded observed $M$-size $20$, exact match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape value $0.0$, and lower-tail value $0.0035$.  These numbers support the claim that the observed window-six boundary is not a typical finite shape under the tested null ensembles.  They do not say that the codon table was generated by the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Code-label recovery checks.}
The code-layer tests are stronger than a bare spectral annotation.  In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ tables were evaluated against $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and adding the boundary raised the recorded rate to $0.9841269841269841$.  In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the high closure-coverage count was $8$, the predictable count was $8$, and the recovery rate was $1.0$.  Thus the finite record behaves as a code-label readback test for local codon labels, not as an experiment on protein structure or cellular realization.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row separates the same behavior across five families.  The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate tables.  The module classification also accounts for all thirteen codons in $R$: the $agr$ module is $\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys$ is $\{AAA\}$, $cun$ is $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met$ is $\{AUA\}$, $ssn$ is $\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp$ is $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  The classification row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  This is the finite sense in which the local tile has a complete code-layer decomposition.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The contact used by this chapter is the curated standard genetic-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code readback and a local split of codon labels.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  The record must therefore be read with a strict layer boundary: window-six geometry may expose a local boundary tile, and the curated table may label that tile, but no claim about translation, folding, admissible physical structure, function, mechanism, or universality follows unless an independent contact for that higher layer is supplied.
