\subsection{Window-Six Codon Spectrum as a Code-Layer Record}
\label{subsec:biorreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite display.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table after it has been placed on a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$.  The biological input is only the curated assignment of the $64$ codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The mathematical object tested here is not a mechanism for translation.  It is a finite coordinate question: whether the label table, when read through a window-six codon arrangement, exposes a local boundary tile with a stable spectral signature.  The claim is therefore confined to the code-read layer.  It does not assert folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, translational implementation, a universal mechanism, or a biological law.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary row is the $13$-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit reports $|R|=13$ and identifies the same row as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed boundary is not merely a count; it carries a concrete local incidence shape inside the codon cube.  The row should be read as a coordinate-local separation of labels in the standard table, not as a statement that the physical cell must instantiate this geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
For the spectrum claim, the verified run with identifier $1026324eff41$ gives the scalar values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported residual for the governing cubic relation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\lambda_M,\lambda_R,\mu) = -6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same value recorded as the radial residual.  At the numerical tolerance used by the finite run, this is a vanishing residual.  The spectral radius entry is again $0.6752479308830213$, so the spectrum record is internally consistent: the same leading value appears both as $\lambda_M$ and as the accepted radius.

\paragraph{Shape comparison.}
The surrounding Monte Carlo control is finite and explicit.  In the shape-significance run with seed $42$, $N=2000$ distinct comparison samples were accepted after $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sample matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} = 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null model with the same sample count and seed recorded observed $M$-size $20$, no exact shape match, and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed size.  Its finite probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the narrow statement that the standard-code label table has an uncommon local boundary shape under the tested coordinate model.  They do not promote the coordinate pattern into an explanatory account of the genetic code.

\paragraph{Readback stability.}
Two leave-one tests probe whether the boundary is a fragile artifact of a single table row.  In the code-table leave-one run, $23$ tables were evaluated against $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adding the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  In the codon leave-one run, the predictable count was $8$, the boundary count was $5$, the anchor count was $0$, and the recovery rate was $1.0$.  These checks show that the local code-read pattern is recoverable under the tested omissions, again only as a finite label-table phenomenon.

\paragraph{Family and module localization.}
The family-level audit divides the leave-one-code behavior into $5$ families.  Their hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The codon-module classification assigns all $13$ boundary codons and leaves no extras or omissions.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\

$$
The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from modules, and no unassigned codons.  The row is therefore completely accounted for by the displayed modules.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The only biological contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and permits finite checks of local label splits.  It contains no observation of ribosomal dynamics, tRNA abundance, kinetic selection, molecular folding, structural order, energetic admissibility, phenotype, or organism-level function.  Consequently, the result remains a code-layer spectral and combinatorial record.  Any claim about translation, protein structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate empirical contact of the corresponding kind.
