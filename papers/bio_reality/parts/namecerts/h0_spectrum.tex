\subsection{Window-six spectral record for the standard-code label table}
\label{subsec:h0-spectrum-window-six-standard-code}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate packet.}
The claim is evaluated on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon is treated as a six-bit display and the curated standard genetic-code table supplies the amino-acid or stop label attached to each codon.  The record is deliberately restricted to the code-read layer: it asks whether the local six-bit coordinates expose a boundary-tile pattern in the standard-code label table, not whether this pattern explains translation or protein behavior.  The active boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite set is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the observed object is not an arbitrary list of codons: it is a thirteen-vertex local tile with sixteen one-dimensional faces and four two-dimensional faces inside the six-bit display.

\paragraph{Spectral finite-row witness.}
The spectral row attached to this packet records two radii and one radial parameter:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic residual and the radial residual are both reported as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical row is within the recorded tolerance of the cubic relation used by the probe.  The value named as the spectral radius in tolerance is again $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$.  In this chapter the role of these numbers is narrow: they certify a stable finite computation on the codon-coordinate packet and its boundary set.  They do not by themselves create a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape significance against coordinate nulls.}
The observed $M$-shape row was compared with $N=2000$ sampled alternatives using seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and logged $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among these alternatives, the exact shape match count was $1$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  These two values say that the recorded local shape is uncommon under the stated finite coordinate sampling procedure, while the high-Jaccard tail is still visibly larger than the exact-match tail.

A second null preserves amino-acid class information.  In that row the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-match count among $2000$ samples is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$.  The recorded exact-shape value is $p=0.0$ at the displayed precision, and the lower-tail value is $0.0035$.  This is a stronger stress test because it keeps label-class information in the randomization rather than ignoring it entirely.  The packet therefore has two separate finite-row checks: one for coordinate shape and one for amino-acid-class-preserving shape pressure.

\paragraph{Readback and leave-one checks.}
The standard-code table is the only contact used here.  It says that each codon has a curated code label, and that is enough to test local label split and codon-label readback.  In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The recorded hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adding the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$; the same $0.9841269841269841$ is reported as the rate above baseline.  In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count is $0$, the boundary count is $5$, the predictable count is $8$, the high-coverage count is $8$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$.  These rows support the statement that the local coordinate packet has predictive readback behavior at the code-label level.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen codons in $R$ are fully assigned by the module classification row.  The classification has $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The listed modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has at least one predictable or boundary codon according to the finite row.  The family-level leave-one-code-out row covers $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The evidence in this packet is a finite code-read observation: six-bit codon coordinates, a thirteen-codon boundary set, a punctured-cube face count, spectral radii, null-model comparisons, leave-one recovery rows, and module accounting against a curated standard-code label table.  It is not evidence that the local tile is a global rule of biology.  No translation process, folded structure, physical admissibility condition, biological function, universal mechanism, or cross-organism law follows from these rows unless a different contact supplies that higher-layer observation.  Amino-acid label readback remains label readback; it is not protein structure and it is not a claim about realized molecular function.
