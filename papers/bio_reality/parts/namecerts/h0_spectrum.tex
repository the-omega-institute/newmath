\subsection{Window-six spectrum for the standard-code codon table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer.  Codons are represented as points of a six-coordinate codon cube, and the observed labels are read from a curated standard genetic-code table.  The relevant finite row isolates a boundary set $R$ of thirteen codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row $h0.R.cardinality.13$ reports $|R|=13$, and the row $h0.R.punctured.cube$ identifies the same set with a punctured-cube face pattern whose finite face vector is $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the phenomenon being recorded is not a full biological mechanism; it is a local coordinate-and-label regularity inside the sixty-four-codon table.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The spectral row $h0.spectrum$ assigns three numerical values to the local tile: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The same run records a radial polynomial residual of $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, matching the reported cubic-polynomial residual to numerical tolerance:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,\\
P_{\mathrm{rad}}(\lambda_M,\lambda_R,\mu)&=-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The value $0.6752479308830213$ is also recorded as the spectral-radius-in-tolerance value.  This makes the spectrum a finite numerical certificate for the local codon-tile shape: the row supplies a stable radius, a companion radius on the $R$ side, and a near-zero cubic residual.

\paragraph{Shape rarity against finite randomized rows.}
The same codon-tile pattern is compared with sampled alternatives.  In $h0.M.shape.significance$, the experiment used $N=2000$ accepted samples with seed $42$.  The sampling process made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exact shape agreement occurred once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  High-overlap agreement at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2000,\\
\#\{\mathrm{exact\ shape\ matches}\}&=1,\\
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
\#\{J\geq 0.9\}&=25,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null row, $h0.null.model.aa_class_preserving$, keeps amino-acid class structure fixed while testing the tile.  It reports observed $M$-size $20$, no exact-shape matches among $2000$ samples, and a lower-tail count of $7$, hence $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0$ in the sampled table and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  The finite data therefore support a narrow claim: this window-six codon tile is uncommon under the two recorded null procedures.  The row does not provide an explicit twenty-element membership list for $M$, so only the verified size $|M|=20$ is used here.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The predictive rows describe how the local tile behaves under deletions of known code information.  The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closures, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  In family form, five families are reported: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.

\paragraph{Module partition of the boundary set.}
The module-classification row assigns every one of the thirteen $R$ codons to a named module and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The complete partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing boundary codons, and no unassigned codons.  This is the finite combinatorial content of the boundary split: all observed exceptional codons in $R$ are accounted for by the six module names above.

\paragraph{Biological scope.}
The empirical contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  It tests code-layer readback and local label splitting; it does not observe translation dynamics, folded protein geometry, molecular admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently, the spectrum and module partition may be used to describe a local codon-coordinate regularity in the standard-code table, but they do not by themselves imply that DNA must follow this geometry, that codon labels determine protein structure, or that the same local tile is a global law across organisms.  Any claim about translation, folding, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate experimental or curated contact at that higher layer.
