\subsection{Window-Six Codon Spectrum}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table after it has been placed on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is treated as a finite coordinate word, and the biological label attached to that coordinate is read only as a curated code label: amino acid or stop.  The chapter records a local boundary pattern in that finite table.  It does not treat the pattern as a law of molecular biology, and it does not promote label readback into translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\\
&\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a cell complex inside the codon cube, the recorded boundary has $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row is not merely a scalar statistic: it names the full set of codons in the boundary and the local face count carried by that set.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The spectrum attached to this window-six record separates the main coordinate block from the boundary block by two reported radii.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports the cubic residual and the radial residual as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{residual}=-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This residual is numerically at tolerance scale for the recorded finite calculation.  The chapter therefore treats the spectrum as a reproducible coordinate signature of the window-six table, not as an explanation of why biological translation has the form it has.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite null models.}
The boundary shape was compared against $2000$ sampled finite alternatives using seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled alternative matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A looser comparison using Jaccard threshold $0.9$ found $25$ matches, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserved amino-acid class constraints.  In that test the observed main set size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$ among $2000$ samples, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$.  The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the narrower claim that the standard-code table carries an unusual local codon-coordinate shape.  They do not supply a higher-layer biological cause.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
The code-table leave-out test evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$.  At the codon level, the leave-one-codon row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.

Family-level leave-out behavior was recorded across $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is evidence that the same finite coordinate pattern has predictive contact with curated code-label variation.  It is still code-layer evidence: the rows do not observe ribosomal realization, tRNA kinetics, protein folding, stability, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The $13$ codons in $R$ are completely assigned by the recorded module classification.  The module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Every module has at least one predictable or boundary member.  This complete accounting matters because it prevents the spectral row from depending on an unnamed residue of codons outside the displayed finite data.

\paragraph{Contact boundary.}
The sole biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact permits codon-to-label readback and local split checks.  It does not contain an observation of translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently the chapter's claim is deliberately narrow: window-six coordinates expose a finite local boundary-and-spectrum signature in the standard-code label table.  Any statement about protein structure, molecular implementation, functional effect, or cross-organism mechanism requires an additional contact of the appropriate kind before it can be asserted.
