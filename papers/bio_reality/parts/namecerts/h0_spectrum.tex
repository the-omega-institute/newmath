\begin{aligned}

\paragraph{Shape tests against finite null rows.}
Two independent finite randomization rows place the local tile in context.  In the unconstrained shape test, $N=2000$ samples were drawn with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sample matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $25$ samples at Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null row, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed module size was $|M|=20$.  The exact-shape count was $0$, so the recorded exact-shape value is $p=0.0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed module size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the statement that the observed local tile is uncommon under the stated finite resampling schemes.  They do not show that the tile is forced by biology; the null models are tests of coordinate-and-label structure, not of translation dynamics or protein-level outcomes.
