\subsection{Window-six spectral row for the standard-code table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and code-layer contact.}
This row concerns the standard-code codon table as a finite code-read object.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with codons read as finite coordinate words over the four nucleotide letters and then compared against the curated standard-code label table.  The finite distinctions used here are the six-bit codon display, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The contact is deliberately narrow: the observed fact is that a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  That contact can test codon-label readback and local label splitting, but it does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.

Thus the chapter records a local codon-tile boundary rather than a biological law.  The row says that these thirteen codons form the tested boundary pattern inside the window-six display; it does not say that the geometry forces the biological code.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape row was tested against $N=2000$ sampled alternatives using seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ high-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported exact-shape and high-Jaccard significance values are therefore $0.0005$ and $0.0125$, respectively.  A second null preserving amino-acid class structure used $N=2000$ samples with the same seed.  It observed $0$ exact matches, $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size, and an observed $M$ size of $20$.  Its exact-shape value is $0.0$, and its lower-tail value is $0.0035$.  The finite row therefore supports a non-generic local code-table pattern, while still remaining inside the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The result is a finite spectral and combinatorial record for the standard-code label table.  It supports the statement that window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern in the curated code labels.  It does not promote that pattern to translation, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate empirical contact at the corresponding layer.  In particular, amino-acid label readback is not folded protein structure, and cross-organism wording about codon families is not structure evidence by itself.  The present row is ready only as a code-read claim: the finite numbers identify a local spectral boundary in the standard-code table and simultaneously mark where the claim must stop.
