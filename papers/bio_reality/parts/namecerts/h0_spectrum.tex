\subsection{Window-six spectral readback for the standard code}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-layer contact placed on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The cube coordinate is only a display and counting carrier: each codon is represented by a six-bit vertex, while the biological labels are read from a curated standard-code table.  The claim examined here is the spectral finite-row claim $h0.spectrum$: the observed codon subset selected by the local tile construction has a stable spectral signature, but the signature is not promoted beyond codon-label readback.

\paragraph{Observed boundary row.}
The selected boundary set $R$ has exactly $13$ codons.  The finite row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|R|=13$.  The same row identifies $R$ with a punctured-cube shape whose face data are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local tile is not merely a named collection of codons: in this finite display it has $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ square faces.  The result is still a code-layer shape statement; it says where the curated labels sit in the six-bit display and does not say that molecular translation is governed by this geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum row gives three scalar quantities attached to the same finite carrier:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial-polynomial residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the same value is recorded for the cubic-polynomial check.  Within the numerical tolerance of the run, the spectral radius witness is therefore the row $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$.  This is a finite numerical certificate for the displayed codon tile: it records that the chosen $M$-side local shape and the $R$-boundary row have a reproducible spectral contrast, with $\lambda_M$ larger than $\lambda_R$ in the reported run.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite resampling.}
The shape-significance row used $N=2000$ distinct sampled alternatives after $57377$ sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact tile shape was observed once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
High-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null used $N=2000$, observed $0$ exact shape matches, and reported an observed $M$ size of $20$.  Its lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a code-table anomaly statement at the finite-row level: the tile is scarce under the sampled nulls.  They do not establish an evolutionary cause, a chemical constraint, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Predictive rows within the code table.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast.  The leave-one-codon-out row reported $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor cases, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These checks say that the finite codon-label table can often recover the held-out code-layer target from the local construction; they do not observe ribosomal realization, folded structure, physical viability, or biological function.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module-classification row assigns all $13$ elements of $R$ without extras or omissions.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\

\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\

$$
The row records $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Every listed module has either a predictable member or a boundary member in the finite table analysis, so the module split is complete for the observed $R$ row.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The reality contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table: it supplies codon-to-label assignments at codon-label resolution.  It is adequate for testing whether the six-bit coordinate display exposes a local tile, whether $R$ has the stated punctured-cube data, whether the reported spectrum is numerically satisfied, and whether held-out code labels are recovered in the finite experiments.  It is not a contact for translation dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Consequently the present record is ready only as a code-read spectral and boundary-tile statement.  Any claim about translation, folding, admissible chemistry, organismal function, or a global biological law would require a different observation source and cannot be inferred from these rows.
