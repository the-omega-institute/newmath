\subsection{Window-Six Spectral Boundary for the Standard Codon Table}
\label{subsec:bior-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record for claim $h0.spectrum$ is a finite code-layer observation on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  Its biological contact is the curated standard-code table: each codon is read as an amino-acid or stop label, and no further biological realization is supplied by that contact.  The working distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The internal bookkeeping has coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation components, but the only biological readout asserted here is the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished relation-side set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The verified member list is
$$
\begin{aligned}

The same run certifies that this is the punctured-cube set used by the local tile calculation, with face-vector

\begin{aligned}

Thus the finite object is not merely a count: it has a specific codon support and a specific local cubical incidence profile.  The list concentrates the familiar reassignment-sensitive codon families $\mathrm{AGR}$, $\mathrm{AAR}$, $\mathrm{CUN}$, $\mathrm{AUA}$, $\mathrm{UUR}$, $\mathrm{UCN}$, and stop-related codons, but this sentence is only a code-table description.  It is not a statement about translation machinery, folding, cellular fitness, or a universal mechanism.

Since the row supplies $M$ by size and spectral behavior rather than by an explicit codon member list, the present chapter states the $M$ evidence only at those supplied levels.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite shuffles.}
The shape comparison used $N=2000$ null samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one null sample reproduced the exact observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} = 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
Twenty-five null samples reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers make the local shape unusual relative to that finite null ensemble.  They do not make the local shape a biological law; they say that, after the codon labels are supplied by the curated code table, the induced six-bit tile is not a common outcome of that particular sampling procedure.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid classes.  In $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$, the exact-shape match count is $0$, so the reported exact-shape probability is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed $M$ size is $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below that observed size is $7$, yielding
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is stronger evidence that the observed code-layer geometry is not a routine artifact of merely preserving amino-acid class counts.  It still remains evidence about a label table, not about protein structure or biological function.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The conclusion is narrow.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary-tile pattern in the curated standard-code label table.  The evidence consists of an explicit $13$-codon punctured-cube set, a certified cubical face-vector, stable spectral values, rare-shape behavior under two finite null models, and high recovery rates in leave-one-code and leave-one-codon probes.  The chapter therefore treats $h0.spectrum$ as ready at the code-read layer.

\paragraph{What is not licensed.}
No claim of translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row.  The curated code table supplies codon-to-label assignments; it does not observe ribosomal dynamics, tRNA availability, folding, energetic admissibility, organismal function, or evolutionary necessity.  Any move beyond the code-read layer requires a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.  Amino-acid label-sequence readback remains label readback, and a local codon-tile split remains a local code-table fact.
