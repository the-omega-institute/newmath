\subsection{Window-six spectrum for the standard-code codon table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The record \texttt{h0.spectrum} concerns a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$, used only as a finite coordinate system for reading the curated standard genetic-code label table. The biological object is the standard-code codon table, and the layer under test is the code-reading layer: codons are compared through their labels, local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes. The computation does not assert that the coordinate geometry is a biological mechanism. It asks a narrower question: whether the standard-code labels determine a local boundary tile whose adjacency spectrum is numerically stable and whose exceptional codons can be listed without ambiguity.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and boundary set.}
The row identifies the carrier as the codon cube $Q_6$. The verified boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this is a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite observation is not merely a scalar count. It records a concrete codon list, an induced local shape, and the low-dimensional incidence data of that shape. The codons split into six named modules in the supplied classification: $\mathrm{AGR}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{CUN}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{SSN}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The module audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral computation attached to \texttt{h0.spectrum} reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic residual and the radial residual are both recorded as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius entry repeats $0.6752479308830213$ as the in-tolerance value. These numbers supply a finite numerical certificate for the local codon-label tile: the $M$-side radius is larger than the $R$-side radius, while the residual magnitude is at floating-point tolerance rather than at biological scale. The row therefore supports the statement that the selected standard-code labels give a stable local spectral profile inside the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape-significance run used seed $42$ and $N=2000$ samples. It reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the sampled alternatives, exactly $1$ matched the observed shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. The number with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ was $25$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$-size $20$, exact match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape probability $0.0$, and lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These rows make the local geometry unlikely under the two supplied finite null comparisons, but they do not turn the coordinate description into a law of molecular translation.

\paragraph{Readback tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level row gives $5$ families, with above-baseline hit rates $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families. The leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. These readback rows say that the local tile has predictive contact with codon-label assignments under the supplied removal tests. They do not say that the tile realizes translation, protein folding, physical feasibility, organismal function, or universal biological necessity.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The curated standard-code table is the only contact used here. It supplies codon-to-label assignments at the code layer, not ribosomal dynamics, not tRNA charging, not folded protein structure, not thermodynamic admissibility, and not a phenotype or function assay. Consequently, an amino-acid label sequence read from the table remains a code-layer output. No claim about translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter unless an additional contact supplies that higher-layer observation. In particular, the CUN, UUR, stop, tryptophan, isoleucine, methionine, arginine, lysine, serine, and leucine module language is only a finite codon-label classification in the supplied table; it is not evidence of folded structure.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The verified finite row supports a precise code-layer claim. In the six-coordinate codon display, the curated standard-code labels determine a $13$-codon boundary set with face-vector $(13,16,4)$, a complete six-module classification, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, and small null-model probabilities for the observed shape. The result is therefore ready as a local codon-table observation: a stable boundary-tile spectrum appears in the standard-code readback. Its scope ends at that sentence. Higher biological layers require separate observations.
