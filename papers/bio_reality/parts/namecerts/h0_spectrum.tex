\subsection{Window-Six Spectrum for the Standard-Code Boundary Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record $h0.spectrum$ places the standard-code codon table on a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The finite carrier is not a molecular mechanism; it is a coordinate display in which codons are read as six-bit points and then compared with the curated standard-code labels.  In this row the distinguished boundary set $R$ has exactly $13$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies $|R|=13$, and the punctured-cube calculation reports the face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the boundary is not merely a list of exceptional codons: as a finite subcomplex of the window-six cube, it carries thirteen vertices, sixteen local edges, and four two-dimensional faces.  This is the geometric content of the row.

The point of the witness is local: a codon-coordinate tile cut out from $Q_6$ has a stable spectrum.  Nothing in this number says that the tile is translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal law.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The standard-code shape is compared against $2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  The sampler attempted $57377$ distinct draws and rejected $55377$ duplicates before reaching the reported sample size.  Exact shape agreement occurred once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  High-overlap agreement at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  These values say that the observed finite geometry is rare under that sampled coordinate null model.  They do not identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact has enough resolution to test code-label readback and local label splitting inside the six-bit codon display.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, tRNA availability, organism-specific expression, protein folding, physical admissibility, or biological function.  Consequently, the admissible conclusion is narrow: the standard-code label table contains a finite thirteen-codon boundary whose punctured-cube shape, spectrum, null-model rarity, recovery behavior, and module partition are all recorded by the rows above.  Any claim about translation, folded structure, admissible biophysics, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact with that higher layer.
