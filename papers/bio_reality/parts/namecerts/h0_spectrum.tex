\subsection{Window-six spectral contact for the standard-code table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a code-layer contact: a curated table assigns each of the $64$ codons in the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ to an amino-acid or stop label. The finite object under inspection is not a biochemical mechanism. It is the label table read through six-bit codon coordinates, with local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes available as finite distinctions. The observed boundary set is the $13$-codon row
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded row count is $|R|=13$, and the same row is reported as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the finite contact is strong enough to identify a local codon-tile boundary inside $Q_6$, while still remaining only a code-read observation.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral calculation attached to this row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial residual and the cubic residual agree numerically at $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, so the finite computation satisfies its spectral constraint to numerical tolerance. The value reported as the spectral radius in tolerance is again $0.6752479308830213$. This does not make the codon table a spectral law of biology; it says that the curated standard-code labels, when projected onto this window-six coordinate display, carry a reproducible local spectral signature.

\paragraph{Shape and null comparison.}
The shape comparison used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. Among these samples, the exact local shape appeared once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, while $25$ samples reached Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. A second amino-acid-class-preserving null, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, observed $M$-size $20$, found $0$ exact matches, and had $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size. Its exact-shape value is $0.0$, and its lower-tail value is $0.0035$. These rows support the claim that the local tile is not a generic consequence of arbitrary label shuffling, including under an amino-acid-class-preserving comparison.

\paragraph{Predictive checks.}
The leave-one-code-out check evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, whereas the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast. The leave-one-codon-out check recorded $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $0$ anchors, and recovery rate $1.0$. These are finite row-level checks: they show that the local codon boundary has recoverable structure under omission tests, not that translation or physiology has been derived from geometry.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The $13$ boundary codons are completely assigned to six modules. The agr module is $\{AGA,AGG\}$, ar\_lys is $\{AAA\}$, cun is $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, ile\_met is $\{AUA\}$, ssn is $\{UCA,UUA\}$, and stop\_trp is $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$. The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from modules, and no unassigned codons. This exact accounting is the main reason the spectral row can be read as a local boundary-tile record rather than as an uninterpreted numerical coincidence.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label readback and local split tests. It does not observe translation as a molecular process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law. Amino-acid label sequences are still code-layer outputs; they are not folded structures. Any move from this finite codon-coordinate record to translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact that directly measures the promoted layer. Without that additional contact, the correct conclusion is narrower: the standard-code labels expose a statistically sharp, spectrally coherent, and omission-stable local boundary pattern in the window-six codon cube.
