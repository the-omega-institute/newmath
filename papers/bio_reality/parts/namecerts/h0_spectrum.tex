\subsection{Window-six spectral record for the standard-code table}
\label{sec:h0-spectrum-window-six-standard-code}

\paragraph{Claim and finite carrier.}
The object recorded here is the standard-code codon table read at the code-label layer.  The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with codons treated as coordinate points and with amino-acid or stop labels supplied by the curated standard-code table.  The claim is not that the cube geometry causes translation.  It is the narrower claim that, after the standard labels are attached, a local boundary-tile pattern appears in the finite coordinate display and has a reproducible spectral signature.

The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In lexicographic codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
   &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is reported as a punctured-cube face pattern with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed local shape is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it carries a finite incidence profile inside the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The spectral row attached to the same finite pattern reports three numerical quantities:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated cubic check has residual
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the radial polynomial evaluation gives the same residual.  The reported spectral radius in tolerance is again $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$.  For this finite record, the spectral datum is therefore a numerical invariant of the observed codon-label arrangement, not an independent biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape significance against randomized label arrangements.}
A shape-randomization experiment with $N=2000$ distinct sampled tables records $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact observed shape occurs once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ is $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the finite claim that the observed local tile is atypical under the sampled null distribution.  They do not show that the geometry is a biological law, because the experiment only compares finite code-label patterns.

A second null model preserves amino-acid class information.  In that row, the observed $M$-size is $20$, exact-shape count is $0$ among $2000$ samples, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$.  The corresponding reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The class-preserving comparison keeps the signal at the label-geometry level: it says that the finite local arrangement is hard to reproduce under that sampling rule, not that a downstream protein has any specified fold or function.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact supports codon-label readback and local split checks.  It does not contain an observation of translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or any claim that DNA must obey this finite geometry.  Any future statement crossing those boundaries would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind.  In the present record, the justified conclusion is exactly the local one: the standard-code label table, when displayed on $Q_6$, contains a $13$-codon boundary row with the listed incidence vector, spectral values, null-model significance, recovery behavior, and complete module classification.
