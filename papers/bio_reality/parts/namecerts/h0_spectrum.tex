\subsection{Window-Six Codon Spectrum and Local Boundary Readback}\label{sec:biores-h0-spectrum}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record considered here is a window-six presentation of the standard-code codon table.  Each codon is treated as a six-bit coordinate in the codon cube $Q_6$, while the amino-acid or stop assignment is treated as a curated label attached to that coordinate.  The coordinate system supplies a finite geometry; the label table supplies the observed code-layer readback.  The chapter therefore studies a local boundary pattern in the standard code, not a biological mechanism for translation and not a claim about folded proteins.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its observed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row verifies that $R$ is the punctured-cube boundary pattern used by the computation.  Its face-count vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is not merely a list of labels: it also fixes the local tile combinatorics by giving the number of vertices, edges, and two-faces retained by the boundary object.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral computation attaches three scalar quantities to the local split.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same value is reported as the radial polynomial residual.  At this numerical precision the residual is effectively zero for the finite computation, while the reported spectral-radius value is $0.6752479308830213$.  Thus the row supports a definite code-layer spectral signature for the window-six tile: the measured radius belongs to the $M$ side of the split, the boundary side has the smaller value $\lambda_R$, and the cubic relation is satisfied to numerical tolerance.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape row was tested against $2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so the empirical comparison is over a hard finite sample rather than an unconstrained continuum.  The exact shape was observed once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap event with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values say that the local tile is uncommon under the tested coordinate-preserving sample.  They do not say that the genetic code was produced by this geometry, nor do they identify a biochemical cause.

A second null row preserves amino-acid class information.  In $2000$ trials, the exact shape occurred $0$ times.  The observed $M$-side size was $20$, and only $7$ samples were at or below that size in the lower tail.  The corresponding reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite contrast: even after preserving the coarse amino-acid class constraint used by that null model, the observed code-layer geometry remains rare in the tested sample.

\paragraph{Predictive stress rows.}
Two leave-out rows test whether the boundary pattern remains informative when code entries are withheld.  In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ tables were evaluated across $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$.  Adding the boundary raised the rate to $0.9841269841269841$, which is also the reported value above baseline.  This is a code-table recovery statistic: it measures how well the finite local pattern points back to withheld code labels under the stated procedure.

In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the predictable count was $8$, and the high-closure coverage count was $8$.  The recovery rate was $1.0$, also reported as $1.0$ above threshold.  The row therefore records perfect recovery for the tested withheld-codon condition, but only at the codon-label layer.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The $13$ boundary codons are partitioned into six named modules without residue.  The row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The module assignment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has a predictable or boundary status.  This matters because the spectral and punctured-cube rows are not isolated scalar coincidences; the same finite boundary set is also exhausted by biologically recognizable codon-family groupings at the label-readback level.

The leave-one-code-out family row reports $5$ families.  Their hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  The non-uniform family list is important: the result is not stated as a universal law across all conceivable code variants, but as a finite family-indexed recovery row for the tested tables.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  It can test code readback and the local codon-tile label split.  It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or functional realization.  Consequently the chapter's conclusion is deliberately narrow: the window-six codon coordinates expose a rare local boundary tile with a stable spectral row and strong finite recovery statistics against the listed tests.

No conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows.  A codon-to-label table is not a ribosome, not a folding assay, not a thermodynamic admissibility experiment, and not an organism-level functional study.  Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate contact with observations at that higher layer.  Within the present data, the justified statement is only that the standard-code label table, when read through the six-bit codon coordinate display, contains a finite local boundary pattern with $|R|=13$, face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, together with the empirical rarity and recovery rows stated above.
