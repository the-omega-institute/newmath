\subsection{Window-six spectrum for the standard-code boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite claim.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$. The biological contact is not a biochemical mechanism. It is the curated assignment table that sends each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. The claim is therefore restricted to the code-read layer: a finite coordinate representation may expose a local boundary tile, and the labels used to name that tile are supplied by the curated table. The operative distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, singleton label corners, and a three-point label class. The internal data used here are coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data; the readback is only the standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The verified boundary row records a thirteen-codon set $R$. Its members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cardinality check gives $|R|=13$. A second finite check identifies the same list as a punctured-cube boundary with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary is not being inferred from a verbal description of the genetic code. It is a finite row object with an explicit codon support, an independently recorded size, and a matching punctured-cube face vector.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}

$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
These rows support the statement that the observed local shape is not a typical draw under the recorded comparison procedure. They do not turn the local tile into a universal biological principle.

This strengthens the finite-row reading: even when label classes are preserved in the comparison, the observed local configuration remains unusually constrained. The row still measures code-label geometry, not folding, thermodynamics, translation dynamics, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only contact used by this chapter is the curated standard-code table. It establishes that codons have amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code and thereby permits finite code-label readback. It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Any assertion at one of those higher layers would need a different contact, with its own observation and its own failure modes.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The ready claim is that the window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary-tile spectrum for the standard-code label table. The supporting rows are the explicit $13$-codon boundary, the punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, the near-zero polynomial residual, the shape and class-preserving null statistics, and the leave-one-out recovery rows. The conclusion remains local: it is a code-read statement about finite codon-label data, not a claim about translation, folding, admissible molecular physics, biological function, mechanism, or universality.
