\subsection{Window-six spectral packet for the standard-code table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The packet indexed by $h0.spectrum$ treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-layer object.  Its carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a display of the $64$ codons by three nucleotide positions with two binary coordinates per position.  The relevant distinctions are a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The record does not identify a biological mechanism.  It says only that a window-six coordinate presentation can expose a sharply delimited boundary pattern once the curated standard-code labels are attached.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label.  This contact resolves codon-label readback, not translation dynamics.  It contains no folded structure observation, no physical admissibility assay, no functional measurement, and no universality theorem.  Therefore every conclusion below remains at the code-read layer.  Promotion to translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological law would require a separate contact with the corresponding empirical content.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The verified boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this set as the punctured-cube row for the packet, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed boundary is not merely a loose list of reassigned codons; it has a finite cubical footprint: thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four square faces in the local six-bit coordinate display.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
A coordinate-null experiment with $N=2000$ samples used seed $42$ and required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the narrower statement that the observed local shape is uncommon under the sampled coordinate null.  They do not show why the biological code has this shape, and they do not turn the coordinate description into a causal account of coding biology.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class structure.  In $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$, it found no exact shape match.  The observed $M$ size is $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below that observed size is $7$.  The recorded probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This strengthens the finite code-layer observation: even when the sampling respects amino-acid-class information, the exact local shape is absent in the sampled rows and the observed size lies in a small lower tail.  The row still remains a label-table comparison, since no independent structure, translation, or function contact is present.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The packet reports a hit rate above baseline of $0.9841269841269841$, a hit rate inside $M-T$ of $0.9365079365079365$, and a hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary of $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, there are $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.  These rows say that the local coordinate-and-boundary pattern is predictive for code-table perturbation tests at the label level.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row.}
The leave-one-codon-out test records no anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  This is a strong finite recovery result for the codon-label packet.  It should be read as recovery inside the codon-table coordinate presentation, not as recovery of protein realization or folded structure.

\paragraph{Module partition.}

$$
\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\

\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\

The classification row reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  The module-level assertion is therefore exactly closed over the finite row displayed above.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite data support a ready code-layer claim: the standard-code label table, when displayed on the window-six codon cube, contains a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary whose spectral row satisfies the recorded cubic check to numerical tolerance, whose shape is rare under the sampled coordinate nulls, and whose perturbation rows recover the listed code-level targets at high rates.  The same data also enforce the local boundary: codon-label readback is not translation, not folding, not physical admissibility, not biological function, not mechanism, and not universality.  Any claim past the code layer must bring its own empirical contact.
