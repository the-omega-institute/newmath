\subsection{Window-six codon spectrum}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and label contact.}
The record is built on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon is read as a six-bit display of three nucleotide positions. The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. That contact is sufficient for code-layer readback and for checking finite label partitions on $Q_6$. It is not a translation experiment, not a structural assay, not a physical-admissibility test, not a functional assay, and not evidence for a universal biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The finite boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is verified as a punctured-cube face pattern with $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local tile is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it carries a finite face incidence signature inside the codon cube. The four-codon $CUN$ block is present as $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$; the stop-side block is $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$; the $AGR$ block is $\{AGA,AGG\}$; and the singleton rows $AAA$ and $AUA$ remain visible as one-codon corners in the same boundary set.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum attached to this finite tile separates the measured $M$ side from the boundary $R$ side. The verified spectral scalars are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\

$$
The evaluated cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same value is reported for the radial polynomial evaluation. This is effectively zero at the displayed numerical tolerance, so the row is a concrete spectral constraint on the finite codon-tile data rather than an informal visual analogy. The recorded spectral radius in tolerance is again $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite randomization row used $N=2000$ comparison samples. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Among the samples, exactly $1$ matched the observed shape exactly, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null row preserving amino-acid class found observed $M$-size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$-size. Its recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These rows support the claim that the tile is a statistically sharp finite code-layer pattern. They do not say that the pattern causes translation, protein folding, physical viability, organismal function, or any global biological law.

\paragraph{Readback and leave-out checks.}
The code-table leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$; the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$; and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out row had anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. These checks say that the finite tile is stable under code-layer omission tests. They do not convert the label table into a biochemical mechanism.

The by-family row contains $5$ families. The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. The module-classification row assigns all $13$ boundary codons with no missing or extra module codons. The complete module partition is

The row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the module list, and no unassigned codons. Every module has either predictable or boundary status. This gives a finite module-level account of the local code split.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The verified content of claim $h0.spectrum$ is therefore a finite code-read statement. In $Q_6$, the curated standard-code labels expose a $13$-codon boundary tile with a punctured-cube face signature, a numerically stable spectral row, significant shape rows against two finite null tests, strong leave-out recovery, and complete module accounting. The correct scientific scope is narrow: the contact reads codon labels, not ribosomal realization, not folded structure, not physical admissibility, not biological function, not a mechanism of inheritance, and not universality. Any move beyond code-layer label readback needs a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.
