\subsection{Window-six codon spectrum and local code-layer boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Record.}
The record named $h0.spectrum$ concerns the standard-code codon table only at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, used as a display for the $64$ codons after each nucleotide position is resolved into two binary coordinates. The biological contact is a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The claim is therefore not that codons biologically obey this coordinate system, but that the coordinate display exposes a reproducible local boundary pattern once the curated labels are read as fixed finite data.

\paragraph{Boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary row $R$ has cardinality $13$. The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite observation is not merely that thirteen codons have been selected; it is that the selected codons occupy a local cubical shape with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces inside the six-coordinate display. The singleton-corner and three-point-label-class distinctions are interpreted within this local pattern, while the amino-acid and stop labels themselves remain supplied by the curated code table.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape test for the associated $M$ pattern used $N=2000$ sampled trials with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, of which $55377$ were duplicate retries. Exactly one sampled shape matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} = 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null row preserving amino-acid classes used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. In that row the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$. The corresponding values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite tests support the claim that the local code-table pattern is non-generic under the stated sampling procedures. They do not identify a biochemical cause.

The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The recorded hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the predictable count was $8$, and the recovery rate was $1.0$. The high-coverage closure count was $8$. These numbers say that the local coordinate split has predictive stability under the specified omissions, while still remaining a code-label phenomenon.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The scientific reading is deliberately narrow. The six-coordinate display, the set $R$, the size-$20$ observation for $M$, the spectral numbers, the sampling $p$-values, the leave-out rates, and the module partition together support a local boundary-tile description of the standard-code label table. The curated table is the contact that supplies codon-to-label readback; the coordinate construction supplies a finite way to inspect that table. No step in this chapter observes ribosomal translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or an organism-independent necessity. Any one of those stronger claims would require a separate contact aimed at that layer rather than a promotion of this code-table row.
