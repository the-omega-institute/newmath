\subsection{Window-six spectral boundary for the standard code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record is a code-layer observation on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$. Its finite display is the usual codon coordinate cube, read through a curated standard-code table that assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label. The local object extracted from the table is not a claim about translation as a cellular process. It is a finite coordinate-and-label pattern: a codon has a six-bit address, adjacent codons determine local faces, singleton label corners can be isolated, and three-point label classes can be compared as finite subsets of $Q_6$. The verified boundary set has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that this set is a punctured-cube boundary with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite contact is sharper than a verbal description of codon degeneracy: it fixes the thirteen displayed codons, the sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and the four local square faces used by the boundary computation.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum row attaches a numerical invariant to the same finite object. The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic residual and radial residual coincide in the row, both equal to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the polynomial check is zero to the recorded numerical tolerance. The spectral radius reported for the tolerance check is again $0.6752479308830213$. This makes the row a finite spectral certificate for the local codon tile: the full module $M$ has the larger recorded radius $\lambda_M$, while the boundary subset $R$ has radius $\lambda_R$, and the auxiliary scalar $\mu$ records the radial normalization used by the computation. The interpretation is deliberately modest. These numbers distinguish a finite code-table pattern; they do not make the pattern a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Shape significance against label-preserving tests.}
The null rows make the local pattern nontrivial without enlarging its biological scope. In the shape-significance run with seed $42$, the experiment evaluated $N=2000$ samples after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. Exactly one sampled object matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap test found $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserving amino-acid class evaluated $N=2000$ cases with observed $|M|=20$. It had zero exact shape matches, seven lower-tail cases at or below the observed module size, exact-shape value $0.0$, and lower-tail value $0.0035$. These rows say that the displayed geometry is uncommon under the recorded finite randomizations. They do not say that the geometry causes translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or any cross-organism necessity.

\paragraph{Recovery checks.}
Two leave-one-out rows measure how much of the code-layer pattern is recoverable from the finite tile. In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ tables were evaluated against $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$ and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The recorded rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$. In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count is $0$, the boundary count is $5$, the predictable count is $8$, the high-coverage count is $8$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$. These are code-read statistics only: they measure finite recovery of labels or boundary positions from the displayed codon table, not synthesis of proteins or realization of cellular behavior.

\paragraph{Family and module resolution.}
The family-level row splits the leave-one-code-out behavior into five families. The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate. The module-classification row accounts for all thirteen boundary codons with no missing, extra, or unassigned entries: $R$ has size $13$, assigned count $13$, extra module codons $\varnothing$, missing-from-modules $\varnothing$, and unassigned codons $\varnothing$. The six named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification. This closes the finite accounting of the boundary row: all thirteen codons are named, all are assigned to one of the six modules, and the spectrum is attached to the same finite coordinate object.

\paragraph{Local reality contact and scope.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard-code label table. It supports codon-to-label readback and local split checks at the code layer. It does not observe ribosomal translation, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Therefore the chapter records a finite local boundary phenomenon in the standard-code table: a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary with face-vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, nontrivial null-model behavior, and complete module accounting. Any assertion beyond code-layer label readback would require a separate contact designed for that higher layer.
