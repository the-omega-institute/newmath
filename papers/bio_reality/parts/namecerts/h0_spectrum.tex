\subsection{Window-six spectral record for the standard codon table}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record treats the standard genetic-code table as a finite code-layer contact: each codon is read as a six-bit point in the codon cube $Q_6$, and the curated table supplies only the amino-acid or stop label attached to that point. The object under inspection is therefore not translation dynamics and not molecular structure. It is the finite labelled cube obtained by placing the standard-code labels on the $64$ codon positions and asking whether the window-six coordinate presentation exposes a local boundary-tile pattern. The verified distinguished boundary row is the set
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The row audit gives $|R|=13$, and the same row is identified as a punctured cube with face-vector
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the primary finite claim is not merely that thirteen codons were selected, but that the selected codons carry a small cubical incidence profile inside $Q_6$.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The spectral row records three numerical parameters attached to the local tile calculation. The two reported radii are
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,
\end{aligned}$$
and the radial coupling parameter is
$$\begin{aligned}
\mu=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The cubic relation is numerically closed to floating tolerance: both the reported cubic residual and the radial-polynomial residual are
$$\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row reports the spectral radius in tolerance as $0.6752479308830213$, agreeing with $\lambda_M$. These numbers are the finite spectral content of the packet: they say that the local codon tile has a stable numerical spectrum under the stated coordinate construction. They do not say that the spectrum is a biochemical force, a selection law, or a universal principle of organisms.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite null rows.}
A separate finite null audit compares the observed tile shape against sampled alternatives. In the shape-significance run with seed $42$, the audit used $N=2000$ samples and required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, with $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$. The corresponding empirical probabilities are
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null row gives a stricter comparison. With $N=2000$ and seed $42$, it reports an observed $M$-tile size of $20$, no exact shape match, and $7$ sampled rows in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size. The recorded probabilities are
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
These null rows support the narrower statement that the observed window-six tile is unusual among the sampled finite alternatives. They do not license promotion from code labels to a claim about translation realization, folded form, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Module decomposition of the boundary row.}
The boundary row is also partitioned into six named codon modules, and the audit records that the classification is complete. The assigned modules are
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The row reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. It also records that every module has either predictable or boundary status. This matters because the spectral row is not an isolated scalar coincidence: the same finite boundary set admits a complete codon-module accounting.

\paragraph{Predictive checks inside the code layer.}
The leave-one-code-out audit evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the above-baseline hit rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid codes, $1.0$ for the candidate-other family, $1.0$ for mitochondrial codes, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate codes, across $5$ families. The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$ with the threshold flag also $1.0$. These numbers make the code-layer readback test sharp: after removing one code or one codon, the local tile and its boundary still recover a high fraction of the finite targets relative to the recorded baseline.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, understood as a finite codon-to-label bridge. It confirms that codons have the listed amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code, and it permits local split checks in the labelled $Q_6$ display. It does not observe ribosomal translation, tRNA charging, peptide folding, structural order, energetic admissibility, organismal function, or a cross-organism law. Consequently, the conclusion of this subsection is deliberately local: the standard-code label table, when placed in window-six coordinates, contains a thirteen-codon boundary set with punctured-cube incidence, a stable spectral row with $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, rare finite-shape behavior under the recorded null rows, and complete module accounting over the thirteen boundary codons. Any stronger biological statement requires an additional contact that directly measures the stronger layer.
