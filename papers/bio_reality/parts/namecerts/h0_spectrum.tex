\subsection{Window-Six Spectrum for the Standard-Code Boundary Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed label contact.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The internal carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, while the observed labels come from a curated standard genetic-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label. This chapter therefore treats the table as a code-layer contact: it can check codon-label readback and local label splits, but it does not by itself observe translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Any statement crossing one of those boundaries would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. The verified row $h0.R.cardinality.13$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\qquad |R|=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent punctured-cube row $h0.R.punctured.cube$ returns the same codon list and the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is not merely a count: it has a finite incidence shape inside the six-coordinate cube. The supplied data do not include a codon-by-codon membership list for $M$, but the null-model rows record the observed size $|M|=20$ and compare its shape against random alternatives.

\paragraph{Shape tests.}
The shape-significance row $h0.M.shape.significance$ used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. It reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, showing that the finite search space around the tested condition is highly collision-prone under the sampling procedure. Among the $2000$ samples, the exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap comparison found $25$ cases with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null row $h0.null.model.aa_class_preserving$ also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It observed $|M|=20$, no exact shape matches, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed $M$ size, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a local finite irregularity relative to the supplied null procedures. They do not assert that the cube geometry is a biological necessity.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module-classification row $h1.leave_one_codon_out.module_classification$ assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ to six modules with no extras, no missing elements, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

Each module has either a predictable or boundary status in the finite withholding audit. The partition gives a compact local organization of the observed boundary row, but it remains a classification of codon labels inside the table contact.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following code-layer claim. In the six-bit codon cube, the standard-code label table exhibits a $13$-codon boundary set with punctured-cube face-vector $(13,16,4)$, a reproducible spectral triple $(\lambda_M,\lambda_R,\mu)$, significant shape behavior under the recorded null procedures, and strong leave-out recovery across codon and code-table withholding tests. The claim stops there. It does not say that the local tile is translation itself, a folded protein structure, a physical admissibility criterion, a functional mechanism, or a universal biological law.
