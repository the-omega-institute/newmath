\subsection{Window-six spectrum for the standard-code codon table}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Codon display and observational contact.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite code-layer object.  The working carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, obtained by displaying each of the three nucleotide positions with two binary coordinates.  The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label.  That contact is sufficient for label readback and for finite tile tests inside the codon cube, but it is not a contact with translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has thirteen codons.  In the verified row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|R|=13$.  The same computation identifies this row with a punctured-cube shape whose face-count vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the boundary is not merely a list of reassignment-sensitive codons: it has a small local cubical footprint in $Q_6$.  The row consists of the AGR pair $\{AGA,AGG\}$, the singleton $AAA$, the CUN block $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, the singleton $AUA$, the SSN-side pair $\{UCA,UUA\}$, and the stop--tryptophan-side triple $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The spectrum row records three scalar values.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial equation check returns
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is also the recorded cubic-polynomial residual.  Within the numerical tolerance of the finite computation, this is a zero residual.  The spectral radius recorded for the $M$-side row is $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The finite randomization row for the $M$-shape used $N=2000$ accepted samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Only one exact shape match occurred, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null row also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that row the observed $M$-size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$.  The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite local regularity statement at the code-label layer.  They do not identify a biological cause.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The recorded rate above baseline is likewise $0.9841269841269841$.  These values indicate that the finite codon-coordinate row has strong local recovery behavior for code-label changes in the tested table family.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon tests.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records no anchors, five boundary cases, eight high-coverage closure cases, and eight predictable cases.  The recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the same value recorded for recovery above the threshold.  This test is still a code-layer test: it reads labels and local coordinate membership, not a ribosomal process or a protein structure.

\paragraph{Family stratification.}
The by-family leave-one-code-out row partitions the tested alternatives into five families.  The family hit-rate-above-baseline values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast}&:0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&:1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate}&:1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The weaker alternative-yeast value is not a failure of the boundary claim; it says that the finite local rule is not being asserted as a universal generator of all code-table variation.  The object under test is a local coordinate-and-label pattern.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete for the thirteen-codon boundary row.  It reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  The module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable case or a boundary case in the recorded classification.  This supplies finite row-level coverage for the codon groups that make up $R$.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The ready claim is that the window-six codon coordinates expose a local spectral and boundary-tile structure in the standard-code label table.  The evidence is finite: a thirteen-codon boundary row with punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, a near-zero spectral-polynomial residual, low randomization rates for shape recurrence, strong leave-one-code and leave-one-codon recovery, and complete module assignment for the boundary row.  The result is deliberately confined to code readback.  No claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, general mechanism, or universal biological law follows from these rows unless a separate observation at that higher layer is supplied.
