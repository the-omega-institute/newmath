\subsection{Window-six codon spectrum}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record uses the standard-code codon table only as a codon-to-label contact.  The internal carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where a codon is read as a finite six-bit display rather than as a biochemical mechanism.  The distinguished local boundary row is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count is therefore $|R|=13$.  Its finite cubical shape has face-vector $(13,16,4)$ in the recorded punctured-cube presentation.  These numbers are not being used as a claim about translation or physiology; they only say that, after the codon labels are placed on $Q_6$, the selected local row has a reproducible combinatorial boundary shape.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}

\begin{aligned}

The recorded spectral radius in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  Thus the spectral row is finite and numerically pinned: it is not merely a visual tile, but a local coordinate-and-spectrum observation on the codon cube with an explicit residual small enough to identify numerical agreement at the reported precision.

\paragraph{Shape tests against shuffled code labels.}
The observed $M$-shape was tested against $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled table matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$.  A second null model that preserves amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  Under that null, the exact-shape match count was $0$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These tests support the limited statement that the standard-code labels, when placed in the six-coordinate display, produce a rare local shape under the recorded randomization regimes.  They do not identify a chemical cause.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen boundary codons are completely assigned to six modules.  The $AGR$ module is $\{AGA,AGG\}$, the $AR$-lysine module is $\{AAA\}$, the $CUN$ module is $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, the isoleucine/methionine module is $\{AUA\}$, the $SSN$ module is $\{UCA,UUA\}$, and the stop/tryptophan module is $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  The classification row reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  This is the local reason the row can be discussed as a boundary tile rather than as a loose list: every selected codon has a finite module address.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of interpretation.}
The only contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table: codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact tests code readback and local label splitting, but it does not observe a ribosome, a folded protein, a thermodynamic admissibility condition, a biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently the row supports a narrow claim: window-six codon coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary-and-spectrum pattern in the standard-code label table.  It does not follow that DNA must obey this geometry as a law, that translation is explained by the tile, that amino-acid label strings determine folded structure, or that cross-organism behavior has been established.  Any claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate contact with observations at that layer.
