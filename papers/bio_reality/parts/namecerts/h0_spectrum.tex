\paragraph{Claim packet.}
The packet $h0.spectrum$ records a finite code-layer observation on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The object under inspection is the standard-code codon table, read only as a curated assignment from codon strings to amino-acid or stop labels.  The six-bit display is used as a coordinate carrier for local tile membership, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The claim is not that the genome, translation apparatus, or folded proteins are generated by this coordinate system.  The claim is narrower: in this display, the observed code labels expose a reproducible local boundary tile whose spectral data are numerically stable and whose scope remains confined to label readback.

\paragraph{Local scope.}
All evidence in this packet is tied to the curated standard-code table as a codon-to-label contact.  The contact can test code-layer label readback and local codon-tile splits.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or any claim that DNA must obey this coordinate geometry as a law of nature.  An amino-acid label sequence is not a protein fold.  A local codon tile is not a translation apparatus.  Cross-organism wording, CUN/UUR notation, or tRNA-Leu terminology would require its own organism-matched contact before it could be used outside this code-read layer.
