\subsection{Window-six codon spectrum}
\label{sec:h0-spectrum-window-six-codon-spectrum}

\paragraph{Coordinate object.}
The record studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed through the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is treated as a finite display point, not as a biochemical mechanism.  The relevant distinctions are the six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The observed labels come from the curated standard-code table: the table assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label, and the present analysis only asks how that label table is organized when pulled back to the $Q_6$ window.  The readback layer is therefore code-level label readback, not translation dynamics.

\paragraph{Distinguished boundary set.}
The finite row identifies a boundary set $R$ of size $13$.  The verified codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This same row is also reported as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector $(13,16,4)$.  The point count gives the singleton and three-point label phenomena a concrete finite support: there are thirteen codon positions in the observed boundary set, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies in the induced tile, and four two-dimensional faces.  The statement is local.  It says that the standard-code label table leaves a recognizable finite trace in the six-bit window; it does not say that the geometry is a law of biology.

\paragraph{Spectral finite row.}
The spectral row gives three numerical parameters:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports the radial cubic residual as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, with the same value listed for the cubic-polynomial check.  At the precision of the finite computation, the displayed parameters satisfy the radial cubic relation used by the row.  The quantity $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ is also recorded as the spectral radius in tolerance.  Thus the spectrum is not merely a verbal description of the tile: it is a finite numerical invariant attached to the windowed codon-label split.

\paragraph{Shape significance against shuffled controls.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ sampled controls with seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the controls, the exact shape appeared once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  A high-overlap threshold at Jaccard at least $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  These numbers are small enough to mark the observed $Q_6$ tile as a non-generic arrangement under that null, while still leaving the interpretation at the code-label layer.  The row is a finite comparison against sampled alternatives, not a demonstration of causal necessity.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving control.}
A second null keeps amino-acid class information in the sampling design.  In that row, the observed $M$ size is $20$, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  The exact-match count is $0$, so the reported class-preserving exact-shape value is $0.0$.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving $p=0.0035$ for the lower-tail event.  This control is important because it separates the tile from a trivial class-count artifact: even when the shuffle respects amino-acid class structure, the observed finite shape remains uncommon in the sampled population.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary rises to $0.9841269841269841$.  The same $0.9841269841269841$ is recorded as the hit rate above baseline.  By family, the hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, over $5$ families.  These are recovery statistics for code-table labels under a finite leave-out test.  They do not turn the local code window into a universal account of translation.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$ and threshold comparison $1.0$.  The module classification is complete for the thirteen codons in $R$: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ are assigned, and there are no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable case or a boundary case.  This makes the local tile useful as a finite code-read diagnostic: it organizes the recurrent codons into named modules without requiring any claim about folded structure or cellular realization.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite evidence supports the following chapter-level reading.  The six-bit codon cube exposes a local boundary-tile pattern in the curated standard-code label table.  The pattern has a verified thirteen-codon boundary set, a punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, a spectral row with $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, and nontrivial behavior under two sampled nulls.  The leave-out rows then show that the same local organization has strong label-recovery behavior across code tables and codon modules.  The chapter therefore records a finite code-read phenomenon: codon labels, when placed in the $Q_6$ window, concentrate into a small boundary tile whose spectrum and recovery behavior are measured directly.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality.  The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code label table.  That contact observes codon-to-label assignments; it does not observe ribosomal implementation, organism-specific expression context, three-dimensional protein structure, thermodynamic feasibility, or downstream function.  Any claim crossing into those layers would require a separate contact with measurements at that layer.  Until such a contact is attached, the correct status of the result is a local code-read spectrum for the standard-code table, with higher biological interpretations deliberately withheld.
