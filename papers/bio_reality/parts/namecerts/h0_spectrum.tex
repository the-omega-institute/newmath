\subsection{Spectral Boundary of the Window-Six Codon Tile}
\label{sec:h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim $\mathrm{h0.spectrum}$ records a finite spectral signature attached to a window-six view of the standard-code codon table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the three codon positions after choosing the usual two-bit nucleotide coding.  The relevant distinctions are not the full biophysical process of translation, but the finite code-layer features visible inside this carrier: a local tile face, singleton label corners, three-point label classes, coordinate closure, the induced spectrum, and the adjacency relation used for the numerical test.  The biological object in contact with the construction is only the curated standard-code codon table, namely a table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  In codon notation the finite row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},
\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object under discussion is a concrete finite subset of codons, not an unspecified geometric metaphor.  The row contains the four $\mathrm{CUN}$ codons, the three stop-related codons $\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}$, the two $\mathrm{AGR}$ codons, the single $\mathrm{AUA}$ codon, the single $\mathrm{AAA}$ codon, and the two-codon $\mathrm{SSN}$ edge fragment $\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}$.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The numerical spectrum attached to the local tile gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cubic residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the same value is reported as the radial polynomial residual.  This is numerically zero at the precision of the finite computation.  The spectral radius entry is $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$ in the supplied row.  The claim is therefore a finite spectral observation: the coordinate tile determines a distinguished radius and a companion boundary radius, with the cubic check closing to numerical tolerance.

\paragraph{Shape statistics.}
The shape test was run with $N=2000$ sampled comparison rows using seed $42$.  The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the $2000$ accepted comparison rows there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a narrow statement: the observed local shape is rare under the stated finite sampling procedure.  They do not turn the tile into a biological law, because the comparison is still performed at the codon-label level.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A stricter null model preserving amino-acid class also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that run the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact\ shape}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row therefore remains unusual even after retaining amino-acid-class information in the comparison.  Since the supplied finite data give the size of $M$ but not a codon-by-codon membership list for $M$, this chapter cites $|M|=20$ and the spectral radius $\lambda_M$, while treating the explicit codon list as available only for $R$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ predictable codons, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These tests say that the finite coordinate pattern is predictive for held-out code-layer labels in this dataset.  They do not say that a ribosome, folded protein, chemical stability condition, or organism-level function has been reconstructed.

The family-level leave-one-code-out row separates $5$ families.  The family hit-rate-above-baseline values are

\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{candidate\ other} &\mapsto 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &\mapsto 1.0,\\

$$
The module classification covers all $13$ codons of $R$ with no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\

Each listed module has either predictable status or boundary status in the supplied classification.  This gives a finite decomposition of the boundary row into named codon modules, and it is the main code-layer readout of the claim.

\paragraph{Contact with the code table.}
The sole reality contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table.  Its observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  Its resolution is codon label readback.  Consequently the contact can test the code-read layer and the local codon-tile label split, but it cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or function realization.  The table is a label bridge only: it carries the finite codon-to-label assignments needed for the spectral and module rows, but it contains no observation of protein folding, no chemical admissibility certificate, no cellular mechanism, and no functional assay.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The correct reading of $\mathrm{h0.spectrum}$ is therefore deliberately limited.  The window-six coordinate system exposes a rare local boundary-tile pattern in the standard-code label table.  The spectrum, the punctured-cube face-vector, the $13$-codon row $R$, the $20$-element $M$ size, the p-values, and the leave-one-out recovery statistics all belong to the code-read layer.  They justify a finite statement about codon-label geometry.  They do not justify a statement that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological necessity, that amino-acid label strings determine folded protein structure, that the row is physically admissible in any separate biochemical sense, that a function is realized, that translation has been mechanistically reconstructed, or that the local tile is universal across biology.  Any such promotion would require an additional contact measuring the higher layer directly.
