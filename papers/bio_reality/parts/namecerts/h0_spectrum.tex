\paragraph{Finite object under inspection.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer. The coordinate carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with a codon displayed as a finite coordinate word and then compared with the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The evidence row does not assert that the cube is a biological mechanism. It says that a window-six coordinate description exposes a finite local tile, and that the label table supplies the reality contact needed to name which codons carry which labels.

The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the finite row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records that this set has the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object is not merely a count of codons. It has a local cubical shape: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces after the relevant puncture is imposed.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The curated standard-code table is the sole reality contact used here. It supplies codon-to-label assignments and permits local split checks in the six-bit coordinate display. It does not observe translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Amino-acid label sequences remain label sequences unless another contact observes a structure or function-bearing object. Cross-organism wording, tRNA notation, and module names likewise remain code-layer descriptors unless their own matched external records are supplied.

The claim is therefore ready in a restricted sense. The finite rows certify a local spectral and cubical boundary pattern for the standard-code codon table: $R$ has size $13$, has punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, satisfies the recorded spectral residual to numerical tolerance, is rare under the tested null families, and is completely covered by the six listed modules. The result is a finite code-read regularity with explicit scope limits, not a biological necessity theorem and not a promotion from codon labels to protein structure.
