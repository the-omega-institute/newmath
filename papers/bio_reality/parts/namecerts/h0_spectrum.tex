\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or a stop label, and the present packet only tests whether a local codon-tile pattern is visible at that label-read level. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes treated as finite distinctions. The observed boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The Monte Carlo row for the $M$ shape uses $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Only $1$ sampled object matched the exact shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap count was $25$ at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information. In that model, with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact shape count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These rows support a code-layer statement: the displayed local geometry is unusually specific under the tested finite nulls. They do not identify a biochemical cause.
