\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record starts from the codon cube $Q_6$, read as a six-coordinate display for the $64$ RNA codons after choosing the usual two-bit presentation of each base.  The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code label table: each codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label.  This contact is adequate for checking code-layer label readback and local split geometry, but it is not an observation of translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, realized function, mechanism, or universality.  The object tested in this row is therefore a finite labelled cube, not a global law of biology.

The singleton-corner and three-point-class distinctions remain code-label distinctions: they are positions and label classes in the standard table, not structural or functional assertions about molecules.

\paragraph{Shape tests.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled comparison rows with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among those samples, the exact shape occurred once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  A high-overlap threshold at Jaccard at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  A second null that preserves amino-acid class structure found $0$ exact matches in $2000$ trials, reported $p=0.0$ for exact shape, and placed the observed size $|M|=20$ in a lower tail with $7$ samples at or below it, giving $p=0.0035$.  These finite rows support the claim that the observed code-layer tile is uncommon under the sampled nulls.  They still do not replace the curated table as a reality contact; the labels remain imported from the standard-code table and the computation only tests their induced finite geometry.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The row is ready as a BioReality code-read record because every cited number is finite, table-bound, and reproducible inside the supplied packet.  Its negative scope is equally part of the statement.  The standard-code contact observes codon-to-label assignment only.  From the spectral radius, the punctured cube, the $13$-codon boundary, the $20$-element observed $M$ size, the sampled $p$-values, or the leave-one-out rates, one may not infer translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.
