\subsection{Window-six spectrum for the standard-code boundary tile}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}
\paragraph{Finite carrier and label contact.}
The record uses the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ as its finite carrier. A codon is displayed as a six-bit coordinate, while its biological label is supplied only by the curated standard-code table. The coordinate side can discuss adjacency, local faces, closures, singleton corners, three-point label classes, and spectral data. The label side is a separate contact: it states that a standard-code codon is assigned to an amino-acid or stop label. Thus the object under test is not translation itself, but a finite readout of code-layer labels on $64$ codon positions.

\paragraph{Distinguished boundary row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation the observed row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is also recorded as a punctured-cube shape with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite geometric signal used by the chapter. It says that the listed codons form a local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ square faces after the relevant puncture. It does not say that this tile is a law of biology, nor that organisms must implement it as a mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under coordinate-preserving probes.}
The shape row was compared against $2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. Among these, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ cases with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite comparison is not being summarized as an informal impression. It is a concrete row-level test: the observed boundary shape is uncommon in the sampled coordinate alternatives.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null row.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class data. In that test $N=2000$, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$. The corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.

$$
This strengthens the local reading: even when the null model keeps class information fixed, the exact boundary shape does not appear in the sampled rows, and the lower-tail event remains small. The conclusion is still finite and local. It supports a code-layer boundary pattern, not a biological necessity theorem.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out and module closure.}
For leave-one-codon-out recovery, the row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. Every listed module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification. This is the most concrete finite form of the local claim: the same $13$ codons that carry the punctured-cube shape also admit a complete module accounting.

\paragraph{Local conclusion and boundary of use.}
The chapter therefore records a finite code-layer phenomenon: the six-coordinate codon display isolates a $13$-codon boundary tile, the tile has face-vector $(13,16,4)$, it carries the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, and it remains informative in the reported leave-one-code-out and leave-one-codon-out probes. The curated standard-code table is enough to read codon labels and test the local split. It is not enough to infer translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, universal mechanism, or organism-level necessity. Any such higher-layer claim requires a distinct observation source aimed at that layer.
