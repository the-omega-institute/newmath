\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed label contact.}

The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and this table is used only as a label readback at the code layer.  The local distinction under inspection is the boundary set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified row count is $|R|=13$.  The same finite set is also recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$

The surrounding distinguished set $M$ is observed with size $20$ in the amino-acid-class preserving null experiment; no stronger biological status is inferred from that cardinality alone.

These rows support a finite significance statement about the local codon tile and its class-preserving alternatives.  They do not identify a biochemical cause, a translational mechanism, or a universal constraint on genetic codes.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The contact supplies codon-to-label assignments and supports local split checks at the code-read layer.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, molecular folding, structural order, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Amino-acid label sequences read from the table are still labels; they are not folded proteins.  Cross-organism wording, CUN/UUR notation, or tRNA-Leu phrasing must remain on a separate organism-matched contact route before it can carry biological or structural force.  Thus the packet is ready as a finite code-layer spectral and boundary record, while every higher-layer statement remains blocked unless an independent contact supplies that layer.
