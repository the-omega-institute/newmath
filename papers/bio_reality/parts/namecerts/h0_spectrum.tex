\subsection{Window-Six Spectrum of the Standard-Code Local Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
This record concerns the standard genetic-code label table viewed only at the code-read level. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with one vertex for each codon display. The internal distinctions used here are coordinate position, closure under the local rule, spectral size, and the relation between a singleton label corner and a three-point label class. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated standard-code table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or stop label. That contact permits codon-to-label readback and local split checks, but it does not itself observe translation events, folded structures, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, or biological universality.

\paragraph{The boundary set.}
The verified boundary row isolates a set $R$ of thirteen codons:
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The cardinality certificate gives $|R|=13$. The punctured-cube computation gives the same codon list and records the face-vector signature
$$\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the observed finite row is not merely a named list of codons; it is also a cubical boundary pattern with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-dimensional faces in the induced local complex.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral computation attached to this same window-six object gives
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same residual is reported for the cubic polynomial check. The recorded spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$, matching $\lambda_M$ at the displayed precision. The interpretation is finite and local: the row supplies a stable spectral measurement for the code-table tile, not a claim that the geometry enforces biological realization.

\paragraph{Shape significance against shuffled label tables.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$. It needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. Among those samples, the exact shape match count was $1$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}$$
The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
These values support the statement that the observed local tile is uncommon under that finite null model. They do not promote the tile to a law of translation or structure; they only say that this code-read geometry is a low-frequency finite-row outcome under the sampled comparison.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null comparison.}
A second null model preserved amino-acid class data. It again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. The observed size of the $M$-set was $20$. The exact-shape match count was $0$, so the reported exact-shape probability was $0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
The row therefore remains unusually constrained even when the comparison respects amino-acid class information. Since the supplied finite data give the size of $M$ but not an explicit codon list for $M$, this chapter uses $M$ only through its reported size and spectral value.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, while the rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the finite hit rates above baseline were
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\ yeast} &: 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\ plastid} &: 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\ other} &: 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &: 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\ ciliate} &: 1.0.
\end{aligned}$$
This supports a code-layer recovery claim for the finite comparison set. It still remains a claim about codon labels and local boundary membership, not about protein folding, physical chemistry, or organism-level function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The threshold comparison also reports $1.0$. This means that, inside the finite test row supplied here, every target codon in the evaluated leave-one-codon-out condition is recovered by the local closure-or-boundary criterion. The result is strong for the declared code-read task, but it has no independent contact with translation realization or folded protein structure.

\paragraph{Module partition of the thirteen boundary codons.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons of $R$ and leaves no extra or missing codons. The six modules are
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The certificate reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from modules, and no unassigned codons. Hence the finite boundary set is completely accounted for by the displayed module partition.

\paragraph{Scope of the conclusion.}
The conclusion is that the standard-code table, when read as a six-coordinate codon display, contains a reproducible local tile with a thirteen-codon boundary set, face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, together with low finite null probabilities and strong leave-one-out recovery behavior. The same conclusion explicitly stops at the code-read boundary. A codon label is not a translation event. A label sequence is not a folded structure. A local boundary pattern is not physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or a universal law. Any such stronger statement would require a distinct empirical contact that is absent from the finite row recorded here.
