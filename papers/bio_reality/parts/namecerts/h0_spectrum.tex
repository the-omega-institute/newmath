\subsection{Window-six spectrum for a local standard-code tile}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.} The record studies the standard-code codon table only at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as the $64$ codon displays formed from three nucleotide positions with four possibilities at each position. The row under inspection is not a claim about translation physics or protein realization. It is a finite coordinate-and-label observation: a local window-six geometry is compared with the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The contact used here therefore supplies label readback only. It does not supply a ribosome model, a folding experiment, a thermodynamic admissibility test, a functional assay, a universal mechanism, or a law governing all biological codes.

\paragraph{Boundary row.} The boundary subset $R$ has size $13$. In the verified row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local tile has thirteen displayed codon vertices, sixteen displayed codon-neighbor edges, and four displayed square faces in this finite representation. The list is deliberately kept at codon-label resolution. It says that these thirteen codons occupy the distinguished local boundary row in the six-bit display; it does not say that the codons cause a structural fold, implement a physical pathway, or force a biological phenotype.

\paragraph{Shape significance.} Against the reported finite sampling null, the observed shape is rare. With $N=2000$ sampled rows, seed $42$, and experiment run $0b267c88f8e4$, the exact shape occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold also remains nontrivial: there were $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. These figures matter because they show that the finite tile is not being described merely as a hand-picked picture. It is a particular local codon-label shape whose exact geometry and high-overlap neighborhood are measured under a concrete finite sampling procedure.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null row.} A second null keeps amino-acid label classes fixed while testing whether the observed $M$-shape is reproduced. In $N=2000$ trials, again with seed $42$, the exact match count was $0$. The row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035,\\
|M|_{\mathrm{observed}}&=20.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$. Thus, even after preserving amino-acid class information, the observed local size-and-shape row remains exceptional in the finite comparison. This supports the statement that the standard-code labels make contact with a distinctive local tile, while still staying strictly within the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Local interpretation and boundary.} The scientific content of this chapter is therefore narrow and testable. A curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label observations. On those observations, the window-six display exposes a thirteen-codon boundary row with face vector $(13,16,4)$, spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$, rare-shape sampling values $0.0005$ and $0.0125$, class-preserving null values $0.0$ and $0.0035$, and complete module coverage of all thirteen boundary codons. No claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from it unless a separate empirical contact supplies that higher-layer observation.
