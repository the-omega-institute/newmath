\subsection{Window-Six Spectrum for the Standard-Code Boundary Tile}
\label{subsec:h0-spectrum-window-six-standard-code-boundary-tile}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate packet.}
The record studied here places the standard-code codon table on the finite codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is displayed by six binary coordinates and the biological contact is only the curated codon-to-label table.  The intended observation is local: a window-six coordinate system can expose a boundary tile inside the codon cube, but the amino-acid or stop labels are supplied by the curated table and are not generated as a biological mechanism by the coordinate system.  The distinguished boundary row is the set $R$ of thirteen codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit records $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons are also reported as the punctured-cube row.  Its cell count, edge count, and two-face count are
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a named codon list: it is a finite subcomplex of the six-bit codon display with a specified combinatorial boundary shape.

At this numerical resolution the residual is effectively zero for the finite computation, while the spectral radius in tolerance is recorded as $0.6752479308830213$.  The mathematical content of the row is therefore a finite spectral comparison between the larger local object $M$ and the boundary row $R$, not a claim that spectra explain translation or protein behavior.

\paragraph{Shape statistics.}
The supplied randomized comparisons make the boundary-tile signal concrete.  In the shape-significance run with seed $42$, there were $N=2000$ samples, $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, and $55377$ duplicate retries.  Exactly one sampled object matched the observed shape, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also at $N=2000$ and seed $42$, records observed $|M|=20$, exact-match count $0$, lower-tail count $7$, exact-shape probability $0.0$, and lower-tail probability $0.0035$.  These numbers support the claim that the observed local shape is not a typical draw under the stated finite nulls.  They do not identify a causal law for life, a folding rule, or a universal coding principle.

The classification audit reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  The leave-one-codon-out row records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  This is a finite internal stability statement about the codon-label tile: when one codon is hidden, the local closure-and-boundary bookkeeping recovers the tested row within the reported criterion.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for code-layer readback and for checking a local split in the codon-label table.  It is not a contact with translation dynamics, ribosomal realization, molecular admissibility, folded structure, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently the row supports the narrower statement that window-six codon coordinates reveal a finite local boundary tile with a stable spectral and combinatorial profile.  Any statement about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require an additional independent observation targeted at that higher layer.
