\subsection{Spectral read of the window-six codon tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The packet records a finite code-layer observation for the standard genetic-code table. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the three nucleotide positions. The observed object is not a molecule, a ribosome, a folding pathway, or a phenotype. It is the curated codon-to-label table, used only at the level of amino-acid and stop-label readback. Within that scope, the row isolates a local boundary tile: a distinguished set $R$ of reassignment-facing codons, its punctured-cube incidence shape, a complementary label-supported window $M$ of size $20$, and a spectral pair attached to the induced local relation.

\paragraph{Finite codon support.}
The verified reassignment-facing set has cardinality $13$. The codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies the punctured-cube shape by the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the code-layer datum is not merely a list of exceptional codons. It has a small cubical incidence pattern: thirteen vertices, sixteen local one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional square faces inside the six-bit codon display. The row also records an observed $M$-window size of $20$ for the shape-significance comparison. The supplied finite table gives the size and test statistics for $M$, but not an explicit codon-by-codon listing of $M$.

The scientific content of this row is therefore narrow but concrete: the observed code-label window supports a stable finite spectral signature for the local tile, with the $R$ subpattern carrying the smaller radius $0.4758352843197002$.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The shape signal was tested against two finite randomized baselines. In the unconstrained comparison with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run found $25$ cases with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite null space was being sampled under a strong distinctness constraint rather than by a loose independent draw. In the amino-acid-class-preserving comparison, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$. The corresponding values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the modest conclusion that the local tile is not a typical draw under the recorded finite controls. They do not establish a causal biochemical principle.

The family split keeps the same finite character: five families were recorded, with above-baseline hit rates $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families. The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. These checks say that the local boundary description is useful for code-label recovery in the recorded finite panels.

\paragraph{Reality contact and limit of inference.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is sufficient for code-label readback and for checking the local split described above. It is not a contact with translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism, or a universal biological rule. None of those higher-layer claims follows from the window-six geometry, the spectral residual, the randomized comparisons, or the recovery rates unless a separate observation is supplied for the relevant layer. In particular, amino-acid label sequences are still label sequences in this chapter; they are not folded protein structures, functional assays, or organism-level mechanisms.
