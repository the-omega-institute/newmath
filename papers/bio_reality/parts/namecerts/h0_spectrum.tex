\subsection{Window-six spectral boundary in the standard codon table}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Record.}
This section records a finite window-six observation on the standard-code codon table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the three nucleotide positions.  The finite distinctions used here are a six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The claim is not that biology is generated by the cube.  The claim is narrower: once the curated standard-code labels are placed on the codon cube, the local boundary subset has a definite finite shape and a measured spectral signature.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only direct contact used in this section is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  It tests code-layer readback and local label splitting.  It does not test translation as a process, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality.  Any statement crossing one of those layers requires a separate observation of that layer.  Consequently, the window-six calculation below is a code-read calculation over a finite labeled table, not a claim about molecular realization.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The verified boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  The listed codons are
$$\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same row verifies that this set is the punctured-cube boundary used by the calculation.  Its face-count summary is
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the object under discussion is not an inferred continuum or a biological law; it is a thirteen-codon subset with sixteen verified edges and four verified two-faces inside the finite codon display.

\paragraph{Spectrum.}
The spectral row gives the three numerical parameters
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the cubic residual is the same value.  The reported spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  At the finite precision of the run, the local window therefore behaves as a stable spectral boundary: the measured value of $\lambda_M$ is reproduced by the polynomial check to a residual essentially at numerical roundoff scale.  The interpretation is deliberately local.  A stable spectral check over the code labels does not by itself identify a translation mechanism or a protein-structure principle.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The null comparison for the boundary shape used $N=2000$ accepted samples, with $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries under seed $42$.  In that run there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$.  There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  These two finite numbers say that the observed tile shape is uncommon under the tested sampling route.  They do not say that the shape is forced by life, chemistry, or evolution; they only say that, relative to the stated finite null process, the observed code-label tile is not a typical draw.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class-preserving null.}
A second null comparison preserved amino-acid class information.  It again used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The observed size of $M$ was $20$.  The exact-shape count was $0$, giving an exact-shape value reported as $0.0$.  The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p=0.0035$.  This strengthens the code-layer observation: even when the comparison is constrained by amino-acid class, the observed arrangement remains small-tail and exact-shape absent in the sampled finite row.  Since the row contains only label information, it still cannot certify folding, function, or physical feasibility.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code readback.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Grouped by family, the row reports $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above the baseline comparison.  These numbers support the use of the local tile as a code-label predictor across the evaluated finite table collection, while leaving the biological interpretation at the readback layer.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon readback.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison also reports $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all $13$ codons in $R$: $AGR=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  The classification row records $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no missing module codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The finite closure statement is therefore complete for this boundary set: every codon in the thirteen-row boundary has a module assignment, and every module has a predictable or boundary role.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The verified rows support the following bounded conclusion.  In the six-coordinate codon display, the standard-code labels determine a thirteen-codon boundary subset with face vector $(13,16,4)$, a spectral value $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, a companion value $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and a polynomial residual near $6.23\times 10^{-12}$ in magnitude.  The same finite record gives low sampled probabilities for exact or near-exact shape recurrence under the tested nulls, and it gives strong leave-one-code and leave-one-codon recovery rates.  The result is ready as a code-read chapter because all cited claims are finite table checks.  The chapter does not assert translation, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality; those layers remain outside the present contact.
