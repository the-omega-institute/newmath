\subsection{Window-Six Spectrum of the Standard-Code Boundary Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The claim recorded here is a code-layer statement about the standard genetic-code table.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of the three nucleotide positions.  The observable contact is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Within that contact, the construction isolates a local boundary tile $R$ and a complementary label-structured region $M$, then records a spectral fingerprint of the split.  This is not a claim about ribosomal translation, folded protein geometry, energetic feasibility, organismal function, evolutionary necessity, or a universal biological mechanism.  Those stronger layers would require separate observations not supplied by the code table.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The verified boundary set has exactly thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality row gives $|R|=13$.  The punctured-cube row gives the associated face-count signature
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local tile is not merely a list of exceptional labels; it has a finite cubical shape with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces under the window-six adjacency used by the probe.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum row records three scalars for the same finite split:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the same value is listed as the cubic residual.  At the stated numerical tolerance, this places the observed spectral radius at $0.6752479308830213$ for the $M$ side of the split.  The evidential content is finite and numerical: the code table induces a reproducible local decomposition whose spectrum satisfies the recorded cubic relation to near-zero residual.  The computation does not identify a biochemical force or a translational dynamics; it only records a spectral signature of the codon-label geometry.

\paragraph{Shape significance row.}
The null comparison for the $M$ shape used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  Because duplicate samples were rejected, the run made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  Among the accepted samples, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the narrow claim that the observed standard-code split is unusually shaped relative to the sampled coordinate-preserving comparison class.  They do not say that the shape is selected by life, required by physics, or sufficient for a protein-level conclusion.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving row.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class information and again uses $N=2000$ with seed $42$.  The observed $M$ size is $20$.  In that run, the exact-shape count is $0$, so the recorded exact-shape value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This row is important because it keeps part of the label composition fixed while still finding the observed local form rare in the sampled comparison.  The result strengthens the code-layer geometry claim but still leaves translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and universality outside the demonstrated scope.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The cross-table probe evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.19821739130434785,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the hit rate above baseline is
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline rates are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other rows, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These rows say that the local split is predictive for held-out code-table labels in the tested family grouping.  They do not convert the code table into a structure experiment or a universal law of biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row.}
The codon-level recovery row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases.  Its recovery rate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$.  The module classification covers all thirteen boundary codons with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  The six module rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The assigned count is $13$, matching $|R|=13$.  This supplies a finite audit of the boundary tile by module rather than an informal list of interesting codons.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The verified rows together support the following bounded reading.  In the window-six codon cube, the standard-code table exposes a thirteen-codon local boundary tile with punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, a reproducible spectral signature with $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, rare-shape behavior under two finite null comparisons, and strong held-out recovery at the code-label layer.  The amino-acid and stop labels are supplied by the curated code table, so the construction remains connected to observed biological annotation rather than to a purely formal alphabet.  However, the same fact also fixes the boundary of the claim: the table contains codon-to-label assignments, not a measurement of protein folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, organismal fitness, or a general mechanism of life.  Any step beyond codon-label readback must be justified by a distinct contact of the appropriate kind.
