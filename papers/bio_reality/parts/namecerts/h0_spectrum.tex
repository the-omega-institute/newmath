\subsection{Window-Six Spectrum of the Standard-Code Boundary Tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-spectrum}

\paragraph{Claim record.}
The packet \texttt{h0.spectrum} records a finite codon-coordinate observation at the code-read layer.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as a coordinate display for the sixty-four codons of the standard genetic code.  The biological contact is a curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label, and it is used here only as a label table.  The claim is therefore deliberately local.  Window-six coordinates expose a boundary-tile pattern in the code labels, while the labels themselves remain supplied by the curated table rather than by the coordinate geometry.

\paragraph{Observed boundary set.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has size $13$.  In codon notation the verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\text{AAA},\text{AGA},\text{AGG},\text{AUA},\text{CUA},\text{CUC},\text{CUG},\text{CUU},\\
&\text{UAA},\text{UAG},\text{UCA},\text{UGA},\text{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube calculation gives the same thirteen codons and records the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a cardinality report: it fixes the actual codon support, its finite adjacency content, and the four local two-faces selected by the window-six construction.  The associated module partition accounts for all thirteen codons with no missing or extra elements.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{agr}&=\{\text{AGA},\text{AGG}\},&
\text{ar\_lys}&=\{\text{AAA}\},&
\text{cun}&=\{\text{CUA},\text{CUC},\text{CUG},\text{CUU}\},\\
\text{ile\_met}&=\{\text{AUA}\},&
\text{ssn}&=\{\text{UCA},\text{UUA}\},&
\text{stop\_trp}&=\{\text{UAA},\text{UAG},\text{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification row reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, and empty lists for extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Spectral certificate.}
The spectral computation attaches three scalar values to the same finite tile.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the radial residual is the same number.  The spectral-radius row also records $0.6752479308830213$ within tolerance.  These values give a numerical signature of the finite code tile: $M$ carries the larger radius, $R$ carries the smaller boundary radius, and the residuals show that the fitted cubic relation is satisfied to numerical precision at the recorded run.  This is a finite numerical certificate of the coordinate-and-label split, not a derivation of biology from geometry.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
The shape test uses $N=2000$ sampled comparators with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and rejected $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  A second null model preserving amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that run the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The corresponding reported probabilities are $p=0.0$ for exact shape and $p=0.0035$ for the lower tail.  These rows support the statement that the observed local tile is not a typical member of the sampled null families, while still leaving the biological interpretation bounded by the data source.

\paragraph{Readback tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The row also reports the same $0.9841269841269841$ value as the hit rate above baseline.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate-other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables, across $5$ families.  The leave-one-codon-out row reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These tests show that the tile has predictive stability for code-label recovery under the finite leave-one protocols recorded here.

\paragraph{Reality contact and limitation.}
The only reality contact in this packet is the curated standard-code label table.  It can test codon-label readback and local label splits in the window-six display.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality.  The local boundary tile therefore remains a code-layer observation.  Amino-acid label sequences are not protein structures; CUN, UUR, AGR, stop, or tryptophan labels do not by themselves supply a translation experiment; and no claim about organism-wide necessity follows from the finite cube pattern.  Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite row supports the following chapter-level statement: in the six-bit codon cube, the standard-code label table selects a thirteen-codon boundary set with face-vector $(13,16,4)$, a complete six-module partition, a stable numerical spectrum with $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and statistically uncommon shape behavior under the recorded null comparisons.  The result is ready as a code-read claim.  Its force is local and finite: it concerns the geometry of curated codon labels in $Q_6$, not the physical production, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal law of biological molecules.
