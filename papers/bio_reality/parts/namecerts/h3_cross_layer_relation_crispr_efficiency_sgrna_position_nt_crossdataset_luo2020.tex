\subsection{Cross-protocol sgRNA-position rows and the f\_3-stress translation boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-luo-f3-stress-position-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record considered here is the finite cross-protocol sgRNA-position table associated with the claim
$h3.cross\_layer\_relation.crispr\_efficiency.sgrna\_position\_nt\_crossdataset\_luo2020$.  Its verified finite-row contact is a Luo-style CRISPR-efficiency panel with $10543$ Luo rows, marked as cross-protocol, and taken from a frozen Doench2016 repository source.  The run identifier is $2e9cce255c01$, and the recorded runtime is $33.705459$ seconds.  A linked Doench2016 sgRNA-position check has $5310$ rows and a GC main correlation $r=-0.0582917$ under run identifier $b6c6363256e0$.  These numbers support a concrete claim about a finite CRISPR design/readout surface: guide-position nucleotide features can be carried across a protocol boundary and audited against an observed sgRNA-efficiency response.

\paragraph{What the finite table does and does not contain.}
The table is not a ribosome-profiling table.  Its rows concern sgRNA-position and CRISPR-efficiency readout, not codon-indexed ribosome footprints, translation efficiency, matched mRNA abundance, local synonymous codon counts, or an organism-scoped wobble-weight surface.  Thus the finite CRISPR panel can witness that the sgRNA-position coordinate has a cross-protocol empirical contact, but it cannot by itself instantiate the proposed $f_3$-stress translation-survival statistic.  The proposed statistic would require rows of the following form: a coding-sequence window, an $f_3$-stress residual after amino-acid quotienting, local synonymous codon counts, a wobble-weight context with $w_c\ne w_c^{f_3}$, matched position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, matched mRNA control, and amino-acid or position-bin covariates.

\paragraph{Local mathematical boundary.}
For the translation-side packet, the survival quantity is a controlled held-out improvement of the form $S_{f3}(T_{position}\mid Z)$, where $T_{position}$ denotes a same-window translation readout and $Z$ contains amino-acid composition or position-bin controls, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA abundance, codon-count controls, coverage and mappability limits, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations.  In the present finite data, the observed CRISPR-efficiency matrix supplies neither $T_{position}$ nor the required same-window coding-sequence join.  The appropriate local verdict is therefore a separation of two empirical surfaces: sgRNA-position efficiency is observed in finite rows, while $f_3$-stress translation survival remains uncomputed for this row family.

\paragraph{Non-promotion rule.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, protein abundance, structural order, or universal biological rule follows from the verified Luo/Doench CRISPR row counts.  The CRISPR surface is an efficiency-readout contact for guide features.  A translation-layer statement would need a distinct same-organism, same-CDS, same-window join to ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency rows, with matched mRNA and codon-window controls.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights would still be insufficient as observed ribosome occupancy unless a measured or explicitly bounded readout row is also attached.

\paragraph{Audited interpretation.}
The finite evidence is positive only for the existence of a cross-protocol sgRNA-position efficiency surface: $n_{Luo}=10543$, cross-protocol status is true, and the source is frozen to the Doench2016 repository.  The linked Doench finite panel gives $n=5310$ and $r_{GC}=-0.0582917$, which records a concrete scalar control feature rather than a translation mechanism.  Consequently, the BioReality entry is ready as a bounded, self-contained chapter about a CRISPR-efficiency row surface and its refusal to promote into an $f_3$-stress translation claim without additional layer-matched data.
