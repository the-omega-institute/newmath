\subsection{Cross-protocol sgRNA position signal and the blocked f\_3-stress translation route}\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-sgrna-position-luo2020}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The chapter records a finite cross-protocol CRISPR-efficiency surface rather than a translation-realization result.  The checked Luo-side table contains $10543$ sgRNA rows and was evaluated against a frozen source declared as the Doench2016 repository, with the cross-protocol flag set to true and a recorded runtime of $33.705459$ seconds.  The linked Doench-side finite table contains $5310$ rows, with a main GC association $r=-0.0582917$ and runtime $24.6506$ seconds.  These two row counts are the usable numerical anchors for this chapter: they establish that the observed contact is a position-resolved sgRNA-efficiency comparison across protocols, not a same-CDS codon-window, wobble-weight, mRNA-control, and ribosome-footprint panel.

\paragraph{Local carrier.}
The mathematical carrier used here is a finite row set $T$ whose rows are indexed by sgRNA observations and protocol provenance.  For a row $t\in T$, the available coordinates include a guide-level efficiency readout, nucleotide-position features, protocol membership, and simple composition controls such as GC content where the source supplies them.  The cross-protocol part of the carrier is summarized by
$$
\begin{aligned}
|T_{\mathrm{Luo}}| &= 10543,\\
|T_{\mathrm{Doench}}| &= 5310,\\
r_{\mathrm{GC,Doench}} &= -0.0582917,\\
\mathrm{crossProtocol}(T_{\mathrm{Luo}},T_{\mathrm{Doench}}) &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough to support a bounded statement about whether position-indexed sgRNA nucleotide information can be transported or compared across two CRISPR-efficiency data surfaces.  It is not enough to support a statement about coding-sequence translation, because the rows are sgRNA-efficiency rows rather than codon-indexed CDS translation rows.

\paragraph{Claim tested by the finite rows.}
The finite contact tests only a CRISPR-layer relation: whether a position-resolved guide feature system has cross-dataset contact with measured sgRNA efficiency.  The Luo table gives the larger held surface, while the Doench table supplies the frozen source named by the run metadata.  The negative GC scalar in the Doench-side run is small in magnitude and is treated only as a finite control fact, not as a mechanistic directionality law.  Thus the admissible local statement is: a cross-protocol sgRNA-position claim has finite row support at $10543$ Luo rows against a $5310$-row Doench reference surface, with the recorded Doench GC association $r=-0.0582917$ available as one composition-level scalar.

\paragraph{Relation to the proposed f\_3-stress coordinate.}
The proposed $f_3$-stress survival coordinate asks for a different row type.  Its required table would need, in each same CDS window, an $f_3$-stress residual after amino-acid quotienting, local synonymous-codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight context with $w_c\ne w_c^{f_3}$ observed rather than assumed, a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, matched mRNA abundance, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The CRISPR sgRNA tables do not supply those variables.  Consequently the statistic
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)>0
\end{aligned}
$$
is not computed here.  The finite CRISPR rows can motivate a general discipline of position-resolved testing, but they do not instantiate the translation-readout matrix required for $f_3$-stress survival.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The boundary breaks if the $10543$ Luo rows or the $5310$ Doench rows are described as same-CDS ribosome-footprint rows, codon-indexed translation-efficiency rows, or direct evidence for $f_3$-stress translation survival.  It also breaks if the Doench GC scalar $r=-0.0582917$ is promoted to a general law about codon geometry, wobble asymmetry, or biological directionality.  The permissible inference remains narrower: the data surface is cross-protocol CRISPR-efficiency evidence with position-indexed guide features; it is not a translation-layer contact.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from this chapter.  No ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from these finite CRISPR rows.  Any such statement would require a separate layer-matched contact.  In particular, a later $f_3$-stress claim would need a joined same-organism and same-CDS matrix containing codon counts, wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA controls, position-resolved ribosome-footprint or bounded translation-efficiency readouts, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations.  Until that matrix exists, this chapter remains a bounded CRISPR position-efficiency record and a null boundary for the proposed $f_3$-stress translation-survival route.
