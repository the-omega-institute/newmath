\subsection{CRISPR guide-position transfer is a control example, not a translation readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-efficiency-sgrna-position-nt-crossdataset-luo2020}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The finite evidence row for this record is the cross-protocol CRISPR guide table, not a coding-sequence translation panel.  The row surface used for the Luo-side check contains $10543$ Luo guide entries, with the source frozen against the Doench2016 repository and the cross-protocol flag recorded as true.  The paired Doench-side guide-position run contributes $5310$ rows and reports a GC main-effect correlation of $-0.0582917$.  These numbers make the contact concrete: the observed object is a finite table of guide sequence positions and guide-efficiency readouts across protocols, with runtime records of $33.705459$ seconds for the Luo cross-dataset run and $24.6506$ seconds for the Doench position-nucleotide run.  The biological row is therefore an sgRNA-position row with nucleotide and protocol coordinates, not a codon-window row with ribosome occupancy, matched mRNA abundance, wobble-weight context, or amino-acid quotient residuals.

\paragraph{Local statistic and admissible meaning.}
The admissible content of the chapter is the following bounded statement.  A position-indexed nucleotide feature can be carried across the Doench-to-Luo protocol boundary as a finite CRISPR-efficiency coordinate, because the Luo run explicitly records a cross-protocol construction over $10543$ rows and names the Doench2016 repository as the frozen source.  The Doench run supplies an independent finite anchor with $5310$ rows and a small GC main-effect correlation, so the guide-position signal is not being silently replaced by a GC-only statement.  In the notation of the record, the finite guide table supports a cross-dataset guide-efficiency boundary, while the proposed $f_3$-stress translation-survival coordinate remains unattached to the necessary same-window translation data.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= 10543,\\
N_{\mathrm{Doench}} &= 5310,\\
r_{\mathrm{GC,Doench}} &= -0.0582917,\\
\mathrm{cross\_protocol} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This display is not a survival equation for $S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$.  It is only the finite audit surface available for the CRISPR guide-position claim.  The rows justify saying that a guide-position nucleotide coordinate was evaluated across a Luo/Doench protocol boundary.  They do not justify saying that an $f_3$ synonymous residual coordinate survived against ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.

\paragraph{Separation from the $f_3$ translation-survival conjecture.}
The associated vision-level conjecture asks for a stricter table: each coding-sequence window would need an $f_3$-stress residual after amino-acid quotienting, local synonymous codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight surface, matched position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency readback, matched mRNA abundance, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The CRISPR finite rows do not contain those columns.  They are guide-design rows, not ordered CDS-window rows.  Thus the Luo/Doench evidence can illustrate cross-dataset survival of a sequence-position predictor in the CRISPR layer, but it cannot be promoted to a translation-layer result.

The break condition for the $f_3$ conjecture is correspondingly local.  A positive translation-survival entry would require a held-out improvement after amino-acid composition, position-bin, GC3, CDS length, M-density, mRNA, codon-count, coverage, mappability, rank, and simpler-baseline checks.  The present finite CRISPR record has no such joined matrix.  It has a Luo row count, a Doench row count, a cross-protocol source relation, and a GC main-effect scalar.  Those are enough for a CRISPR guide-efficiency boundary chapter and not enough for a ribosome-occupancy survival chapter.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from this CRISPR guide-position table.  No protein abundance, folding state, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universal rule follows from the recorded Luo/Doench rows.  A modeled tAI or stAI surface would also not repair this gap by itself, because a modeled codon-adaptation weight is not an observed ribosome-footprint row.  To leave the boundary, a separate layer-matched contact would have to supply same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency measurements, followed by the declared controlled survival statistic.  Until that table exists, the chapter records a finite cross-protocol CRISPR-position result and a blocked promotion into $f_3$ translation survival.
