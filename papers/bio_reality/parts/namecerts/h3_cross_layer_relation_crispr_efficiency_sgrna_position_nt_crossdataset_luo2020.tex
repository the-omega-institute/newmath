\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The finite table for this record is anchored by a cross-protocol CRISPR-efficiency row set rather than by a joined translation-readout panel.  The Luo-side audit records $n_{\mathrm{Luo}} = 10543$ sgRNA rows, marks the protocol bridge as present by the Boolean value $\mathrm{cross\_protocol}=\mathrm{true}$, and identifies the frozen source for the comparison surface as the Doench2016 repository.  The corresponding Doench-side finite run contains $n_{\mathrm{Doench}} = 5310$ rows and reports a GC main-effect correlation $r_{\mathrm{GC}}=-0.0582917$.  The two run times, $33.705459$ seconds for the Luo cross-protocol pass and $24.6506$ seconds for the Doench position-nucleotide pass, are retained only as audit metadata for reproducibility of the finite computation.  They are not biological effect sizes.

\paragraph{What the rows certify.}
The row-level certificate is a finite association surface for sgRNA position and nucleotide features across a Doench-derived reference source and a Luo-sized cross-protocol panel.  Its admissible biological reading is narrow: the table can support a claim that position-indexed guide-sequence information has been carried through a cross-protocol CRISPR-efficiency comparison with explicitly recorded row counts.  In symbols, the finite support is the existence of a joined guide table $G$ with two recorded panel sizes and a cross-protocol flag,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|G_{\mathrm{Luo}}| &= 10543,\\
|G_{\mathrm{Doench}}| &= 5310,\\
\mathrm{cross\_protocol}(G) &= \mathrm{true},\\
r_{\mathrm{GC}}(G_{\mathrm{Doench}}) &= -0.0582917.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough for a bounded CRISPR-efficiency chapter about finite sgRNA position rows.  It is not enough to promote the same record into a translation-layer or f\_3-stress survival result.

\paragraph{Why the f\_3-stress reading remains blocked.}
The conjectural f\_3-stress interpretation requires a different kind of row.  It asks for coding-sequence windows carrying an f\_3-stress residual coordinate, local synonymous codon counts, a wobble-weight surface with $w_c \ne w_c^{f_3}$, position-resolved ribosome occupancy or an explicitly bounded translation-efficiency readout, matched mRNA abundance, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  None of those translation-window rows is supplied by the CRISPR finite table above.  The CRISPR table is indexed by guide-sequence and efficiency evidence; it is not a same-CDS, same-window ribosome-footprint matrix.  Therefore the survival entry
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)
\end{aligned}
$$
is not computed here.  The correct local reading is that the cross-protocol CRISPR rows are real finite data for sgRNA efficiency, while the f\_3-stress translation survival statistic remains absent.

\paragraph{Controls required before any translation statement.}
A positive f\_3-stress translation claim would require a held-out improvement after amino-acid composition, position-bin controls, $\mathrm{GC3}$, coding-sequence length, methionine-density baseline, matched mRNA abundance, codon-count controls, coverage and mappability limits, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The finite facts available here contain no p-value, no survival rank, no wobble-weight asymmetry statistic, no ribosome-occupancy matrix, no matched mRNA denominator, and no held-out translation improvement.  Consequently the strongest sound statement is a separation statement: CRISPR position-nucleotide efficiency rows exist at the recorded sizes, but they do not supply the translation-window carrier demanded by the f\_3-stress proposal.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation realization follows from this chapter.  No protein abundance, folding state, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from the sgRNA rows, from the GC correlation, from the cross-protocol flag, or from the name of the f\_3-stress packet.
