\subsection{CRISPR Position Signal as a Bounded Cross-Dataset Reality Check}\label{subsec:bior-h3-crispr-luo-crossdataset}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record concerns a finite CRISPR guide-efficiency table rather than a translation table.  Its row surface is the cross-protocol single-guide RNA position feature panel keyed by guide sequence, position-resolved nucleotide content, and measured guide activity.  The held finite contact is the Luo panel with $n=10543$ rows, recorded under a cross-protocol flag equal to true and sourced through the frozen Doench2016 repository route.  The associated Doench2016 guide-efficiency run supplies the companion finite baseline with $n=5310$ rows and a GC main-effect correlation $r=-0.0582917$.  The Luo-side runtime was $33.705459$ seconds; the Doench-side runtime was $24.6506$ seconds.  These numbers are used only as finite-row audit data: they say that a cross-dataset guide-position contact exists, not that any f$_3$-stress or translation-layer claim has been measured in those rows.

\paragraph{Local object.}
The biological object available here is a cross-dataset CRISPR efficiency readout for position-indexed guide sequence features.  Its carrier is therefore a finite matrix of guide rows and position-specific nucleotide features with a measured efficiency target.  The chapter treats this as a bounded reality contact for sequence-position effects in CRISPR activity.  It does not treat the same rows as coding-sequence windows, synonymous codon counts, wobble-weight surfaces, ribosome-footprint windows, translation-efficiency observations, mRNA controls, or amino-acid quotient residuals.  In symbols, the available row relation is a guide-position relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{CRISPR}} &= (g, p, b_{g,p}, y_g, Z_g),\\
|R_{\mathrm{Luo}}| &= 10543,\\
|R_{\mathrm{Doench}}| &= 5310,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g$ is a guide, $p$ is a guide position, $b_{g,p}$ is the base observed at that position, $y_g$ is the measured guide-efficiency response, and $Z_g$ denotes admissible guide-level controls.  This is not the same data type as a coding-sequence window row
$$
\begin{aligned}
(c, x_{f3}, w_c, T_{\mathrm{position}}, mRNA, Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
which would be needed before an $f_3$-stress translation-readout statistic could be evaluated.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset interpretation.}
The cross-protocol flag is important because it restricts the interpretation to a portability question: whether a guide-position signal survives when a Doench-style source route is used to reach the Luo finite panel.  The Luo row count $10543$ is large enough to state that the contact is not a toy identifier match.  The Doench baseline count $5310$ and the observed GC main-effect correlation $-0.0582917$ also show that the companion guide-efficiency surface contains a measurable composition axis.  Still, the finite facts do not include a list of position-wise coefficients, a held-out improvement value, a permutation $p$-value, a module class, a hit rate, or any lambda path for a fitted model.  The safe statement is therefore structural: the CRISPR contact is a cross-dataset guide-efficiency row surface with concrete sample sizes and a known GC baseline scalar, not a complete statistical survival certificate for every guide-position feature.

\paragraph{Separation from the proposed $f_3$ translation packet.}
The conjectural $f_3$ packet asks for a different row geometry: a coding-sequence window table with an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate after amino-acid quotient, local synonymous codon counts, a wobble-weight asymmetry $w_c \neq w_c^{f_3}$, matched position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency readback, matched mRNA abundance, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The CRISPR Luo and Doench guide-efficiency rows do not supply those fields.  Consequently the statistic
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
is not computed by this contact.  The available CRISPR data can support a bounded claim about position-indexed guide-efficiency portability across datasets; it cannot by itself support a claim about synonymous coding windows, wobble-weight asymmetry, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or translation-conditioned survival.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
A positive promotion would require a row join that is absent from the stated facts.  The required table would have to join, for the same organism and same coding-sequence window, ordered CDS coordinates, codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight surface, matched mRNA abundance, and codon-indexed ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency readouts.  It would then need a controlled held-out statistic after amino-acid composition or position-bin controls, GC3, CDS length, M-density, mRNA, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The present CRISPR guide-efficiency facts fail that requirement for a simple reason: guide-position nucleotides and guide activity are not ribosome-footprint rows.  Treating the Luo $10543$ rows or the Doench $5310$ rows as if they were coding-window translation rows would erase the data boundary.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
The admissible local claim is therefore narrow.  There exists a finite cross-protocol CRISPR efficiency contact in which the Luo panel contributes $10543$ guide rows and the Doench source route contributes a companion finite panel with $5310$ rows and GC main-effect scalar $r=-0.0582917$.  This supports the existence of a concrete guide-position reality surface for CRISPR efficiency testing.  It does not establish translation realization, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such higher-layer statement would need its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic at that layer.
