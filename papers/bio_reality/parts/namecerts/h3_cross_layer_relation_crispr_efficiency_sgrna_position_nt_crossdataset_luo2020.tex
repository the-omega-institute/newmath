\subsection{Cross-protocol sgRNA position records and the non-promotion boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-sgrna-position-luo2020-crossdataset}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite cross-protocol CRISPR guide-efficiency table, not a translation-readout table. The active finite contact for the named claim is the Luo 2020 sgRNA-position surface with $10543$ Luo rows, evaluated in a cross-protocol setting against a frozen source recorded as the Doench 2016 repository. The corresponding run identifier is $2e9cce255c01$, and the recorded runtime is $33.705459$ seconds. A separate linked Doench 2016 guide-efficiency run supplies $5310$ rows, a main GC association $r=-0.0582917$, and runtime $24.6506$ seconds. These numbers are enough to certify that the chapter is grounded in finite guide-level data with a cross-protocol comparison, but they are not enough to certify any ribosome-occupancy, translation-efficiency, protein-output, folding, physical-admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality claim.

\paragraph{Objects carried by a row.}
A row in this chapter is a guide-design observation keyed by guide sequence context and position-indexed nucleotide features, together with an efficiency readout from a CRISPR assay. The finite evidence used here has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= \{x_1,\ldots,x_{10543}\},\\
D_{\mathrm{Doench}} &= \{y_1,\ldots,y_{5310}\},\\
\rho_{\mathrm{GC,Doench}} &= -0.0582917.
\end{aligned}
$$
The intended cross-layer question is therefore narrow: whether position-resolved guide-sequence features retain predictive contact with measured CRISPR efficiency across a protocol boundary. The recorded value $\mathrm{cross\_protocol}=\mathrm{true}$ says that the Luo-side rows are not merely a rephrasing of the same finite assay panel. It does not say that a biological mechanism has been isolated; it only says that the row family spans a protocol separation.

\paragraph{Controlled reading of the finite evidence.}
The $10543$ Luo rows and the $5310$ Doench rows support a finite empirical interface for sgRNA efficiency. The Doench GC scalar is small in magnitude and negative, so GC content is not to be treated here as a standalone law for guide activity. At most, the finite record permits a position-aware guide-efficiency statistic of the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{guide}} &= \Delta\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathrm{position\ nucleotide\ features}\mid Z_{\mathrm{guide}}\bigr),\\
Z_{\mathrm{guide}} &= \{\mathrm{GC\ controls},\ \mathrm{protocol\ controls},\ \mathrm{simpler\ sequence\ baselines}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $S_{\mathrm{guide}}$ is a guide-efficiency survival entry only if it improves held-out prediction beyond simpler baselines. The present finite facts identify the row counts, the cross-protocol status, the frozen Doench source, and a GC scalar; they do not list a full held-out improvement value, a permutation $p$-value, a module class, a hit rate, or a fitted coefficient vector. For that reason the chapter states the finite contact conservatively: a cross-protocol guide-efficiency surface exists, while the exact survival strength is not promoted beyond the reported scalar data.

\paragraph{Relation to the proposed $f_3$-stress translation packet.}
The surrounding conjectural packet speaks about an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate in coding-sequence windows, wobble-weight asymmetry, matched mRNA control, and position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readback. None of those translation-layer rows is supplied by the Luo or Doench CRISPR efficiency facts. The sgRNA table is therefore not evidence for an $f_3$-stress translation-survival statistic. In particular, the existence of a cross-protocol CRISPR guide table does not create same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts; it does not create a wobble-weight surface; it does not create matched mRNA abundance; and it does not create codon-indexed ribosome-footprint occupancy.

\paragraph{Local admissible claim.}
The admissible claim is that a finite CRISPR guide-efficiency contact is present for position-indexed nucleotide features across a Luo-side panel of $10543$ rows and a Doench-side source with $5310$ rows. This contact is compatible with a cross-layer-relation label only in the guide-design sense: nucleotide-position features are compared with measured CRISPR efficiency across protocols. The record may be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{GuideContact} &= (D_{\mathrm{Luo}},D_{\mathrm{Doench}},\mathrm{cross\_protocol}),\\
|D_{\mathrm{Luo}}| &= 10543,\\
|D_{\mathrm{Doench}}| &= 5310,\\
\mathrm{cross\_protocol} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite empirical contact, not a universal guide-design theorem. The negative GC scalar from the Doench run is retained as a control warning: a single compositional feature should not be substituted for the position-resolved guide surface.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation claim follows from this chapter. The guide-efficiency rows do not measure ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular phenotype, biological mechanism, or a universal biological law. Such claims would require separate row-level contacts at their own layer. In particular, the $f_3$-stress translation-survival notation remains uncomputed here because the required position-resolved ribosome-footprint matrix, matched mRNA control, wobble-weight rows, codon counts, amino-acid or position-bin controls, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations are absent from the supplied finite facts.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The chapter would be overstated if it treated the cross-protocol CRISPR guide record as evidence for translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. It would also be overstated if it treated the Luo row count or the Doench GC scalar as a completed $f_3$-stress survival statistic. The record remains valid only while the CRISPR guide-efficiency contact is kept separate from the unprovided translation-window contact. A later positive translation statement would need a distinct finite panel whose rows jointly contain the residual coordinate, codon-window counts, wobble-weight context, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome or translation-efficiency readout under the declared controls.
