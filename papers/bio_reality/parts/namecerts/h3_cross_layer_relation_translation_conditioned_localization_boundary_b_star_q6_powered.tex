\subsection{Translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-indexed surface whose coordinates combine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, matched translation readouts, mRNA controls, and a protein-localization readout slot. The biological question is not whether a codon residual is itself a localization mechanism. The question is narrower: whether a residual coordinate $q_i$ can be carried through a translation readout $T_j$ before a downstream localization label $L_k$ is interpreted. The admissible readback is therefore the gated relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the direct residual-to-localization reading held outside the claim unless the translation boundary is first crossed or explicitly conditioned.

The finite run attached to this record used experiment run id \texttt{ab861f8b1269}. The classifier was a standard-library linear discriminant analysis with train-fold z-scoring and ridge shared covariance, evaluated with $5$ folds. The joined localization rows were organism-specific: $1908$ CDS rows for \textit{Danio rerio}, $10187$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $4304$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Thus the record is not an abstract universality claim; it is a finite three-organism table with unequal row support.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate test.}
The first finite question asks whether $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain held-out information about localization labels at all. In the joined localization panel, the reported change in accuracy for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ discrimination of localization ranged from $-0.008385744234800874$ to $0.045548247766761585$, with mean $0.022068418153776248$ and median $0.029042750929368033$ across $n=3$ organism summaries. The corresponding macro-AUC change ranged from $0.073410178078059$ to $0.08299262296923093$, with mean $0.07848683797295208$ and median $0.07905771287156632$. Accuracy was positive in $2$ of $3$ organism summaries, while macro-AUC was positive in all $3$ of $3$ summaries.

These numbers justify a limited finite-row statement: the residual coordinates carry some held-out localization-discriminating signal in this panel. They do not by themselves justify a localization mechanism, trafficking rule, compartment identity rule, or direct biological interpretation of codon residuals. The accuracy range includes one negative organism-level value, so the claim is not a uniformly positive accuracy result across organisms. The macro-AUC result is more stable in sign, but it still remains a classifier-level readout on joined rows rather than a mechanistic localization assay.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned gate.}
The central boundary is the comparison between a translation-conditioned increment and a stronger conditioning set that includes translation, length, amino-acid composition, and GC controls. For the translation-conditioned increment, the accuracy change was positive in all $3$ of $3$ organism summaries, with minimum $0.017295597484276726$, maximum $0.02927509293680297$, mean $0.023049495216764453$, and median $0.02257779522921366$. The macro-AUC change was also positive in all $3$ of $3$ organism summaries, with minimum $0.015494311048138809$, maximum $0.02573588939607574$, mean $0.021063283115180725$, and median $0.021959648901327622$.

The stronger translation-length-amino-acid-GC conditioned increment does not preserve the same signal. Its accuracy change ranged from $-0.010482180293501064$ to $0.010455390334572545$, with mean $-0.000008929986309506402$ and median $0.0$; only $1$ of $3$ organism summaries had positive accuracy change. Its macro-AUC change was negative in all organism summaries, with minimum $-0.002467684182081209$, maximum $-0.0008777321759119161$, mean $-0.001407768101771234$, and median $-0.000877887947320577$. In symbols, the finite result has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{trans}}(q_i,L_k) &> 0 \quad \text{in all three organism summaries for accuracy and macro-AUC},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{trans+length+aa+gc}}(q_i,L_k) &\not> 0 \quad \text{as a stable macro-AUC increment}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the reason the finite verdict is partial rather than a clean crossing into a localization-layer claim.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The organism-level call counts classified $1$ organism summary as crossing to the localization layer and $2$ summaries as partial. The stored verdict for the translation-conditioned boundary is therefore \texttt{partial}. The chapter-level interpretation follows that classification: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals can be used as a finite candidate coordinate for a translation-conditioned localization boundary, but the current evidence is not a license to read localization directly from synonymous residual geometry.

The correct local statement is conditional. A residual coordinate may be proposed as localization-relevant only after the translation readout boundary is represented in the same finite surface, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, mRNA, and simpler-baseline controls kept visible. If the translation step is absent, saturated, rank-degenerate, or only modeled without sufficient power, the localization interpretation is refused. If a localization readout is present but the translation-conditioned statistic is unstable under the fuller conditioning set, the output is a boundary result rather than a positive biological mechanism.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule.}
The finite numbers above do not establish translation realization in the strong biological sense. They also do not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, trafficking mechanism, compartment identity mechanism, or a universal biological law. Modeled tAI or stAI weights may serve as bounded translation-layer annotations only when kept separate from measured ribosome or translation-efficiency rows. mRNA abundance is a control for separating transcription-layer variation from translation-layer variation; it is not itself a translation readout. Bulk protein abundance cannot replace a localization readout. A localization claim requires a layer-matched localization measurement, and a mechanism claim requires a separate mechanism-bearing contact.

Thus the finite conclusion is deliberately narrow. The record supports a partial, translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals on the three-organism joined panel, with positive translation-conditioned increments but loss of stable macro-AUC gain under the stronger translation-length-amino-acid-GC conditioning. The scientific content is the guarded pathway $q_i \to T_j \to L_k$, not a direct map from synonymous residuals to compartments.
