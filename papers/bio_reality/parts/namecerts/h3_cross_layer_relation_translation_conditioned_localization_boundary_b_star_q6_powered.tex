\subsection{A translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite CDS-indexed surface whose coordinates combine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals, matched translation readout variables, mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization label.  The residual coordinate is not read as localization evidence by itself.  The admissible readback has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is a synonymous residual coordinate after the amino-acid quotient, $T_j$ is a translation-layer readout or explicitly bounded translation model, and $L_k$ is a localization readout.  The boundary condition is that a localization interpretation may only be reported after the same coordinate has passed through the translation-readout leg or has been explicitly conditioned at that leg.  A direct $q_i \to L_k$ association is therefore treated as an exploratory surface, not as a localization mechanism.

\paragraph{Rows and classifier.}
The finite run used three organism panels with localization rows joined to CDS-level features: $1908$ rows for \emph{Danio rerio}, $10187$ rows for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $4304$ rows for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The reported classifier was a standard-library LDA with train-fold z-scoring and a ridge shared covariance estimate, evaluated with $5$ folds.  The run identifier was \texttt{ab861f8b1269}, and the fixed seed was \texttt{sha256:93467a1602ce6c3d589b8b5452f233689fb87fa1bbc0f686694db4bb01da7b1f}.  These details matter because the claim is finite-row and held-out: it is not a population-level law, and it is not a statement about every possible localization annotation scheme.

\paragraph{Residual-to-localization signal before the full boundary.}
Across the three organism panels, adding $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates to the localization classifier gave a positive macro-AUC increment in all three panels.  The macro-AUC increment had minimum $0.073410178078059$, median $0.07905771287156632$, mean $0.07848683797295208$, and maximum $0.08299262296923093$.  The accuracy increment was weaker: minimum $-0.008385744234800874$, median $0.029042750929368033$, mean $0.022068418153776248$, and maximum $0.045548247766761585$, with $2$ of $3$ panels positive.  Thus the finite surface contains held-out localization information in the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, especially by macro-AUC, but this alone does not certify translation mediation or any biological localization mechanism.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary statistic.}
The boundary run then tested the translation-conditioned increment.  For the translation-conditioned comparison, the accuracy increment was positive in all three organism panels, with minimum $0.017295597484276726$, median $0.02257779522921366$, mean $0.023049495216764453$, and maximum $0.02927509293680297$.  The macro-AUC increment was also positive in all three panels, with minimum $0.015494311048138809$, median $0.021959648901327622$, mean $0.021063283115180725$, and maximum $0.02573588939607574$.  On this finite readback, one organism panel crossed to the localization layer and two panels remained partial, so the organism-level call counts were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\text{crosses to localization layer}\} &= 1,\\
\#\{\text{partial}\} &= 2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The resulting verdict is partial rather than closed: the run supports a bounded cross-layer boundary, not a completed localization explanation.

\paragraph{Control-sensitive refusal.}
The stricter translation-length-amino-acid-GC conditioned increment is the limiting observation.  Under that comparison, the accuracy increment had minimum $-0.010482180293501064$, median $0.0$, mean $-0.000008929986309506402$, and maximum $0.010455390334572545$, with only $1$ of $3$ panels positive.  The macro-AUC increment was negative in every panel: minimum $-0.002467684182081209$, median $-0.000877887947320577$, mean $-0.001407768101771234$, and maximum $-0.0008777321759119161$.  This is the main reason the chapter records a boundary rather than a biological promotion.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate has finite held-out localization information, and the translation-conditioned increment is positive on the reported summary, but the stricter controlled comparison does not show a stable positive macro-AUC increment.

\paragraph{Local claim.}
The supported statement is therefore narrow.  In the finite panels listed above, $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals carry partial held-out information for protein-localization labels, and the translation-conditioned comparison gives positive finite increments across the three panels.  However, the control-sensitive surface prevents a stronger conclusion.  The correct local reading is that $B^*_{Q6}$ can be used to define a translation-conditioned localization boundary and a prioritized data route for the chain $q_i \to T_j \to L_k$.  It is not yet a claim that the codon residual causes localization, encodes trafficking, determines compartment identity, or supplies a localization mechanism.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon residual geometry unless an organism-matched translation readout supplies the relevant statistic.  No folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, trafficking mechanism, compartment mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter.  Modeled tAI or stAI variables remain bounded translation annotations unless matched measured rows justify a stronger readout.  Bulk protein abundance cannot replace a layer-matched localization measurement.  The present record should be used as a finite, control-aware boundary for deciding what has to be joined next: same-CDS codon counts, measured or explicitly bounded translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, optional protein-abundance controls, and localization rows evaluated under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and simpler-baseline controls.
