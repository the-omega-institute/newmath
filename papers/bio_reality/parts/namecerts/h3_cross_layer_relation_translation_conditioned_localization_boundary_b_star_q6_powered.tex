\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-indexed surface whose retained coordinates are synonymous $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals, matched translation readouts, mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization readout slot.  The point of the surface is not to read localization directly from codon geometry.  It asks whether a residual coordinate $q_i$ can pass through an explicit translation boundary before a downstream localization coordinate $L_k$ is interpreted.  The permitted readback is therefore the gated chain
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_j$ denotes a measured or explicitly bounded translation readout and $L_k$ denotes a matched localization label.  The null outcome is a local boundary: if the localization rows are absent, rank-defective, or not joined to the same CDS panel, then the record stops at the translation-conditioned proposal and does not become a biological localization claim.

\paragraph{Layer discipline.}
The admissible biological statement is narrow.  A $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate may be proposed as relevant to localization only through a tested or explicitly bounded $q_i \to T_j$ translation step followed by a matched localization readout.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights can serve only as bounded translation annotations when used under rank-sufficient design; they are not observed ribosome occupancy.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control for translation-efficiency residualization, not a translation readout.  Bulk protein abundance is not a substitute for protein localization.  A localization label, when present, supplies only the downstream readout class and not a trafficking mechanism or compartment-causation mechanism.
