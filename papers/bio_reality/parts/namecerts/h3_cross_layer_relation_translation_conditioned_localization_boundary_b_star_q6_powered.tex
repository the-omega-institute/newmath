\subsection{Translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates as a finite coding-sequence surface whose rows carry four separated kinds of information: a residual coordinate $q_i$ surviving the amino-acid quotient, a translation readout $T_j$, matched mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization slot $L_k$. The intended readback is not a direct arrow from codon residual geometry to localization. The admissible relation has the gated form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the first arrow must be tested at the translation layer before the localization leg is interpreted. The coordinate $q_i$ is therefore allowed to enter the localization analysis only after the same finite row has supplied a measured or explicitly bounded translation readout, together with amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and mRNA controls. Bulk protein abundance is not a localization measurement, and a modeled codon-adaptation score is not by itself a measured localization contact.

Thus the finite panel is not a purely symbolic proposal: it contains organism-specific localization joins large enough to test held-out localization discrimination, while still small enough in organism count to require a cross-organism caution label.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary.}
The boundary test asked whether localization readback remains admissible after forcing the residual relation through a translation-readout step. $$
\begin{aligned}

$$
The organism-level calls were not uniform: one organism crossed to the localization layer, and two organisms remained partial. The finite verdict is therefore \texttt{partial}. The result allows a bounded statement: on this joined panel, the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block has some localization-predictive content and the translation-conditioned route is not empty. It does not justify reading localization directly from $q_i$ without the $T_j$ boundary.

\paragraph{Control-conditioned attenuation.}
When translation, length, amino-acid, and GC controls were included, the localization increment contracted sharply. The corresponding accuracy changes were
$$
\begin{aligned}

\Delta\mathrm{accuracy}_{\mathrm{mean}} &= -0.000008929986309506402,\\
\Delta\mathrm{accuracy}_{\mathrm{median}} &= 0.0,\\
\Delta\mathrm{accuracy}_{\max} &= 0.010455390334572545,
\end{aligned}
$$
with only $1$ of $3$ organisms positive for accuracy. The macro-AUC changes were all negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\mathrm{macroAUC}_{\min} &= -0.002467684182081209,\\
\Delta\mathrm{macroAUC}_{\mathrm{mean}} &= -0.001407768101771234,\\
\Delta\mathrm{macroAUC}_{\mathrm{median}} &= -0.000877887947320577,\\
\Delta\mathrm{macroAUC}_{\max} &= -0.0008777321759119161.

The zero positive macro-AUC count after these controls is the main reason the chapter reports a boundary rather than a localization mechanism. The residual coordinates are not being promoted to an autonomous localization principle; they are being recorded as a finite, translation-conditioned signal whose downstream interpretation weakens under stricter controls.

\paragraph{Local refusal rule.}
The chapter's admissible conclusion is narrow. A $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate may be discussed as localization-relevant only through a rowwise route in which $q_i$ first survives, or is explicitly conditioned through, a translation readout $T_j$, and only then is compared with a matched localization readout $L_k$. Without that intervening translation boundary, the same residual coordinate cannot be used to infer translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, trafficking mechanism, compartment identity mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Modeled tAI or stAI weights remain bounded translation annotations unless a measured readout supplies the relevant contact. Matched mRNA abundance is a control for separating transcription-layer signal from translation-layer signal, not a substitute for ribosome occupancy or protein localization. Protein abundance, when present, is likewise not a replacement for a localization readout.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is \texttt{partial}. The positive direct localization macro-AUC increments and the positive translation-conditioned increments show that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface is not inert on the joined localization panel. The collapse of the stricter translation-length-amino-acid-GC conditioned macro-AUC increments below zero in all three organisms prevents a stronger biological reading. The chapter therefore records a guarded cross-layer boundary: $q_i \rightarrow T_j \rightarrow L_k$ is a testable finite relation with partial support, while any claim of direct localization control, trafficking mechanism, structural consequence, physical admissibility, biological function, or general law remains outside the evidence supplied here.
