\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein-localization boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{subsec:biorreality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record studies a finite CDS-indexed surface in which the coordinate side is a family of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates and the readout side is split into three non-interchangeable layers: matched translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization slot. The intended readback is not a direct arrow from codon residual geometry to localization. It is the gated chain
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is a surviving $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, $T_j$ is a translation-layer readout or an explicitly bounded translation proxy, and $L_k$ is a matched protein-localization readout. The amino-acid quotient is imposed before the higher-layer question is asked: a synonymous residual coordinate is considered only after amino-acid composition has been separated from the codon-level residue. The translation leg and the localization leg are therefore different tests, not two names for the same association.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The powered run attached to this record has run identifier \texttt{ab861f8b1269} and was verified at \texttt{2026-06-17T15:54:50+00:00}. It used a standard-library linear discriminant classifier with train-fold z-scoring and ridge shared covariance, evaluated with $5$ folds. The joined localization rows were $1908$ CDS records for \textit{Danio rerio}, $10187$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $4304$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Thus the surface is not an abstract promise of a future assay: it contains a concrete three-organism finite panel, but the panel remains small at the organism level, with $n=3$ organism summaries for the cross-organism verdict.

This control-conditioned surface blocks promotion from a useful classifier signal to a robust localization claim. The residual coordinates have partial held-out localization information, and the translation-conditioned increment is positive in the finite summary, but the length, amino-acid, and GC-conditioned increment is not stable enough to certify a localization-layer conclusion across organisms.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The valid statement is therefore narrow. In the finite three-organism panel, $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals show partial held-out localization information, and the translation-conditioned increment is positive before the stricter control surface is imposed. The correct readback is a boundary statement: a localization interpretation may be considered only after the same coordinate has been tested through a translation-readout leg and after a matched localization row is present. The observed verdict is \texttt{partial}, not a positive mechanism call.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon residual geometry alone. No protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, trafficking mechanism, compartment-identity mechanism, system phenotype, or universal biological law follows from the localization classifier deltas. Modeled tAI or stAI weights may be used only as bounded translation-layer annotations unless organism-matched measured translation rows support them. Bulk protein abundance cannot substitute for a localization readout. A direct $q_i \to L_k$ association is not admissible as a biological interpretation unless the same finite surface also supplies the required $q_i \to T_j$ survival or conditioning step and keeps mRNA, length, GC3, amino-acid composition, $M$-density, and simpler-baseline controls visible.
