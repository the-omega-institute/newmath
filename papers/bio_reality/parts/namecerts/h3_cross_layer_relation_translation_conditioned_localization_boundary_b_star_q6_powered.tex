\subsection{Translation-Conditioned Localization Boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ Residuals}
\label{subsec:bior reality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record considered here is a finite CDS-indexed measurement surface whose coordinates combine synonymous residual coordinates $q_i$ from $B^*_{Q6}$, matched translation readouts $T_j$, mRNA controls, and a downstream localization label slot $L_k$.  The intended readback is not a direct residual-to-localization assertion.  It is the gated relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the residual coordinate must first survive the amino-acid quotient and then pass through a translation-readout boundary before any localization interpretation is permitted.  The biological object is therefore a conditional cross-layer boundary: $B^*_{Q6}$ codon residuals in coding sequences are tested for whether they carry finite held-out information about localization only after the translation layer and the control covariates have been kept explicit.

\paragraph{Finite rows and classifier.}
The powered finite run is identified by experiment run \texttt{ab861f8b1269}, with verification timestamp \texttt{2026-06-22T17:40:58+00:00}.  The run status is \texttt{passed}.  The classifier was a standard-library LDA with train-fold z-scoring and ridge shared covariance, evaluated with $5$ folds under seed \texttt{sha256:93467a1602ce6c3d589b8b5452f233689fb87fa1bbc0f686694db4bb01da7b1f}.  The localization-joined row counts were $1908$ CDS rows for \textit{Danio rerio}, $10187$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $4304$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Thus the finite panel is not merely a proposed routing rule: it contains an organism-indexed localization readout table large enough to evaluate held-out discrimination, while still requiring the translation boundary to remain explicit.

\paragraph{Residual-to-localization signal.}
Across the three organism panels, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates carried partial held-out localization information.  For the direct localization-discrimination comparison, the accuracy increment ranged from $-0.008385744234800874$ to $0.045548247766761585$, with mean $0.022068418153776248$ and median $0.029042750929368033$.  Accuracy improved in $2$ of $3$ organism panels.  The macro-AUC increment was more stable: it ranged from $0.073410178078059$ to $0.08299262296923093$, with mean $0.07848683797295208$ and median $0.07905771287156632$, and was positive in all $3$ of $3$ panels.  The finite observation is therefore a partial discriminative contact: the residual basis contains localization-relevant held-out information, but the accuracy sign is not uniform across organisms.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary.}
The translation-conditioned increment is the relevant boundary statistic for this chapter.  In the finite run, the translation-conditioned increment summary over $n=3$ organism panels had accuracy increments between $0.017295597484276726$ and $0.02927509293680297$, with mean $0.023049495216764453$ and median $0.02257779522921366$.  Macro-AUC increments ranged from $0.015494311048138809$ to $0.02573588939607574$, with mean $0.021063283115180725$ and median $0.021959648901327622$.  Both accuracy and macro-AUC increments were positive in all $3$ of $3$ panels.  The organism call counts were $1$ panel crossing to the localization layer and $2$ panels remaining partial.  The boundary verdict is therefore \texttt{partial}, not universal promotion.

\paragraph{Control ablation.}
The stronger control surface, which conditions on translation, length, amino-acid composition, and GC-related controls, removes the robust positive localization increment.  Its accuracy increment ranged from $-0.010482180293501064$ to $0.010455390334572545$, with mean $-0.000008929986309506402$ and median $0.0$; only $1$ of $3$ panels remained accuracy-positive.  Its macro-AUC increment ranged from $-0.002467684182081209$ to $-0.0008777321759119161$, with mean $-0.001407768101771234$ and median $-0.000877887947320577$; $0$ of $3$ panels were macro-AUC-positive.  In symbols, the finite readback is better summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\not\Rightarrow L_k \quad \text{as an unconditional biological mechanism},\\
q_i &\Rightarrow L_k \quad \text{as partial held-out localization information},\\
q_i \to T_j \to L_k &\quad \text{as the required interpretation boundary}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last line is the only admissible scientific reading of the finite rows.

\paragraph{Allowed interpretation.}
The supported claim is narrow.  The run shows that $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can carry partial held-out information about localization labels on the joined finite panels, and that the translation-conditioned boundary has positive increments before the stricter length, amino-acid, and GC-conditioned surface collapses to near zero or negative macro-AUC.  This supports the use of $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals as a finite coordinate system for asking whether localization information is mediated, attenuated, or blocked at the translation-readout interface.  It does not by itself identify a trafficking route, compartment mechanism, localization signal, folding state, or functional consequence.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon residual geometry alone.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the localization-discrimination result.  Modeled tAI or stAI coupling cannot be treated as observed ribosome occupancy when the organism panel is rank-degenerate or underpowered, and bulk protein abundance cannot substitute for a localization readout.  Any stronger claim requires a separately attached measurement at the relevant layer: measured translation efficiency for $T_j$, matched localization rows for $L_k$, and additional contacts for trafficking mechanism, folding, physical assays, function, phenotype, or universality.  The present chapter records a finite, partial, translation-conditioned localization boundary, not a direct biological mechanism.
