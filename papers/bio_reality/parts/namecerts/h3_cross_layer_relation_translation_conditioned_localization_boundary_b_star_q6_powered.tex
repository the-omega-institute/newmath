\subsection{Translation-Conditioned Localization Boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ Residuals}\label{subsec:biorality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether synonymous residual coordinates in $B^*_{Q6}$ carry localization-relevant information only after the translation layer has been kept explicit.  The finite surface has one row class for coding sequences with $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates $q_i$, matched translation readouts $T_j$, mRNA controls, amino-acid composition controls, organism labels, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and a downstream localization slot $L_k$.  The intended readback is not a direct map from codon residuals to localization.  It is the gated relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the first arrow asks whether the residual survives at the translation-readout boundary, and the second arrow asks whether a layer-matched localization readout remains associated after the translation slot has been exposed.  A localization interpretation is therefore refused unless the $q_i$ coordinate either survives the amino-acid quotient before the downstream test or is explicitly conditioned through the translation-readout boundary.

\paragraph{Empirical row set.}
The powered finite run is identified by experiment run $\mathrm{ab861f8b1269}$ and uses a standard-library LDA classifier with train-fold z-scoring and a ridge shared covariance estimate.  The experiment used $5$ folds and joined localization rows for three organisms: $1908$ CDS rows in \textit{Danio rerio}, $10187$ in \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $4304$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The recorded seed is $\mathrm{sha256{:}93467a1602ce6c3d589b8b5452f233689fb87fa1bbc0f686694db4bb01da7b1f}$.  These rows support a finite cross-organism audit, not a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Residual-to-localization signal.}
Across the three organism panels, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ localization discrimination surface is positive for macro-AUC in all three cases and positive for accuracy in two of three cases.  The accuracy increment ranges from $-0.008385744234800874$ to $0.045548247766761585$, with mean $0.022068418153776248$ and median $0.029042750929368033$.  The macro-AUC increment is uniformly positive, ranging from $0.073410178078059$ to $0.08299262296923093$, with mean $0.07848683797295208$ and median $0.07905771287156632$.  Thus the residual coordinates contain held-out localization information on this finite surface, but this fact alone does not identify a translation mechanism, a trafficking route, a compartmental mechanism, or any physical admissibility claim.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary.}
The translation-conditioned increment has a more modest but directionally stable finite readback.  Over $n=3$ organisms, the translation-conditioned accuracy increment is positive in all three cases, with minimum $0.017295597484276726$, maximum $0.02927509293680297$, mean $0.023049495216764453$, and median $0.02257779522921366$.  The corresponding macro-AUC increment is also positive in all three cases, with minimum $0.015494311048138809$, maximum $0.02573588939607574$, mean $0.021063283115180725$, and median $0.021959648901327622$.  The organism-level calls are not homogeneous: one organism crosses to the localization layer, while two remain partial.  The finite verdict is therefore partial rather than promoted.

\paragraph{Control-conditioned collapse.}
The stronger control surface, which conditions on translation together with length, amino-acid composition, and GC-context controls, does not preserve a positive macro-AUC increment.  Its accuracy increment ranges from $-0.010482180293501064$ to $0.010455390334572545$, with mean $-0.000008929986309506402$ and median $0.0$; only one of three organisms has a positive accuracy increment.  Its macro-AUC increment is negative in all three organisms, ranging from $-0.002467684182081209$ to $-0.0008777321759119161$, with mean $-0.001407768101771234$ and median $-0.000877887947320577$.  This is the key boundary fact: the residual coordinates can discriminate localization in held-out classification, and the translation-conditioned increment is positive on the finite panel, but the result does not remain strong after the broader length, amino-acid, and GC control surface is imposed.

\paragraph{Local claim.}
The chapter-level claim is consequently narrow.  The finite data support a partial $B^*_{Q6}$ cross-layer relation in which localization information is visible on held-out rows and remains directionally positive through the explicit translation-conditioned boundary.  They do not support direct interpretation of $q_i \to L_k$ as a localization mechanism.  The admissible statement is only that the finite panel exhibits a bounded relation of the form $q_i \to T_j \to L_k$, with partial cross-organism stability and with loss of macro-AUC support under the stricter translation-length-amino-acid-GC conditioning surface.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
No translation realization, ribosome occupancy mechanism, protein folding claim, physical admissibility statement, molecular function claim, trafficking mechanism, compartment identity mechanism, phenotype claim, causation claim, or universal law follows from these rows.  Modeled tAI or stAI couplings are not equivalent to observed ribosome dynamics, mRNA abundance is only a control for translation-efficiency residualization, and bulk protein abundance cannot replace a matched localization readout.  Any stronger biological interpretation would require a separate layer-matched contact: same-organism and same-CDS codon counts, measured or explicitly bounded translation readouts, matched mRNA rows, and localization rows, followed by a nondegenerate $S^{QT}$ step before estimating the translation-conditioned $q_i$-to-localization statistic.
