\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-indexed surface whose rows carry four separated kinds of coordinates: a synonymous residual coordinate $q_i$ from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ basis, matched translation readouts $T_j$, mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization label $L_k$.  The biological question is not whether a codon residual by itself determines localization.  The admissible question is narrower: whether a residual coordinate that has survived the amino-acid quotient can be tested through a translation boundary before any localization readout is interpreted.  Thus the only permitted finite relation has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the first arrow is the translation-readout step and the second arrow is the downstream localization readback conditioned on that step.  A direct $q_i \to L_k$ reading is treated as insufficient unless the translation boundary is also represented in the same finite design or explicitly declared as absent.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion boundary.}
No translation realization follows merely from a codon residual, a modeled tAI or stAI value, or a codon-usage table.  No protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, trafficking mechanism, compartment identity mechanism, system phenotype, causation, or universality follows from the localization classifier increments reported here.
