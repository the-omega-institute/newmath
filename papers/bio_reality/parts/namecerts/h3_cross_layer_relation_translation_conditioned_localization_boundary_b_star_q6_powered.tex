\subsection{Translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}\label{subsec:biorreality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-indexed table whose coordinates are $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals, matched translation readouts, mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization label. The intended readback is not a direct map from a synonymous residual coordinate to a protein compartment. It is the gated relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is a residual coordinate that survives the amino-acid quotient, $T_j$ is a separate translation-layer readout, and $L_k$ is a localization readout attached only after the translation boundary is respected. The experiment is therefore interpreted as a cross-layer boundary test: first ask whether the residual coordinate carries translation-conditioned information under the declared controls, and only then ask whether a localization readout can be read in the same joined finite rows.

The verified run is \texttt{ab861f8b1269}, verified at \texttt{2026-06-17T11:26:39+00:00}, with status \texttt{passed}. The classifier was a standard-library LDA using train-fold z-scoring and a ridge shared covariance estimate. Five folds were used. Thus the numerical claim is supported only over these three organism-specific joined surfaces and not over an unrestricted biological universe.

In compact form, the observed held-out surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \operatorname{Acc}_{Q6 \to L} &\in [-0.008385744234800874,\ 0.045548247766761585],\\
\operatorname{mean}(\Delta \operatorname{Acc}_{Q6 \to L}) &= 0.022068418153776248,\\

This supports a limited statement: the residual coordinates contain some held-out localization information on the joined rows. It does not by itself identify a trafficking mechanism, compartmental targeting rule, or protein-state process.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned boundary.}
The more restrictive boundary statistic asks whether the localization signal remains meaningful only through the declared translation-conditioned route. For the translation-conditioned increment, the accuracy increment was positive in all three organisms, with minimum $0.017295597484276726$, mean $0.023049495216764453$, median $0.02257779522921366$, and maximum $0.02927509293680297$. The macro-AUC increment was also positive in all three organisms, with minimum $0.015494311048138809$, mean $0.021063283115180725$, median $0.021959648901327622$, and maximum $0.02573588939607574$. The organism calls were one \texttt{crosses\_to\_localization\_layer} and two \texttt{partial}. The finite summary is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \operatorname{Acc}_{Q6 \to T \to L} &\in [0.017295597484276726,\ 0.02927509293680297],\\
\Delta \operatorname{AUC}_{Q6 \to T \to L} &\in [0.015494311048138809,\ 0.02573588939607574],\\
\#\{\Delta \operatorname{Acc}>0\} &= 3,\\
\#\{\Delta \operatorname{AUC}>0\} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the positive part of the record: the boundary is not empty on the supplied finite rows. The result is nevertheless classified as \texttt{partial}, because a stronger control surface removes the robust localization interpretation.

\paragraph{Control absorption.}
After conditioning on translation, CDS length, amino-acid composition, and GC-linked controls, the residual localization increment is no longer stable. The accuracy increment had minimum $-0.010482180293501064$, mean $-0.000008929986309506402$, median $0.0$, and maximum $0.010455390334572545$, with only one positive accuracy count among three organisms. The macro-AUC increment was negative in all three organisms, with maximum $-0.0008777321759119161$, mean $-0.001407768101771234$, median $-0.000877887947320577$, and minimum $-0.002467684182081209$. Thus the controlled surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \operatorname{Acc}_{Q6 \to L\mid T,Z} &\in [-0.010482180293501064,\ 0.010455390334572545],\\
\operatorname{mean}(\Delta \operatorname{Acc}_{Q6 \to L\mid T,Z}) &= -0.000008929986309506402,\\
\Delta \operatorname{AUC}_{Q6 \to L\mid T,Z} &\in [-0.002467684182081209,\ -0.0008777321759119161],\\
\#\{\Delta \operatorname{AUC}>0\} &= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ denotes the local baseline controls: amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, mRNA control, and simpler codon baselines where available. This is why the verified conclusion is not a localization mechanism claim. The data support partial held-out information and a nonempty translation-conditioned boundary, while the stricter controlled increment is absorbed.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow. A $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate may be discussed as localization-relevant only through the finite route $q_i \to T_j \to L_k$, and only with the translation readout kept separate from the localization readout. The chapter does not license direct inference from synonymous residuals to protein localization, trafficking, compartment identity, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. A modeled tAI or stAI contact is not an observed ribosome-occupancy measurement by itself; matched mRNA abundance is a control covariate rather than a translation readout; and bulk protein abundance is not a substitute for a localization measurement. Any stronger biological statement would require an additional layer-matched contact and a new controlled statistic on the same organism- and CDS-matched rows.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is \texttt{partial}. The run establishes that $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals carry some held-out localization information and that the translation-conditioned increment is positive across the three organism surfaces. It also establishes the limiting fact: once translation, length, amino-acid composition, GC-linked sequence structure, M-density, and mRNA controls are imposed, the localization increment is not stable enough to promote into a biological localization claim. The record is therefore a powered boundary result, not a direct mechanistic claim.
