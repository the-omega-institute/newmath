\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite coding-sequence surface whose active coordinates are $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals, matched translation readouts, mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization slot. The operative relation is not a direct residual-to-localization assertion. The admissible readback is the gated chain
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate, $T_j$ is a translation-layer readout, and $L_k$ is a protein-localization label. The first boundary asks whether the residual coordinate carries held-out localization information at all; the second asks whether that signal remains meaningful only after a translation-readout step has survived the declared controls. \paragraph{Local claim.}
The chapter claim is the following bounded statement. A $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate may be considered relevant to protein localization only through a tested chain in which the residual coordinate first passes, or is explicitly conditioned by, a translation-readout boundary. The present finite run supports a partial boundary verdict: residual localization discrimination is visible, translation-conditioned increments are positive across the three panels, and the stricter translation-length-amino-acid-GC conditioned surface does not preserve macro-AUC gain. Consequently the admissible readback is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{partial signal: } q_i \rightsquigarrow L_k,\\
&\text{gated interpretation: } q_i \to T_j \to L_k,\\
&\text{refused interpretation: } q_i \to L_k \text{ as an ungated localization mechanism.}
\end{aligned}
$$
