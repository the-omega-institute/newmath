\subsection{Translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{subsec:biorality-q6-translation-conditioned-localization-boundary}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-layer test for synonymous residual coordinates in coding sequences. The carrier is a CDS-indexed table with one row per joined coding sequence and with separated coordinate groups: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates $q_i$, translation readouts $T_j$, baseline controls, and a downstream protein-localization slot $L_k$. The biological question is not whether a codon residual directly names a compartment. The question is narrower: whether a residual coordinate that has already survived the amino-acid quotient can contribute to localization prediction only through a declared translation boundary.

The readout is therefore the gated relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow L_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the second arrow may be interpreted only after the translation leg has been kept separate from amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, and matched mRNA controls. If a localization table is absent or fails to join to the same CDS surface, the construction returns a null localization boundary rather than a localization claim.

\begin{aligned}

N_{\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}} &= 10187,\\

These row counts are sufficient for a finite held-out classifier comparison, but they do not by themselves supply a mechanism for trafficking, a compartment-determining rule, or a physical account of localization.

Only one panel crossed to the localization layer, while two panels remained partial. The chapter-level verdict is therefore partial: $B^*_{Q6}$ contains held-out localization information on the joined finite panels, and the translation-conditioned comparison is positive in the recorded aggregate, but the stronger conditioned surface does not support a stable cross-organism localization interpretation.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The finite result supports only the following statement: on the joined panels, $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can improve held-out localization classification under the reported LDA protocol, and a translation-conditioned boundary comparison remains positive in aggregate. It does not establish that synonymous residual geometry directly causes localization, identifies a trafficking route, names a compartment mechanism, or determines folded structure. It also does not turn modeled tAI or stAI coupling into measured ribosome occupancy when the organism panel is rank-degenerate or underpowered.

Consequently, no translation mechanism, protein folding statement, physical admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, system-phenotype statement, localization mechanism, compartment-identity mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row set alone. Each such higher claim requires its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. Protein abundance is not a substitute for a localization readout, and a direct $q_i \to L_k$ association is not promoted unless the $q_i \to T_j$ leg survives or is explicitly conditioned through the translation-readout boundary.
