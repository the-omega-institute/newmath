\subsection{Turnover Residual Audit After Translation and Proteostasis Controls}
\label{sec:bioq6-turnover-residual-audit}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object audited here is a finite cross-layer table whose rows are coding sequences with a measured protein-turnover readout and whose columns separate three roles.  The first role is the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual block, with coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The second role is the already separated translation layer, represented in adjacent audits by modeled or measured translation-readout quantities such as per-protein tAI, ribosome-footprint rate slots, or translation efficiency.  The third role is the proteostasis target, here a measured turnover response.  The turnover audit used $n=3290$ genes and $B=200$ finite null draws.  It does not ask whether synonymous residual geometry can be drawn or whether it is associated with earlier sequence and translation summaries; it asks the narrower question of whether the residual block still improves measured turnover after expression, translation, structure-confidence, N-end, and related proteostasis controls have already occupied their slots.

\paragraph{Nested comparison.}
Let $Y$ be the turnover readout, let $Z_2$ denote the finite control design through expression, translation-efficiency and proteostasis summaries, and let $Q$ denote the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block.  The registered comparison is the incremental transition from the control model $L_2$ to the residual-augmented model $L_3$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_2) &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2) = -0.005461979433706483.
\end{aligned}
$$
The description-length comparison has the same sign:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -13.68819707902732,\\
Null99_{matched\ bin}(\Delta DL_{bits}) &= 3.51918934107672,\\
Null99_{recoded}(\Delta DL_{bits}) &= 1.9038288477880905.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed increment is below zero and is also far below both finite null thresholds.  The two empirical tail probabilities were $p_{matched\ bin}=0.8855721393034826$ and $p_{recoded}=1.0$.  Thus the audit returns a negative powered result for the turnover layer: on the present rows, the residual block does not improve measured turnover once the stronger upstream and proteostasis covariates are present.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated protein-abundance surface.}
This negative result is not a denial that the same residual coordinates have bounded contacts at earlier layers.  In the protein-abundance mediation audit, the finite matrix joined matched CDS and abundance rows across organisms, with examples including $5815$ yeast proteins, $3739$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 proteins, $19053$ human proteins, and $19395$ mouse proteins.  In that table the joint $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition produced positive mediated fractions in the unit interval, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse.  Those numbers describe a statistical translation-conditioned protein-abundance decomposition, not a turnover result.  The present turnover audit deliberately places a later proteostasis readout behind those earlier contacts and asks whether any remaining $Q$ signal survives.  The answer on the finite turnover panel is no.

\paragraph{Compatibility with other bounded contacts.}
The surrounding finite records also show why the present refusal is layer-specific rather than a global erasure of the coordinate system.  A rate-slot audit in Saccharomyces cerevisiae used $3289$ genes and reported an observable execution subblock after both effective null classes were passed.  A mass-balance audit used $120$ genes over $718$ intervals and found that the stronger dynamic layer did not improve the held-out fit, with $R^2(L_2)=0.9712443377743347$, $R^2(L_3)=0.8953999568421264$, $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $p_{TEbin}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{timeshuffle}=0.9306930693069307$.  A structural-order axis audit identified $f_3$-stress as the single significant coordinate, with held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip probability $0.03125$.  These are distinct bounded readbacks.  They do not license promotion to turnover; they only explain why the turnover question must be tested as its own finite panel.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The allowed conclusion is a current-data closure statement for the turnover target.  On $3290$ genes, adding the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block beyond the registered expression, translation, pLDDT, N-end, and proteostasis controls decreased held-out explanatory power and failed both finite null comparisons.  The chapter therefore records a bounded negative: there is no evidence in this table that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts measured protein turnover beyond those controls.  The claim remains admissible only as a turnover-residual audit with a negative result, not as a positive proteostasis mechanism.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation-realization claim follows merely from this turnover table.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from the residual coordinates, from modeled tAI, from ribosome-readout routing, from matched protein abundance, or from the negative turnover increment.  A positive statement at any one of those layers would require its own layer-matched contact, its own finite joined rows, and its own controlled statistic.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ or $Q_i\to turnover$ association is not accepted as protein realization unless a same-coordinate $Q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout step has first survived the declared controls; in the turnover audit, even the later residual increment after stronger controls is nonpositive.
