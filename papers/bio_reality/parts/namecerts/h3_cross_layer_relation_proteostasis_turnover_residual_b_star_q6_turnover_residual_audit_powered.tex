\subsection{Proteostasis Turnover Residual Audit for $B^*_{Q6}$}\label{subsec:bioreality-proteostasis-turnover-residual-bstar-q6}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual panel carries any additional signal for measured protein turnover after the ordinary expression and protein-state covariates have already been admitted.  The empirical table has $3290$ genes and compares a baseline turnover model against the same model augmented by the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  The baseline already contains expression, translation-efficiency information, predicted structural confidence, N-end features, and matched controls; the tested residual coordinates are therefore not allowed to inherit signal merely from abundance, translation, or protein identity.  The fitted comparison is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= R^2_{L3}-R^2_{L2}=-0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -13.68819707902732.
\end{aligned}
$$

The augmentation is therefore not a positive turnover refinement.  It reduces held-out explanatory performance and pays a negative description-length balance.  The audit used $B=200$ null resamples.  The matched-bin null has $p=0.8855721393034826$ and the recoded null has $p=1.0$; their $99\%$ reference values are $3.51918934107672$ bits and $1.9038288477880905$ bits, respectively.  Since the observed $\Delta \mathrm{DL}$ is below zero rather than above either positive null reference, the admissible readback is a bounded negative one: these data give no evidence that $B^*_{Q6}$ predicts measured turnover beyond the listed expression, translation-efficiency, structural-confidence, and N-end covariates.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance contacts.}
The negative turnover result is not a denial that the same coordinate family can touch earlier biological readouts.  A separate translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation audit joined codon residuals, modeled per-protein tAI, and measured PAXdb abundance across twelve organisms.  In that table the joint $9$-coordinate residual panel had positive statistical mediation fractions for many organism rows; for example, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} had

$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
c_{\mathrm{total}} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
c'_{\mathrm{given}\ T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
c-c' &= 0.28320534058057895.
\end{aligned}
$$

The same mediation table reports $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.6084594553474505$ in \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, and $0.6165167748739756$ in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}.  Those numbers justify treating the protein-abundance route as an earlier bounded statistical contact, not as a turnover result.  The turnover audit asks a stricter question: after expression, translation-efficiency, pLDDT-like structural confidence, N-end information, and matched controls are already present, does the residual panel still improve the turnover fit?  The answer in this finite table is no.

\paragraph{Mass-balance comparison.}
A dynamic RPF--protein mass-balance audit gives the same local caution from a different angle.  In that experiment, $120$ eligible genes were sampled over $718$ intervals.  The model with measured abundance alone had $R^2=0.9960628201518571$, adding estimated translation terms gave $R^2=0.996410628851051$, while the later $B^*_{Q6}$-augmented stages fell to $R^2=0.9712443377743347$ and $0.8953999568421264$.  The final increment was

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928,\\
p_{\mathrm{TEbin}} &= 0.6831683168316832,\\
p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$

This comparison does not substitute for the turnover audit, because it uses a different dynamic surface and only $120$ genes.  It does, however, supports the same scientific separation: a residual coordinate may organize translation-adjacent variation without becoming a general post-translational rate coordinate.  The observed execution audit likewise remained limited to a bounded rate-proxy slot in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with $3289$ genes and the verdict that at least one observed rate proxy passed its effective null classes.  That result supplies a local rate-contact precedent, not a license to promote every downstream protein-state quantity.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The finite conclusion is a current-data closure for the turnover endpoint.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ panel is retained as a codon-residual and translation-adjacent coordinate family, and it may still participate in explicitly mediated $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ abundance decompositions.  In the turnover table, however, adding those residual coordinates after expression, translation efficiency, structural confidence, N-end features, and matched controls gives $\Delta R^2<0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}<0$.  The only supported claim is therefore:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{TurnoverGain}_{B^*_{Q6}} &\leq 0\quad\text{on the audited finite surface},\\
\mathrm{ProteinTurnoverPromotion}_{B^*_{Q6}} &= \text{blocked for these rows}.
\end{aligned}
$$

No translation realization, folding statement, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, mechanism claim, selection claim, phenotype claim, or universal law follows from this audit.  Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, a direct $Q_i\to$ turnover association is not admissible as a protein-realization claim when the fitted turnover increment is negative after the stronger baseline has already absorbed expression, translation-efficiency, structural-confidence, and N-end information.
