\subsection{Turnover residual audit after translation and protein-state controls}\label{sec:biorality-q6-turnover-residual-audit}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual packet still carries measured protein-turnover information after the finite row has already paid for the ordinary explanatory routes.  The data surface has one row per matched gene and separates three roles: $Q$ is the residual codon-coordinate block, $C$ is the baseline control block, and $Y$ is the measured turnover readout.  The audit used $n=3290$ genes and compared a control model $L_2$ against a control-plus-$B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ model $L_3$.  The controls include expression, translation-efficiency information, pLDDT-like structural-order information, N-end information, and the ordinary sequence-composition baselines used to prevent a codon residual from silently standing in for amino-acid composition, GC3, length, or residual codon-density mass.

\paragraph{Readout equation.}
The tested question is not whether $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ can be named in a coding sequence.  It is whether it improves the held finite turnover fit once the turnover-relevant covariates are already present.  In the notation of this audit, the measured comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_2) &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2) = -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= -13.68819707902732\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign is the decisive finite-row feature: adding the residual packet after the existing turnover controls worsens the predictive surface rather than improving it.  The permutation and recoding checks agree with that reading.  With $B=200$ actual resampling arms, the matched-bin right-tail value is $p=0.8855721393034826$, and the recoded-null value is $p=1.0$.  The corresponding null $99\%$ description-length cutoffs are $3.51918934107672$ bits for the matched-bin null and $1.9038288477880905$ bits for the recoded null.  The observed $\Delta\mathrm{DL}$ is below both positive gates, so the finite audit does not admit a positive turnover-residual claim.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-protein surface.}
The negative turnover result is compatible with the separate translation-mediated protein-abundance surface.  On that surface, the same nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates were evaluated through modeled per-protein tAI and protein abundance across twelve organism-level joins: for example $n=5815$ proteins in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=3739$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=19053$ in human, and $n=19395$ in mouse.  That protein-abundance mediation audit reported bounded mediated fractions such as $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse.  Those values belong to a statistical abundance decomposition using modeled translation weights; they do not transfer automatically to turnover.  The present turnover row asks for an additional residual after expression, translation, structure-order, N-end, and baseline sequence controls, and that additional residual is absent.

\paragraph{Auxiliary finite contacts.}
The surrounding finite facts sharpen the boundary.  A structural-order audit over the same coordinate family found that the dominant surviving structural-order axis was $f_3\_\mathrm{stress}$, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip $p=0.03125$; the full coordinate block was significant only through that axis.  A dynamic mass-balance audit with $n=120$ genes and $718$ intervals gave $R^2(L_0)=0.9960628201518571$, $R^2(L_1)=0.996410628851051$, $R^2(L_2)=0.9712443377743347$, $R^2(L_3)=0.8953999568421264$, and $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, with $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$ and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  A separate observable-execution audit in yeast used $n=3289$ genes and concluded that at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed its effective null classes.  These rows show that the coordinate family can touch translation-adjacent and structure-adjacent summaries in bounded ways, but they also show why the turnover residual must be tested as its own finite question rather than inherited from a neighboring layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The current finite table supports a closed negative turnover-residual statement: in these matched rows, there is no evidence that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts measured protein turnover beyond expression, translation-efficiency information, pLDDT-like structure information, N-end information, amino-acid composition, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the residual density baseline.  The admissible readback is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q \not\leadsto Y_{\mathrm{turnover}}\ \mathrm{after}\ C
\end{aligned}
$$
for the present data surface, where the symbol records a failed residual improvement rather than a biological impossibility theorem.  The packet remains available as a translation-readout or codon-count residual coordinate in other finite panels, and it may participate in protein-abundance mediation where a separately computed $Q\to T\to P$ surface exists.  It does not enter a positive turnover-realization fit on this row set.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
This audit does not establish translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, degradation mechanism, selection, phenotype, or universality.  A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each of those conclusions.  In particular, a direct $Q\to$ turnover association is not admissible unless the relevant translation step and downstream protein-state readout are both present on the same controlled finite surface.  The present result is narrower and stronger: after the named turnover controls are already in the model, the additional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block has negative held fit and fails both resampling gates.
