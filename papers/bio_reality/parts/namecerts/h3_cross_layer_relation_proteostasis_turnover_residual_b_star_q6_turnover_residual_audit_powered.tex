\subsection{Proteostasis turnover residual audit for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-proteostasis-turnover-residual-audit}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The proteostasis audit is a finite-row test on matched coding-sequence, expression, translation, structural-confidence, N-end-rule, and measured protein-turnover rows. Its coordinate side is the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The downstream readout is not protein abundance and not translation efficiency; it is an observed turnover response after the lower-layer measurements have already been placed in the design. The question is therefore narrow: after expression, measured or bounded translation information, predicted-order or confidence information, and N-end features are controlled, does the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel still improve prediction of measured protein turnover?

\paragraph{Design and statistic.}
Let $Y$ denote the turnover readout on the joined protein rows. Let $L_2$ be the control model containing expression, translation-efficiency information, $pLDDT$-type structural-confidence information, N-end features, and the ordinary sequence controls used in the row join. Let $L_3$ be the same model with the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel appended. The registered comparison is the finite difference
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2),\\
\Delta DL &= DL(L_2)-DL(L_3),
\end{aligned}
$$
with positive values interpreted as evidence that the residual panel contributes beyond the lower-layer and proteostasis covariates. The audit used $n=3290$ genes and $B=200$ resampling or null draws. The fitted values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_2) &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta DL &= -13.68819707902732\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both contrasts move in the wrong direction for a positive turnover claim. The matched-bin null placed its $99\%$ reference value at $3.51918934107672$ bits and gave $p=0.8855721393034826$; the recoded null placed its $99\%$ reference value at $1.9038288477880905$ bits and gave $p=1.0$. Thus the residual panel does not clear either finite null boundary.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-protein mediation rows.}
The negative turnover result does not erase the lower-layer mediation record. In the matched translation-to-protein abundance matrix, the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates were evaluated across twelve organism-level joins. The mediation scalar was positive and within the unit interval in each listed organism, with examples including $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} on $5815$ joined proteins, $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 on $3739$ joined proteins, and $M^{QTP}=0.27206289160119157$ in \textit{Homo sapiens} on $19053$ joined proteins. Those numbers certify a bounded statistical decomposition for modeled translation readout and matched protein abundance. They do not license a turnover conclusion, because the turnover audit asks whether the same residual coordinates survive after proteostasis-relevant controls have already absorbed expression, translation, confidence, and N-end information.

\paragraph{Mass-balance comparison.}
A separate dynamic mass-balance run gives the same caution from a neighboring readout. On $120$ genes and $718$ intervals, the model recorded $R^2(L_0)=0.9960628201518571$ for the mass-only baseline, $R^2(L_1)=0.996410628851051$ after adding the estimated synthesis term, $R^2(L_2)=0.9712443377743347$, and $R^2(L_3)=0.8953999568421264$. The final residual addition had
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
\Delta DL &= -668.8046070381928\ \mathrm{bits},\\
p_{TE\,bin} &= 0.6831683168316832,\\
p_{time\,shuffle} &= 0.9306930693069307.
\end{aligned}
$$
This companion result is not a duplicate of the turnover audit, but it supports the same local interpretation: once the lower-layer rate and mass-balance slots are represented, appending the residual coordinates does not create a stronger proteostasis-level readout.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For the present data, $B^*_{Q6}$ remains bounded below the proteostasis-turnover layer. The admitted statement is only that no evidence was found that the residual coordinates predict measured turnover beyond expression, translation-efficiency information, $pLDDT$-type confidence, N-end features, and the ordinary sequence controls in the joined design. The correct local readback is a negative residual audit, not a biological impossibility theorem: the result closes the current joined-data domain against promotion, while leaving open the possibility that a different turnover assay, perturbation panel, or condition-matched proteostasis contact could be tested later.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
This chapter does not infer translation realization from the turnover fit, because translation requires its own readout contact and survival statistic. It does not infer folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality from $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates or from a direct residual-to-protein association. A positive protein-abundance mediation surface and a negative turnover residual audit may coexist: the first is a bounded statistical relation through a translation readout, while the second says that, after proteostasis controls are present, the residual coordinates do not add measurable turnover information in this finite row set.
