\subsection{Proteostasis Turnover Residual Audit for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}\label{sec:bioreality-proteostasis-turnover-residual-b-star-q6}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite audit surface.} This record tests whether the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis supplies information about measured protein turnover after the obvious upstream and protein-state summaries have already been accounted for. The finite row surface has $3290$ joined genes. Each row carries a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate vector, expression and translation-efficiency summaries, protein-state controls including pLDDT and N-end information, and a measured turnover readout. The audit therefore asks for a residual gain beyond expression, translation readout, predicted structural confidence, N-end information, and the other stated turnover controls; it does not ask whether the residual basis has translation contact in general.

\paragraph{Models compared.} The registered comparison has a control model $L_2$ and an augmented model $L_3$ in which the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ turnover residual block is added after the control surface. The finite-row values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 3290,\\
 R^2(L_2) &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
 R^2(L_3) &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
 \Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2)=-0.005461979433706483,\\
 \Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -13.68819707902732.
\end{aligned}
$$
The augmented residual block therefore lowers held-out explanatory performance on this surface. The direction is not a weak positive waiting for a permissive interpretation; it is a negative finite-row result for the turnover target after the declared covariates are present.

\paragraph{Null calibration.} The audit used $B=200$ null draws. Against the matched-bin null the empirical value was $p=0.8855721393034826$, with a null $99\%$ reference value of $3.51918934107672$ description-length bits. Against the recoded null the value was $p=1.0$, with a null $99\%$ reference value of $1.9038288477880905$ description-length bits. In finite-row terms, the observed $\Delta \mathrm{DL}$ is not merely below a promotion threshold; it is negative while both null calibrations place ordinary positive fluctuations above zero. The current audited conclusion is therefore that there is no evidence, on this joined turnover panel, that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts measured protein turnover beyond expression, TE, pLDDT, N-end, and the remaining controls.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance contacts.} This turnover result is deliberately narrower than the translation-mediated protein-abundance surface. On the protein-abundance mediation audit, twelve organisms had joined CDS and protein-abundance rows, including $5815$ proteins in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $19053$ in human, $19395$ in mouse, $3739$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, and $1656$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}. In that separate mediation surface, the joint nine-coordinate $Q \to T \to P$ decomposition had positive mediated fractions such as $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{S. solfataricus}. Those values justify a bounded statistical protein-abundance mediation readback on their own surface, but they do not transfer automatically to turnover. The turnover audit is a separate target, and its measured residual increment is negative.

\paragraph{Consistency with nearby negative checks.} The dynamic mass-balance audit gives the same caution at another downstream boundary. There the finite run used $120$ genes and $718$ intervals, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
 R^2(L_0,M\text{-only}) &= 0.9960628201518571,\\
 R^2(L_1,M,E_{\mathrm{stat}}) &= 0.996410628851051,\\
 R^2(L_2) &= 0.9712443377743347,\\
 R^2(L_3) &= 0.8953999568421264,\\
 \Delta R^2 &= -0.07584438093220836,\\
 \Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -668.8046070381928.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its TE-bin and time-shuffle calibrations, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$ and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$, also refuse promotion. Thus the turnover result is not an isolated wording choice; it is part of a pattern in which downstream protein-state or rate targets must earn their own finite-row gains.

\paragraph{Coordinate information retained.} The negative turnover verdict does not erase earlier coordinate-level contacts. The structural-order axis audit computed the same nine axes, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. In that surface, $f3\_stress$ was the only fold-level sign-flip survivor, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$, weighted absolute unit loading $0.5109586606724208$, and sign-flip probability $0.03125$. This says that a coordinate can remain useful for one bounded readout while failing to improve another. The turnover audit keeps precisely that separation: the basis is not globally rejected, but its turnover-residual promotion is refused on the present data.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.} The admissible statement is only this: on the audited $3290$-gene turnover surface, adding the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block after expression, TE, pLDDT, N-end, and related controls gives $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$ and $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=-13.68819707902732$ bits, with matched-bin and recoded null probabilities $0.8855721393034826$ and $1.0$. Therefore the present panel closes as a current-data negative for turnover-residual prediction. No translation realization, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter. Any such assertion would require its own layer-matched measurement contact and its own controlled statistic. A direct $Q_i \to$ turnover or $Q_i \to$ protein-state association is not admissible as a protein-realization result unless the same coordinate first survives the relevant translation-readout step and then improves the downstream readout under the stated controls.
