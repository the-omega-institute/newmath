\subsection{Proteostasis Turnover Residual Audit for $B^*_{Q6}$}\label{subsec:bioreality-proteostasis-turnover-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel has any remaining predictive contact with measured protein turnover after the usual expression and translation-linked covariates have already been admitted.  The finite rows are protein-indexed yeast rows with $n=3290$ eligible genes.  The candidate residual coordinates are not treated as direct protein-state measurements.  They enter only as a finite residual panel to be compared against a stronger baseline containing expression, translation-efficiency information, structure confidence through pLDDT where available, N-end information, and the simpler sequence controls.  The audited question is therefore narrow: after those controls are present, does $B^*_{Q6}$ add held-out explanatory power for turnover?

\paragraph{Audited statistic.}
The registered run \texttt{eab3c5dee757} evaluated a level-$2$ baseline and a level-$3$ baseline-plus-$B^*_{Q6}$ model on the same finite surface.  The baseline score was $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, while the augmented score was $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$.  Thus the observed increment was negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2_{L3}-R^2_{L2} \\
&= -0.005461979433706483,\\
\Delta DL_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -13.68819707902732.
\end{aligned}
$$
The permutation and recoding checks did not support a positive residual effect.  With $B=200$ resamples, the matched-bin null comparison gave $p=0.8855721393034826$ and a $99\%$ null reference value of $3.51918934107672$ bits.  The recoded comparison gave $p=1.0$ and a $99\%$ null reference value of $1.9038288477880905$ bits.  Since the observed description-length change is negative and far below both null reference values, the turnover panel supplies an honest non-promotion result: in the current data, there is no evidence that $B^*_{Q6}$ predicts measured turnover beyond expression, translation, pLDDT, N-end, and the declared sequence baselines.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
The negative turnover result is not a contradiction of the separate protein-abundance mediation surface.  On the broader $Q\to T\to P$ abundance analysis, organism-specific rows showed positive statistical mediation fractions for matched protein abundance after conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI.  For example, the yeast abundance panel had $n=5815$ joined proteins, $c_{\mathrm{total}}=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, an indirect component of $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  Several other organisms also had positive finite fractions, including \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 with $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$ on $n=3739$ joined proteins and \textit{Drosophila melanogaster} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$ on $n=13295$ joined proteins.  Those rows concern steady-state abundance under a modeled translation readout.  The present turnover audit asks a different question: whether the residual panel adds information for measured degradation or turnover after stronger proteostasis covariates are already present.  The answer on the audited surface is negative.

\paragraph{Relation to dynamic mass balance.}
The same caution is reinforced by the dynamic mass-balance run.  In the finite mass-balance panel, $n=120$ genes and $718$ intervals were used.  The $M$-only baseline already reached $R^2=0.9960628201518571$, adding the expression-rate statistic gave $R^2=0.996410628851051$, and later levels dropped to $R^2=0.9712443377743347$ and $R^2=0.8953999568421264$.  The audited increment was again negative, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  This means the proteostasis interpretation cannot be rescued by appealing to a dynamic closure surface: the available finite rows do not show a positive $B^*_{Q6}$ improvement once mass balance and rate proxies are already in the model.

\paragraph{Coordinate-specific context.}
The negative turnover audit also remains compatible with lower-layer coordinate contacts.  The modeled-tAI survival calculation computed nine residual axes, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  It found $81$ modeled coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ across only $3$ organisms, with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$, but that run explicitly remained an underpowered modeled-tAI contact rather than a ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.  A separate structural-order audit identified $f3\_stress$ as the only coordinate passing its fold-level sign-flip gate, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip $p=0.03125$.  These facts localize possible lower-layer structure in the coordinate system, but they do not overturn the turnover result, where the full residual panel worsened held-out performance.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The allowed conclusion is therefore a current-data closure statement for turnover: on the finite panel with $3290$ genes and $200$ null resamples, $B^*_{Q6}$ has no supported residual turnover signal beyond expression, translation-efficiency, pLDDT, N-end, and the declared sequence controls.  No translation mechanism, protein folding pathway, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, organism-level phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this audit.  A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as a direct perturbation or pulse-chase turnover surface, and it would still have to show that the same residual coordinate first survives the translation-readout step before any downstream protein-state interpretation is admitted.
