\subsection{Proteostasis Turnover Residual Audit for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}\label{sec:bioreality-proteostasis-turnover-residual-bstar-q6}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite audit surface.} The proteostasis turnover audit is a finite cross-layer test on matched gene rows. Each row carries a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual vector, expression and translation covariates, protein-state controls, and a measured turnover target. The tested residual coordinates are the nine projected axes $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The audit asks whether these coordinates retain predictive content for measured protein turnover after the ordinary expression, translation-efficiency, structural-confidence, and N-end-rule style covariates have already been admitted. The carrier is therefore not a direct codon-to-proteostasis assertion. It is a ranked residual comparison on $3290$ eligible genes with $200$ null draws, where the extra $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block is allowed to enter only after the lower-layer controls have taken their share of the variation.

\paragraph{Nested residual models.} Let $L_2$ denote the controlled turnover model before the final $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block, and let $L_3$ denote the same model after that block is appended. The registered run reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2(L_2) &= 0.055740851626933985,\\
R^2(L_3) &= 0.050278872193227464,\\
\Delta R^2 &= R^2(L_3)-R^2(L_2) = -0.005461979433706483.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the fitted turnover surface moves in the wrong direction under the stated held-out criterion: adding the residual coordinates does not improve measured turnover prediction. The description-length comparison is likewise negative, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta DL &= -13.68819707902732\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row readback is consequently a refusal of turnover promotion, not a weak positive. The allowed scientific statement is that, in the current data surface, there is no evidence that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicts measured protein turnover beyond expression, translation efficiency, pLDDT-like structural confidence, N-end related terms, and the other admitted controls.

\paragraph{Null calibration.} The audit uses two finite null comparisons. The matched-bin null gives $p=0.8855721393034826$, while the recoded null gives $p=1.0$. Their $99\%$ null reference levels for the description-length improvement are positive, not negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
DL^{99}_{\mathrm{matched\ bin}} &= 3.51918934107672,\\
DL^{99}_{\mathrm{recoded}} &= 1.9038288477880905.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against these references, the observed $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits is far below the empirical positive tail. The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block therefore fails both the direction test and the null-calibrated support test. This matters because the same residual family can have bounded contacts at lower or adjacent layers without earning proteostasis status. For example, in the translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition, the organism-level mediated fractions $M^{QTP}$ include $0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.6084594553474505$ in \textit{Bacillus subtilis} strain 168, and $0.27206289160119157$ in human rows, all computed as statistical mediation fractions rather than causal effects. Those protein-abundance contacts do not authorize a turnover claim unless the turnover audit itself improves.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring readouts.} The negative turnover result is also distinct from measured or modeled translation and mass-balance behavior. A dynamic RPF--protein closure run on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals reported $R^2(L_0)=0.9960628201518571$, $R^2(L_1)=0.996410628851051$, $R^2(L_2)=0.9712443377743347$, and $R^2(L_3)=0.8953999568421264$, with $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{TE\mathrm{bin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{time\ shuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$. That separate mass-balance surface likewise refuses a stronger dynamic propagation interpretation. A structural-order axis audit, by contrast, identified $f3\_stress$ as the single significant axis, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip $p=0.03125$ over $5$ folds. The present turnover chapter keeps these facts separated: an axis can be detectable for one bounded readout and still fail to improve a turnover target after proteostasis controls.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.} The finite conclusion is a current-data closure at the turnover boundary. The positive content is the completed audit: $3290$ rows were tested, $200$ null draws were used, the controlled $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ increment was negative, and both null p-values were non-supportive. The prohibited content is equally important. No translation realization, protein folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, mechanistic degradation claim, organism-level phenotype claim, or universal biological rule follows from these rows. A future turnover-positive statement would require a separate layer-matched contact that directly measures turnover or degradation rate, a same-row translation step that survives its own controls, and a positive held-out increment after expression, translation efficiency, protein-structure confidence, N-end and abundance controls, and simpler codon baselines have all been accounted for. Until then, $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ remains bounded here as a residual coordinate family with no demonstrated extra turnover power in the registered finite surface.
