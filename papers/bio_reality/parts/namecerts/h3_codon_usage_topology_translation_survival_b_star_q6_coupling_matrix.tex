\subsection{B-star-Q6 codon-usage residuals against bounded translation readouts}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-translation-survival-matrix}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This chapter records a finite BioReality test for the nine synonymous-residual coordinates denoted here by $B^{*}_{Q6}$.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f3$-stress.  The intended survival surface is a $9 \times K$ matrix $S_{ij}=S_{q_i}(r_j\mid Z)$, where $q_i$ is one of these residual codon-usage coordinates and $r_j$ is a translation-layer readout such as modeled tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein abundance only when that abundance is explicitly treated as a downstream readout.  The control block $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine coordinates.

\paragraph{Proxy coupling finite row.}
The direct finite-row coupling check used a $61$-codon count space and loaded three organisms: Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12.  The readout was not ribosome profiling and not measured tAI; it was the tRNA-Leu CUN-vs-UUR log-copy spectrum.  Against this proxy readout, all nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals were computed.  The break rule for this proxy was that the row would be null if every coordinate had $|A_{ij}|<0.01$.  The observed maximum absolute coupling was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}| &= 1882464.1106377328,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the number of nonzero entries above the stated threshold was $9$.  Thus the finite proxy coupling row did not meet its null break condition.  This is evidence that the chosen residual coordinates are not numerically invisible to the tRNA-Leu copy-spectrum proxy, but it is not yet a measured translation-survival matrix.

\paragraph{Modeled tAI survival surface.}
A separate rank-powered modeled-readout computation joined $40$ organisms and computed a $9\times 9$ matrix using modeled per-codon tAI from GtRNAdb anticodon copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The same nine residual coordinates were used as both the codon-side residual basis and the modeled tAI readout columns.  The residualized control design had $24$ control columns, rank $23$, residual degrees of freedom $17$, and residualized coordinate rank $9$.  The reported modeled translation-readout coefficient of determination was
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive-entry threshold for partial $R^2$ was $10^{-12}$, and the degenerate-fit cutoff was $0.999999$, so this modeled surface was rank-sufficient under its own diagnostic rule.  This row is stronger than the three-organism proxy coupling because it estimates a controlled $S^{QT}$-type matrix, but its readout remains modeled tAI from tRNA gene-copy and wobble weights.  It is therefore not a ribosome-occupancy observation, not measured elongation speed, and not a protein-output experiment.

\paragraph{Leucine gate sanity check.}
The CUN/UUR leucine check supplies a small-organism diagnostic for the same neighborhood of codon-usage geometry.  In the three loaded organisms, the partial Pearson association between log CUN/UUR codon usage and the tRNA-Leu copy ratio was $1.0$ with $n=3$, and the ordinary Pearson value was also $1.0$.  The observed log-ratio signs were $+1,-1,+1$, with numerical log ratios $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$.  The sign was therefore not uniform across organisms.  The point of this row is not universal direction; it is that the CUN/UUR coordinate has a finite contact with a translation-adjacent tRNA-copy spectrum while already showing context dependence.

\paragraph{Downstream abundance and complement diagnostics.}
Several related rows describe how the same nine coordinates behave when protein abundance or residual complement readouts are attached.  In yeast GFP-tagged abundance data, the two-condition comparison between YEPD and SD used $2209$ shared proteins.  The $S^{QP}$ surfaces had $R^2_{QP}=0.3280825603424665$ in YEPD and $R^2_{QP}=0.3050385538305022$ in SD, with $f3$-stress dominant in both conditions.  Their cosine was $0.9993775935182967$ and their Pearson similarity was $0.9983880256003688$ over $1000$ permutations.  In human tissue proteomics, the joined row counts were $9244$ for adult B cells, $6468$ for adult heart, and $8664$ for adult liver.  The corresponding $R^2_{QP}$ values were $0.03626117434944674$, $0.02320896321347296$, and $0.026133456228248052$, with dominant coordinates $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $Ile_{AUA}$ respectively.  Pairwise tissue cosines ranged from $0.6883655232187615$ to $0.9176793373754919$ over $1000$ permutations.

For the modeled-tAI complement of the abundance component, six organism rows were computed.  The complement fractions $d$ were $0.9208949355679455$ for Homo sapiens, $0.21173219572706703$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.6629960038578532$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.874628721049266$ for Danio rerio, $0.9400341806302775$ for Gallus gallus, and $0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. mc2 155.  The corresponding modeled mediation-side residual fractions $m=1-d$ were $0.07910506443205445$, $0.788267804272933$, $0.3370039961421468$, $0.12537127895073397$, $0.05996581936972245$, and $0.10273279812535108$.  These entries say that the abundance-associated $B^{*}_{Q6}$ component is not uniformly exhausted by modeled tAI; for example, yeast leaves a much larger modeled complement than human or chicken under this definition.

\paragraph{Ranking rows and regularization.}
The direction-aware supplementary-readout ranking used a stated regularization choice $\lambda=0.01$ with $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{col})$.  For Danio rerio, measured translation efficiency was the top ranked candidate with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_H C_H=0.011566051846174291$, score $0.001566051846174291$, $n=13140$, and a Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$.  For Homo sapiens, the top ranked candidate was complex membership with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_H C_H=0.006566030115340095$, $n=18335$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0002646171322365322$.  For Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the top ranked candidate was mRNA with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_H C_H=0.0011462622274400686$, $n=5128$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0013014749238591248$.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 had no selected $H_o^*$ in that ranking row, and Mycobacterium smegmatis str. mc2 155 had no assembled candidate ranking.  These values are descriptive alignments of residual coordinate support; they do not convert a candidate readout into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite record supports a bounded statement: the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates have nonzero proxy coupling against a tRNA-Leu copy-spectrum row, and they have a nondegenerate controlled modeled-tAI survival surface across $40$ organisms with $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$.  The stronger biological survival claim, however, requires organism-matched direct translation measurements or explicitly bounded translation readouts, paired to the same codon rows and residualized against amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline.  Nothing in the proxy coupling row, the modeled tAI row, or the abundance-side supplementary rows by itself establishes ribosome-mediated translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Each of those interpretations would require its own layer-appropriate contact and its own controlled statistic.
