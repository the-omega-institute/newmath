\subsection{The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Coupling Matrix Against Translation-Side Readouts}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-coupling-matrix-translation-survival}

\paragraph{Record.}
This chapter records a finite-row BioReality test for the claim that the residual codon-usage coordinates grouped as $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ are not exhausted by amino-acid composition and coarse organism context when they are compared with translation-side observables. The tested carrier is a $9 \times K$ matrix of residual coordinates $q_i$ against readout directions $r_j$. In the present finite row the computed coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q = \{&\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The codon count ambient space has $61$ coding codon coordinates. The organisms loaded in the finite row are $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$. The available readout for the coupling computation is the tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum. This is a proxy readout, not a direct ribosome-profiling, stAI, tAI, or protein-abundance survival matrix.

\paragraph{Residual geometry.}
For a residual codon coordinate $q_i$ and a readout weight $\ell_j$, the intended survival computation separates the synonymous component of $\ell_j$ from amino-acid composition and then tests whether the coordinate gives additional explanatory power after controls. The coupling statistic already computed in the finite row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ij} = q_i^{\mathsf T}\Sigma \ell_j^{\perp},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\ell_j^{\perp}$ denotes the readout direction after synonymous projection and $\Sigma$ is the residual count covariance. The full survival statistic is a controlled gain score
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{ij} = S_{q_i}(r_j \mid Z),\qquad
Z = (\mathrm{amino\ acid\ composition},\ \mathrm{organism},\ \mathrm{GC3},\ \mathrm{length},\ \mathrm{M\ density\ baseline}).
\end{aligned}
$$
In words, $S_{ij}$ is positive only when the residual coordinate improves the controlled description of the readout after the simpler baseline has already been allowed to explain what it can. A large or nonzero $A_{ij}$ is therefore only a coupling contact; it is not by itself a positive survival entry.

\paragraph{Finite-row coupling result.}
The break condition for the proxy coupling row was that all nine residual coordinates satisfy $|A_{ij}| < 0.01$ against the proxy readout. That break condition was not met. The reported threshold is $0.01$, the number of nonzero entries above the threshold is $9$, and the maximum absolute coupling magnitude is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j} |A_{ij}| = 1882464.1106377328.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every computed $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate has a nonzero proxy coupling entry against the tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum in the three-organism row. This supports the narrow statement that the nine-coordinate residual basis is visibly coupled to the available tRNA-copy proxy after the synonymous residual construction has been applied. It does not establish that any controlled survival score $S_{ij}$ is positive, because the direct translation readout rows and the full control design have not been attached to this matrix.

\paragraph{Leu CUN/UUR finite row.}
A companion finite row examines the Leu CUN/UUR codon-usage ratio against the tRNA-Leu copy ratio across the same three organisms. The recorded organism count is $n=3$. The Pearson correlation is $1.0$, and the reported partial Pearson correlation after the available amino-acid-composition control is also $1.0$. The signed log-ratio entries are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\ -0.24869078488829677,\ 1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with signs
$$
\begin{aligned}
(+1,\ -1,\ +1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The signs are not uniform across organisms. Consequently the finite row is best read as a context-dependent three-organism contact rather than as a direction law. The value $1.0$ in a three-row Pearson or partial Pearson statistic is too small a basis for a population-level conclusion, and the sign reversal is part of the data rather than a nuisance to be suppressed.

\paragraph{Survival status.}
The survival-matrix claim remains open at the translation-readout layer. A positive survival assertion would require at least one entry with
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{ij} > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
after amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, length, and M-density baseline controls, with the readout $r_j$ supplied by a direct translation-side or downstream measurement such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or matched protein abundance. The present finite row contains a proxy coupling matrix and a Leu tRNA-copy association, but it does not contain a Z-controlled partial-$R^2$ table or description-length-improvement table for true translation readouts. The strict local conclusion is therefore: $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ is not proxy-orthogonal to the tRNA-Leu CUN/UUR log-copy spectrum in the three-organism row, while the stronger survival statement still waits for matched organism-level readout rows and baseline ablation.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
No ribosome-mediated translation realization follows from the proxy coupling alone. No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, protein-output conclusion, or universal biological law follows from these rows. tRNA gene-copy counts are genome-annotation summaries; they are not tRNA abundance, charging, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein output unless a separate measurement supplies that contact. Likewise, mRNA abundance is only a control for translation-efficiency residualization, and proteomics is only a downstream abundance contact when identifier matching and the declared controls are actually present. The chapter therefore records a finite proxy-coupling contact and its open survival test, not a completed biological mechanism.
