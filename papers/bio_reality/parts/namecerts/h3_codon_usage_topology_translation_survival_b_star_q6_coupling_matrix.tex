\subsection{B-star-Q6 codon residuals and translation-layer survival tests}\label{sec:b-star-q6-codon-residuals-translation-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Coordinate packet.}
The object under study is the nine-dimensional synonymous-residual coordinate family denoted here by B$^{*}_{Q6}$.  The coordinates are the lysine, arginine, isoleucine, leucine, serine, threonine, and third-position stress contrasts
$K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}/\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f_3$-stress.  They live in the $61$-codon sense-count space after the amino-acid quotient is enforced, so their intended readback is not amino-acid composition itself but residual codon choice inside synonymous families.  The survival statistic for a translation readout $r_j$ is recorded as $S_{ij}=S_{q_i}(r_j\mid Z)$, where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, CDS length, and the simpler B$^{*}_{Q6}$ support-density baseline.  In the finite rows below, the usual control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ when organism-level rows are not included separately.

\paragraph{Proxy coupling row.}
The direct finite coupling computation used three organisms, Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K-12, and tested the nine B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates against a proxy readout: the tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum.  The break rule for this row was the null condition $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ for all nine coordinates.  The break rule was not met: all nine entries were nonzero at the stated threshold, and the maximum absolute coupling was $1882464.1106377328$.  Thus the proxy matrix contains a real finite-row codon-usage/tRNA-copy contact rather than a vacuous zero surface.

$$\begin{aligned}
\max_i |A_{ij}| &= 1882464.1106377328,\\
\#\{i: |A_{ij}|\geq 0.01\} &= 9,\\
\#\{\hbox{organisms}\} &= 3.
\end{aligned}$$

This row is deliberately weaker than a survival row.  It supplies a signed covariance-style coupling between residual codon coordinates and a tRNA-copy proxy, but it does not by itself estimate $S_{ij}$ against ribosome profiling, measured translation efficiency, protein abundance, or matched mRNA-controlled translation output.  The same three-organism Leu gate produced a Pearson value $1.0$ and a partial Pearson value $1.0$ for log CUN/UUR usage against the tRNA-Leu ratio, but the organism signs were $(1,-1,1)$ with log-ratios $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$.  The finite row therefore supports contact and sign heterogeneity, not a universal direction.

\paragraph{Modeled translation-survival row.}
A stronger but still bounded row replaces the three-organism proxy with modeled per-codon tAI derived from GtRNAdb anticodon copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  Across $40$ organisms, the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residual block and the modeled tAI readout block both had dimension $9$.  After the $24$-column control design was residualized, the rank of $Z$ was $23$, the residual degrees of freedom were $17$, and the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ design retained rank $9$.  The resulting modeled translation surface had shape $9\times 9$ and total $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$, below the declared degeneracy cutoff $0.999999$.

$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579,\\
\operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 23,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X) &= 9,\\
\mathrm{df}_{\mathrm{res}} &= 17.
\end{aligned}$$

A broader organism sweep of the same modeled-tAI type covered $41$ organisms.  Its per-organism $R^2_{QT}$ distribution had minimum $0.1579271837024072$, first quartile $0.26353954700074694$, median $0.33934845811598124$, mean $0.38466555440501393$, third quartile $0.5143951545826744$, and maximum $0.8213457220900503$, using $1000$ permutations.  These values show that the modeled readout is not concentrated in a single organismal row, although shared ancestry and gene-copy modeling remain part of the interpretation.

\paragraph{Measured ribosome-contact row.}
A separate ribosome-occupancy computation joined $5754$ genes after length checks, with $5754$ overlapping gene identifiers between the ordered CDS table and the occupancy file and no length mismatches.  The test demeaned occupancy within file-provided codon-position and amino-acid bins, then shuffled synonymous labels only inside the same bin.  All nine synonymous families had at least $200$ permutations.  With description-length penalty $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, $\mathrm{DL}=2$, and a positive-support rule $C_H>0$, the passing families were $K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, and $f_3$-stress.  This is a local ribosome-position separation result, not a full organism-wide $S_{ij}$ matrix, but it supplies measured ribosome evidence at the codon-window scale.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance and complement rows.}
For protein abundance, the translation-complement computation used six organisms.  The control residualizations again used $24$ columns and rank $23$.  The abundance component explained by B$^{*}_{Q6}$ had $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624987287115$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.10767526667768725$ in Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.04613529805760884$ in Danio rerio, $0.04454565117192484$ in Homo sapiens, $0.03980793595345331$ in Gallus gallus, and $0.032090091625816136$ in Mycobacterium smegmatis.  The modeled-tAI-complement fraction $d$ ranged from $0.21173219572706703$ in yeast to $0.9400341806302775$ in chicken, while the yeast complement mass $m=0.788267804272933$ was the largest recorded complement share in that table.  The support vectors $a_\perp$ remained nine-dimensional in every organism, with dominant supports differing by organism: yeast emphasized $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$ and $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, human emphasized $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$ and $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, and E. coli emphasized $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$ and $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$.

The direction-aware H-ranking computation reinforces the same boundary.  In Danio rerio, measured translation efficiency had $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, score $0.001566051846174291$, and exceeded a Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$ with null $95$th percentile $0.0003297458343745959$.  In Homo sapiens, complex membership, mRNA, PTM density, transmembrane count, measured TE, and localization exceeded their Bonferroni gates, whereas measured tRNA-tAI did not.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, mRNA and localization exceeded their gates, while measured tRNA-tAI and measured TE did not.  In Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 no H candidate exceeded the stated Bonferroni gate.  These entries make the non-translation residual organism-specific rather than globally resolved.

\paragraph{mRNA-stability and abundance mediation rows.}
Yeast mRNA half-life supplied a separate molecular readout on $5085$ joined rows.  The B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residuals explained $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$ of residualized log half-life, with dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$, and cosine $0.8040935468384264$ against the same-join protein-abundance surface.  The same yeast join had $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ and $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, so the half-life-conditioned absorbed component was $0.027596042074056304$ and the mediation fraction was $0.09994436477872035$.  The permutation null mean for that mediation fraction was $0.00019674487584783138$, the null $95$th percentile was $0.0014571003414947088$, and the right-tail permutation probability was $0.000999000999000999$.

The same stability analysis is not species-invariant.  In E. coli, $2720$ joined rows gave $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, cosine $-0.7784923375569599$, and permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$.  In human, $16165$ joined rows gave $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, same-join $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$, cosine $0.6351183883924848$, and permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$.  The human half-life join used $54$ consensus dataset columns, with $16150$ Ensembl-gene joins and $15$ symbol-fallback joins; the reported CDS join hit rate was $0.9779294769799313$.  The E. coli half-life join reported a CDS hit rate $0.9978649586335735$, while the yeast Neymotin join reported $0.9156038419146592$.

\paragraph{Condition, tissue, and coordinate decomposition rows.}
In yeast protein-abundance rows, the same B$^{*}_{Q6}$ design survived both rich and minimal media in a stable descriptive way.  On $2209$ shared proteins, YEPD had $R^2_{QP}=0.3280825603424665$ and SD had $R^2_{QP}=0.3050385538305022$; the dominant coordinate was $f_3$-stress in both conditions, with Pearson similarity $0.9983880256003688$ and cosine $0.9993775935182967$ across the two $S^{QP}$ surfaces.  In human tissue-specific protein abundance, adult B cells had $9244$ joined rows, $R^2_{QP}=0.03626117434944674$, and dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$; adult heart had $6468$ joined rows, $R^2_{QP}=0.02320896321347296$, and dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$; adult liver had $8664$ joined rows, $R^2_{QP}=0.026133456228248052$, and dominant coordinate $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$.  The tissue-pair cosines were $0.6883655232187615$ for B cells versus heart, $0.8990093450963941$ for liver versus B cells, and $0.9176793373754919$ for liver versus heart.

A per-coordinate reducibility audit on yeast used $1258$ joined rows, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, a base size $1800$, and control-basis rank $24$.  It produced all nine coordinate rows and found real-source support for eight named coordinates: $K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}/\mathrm{UCG}}$ coordinate was present in the table but was not listed among coordinates with a real measured source under that held-out criterion.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite rows justify a chapter-level positive statement of bounded residual survival: B$^{*}_{Q6}$ is not absorbed by amino-acid composition, GC3, length, organism context, and the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ support-density baseline on several translation-adjacent and downstream measured surfaces.  The strongest direct survival number in the provided table is the modeled-tAI matrix with $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$ across $40$ organisms and a nondegenerate residual rank.  Measured ribosome occupancy supplies a separate codon-window contact with three families passing the position-controlled gate under $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$.  Protein-abundance, mRNA-stability, condition, tissue, and decomposition rows show that the residual coordinates also interact with downstream readouts, but those rows must not be collapsed into a single mechanism.

The proxy coupling row remains narrower: it establishes nonzero B$^{*}_{Q6}$ coupling to a tRNA-copy Leu spectrum in three organisms, with maximum $|A_{ij}|=1882464.1106377328$, but it is not a ribosome-mediated survival estimate.  No conclusion about translation mechanism, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from these rows unless a separate matched contact supplies that layer and the corresponding controlled statistic is computed.
