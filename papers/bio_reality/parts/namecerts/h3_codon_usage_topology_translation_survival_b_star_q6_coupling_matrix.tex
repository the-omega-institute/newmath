\subsection{B-star-Q6 residual coupling against translation-layer readouts}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-residual-coupling-translation-readouts}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object of the finite row.}
The record concerns a nine-coordinate residual panel extracted after imposing the amino-acid quotient on codon-count space. The coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and the $f_3$ stress coordinate. The ambient codon-count space has $61$ codon columns before synonymous residualization. The intended survival matrix has shape $9 \times K$, where the columns are translation-relevant readouts such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, or protein abundance when those rows are identifier-matched and controlled. For a residual coordinate $q_i$ and a readout $r_j$, the readout weight is first projected to its synonymous component $\ell_j^{\perp}$, and the finite-row coupling is computed on residual count covariance as
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ij} &= q_i^{T}\Sigma \ell_j^{\perp}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The survival entry $S_{ij}$ is stricter: it is the controlled partial-$R^2$ or description-length improvement left after amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, CDS length, and the simpler $M$-density baseline have already been included.

\paragraph{Proxy coupling contact.}
The finite coupling run with identifier $4a783972e74b$ loaded three organism-level rows: $Homo\ sapiens$, $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, and $Escherichia\ coli$ K-12. Its readout was not a measured ribosome or protein row; it was the tRNA-Leu $CUN$-versus-$UUR$ log-copy spectrum. Against that proxy, all nine residual coordinates were computed, and the break condition was declared to be null if every coordinate satisfied $|A_{ij}| < 0.01$. The observed result did not meet the break condition: the nonzero-entry count was $9$, the threshold was $0.01$, and the maximum absolute coupling was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}| &= 1882464.1106377328.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite-row codon-usage topology result. It shows that the nine-coordinate residual panel is not numerically invisible to the stated tRNA-copy proxy under the synonymous projection used in the run. It does not by itself establish a positive survival entry $S_{ij}$, because no ribosome-profiling, stAI, tAI, TE, or protein-abundance measurement was paired with the full control set in this three-organism proxy row.

\paragraph{Leucine gate row.}
The same three-organism contact family also contains a focused $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$ gate run with identifier $675fdddb3187$. The reported Pearson value was $1.0$, and the amino-acid-controlled partial Pearson value was also $1.0$ at $n=3$. The organism-level log-ratio signs were not uniform:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\,-0.24869078488829677,\,1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
with corresponding signs $(1,-1,1)$. This row is therefore best read as a finite contact showing that the $CUN/UUR$ leucine residual can couple to the tRNA-Leu copy-ratio readout in a small organism panel, while the direction is context-sensitive rather than universal. The $n=3$ perfect correlations are not population estimates and are not promoted to a general biological law.

\paragraph{Modeled translation-weight survival row.}
A stronger but still bounded modeled-readout run is recorded under identifier $09209aa516a0$. This run used $40$ organisms and computed modeled per-codon tAI readouts from GtRNAdb anticodon copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights, then projected those readouts onto the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates. The readout matrix had $9$ columns, matching the nine residual coordinates, and the reported survival surface had shape $9 \times 9$. The control matrix had $24$ columns and rank $23$, leaving residual degrees of freedom $17$ after control residualization. The residual design for the $Q$ coordinates had rank $9$, and the run reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive partial-$R^2$ threshold used for entries was $10^{-12}$, and the degeneracy guard was $0.999999$; the observed $R^2_{QT}$ is below that guard. Thus this modeled row supplies a powered finite matrix for translation-weight survival under the declared controls, but only for modeled tAI derived from tRNA gene-copy and wobble-weight annotations. It is not measured ribosome occupancy, not measured elongation speed, not proteomics, and not a protein-folding or function assay.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following bounded statement. After the amino-acid quotient is imposed, the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain nontrivial contact with translation-adjacent readouts: the three-organism proxy coupling matrix has all nine entries nonzero above the $0.01$ coupling break threshold, the focused leucine gate has a partial Pearson value of $1.0$ at $n=3$ with nonuniform organism signs, and the $40$-organism modeled tAI panel has a controlled $9 \times 9$ survival surface with $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$ and residual degrees of freedom $17$. The scientific content is therefore a controlled, finite-row survival-and-coupling record for codon-usage residual geometry at the modeled translation-weight boundary.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation mechanism follows from codon usage and tRNA gene-copy tables alone. No ribosome-mediated translation realization follows unless a measured ribo-seq, TE, stAI, tAI, or equivalent translation contact is attached and analyzed with the stated controls. No protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from any positive $A_{ij}$ value, from the $n=3$ leucine gate, or from the modeled tAI survival surface. A positive coupling $A_{ij}$ is not the same object as a positive survival score $S_{ij}$; the latter requires explicit controlled partial-$R^2$ or description-length estimation after amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, length, and the $M$-density baseline have already been accounted for.
