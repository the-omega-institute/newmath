\subsection{Nieuwkoop E. coli mRFP Synonymous Window and Protein Output}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
This record concerns a finite synonymous-variant panel in an \emph{E. coli} mRFP reporter setting.  The row object is a variant-level table with $n=1459$ synonymous variants.  Each row carries the measured reporter protein-output readout, ordinary sequence controls, a real codon-adaptation index covariate, and the residual window coordinate denoted here by $B^{\ast}_{\perp}$.  The admissible readback is therefore not a genome-wide codon-usage law and not a free residual-to-protein assertion.  It is a finite comparison between a controlled baseline model and the same model after adding the projected synonymous-window coordinate.

The reported control-only baseline is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.12648386762689323,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the model that includes the residual window coordinate gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L3} &= 0.1798829276988051.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row increment is consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= R^2_{L3}-R^2_{L2} \\
&= 0.05339906007191191.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports a description-length improvement of
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the positive readout is not only a regression-scale increment but also an encoded finite-table improvement under the registered scoring rule.

\paragraph{Null comparison and finite resampling.}
The null audit used $B=200$ finite resampling draws.  Both the matched null and the within-design null place the observed improvement at
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453,\\
p_{\mathrm{within}} &= 0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
These two equal values are the finite-resolution one-hit tail probability for the reported audit size, and they give the row-level positive verdict its statistical meaning.  The result is not merely that the residual coordinate is nonzero in the table; it improves held-out protein-output fit relative to the declared controls and survives both null routes at the recorded resolution.

\paragraph{Real codon-adaptation control.}
The same finite run explicitly includes a real CAI comparison.  The real CAI covariate does not absorb the residual-window signal:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{real\_cai\_absorbs} &= \mathrm{false},\\
\mathrm{real\_cai\_robust} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The measured association between the real CAI axis and the proxy axis is modest,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\mathrm{real\ CAI},\mathrm{proxy}) &= 0.17884920449487354.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the chapter-specific claim is not that codon adaptation alone explains the reporter output.  The supported statement is narrower: after the recorded controls and the real CAI comparison, the projected synonymous-window coordinate still accounts for additional finite-row protein-output variation in this mRFP perturbation panel.

\paragraph{Start-window boundary audit.}
A nearby boundary audit on the same Nieuwkoop perturbation family used the same finite audit size, $B=200$, and the same variant count, $n=1459$.  Its start-window term was numerically negligible:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{start}} &= 1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9},\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{start}} &= 3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The downstream term did not support a stable positive boundary call:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{downstream}} &= -2.082967365015851,\\
p_{\mathrm{block}} &= 0.1691542288557214,\\
\mathrm{null}_{95} &= 0.5254770343527547,\\
\mathrm{sign\ stable} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The staged scores in that audit were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step1}} &= 0.06748192482378434,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step2}} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step3}} &= 0.6049438075310105.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary information matters because it prevents over-reading the positive mRFP protein-output result as a generic first-position or downstream-window rule.  The supported object is the registered residual-window protein-output increment in the finite panel, not a universal positional law.

\paragraph{Layer status.}
The finite result reaches a bounded protein-output association for this synonymous perturbation table.  It does not, by itself, establish a translation-rate measurement, a ribosome-occupancy mechanism, a folding consequence, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, causation, or a universal biological rule.  Any such stronger conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact: for example, direct translation readouts for translation, structural measurements for folding or order, physical assays for admissibility, functional assays for molecular function, and phenotype or fitness assays for system-level claims.  The present row set permits only the controlled statement that $B^{\ast}_{\perp}$ improves the finite mRFP protein-output fit beyond the declared baseline and real CAI comparison.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The claim is accepted at the finite-row protein-output level.  The decisive numbers are $n=1459$, $R^2_{L2}=0.12648386762689323$, $R^2_{L3}=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}}=19.089327473282296$, $p_{\mathrm{matched}}=p_{\mathrm{within}}=0.004975124378109453$, and the real-CAI audit outcome $\mathrm{real\_cai\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$ with $\mathrm{real\_cai\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$.  The computation completed in $305.514$ seconds for the primary mRFP protein-output run.  A separate cross-dataset transfer audit is recorded with $B=200$ and runtime $974.407$ seconds, but no additional scalar transfer verdict is used here.  The chapter therefore records a positive, controlled perturbation-level protein-output association, with all higher biological interpretations left outside the claim.
