\subsection{Nieuwkoop \\emph{E. coli} mRFP Synonymous-Window Protein Output}\label{subsec:bior reality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
This record concerns a finite synonymous-variant panel in an \\emph{E. coli} mRFP reporter, with each row carrying a coding-window perturbation, a protein-output readout, and controls that keep ordinary codon adaptation separate from the residual coordinate being tested. The registered run used $1459$ variants and compared a controls-only layer against a layer that adds the orthogonalized $B^{*}$ window coordinate, denoted here by $B^{\perp}$. The baseline controlled model had held-row explanatory power
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.12648386762689323,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the augmented model achieved
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L3} &= 0.1798829276988051.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row improvement was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched and within-window randomization checks both returned
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453,\\
p_{\mathrm{within}} &= 0.004975124378109453,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $B=200$ randomization draws. These numbers are the admissible readback for this chapter: a finite mRFP synonymous-window perturbation panel contains a residual protein-output signal after the stated controls, and the signal is not removed by the real-CAI control used in the run.

\paragraph{CAI separation.}
The CAI audit is essential because a residual synonymous coordinate is not allowed to inherit the usual codon-adaptation explanation by name alone. In this run the real CAI surface did not absorb the $B^{\perp}$ contribution, recorded as $\mathrm{real\_cai\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$, while the robustness flag was $\mathrm{real\_cai\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$. The measured correlation between real CAI and the proxy used for comparison was only
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\mathrm{real\ CAI},\mathrm{proxy}) &= 0.17884920449487354.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently the chapter does not read the positive residual as a disguised CAI effect. It reads it more narrowly: within this reporter and variant design, an orthogonalized synonymous-window coordinate improves protein-output prediction beyond the finite controls that were present in the run.

\paragraph{Start-window boundary check.}
A companion boundary audit on the same $1459$ variants prevents the result from being inflated into a generic start-proximal rule. In that audit the first-stage model had $R^2=0.06748192482378434$, the start-proxy step raised the fit to $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ with $\Delta\ell=628.6217154500001$, but the downstream $B$ component did not provide a stable positive residual after the preceding step. Its downstream increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
\Delta\ell_{\mathrm{downstream}} &= -2.082967365015851,\\
p_{\mathrm{block}} &= 0.1691542288557214,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $\mathrm{sign\_stable}=\mathrm{false}$. This localizes the powered mRFP finding: the accepted object is not a universal downstream-window law, but a finite protein-output association for the registered $B^{\perp}$ reporter design under the listed controls.

\paragraph{Relation to translation mediation.}
The broader translation-mediated protein-abundance record gives a separate benchmark for what a mediated cross-layer claim would require. In the matched multi-organism protein-abundance panel, the \\emph{E. coli} K12 entry had $3739$ joined proteins, total residual-to-protein fit $c=0.10767526677985896$, direct fit after modeled tAI conditioning $c'=0.04167869969498872$, indirect component $c-c'=0.06599656708487024$, and mediation fraction $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$. That benchmark is not imported as a proof of the mRFP perturbation mechanism. It is used only to keep the logical form explicit: a direct $Q_i \to P_k$ improvement is not the same thing as a demonstrated $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ chain. The mRFP run supplies a perturbation-level protein-output association; it does not by itself supply matched ribosome occupancy, measured translation efficiency, or a causal mediation path.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The finite verdict is therefore positive but bounded. The run supports the statement that, for the Nieuwkoop \\emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-window panel, adding $B^{\perp}$ to the controlled model improves protein-output prediction by $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ and $19.089327473282296$ description-length bits, with both registered randomization $p$-values equal to $0.004975124378109453$. The result remains a reporter-specific protein-output readout. It does not establish translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, mechanism, or universality. Any such stronger claim would require its own layer-matched contact: for example measured ribosome or translation-efficiency rows for translation, structural or physical assays for admissibility, and functional or phenotypic assays for biological consequence.
