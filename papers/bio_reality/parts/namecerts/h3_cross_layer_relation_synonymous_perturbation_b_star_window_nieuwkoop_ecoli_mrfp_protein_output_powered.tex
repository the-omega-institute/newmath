\subsection{Synonymous mRFP perturbation window in \emph{E. coli}}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite perturbation table for synonymous variants of an \emph{E. coli} mRFP coding sequence.  The tested surface has one protein-output column and a controlled synonymous-residual block derived from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate family.  The row count is not symbolic: the registered computation used $1459$ variants.  The readout is protein output for the engineered mRFP construct, while the fitted residual block is admitted only after the ordinary sequence controls and a real codon-adaptation baseline have had their chance to explain the same rows.  In this chapter the notation $Q$ denotes the finite $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate block, $C$ denotes the nuisance and codon-adaptation controls, and $Y$ denotes the measured mRFP protein-output readout on the variant rows.

\paragraph{Registered comparison.}
The finite test compares the control-only layer $L_{2}$ against the layer $L_{3}$ obtained by adding the residual block orthogonal to the controls.  The reported control fit is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}(Y\mid L_{2}) &= 0.12648386762689323,
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas the full fit after adjoining the controlled residual block is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}(Y\mid L_{3}) &= 0.1798829276988051.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite improvement on the registered table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^{2} &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both matched and within-null tests returned the same finite tail probability,
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453,\\
p_{\mathrm{within}} &= 0.004975124378109453,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $200$ null draws recorded.  The point of the comparison is not that a synonymous coordinate is visible before adjustment; it is that the residual block remains useful after the real codon-adaptation baseline is included.

\paragraph{Codon-adaptation control.}
The run explicitly checked whether a real CAI-style baseline absorbs the residual improvement.  It did not: the audit reports \texttt{real\_cai\_absorbs = false} and \texttt{real\_cai\_robust = true}.  The correlation between the real CAI baseline and the proxy baseline was only $0.17884920449487354$, so the positive residual result is not being read as a disguised copy of the proxy.  The finite claim is therefore narrower and more useful: in this synonymous mRFP perturbation table, the controlled $B^{*}_{Q6}$ window block adds protein-output information beyond the measured codon-adaptation control surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated matrix.}
The organism-matched cross-layer matrix supplies useful scale for this perturbation record but does not replace the perturbation table.  In the broader protein-abundance mediation computation, the \emph{E. coli} row joined $3739$ proteins and used one modeled translation readout together with $9$ residual coordinates after $24$ control columns.  For \emph{E. coli} the total residual-to-protein abundance association was
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{QP} &= 0.10767526677985896,
\end{aligned}
$$
with direct association after conditioning on the modeled translation readout
$$
\begin{aligned}
c'_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04167869969498872,
\end{aligned}
$$
and an indirect difference
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{QP}-c'_{QP\mid T} &= 0.06599656708487024.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding mediation fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= 0.612922252793667.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those numbers show that the same residual family can sit on a controlled translation-conditioned protein-abundance surface in \emph{E. coli}; they do not by themselves prove the causal path for the mRFP synonymous edit panel.

\paragraph{Coordinate identity.}
The computed residual coordinate list is finite and explicit:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The modeled translation-survival audit used the same nine-coordinate family and found $81$ bounded modeled-tAI coupling entries above the $0.01$ threshold across the three-organism modeled panel, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  That panel had only $3$ organisms, so it remained a bounded modeled-readout result rather than a powered measured-ribosome result.  A separate structural-order audit also identified $f3\_stress$ as the only fold-level sign-flip surviving axis, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip tail probability $0.03125$.  In the present mRFP chapter these numbers serve only to identify and constrain the coordinate family; they do not turn the perturbation result into a folding or structure claim.

\paragraph{Allowed conclusion.}
The finite-row conclusion is that the synonymous mRFP perturbation table supports a controlled protein-output residual association for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ window block.  The positive evidence is the simultaneous combination of $1459$ variant rows, $\Delta R^{2}=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, matched and within-null probabilities both equal to $0.004975124378109453$, and failure of the real CAI baseline to absorb the signal.  This licenses the local statement that, on this engineered \emph{E. coli} mRFP surface, synonymous residual coordinates retain predictive contact with protein output after the declared codon-adaptation and sequence controls.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
Nothing stronger is inferred here.  The chapter does not claim measured translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection, mechanism, or universality from the mRFP protein-output association.  A direct $Q\to Y$ improvement in the perturbation table is not treated as a proof of a $Q\to T\to P$ mechanism unless a separate same-row translation contact supplies the $Q\to T$ step and shows that conditioning on $T$ explains the downstream protein-output association.  Likewise, the modeled tAI and broader mediation numbers remain bounded readout context rather than direct evidence of ribosome dynamics in the mRFP assay.
