\subsection{Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP Synonymous-Window Protein Output}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite perturbation table for synonymous variants in an \emph{E. coli} mRFP coding context.  The tested surface has $1459$ variants and asks whether a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-window residual contributes to measured protein output after the declared lower-level controls have already been charged.  The row object is not a free association between codon geometry and protein abundance.  It is a controlled comparison between an $L_2$ design containing the baseline controls and an $L_3$ design in which the orthogonalized $B^{\ast}$ window term is added.  In the registered run, the baseline control fit had
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L_2} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{L_3} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191.
\end{aligned}
$$
The description-length gain was $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits.  Both the matched null and the within-design null gave $p=0.004975124378109453$ with $B=200$ resamples.  Thus the finite table supports a positive residual protein-output association in this perturbation setting, with the support localized to the measured mRFP output surface and to the specified control design.

\paragraph{Control role of codon-adaptation baselines.}
The run explicitly checked whether a real \emph{E. coli} CAI axis absorbs the residual.  It did not: $\mathrm{real\_cai\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$ and $\mathrm{real\_cai\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$.  The measured correlation between the real CAI axis and the proxy axis was only $0.17884920449487354$, so the positive increment is not simply a relabeling of a standard codon-adaptation score in this finite table.  This matters because amino-acid composition, organism context, GC3, length, and a simpler M-density baseline are treated as controls rather than promoted coordinates.  The admissible readback is therefore narrow: the added residual direction improves protein-output prediction beyond the declared baseline, but the coordinate remains a controlled synonymous-window residual rather than an unrestricted biological cause.

\paragraph{Boundary audit at the five-prime edge.}
The companion boundary audit used the same $1459$-variant scale to test whether the result collapses into a simple five-prime placement effect.  In that audit, the first-stage fit had $R^2=0.06748192482378434$, the second-stage model reached $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ with a log-likelihood gain of $628.6217154500001$, and the third-stage downstream residual term did not improve the fit: $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$ and $\Delta LL=-2.082965600280344$.  The downstream block null had $p=0.1691542288557214$, the downstream null $95\%$ cutoff was $0.5254770343527547$, and the sign-stability flag was false.  The start-window increment was effectively zero, with $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$ and $\Delta LL=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$.  This audit prevents the chapter from reporting a broad positional law: the positive mRFP protein-output result is retained as a controlled perturbation-table signal, while the five-prime boundary interpretation remains unsupported by that separate finite check.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
A broader multi-organism mediation calculation supplies useful calibration but does not replace the perturbation evidence here.  In that calculation, the \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655 row joined $3739$ proteins to codon counts and PAXdb abundance, used one modeled per-protein tAI readout, and retained a rank-sufficient design with $9$ residual coordinates and $23$ control rank.  For the \emph{E. coli} entry, the total residual-to-protein abundance value was $c=0.10767526677985896$, the direct value after conditioning on modeled tAI was $c'=0.04167869969498872$, the indirect difference was $0.06599656708487024$, and the reported mediation fraction was $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$.  Those numbers show that the same residual family has a bounded translation-conditioned abundance decomposition in an organism-matched abundance table.  They are not used to turn the Nieuwkoop perturbation result into a causal ribosome mechanism; they only provide a separate finite context in which translation-readout conditioning can be computed without treating direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association as sufficient by itself.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following bounded claim.  In the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous perturbation table, adding the orthogonalized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window residual to the declared control design improves measured protein-output prediction by $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ and $19.089327473282296$ bits, with matched and within-null $p$-values equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  The signal is robust to the real CAI check and is not licensed as a generic five-prime positional effect by the boundary audit.  The admissible interpretation is therefore a controlled synonymous-window protein-output association for this finite perturbation surface.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter by itself establishes observed translation dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or a universal biological rule.  A translation claim would require a separate translation readout such as matched ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.  A folding, physical, functional, or organismal claim would require its own layer-matched contact and statistic.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ improvement is not treated as protein realization unless the corresponding $Q_i\to T_j$ step is separately evaluated and survives the stated controls.  The present record stops at a finite, controlled mRFP protein-output association under the reported perturbation design.
