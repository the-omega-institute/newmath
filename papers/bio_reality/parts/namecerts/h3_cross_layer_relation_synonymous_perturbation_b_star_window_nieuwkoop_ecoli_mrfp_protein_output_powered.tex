\subsection{Nieuwkoop mRFP synonymous-window protein-output contact}\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
This record concerns a finite synonymous-variant panel for an \textit{Escherichia coli} mRFP coding sequence. The observed response is protein output on $n=1459$ variants, and the query coordinate is the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-window residual after the amino-acid sequence and the elementary codon-composition baselines have already been held fixed. The powered statistic used $B=200$ null draws. With the local controls alone, the fitted surface has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta DL &= 19.089327473282296\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched and within-panel permutation tails are both $0.004975124378109453$. Thus the finite row set supports a local protein-output association for this synonymous perturbation design: after the declared controls, adding the orthogonalized $B^*_{Q6}$ window coordinate improves held-out description of mRFP output by about $5.34\%$ of variance on the registered scale.

\paragraph{CAI ablation and residual status.}
The run explicitly checked whether ordinary codon-adaptation information could absorb the signal. The compact artifact used the real-CAI file at \texttt{/tmp/nieuwkoop\_mrfp\_compact\_real\_cai.json}; the real CAI coordinate had correlation $0.17884920449487354$ with the proxy axis. The audit reports \texttt{real\_cai\_absorbs=false} and \texttt{real\_cai\_robust=true}. In finite-row terms, this means the observed gain is not merely a rephrasing of the available CAI weight in this panel. The valid reading is narrower: the synonymous-window residual supplies predictive information about protein-output variation in this perturbation assay after the tested CAI control, not a global law for codon adaptation.

\paragraph{Five-prime boundary check.}
A companion boundary audit used the same $n=1459$ variant count and $B=200$ null draws to separate the start-proximal explanation from downstream window structure. Its three-step fit records
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step\ 1}} &= 0.06748192482378434,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step\ 2}} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step\ 3}} &= 0.6049438075310105.
\end{aligned}
$$
The step-two gain over step one is $628.6217154500001$ log-likelihood units, whereas the downstream addition has $\Delta LL=-2.082965600280344$ and $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$. The start-window increment is essentially zero, $\Delta LL=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$ and $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$, and the sign-stability flag is false. This audit prevents a stronger localization story: the mRFP panel is positive for the registered residual-output prediction, but it does not by itself isolate a stable five-prime start-window mechanism or a downstream codon-position mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to cross-layer mediation.}
The broader protein-abundance mediation surface is useful as a calibration of scope. In the organism-level translation-mediated protein-abundance audit, the \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 matched CDS/protein set contained $3739$ joined proteins. For that finite organism row set, the joint nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ decomposition recorded $c_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$, $c'_{QP\mid T}=0.04167869969498872$, indirect increment $0.06599656708487024$, and mediated fraction $0.612922252793667$ using modeled per-protein tAI as the translation readout. The same audit reports rank sufficiency for this organism with residual degrees of freedom $3716$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(T)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(X,T)=10$. These figures show that the mRFP perturbation result sits in the same empirical direction as a larger \textit{E. coli} protein-abundance decomposition, but they are not interchangeable: the large audit is cross-sectional and modeled-tAI mediated, while the mRFP result is a synonymous-variant protein-output perturbation panel.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
A separate modeled-tAI survival matrix supplies only a bounded translation-layer contact. It used three organisms, computed nine residual coordinates $\{\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},f3\_\mathrm{stress}\}$, and found $81$ entries above the $0.01$ bounded-coupling threshold, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$. That audit also states that three organisms are insufficient for a powered survival claim requiring at least $40$ matched species. Consequently, the mRFP panel should not be read as direct evidence for ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency unless a matched translation measurement is attached to the same variant rows.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite conclusion is positive but deliberately small: in this \textit{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-perturbation table, the registered $B^*_{Q6}$ window residual improves protein-output prediction beyond the local controls and the tested real-CAI covariate. The admissible statement is a controlled perturbation-level protein-output association. It is not, by itself, evidence for translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality. Any such higher-layer reading would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic on rows that include the relevant measurement.
