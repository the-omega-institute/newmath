\subsection{Nieuwkoop E. coli mRFP synonymous-window protein-output contact}\label{sec:nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} The finite record considered here is a synonymously perturbed coding-window surface for the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP assay.  Its rows are variant-level observations indexed by a controlled synonymous-window coordinate, an output readout for mRFP protein production, and covariates that keep codon-adaptation and gross sequence effects from being promoted into the coordinate itself.  The analyzed panel contains $1459$ variants.  The fitted comparison uses $B=200$ null or resampling blocks and compares an $L_2$ control surface against an $L_3$ surface in which the orthogonalized $B^{\ast}$ window coordinate is admitted after the controls.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row separation.} The registered run reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L_2} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{L_3} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched and within-design probabilities are both small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453,\\
p_{\mathrm{within}} &= 0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite assay does not merely record a codon-count contrast; it records a protein-output improvement for the controlled window coordinate on the finite mRFP perturbation surface.  The real-CAI audit is also informative: the real CAI proxy has correlation $0.17884920449487354$ with the proxy used in the compact construction, the CAI absorption flag is false, and the robustness flag is true.  In local terms, the $B^{\ast}$ window coordinate remains visible after the CAI route is exposed, rather than being absorbed by that simpler codon-adaptation scalar.

\paragraph{Boundary audit for the five-prime alternative.} The companion boundary audit prevents the mRFP result from being read as a generic first-window or start-proximal law.  On the same $1459$-variant scale with $B=200$, the start-window term has $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$ and $\Delta \ell=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$, while the downstream term has $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$ and $\Delta \ell=-2.082967365015851$.  The downstream null $95$ percent reference is $0.5254770343527547$, the block probability is $0.1691542288557214$, and the sign-stability flag is false.  The nested fit moves from $R^2=0.06748192482378434$ at step $1$ to $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ at step $2$, then to $R^2=0.6049438075310105$ at step $3$, with step-$2$ log-likelihood gain $628.6217154500001$ and step-$3$ change $-2.082965600280344$.  This keeps the mRFP support attached to the declared $B^{\ast}$ window statistic rather than to an unrestricted five-prime-position story.

\paragraph{Translation-readout limit.} The modeled translation-survival audit records only $3$ organisms for the modeled tAI bounded-coupling computation, with $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$. This matters locally: the mRFP perturbation result can be placed on a protein-output contact surface, but a translation-realization claim would require a separate measured or explicitly bounded translation readout tied to the same rows.

\paragraph{Local verdict.} The finite mRFP record supports a chapter-level statement of the following form: after controls, the $B^{\ast}$ synonymous-window coordinate improves the finite protein-output fit for the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP variants, with $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched and within probabilities equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  The claim remains confined to this controlled perturbation-output surface.  It does not establish ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organism-level phenotype, selection, or a universal codon law.  Any such stronger reading would need its own layer-matched contact and a separate finite statistic on rows that carry that layer directly.
