\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns the finite table of synonymous mRFP variants in \textit{E. coli} measured through protein output.  The readout is not a free association between a coding-sequence coordinate and an arbitrary phenotype.  The admissible surface is the controlled row system in which each variant contributes a synonymous coding-window perturbation, the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ window coordinates, the declared baseline controls, and the measured protein-output response.  The target run contains $1459$ variants and uses $B=200$ resampling or null draws.  The controlled comparison is the move from the level-$2$ control model to the level-$3$ model in which the orthogonalized $B^\ast$ window component is admitted.  In finite-row terms the observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{L3} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= 19.089327473282296\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two reported null comparisons agree: $p_{\mathrm{matched}}=0.004975124378109453$ and $p_{\mathrm{within}}=0.004975124378109453$.  The real-CAI robustness audit is also finite and local: the real CAI readout does not absorb the signal, with \texttt{real\_cai\_absorbs=false}, \texttt{real\_cai\_robust=true}, and real-CAI versus proxy correlation $0.17884920449487354$.

\paragraph{Protein-output interpretation.}
The positive entry is therefore a protein-output perturbation result for this specific mRFP synonymous-window panel.  The result says that, after the declared controls, the $B^\ast$ window component improves held-out or null-checked protein-output fit by about $5.34$ percentage points of $R^2$ and by $19.09$ description-length bits.  The row count is large enough for a finite perturbation claim within the panel, and the matched and within-panel null probabilities are both below $0.005$.  The appropriate readback is consequently narrow: these synonymous windows carry a measurable residual component for mRFP protein output in this experiment.  The result is not a population law over organisms, not a universal codon rule, and not a statement that any one coordinate direction such as CUN/UUR has a fixed biological sign outside the assayed table.

\paragraph{Translation-contact boundary.}
The modeled translation-contact packet remains bounded.  Across the three-organism modeled-tAI construction, the available organisms are \textit{E. coli} K-12, human, and yeast, with $61$ sense codons represented and no zero raw $W$ fallback in those three modeled-tAI readouts.  The modeled coupling matrix has $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$, but the same record explicitly states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival.  Thus the mRFP protein-output result cannot by itself be read as observed ribosome occupancy or as a completed measured translation-survival matrix.  It is a finite perturbation contact with protein output, supported by its own null checks, and it remains separate from any direct claim about ribosome dynamics.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter therefore admits the following statement and no stronger one.  In the Nieuwkoop \textit{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-window table, the controlled $B^\ast$ window component improves protein-output prediction relative to the declared controls, with $n=1459$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched and within-panel $p$-values both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  This supports a bounded cross-layer protein-output readout for this panel.  It does not establish translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality.  Any such higher-layer statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite controlled statistic.
