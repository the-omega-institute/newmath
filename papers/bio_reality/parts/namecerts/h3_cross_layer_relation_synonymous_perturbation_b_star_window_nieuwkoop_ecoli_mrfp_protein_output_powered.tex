\subsection{Nieuwkoop E. coli mRFP synonymous-window protein-output audit}\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite perturbation table from the Nieuwkoop $E. coli$ mRFP synonymous-variant assay.  The rows are individual synonymous constructs, the observed endpoint is protein-output variation for the reporter, and the tested BEDC-side object is the projected $B^*$ window residual component after the ordinary coding-sequence controls have already been fitted.  The finite table contains $1459$ variants.  The registered comparison used $B=200$ matched null draws and evaluated whether the residual window coordinate $b_{\perp}$ adds held-out explanatory power beyond the control layer.  In this chapter the notation $Q$ denotes the finite residual-coordinate block, $P$ denotes the reporter protein-output readout, and $Z$ denotes the local control surface including the ordinary synonymous and adaptation baselines available for this experiment.  The admissible readback is therefore not a free biological interpretation of codon geometry; it is the bounded finite-row statement that $Q$ improves a controlled model for the mRFP output rows in this dataset.

\paragraph{Controlled improvement.}
The fitted control model without the $B^*$ residual term gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2}(P\mid Z) &= 0.12648386762689323.
\end{aligned}
$$
After adding the residual window component, the full model gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L3}(P\mid Z,b_{\perp}) &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191.
\end{aligned}
$$
The description-length comparison points in the same direction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \mathrm{DL} &= 19.089327473282296\ \mathrm{bits}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched and within-design randomization tests both returned
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453,\\
p_{\mathrm{within}} &= 0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row verdict is positive for this particular reporter-output surface: the $B^*$ window residual is not absorbed by the registered control model and contributes a nonzero, permutation-supported increment for mRFP protein output in the Nieuwkoop $E. coli$ variant rows.

\paragraph{Adaptation-baseline audit.}
A separate real-CAI audit was attached because a positive synonymous-window result could otherwise be a disguised codon-adaptation effect.  The run reports that real CAI does not absorb the residual signal, with \texttt{real\_cai\_absorbs=false} and \texttt{real\_cai\_robust=true}.  The absolute correlation between the real-CAI score and the proxy adaptation score was only
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{real\ CAI,proxy}} &= 0.17884920449487354.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is important for the scope of the result.  The chapter is not claiming that CAI is irrelevant in general, nor that a single reporter experiment determines a species-wide law.  It says only that, on these $1459$ rows, the registered $B^*$ residual term remains informative after the available real-CAI control is included.

\paragraph{Five-prime boundary check.}
The companion boundary audit separates the main protein-output increment from a simple first-codons explanation.  In the $1459$-variant boundary run, the start-window residual increment was essentially zero,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{start}} &= 1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9},\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{start}} &= 3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The downstream residual did not provide a stable positive addition after the stronger intermediate model: the downstream increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{downstream}} &= -2.082967365015851,\\
p_{\mathrm{block}} &= 0.1691542288557214.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same audit reports \texttt{sign\_stable=false}, with a downstream null $95\%$ reference value of $0.5254770343527547$.  The stepwise model values were $R^2=0.06748192482378434$ at step $1$, $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ at step $2$, and $R^2=0.6049438075310105$ at step $3$.  These numbers prevent the chapter from being read as a broad positional law.  The positive result belongs to the registered finite residual window used in the protein-output audit, not to an unrestricted claim about every downstream synonymous position.

\paragraph{Relation to translation mediation.}
The broader $B^*_Q6$ mediation program supplies useful guardrails but does not replace the experiment-specific evidence.  In a separate modeled-tAI survival audit, only $3$ organisms were available, the modeled-tAI matrix had $81$ entries above the $0.01$ bounded-contact threshold, and the largest absolute entry was $3801961.031607896$.  That run explicitly remained modeled and underpowered for species-level survival because $3$ organisms are fewer than the requested $40$ matched species.  In a different cross-organism protein-abundance decomposition, $12$ organisms were joined, including $3739$ proteins for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, and the \textit{E. coli} modeled mediation fraction was $0.612922252793667$ with $c=0.10767526677985896$, $c'=0.04167869969498872$, and indirect increment $0.06599656708487024$.  Those surrounding audits show that translation-conditioned protein-abundance questions are finite and testable, but they do not turn the Nieuwkoop reporter perturbation into a measured ribosome-occupancy result.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The supported local statement is the following.  On the Nieuwkoop $E. coli$ mRFP synonymous-variant table, the registered $B^*$ window residual improves the controlled protein-output fit by $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ and $19.089327473282296$ description-length bits, with both matched and within-design randomization $p$-values equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  The signal is not removed by the real-CAI audit, and the separate five-prime boundary audit blocks a broad positional extrapolation.  The result is therefore a positive finite reporter-output contact for this construct panel.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No stronger biological layer follows from these rows alone.  The result does not by itself establish translation-rate causation, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal fitness, mechanism, selection, or a universal rule.  A direct $Q\to P$ improvement is admissible here only as controlled reporter protein-output prediction.  Any claim that the same coordinate realizes a translation step would require a separate translation readout such as ribosome profiling, translation efficiency, or a matched tAI/stAI contact evaluated on the same relevant rows.  Any claim about folding, function, phenotype, or physical feasibility would require its own layer-matched measurement rather than being inferred from synonymous residual geometry or from the mRFP output association.
