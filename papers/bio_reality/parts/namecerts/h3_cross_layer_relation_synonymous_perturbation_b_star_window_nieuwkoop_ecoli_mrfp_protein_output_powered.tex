\subsection{Nieuwkoop \textit{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-window protein-output readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-ecoli-mrfp-protein-output}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The Nieuwkoop \textit{E. coli} mRFP packet is a bounded perturbation readout for synonymous coding variation in a reporter setting.  The finite surface has $1459$ measured variants, and the fitted comparison uses $B=200$ resampling blocks.  The observed endpoint is reporter protein output rather than a direct molecular event such as ribosome transit, folding, catalytic activity, or fitness.  The coordinate used in the final row table is the B-star window component orthogonalized against the declared control surface, written here as $B_{\perp}$, and the comparison is made after retaining real CAI information as a separate baseline rather than letting the synonymous coordinate silently absorb it.

\paragraph{Controlled protein-output increment.}
The registered fit compares an $L_2$ control model against an $L_3$ model that adds $B_{\perp}$.  The control-only fit has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L_2} &= 0.12648386762689323,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the augmented fit has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L_3} &= 0.1798829276988051.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row gain is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta DL_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched and within-design tests both report
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453,\\
p_{\mathrm{within}} &= 0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, on this finite reporter surface, the B-star window residual contributes a positive and resampling-supported increment to mRFP output after the declared control layer is present.  The local conclusion is not that synonymous geometry is generally protein-realizing; it is that this specific perturbation table contains a measurable reporter-output component that is not removed by the control model used in the run.

\paragraph{CAI separation.}
The run records that real CAI does not absorb the B-star window increment: \texttt{real\_cai\_absorbs=false} and \texttt{real\_cai\_robust=true}.  The real CAI versus proxy correlation is only
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{CAI,proxy}} &= 0.17884920449487354,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is small enough to keep the CAI baseline and the B-star residual from being treated as the same finite coordinate.  This matters because the admissible claim is a residual-output statement under controls, not a restatement of ordinary codon-adaptation preference.  The compact finite artifact used by the computation is recorded as \texttt{/tmp/nieuwkoop\_mrfp\_compact\_real\_cai.json}; in this prose record it functions only as the row source for the reported scalars, not as an additional biological assumption.

\paragraph{Five-prime boundary audit.}
A companion boundary audit on the same Nieuwkoop family reports $1459$ variants and $B=200$ blocks.  Its stepwise fit separates a start-neighborhood component from the downstream B-star window component.  The first step gives $R^2=0.06748192482378434$; adding the second-stage term raises the fit to $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ with $\Delta LL=628.6217154500001$ against the first step.  The downstream third step then gives $R^2=0.6049438075310105$ and $\Delta LL=-2.082965600280344$ against the second step, with a downstream $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, block $p=0.1691542288557214$, and null95 threshold $0.5254770343527547$.  The start-window scalar itself is essentially zero, with $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$ and $\Delta LL=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$.  The audit also records \texttt{sign\_stable=false}.  Taken together, the positive mRFP-output result is best read as a controlled reporter-output increment on the declared B-star residual table, while the separate boundary audit prevents it from being promoted into a stable positional law about the five-prime boundary.

\paragraph{Allowed conclusion.}
The finite evidence supports a narrow claim: in the Nieuwkoop \textit{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-variant table, adding the B-star window residual to the control model improves held finite-row description of reporter protein output by $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$ and $19.089327473282296$ bits, with matched and within-design $p$-values both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  Because real CAI remains explicitly separated and does not absorb the increment, the result is a reporter-output residual effect rather than a codon-adaptation-only explanation.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter does not infer translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality from the reporter-output increment.  A protein-output association is not the same object as a measured translation step.  To cross that boundary, a separate layer-matched contact would have to provide translation readouts such as ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, matched mRNA controls, and then a controlled $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ mediation statistic.  Without that additional contact, the present result remains a finite synonymous-perturbation reporter-output record for \textit{E. coli} mRFP, not a general biological law and not a claim about folding, function, physical feasibility, or mechanism.
