\subsection{Human consensus mRNA half-life as a B$^*_{{Q6}}$ residual readout}\label{sec:bioreality-human-mrna-stability-q6}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Empirical question.}
This record asks whether the nine synonymous-residual coordinates of B$^*_{{Q6}}$ retain measurable association with human mRNA stability after the usual coding-sequence controls have already been removed.  The response variable is not raw decay time in minutes.  It is the unlogged mean normalized relative mRNA half-life consensus assembled across 54 human dataset columns in the Agarwal--Kelley table.  The tested biological layer is therefore a cross-layer association between codon residual geometry and a transcript-stability readout, not a direct translation, protein, structure, or function statement.

\paragraph{Joined human row set.}
The human computation joins coding-sequence codon counts to the consensus half-life table by Ensembl gene identifier after removing version suffixes, with a unique gene-symbol fallback.  The finite row audit reports 19,480 valid consensus records by Ensembl identifier, 19,053 joined CDS records before the half-life join, and 16,165 rows in the final regression table.  The source split is 16,150 rows through Ensembl gene identifiers and 15 rows through the symbol fallback.  There are no ambiguous symbol-fallback keys.  The reported CDS join hit rate is 0.9779294769799313, and the skipped CDS records are concentrated in 2,876 rows with no half-life match, with 12 duplicate-gene skips and no skips from empty sense-codon counts, invalid length, missing CDS header, missing gene keys, or nonpositive abundance.  On the consensus side, the audit records no duplicate Ensembl identifiers, no missing Ensembl identifiers, no nonnumeric consensus entries, and no non-object records.

\paragraph{Residual design.}
The tested coordinate block has nine columns:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control design has 24 columns: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions A, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, K, L, M, N, P, Q, R, S, T, V, W, Y computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected B$^*_{{Q6}}$ vectors.  The human row has $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ and residual degrees of freedom 16,142 after control removal.  Thus the statistic is not an amino-acid-composition proxy and is not a raw codon-usage correlation.

\paragraph{Human survival statistic.}
Let $M$ denote the human consensus relative mRNA half-life readout and let $Q$ denote the nine-dimensional B$^*_{{Q6}}$ residual coordinate matrix after projecting away $Z$.  The finite row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 16165,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 23,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q) &= 9,\\
 \|\widetilde M\|^2 &= 1422.0291144063049,\\
 \|\operatorname{Proj}_{\widetilde Q}\widetilde M\|^2 &= 14.936525062963877,\\
 R^2_{QM} &= 0.010503670362051522.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dominant coordinate in the human $S^{QM}$ profile is $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$.  A 1000-permutation comparison gives right-tail value $p=0.000999000999000999$, so the finite row is not treated as a null fluctuation under that permutation design.  The same joined human row set gives $R^2_{QP}=0.04095215557813418$ for protein abundance, and the angle comparison between the mRNA-stability and protein-abundance residual profiles has positive cosine
$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos(S^{QM}_{human},S^{QP}_{human})=0.6351183883924848.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dominant coordinate differs across the two profiles on the same join: $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$ for mRNA stability and $Arg_{AGR}$ for protein abundance.

\paragraph{Cross-organism orientation rows.}
Two companion finite rows give scale and sign context without being recomputed inside the human experiment.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the analogous mRNA half-life row has $n=5085$, $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ on the same join, dominant $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, and cosine $0.8040935468384264$ between $S^{QM}$ and $S^{QP}$.  In Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, the corresponding row has $n=2720$, $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$, dominant $Arg_{AGR}$, and cosine $-0.7784923375569599$.  These rows show that the human association is smaller in explained fraction than the yeast and E. coli anchors, while its mRNA--protein profile orientation is positive like yeast and unlike the E. coli sign.  They do not establish a phylogenetic law; they only locate the human row in a finite three-row comparison.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The human result supports a bounded statistical statement: after amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and M-density controls, B$^*_{{Q6}}$ residual coordinates explain a small but permutation-supported fraction of variance in a normalized human mRNA half-life consensus.  The strongest coordinate is the $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$ residual, and the human $S^{QM}$ direction is positively aligned with the same-row protein-abundance direction even though their dominant coordinates differ.  The association is therefore a transcript-stability readout attached to codon residuals, not a claim that codons cause mRNA decay.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter does not infer ribosome-mediated translation from the mRNA-stability row.  It also does not infer protein folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  The readout is a normalized cross-dataset relative half-life consensus, not raw minutes and not a direct decay-rate perturbation.  Any translation, protein-output, structure, physical-assay, functional, mechanistic, or universal claim would require its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic beyond the finite row reported here.
