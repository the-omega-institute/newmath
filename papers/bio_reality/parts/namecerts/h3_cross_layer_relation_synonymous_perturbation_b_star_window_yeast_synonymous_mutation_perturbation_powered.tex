\subsection{Yeast synonymous mutation windows with codon-stability correction}\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-window}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} The yeast perturbation packet is a finite-row test of whether a synonymous-window coordinate derived from $B^*_{Q6}$ retains predictive force after a codon-stability correction is attached. The observed panel contains $1783$ synonymous mutants mapped onto $20$ yeast genes, with $21$ coding-sequence map hits available for the correction step. The finite resampling depth is $B=200$, and the run completed in $289.093$ seconds under experiment id $6b547c2f841d$. The correction source is the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life table, read as a codon-stability coefficient
$$
\begin{aligned}
  CSC_c &= \operatorname{corr}\bigl(\operatorname{count}_c/n_{\mathrm{sense}},\log(t_{1/2})\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
computed across genes. The observed agreement between the real codon-stability correction and the proxy correction is $0.39717918221186976$, so the correction is not a duplicate of the proxy route. The finite verdict records $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$ and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$: in this panel the real codon-stability surface does not absorb the $B^*_{Q6}$ window contribution, and the window effect remains visible after the stated correction.

\paragraph{What is being tested.} The object is not a generic codon-bias association. It is a controlled perturbation readback in which synonymous substitutions keep the amino-acid sequence fixed while changing the local coding-window coordinates. The admissible readback is therefore a finite relation among a window coordinate $q_i$, a mutation-row response, and the codon-stability correction derived from matched yeast half-life information. The result can be written schematically as
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{window}\mid CSC} &> 0,\\
  \operatorname{corr}(CSC_{\mathrm{real}},CSC_{\mathrm{proxy}}) &= 0.39717918221186976,\\
  n_{\mathrm{mutants}} &= 1783,\qquad n_{\mathrm{genes}}=20,\qquad B=200.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\Delta_{\mathrm{window}\mid CSC}$ denotes the surviving finite-row improvement of the synonymous-window coordinate after the real codon-stability correction is included. The recorded booleans are part of the claim: the positive reading is not that codon stability is absent, but that the real codon-stability correction fails to eliminate the window signal on this finite yeast panel.

\paragraph{Relation to the larger translation boundary.} The yeast perturbation result sits downstream of a broader bounded translation route, but it does not by itself establish translation realization. In the multi-organism modeled-tAI mediation audit, the yeast organism has $5815$ joined proteins, $275$ all-tRNA copy counts, and no zero raw $W$ entries filled by the geometric mean. For yeast in that audit, the joint $9$-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ surface gives $c_{\mathrm{total}}=0.29837624994140827$, $c'_{\mathrm{given}\,T}=0.0151709093608293$, and an indirect component $c-c'=0.28320534058057895$, with mediated fraction $0.9491551041216973$. Those numbers show that the yeast background is compatible with a strong modeled translation-conditioned abundance decomposition, but the present synonymous-mutation packet remains narrower: it uses perturbation rows and codon-stability correction, not a direct ribosome-footprint or protein-abundance mediation matrix.

\paragraph{Yeast-specific control context.} A separate yeast structure-mediation audit reports $4759$ joined rows for a five-way yeast panel and uses controls including $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline. In that panel the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance association has $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, while conditioning on measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability lowers the direct component to $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$. Conditioning additionally on the 5-prime CDS MFE proxy gives $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$. These values are useful local context because they show that yeast has enough matched molecular rows to separate codon-window, transcript, translation, and structure-proxy covariates. They do not turn the present synonymous-mutant result into a structural or causal mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.} The admissible conclusion is that, on the finite yeast synonymous-mutant panel, the $B^*_{Q6}$ window coordinate remains predictive after a real codon-stability correction derived from mRNA half-life data. The strongest local statement is therefore a controlled perturbation-survival statement:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  &\text{synonymous amino-acid-preserving rows}\\
  &\quad +\ \text{real yeast codon-stability correction}\\
  &\quad +\ B=200\ \text{finite resampling}\\
  &\Longrightarrow\ \text{window signal not absorbed by the correction.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The claim is deliberately bounded. It does not assert that the synonymous window directly measures translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, selection, or a universal biological rule. Any such higher-layer statement would require a separate matched contact at that layer, with the same-coordinate translation step or downstream assay explicitly measured and controlled. This packet only licenses the finite yeast perturbation readback: $1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ genes, $21$ coding-sequence map hits, real codon-stability correlation $0.39717918221186976$ against the proxy, and a robust window signal not absorbed by the real correction.
