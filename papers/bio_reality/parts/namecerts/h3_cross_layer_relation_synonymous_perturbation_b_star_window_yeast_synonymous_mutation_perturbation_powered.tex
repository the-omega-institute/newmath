\subsection{Yeast synonymous perturbation under a B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window audit}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-perturbation-bstar-window}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
The yeast synonymous-perturbation record is a finite audit of codon-window changes rather than an unrestricted claim about synonymous biology.  The carrier consists of observed synonymous mutant rows whose coding-sequence windows can be mapped back to B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and then compared with a codon-stability-correlation control.  In the registered run, the finite surface contained $1783$ synonymous mutants, with $20$ genes retained in the gene-level audit and $21$ coding-sequence map hits.  The resampling depth was $B=200$, and the run completed in $289.093$ seconds.  The codon-stability-correlation score was computed from the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life table as $\mathrm{CSC}_c=\operatorname{corr}(\operatorname{count}_c/n_{\mathrm{sense}},\log t_{1/2})$ across genes.  The observed finite-row contrast was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{CSC}} &= 0.39717918221186976,\\
 n_{\mathrm{mut}} &= 1783,\\
 n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 20,\\
 B &= 200.
\end{aligned}
$$
The Boolean audit flags record that the real CSC control remained robust, $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$, while it did not absorb the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window signal, $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$.  Thus the allowed conclusion is a finite perturbation association: the yeast rows support a residual codon-window effect that survives the named codon-stability proxy rather than being reduced to that proxy.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The perturbation matrix is read as a relation between synonymous sequence edits and bounded sequence-window residuals.  It is not read as direct evidence for translation dynamics, folded state, molecular activity, cellular phenotype, or a general rule across organisms.  A direct residual-to-output association is not admissible as a protein-realization statement unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled translation-readout step.  The broader protein-abundance mediation audit supplies the relevant comparison scale: across $12$ organism-matched CDS/protein panels, the yeast row had $5815$ proteins, residual degrees of freedom $5792$, $9$ B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates, and a modeled-tAI mediation fraction $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ with $c_{\mathrm{total}}=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect component $0.28320534058057895$.  Those numbers show that a translation-conditioned protein-abundance decomposition exists elsewhere in the finite ledger, but they do not turn the synonymous-mutant window audit itself into a translation or protein-abundance perturbation experiment.

\paragraph{Yeast-specific controls and neighboring evidence.}
The yeast structure-mediation audit gives an additional local boundary for interpreting this chapter.  In the five-way yeast join, $4759$ rows were joined, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and an overall join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  The same yeast matrix recorded $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the structure-incremental fraction was $0.0005507778820193332$, and the right-tail permutation probabilities were $0.027972027972027972$ for $S_Q$ structure and $0.1258741258741259$ for the incremental structure term.  These neighboring yeast values help fix scale: B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals can register in finite cross-layer decompositions, but the synonymous-mutant record here remains a smaller perturbation surface with $20$ genes and must not inherit stronger claims from the abundance or structure partitions.

\paragraph{Cross-organism comparator.}
A human prime-editor synonymous perturbation audit supplies a scale comparison without being merged into the yeast conclusion.  That comparator used $9000$ synonymous variants, a compact synonymous surface of $24000$ rows, $1822$ genes, $B=200$, and a maximum of $3000$ analyzed variants per cell line.  Its gene-control note states that held-out folds were gene-block folds and that direct gene dummy fixed effects were not included because test genes were unseen in the training folds.  This comparator shows that the perturbation protocol can be applied to a substantially larger finite surface, while the yeast conclusion remains tied to the yeast row counts and the Neymotin-derived CSC control reported above.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite yeast result is therefore positive only at the bounded synonymous-window level: among $1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ audited genes, the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window signal is not absorbed by the real codon-stability-correlation control, with $\Delta_{\mathrm{CSC}}=0.39717918221186976$ and $B=200$.  The chapter permits the statement that a yeast synonymous perturbation surface retains B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual information beyond the named CSC proxy.  It does not permit a claim of translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  Those stronger readings require their own layer-matched measurements and controlled finite-row statistics.
