\subsection{Yeast synonymous-mutation perturbation window}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-perturbation-window}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This BioReality record uses a yeast synonymous-mutant table as a bounded perturbation contact for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates.  The finite surface contains $1783$ synonymous mutant rows distributed over $20$ genes, with $21$ CDS map hits in the run ledger.  The resampling depth was $B=200$, and the computation completed in $289.093$ seconds.  The row object is not an unconstrained genotype-to-phenotype assertion: each row is read only as a synonymous coding perturbation whose admissible comparison is against a controlled coding-sequence coordinate surface.

The coordinate side is the nine-dimensional residual packet already used by the surrounding $Q6$ readout computations:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{Q6}=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The yeast perturbation calculation therefore has a finite carrier of mutant-window rows, mapped CDS identifiers, $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, and codon-stability-correlation controls.  Its permitted readout is a perturbation-window statistic, not a direct claim about ribosome motion, folded protein state, organismal adaptation, or a general law of synonymous mutation.

\paragraph{Control channel.}
The run used a real codon-stability-correlation channel rather than only a proxy score.  The source was the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life table, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
CSC_c=\operatorname{corr}\left(\frac{\operatorname{count}_c}{n_{\mathrm{sense}}},\log(t_{1/2})\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
computed across genes.  The finite audit reported a real-versus-proxy correlation shift of $0.39717918221186976$.  The important bounded finding is that the real CSC channel was robust and did not absorb the perturbation-window signal: the run records $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$ and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$.  Thus the yeast mutant surface is not being explained away by replacing the proxy stability channel with the measured half-life-derived codon-stability channel.

\paragraph{Translation and abundance context.}
The same $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate family is compatible with a translation-readout route, but the present yeast mutant packet must stay inside its finite perturbation scope.  A separate modeled translation-survival calculation used $3$ organisms and reported $81$ modeled-tAI coupling entries above threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$ under dos Reis wobble weights.  That calculation explicitly remains modeled and underpowered for a broad survival claim because $3$ organisms are below the stated powered-survival requirement of at least $40$ matched species.  In the multi-organism protein-abundance mediation ledger, the yeast row had $5815$ joined proteins, $275$ all-tRNA gene-copy total, and no zero raw tAI weights filled by a geometric-mean fallback.  Its yeast mediation scalar was large on that bounded surface: $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, with total $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect difference $0.28320534058057895$.  These numbers show why the yeast perturbation window is biologically interesting, but they do not make the synonymous-mutant table itself a causal translation mechanism.

A separate yeast structure-abundance decomposition also warns against promotion.  On $4759$ joined yeast rows, the $Q$ to protein-abundance residual association had $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, while conditioning on residualized measured TE plus mRNA stability reduced the incremental surface to $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$.  The 5-prime CDS MFE component was small: $S_Q$ structure $R^2=0.003774248455422586$, structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$, and structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507778820193332$.  The perturbation-window record should therefore be read through controlled finite rows and attenuation checks, not as evidence that a synonymous coordinate has reached folded structure or molecular function.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For this yeast synonymous-mutation packet, the finite evidence supports a bounded perturbation-window readback: $1783$ mutant rows over $20$ genes were evaluated with $B=200$, a measured half-life-derived codon-stability channel was attached, and the real CSC channel did not absorb the window signal.  In compact form, the admissible conclusion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&n_{\mathrm{mut}}=1783,\quad n_{\mathrm{gene}}=20,\quad B=200,\\
&\Delta\rho_{CSC}=0.39717918221186976,\\
&\mathrm{real\ CSC\ robust}=\mathrm{true},\quad \mathrm{real\ CSC\ absorbs}=\mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough to keep the record as a positive finite perturbation contact for the yeast $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window, with the specific claim that the signal survives replacement of the proxy stability channel by the real CSC channel used in the run.

\paragraph{Non-promotion boundary.}
The chapter does not assert that the synonymous residual directly realizes translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  A $Q_i\to P_k$ protein-abundance association is not admissible as protein realization unless a same-coordinate $Q_i\to T_j$ translation readout has first survived its own controlled test.  Likewise, modeled tAI, codon-stability correlation, protein abundance, and mRNA half-life are separate measurement surfaces with different biases.  The present yeast mutant result is a finite controlled perturbation-window result; any stronger statement requires an additional layer-matched contact and its own statistic.
