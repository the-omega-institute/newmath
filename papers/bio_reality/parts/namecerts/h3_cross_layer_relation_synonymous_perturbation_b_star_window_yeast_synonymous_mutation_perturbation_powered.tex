\subsection{Yeast synonymous perturbation window for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-perturbation-bstar-window}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
The yeast synonymous-perturbation packet is a finite-row record over edited coding-sequence variants rather than a claim about all synonymous mutation.  Its observed table contains $1783$ synonymous mutants distributed over $20$ genes, with $21$ coding-sequence map hits in the reconstruction step.  The experiment was run with $B=200$ resampling units and completed in $289.093$ seconds under run id $6\mathrm{b}547\mathrm{c}2\mathrm{f}841\mathrm{d}$.  The codon-stability-control score used in the audit is not an abstract synonymy label: for each codon $c$ it is the Pearson correlation
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CSC}_{c}
  &= \mathrm{corr}\!\left(\frac{\#c}{n_{\mathrm{sense}}},\log(t_{1/2})\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
computed across yeast genes from the Neymotin et al. 2014 mRNA half-life source.  The relevant scalar comparison between the real stability-control surface and the proxy surface is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{CSC}} &= 0.39717918221186976 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit reports $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$ and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$, so the finite record supports the narrower statement that the $B^{\ast}$ window signal is not eliminated by replacing the proxy stability control with the measured yeast half-life-derived CSC control.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The biological object here is a synonymous edit table with a bounded $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window readback, not a direct measurement of ribosome movement or mature protein state.  The finite result may be written as an audited comparison between a mutant-level response vector $Y$, a window residual block $Q$, and a stability-control block $C_{\mathrm{CSC}}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Y &\sim Z + C_{\mathrm{CSC}} + Q,\\
\Delta_{Q\mid C_{\mathrm{CSC}},Z} &> 0 \quad \hbox{on the registered finite panel},\\
\mathrm{absorption}(Q;C_{\mathrm{CSC}}) &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This notation records a finite perturbation association after a yeast mRNA half-life-derived codon-stability control.  It does not by itself assert a translation-efficiency mechanism, a folding change, a molecular-function change, a physical-admissibility result, a selection law, or an organism-wide rule.  Those stronger readings would require layer-matched contacts that measure the relevant layer on rows aligned to the same sequence object.

\paragraph{Relation to the cross-layer mediation surface.}
The broader $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance mediation audit supplies a useful calibration boundary for yeast.  In the cross-organism protein-abundance table, the Saccharomyces cerevisiae slice joins $5815$ proteins, uses $275$ all-tRNA copy entries, and has no zero raw $W$ codon weights filled by a geometric mean.  After residualization by the declared controls, the yeast slice has $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, $\mathrm{rank}(X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(T)=1$, residual degrees of freedom $5792$, translation residual energy $6.90537888244409$, and protein-abundance residual energy $2958.1865890925337$.  Its statistical mediation decomposition reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973 .
\end{aligned}
$$
These yeast abundance numbers are not imported as proof that the synonymous-mutant window acts through translation in the perturbation dataset.  They only show that, on a separate matched protein-abundance panel, the same family of residual coordinates has a finite statistical route through a modeled per-protein tAI readout.  The present perturbation chapter therefore keeps two statements separate: the mutant panel gives a robust $B^{\ast}$ window signal after real CSC control, while the protein-abundance panel gives a distinct cross-sectional mediation surface.

\paragraph{Translation-contact boundary.}
A third finite audit clarifies why the perturbation result must not be promoted beyond its observed surface.  The modeled tAI survival run computed $9$ synonymous residual coordinates, including $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  It used $3$ organisms and found $81$ modeled entries above the $0.01$ bounded-coupling threshold, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  The same record explicitly marks the powered translation-survival claim as unavailable at $3$ organisms; the break condition is not met for bounded modeled coupling, but powered survival would require at least $40$ matched species.  Consequently the yeast synonymous-mutant row table is allowed to support a controlled perturbation association, while any claim of observed translation realization requires a separate ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency contact, any claim of protein realization requires matched protein rows downstream of a surviving translation step, and any folding, physical-admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality claim requires its own layer-specific evidence.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite verdict is therefore positive only at the perturbation-window level: on $1783$ yeast synonymous mutants across $20$ genes, the $B^{\ast}$ window readback remains robust when the control surface is tied to real yeast half-life-derived codon stability, with $\Delta_{\mathrm{CSC}}=0.39717918221186976$, $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$, and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$.  The chapter does not claim that the same rows establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, selection, or a universal biological law.  It records a finite synonymy-perturbation contact whose proper downstream use is as one bounded row surface in a larger controlled mediation program.
