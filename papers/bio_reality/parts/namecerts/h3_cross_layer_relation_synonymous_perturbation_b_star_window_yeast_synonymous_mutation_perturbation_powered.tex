\subsection{Yeast synonymous-mutation window contact for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-window}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The yeast synonymous-mutation audit tests whether a $B^*_{Q6}$ window score remains informative on a finite perturbation surface rather than only on organism-level codon summaries.  The observed table contains $1783$ synonymous mutants assigned to $20$ yeast genes, with $21$ successful CDS-map hits in the local reconciliation step.  The mutation rows are not read as amino-acid changes: they are synonymous edits whose admissible coordinates live in codon-window residual space after the amino-acid sequence is held fixed.  The row object is therefore a finite list of triples
$$
\begin{aligned}
  (w_a, g_b, y_c),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $w_a$ is the $B^*_{Q6}$ window quantity attached to the synonymous substitution, $g_b$ is the matched yeast gene or CDS key, and $y_c$ is the perturbation readout available in the mutation dataset.  The audit uses $B=200$ resampling units for the null comparison, and its runtime was $289.093$ seconds.  These numbers matter because the chapter is not claiming a qualitative anecdote; it is recording a bounded finite-row calculation with a declared row count, gene count, reconciliation count, and null budget.

\paragraph{Codon-stability control.}
The control used to guard against a trivial codon-frequency explanation is a real codon-stability-coefficient surface rather than a proxy-only baseline.  The coefficient source is the Neymotin et al. 2014 yeast mRNA half-life table, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{CSC}_c
  &= \mathrm{corr}\bigl(\mathrm{count}_c / n_{\mathrm{sense}},\, \log(t_{1/2})\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$
computed across genes for each codon $c$.  In the completed run, the real CSC surface did not absorb the $B^*_{Q6}$ window signal: \texttt{real\_csc\_absorbs=false}.  The robustness flag was positive, \texttt{real\_csc\_robust=true}, and the measured correlation between the real CSC contrast and the proxy contrast was $0.39717918221186976$.  The interpretation is narrow.  The result says that, on this finite yeast synonymous-mutant surface, the recorded window coordinate is not merely a rephrasing of the codon-stability proxy used in the control audit.  It does not say that the coordinate is a molecular cause, a ribosome-speed measurement, or a general law of synonymous mutation.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation and protein-abundance surfaces.}
The surrounding $B^*_{Q6}$ program contains larger translation-mediated protein-abundance computations, but this yeast perturbation chapter keeps them separate from the mutation-row claim.  In the cross-organism mediation surface, $12$ organisms were joined to CDS, tRNA-copy, and PAXdb abundance rows; the yeast row in that matrix used $5815$ proteins and produced a joint mediated fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
  M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
  R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
  R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
  R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those figures show that yeast is not an isolated bookkeeping accident in the broader finite matrices.  However, they are cross-sectional abundance statistics built with modeled per-protein tAI and PAXdb abundance, not direct evidence for what any individual synonymous mutant did at translation time.  Likewise, the yeast structure-mediated abundance decomposition joined $4759$ rows and measured
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
  R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
  R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
  R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542,
\end{aligned}
$$
with a structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$ and a structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507778820193332$.  These auxiliary surfaces supply context for why the perturbation audit is worth recording, but they do not promote the $1783$ synonymous-mutant result into a folding, abundance, or causal mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The correct readback is therefore a constrained perturbation statement.  A finite yeast synonymous-mutant table was evaluated with $1783$ mutation rows over $20$ genes, $B=200$ null resampling units, a real CSC control derived from yeast mRNA half-life, and a real-versus-proxy CSC correlation of $0.39717918221186976$.  Under that audit, the real CSC control did not absorb the $B^*_{Q6}$ window quantity, while the robustness flag remained positive.  The supported statement is that the $B^*_{Q6}$ window coordinate has finite-row perturbation signal in this yeast synonymous-mutation surface after the declared codon-stability check.  The unsupported statement would be stronger: that the coordinate itself establishes translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, selection, or universality.  None of those stronger readings follows from this table.  Each would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as measured ribosome profiling for translation, matched protein abundance for protein-output mediation, structure data for folding, physical assays for admissibility, functional assays for molecular role, perturbation-rescue designs for mechanism, and multi-organism controls before any population-level rule is asserted.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter is intentionally placed at the synonymous-perturbation boundary.  The positive finite-row audit survives a real CSC control, but the measured objects are still synonymous mutation rows, CDS mappings, gene assignments, and codon-stability-controlled window quantities.  Direct promotion from $Q_i$ to a protein or phenotype coordinate is not allowed here.  A later claim may use the same $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate only if it first attaches the appropriate readout surface and repeats the relevant controls: amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, the $M$-density baseline, matched mRNA abundance where translation efficiency is invoked, and simpler codon baselines.  Without that additional contact, the present result remains a bounded yeast synonymous-mutation perturbation audit, not a claim about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, selection, or a universal biological law.
