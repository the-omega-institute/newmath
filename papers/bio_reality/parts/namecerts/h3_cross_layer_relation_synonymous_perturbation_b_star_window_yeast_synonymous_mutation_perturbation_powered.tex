\subsection{Yeast synonymous mutation window audit}
\label{subsec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-window}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record isolates a yeast synonymous-perturbation panel as a finite cross-layer test surface.  The rows are synonymous coding-sequence mutants, not organism-wide comparative summaries.  Each row is read through a residual window coordinate derived from $B^*_{Q6}$, a codon-stability-correlation control computed from yeast mRNA half-life information, and the measured perturbation output attached to the mutant panel.  The carrier therefore has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{syn}} = \{(m_r,q_r,c_r,y_r): 1 \leq r \leq 1783\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $m_r$ is a synonymous mutant row, $q_r$ is the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ window value, $c_r$ is the matched codon-stability-correlation covariate, and $y_r$ is the reported perturbation readout for that row.  The executed audit used $1783$ synonymous mutants and mapped them onto $20$ yeast genes, with $21$ coding-sequence map hits recorded by the run.  The codon-stability-correlation source was the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life table: for each codon $c$, the control statistic was defined as the Pearson correlation between $\operatorname{count}_c/n_{\mathrm{sense}}$ and $\log(t_{1/2})$ across genes.  This is a row-level perturbation audit, not a claim that a codon coordinate has become a protein state.

\paragraph{Executed statistic.}
The registered run reported $B=200$ resampling or matched-null units, $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=20$, $n_{\mathrm{mutants}}=1783$, and runtime $289.093$ seconds.  Its central finite-row comparison was that the real codon-stability-correlation control did not absorb the residual-window signal.  In symbols, the audit outcome can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
  B &= 200,\\
  n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 20,\\
  n_{\mathrm{syn}} &= 1783,\\
  h_{\mathrm{CDS}} &= 21,\\
  \rho(\Delta \mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{real}},\Delta \mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{proxy}}) &= 0.39717918221186976,\\
  \mathrm{absorbed}_{\mathrm{real\ CSC}} &= \mathrm{false},\\
  \mathrm{robust}_{\mathrm{real\ CSC}} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The phrase ``not absorbed'' is used here in the narrow statistical sense supplied by the run: after replacing the proxy codon-stability direction by the real yeast half-life-derived codon-stability-correlation control, the residual-window audit still retained the registered robustness flag.  The finite number $0.39717918221186976$ is informative because it shows that the real and proxy codon-stability directions were related but far from identical; the robustness flag therefore does not merely restate that two identical controls were used.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
A separate matched cross-organism protein-abundance audit gives the larger mediation setting in which this yeast perturbation record should be read.  In that run, $B^*_{Q6}$ supplied $9$ residual coordinates, and the yeast protein-abundance panel joined $5815$ proteins.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the modeled per-protein tAI route had $275$ all-tRNA gene-copy entries and no zero raw-weight geometric-mean fallback.  After the declared controls, the yeast mediation scalar was
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
  R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
  R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
  M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers show why the yeast perturbation audit is scientifically relevant: the yeast background is one in which a modeled translation-readout conditioning variable can account for a large fraction of a residual-to-protein-abundance association in a separate finite panel.  They do not, by themselves, convert the synonymous-mutant window audit into a demonstrated translation mechanism.  The perturbation result is evidence that the $B^*_{Q6}$ window is not trivially erased by the real codon-stability-correlation control in the yeast synonymous-mutant rows; the mediation panel is a separate bounded comparison that helps locate the same coordinate family near a translation-conditioned protein-abundance interface.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The modeled translation-survival audit computed $9$ synonymous residual coordinates, including $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  It used $61$ sense codons and three organisms, with $81$ modeled tAI coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  The same audit explicitly kept the claim below powered survival because $3$ organisms are insufficient for the requested powered survival setting.  Thus the yeast synonymous-mutant record should be placed between two limits: it is stronger than a purely formal codon-coordinate note because it touches $1783$ finite perturbation rows, yet it is weaker than a measured ribosome-occupancy, translation-efficiency, or causal protein-realization claim.

\paragraph{Cross-panel calibration.}
The yeast outcome is also consistent with the broader synonymous-perturbation pattern without being reduced to it.  In the E. coli mRFP synonymous-output audit, the finite panel had $1459$ variants, $B=200$, $R^2_{\mathrm{controls}}=0.12648386762689323$, $R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B_\perp}=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched-null and within-null probabilities both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  That neighboring panel reports a direct protein-output readout in a different organism and construct system.  The yeast panel is narrower: it says that, in $20$ yeast genes and $1783$ synonymous mutants, the $B^*_{Q6}$ window remains robust after a real mRNA-half-life-derived codon-stability-correlation control.  The comparison supports an axis-transfer hypothesis across perturbation panels, but the yeast chapter itself only certifies the yeast finite-row outcome and its bounded relation to the translation-mediated protein-abundance route.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The admissible statement is therefore local.  There exists a yeast synonymous-mutant finite surface on which the $B^*_{Q6}$ window audit survives replacement of a proxy codon-stability control by a real codon-stability-correlation control derived from yeast mRNA half-life data.  The recorded correlation between real and proxy stability directions is $0.39717918221186976$, and the run explicitly reports $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$ and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$.  The result is compatible with a translation-mediated protein-abundance interface, especially in view of the separate yeast mediation scalar $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, but it remains a controlled perturbation-window readout unless joined translation and protein contacts are evaluated on the same required row structure.

\paragraph{What is not licensed.}
No translation-realization claim follows from this yeast window audit alone.  No ribosome dynamics, folding pathway, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule is established by the $1783$ synonymous-mutant rows.  A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not enough: the $Q_i \to T_j$ step must be supplied by a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, and the downstream $T_j \to P_k$ relation must be evaluated with matched protein abundance and matched mRNA abundance controls.  Amino-acid sequence, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline remain controls throughout this chapter rather than promoted coordinates.
