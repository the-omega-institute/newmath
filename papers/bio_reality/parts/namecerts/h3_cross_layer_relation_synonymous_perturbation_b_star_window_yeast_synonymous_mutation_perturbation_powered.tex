\subsection{Yeast synonymous-mutation perturbation under a bounded B-star window}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-perturbation-bstar-window}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a perturbation-level yeast contact for the claim key h3.cross\_layer\_relation.synonymous\_perturbation.b\_star\_window\_yeast\_synonymous\_mutation\_perturbation\_powered.  The finite rows consist of synonymous coding variants mapped back to yeast coding sequences, a B-star window score on the synonymous edit, and a codon-stability-control readout computed from yeast mRNA half-life data.  The run identified $1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ genes, with $21$ coding-sequence map hits in the local CDS map used by the analysis.  The permutation or resampling budget was $B=200$, and the registered run finished in $289.093$ seconds.  The codon-stability-control source was the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life repository: for each codon $c$, the statistic used
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CSC}_c &= \mathrm{Pearson}\left(\frac{\mathrm{count}_c}{n_{\mathrm{sense\ codons}}},\log(t_{1/2})\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
across genes.  This makes the readout a finite synonym-perturbation surface, not a direct assertion about translation dynamics or protein function.

\paragraph{Controlled question.}
The local question is whether the B-star window score on yeast synonymous mutations retains a perturbation signal after a real codon-stability-control axis is supplied, rather than after a proxy-only stability surrogate.  In the finite record, the real codon-stability-control axis did not absorb the B-star window signal: \texttt{real\_csc\_absorbs=false}.  The robustness flag was positive: \texttt{real\_csc\_robust=true}.  The measured agreement between the real CSC increment and the proxy CSC increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\Delta \mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{real}},\Delta \mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{proxy}}} &= 0.39717918221186976.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row supports a narrow perturbation statement: in this yeast synonymous-mutation panel, the B-star window coordinate is not merely a restatement of the supplied real codon-stability-control covariate.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The object carried by this chapter is a mutation-window perturbation readout, not a free-standing biological mechanism.  The admissible readback is the controlled association between a synonymous-window coordinate and the measured perturbation output after the codon-stability-control audit.  The chapter therefore treats amino-acid preservation, coding-window location, gene identity, codon composition, and the CSC construction as controls or audit surfaces.  They are not promoted into additional biological coordinates.  In particular, a direct residual-to-output association is not by itself a translation readout, a folding readout, a physical-admissibility readout, or a molecular-function assay.

\paragraph{Relation to matched protein abundance.}
A separate finite cross-layer record, h3.cross\_layer\_relation.translation\_mediated\_protein\_abundance.b\_star\_q6\_mqtp\_powered, provides a useful boundary check.  In that record, the Saccharomyces cerevisiae abundance panel used $5815$ proteins, with residual degrees of freedom $5792$ after controls.  The yeast mediation decomposition reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those numbers belong to a matched CDS, modeled-tAI, and protein-abundance panel, not to the synonymous-mutation experiment in this chapter.  They are included here only to keep the layer distinction explicit: the yeast perturbation result may be compared with a separately joined protein-abundance mediation surface, but it does not inherit the protein-abundance interpretation unless the same finite rows contain the needed translation and protein contacts.

\paragraph{Perturbation comparison.}
A second perturbation row, h3.cross\_layer\_relation.synonymous\_perturbation.b\_star\_window\_nieuwkoop\_ecoli\_mrfp\_protein\_output\_powered, gives a cross-system check on what a protein-output perturbation contact looks like when it is actually present.  That E. coli mRFP panel used $1459$ variants, had $B=200$, and reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{controls}} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B\perp} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296,\\
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= 0.004975124378109453.
\end{aligned}
$$
That comparison is deliberately conservative.  The yeast row is not rewritten as an mRFP protein-output claim; instead, the E. coli row illustrates that a protein-output assertion requires its own protein-output measurement surface.  The yeast row remains a yeast synonymous-mutation perturbation audit with a real CSC control.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
The finite verdict is positive only at the stated level.  The yeast synonymous-mutation panel supplies a bounded perturbation contact in which $1783$ synonymous mutants over $20$ genes survive the real CSC audit, with real CSC not absorbing the B-star window signal and with a real-versus-proxy CSC increment correlation of $0.39717918221186976$.  The result is therefore ready as a finite BioReality chapter for synonymous perturbation under codon-stability control.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No claim of translation realization follows from this chapter alone.  No claim of protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, selection, or universality follows from the yeast perturbation rows alone.  A claim at any of those layers would require a separate layer-matched contact, joined on the appropriate finite rows, with its own controls and null comparison.  The only supported statement here is that the yeast synonymous-mutation perturbation surface retains a B-star window signal after the registered real codon-stability-control audit.
