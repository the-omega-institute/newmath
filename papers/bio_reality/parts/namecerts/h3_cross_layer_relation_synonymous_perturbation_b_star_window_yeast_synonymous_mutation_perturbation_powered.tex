\subsection{Yeast synonymous perturbation with codon-stability control}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-perturbation}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
This record evaluates a yeast synonymous-mutation panel as a bounded perturbation test for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coding-window coordinates.  The finite surface contains $1783$ synonymous mutants distributed across $20$ genes, with $21$ coding-sequence map hits in the local reconciliation used by the run.  Each row is read as a same-gene synonymous edit together with its coding-window residual coordinates and the available yeast sequence-derived controls.  The run used $B=200$ resampling or null-evaluation units, so the reported conclusion is a finite-row perturbation audit rather than an unrestricted biological law.

\paragraph{Codon-stability coefficient contact.}
The yeast-specific control added in this audit is a real codon-stability coefficient rather than a proxy-only codon score.  The coefficient source is the Neymotin et al. 2014 yeast mRNA half-life table, with
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CSC}_{c} &= \operatorname{Pearson}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{count}_{c}}{n_{\mathrm{sense\ codons}}},\log(t_{1/2})\right)
\end{aligned}$$
computed across genes for codon $c$.  The finite run reports
$$\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{mutants}} &= 1783,\\
n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 20,\\
B &= 200,\\
\operatorname{corr}(\Delta \mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{real}},\Delta \mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{proxy}}) &= 0.39717918221186976.
\end{aligned}$$
The audit also records $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$ and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$.  Thus the real CSC axis is related to, but not interchangeable with, the proxy axis: the observed correlation is positive and moderate, while the finite decision says that the real CSC control does not absorb away the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window signal under the run's design.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated abundance surface.}
The perturbation panel is smaller than the cross-organism abundance mediation surface and should be read against that scale difference.  In the yeast row of the controlled $Q\to T\to P$ abundance decomposition, $5815$ proteins were joined, the residual degrees of freedom after the control set were $5792$, the per-protein modeled-translation residual energy was $6.90537888244409$, and the protein-abundance residual energy was $2958.1865890925337$.  The yeast mediation scalars in that surface were
$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}$$
Those numbers place yeast among the organisms where the modeled translation readout accounts for a large fraction of the residual-to-abundance association, but they do not by themselves validate the synonymous-mutation panel as a protein-output experiment.  The perturbation record is narrower: it asks whether real CSC control eliminates the window-coordinate signal in $1783$ synonymous edits across $20$ genes, and the finite verdict says that it does not.

\paragraph{Structure and mRNA-control context.}
A separate yeast five-way join supplies useful scale information for the local control environment.  That computation joined $4759$ yeast genes across coding sequence, protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, mRNA half-life, and a $5'$ coding-region minimum-free-energy proxy.  In that surface the single-and-joint residual values were
$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}$$
The same run reports $S_Q$ structure $R^2=0.003774248455422586$, a structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$, and a structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507778820193332$.  These values keep the present chapter focused: the yeast synonymous perturbation audit is not a folded-structure result, and the available structure proxy has only a small residual relation to the $Q$ coordinates in that joined surface.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a bounded statement: in this yeast synonymous-mutation panel, real codon-stability control is not sufficient to collapse the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window-coordinate perturbation signal.  The decisive row counts are $1783$ mutants, $20$ genes, $21$ coding-sequence map hits, and $B=200$ null units; the decisive scalar checks are the real-versus-proxy CSC correlation $0.39717918221186976$, $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$, and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$.  The allowed interpretation is therefore a controlled synonymous-perturbation contact at the codon-window and mRNA-stability-control boundary.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No translation claim follows from this chapter unless a separate matched translation readout supplies the $Q_i\to T_j$ step for the same perturbation rows or for a declared finite matching surface.  No protein-abundance, folding, physical-admissibility, molecular-function, organismal-phenotype, mechanistic, selective, or universal conclusion follows from the CSC audit alone.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not admissible here as protein realization unless it first passes through a controlled translation readout and then remains attached to a matched protein-abundance contact with mRNA abundance and simpler-baseline controls.
