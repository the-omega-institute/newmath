\subsection{Yeast synonymous perturbation inside a bounded $B^*_{Q6}$ window}
\label{subsec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-perturbation-bstar-window}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
The yeast synonymous-perturbation record is a finite row experiment on local coding-sequence edits rather than a claim about protein function or organismal phenotype.  Its carrier is the table of synonymous mutant rows for which a coding-window coordinate, an mRNA-stability codon score, and a sequence-matched yeast coding entry can be compared.  The run identified $1783$ synonymous mutant rows over $20$ genes, with $21$ coding-sequence map hits, and used $B=200$ null draws.  The codon-stability coefficient was defined from the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life table as a Pearson coefficient
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{CSC}_{c}
  &= \operatorname{corr}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{count}_{c}}{n_{\mathrm{sense}}},\log(t_{1/2})\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
across genes.  The reported finite scalar comparing the real coefficient to the proxy coefficient was
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{CSC}} &= 0.39717918221186976 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The two Boolean audit outcomes were asymmetric: the real coefficient was robust, while it did not absorb the perturbation signal.  In the row language of this chapter, that means the synonymous-window signal is not merely erased by substituting the measured half-life-derived codon-stability coordinate, but the result remains a local perturbation readout rather than a higher-layer biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated abundance rows.}
The same $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate family has a larger yeast protein-abundance mediation audit, and that audit fixes the interpretation boundary for the perturbation result.  In the yeast organism row, the joined protein-abundance table had $5815$ proteins, with residual degrees of freedom $5792$ after the control block.  The controlled covariates had rank $23$, the residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate block had rank $9$, and the modeled translation readout had rank $1$.  For yeast, the total residual-to-protein association was
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827, \\
  R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293, \\
  R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895, \\
  M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This comparison is not used to upgrade the perturbation rows into a causal translation mechanism.  It is used only to state that, in a separate finite surface, yeast is one of the organisms where conditioning on a modeled translation coordinate nearly removes the direct $Q\to P$ residual association.  The perturbation run therefore fits the same conservative grammar: a synonymous edit may be read as affecting a bounded coding-window coordinate, and the coordinate may be compared with translation or abundance rows only after the relevant readout is actually present.

\paragraph{Structure and mRNA-control context.}
A yeast five-way mediation table gives an additional guardrail against overreading the perturbation signal.  That table joined $4759$ yeast genes across coding sequence, protein abundance, measured translation efficiency, mRNA half-life, and a $5'$ coding-region folding proxy.  Its reported join hit rate was $0.8818572656921754$, and the coding-sequence join hit rate was $0.9156038419146592$.  In that table,
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803, \\
  R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995, \\
  R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287, \\
  R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the incremental structure fraction beyond translation and mRNA stability was $0.0005507778820193332$, and the corresponding permutation probabilities were $0.027972027972027972$ for the $Q\to S$ structure association and $0.1258741258741259$ for the incremental structure fraction.  These numbers show why the perturbation chapter must keep mRNA stability, translation efficiency, and protein abundance as separated readout axes.  A synonymous mutation row can be informative about a local coding-window residual, but a structure explanation is not licensed merely by the presence of a coding-window signal.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The broader modeled-tAI survival screen computed $9$ $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  That modeled screen used $3$ organisms, recorded $81$ entries above the coupling threshold $0.01$, and reached a maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  The same audit explicitly records that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival.  Thus the yeast perturbation row is compatible with bounded translation-readout contact, but it is not itself a ribosome-profiling measurement, a protein-abundance perturbation assay, or a universal codon law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is positive only in the restricted sense that the yeast synonymous-mutant table supports a robust codon-stability-related perturbation signal that is not absorbed by the real mRNA half-life-derived codon-stability coefficient.  The admissible readback is a controlled perturbation matrix over synonymous mutant rows, coding-window residual coordinates, and yeast mRNA-stability codon scores.  No translation realization follows from this table alone; no folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection principle, or universality follows without a separate layer-matched contact and a new controlled statistic at that layer.
