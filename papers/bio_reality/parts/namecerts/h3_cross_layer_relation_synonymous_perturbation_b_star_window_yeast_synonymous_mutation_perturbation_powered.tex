\subsection{Yeast Synonymous-Mutation Window Under a Coding-Stability Contrast}\label{subsec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-window}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record studies a yeast perturbation surface in which synonymous coding variants are treated as finite rows rather than as a license to infer a full biological mechanism.  The row carrier is a matched table of synonymous mutants, mapped coding sequences, a codon-stability-contrast score, and the bounded B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinates used to ask whether a synonymous perturbation carries a residual signal beyond a simpler codon proxy.  The realized audit contains $1783$ synonymous mutants, $20$ genes in the analyzed finite gene set, and $21$ CDS-map hits.  The computation used $200$ null draws and completed in $289.093$ seconds.  Its codon-stability-contrast contact is defined from Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life data by
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{CSC}_{c}
  &= \operatorname{corr}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{count}_{c}(g)}{n_{\mathrm{sense}}(g)},\log t_{1/2}(g)\right)_{g},
\end{aligned}
$$
where the correlation is taken across genes and $c$ ranges over codons.  The finite comparison reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{mut}} &= 1783, & n_{\mathrm{gene}} &= 20, & B &= 200,\\
  h_{\mathrm{CDS}} &= 21, & \rho_{\Delta\mathrm{CSC}} &= 0.39717918221186976.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\rho_{\Delta\mathrm{CSC}}$ is the reported correlation between the real codon-stability contrast and the proxy contrast.  The recorded decision bits are $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$ and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$, so the finite surface does not collapse into the real CSC covariate even though that covariate is visibly related to the proxy axis.

\paragraph{Readout discipline.}
The allowed conclusion is deliberately narrower than a biological mechanism claim.  The perturbation rows support a bounded statement about synonymous mutations in yeast: on this finite table, the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window retains a stable residual relation to the mutation-level readout after comparison with a real codon-stability contrast derived from yeast mRNA half-life.  The contact is therefore a perturbation-level codon-window readout with a measured finite response table; it is not, by itself, a direct observation of translation speed, ribosome transit, folding, physical feasibility, molecular function, adaptation, or a universal rule.  In particular, a direct residual-to-output association is not promoted unless a layer-matched readout supplies the missing contact.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation surface.}
The wider B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ mediation audit gives a useful bound on what may and may not be imported into this perturbation chapter.  In the cross-organism translation-mediated protein-abundance table, the yeast row has $5815$ joined proteins, $275$ all-tRNA copy counts, no zero raw modeled-tAI weights needing geometric-mean filling, residual degrees of freedom $5792$ after controls, and a modeled mediation fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
  M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
  R^{2}_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
  R^{2}_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
  R^{2}_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those numbers show that a yeast protein-abundance mediation surface exists in the finite archive, but the present synonymous-mutation window does not become a protein-realization result merely by sitting near that surface.  The perturbation chapter uses the mutation table, the CDS mapping count, the CSC comparison, and the robustness bits.  Translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and universality remain outside the chapter unless their own matched contacts are attached to the same finite rows.

\paragraph{Yeast controls and nearby measurements.}
A separate yeast abundance-residual computation supplies the scale of available yeast controls.  In that joined table, $4759$ genes are matched across CDS, translation efficiency, protein abundance, mRNA half-life, and a 5-prime CDS MFE proxy.  It reports $R^{2}_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^{2}_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^{2}_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^{2}_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  Its structure-only fraction is $0.003284564452662019$, its structure-incremental fraction is $0.0005507778820193332$, and the right-tail permutation probabilities are $0.027972027972027972$ for the $S$-from-$Q$ structure score and $0.1258741258741259$ for the structure-incremental score.  These values are not used as substitutes for the synonymous-mutation result; they only demonstrate why the perturbation claim must stay tied to its own finite rows and explicit controls.

\paragraph{Modeled translation boundary.}
The modeled tAI survival audit supplies another nearby but distinct contact.  It computed nine B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, namely $\{\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},f3\_\mathrm{stress}\}$, over three organisms.  It found $81$ modeled entries above threshold $0.01$ and a maximum absolute modeled coupling of $3801961.031607896$, but it also records that $3$ organisms are insufficient for a powered survival claim requiring at least $40$ matched species.  Consequently, the yeast mutation window may use the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate family as a controlled residual basis, but it may not recast modeled tAI coupling as measured ribosome occupancy or as a cross-species law.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is positive only at the perturbation-window level.  The real codon-stability contrast is related to the proxy axis with correlation $0.39717918221186976$, yet it does not absorb the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window signal; the recorded robustness bit remains true on the $1783$-mutant, $20$-gene yeast table.  The correct readback is therefore: a yeast synonymous-mutation surface retains a B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window residual after comparison with a real CSC covariate derived from mRNA half-life data.  No claim of translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organism-level phenotype, selection, or universality follows from this chapter without a separate layer-matched contact and a same-row statistic.
