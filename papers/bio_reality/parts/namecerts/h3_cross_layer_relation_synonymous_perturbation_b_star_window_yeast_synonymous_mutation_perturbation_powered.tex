\subsection{Yeast synonymous perturbation window under codon-stability control}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-mutation-perturbation}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
This record fixes a yeast perturbation panel in which synonymous coding changes are read through a bounded codon-window coordinate and then tested against a codon-stability control surface.  The finite table contains $1783$ synonymous mutants distributed over $20$ mapped genes, with $21$ CDS-map hits available to connect the perturbation rows back to coding-sequence coordinates.  The audit used $B=200$ null or resampling units and completed in $289.093$ seconds.  The codon-stability comparator was not a symbolic covariate: it was computed from the Neymotin et al. 2014 yeast mRNA half-life source, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CSC}_c &= \mathrm{Pearson}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{count}_c}{n_{\mathrm{sense\ codons}}},\log(t_{1/2})\right),
\end{aligned}
$$
where the correlation is taken across genes for each codon $c$.  Thus the tested surface has four finite ingredients: synonymous variant rows, mapped coding-sequence windows, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ nine-coordinate residual family, and an independently computed codon-stability coefficient vector.

\paragraph{Readout being tested.}
The question is not whether a synonymous change has some possible biological consequence in general.  The narrower question is whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window residual retains perturbation information after a real yeast codon-stability signal is attached.  The finite audit reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 20,\\
 n_{\mathrm{synonymous\ mutants}} &= 1783,\\
 B &= 200,\\
 \mathrm{CDS\ map\ hits} &= 21,\\
 \Delta\rho(\mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{real}},\mathrm{CSC}_{\mathrm{proxy}}) &= 0.39717918221186976.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive value of $\Delta\rho$ records that the real codon-stability coefficients are not merely interchangeable with the proxy control used in the same slot.  The run also records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs} &= \mathrm{false},\\
\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the local support statement: the real codon-stability coefficient does not absorb away the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window contribution on this finite yeast synonymous-mutant panel, and the conclusion remains stable under the declared $B=200$ audit.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated protein-abundance surface.}
The perturbation panel is interpreted against the broader translation-mediated abundance audit, not as an isolated proof of translation mechanism.  In the matched yeast abundance surface, the finite join contained $5815$ proteins for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  After residualizing the declared controls, the yeast entry had $\mathrm{rank}(X)=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(T)=1$, $\mathrm{rank}(X,T)=10$, residual degrees of freedom $5792$, and a valid mediated fraction in the unit interval.  The reported yeast mediation scalars were
$$
\begin{aligned}
 R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
 R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
 R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
 M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers explain why the synonymous-perturbation result is kept in the protein-abundance boundary family rather than treated as a free-standing codon-count association.  The yeast abundance surface says that a modeled translation readout absorbs most of the joint $Q\to P$ association in that cross-sectional protein-abundance panel; the synonymous-mutant audit says that real codon-stability control does not erase the local window perturbation signal in a smaller finite perturbation panel.

\paragraph{Controls and separated coordinates.}
The coordinate is deliberately not promoted into amino-acid identity, GC3, CDS length, organism identity, or M-density.  Those terms remain controls or baseline surfaces.  In the yeast abundance audit the control matrix had $23$ effective rank after including intercept, length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and M-density-style support.  The perturbation audit therefore reads the residual window signal only after the codon-stability comparator has been attached.  It does not license a direct statement of the form $Q_i\to P_k$ unless the intervening translation or stability readout is part of the finite table being evaluated.

\paragraph{Consistency with adjacent yeast readouts.}
Several finite audits constrain the interpretation.  A yeast structure-mediation join over $4759$ rows reported $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$, while the structure-only fraction was only $0.003284564452662019$.  This keeps the present chapter from reading a synonymous-window perturbation as a folding result.  A rate-slot audit in yeast used $3289$ genes and returned the finite conclusion that at least one observed rate-proxy subblock passed its effective null classes.  By contrast, a dynamic mass-balance audit on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals gave $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ with $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$ and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$, while the turnover audit on $3290$ genes gave $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  These neighboring negatives are part of the bounded reading: the synonymous-mutant signal survives the codon-stability control in its own finite panel, but does not become a general turnover, mass-balance, folding, or organism-level law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The ready verdict is therefore narrow.  On the finite yeast synonymous-mutant table, a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window residual remains informative after attaching a real codon-stability coefficient computed from yeast mRNA half-life data.  The decisive local scalars are $n=1783$ synonymous mutants, $20$ genes, $21$ CDS-map hits, $B=200$, $\Delta\rho=0.39717918221186976$, $\mathrm{real\_csc\_absorbs}=\mathrm{false}$, and $\mathrm{real\_csc\_robust}=\mathrm{true}$.  The result supports a controlled synonymous-perturbation readout in yeast.  It does not by itself establish translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, selection, phenotype, or universality.  Any such statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic computed on the corresponding finite rows.
