\subsection{Yeast synonymous perturbation window under codon-stability control}
\label{sec:bioreality-yeast-synonymous-perturbation-window}

\paragraph{Finite perturbation surface.}
The yeast synonymous-mutation contact is a finite perturbation table rather than a free association between a codon coordinate and a mature protein state.  Its rows are synonymous coding variants in yeast genes, its readout is restricted to the measured perturbation panel available for those variants, and its stabilizing covariate is a real codon-stability coefficient computed from yeast mRNA half-life data.  The registered run used $1783$ synonymous mutants drawn from $20$ genes, with $21$ CDS map hits and $B=200$ resampling units.  The codon-stability coefficient was not a proxy geometry term: for each codon $c$, $CSC_c$ was computed as the Pearson correlation between $count_c/n_{sense}$ and $\log(t_{1/2})$ across genes in the Neymotin et al. yeast mRNA half-life source.  The finite audit therefore attaches the perturbation rows to a yeast-specific transcript-stability readout before asking whether the B-star window coordinate still carries perturbation signal.

\paragraph{Controlled residual statement.}
The local claim is only that a B-star window residual can remain informative in this yeast synonymous-mutant table after replacing proxy codon-stability structure by the measured yeast CSC surface.  The relevant scalar diagnostic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{CSC} &= \operatorname{corr}(CSC_{real}, CSC_{proxy}) \\
             &= 0.39717918221186976,
\end{aligned}
$$
so the real CSC vector is related to, but not identical with, the proxy stability direction.  The run reports $real\_csc\_robust=true$ and $real\_csc\_absorbs=false$.  In this chapter that is read as a bounded residual result: the measured yeast CSC control does not fully absorb the B-star window perturbation signal on the finite mutant table.  It is not read as a protein-output theorem, a translation mechanism, or a universal law about synonymous mutations.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation-mediated protein-abundance matrix.}
The perturbation result is consistent with the broader Q-to-translation-to-protein mediation bookkeeping, but it is not a substitute for that bookkeeping.  In the cross-organism mediation run, the yeast protein-abundance join had $5815$ proteins, a residual degree count after controls of $5792$, one modeled translation readout, and nine B-star Q6 coordinates.  For yeast the total residual association between the joint Q coordinates and protein abundance was
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
R^2_{indirect} &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same yeast row recorded $X$-explained energy on $T$ equal to $5.671703404506426$, $T$-explained energy on $Y$ equal to $941.8858618613751$, and $X$ direct improvement energy given $T$ equal to $44.87838061554362$.  These numbers justify keeping a translation-conditioned route in the finite matrix, but the route is modeled through per-protein tAI and matched abundance rows; it does not turn the synonymous-mutant panel into a ribosome-occupancy experiment.

\paragraph{Yeast structure and mRNA controls.}
A separate yeast five-way join supplies context for why the perturbation result must remain residualized rather than causal.  That join used $4759$ matched yeast genes, reported a CDS join hit rate of $0.9156038419146592$ and an overall join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$, and decomposed B-star Q6 association with protein abundance after controls for length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and an M-density baseline.  The single and joint residual values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid S} &= 0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M} &= 0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S} &= 0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}
$$
The 5-prime CDS folding proxy accounted for only a small increment in this decomposition: $S_Q$ structure $R^2$ was $0.003774248455422586$, the structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, and the right-tail permutation value for the structure-increment statistic was $0.1258741258741259$.  Thus the yeast perturbation row should not be rewritten as a folding claim.  The measured CSC control and the structure proxy are finite covariates for bounding the residual, not evidence that the synonymous variants determine folded structure or physical admissibility.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
The modeled-tAI survival audit computed nine residual coordinates: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  It used three organisms, $61$ sense codons, a coupling threshold of $0.01$, and found $81$ modeled entries above threshold, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}$ equal to $3801961.031607896$.  The same audit explicitly classified the three-organism surface as modeled and underpowered for a powered survival statement because $3$ organisms are not enough for the requested multi-organism survival claim.  This matters for the present yeast perturbation packet: a real CSC-robust synonymous-mutant result may be registered as a bounded finite-row perturbation contact, but it must not be promoted to an observed translation-readout survival law without the matched translation rows.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite data support the following chapter-level statement.  In the yeast synonymous-mutant window panel, the B-star residual direction remains nonabsorbed after introducing a yeast mRNA-half-life-derived CSC control, with $1783$ mutant rows, $20$ genes, $21$ CDS map hits, $B=200$, and $\Delta_{CSC}=0.39717918221186976$.  The admissible reading is residual perturbation power under a real yeast stability covariate.  No translation realization, ribosome mechanism, mature protein abundance effect, folding conclusion, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, organismal phenotype claim, selection law, or universality follows from this row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant measurement and the same coordinate first survives the required controlled translation step.
