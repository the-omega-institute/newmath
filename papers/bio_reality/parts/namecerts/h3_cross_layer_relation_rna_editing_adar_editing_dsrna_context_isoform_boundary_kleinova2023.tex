\subsection{ADAR editing at a dsRNA isoform boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-adar-editing-dsrna-context-isoform-boundary-kleinova2023}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The ADAR-editing packet is recorded as a cross-layer relation rather than as a mechanism claim.  Its finite observed row is the Kleinova-style dsRNA-context and isoform-boundary test: the run identifier is \texttt{599de6cd4a6c}, the site count is $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=3137$, the deterministic permutation budget is $1200$, and the recorded runtime is $15.7578$ seconds.  These numbers are the entire finite readback carried by this row.  They say that a bounded RNA-editing boundary was assembled and replayed on a concrete collection of editing sites; they do not say that the BEDC coordinate geometry has identified the enzyme, the local RNA structure, the editing chemistry, a transcript fate, a protein-level consequence, or a universal rule.

\paragraph{What is being measured.}
The biological object is an ADAR-associated A-to-I editing readout near a double-stranded-RNA context and an isoform boundary.  The mathematical role of the row is narrower: it asks whether a finite collection of editing sites can be kept as a layer-crossing boundary record with explicit row count and null-replay budget.  In this chapter the carrier is therefore not an infinite transcriptome model.  It is the tuple consisting of the site ledger, the boundary predicate used to select the site context, the replay budget, and the final bounded status of the row.  The classification is $\mathrm{cross\_layer\_relation}$ because the test touches RNA sequence/context and transcript annotation at once; the classification does not promote the row to a pathway statement.

\paragraph{Local scoring convention.}
The broader direction-aware ranking machinery used elsewhere in the same BioReality family ranks candidate readouts by absorbed energy $A_H$, coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, and a description-length penalty.  The stated scoring rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda\,DL(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The RNA-editing row does not add a new $H$ channel to that ranking table; it is cited here to keep the boundary language calibrated.  In the ranked B*\_Q6 residual table, the strongest raw direction-aware products remain small: for \textit{Danio rerio}, measured translation efficiency has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, and $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$; for human, complex membership has $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, and $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$; for yeast, mRNA has $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, and $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$.  These comparison numbers matter because they show the intended scale of the finite readback: even when a ranked readout is available, the result is a small descriptive projection, not an explanation of biological cause.

\paragraph{Direction and null discipline.}
The finite ADAR row has $3137$ sites and $1200$ permutations, but no separate scalar in the row asserts a causal excess, a direction cosine, an absorbed-energy value, a Bonferroni pass, or a shared absorber.  Therefore the only admissible local conclusion is that the packet has a computable RNA-editing boundary instance.  The row is compatible with later enrichment, direction, or mechanism tests, but those tests would need their own finite columns: a declared statistic, a null distribution, a p-value or threshold rule, and a separate contact tying the statistic to the biological layer being claimed.  Without those columns, the run remains a bounded site-level record.

\paragraph{Relation to nearby boundary rows.}
The same finite-row discipline appears in other cross-layer packets.  The uORF/Kozak endogenous-translation-efficiency row records $n=10982$ and runtime $68.392$ seconds, while the ADAR row records $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=3137$ and runtime $15.7578$ seconds.  The comparison is not biological equivalence; it is a consistency check on reporting style.  Both rows are allowed to say that a concrete, fetchable panel was processed.  Neither row, by row count alone, is allowed to claim translation realization, RNA-editing mechanism, folding state, protein function, organism-level phenotype, or cross-organism universality.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback of this chapter is the finite assertion
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ADARBoundaryRow} &= (3137\ \mathrm{sites},\ 1200\ \mathrm{permutations},\ 15.7578\ \mathrm{s}),\\
\mathrm{layer} &= \mathrm{cross\_layer\_relation}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is enough for a BioReality chapter because the paper is tracking where a mathematical packet touches measured biological rows.  It is not enough to infer why an editing event occurs, whether ADAR binding is necessary or sufficient, whether a dsRNA stem is structurally realized in vivo, whether an isoform boundary changes translation or protein abundance, or whether the pattern generalizes beyond the assayed context.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this row.  The row also does not license treating $A_H$, $C_H$, Score, rank, or a chosen absorber as causal evidence.  Any future promotion would require a distinct same-row contact: for example, a direct ADAR perturbation, an independent structure-probing readout, an isoform-resolved RNA abundance effect, a translation-efficiency readout, or a protein-level consequence with its own null.  Until such a contact is present, the chapter remains a precise finite record of an RNA-editing boundary calculation.
