\subsection{ADAR editing as a finite isoform-boundary contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-adar-editing-dsrna-context-isoform-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row and scope.}
This record treats the Kleinova-style ADAR editing contact as a bounded cross-layer row rather than as a molecular explanation of the codon-coordinate residual.  The finite run attached to this row is deliberately small in what it certifies: it reports $3137$ editing sites, $1200$ realized permutations, and a runtime of $15.7578$ seconds under experiment run $599de6cd4a6c$.  These numbers certify that the isoform-boundary contact was instantiated and replayed as a finite test surface.  They do not certify an editing mechanism for any $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate, and they do not turn dsRNA context, isoform structure, or editing-site presence into translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Projection ledger used for interpretation.}
The surrounding cross-layer ledger ranks candidate readouts by absorbed residual energy $A_H$, coordinate-support alignment $C_H$, and a penalized score with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}})$.  The ranking rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - 0.01\,DL(H),\\
DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
For one-column readouts this subtracts $0.01$ from the raw directional product.  For the twelve-column localization readout the penalty is $0.03700439718141092$.  The ADAR row is therefore read beside an already conservative ledger: high absorbed energy is not enough, because the support must also point in the residual direction and must survive the stated complexity penalty.

\paragraph{Empirical scale of the background residual.}
The relevant background residual is not weakly biological by default.  In the direction-aware ranking run, human has $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490492302532$ and modeled-complement norm squared $1262.2237678771598$, giving $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455$.  Danio has $P_Q$ norm squared $1149.4722114308006$, complement norm squared $1005.3614101653926$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.874628721049266$.  Gallus has $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9400341806302775$, while yeast is much lower at $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.21173219572706714$.  These values explain why a finite isoform-boundary contact is interesting: a large modeled-translation-complement residual remains in several organisms.  They do not imply that RNA editing is the source of that residual.

\paragraph{Direction filter.}
The direction-aware table shows why the ADAR contact must remain a separate finite row until a same-row statistic is supplied.  In human, complex membership has $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, and $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, whereas PTM density has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but weaker alignment, $C_H=0.3604440741749213$, giving $A_HC_H=0.0053651472982577525$.  Measured TE in human even has negative directional alignment, with $A_H=0.002894714925796667$ and $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$.  Danio places measured TE first by raw directional product, $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, and $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, followed by mRNA with $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$.  Yeast places mRNA first but only at $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$.  The largest raw products stay small, and the best absorber changes by organism.

\paragraph{Null and penalty behavior.}
The finite ledger does contain Bonferroni comparisons, but these comparisons are not mechanism labels.  Danio measured TE exceeds its dof-matched Bonferroni threshold, with threshold $0.000719994971498518$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0003297458343745959$; mRNA also exceeds, with threshold $0.000587830414454545$ and null $95$th percentile $0.00023560090511497663$.  Human complex membership exceeds a threshold of $0.0002646171322365322$, and human localization exceeds a threshold of $0.002033625458961835$.  Yet the score penalty keeps human complex membership negative at $-0.0034339698846599055$, human mRNA at $-0.0037752227820994546$, and human localization at $-0.030109483117882037$.  Danio is the exception with small positive penalized one-column scores for measured TE, $0.001566051846174291$, and mRNA, $0.000734588577977589$.  Thus the table supports a bounded descriptive ranking, not a shared readout channel.

\paragraph{Cross-organism boundary signs.}
The independent Window6 boundary row gives a useful sanity check on the kind of finite biological statement that is allowed.  Across $15$ computed organisms, the $R$ set is avoided in all $15$ directional non-tie cases, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  The significant-organism subset contains $6$ avoided and $0$ preferred cases.  That is a finite association signature for a fixed boundary set.  It is not a license to identify an editing mechanism, an isoform mechanism, or a protein-function realization.  The ADAR row is even narrower: it currently contributes site count and permutation replay, not a direction-aware absorbed-energy table over editing-derived readout columns.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and turnover contacts.}
The broader ledger also shows that layer-matched readouts can be attached without becoming causal explanations.  Measured TE mediation is strongest in yeast, with measured mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$, while human is $0.0027966485036786744$, E. coli is $0.04091158340950673$, and danio truncates to $0.0$ from a raw value of $-0.04801486048721136$.  Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorbs $0.020817102845637092$ in danio, $5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5}$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast.  Human protein turnover is also finite but weak: $8057$ joined rows, $8164$ turnover proteins reported, absorbed fraction $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, null mean $0.0001262572958089998$, null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$, and no null exceedance.  A separate N-end degron turnover boundary has $3898$ joined rows, $12000$ actual permutations, and consistent sign fraction $0.5$.  These comparisons make the ADAR row interpretable as another candidate boundary contact, not as a promoted pathway.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The ADAR editing contact is accepted here only as a finite isoform-boundary replay: $3137$ sites were tested with $1200$ permutations.  The row is compatible with the larger observation that the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual is organism-specific and largely unexplained by the available measured panel.  It does not yet supply an editing-derived $H$ readout with its own $A_H$, $C_H$, Bonferroni threshold, description length, join count, or cross-organism sharing test.  Consequently the strongest warranted statement is a bounded one: ADAR/dsRNA/isoform context is a recorded boundary surface for future replay against the residual-coordinate ledger.  No translation realization, folding state, physical admissibility, protein function, editing mechanism, pathway claim, shared universal absorber, or global biological law follows from this finite row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that statistic.
