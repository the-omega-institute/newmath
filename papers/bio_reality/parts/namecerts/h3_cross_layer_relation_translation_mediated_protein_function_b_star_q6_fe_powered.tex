\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite matrix of rows indexed by a residual codon coordinate $q_i$, an intended translation readout boundary $T_j$, a protein-omics intermediary $P_k$, and a possible function or phenotype surface $F_l$.  In the present finite run, the observed surface is not a direct translation measurement and not a molecular function assay.  It is a lab knockout essentiality binary joined by protein identifier to real coding-sequence codon counts and measured protein abundance.  The run therefore tests a bounded cross-layer association: whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain finite signal against essentiality after amino-acid composition and measured abundance controls.  It does not certify the full chain $q_i \to T_j \to P_k \to F_l$.

\paragraph{Finite association readback.}
The finite readback statistic is an ordinary least-squares linear-probability residualization against the binary knockout phenotype.  With positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$, all nine coordinates are recorded as surviving in each organism, but the magnitude is small:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{FE}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.00966597112980509,\\
R^2_{FE}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.003317521425614316.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are finite residual associations after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, $M$-density, and measured protein-abundance adjustment.  They are not read as causal codon effects.  They are also not read as evidence that synonymous editing changes expression, translation speed, pathway state, or fitness mechanism.  The small positive entries justify only a narrow matrix entry: in these two organism panels, the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates retain nonzero residual association with a knockout-essentiality readout after the stated controls.

\paragraph{Mediation boundary.}
The intended biological chain distinguishes four layers.  The coordinate $q_i$ is an amino-acid-quotient synonymous residual.  A translation readout $T_j$ would have to be a measured or explicitly bounded translation surface, such as ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency with matched mRNA control.  A protein-omics readout $P_k$ would be an intermediary abundance or state measurement.  A function readout $F_l$ would have to be a separate functional or phenotype assay.  The present finite run attaches neither a measured translation row nor a function assay row.  It uses measured protein abundance only as a covariate in $Z$ and uses knockout essentiality only as the binary response.  Therefore it cannot establish the mediation inequality needed for $q_i \to T_j \to P_k$, namely that a surviving $q_i \to T_j$ step exists and that conditioning on $T_j$ absorbs or reduces the direct $q_i \to P_k$ contribution.

The correct local interpretation is consequently a bounded essentiality association at the protein-function boundary, not function realization.  A later matrix may promote the relation only if it joins the same CDS rows to a direct translation measurement, a matched protein-omics measurement, matched mRNA abundance, and then shows the declared attenuation pattern under the same residual controls.  Until that separate contact exists, no conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, organism-general law, or universality follows from this table.

\paragraph{Four-case decision surface.}
The present row set lands outside the full four-case translation/protein discriminant because $T_j$ is absent.  It is not a no-data null: two powered organism panels exist, both with full residualized coordinate rank $9$ and nonzero essentiality association.  It is not translation-only survival, because no measured translation row is joined.  It is not translation-mediated protein-omics survival, because the mediation step is untested.  The most precise case is protein-function-boundary association with function held null: the finite association survives the stated abundance-adjusted residualization, while the biological interpretation stops before translation mechanism, protein mediation, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, and universal law.
