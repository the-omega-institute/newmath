\paragraph{Layer interpretation.}
The intended cross-layer ordering is $q_i \to T_j \to P_k \to F_l$, where $T_j$ is an observed or explicitly modelled translation readout, $P_k$ is a protein-omics intermediary, and $F_l$ is allowed only after a separate function or phenotype contact is attached. The finite data in this packet do not yet supply a measured $T_j$ row such as ribosome profiling or translation efficiency, nor do they show that conditioning on $T_j$ absorbs a $q_i \to P_k$ association. They instead report a protein-abundance-controlled association between $q_i$ and a laboratory essentiality endpoint. Consequently the readback is bounded at the protein-function boundary: it is compatible with a later mediation test, but it is not itself the mediation test.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite-row outcome should be read through four local cases. Case $0$ is no residual survival after the declared controls. Case $1$ is translation-only survival, which would require a positive $q_i \to T_j$ row. Case $2$ is protein-omics or phenotype association not shown to be mediated by the measured translation row. Case $3$ is translation-mediated protein-omics survival, which requires both a positive $q_i \to T_j$ step and attenuation of the direct $q_i \to P_k$ term after conditioning on $T_j$. The present finite evidence occupies the bounded case-$2$ side for essentiality: it is a powered residual association after protein-abundance control, not a demonstrated translation-mediated route. Since no external function assay, perturbation mechanism, structure measurement, physical assay, or universal-panel contact is attached here, no translation realization, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, mechanism claim, phenotype generalization, or universal biological law follows from these rows.
