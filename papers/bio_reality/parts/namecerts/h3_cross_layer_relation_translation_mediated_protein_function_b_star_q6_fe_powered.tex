\subsection{A powered essentiality readback at the protein-function boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-mediated-protein-function-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record tests a bounded cross-layer question for the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates. The finite carrier is a gene-indexed matrix whose columns contain residual codon coordinates $q_i$, measured protein abundance controls, amino-acid composition controls, GC3, CDS length, and a binary laboratory knockout essentiality readout. The observed response is not a translation readout and not a direct molecular-function assay. It is a downstream phenotype-like laboratory binary joined by protein identifier to real CDS codon counts and measured protein abundance. For that reason the admissible conclusion is narrower than a function-realization claim: the row object can report whether $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates retain a beyond-expression association with essentiality after the declared controls, but it cannot by itself supply translation mediation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, or organism-general law.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate basis.}
The run computed nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates. The finite list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{Q6} = \{&K\_{AAA},\ Arg\_{AGR},\ Ile\_{AUA},\ Leu\_{CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ Leu\_{UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&Ser\_{UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ Ser\_{UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ Thr\_{ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ f3\_{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The coordinate count is therefore $9$. These coordinates are retained only as amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates: the control design includes the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, so a surviving entry is not read as ordinary amino-acid composition. The coordinate source was the finite $q$-vector table imported from the powered $B^*_{Q6}$ translation-survival computation route, but the readout in this run is essentiality after abundance control, not ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.

\paragraph{Control design.}
For each organism, the control block $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors, and $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\ ppm})$ as a measured protein-abundance control. Thus the statistical question is not whether codon residuals explain expression scale, but whether any residual association remains after measured protein abundance has already been accounted for.

\paragraph{Rank and row counts.}
Two organism-level finite matrices were powered for this readback. In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, the joined row count used for essentiality was $3737$ genes, with $285$ essential and $3452$ nonessential genes. The essential fraction was $0.07626438319507627$. The abundance join reported $3739$ rows before the final used set, and the unique essentiality label count was $3737$. The residualized design had $25$ control columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=24$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, response residual energy $232.13814987247684$, and explained energy $2.243840654793728$.

In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the joined row count used for essentiality was $5511$ genes, with $1168$ essential and $4343$ nonessential genes. The essential fraction was $0.2119397568499365$. The abundance join reported $5815$ rows before the final used set, and the unique essentiality label count was $5511$. The residualized design had $25$ control columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=24$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, residual degrees of freedom $5487$, response residual energy $826.488384003322$, and explained energy $2.741892921952373$.

\paragraph{Finite statistic.}
The binary essentiality readout was residualized with an ordinary least-squares linear-probability approximation. With the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold fixed at $10^{-12}$, all nine residual coordinates were reported as surviving coordinates in both powered organism matrices. The finite readback values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{FE}(\mathrm{E.\ coli\ K\mbox{-}12\ MG1655}) &= 0.00966597112980509,\\
R^2_{FE}(\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.003317521425614316.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both organisms had both essentiality classes present and both were marked rank-sufficient under the degeneracy threshold $0.999999$. The outcome is therefore a powered finite association result for a downstream binary laboratory readout after amino-acid composition and measured protein-abundance controls. It is small in magnitude, but it is not rank-vacuous in these two matrices.

\paragraph{Layer interpretation.}
The admissible reading is a four-way boundary reading. First, the result is not the null case in which every $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate disappears under the declared finite controls. Second, it is not translation-only survival, because no matched ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency response is the dependent variable in this run. Third, it is not demonstrated translation-mediated protein-omics survival, because no row-wise $q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ mediation matrix was computed here. Fourth, it is best classified as a downstream essentiality association after protein abundance has been controlled: a beyond-expression codon-residual association with a laboratory knockout binary readout. The association may motivate a later mediation test, but it does not already establish the mediation chain.

\paragraph{No-promotion rule for this chapter.}
The finite result must not be promoted beyond the readout actually attached to the rows. Essentiality is a laboratory knockout binary phenotype, not codon causality and not a molecular mechanism. Positive $S^{FE}$ entries are codon-residual associations after amino-acid composition and measured protein-abundance controls. They do not establish synonymous-edit causality, expression causality, translation causality, pathway causality, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype realization in general, or a universal biological law. A separate matched contact would be required for each stronger layer: ribosome or translation-efficiency data for translation, structure or stability data for physical protein state, functional or perturbation assays for molecular function, and broader organism-matched panels for any organism-general statement.

\paragraph{Boundary conclusion.}
The chapter records a powered two-organism finite-row result for the claim identifier $q6.translation\mbox{-}mediated.protein\mbox{-}function\mbox{-}boundary.cross\mbox{-}layer$. The evidence supports only this bounded statement: in the two joined matrices, the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates have nonzero residual association with laboratory essentiality after controlling for CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, $M$-density, and measured protein abundance. The result is compatible with later cross-layer mediation work, but the present rows stop before translation mediation and before function realization.
