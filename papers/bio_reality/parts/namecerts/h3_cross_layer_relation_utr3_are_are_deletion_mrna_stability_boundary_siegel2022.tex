\subsection{A UTR ARE deletion boundary for mRNA stability}
\label{sec:bioreality-utr3-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite panel of coding-RNA or transcript-linked windows in which the edited feature is a $3'$ UTR AU-rich element deletion, the readout is RNA half-life or stability, and the allowed interpretation is restricted to a bounded RNA-stability boundary.  The operative row is not a naked sequence token.  It is a tuple containing the deletion label, cell context, parent transcript or region identifier, RNA half-life readout, matched abundance or transcription controls when available, local sequence and isoform covariates when available, batch or dataset provenance, and a declared refusal condition.  In the verified ARE deletion run, the finite table retained $226$ BEAS-2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows.  The statistic was evaluated with $1000$ actual permutations, while the grading pass records $2000$ permutations as supported.  The corrected null was a pooled label permutation over deletions, because a within-region permutation was degenerate for the per-parent predictor.

\paragraph{Observed stability boundary.}
The finite result is a sign-concordant RNA-stability contact for the ARE deletion panel.  Written schematically, the permitted observation has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_i &\in \{\text{ARE-deleted},\text{matched control}\},\\
H_i &= \text{RNA half-life or stability readout},\\
Z_i &= \text{cell context, parent transcript, composition, isoform, abundance, and batch controls},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= \operatorname{stat}(H_i \mid D_i,Z_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
The run identified by \texttt{a41440ab2792} reports \texttt{sign\_concordant = true}, with runtime $6.93$ seconds.  This is enough to license the statement that the tested ARE deletion coordinate reaches an externally grounded RNA-stability boundary in this finite panel.  It is not enough to identify a decay-factor protein, a binding event, or a causal biochemical route.  The mathematical status is therefore a separated finite relation: deletion-coordinate rows and half-life rows meet under the declared statistic, but the bridge remains bounded by the data columns actually present.

\paragraph{Transfer comparison inside the same biological layer.}
A second ARE-linked stability record gives a useful same-layer calibration without changing the claim type.  The endogenous $3'$ UTR half-life transfer run \texttt{e96bf26e626b} used $14933$ genes, $2000$ actual permutations, and reported $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$ with negative sign, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, and permutation value $p=0.00049975$.  This companion result supports the idea that ARE burden can be read as a bounded RNA half-life feature across an endogenous transcript table, but it does not promote the deletion result into a mechanism.  The two records agree only at the RNA-stability layer: one is a deletion-panel boundary and the other is an endogenous-transfer boundary.

\paragraph{Cross-layer comparison without promotion.}
The nearby mRNA half-life boundary for m6A site load also stays at the RNA-stability layer.  In run \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, $5837$ genes were retained; the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman value between m6A-load change and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  These numbers show that the BioReality table can carry multiple RNA-stability contacts with distinct molecular labels.  They do not collapse those labels into one mechanism.  The ARE deletion statistic and the m6A-load statistic are both finite half-life readbacks, and their comparison is a consistency check on layer discipline rather than a claim that the same molecular process has been measured.

\paragraph{Refusal boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a positive finite RNA-stability boundary and a negative mechanism boundary.  No translation realization follows from the ARE deletion half-life statistic.  No ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the row counts, the sign-concordant deletion result, or the transfer comparison.  A decay-factor RBP bridge would require additional same-window columns: measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, a target-RBP loss condition, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, and controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  Without those columns, the admissible conclusion is exactly that ARE deletion labels meet RNA half-life readouts in the stated finite panels; the mechanism-facing reading remains refused.

\paragraph{Local mathematical summary.}
Let $I$ be the retained finite index set and let each row carry $(D_i,H_i,Z_i,C_i)$, where $D_i$ is the ARE deletion or control label, $H_i$ is the half-life readout, $Z_i$ is the available control vector, and $C_i$ records cell or dataset context.  The verified claim is the existence of a non-null finite statistic $\Delta_{\mathrm{ARE}}$ whose sign is concordant across the declared deletion evaluation.  The stronger RBP-mediated statement would require an expanded row $(D_i,H_i,Z_i,C_i,O_i,L_i,R_i,B_i)$ with occupancy $O_i$, target loss $L_i$, wild-type rescue $R_i$, and binding-defective or non-target control $B_i$.  Those latter coordinates are not present in the verified deletion run.  Hence the accepted object is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{ARE deletion coordinate} \longrightarrow \text{RNA half-life boundary},\\
&\text{not }\text{ARE deletion coordinate} \longrightarrow \text{translation, protein output, RBP mechanism, function, or law}.
\end{aligned}
$$
