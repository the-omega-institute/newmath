\subsection{ARE Deletion as a Bounded RNA-Stability Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-rna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns synonymously addressable coding or transcript-window rows only insofar as they are joined to an RNA-stability readout. The concrete boundary here is not a protein-output claim and not a structural or phenotypic claim. It is a finite comparison in which AU-rich-element deletion labels are evaluated against measured mRNA-stability behavior, with the result kept at the RNA half-life layer unless additional rows carry the missing mechanistic contacts. The verified run for the ARE deletion boundary is \texttt{a41440ab2792}. It retained $226$ BEAS2B deletion rows and $2242$ Jurkat deletion rows, used $1000$ actual permutations, and reported that $2000$ grading permutations were supported. The null procedure was a corrected pooled label permutation over deletions, because a within-region permutation became degenerate for the per-parent predictor. The finite result was sign-concordant, and the computation completed in $6.93$ seconds.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
The row object is a matched deletion/readout tuple rather than an unrestricted sequence law. A convenient way to state the finite comparison is to let $d_i$ mark the deletion state for row $i$, let $H_i$ denote the observed RNA half-life or stability readout, and let $Z_i$ collect the declared controls available at the boundary. The reported test asks whether the ARE-deletion label carries a stable directional signal after permutation of the deletion labels under the finite design:
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= T\bigl((d_i,H_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^{n}\bigr),\\
n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} &= 226,\\
n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}} &= 2242,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive item in this chapter is exactly the bounded observation that the ARE deletion/stability direction survived the declared finite permutation check with sign concordance. The result licenses a local RNA-stability boundary for these rows; it does not license translation realization, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Same-family transfer check.}
A second finite record gives a same-theme endogenous $3'$UTR half-life transfer comparison rather than a deletion experiment. Its run id is \texttt{e96bf26e626b}. That record used $14933$ gene rows and $2000$ actual permutations. The baseline model had $R^2 = 0.0657771$, while the ARE-augmented test had $R^2 = 0.0827561$, so the reported gain was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.016979,\\
\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= -0.0607953,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.00049975.
\end{aligned}
$$
The negative sign of $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}$ is consistent with an ARE-associated reduction in stability in that finite endogenous transfer setting. This companion record strengthens the decision to write the ARE deletion result as an RNA-stability boundary rather than as an isolated naming artifact. It still remains a bounded half-life statement: the transfer statistic does not add measured decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue behavior, or any protein-layer readout.

\paragraph{Required controls and refusal conditions.}
The scientific interpretation is deliberately narrow. A coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference becomes relevant here only when the finite rows keep transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout separated from the half-life response. If those covariates make the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels exchangeable for half-life, then the coordinate label is only a bounded descriptor. If half-life changes but measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not coupled to target-RBP perturbation and wild-type rescue, then the result remains an RNA-stability association and cannot be promoted to an RBP-mediated decay mechanism. If a binding-defective rescue, non-target control, local-structure effect, motif-density artifact, transcription-rate shift, isoform-stability shift, abundance artifact, or batch effect reproduces the pattern, the bridge interpretation is refused.

\paragraph{Layer boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a ready RNA-stability boundary and a blocked mechanism boundary at the same time. The ready part is the finite ARE deletion and ARE-transfer evidence: $226$ BEAS2B deletion rows, $2242$ Jurkat deletion rows, sign concordance under $1000$ corrected pooled-label permutations, and a same-family endogenous transfer result with $n=14933$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$. The blocked part is every claim above RNA stability. No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality follows from these rows unless a separate finite contact supplies that layer directly. In particular, a measured decay-factor RBP bridge would require same-window occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and a declared RBP-decay half-life statistic on the same finite panel. Those rows are not supplied by the present payload, so the chapter stops at a bounded RNA half-life relation.
