\subsection{ARE deletion as a bounded RNA-stability contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a matched finite panel of 3' untranslated-region windows in which AU-rich element deletion labels are joined to RNA half-life readouts.  Each row is treated as a concrete observation of an RNA-stability surface, not as a protein-output, folding, phenotype, or evolutionary fact.  The admissible row variables are the edited or deletion label, the transcript or reporter identity, the cell-context label, the RNA half-life readout, the abundance or transcription-side control when available, and the permutation label used to ask whether the deletion-class sign is exchangeable under the declared null.  The finite statistic for the primary deletion panel used $226$ BEAS-2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, with $1000$ label permutations for the reported test and $2000$ grading permutations available for the same boundary check.  The permutation route was the corrected pooled-label permutation over deletions; the attempted within-region scheme is not used as the operative null because it is degenerate for the per-parent predictor.  The observed result was sign-concordant across the two cell-context panels, and the run completed in $6.93$ seconds.

\paragraph{Statistic readout.}
Let $D_i$ denote the ARE-deletion indicator or deletion-class coordinate for row $i$, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $C_i$ collect the available row controls, including cell context and matched abundance-side information where present.  The test is not a statement that ARE sequence geometry realizes a mechanism.  It is the finite question whether the deletion-labelled rows preserve a same-sign RNA-stability displacement after the declared exchangeability operation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} &= 226, \\
N_{\mathrm{Jurkat}} &= 2242, \\
B_{\mathrm{test}} &= 1000, \\
B_{\mathrm{grading}} &= 2000, \\
\mathrm{sign\_concordant} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary licensed by these rows is therefore narrow: ARE deletion is allowed to be reported as a finite RNA half-life association with concordant direction across the two assayed contexts under the corrected pooled permutation.  The result does not by itself identify the decay factor, does not measure local RBP occupancy, does not prove target-RBP loss or rescue behavior, and does not turn a half-life displacement into translation realization.

\paragraph{Independent endogenous transfer check.}
A second finite contact gives the same chapter a broader RNA-stability consistency check without changing the scope of the claim.  In the endogenous 3'UTR half-life transfer panel, $14933$ genes were retained.  The baseline model had held-out $R^2=0.0657771$, and adding the ARE term raised the test value to $R^2=0.0827561$, so the finite gain was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.016979, \\
\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= -0.0607953, \\
\operatorname{sign}(\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}) &= \mathrm{negative}, \\
B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 2000, \\
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.00049975.
\end{aligned}
$$
This companion panel is useful because it checks that the ARE-side signal is not restricted to the synthetic deletion table alone.  It still remains an RNA half-life transfer statistic: the negative coefficient says that the ARE feature is associated with shorter half-life under the specified model, and the nonzero held-out gain shows finite predictive contribution beyond the baseline used in that run.  It does not supply a measured decay-factor occupancy matrix, a perturbation-rescue chain, or a same-row translation readout.

\paragraph{Accepted boundary.}
The chapter-level conclusion is a bounded cross-layer contact only in the following sense.  The deletion-labelled 3'UTR rows and the endogenous transfer rows jointly support a finite ARE-to-RNA-half-life boundary: deletion rows show sign concordance across $226$ BEAS-2B and $2242$ Jurkat observations under $1000$ operative permutations, while the endogenous transfer panel contributes a negative ARE coefficient over $14933$ genes with $\Delta R^2=0.016979$ and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$.  These numbers license a local RNA-stability statement about ARE-bearing or ARE-edited transcripts.  The scientific content is intentionally modest: the rows contact RNA half-life, not translation, protein abundance, folding, localization, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The positive RNA-stability boundary breaks if high- and low-coordinate or ARE-labelled rows become exchangeable after the declared abundance, transcription, isoform, composition, structure, batch, and cell-context controls.  It also breaks if a purported mechanism claim is made without measured decay-factor RBP occupancy at the same local window, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target controls.  A half-life difference with no occupancy displacement is only an RNA-stability association.  An occupancy displacement with no perturbation and rescue chain is only an occupancy association.  A rescue pattern reproduced by binding-defective, non-target, structure-only, motif-density, transcription-rate, isoform, abundance, or batch controls is not an RBP-mediated decay bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim rule.}
Nothing in this chapter promotes the finite half-life readout to a translation claim.  A separate same-row translation contact would be required before discussing ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, and even that would still not establish protein output.  Separate layer-matched contacts would be required for folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality.  The present result is exactly an RNA half-life boundary for ARE-labelled rows, strengthened by an endogenous 3'UTR transfer check, and it remains silent about every higher biological layer unless that layer is measured by its own finite rows.
