\subsection{ARE deletion as a bounded mRNA-stability boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite deletion panel for AU-rich-element-bearing regions, read only at the level of mRNA-stability response.  Each admissible row has a parent transcript or region identifier, a deletion label, a cell-context label, a measured stability or abundance-derived stability response, and the region class needed to compare deletion signs across matched rows.  The verified finite table contains $226$ BEAS-2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows.  The computational check used $1000$ realized permutations for the reported statistic, while the grading envelope records support for a $2000$-permutation regime.  The implemented null was a pooled label permutation over deletions, with a correction because the within-region permutation is degenerate for the per-parent predictor.  In symbols, the row carrier is the finite set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{ARE}}=\{(g,d,c,y,\rho,b): g \in G,\ d \in D_g,\ c\in\{\mathrm{BEAS2B},\mathrm{Jurkat}\}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g$ is the parent region or transcript key, $d$ is the deletion state, $c$ is the cell context, $y$ is the stability-oriented response, $\rho$ is the local region class, and $b$ records batch or processing provenance when present.  The chapter does not require the rows to be coding-window synonymous edits; the verified contact is narrower: it is an ARE-deletion stability boundary.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}
The Siegel-2022 boundary run is recorded as experiment $\mathrm{a41440ab2792}$.  Its scalar report is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}}&=226,\\
 n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}&=2242,\\
 N_{\mathrm{perm}}&=1000,\\
 N_{\mathrm{grade}}&=2000,\\
 t_{\mathrm{run}}&=6.93\ \mathrm{s},\\
 \mathrm{sign\_concordant}&=\mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign-concordance flag is the finite certificate used here: within the declared deletion panel, the two cell contexts agree in the direction required by the boundary statistic.  The permutation design licenses only a finite mRNA-stability statement.  It does not by itself identify a decay-factor RBP, a binding event, a translation effect, a protein-output effect, or a mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary formula.}
Let $s_{gdc}$ denote the signed deletion response for row $(g,d,c)$ after the dataset-specific normalization already present in the verified payload.  The chapter-level boundary is the existence of a nonrandom sign relation, tested against the pooled deletion-label null:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0 &: (d,y)\ \text{is exchangeable under the pooled deletion-label shuffle},\\
B_{\mathrm{ARE}} &: \operatorname{sgn}(s_{gd,\mathrm{BEAS2B}})\ \text{and}\ \operatorname{sgn}(s_{gd,\mathrm{Jurkat}})\ \text{are concordant on the declared comparison surface}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified result records $B_{\mathrm{ARE}}$ as sign-concordant.  Because the row count is finite and the null method is explicit, this is a bounded contact with the mRNA-stability layer, not an open-ended sequence law.

\paragraph{Relation to other finite stability contacts.}
A separate mRNA-stability contact, experiment $\mathrm{7cd189f15c4b}$, tested an m6A-load versus half-life boundary over $5837$ joined genes.  Its reported static Spearman value was $-0.388929$ for m6A load versus DMSO log half-life, its causal Spearman value for m6A load versus $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, its length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, and its two-sided partial-permutation value was $0.000999001$.  That companion record is not evidence for ARE deletion, but it fixes the intended reading of this family of rows: finite transcript-layer statistics can cross a stability boundary without promoting themselves to protein, function, phenotype, or mechanism.  A further transcript-surveillance contact, experiment $\mathrm{c362df6daa98}$, kept $38576$ rows, used $1000$ permutations, and ran for $55.602$ seconds.  Its role here is again classificatory: it shows that finite transcript-layer readouts are handled as row-bounded reality contacts rather than as global biological principles.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
The stronger decay-factor-RBP bridge would require same-window measured RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, mRNA-abundance controls, isoform-state controls, local-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, and batch separation.  Those rows are not present in the verified ARE-deletion payload.  Therefore the admissible implication is only
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ARE\ deletion\ rows}+\mathrm{concordant\ stability\ statistic}
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{bounded\ mRNA\ stability\ boundary},
\end{aligned}
$$
while the inadmissible implication is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ARE\ deletion\ rows}+\mathrm{concordant\ stability\ statistic}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathrm{measured\ decay\ factor\ RBP\ mechanism}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The second arrow is refused because the finite table does not contain local RBP occupancy or perturbation-rescue rows.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from the ARE-deletion mRNA-stability statistic.  No ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, decay mechanism, RBP mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the row table.  A later positive RBP bridge would need an independent row join carrying measured occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective control, non-target control, and the declared finite statistic under the controls listed above.  Until those rows exist, the present chapter licenses exactly one conclusion: the Siegel-2022 ARE-deletion panel supplies a finite, sign-concordant mRNA-stability boundary with $226$ BEAS-2B rows, $2242$ Jurkat rows, and an explicit pooled deletion-label permutation null.
