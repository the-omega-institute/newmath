\subsection{ARE deletion as an RNA-stability boundary with a translation readout held apart}\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}  The object considered here is a finite matched panel of transcript windows in which AU-rich element deletion labels are compared against RNA stability readouts.  The observed row set is not a general sequence law: it is a bounded table of deletion labels, cell context, abundance or decay readouts, and the declared controls available for the run.  In the Siegel-style ARE deletion record, the finite statistic was evaluated on $226$ BEAS-2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows.  The reported null used $1000$ pooled label permutations over deletions, with a corrected pooled procedure because the within-region version was degenerate for the per-parent predictor.  A grading pass with $2000$ permutations was supported, and the two cell contexts were sign-concordant.  The recorded runtime for the finite computation was $6.93$ seconds.

\paragraph{Readout map.}  The allowed readout is RNA stability, not translation realization.  A row may be read as evidence that an ARE deletion label is associated with a bounded mRNA stability shift only after the row has been kept separate from transcript abundance, cell-context, isoform, and batch explanations.  The signed comparison can be summarized at the level of the finite deletion panel by the schematic statistic
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{c} &= \operatorname{median}\{H_{r}: r\in c,\; r\text{ carries the deletion label}\}
      - \operatorname{median}\{H_{r}: r\in c,\; r\text{ carries the matched reference label}\},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= (D_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}},D_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H_{r}$ denotes the RNA half-life or stability readout available on row $r$, and $c$ ranges over the two assayed cell contexts.  The audit-relevant fact is not the absolute size of $D_c$ in isolation, but the finite-row observation that the deletion-direction comparison was concordant across the two contexts under the declared permutation procedure.

\paragraph{Boundary condition.}  The result licenses a narrow RNA-stability statement: in this finite ARE-deletion panel, the deletion label crosses the RNA half-life boundary as a bounded empirical association.  It does not, by itself, identify a decay-factor RBP mechanism.  That stronger bridge would require the same rows, or a declared matched panel, to include measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target control, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, transcription-rate controls, splice or isoform-state controls, transcript-abundance controls, codon-pair context, batch, and a same-row translation readout that is kept as a boundary variable rather than promoted to the conclusion.

\paragraph{Comparison contact.}  A second finite RNA-stability contact supports the same local discipline without expanding the claim.  In the endogenous $3'$ UTR half-life transfer record, the ARE coefficient was negative, with $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, $14933$ genes, $2000$ permutations, permutation value $p=0.00049975$, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, and $\Delta R^2=0.016979$.  This comparison is useful only as a consistency check on the RNA-stability layer: it says that an ARE-associated feature can survive as a bounded half-life predictor in a separate finite setting.  It does not turn the deletion panel into a universal rule, and it does not supply RBP occupancy or perturbation rows for the Siegel-style deletion claim.

\paragraph{Licensed conclusion.}  The chapter therefore records a positive finite RNA-stability boundary and a negative promotion rule.  The positive part is the sign-concordant ARE deletion association over $226$ BEAS-2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows under the corrected pooled permutation null, with an additional endogenous $3'$ UTR half-life comparison showing a negative ARE coefficient and a small but nonzero predictive increment.  The negative part is equally important: no translation output, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, causal mechanism, adaptation, or universality follows from these RNA half-life rows.  Any such statement would need its own layer-matched contact and a finite statistic declared on that contact before interpretation.
