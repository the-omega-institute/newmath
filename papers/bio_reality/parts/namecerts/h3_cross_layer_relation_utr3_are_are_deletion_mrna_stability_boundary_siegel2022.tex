\subsection{ARE deletion and the bounded RNA-stability readout}\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite panel of coding or transcript-linked RNA windows in which an AU-rich element deletion label is compared against a matched RNA half-life readout.  The usable rows are not treated as a general law about RNA sequence.  They are a finite assay surface whose coordinates record a deletion indicator, a parent region or transcript context, cell condition, measured half-life response, and the controls needed to prevent the deletion label from being promoted into translation, protein output, structure, function, or adaptation.  In the verified ARE deletion run, the retained finite row counts were $226$ BEAS-2B deletion rows and $2242$ Jurkat deletion rows.  The statistic was evaluated with $1000$ actual permutations, and the grading pass used $2000$ permutations.  The implemented null was a pooled label permutation over deletions, with the within-region permutation declared degenerate for the per-parent predictor.  The run time was $6.93$ seconds, and the two assayed settings were sign-concordant.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}
Let $D_i$ denote the finite deletion label for row $i$, let $H_i$ denote the matched RNA half-life readout, and let $Z_i$ collect row-level controls such as parent context, transcript abundance, isoform ambiguity, batch, local composition, and other declared nuisance variables available to the assay.  The boundary claim is only that the finite deletion label has a reproducible RNA-stability association under the declared permutation design:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= \{(D_i,H_i,Z_i): i \in I_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} \cup I_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}\},\\
|I_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}}| &= 226,\\
|I_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}| &= 2242,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
N_{\mathrm{grade}} &= 2000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign-concordant result licenses a bounded statement: deleting the assayed ARE-bearing region changes the RNA half-life readout in the finite assay surface in a direction that is stable across the two retained settings.  It does not by itself identify the molecular executor of decay, and it does not determine whether the same sequence coordinate would behave identically in a different transcriptome, cell state, isoform distribution, or perturbation design.

\paragraph{Transfer check.}
A related endogenous $3'$ UTR half-life transfer calculation supplies a second finite RNA-stability contact.  That run used $14933$ genes, $2000$ permutations, and reported a negative ARE coefficient $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$.  The baseline model had $R^2=0.0657771$, and the model with the ARE term had $R^2=0.0827561$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.016979,\\
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.00049975,\\
\operatorname{sign}(\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}) &= \mathrm{negative}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second calculation is not a proof of the deletion assay.  It is a separate finite-row consistency check showing that ARE burden can carry a small but nonzero half-life signal in an endogenous transcript setting.  Its dependency on the synthetic ARE reporter claim is recorded as part of the finite interpretation: the endogenous result is used as a transfer boundary, not as an unrestricted mechanistic explanation.

\paragraph{Cross-layer guard.}
The RNA half-life association is deliberately kept below a translation or protein-output assertion.  A row may contain or require a same-row translation readout as a boundary condition, but the half-life result does not become a translation realization merely because such a readout is named.  Translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, mature protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, and universality each require a separate layer-matched contact and its own statistic.  In particular, transcript abundance controls are denominators or nuisance controls for separated translation-efficiency reasoning; they are not themselves RNA half-life mechanisms, ribosome measurements, or protein measurements.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
The stronger decay-factor RBP bridge is not accepted from these rows alone.  To claim such a bridge, the same finite panel would need measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a coordinate--occupancy decoupling design, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and separation from transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, translation readout, and batch.  If half-life changes while measured RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not perturbable by the specified loss-and-rescue controls, the observation remains an RNA-stability association.  If binding-defective rescue, non-target control, local structure, motif density, transcription, isoform state, or batch reproduces the pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Licensed conclusion.}
The supported conclusion is therefore narrow and finite: the ARE deletion record crosses an RNA half-life boundary, with $226$ BEAS-2B rows, $2242$ Jurkat rows, $1000$ actual permutations, $2000$ grading permutations, and sign concordance under the declared pooled deletion-label null.  The endogenous $3'$ UTR transfer record adds a compatible half-life signal over $14933$ genes with $\Delta R^2=0.016979$ and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$.  Together these facts justify a bounded RNA-stability statement about ARE-associated rows.  They do not justify a claim about translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, RNA decay mechanism, organismal phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law without new finite contacts at those layers.
