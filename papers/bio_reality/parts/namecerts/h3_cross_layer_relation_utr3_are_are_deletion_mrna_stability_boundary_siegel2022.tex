\subsection{ARE deletion as a bounded RNA-stability contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite panel of coding or transcript-associated RNA rows in which an AU-rich element deletion or matched ARE state is read against an RNA half-life measurement. The biological item under test is not a protein-output claim and not a general codon law. It is a bounded RNA-stability statement: a finite row may support a local association between an ARE perturbation and mRNA half-life when the row also carries enough controls to prevent the association from being reinterpreted as translation, structure, function, adaptation, or mechanism. The relevant row type contains the editable or endogenous RNA label, the RNA half-life readout, transcript or mRNA abundance controls, isoform or splice state when available, local sequence-composition controls, and batch or cell-context labels. A stronger decay-factor RBP interpretation would require additional same-row measurements of decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls; those fields are not supplied by the verified ARE deletion statistic itself.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}
The verified ARE deletion run is identified by experiment run \texttt{a41440ab2792}. It retained two finite row counts, $226$ BEAS-2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, and evaluated the deletion label by a corrected pooled label-permutation test. The permutation design is explicitly described as pooled over deletions because the within-region permutation was degenerate for the per-parent predictor. The run used $1000$ permutations for the recorded statistic and a $2000$-permutation grading pass; the grading permutation route was supported. The sign was concordant across the retained contexts, and the runtime was $6.93$ seconds. In finite-row notation, the readout can be summarized as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} &= 226,\\
 n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}} &= 2242,\\
 B_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
 B_{\mathrm{grade}} &= 2000,\\
 \mathrm{sign\ concordant} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers license only the statement that the ARE-deletion half-life boundary was crossed for the declared finite deletion statistic. They do not by themselves identify the responsible decay protein, show altered RBP binding, establish translation-mediated causation, or supply any downstream phenotype.

\paragraph{Independent transfer check.}
A second ARE-related finite statistic gives useful scope control. The endogenous 3-prime-UTR half-life transfer run \texttt{e96bf26e626b} used $14933$ genes, $2000$ permutations, and reported a negative ARE coefficient $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$ with permutation value $p=0.00049975$. Its baseline coefficient of determination was $R^2=0.0657771$, and the ARE-augmented test value was $R^2=0.0827561$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta R^2 &= 0.016979,\\
 \beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} &< 0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.00049975.
\end{aligned}
$$
This transfer check points in the same RNA-stability direction but remains at the level of an endogenous half-life association. It strengthens the decision to keep the ARE deletion row in the RNA-stability layer rather than discard it as a shape artifact, while it also reinforces the need for separate contact rows before any RBP-mechanism or translation-mediated interpretation is admitted.

\paragraph{Boundary comparison.}
The RNA-stability layer is not unique to ARE biology. A separate m6A site-load half-life run, \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, retained $5837$ genes and reported a static Spearman value of $-0.388929$ between m6A load and DMSO half-life, a causal Spearman value of $0.245804$ between m6A-load change and log-two half-life change, a length-partial Spearman value of $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$. This comparison is not used as evidence for the ARE deletion mechanism. It is used only to calibrate the layer: finite RNA marks or RNA sequence elements may cross an mRNA half-life boundary without thereby becoming evidence for translation realization, protein structure, biological function, physical admissibility, phenotype, adaptation, or universality.

\paragraph{Admitted claim.}
The admissible statement is therefore local and finite. In the Siegel-style ARE deletion panel, the finite row statistic supports an ARE-associated mRNA stability boundary when deletion labels and half-life readouts remain sign-concordant under the declared corrected permutation design. The row counts and permutation protocol make the result stronger than a narrative literature pointer: the claim is tied to retained rows, explicit permutation counts, and a declared null construction. The conclusion may be written schematically as
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{ARE\ label} + \mathrm{half\ life\ row} + \mathrm{controls}
 &\longrightarrow \mathrm{bounded\ RNA\ stability\ readout},\\
 \mathrm{bounded\ RNA\ stability\ readout}
 &\not\longrightarrow \mathrm{translation},\\
 \mathrm{bounded\ RNA\ stability\ readout}
 &\not\longrightarrow \mathrm{RBP\ mechanism}\quad\text{without occupancy and perturbation rows}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mandatory translation-readout condition is a guardrail on interpretation: if a later coding-window version of this packet tries to connect the same coordinate rows to translation, the translation readout must be present in the same finite panel before any downstream interpretation is made. Its absence here does not invalidate the RNA half-life result; it prevents promotion beyond RNA stability.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim fails or is demoted if the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for half-life after transcript abundance, transcription rate, isoform state, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, and batch are controlled. It also fails as an RBP bridge if RNA half-life changes without measured local decay-factor occupancy, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, structure-only effects, motif-density effects, transcriptional shifts, isoform artifacts, or batch effects reproduce the pattern. Thus the present chapter admits an ARE deletion RNA-stability boundary, keeps the endogenous ARE transfer and m6A half-life comparisons at the same layer, and explicitly refuses translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, causation, adaptation, and universal-law readings unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding finite rows.
