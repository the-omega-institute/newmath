\subsection{AU-rich element deletion as an RNA-stability boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-are-deletion-rna-stability-boundary}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows.} The record concerns a finite deletion panel in which a local AU-rich element indicator is compared with an RNA-stability readout. The usable finite rows are not a general sequence law: they are assayed deletion rows with a declared label-permutation null, cell-context labels, and an RNA-stability direction. In the verified run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.utr3\_are.are\_deletion\_mrna\_stability\_boundary\_siegel2022}, the retained deletion rows have sizes $n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} = 226$ and $n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}} = 2242$. The null was computed by pooled label permutation over deletions, with the within-region form declared degenerate for a per-parent predictor. The run used $1000$ realized permutations for the reported statistic, recorded $2000$ grading permutations as supported, and finished in $6.93$ seconds. Its finite directional summary is simply that the two cell-context surfaces are sign-concordant:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} &= 226,\\
 n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}} &= 2242,\\
 N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 1000,\\
 N_{\mathrm{grade}} &= 2000,\\
 \operatorname{sign\_concordant} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This licenses an RNA-stability boundary for the assayed AU-rich element deletion panel, not a claim about translation, protein production, folding, cellular function, or organism-level adaptation.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.} The coordinate is the finite AU-rich element deletion label together with its cell-context and region bookkeeping. The readout is mRNA stability or half-life direction after the declared deletion comparison. The relation is bounded because the statistic is attached to finite rows and a finite permutation null. The result says that, on these rows, the AU-rich element deletion coordinate is not exchangeable with respect to the recorded stability direction under the chosen pooled-deletion null. The verified fact does not include same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control, local RNA-structure decoupling, isoform-resolved transcription-rate control, or a same-row translation readout. Those missing columns are not minor annotations; they are exactly the columns needed before the observation can be promoted from an RNA-stability boundary into a molecular mechanism statement.

\paragraph{Comparison with related finite contacts.} The AU-rich element deletion result is directionally consistent with a separate endogenous $3'$UTR transfer check. In \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.utr3\_are.are\_endogenous\_3utr\_halflife\_transfer\_rnadecaycafe}, the transfer run used $n_{\mathrm{genes}} = 14933$ genes and $2000$ permutations, with baseline $R^2 = 0.0657771$, test $R^2 = 0.0827561$, and $\Delta R^2 = 0.016979$. The AU-rich element coefficient was negative, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}} = -0.0607953$, with permutation value $p = 0.00049975$ and runtime $256.449$ seconds. A second RNA-stability contact, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500}, gives an independent scale check rather than an AU-rich element mechanism: it retained $5837$ genes, reported static Spearman correlation $-0.388929$ between m6A load and DMSO log half-life, causal Spearman correlation $0.245804$ between m6A-load change and half-life change, length-partial Spearman correlation $0.227683$, and two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$. These auxiliary contacts show that finite RNA-stability surfaces can cross their own declared boundaries, but they do not supply the missing decay-factor RBP perturbation columns for the AU-rich element deletion panel.

\paragraph{Admissible statement.} The admissible claim is the following bounded statement. In the deletion rows summarized above, AU-rich element deletion carries a finite, permutation-tested relation to an mRNA-stability direction, and the direction is concordant across the two recorded cell contexts. Written as a local predicate on the assayed table, the claim has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{AREDeletionRows}(D)\;\wedge\;\mathrm{RNAStabilityReadout}(D)\;\wedge\;\mathrm{PooledPermutationNull}(D)\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{BoundedRNAStabilityBoundary}(D).
\end{aligned}
$$
The predicate does not assert that codon choice realizes translation, that the mRNA-stability shift is caused by a named RBP, that protein abundance changes, that a folded structure changes, or that a biological function is realized. A stronger RBP-decay bridge would require same-row measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate control, isoform control, local-structure and motif-density controls, abundance and batch controls, and a translation-readout column kept separate from the half-life interpretation.

\paragraph{Refusal boundary.} The record must be refused as a mechanism claim if the same pattern can be reproduced by batch, transcript abundance, transcription-rate change, isoform mixture, local structure, motif density, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, or a control construct that does not restore the relevant binding state. It must also be refused if RNA half-life differs while the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent or unchanged, or if target-RBP perturbation and wild-type rescue are not present in the same finite panel. Thus the present chapter records a real RNA-stability boundary for AU-rich element deletion rows, while explicitly stopping before translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality.
