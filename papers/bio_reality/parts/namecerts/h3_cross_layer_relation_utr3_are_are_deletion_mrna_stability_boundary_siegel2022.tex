\subsection{ARE deletion as a bounded RNA-stability contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite panel of matched RNA-stability rows in which AU-rich element deletion status is read against measured mRNA stability. The carrier is not a genome-wide law and not a protein-output claim. It is a table-level contact whose rows retain the deletion label, the assayed RNA-stability readout, the parent-region bookkeeping needed for matched deletion interpretation, the cell-state identity, and the declared null distribution used to ask whether the deletion label separates the half-life signal. The verified finite run for the ARE deletion boundary used $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows. The permutation layer used $1000$ actual permutations, and the grading check records $2000$ permutations as supported. The registered null was a pooled label permutation over deletions, corrected because a within-region permutation was degenerate for the per-parent predictor. The sign was concordant across the assayed finite surfaces, and the run time recorded for this specific boundary computation was $6.93$ seconds.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $D_i$ denote the deletion or nondeletion label of row $i$, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $C_i$ collect the finite row controls available to the assay surface. The admissible statement is only that the deletion label carries a bounded RNA-stability separation in the measured rows after the registered randomization test. In this chapter the operative summary is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}} &= 226,\\
N_{\mathrm{Jurkat}} &= 2242,\\
B_{\mathrm{actual}} &= 1000,\\
B_{\mathrm{grading}} &= 2000,\\
\mathrm{sign\ concordant} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers license an RNA-half-life boundary for the finite ARE deletion panel. They do not license a translation readout, a protein abundance effect, a folded-state statement, a physical-admissibility statement, a function claim, a phenotype claim, a mechanism claim, an adaptation claim, or a universal biological law. The only positive reading is that, on the declared rows and under the declared permutation semantics, the ARE deletion surface crosses the local mRNA-stability boundary.

\paragraph{Same-family stability transfer check.}
The same audit packet also contains an endogenous $3'$ UTR half-life transfer surface, which is useful as a same-layer guardrail because it remains inside RNA stability rather than moving to protein or phenotype. That transfer run used $14933$ genes, $2000$ permutations, a baseline $R^2$ of $0.0657771$, a test $R^2$ of $0.0827561$, and a resulting $\Delta R^2$ of $0.016979$. The ARE coefficient was negative, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, with permutation value $p=0.00049975$. This companion result does not enlarge the claim; it only shows that the ARE direction can be represented on a separate endogenous half-life surface while preserving the same layer discipline. In both tables, the permitted endpoint is RNA half-life. No statement about translation, folding, localization, function, phenotype, or evolutionary optimization is inferred from the sign or the permutation value.

\paragraph{Boundary conditions.}
The claim would fail to promote beyond a bounded RNA-stability association if the finite rows lacked matched translation readout, matched mRNA abundance or transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density controls, isoform-state diagnostics, composition controls, batch accounting, and an explicit break condition. It would also fail as an RBP bridge unless the same rows supplied measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. The present verified ARE deletion statistic therefore remains deliberately narrower than an RNA-decay mechanism claim. It says that the deletion label is separable at the RNA half-life contact under the registered finite procedure; it does not identify the decay factor, prove direct binding, prove causation, or cross into any protein-level or organism-level layer.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The chapter records a ready BioReality boundary for ARE deletion and mRNA stability. The positive content is the finite-row separation supported by the BEAS2B and Jurkat deletion panels, the corrected pooled-label permutation procedure, and the concordant sign. The endogenous transfer numbers supply an additional same-layer stability check with $14933$ genes and $p=0.00049975$, but they remain an RNA half-life surface. Any stronger interpretation requires a new finite contact at the stronger layer: ribosome profiling for translation, proteomics for abundance, structural or biophysical assays for folding or admissibility, perturbation and rescue panels for mechanism, and phenotype assays for system-level claims.
