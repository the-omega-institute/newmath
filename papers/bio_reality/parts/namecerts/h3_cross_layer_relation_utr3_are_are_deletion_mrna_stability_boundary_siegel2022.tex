\subsection{ARE Deletion as a Bounded mRNA-Stability Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-are-deletion-mrna-stability-boundary-siegel2022}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns synonymously interpretable coding-RNA windows only after they are joined to an RNA half-life readout and kept separate from translation, protein, structure, phenotype, and mechanism claims.  The finite object is a matched row panel whose declared fields are the coordinate label, the local edit or deletion state, the RNA half-life readout, transcript abundance or transcription control, local sequence composition, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates when available, isoform or splice state when available, batch, and the declared break condition.  In the verified ARE-deletion run, the row counts were $n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}=2242$ and $n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}}=226$.  The reported null used pooled label permutation over deletions because the within-region design was degenerate for the per-parent predictor; the implemented check used $1000$ actual permutations, while the grading configuration recorded support for $2000$ grading permutations.  The observed direction was sign-concordant, and the run completed in $6.93$ seconds.  Thus the row-level statement licensed here is not that an RNA-binding protein mechanism has been discovered, but that the finite ARE-deletion rows carry a reproducible RNA-stability boundary under the declared permutation design.

\paragraph{Statistic readout.}
Let $D_i$ be the deletion indicator for row $i$, let $H_i$ be the measured mRNA half-life readout, and let $Z_i$ collect the available controls: transcript abundance or transcription-rate proxy, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch whenever the corresponding field is present in the panel.  The boundary statistic is a finite signed contrast, not a universal law:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  S_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\Delta(H \mid D,Z)\right),\\
  \mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{ARE}} &= \text{pooled label permutation over deletion labels},\\
  \mathrm{pass} &= \bigl(S_{\mathrm{ARE}}\text{ is concordant across the declared rows}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
For this claim the computed status is $\mathrm{pass}=\mathrm{true}$ with $1000$ actual permutations.  The paired endogenous-transfer check supplies a useful scale comparison: the endogenous $3'$UTR half-life transfer record used $n=14933$ genes, $2000$ permutations, a negative ARE coefficient $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, and $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=0.00049975$.  A separate RNA-modification half-life boundary, included here only as a neighboring RNA-stability calibration surface, used $n=5837$ genes and reported a static Spearman association $-0.388929$, a causal Spearman association $0.245804$, a length-partial Spearman association $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial-permutation value $0.000999001$.  These scalar checks show that the ARE deletion result belongs to the same finite RNA-half-life family of readouts, while preserving the narrower deletion-specific scope.

\paragraph{Licensed conclusion.}
The chapter licenses the following bounded inference: in the finite ARE-deletion rows, the deletion label is associated with an mRNA-stability readout in a sign-concordant direction under the declared pooled permutation null.  The conclusion is local to the assayed rows and their available controls.  It may be used as a cross-layer RNA-stability relation only if the same rows also retain the translation-readout boundary and matched mRNA or transcription controls before any downstream interpretation is attempted.  The translation field is a boundary field rather than a promotion device: its presence prevents RNA half-life from being silently reinterpreted as translation, and its absence blocks any translation-conditioned claim.

\paragraph{RBP bridge refusal.}
A stronger decay-factor RBP bridge would require more than these half-life rows.  It would require same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a prespecified target RBP, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or a non-target control, and a declared statistic showing that the half-life effect follows the occupancy and perturbation pattern.  Those fields are not supplied by the verified ARE-deletion scalar record.  Therefore the positive statement stops at finite RNA-stability association.  If high- and low-coordinate labels become exchangeable after the transcription, structure, motif-density, composition, codon-context, isoform, abundance, and batch controls, the coordinate label remains only a bounded descriptor.  If RNA half-life changes without measured RBP occupancy change, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, or if binding-defective, non-target, structure, motif-density, transcription, isoform, or batch controls reproduce the pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this ARE-deletion half-life statistic.  Such claims require a separate layer-matched contact and a finite statistic at that layer.  The present record is therefore ready as a self-contained BioReality chapter only in the RNA-stability sense: it reports concrete row counts, a declared permutation design, sign concordance, and compatible neighboring RNA-half-life scalars, while explicitly blocking every interpretation that would turn a half-life association into translation, protein output, RBP mechanism, structure, function, phenotype, or universality.
