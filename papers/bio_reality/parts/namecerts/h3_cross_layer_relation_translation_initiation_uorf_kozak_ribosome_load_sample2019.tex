\subsection{Rank-admissible translation initiation boundary for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:biorReality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-initiation-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This chapter records a finite-row translation-readout gate for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The carrier is a residualized design matrix whose rows are organism- or CDS-indexed translation observations, whose $Q$ columns are the nine available $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, and whose control block $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, and M-density.  The biological target is deliberately narrow: a proposed entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is allowed to mean only that residual coordinate $q_i$ retains non-vacuous predictive contact with a translation readout $T_j$ after the declared controls and after a rank audit.  It is not a protein-output, folding, function, mechanism, physical-admissibility, phenotype, or universal-law statement.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The translation-initiation contact supplies two finite Sample2019 row classes: $n_{\mathrm{fixed}}=41862$ and $n_{\mathrm{varying}}=16739$, with the two-sample direction recorded as concordant.  This is enough to state a bounded translation-initiation surface, but it is not by itself an organism-matched $B^{*}_{Q6}$ survival matrix.  The paired codon-topology contact loaded three organisms, Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12, over the 61-codon coding count space.  Its proxy Leu tRNA-copy readout produced nine nonzero coupling entries among the nine named residual coordinates, with threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute coupling $1882464.1106377328$; the proxy break condition that all nine entries have $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ was false.  These numbers show that the residual coordinates can touch a bounded translation-adjacent readout, but they do not certify $S^{QT}_{ij}>0$.

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
For each candidate entry, let $\widetilde q_i$ and $\widetilde T_j$ be the residuals of $q_i$ and $T_j$ after projection away from $Z$.  The admissibility predicate is the finite-rank condition
$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &< \operatorname{rank}([\widetilde q_i,\widetilde T_j]) < n_Z,\\
\operatorname{df}_{ij} &= n_Z-\operatorname{rank}(Z)-\operatorname{rank}([\widetilde q_i,\widetilde T_j]) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_Z$ denotes the row count after the control join.  If the residualized design is rank-saturated, or if the remaining degrees of freedom do not permit a held-out check, then the entry is recorded as coupling-only or needs more data even when an in-sample fit is positive or equal to $1.0$.  Thus an all-perfect small panel is treated as a warning sign rather than a survival certificate.

\paragraph{Residual context.}
The broader residual-basis computation gives a useful negative control for over-promotion.  Four organisms had computable residual-basis curves: Danio rerio with $14410$ joined rows and final $\rho=0.9791828971490305$, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 with $3415$ joined rows and final $\rho=0.9979478314995476$, Homo sapiens with $16008$ joined rows and final $\rho=0.9723341658092314$, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae with $3046$ joined rows and final $\rho=0.9798667977250413$.  Gallus gallus and Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155 remained in the needs-data class.  The extraction used the stated regularizer $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length scale $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  The persistent residual floor is important here: even when readout blocks make statistically detectable contact, most residual geometry remains unexplained by the available blocks and must not be redescribed as a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
The execution-selection decomposition for Saccharomyces cerevisiae used $n=5684$ active genes, five folds, and $240$ target permutations.  It reported $R^2_{QP}=0.30732228260918726$ on the active gene set and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ on the full gene set, with verdict composition artifact rather than cross-layer propagation.  The mRNA half-life contact had $n_{\mathrm{human}}=16500$, $n_{\mathrm{mouse}}=18924$, raw $\rho=0.126224$, and a non-CSC tAI circularity guard.  The branchpoint contact had $n=10584$ with expression controlled.  These rows supply useful neighboring controls, but none of them replaces the required same-row translation-readout join for $S^{QT}_{ij}$.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The admissible output of this chapter is a per-entry certificate.  For a proposed pair $(q_i,T_j)$, the certificate must state the post-control rank, remaining degrees of freedom, whether the held-out translation boundary is available, and whether the refusal condition fired.  A positive proxy coupling $A^{QT}_{ij}$, a modeled tAI or stAI contact, a row identifier repair, or a perfect in-sample regression is not enough.  The gate can therefore return three local statuses: rank-admissible testable entry, coupling-only contact, or needs-data refusal.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The present finite rows support a reviewable rank-admissibility rule for translation-initiation and translation-readout claims.  They do not assert ribosome occupancy, translation realization in the mechanistic sense, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causation, or a universal biological rule.  Any such promotion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled finite statistic.  In particular, the Sample2019 row counts and the three-organism proxy coupling matrix can motivate the next measurement, but the chapter keeps $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual geometry, modeled adaptation weights, mRNA controls, and translation-initiation readouts in separate roles until a non-saturated same-row $S^{QT}$ surface is computed.
