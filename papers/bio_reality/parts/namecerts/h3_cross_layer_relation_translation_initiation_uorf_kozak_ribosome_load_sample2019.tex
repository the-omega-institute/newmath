\subsection{Rank-admissible translation-survival gate for the uORF--Kozak readout}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-survival-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record concerns a finite design problem rather than a biological mechanism claim.  The carrier is a residualized matrix whose rows are organism-indexed or transcript-indexed observations, whose candidate coordinates are the synonymous $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual directions, and whose control block $Z$ contains the declared baseline covariates: amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, length, and an $M$-density baseline.  The target entry is a putative translation-survival score $S^{QT}_{ij}$ from a residual coordinate $q_i$ to a translation readout $T_j$.  The admissibility question is not whether an in-sample regression can be made large, but whether the residual row space left after removing $Z$ has enough rank to make a held-out loss or held-out description-length improvement falsifiable.

\paragraph{Observed row counts and readout boundary.}
The uORF--Kozak translation-initiation contact supplies a finite two-sample readout with $41{,}862$ fixed rows and $16{,}739$ varying rows.  The recorded computation was concordant across the two samples, and its runtime was $131.941$ seconds.  This is useful as a translation-initiation surface, but it does not by itself certify any $B^{*}_{Q6}$ survival entry, because the present record does not include the same-row residualized matrix $(q_i,T_j,Z)$ with a non-saturated post-control rank.  In the notation of the gate, the residualized score is allowed to be considered only after
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^{\perp Z} &= q_i - \Pi_Z q_i,\\
T_j^{\perp Z} &= T_j - \Pi_Z T_j,\\
\operatorname{rank}\bigl([q_i^{\perp Z},T_j^{\perp Z}]\bigr) &< n_{\mathrm{effective}},
\end{aligned}
$$
and after the remaining degrees of freedom are large enough to test the score outside the fitted rows.  If this inequality is lost because the residual design is saturated, then an apparent perfect score is a projection artifact, not a survival observation.

\paragraph{Coupling is not survival.}
A separate finite coupling computation loaded three organisms, namely \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12, in a $61$-codon count space.  It evaluated nine synonymous residual coordinates: $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Against the proxy readout defined by the tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum, the coupling threshold was $0.01$, all nine entries were nonzero above threshold, and the largest absolute coefficient was $1{,}882{,}464.1106377328$.  The break condition for that coupling packet was not met, since it would have required all nine coordinates to satisfy $|A_{ij}|<0.01$.  This establishes contact with a proxy readout, but it still stays below the translation-survival boundary: $A^{QT}_{ij}$ is a coupling coefficient, while $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is a controlled survival certificate.

\paragraph{Residual compression audit.}
The residual-basis extraction record reinforces why the gate is needed.  Four organisms were computed and remained persistent rather than compressible.  The final residual ratios were $0.9791828971490305$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.9979478314995476$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.9723341658092314$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.9798667977250413$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}; \emph{Gallus gallus} and \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155 were marked as needing data.  The smallest final ratio among the computed organisms was $0.9723341658092314$, above the stated residual floor $0.97$, so the finite readout panel did not collapse the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual directions into an explained translation layer.  The regularization scale in that extraction was $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length term $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  Several selected increments exceeded a degree-of-freedom-matched deterministic Gaussian null, for example the human PTM-density step had increment $0.014780177462400674$ against null $p95=0.0002845846156909306$, and the yeast measured-TE step had increment $0.003750101033636422$ against null $p95=0.0011272729395622515$.  These are finite compression contacts, not permission to claim a universal translation law.

\paragraph{Admissibility rule.}
For each candidate entry the gate returns one of two statuses.  It may allow a survival test when the residualized matrix has non-saturated rank, enough independent rows after $Z$, and a held-out translation readout.  Otherwise it returns a null or data-needed status.  The local decision can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Admissible}(i,j) = 1
&\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{df}\bigl(q_i^{\perp Z},T_j^{\perp Z}\bigr)>0\\
&\quad\wedge\ \operatorname{rank}\bigl([q_i^{\perp Z},T_j^{\perp Z}]\bigr)<n_{\mathrm{effective}}\\
&\quad\wedge\ \Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}}(q_i,T_j\mid Z)>0,\\
\mathrm{Admissible}(i,j) = 0
&\Longrightarrow
S^{QT}_{ij}\ \hbox{is not reported as survival.}
\end{aligned}
$$
This rule is deliberately stricter than in-sample association.  A fitted surface with all entries equal to $1.0$, a modeled tAI or stAI contact, or a proxy coupling matrix can guide the next assay, but none of these is a survival certificate when rank is saturated or when the readout is not measured on the same finite rows.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite contacts.}
Two neighboring cross-layer computations provide scale but do not close this gate.  The branchpoint motif usage record used $10{,}584$ rows with expression controlled and runtime $138.47155$ seconds.  The codon-composition mRNA half-life record used $16{,}500$ human rows and $18{,}924$ mouse rows, reported raw $\rho=0.126224$, used a non-CSC tAI circularity guard, and ran in $46.349$ seconds.  These show that row-level cross-layer computations are available in the surrounding memory, but the present gate still requires the particular same-row tuple $(q_i,T_j,Z)$ and a non-saturated rank audit before $S^{QT}_{ij}$ can be promoted.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter therefore records a rank-admissibility boundary for the uORF--Kozak translation-initiation subject.  The finite memory supports a concrete translation-initiation surface with $41{,}862$ fixed and $16{,}739$ varying rows, a three-organism proxy coupling contact over nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates, and persistent residual-basis behavior across four computed organisms.  It does not yet support a positive $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry.  No translation realization, ribosome-occupancy claim, protein abundance claim, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, biological-function statement, mechanism claim, phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measured rows and the controlled statistic survives the same rank gate.
