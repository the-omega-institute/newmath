\subsection{Rank-admissible translation-initiation boundary for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual panel}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-initiation-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record considered here is not a protein-level claim and not a mechanism claim. It is a finite-row audit of whether a proposed $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate can be promoted from codon-topology contact to a controlled translation-readout survival entry. The attached translation-initiation contact supplies two finite row counts from the uORF/Kozak/ribosome-load sample: $n_{\mathrm{fixed}}=41862$ and $n_{\mathrm{varying}}=16739$, with a concordant two-sample check recorded as true and a measured runtime of $131.941$ seconds. These numbers are sufficient for a row-level boundary statement: the contact has nontrivial finite support, but the support must still be joined to the exact $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates, translation readout columns, and controls before any entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is called a surviving translation relation.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates and controlled rank gate.}
Let $q_i$ denote one of the recorded synonymous residual coordinates and let $T_j$ denote a translation-layer readout column. The admissibility test is applied only after both sides are residualized against the declared control block $Z$, including amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding length, and an $M$-density baseline. The gate is finite-dimensional:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^{\perp Z} &= (I-P_Z)q_i,\\
T_j^{\perp Z} &= (I-P_Z)T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij} &> 0 \quad \text{is reportable only when the post-control row space is not rank-saturated.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus an all-perfect in-sample surface is treated as a warning sign rather than as translation survival. If the residualized design has no held-out degrees of freedom, or if the fitted score is forced by rank saturation, the entry remains a coupling candidate or a needs-data entry. In this chapter the finite translation-initiation rows establish the existence of a relevant readout surface, but they do not by themselves complete the joined matrix needed for a positive $S^{QT}_{ij}$ certificate.

\paragraph{Coordinates already present in the codon-side packet.}
The codon-side computation contains nine recorded $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates: $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. In the three-organism proxy coupling run, Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K12 were loaded; the codon count space had $61$ codons; all nine proxy entries were nonzero above the $0.01$ coupling threshold; and the largest absolute proxy coupling was $1882464.1106377328$. This is a contact with translation-adjacent variables, not a survival certificate. The proxy readout was a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum, and the computation itself states that true tAI, stAI, ribosome profiling, or protein-abundance readouts were not present in that matrix.

\paragraph{Residual persistence and the role of the regularizer.}
A separate residual-basis extraction run records the descriptive behavior of the same family of residual coordinates under finite readout blocks. The regularization was $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length term $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$. Four organisms were computed and two were left as needs-data. Homo sapiens had $n=16008$ joined rows, $6$ selected candidates, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321191321$, $d_{\mathrm{difference}}=0.018824203448813415$, and residual curve
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{human}} &= (1.0, 0.9852198225375993, 0.9806696939851618, 0.9792537895870558,\\
&\quad 0.9787609432955213, 0.9787233550315274, 0.9723341658092314).
\end{aligned}
$$
Saccharomyces cerevisiae had $n=3046$ joined rows, $9$ selected candidates, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2199040339633322$, $d_{\mathrm{difference}}=0.008171838236265161$, and final residual value $0.9798667977250413$. Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 had $n=3415$ joined rows, $2$ selected candidates, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.6628211177352172$, and final residual value $0.9979478314995476$. Danio rerio had $n=14410$ joined rows, $1$ selected candidate, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.874628721049266$, and final residual value $0.9791828971490305$. Gallus gallus and Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155 were marked needs-data. The minimum final residual among the computed organisms was $0.9723341658092314$, so the empirical message is persistent residual structure, not compression to a completed translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Null comparisons inside the descriptive extraction.}
The extraction run also recorded degree-of-freedom-matched null checks. In Homo sapiens, the first four selected blocks exceeded their null references: PTM density gave increment $0.014780177462400674$ against null p95 $0.0002845846156909306$; complex membership gave $0.004618389163869967$ against $0.000206488525646134$; transmembrane count gave $0.0014438137599134737$ against $0.0002435268444123588$; measured tRNA/tAI gave $0.0005032876020244136$ against $0.00020173994173736226$. The fifth human block, domain count, did not exceed its null reference, with increment $0.00003840392718095609$ against $0.00021932006910265595$, while localization later exceeded its null reference with increment $0.006528084968494762$ against $0.0011598998794309233$. In yeast, measured translation efficiency, PTM density, transmembrane count, turnover, and localization exceeded their null references at their selected steps, while complex membership, measured tRNA/tAI, domain count, and mRNA did not. These finite comparisons justify treating the residual packet as structured and testable, but they do not replace the rank-admissible $q_i \to T_j$ survival test for the translation-initiation surface.

\paragraph{Nearby finite biological contacts.}
Two additional finite contacts help calibrate the scope. The branchpoint-usage run joined $10584$ rows and controlled expression using UCSC hg19 gtexGeneV8, with runtime $138.47155$ seconds. The mRNA-stability contact had $n_{\mathrm{human}}=16500$, $n_{\mathrm{mouse}}=18924$, raw $\rho=0.126224$, runtime $46.349$ seconds, and a non-CSC tAI circularity guard. These contacts show that the BioReality record is willing to use finite organism-indexed biological surfaces, but each surface remains local to its own readout. A transcript-abundance control is not ribosome occupancy, a half-life statistic is not protein output, and a rank-feasible design is not itself a mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter therefore records a rank-admissibility gate rather than a positive translation-survival discovery. The finite translation-initiation rows, $n_{\mathrm{fixed}}=41862$ and $n_{\mathrm{varying}}=16739$, make the readout boundary concrete. The nine codon-side residual coordinates and the proxy coupling matrix make the candidate interface concrete. The persistent residual curves and null comparisons make the unresolved structure concrete. What remains absent is the decisive same-row matrix in which each residualized $q_i$ and translation readout $T_j$ is tested after $Z$ and after a held-out, non-saturated rank check. Until that matrix exists, the chapter permits only the statement that the proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entries are eligible for rank audit. It does not assert measured translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law. Each of those stronger readings requires its own matched biological contact and its own finite statistic.
