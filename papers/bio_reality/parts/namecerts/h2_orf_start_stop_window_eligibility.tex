\paragraph{Local scientific boundary.}
The start--stop-window packet must be read as a finite enumeration plus a refusal to overstate the layer.  The codon sets $R$ and $M$, the $f_3$ saturation, and the quotient geometry organize a finite coordinate domain.  The one-window ORF run confirms that an externally supplied window was classified under the enumeration rule.  None of these facts says that a ribosome translated the interval, that a peptide folded, that a molecular structure is physically admissible, that a gene has function, that a mechanism has been identified, or that a universal biological law has been obtained.  Those stronger conclusions require their own contact-bearing records.  The linked curated-interval evidence shows a stronger, separate contact protocol, but even there the stated scope remains external ORF interval contact only; it still does not establish translation, protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or global law.  The self-check reports three checked gates and records the same boundary: no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.

\paragraph{Admissible reading.}
The safe conclusion is therefore the following.  A finite start--stop enumeration rule was applied to one externally supplied nucleotide window, all supplied windows were classified, and the classification remains at $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ scope.  The packet is useful because it isolates the exact place where biological evidence must enter: a real interval annotation, sequence observation, transcript observation, or equivalent curated contact must be supplied before an ORF eligibility claim is treated as an observed biological interval.  Until such a contact is attached to this packet, the record is a controlled boundary object for testing what would be needed, not a certificate of translation or biological function.
