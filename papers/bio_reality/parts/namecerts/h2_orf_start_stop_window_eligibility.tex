\paragraph{Finite codon background.}
The ORF-eligibility record is a bounded biological test about whether externally supplied nucleotide intervals pass a start--stop-window enumeration.  It is not a derivation of translation from codon geometry.  The finite codon background used by the record contains the motif set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\\
&\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing and no extra entries in the motif-compression check.  The ambient enumerated set has cardinality $20$ and is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The saturation computation for the third finite closure operator gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the two ambient entries not reached by this saturation are $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, and the recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  The quotient-geometry audit records the vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$ for $M$, quotient size $10$, and quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$.  These finite counts are part of the bookkeeping context only; they do not by themselves say that any nucleotide interval is biologically present.

\paragraph{Window-enumeration contact.}
The direct start--stop-window run loaded an external finite nucleotide-window manifest dated $2026$--$05$--$25$.  Its scope was explicitly $\mathrm{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}$.  The run loaded one external window, and the enumeration classified exactly that one input window:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{input\_windows}&=1,\\
\mathrm{classified\_windows}&=1,\\
\mathrm{window\_count}&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded layer after this run is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  The source text identifies the manifest as curated finite nucleotide-window data for ORF start/stop enumeration and states that the records are annotation inputs to the experiment, not facts produced by the codon-coordinate calculation.  Thus the claim licensed here is narrow: when an externally supplied window is handed to the frozen enumerator, the run reports whether that window satisfies the start--stop pattern being tested.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
A stronger interval contact used NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals from \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$, with snapshot date $2026$--$05$--$30$.  The dataset contained $168$ rows and included the three declared input classes $\mathrm{positive\_curated\_orf}$, $\mathrm{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}$, and $\mathrm{boundary\_case}$.  The protocol was frozen before the external observation: classifier rules, input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were frozen, with protocol time $2026$--$05$--$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$ and external evidence time $2026$--$05$--$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The classifier identifier was $\mathrm{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}$, and the classifier-rules-modified flag was false.

The positive curated intervals were accepted in all $72$ tested cases.  The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value recorded for this positive interval statistic was
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=72,\\
\mathrm{accepted}&=72,\\
\mathrm{accept\_rate}&=1.0,\\
p&=2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched non-ORF decoys were rejected in all $72$ cases, with the same exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value:
$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=72,\\
\mathrm{rejected}&=72,\\
\mathrm{reject\_rate}&=1.0,\\
p&=2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired comparison likewise recorded $72$ wins in $72$ positive/decoy pairs, win rate $1.0$, and the same $p$-value $2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}$.  The boundary cases were not promoted: among $24$ boundary cases, $0$ were promoted, giving promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out split stability.}
The curated contact was also stable across three held-out splits.  For $\mathrm{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}$, $24$ positives had positive accept rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys had decoy reject rate $1.0$.  The same $24$-and-$24$ count and the same two rates were recorded for $\mathrm{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold}$ and $\mathrm{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}$.  All three split records were marked passed.  This is the finite evidence for interval-level ORF eligibility in this chapter: a frozen start--stop classifier separated curated CDS intervals from matched decoys under the declared external annotation contact.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The self-check records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Accordingly, the present chapter does not claim translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, organism-level effect, or universality.  The curated interval contact supplies evidence for the limited ORF-eligibility layer only.  Even the perfect finite rates above are rates inside the stated datasets and split design; they do not classify arbitrary nucleotide windows outside the supplied manifests, and they do not turn a start--stop pattern into an observed transcript, translated product, stable fold, or biological function without a separate contact that tests that stronger layer.
