\subsection{Start--Stop Window Eligibility as a Contact-Bounded ORF Record}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This chapter records a finite start--stop enumeration test for open-reading-frame eligibility.  The object is not a translated product and not a cellular mechanism.  It is the narrower question whether a nucleotide interval, once supplied by an outside biological annotation or sequence record, is classified by the start and stop boundary rule used by the ORF eligibility module.  The finite run for this claim used one externally supplied nucleotide-window record, loaded the window manifest successfully, and classified the single input window.  In finite-row form, the observed classification count was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{input\_windows} &= 1,\\
\mathrm{classified} &= 1,\\
\mathrm{window\_count} &= 1,\\
\mathrm{external\_window\_dataset\_loaded} &= 1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The snapshot date attached to this manifest is 2026-05-25.  The source field states that the records are curated finite nucleotide-window inputs for ORF start--stop enumeration and that they enter the experiment as annotation inputs, not as consequences of the BEDC coordinate geometry.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The start--stop window run and the curated interval contact should therefore be read at different strengths.  The one-window run confirms that the finite module can classify the supplied window by the start--stop enumeration rule.  The curated interval contact confirms that, under a frozen protocol, a larger external annotation dataset separated positives from matched decoys and kept boundary cases from being promoted.  Neither item turns codon geometry into translation evidence by itself.  The self-check attached to the BioReality packet records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Consequently the present chapter closes only the ORF-eligibility enumeration record for the supplied finite inputs.  Any later assertion about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality requires an additional contact whose evidence is of that specific kind.
