\paragraph{Finite nucleotide alphabet packet.}
The record concerns the layer called $\mathsf{orf\_eligibility}$: whether a finite nucleotide interval is eligible, by start and stop codon enumeration, to be treated as an open-reading-frame candidate.  The construction is deliberately weaker than any claim about realized translation.  Its finite combinatorial substrate uses two checked codon families.  The motif-compressed family is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\\
&\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing and no extra entries in the compression check.  The larger enumerated family is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the reported cardinality is $|M|=20$.  A separate saturation check gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the saturated list has $18$ entries and the recorded $f_3$ boundary residue is empty.  These facts are codon-enumeration facts only; they do not identify an expressed sequence, a transcript, a peptide, or a cellular event.

This quotient information supplies a finite coordinate check for the start--stop enumeration, but the biological predicate remains contact-gated.  A coordinate class can help classify a supplied interval; it is not itself an observation that an interval exists in a genome or transcript.

The start--stop-window experiment loaded a curated finite nucleotide-window manifest with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$.  The reported scope is $\mathsf{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}$, the number of loaded windows is $1$, and the enumeration classified all loaded inputs:
$$
\begin{aligned}

The module label reported by the run is $\mathsf{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  Thus the run establishes a finite classification statement for one externally supplied window manifest.  It does not say that the window is translated, that a protein product folds, that the product is physically admissible, or that the sequence has a biological role.

Its dataset contains $168$ rows and includes the three required input classes $\mathsf{positive\_curated\_orf}$, $\mathsf{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}$, and $\mathsf{boundary\_case}$.  The protocol was frozen before the external observation: the protocol timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, and the external evidence timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$. \paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The result of this chapter is therefore an eligibility statement for finite start--stop enumeration under supplied interval data.  The self-check reports $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Consequently, the finite evidence supports only this local conclusion: when an external nucleotide-window or curated-interval input is supplied, the frozen enumerator can classify it at the $\mathsf{orf\_eligibility}$ layer.  Without that supplied contact, the packet remains a boundary test asking what would have to be observed.  Even with the supplied contact, no conclusion follows about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, organism-level effect, or universal genomic law.  Each of those stronger assertions would require its own independently specified contact and cannot be obtained from start and stop codon placement alone.
