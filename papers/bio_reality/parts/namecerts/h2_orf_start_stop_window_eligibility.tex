\subsection{Positional dwell readback for the $f_3$ stress abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-positional-ramp-body}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The record tests whether the $f_3$ stress coordinate in the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual family has a positionally localized ribosome-dwell readback for the part of protein abundance carried by that coordinate. The tested response is not total protein abundance itself. It is
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{f_3} &= \operatorname{proj}_{\widetilde f_3}\bigl(\widetilde{\log_{10} P}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where both $\widetilde f_3$ and $\widetilde{\log_{10} P}$ are residualized against the shared control basis $Z$. The experiment therefore asks a narrower question: after the ordinary codon-composition and sequence controls are removed, does a per-gene positional dwell summary explain held-out variation in the abundance component aligned with $f_3$ stress, and is that explanation concentrated in the 5-prime/head window or in the 3-prime/tail window?

\paragraph{Finite rows and fitted surface.}
The positive finite-row witness is the run `6db78e1bf746` for Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The active positional-dwell join contains $n=1257$ genes inside a base design size of $1800$ rows. The fitted object has $\operatorname{rank}(f_3)=1$ and a residual control basis of rank $24$. The target component has squared norm
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|P_{f_3}\|^2 &= 44.20095750389323,
\end{aligned}
$$
and accounts for
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.1999063916718794
\end{aligned}
$$
of the abundance target used in this readback. The validation used five folds and $120$ deterministic row-label permutations of $P_{f_3}$, with the base and full designs held fixed separately for each segment and for the joint segment design. The seed was `sha256:b\_star\_q6\_f3\_stress\_dwell\_positional\_powered:deterministic`.

\paragraph{Positional decision.}
The positional classification is asymmetric. The 5-prime/head segment passed the held-out dwell criterion, while the 3-prime/tail segment did not:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{five-prime ramp passed} &= \mathrm{true},\\
\text{three-prime body passed} &= \mathrm{false},\\
\text{leading segment by } \Delta R^2 &= \text{5prime},\\
\text{dominant segment} &= \text{5prime}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row surface supports a localized statement: the $f_3$-aligned abundance component has detectable dwell-associated held-out prediction in the 5-prime/head window under the registered null, but the same record does not support a body/tail-dominant call. This is a positional readback of a residual component, not a claim that the codon coordinate directly causes abundance.

\paragraph{Connection with the non-positional dwell fit.}
The companion dwell fit `946f33083caf` gives the scale of the same readout family before splitting the dwell summaries by position. On the same yeast organismal context, it used $n=1257$ joined rows, five folds, $120$ permutations, $\operatorname{rank}(f_3)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=24$. Its base and full held-out coefficients were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{base}} &= 0.1855578757644517,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{full}} &= 0.20412261520502883,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}} &= 0.01856473944057712.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fixed-readout permutation boundary was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Null}_{95} &= 0.0013115362189692937,\\
\mathrm{Null}_{\max} &= 0.005001350039772926,\\
\mathrm{Null}_{\mathrm{mean}} &= -0.0032911642620130563,\\
\mathrm{Null}_{\min} &= -0.013799622026666476.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed dwell gain therefore sits well above the null envelope. The measured correlation between the residual $f_3$ coordinate and the dwell summary was $0.1615441450901653$, with an approximate $p$-value of $7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. These numbers support the narrower positional question by showing that the dwell readout is not merely a named covariate: it already carries detectable held-out information about $P_{f_3}$ before the segment split, and the segment split assigns the leading contribution to the 5-prime window.

\paragraph{Relation to ramp-versus-body structural-order readback.}
A separate structural-order ramp/body analysis, run as `fd29bb46dde6`, supplies a useful boundary rather than a promotion. In that record the primary ramp size was $50$ codons, with five folds and a minimum joined-row requirement of $300$. The $f_3$ ramp term was positive and sign-flip significant, while the body term was not:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{f_3,\mathrm{ramp}} &= 0.0011165640103326878,\\
\Delta R^2_{f_3,\mathrm{body}} &= -0.00026157313923957926,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{ramp}-\mathrm{body}} &= 0.001378137149572267,\\
p_{\mathrm{ramp}>0} &= 0.0478515625,\\
p_{\mathrm{body}>0} &= 0.54296875,\\
p_{\mathrm{ramp}-\mathrm{body}>0} &= 0.05859375.
\end{aligned}
$$
The registered conclusion there was `ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized`, because the paired ramp-body localization gate did not pass even though both primary organisms had positive ramp values and the body signal was absent. This matters here because the dwell-position result is stronger for the ribosome-dwell readout than for structural-order localization: the present claim may say that the dwell association is 5-prime leading, but it must not be rewritten as a folded-structure statement.

\paragraph{Translation and protein-abundance context.}
The same coordinate family has measured translation-efficiency survival in yeast, but that background remains a readout context rather than a mechanism proof. In the measured TE run `58611670ec19`, Saccharomyces cerevisiae contributed $5128$ genes with measured TE, a ribosome-TE join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$, and $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{measured}}=0.13681931343730436$ after the shared controls. In the measured TE-to-abundance decomposition `d71384d16e71`, yeast had $5128$ three-way joined rows, $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S_{QT,\mathrm{measured}}=0.13681931343730436$, and a measured mediation fraction of $0.4793069656345167$. These values show that the organism has a live measured-translation context for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals, but the positional dwell claim remains only the specific $P_{f_3}$ dwell-window statement above.

\paragraph{Compression and remaining residual.}
The yeast readout-dictionary certificate `a4dc9b09533a` places the result in a broader residual-decomposition scale. It used $n=1800$ joined rows from $4758$ available rows, five folds, $120$ permutations, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$. The best selected coverage after selection-aware correction was
$$
\begin{aligned}
C^{\ast} &= 0.24538211439730545,\\
1-C^{\ast} &= 0.7546178856026946,
\end{aligned}
$$
with raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, a fixed-subset null $95$th percentile of $0.00598374993486675$, and a selection-aware null $95$th percentile of $0.029149007242956115$. The selected readout family contains measured TE, mRNA stability, and turnover. This places the 5-prime dwell call inside a partially reducible but still substantially unexplained residual landscape.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is finite and layer-specific: in the yeast matched row set, the $f_3$ stress-aligned abundance component has a positive positional dwell readback, and the passing segment is the 5-prime/head window rather than the 3-prime/tail window. No translation mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal pathway, or universal biological rule follows from this result. Any such stronger statement would need its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic. In particular, the positive 5-prime dwell result does not by itself identify elongation kinetics, folding order, protein localization, stability, function, or evolutionary direction; it only records where the measured dwell summary carries held-out information about the residual abundance component aligned with $f_3$ stress.
