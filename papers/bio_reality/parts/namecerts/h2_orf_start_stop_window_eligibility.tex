\subsection{Start-stop window eligibility from finite codon-window enumeration}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This BioReality record isolates one narrow biological question: whether a nucleotide window passes a start-stop enumeration test for open-reading-frame eligibility.  The record is not a claim about translation, expression, folding, chemistry, cellular role, or evolutionary generality.  Its verified finite input for the start-stop-window experiment consists of a curated nucleotide-window manifest, marked as an external annotation input rather than a fact produced by the codon geometry itself.  The manifest snapshot is dated 2026-05-25, and the run reports exactly one loaded window and exactly one classified window:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{external\_window\_dataset\_loaded} &= 1,\\
\mathrm{window\_count} &= 1,\\
\mathrm{input\_windows} &= 1,\\
\mathrm{classified\_windows} &= 1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported scope is $\mathrm{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}$, and the run records the output layer as $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  Thus the finite observation is a row-level eligibility classification under a preassigned start/stop rule, not an observation that the interval is translated or biologically operative.

The geometry is therefore treated as classifier context only.

\paragraph{Reality-contact comparison.}
A separate curated-interval run is available as a linked reality-facing comparison, not as a promotion of the present start/stop-window record.  That comparison used NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  It loaded $168$ rows, including the input classes $\mathrm{positive\_curated\_orf}$, $\mathrm{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}$, and $\mathrm{boundary\_case}$.  The protocol was recorded as frozen before external observation: classifier rules, input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were fixed at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the external evidence was observed at 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00.

\paragraph{Finite statistical checks.}
The curated-interval comparison reports $72$ accepted positives out of $72$, with one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}$.  It also reports $72$ rejected matched decoys out of $72$, with the same one-sided binomial upper-tail value, and a paired baseline advantage of $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with $2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{positive\ accept\ rate} &= 72/72 = 1.0,\\
\mathrm{matched\ decoy\ reject\ rate} &= 72/72 = 1.0,\\
\mathrm{paired\ baseline\ win\ rate} &= 72/72 = 1.0,\\
p &= 2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same comparison reports $24$ boundary cases and $0$ promotions, so the boundary promotion fraction is $0.0$.  Held-out stability was checked on three splits: $\mathrm{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}$, $\mathrm{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold}$, and $\mathrm{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}$.  Each split had $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$.  These figures support the claim that the frozen start/stop classifier separated the curated positive and matched decoy rows inside that finite contact dataset.  They still do not convert the one-window start/stop enumeration record into a general biological law.

\paragraph{Local admissible conclusion.}
The admissible conclusion is exactly this: in the finite start/stop-window experiment, one externally supplied nucleotide-window row was loaded and classified by the start/stop enumeration procedure, and the output remained within the $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ layer.  In the separate curated-interval contact, $168$ RefSeq-derived rows supplied a stronger finite check, with $72$ positive intervals accepted, $72$ matched decoys rejected, $24$ boundary cases kept unpromoted, and all three held-out splits passing the declared positive/decoy criteria.  These are finite row facts about eligibility classification under external annotation contact.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
No conclusion in this chapter asserts translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, organism-level causality, or universality.  A start codon and a stop codon in a window are only eligibility tokens unless a separate sequence, transcript, curated annotation, or other biological contact supplies the interval being tested.  Even with the curated RefSeq comparison attached, the conclusion remains confined to the stated finite dataset and the stated classifier.  The codon geometry may define the enumeration space, and the external rows may test interval-level eligibility, but neither one licenses a claim that the corresponding biological product exists, folds, functions, or generalizes outside the recorded contact.
