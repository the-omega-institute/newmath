\subsection{Start--stop window eligibility under curated contact}\label{sec:bioreality-h2-orf-start-stop-window-eligibility}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object of record.} This packet concerns the biological question of whether a finite nucleotide window may be admitted to the layer called ORF eligibility by enumeration of start and stop codon boundaries. The mathematical object is deliberately smaller than a claim about realized translation. It records a contact-gated classifier over explicitly supplied nucleotide windows: a window may be classified for start--stop eligibility only after the relevant interval has been supplied by an independent biological annotation or sequence contact. The packet therefore has two parts. The first is the codon-geometric substrate inherited from finite motif enumeration. The second is the reality-contact gate that prevents the geometric substrate from being read as a biological fact by itself.

\paragraph{Local admissible conclusion.} The admissible conclusion is narrow. Given the curated window manifest and curated interval contact, the packet supports ORF-window eligibility for the finite records presented to the frozen start--stop classifier. The support comes from the exact finite counts just listed: one classified manifest window in the direct window run, $168$ interval-contact rows in the stronger curated run, $72/72$ positive acceptance, $72/72$ matched-decoy rejection, $72/72$ paired baseline wins, three stable held-out splits, and $0/24$ boundary-case promotions. These numbers justify the layer label $orf\_eligibility$ for the contacted finite records. They do not justify a statement about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, mechanism, genome-wide universality, or any law outside the supplied records.

\paragraph{Failure condition.} The record would fail if start--stop enumeration alone were used to assert an observed ORF without a sequence, transcript, or curated-annotation interval. It would also fail if the quotient geometry, the saturation table, or the finite codon lists were treated as evidence for translation, folding, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, or universality. The governing boundary is therefore local and operational: codon geometry can define the classifier input language, but only a separate biological contact can place a nucleotide interval into the ORF-eligibility layer, and no higher biological layer follows from that placement.
