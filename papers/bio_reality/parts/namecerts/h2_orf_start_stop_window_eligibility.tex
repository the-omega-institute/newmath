Its biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility at the boundary layer $orf\_eligibility$, not realized translation and not protein-level evidence. $$
\begin{aligned}

$$
The recorded scope is $external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only$. It does not say that the interval is translated in a cell, that a polypeptide is produced, that a fold exists, that the product is physically admissible, or that a biological role is present.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
The row's own promotion flag is $orf\_eligibility\_only$, and the self-check records a boundary statement with checked gate count $3$: no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Consequently, the admissible conclusion is intentionally small.  Start/stop enumeration can say that the supplied external window satisfies the finite eligibility rule, and the linked curated interval contact can test that rule against a finite annotated interval dataset.  Neither result establishes translation, folding, physical admissibility, mechanism, biological function, organism-wide universality, or a global law.  Any such stronger claim would need another contact whose data are able to test that stronger layer directly.
