\subsection{Start--Stop Window Eligibility as a Boundary-Level Biological Record}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This section records the finite biological claim identified by \texttt{h2.orf.start\_stop.window\_eligibility}.  The object is not protein production, transcript abundance, molecular folding, physical feasibility, or organismal role.  It is the narrower question of whether an externally supplied nucleotide interval is eligible under a start--stop codon enumeration rule.  The verified row reports scope \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}, source text identifying a curated finite nucleotide-window manifest, snapshot date \texttt{2026-05-25}, and one loaded external window.  The classifier count is exact: one input window was classified and one window was present, so the finite check is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\mathrm{windows} &= 1,\\
\#\mathrm{classified} &= 1,\\
\#\mathrm{input\ windows} &= 1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row also records \texttt{no\_geometry\_to\_translation\_promotion = orf\_eligibility\_only}.  This is part of the assertion itself: the start--stop enumeration supplies only an eligibility layer, and any stronger biological statement must come from a separate observation or curation event capable of testing that stronger layer.

\paragraph{Local cannot-promote boundary.}
The finite row is therefore read as a restrained biological statement.  Start and stop codons can delimit an eligibility window only when an external sequence, transcript, or curated annotation supplies the interval being tested.  Even then, the result remains at the open-reading-frame eligibility layer.  No claim of translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, universality, or global biological law follows from the codon geometry, the quotient shape, the single-window manifest, or the curated interval contact alone.  The self-check row records the same separation: three checked gates were inspected, and no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
