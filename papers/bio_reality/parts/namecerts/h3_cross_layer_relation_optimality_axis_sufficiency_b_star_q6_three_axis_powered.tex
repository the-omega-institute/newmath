\paragraph{Powered axis result.} The optimality-axis sufficiency run was completed under experiment id \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_optimality\_axis\_sufficiency\_powered}, run \texttt{2436cae9d455}. In this chapter that label is read narrowly: the three-axis model is not sufficient for the observed residual surface, so an additional explanatory direction is required inside the finite design. It is not read as a biochemical mechanism, a ribosome-dynamic observation, or a downstream protein claim.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding axis checks.} The two-axis closure run on the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ tRNA and $f_3$ span also computed 11 of 16 requested organisms, with 5 folds, a 500-protein minimum, and 41 synonymous-design columns; it concluded \texttt{third\_axis\_present}, because the perpendicular direction had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms. These rows justify the local reading of the present claim: a first tRNA direction, an $f_3$-translation-selection direction, and the identified perpendicular usage-frequency direction do not exhaust the finite residual signal.

\paragraph{Wobble and tRNA boundary.} The relevant boundary-set test used 40 computed organisms out of 46 requested organisms, skipped 6 organisms, applied 1000 permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and passed with the call that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. This supplies a finite tRNA-supply asymmetry context for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ surface. It does not by itself show that the $f_3$-stress coordinate changes ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency. The $f_3$ coordinate becomes a translation-layer candidate only where context tRNA or wobble weights break the third-position symmetry and a matched translation row records a surviving signal.

Nothing stronger follows inside this chapter.
