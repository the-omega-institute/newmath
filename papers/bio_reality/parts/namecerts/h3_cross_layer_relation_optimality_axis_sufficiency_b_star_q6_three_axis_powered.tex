\subsection{Three-axis insufficiency for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ optimality surface}
\label{sec:bq6-three-axis-optimality-sufficiency}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The finite object considered here is the residual synonymous-codon surface obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate family and then tested against organism-scoped translation-readout rows.  The relevant third-position involution is $f_{3}$, and its saturated finite row is not empty.  In the checked saturation row, the ambient set $M = \operatorname{Med}_{R} \cup \operatorname{ACA}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_{3}$-saturated residual row removes exactly the two $
\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ entries from this ambient list, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_{3}}(R) = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with empty $f_{3}$ boundary in the recorded finite row.  Thus the local coordinate has a definite finite support: the ambient list has $20$ codons, the saturated row has $18$ codons, and the boundary list has cardinality $0$.

\paragraph{Three-axis test.}
The powered optimality-sufficiency assay asks whether the first three axes already span the observed residual optimality surface.  The finite run used $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms.  Its conclusion is not that the three axes close the surface.  The recorded call is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{conclusion} &= \mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present},\\
\mathrm{organisms\_computed} &= 11,\\
\mathrm{organisms\_requested} &= 16,\\
\mathrm{fold\_count} &= 5,\\
\mathrm{synonymous\_design\_columns} &= 41.
\end{aligned}
$$
The intended reading is finite and negative with respect to sufficiency: after the model has been allowed to use the established $B^{*}_{Q6}$ axes, a residual fourth direction remains detectable in the powered panel.  This is an optimality-axis statement, not a claim about protein output or organismal advantage.

\paragraph{Relation to the preceding axis geometry.}
The three-axis failure is consistent with the surrounding finite-row facts.  A two-axis closure assay over the same powered design returned $\mathrm{third\_axis\_present}$ with the reason that the perpendicular residual direction had systematically positive held-out $R^{2}$ and same-signed directions across organisms.  A separate residual-axis assay identified the dominant non-tRNA residual optimality direction as $\mathrm{f3\_translation\_selection}$.  The third-axis identity row then matched $d_{\perp}$ to $\mathrm{usage\_frequency\_within\_family}$ with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p = 0.000488$.  The mechanism row further reported $\mathrm{selection\text{-}not\text{-}GC}$ and $\mathrm{not\text{-}wobble\text{-}clustered}$, with the CSC or positional identity still requiring a separate contact.  These facts make the three-axis insufficiency result specific rather than generic: the first missing direction is not merely a GC3 artifact, and it is not licensed as a solved wobble cluster mechanism.

\paragraph{Axis-strength spectrum.}
The per-organism spectrum supplies the scale of the finite panel.  In the recorded $11$-organism computation, every per-organism dominant count was assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, so the dominant-count summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\#\{\mathrm{dominant}=d_{\mathrm{resid4}}\} &= 11.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same record states that $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ leads the captured spectra on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, while the domain means are descriptive only because the domain sample is imbalanced: $1$ archaeal organism, $2$ bacterial organisms, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms.  The largest reported GC3 rank effect is for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$, with $\rho = 0.136$.  Therefore the fourth-axis call is not a universal biological law; it is a finite-spectrum statement over the computed design rows.

\paragraph{Mechanistic exclusions and surviving boundary.}
Several candidate identities for the fourth direction are already constrained by finite assays.  The mRNA-stability assay reports $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ is low under the fixed gates.  The codon-pair assay reports $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ using $50$ shuffle-null replicates.  The translation-accuracy assay reports $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms with $200$ permutations.  The elongation-dwell assay, restricted to \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, reports only $\mathrm{partial}$.  In parallel, an $R$-sense tRNA-supply assay computed $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, and used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha = 0.05$; it found that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights.  The combined interpretation is that a fourth mechanism is present in the powered optimality surface, while the available rows do not reduce it to mRNA half-life, codon-pair structure, translation accuracy, or a fully closed elongation-dwell identity.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
For the $f_{3}$-stress coordinate, the bounded readout is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_{j}}$ for a translation row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  A positive downstream interpretation requires a nondegenerate row after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls.  The finite optimality-axis result above does not by itself supply that row.  In particular, modeled tAI or stAI weights can be used as bounded codon-adaptation readbacks, but they are not observed ribosome footprints.  A three-organism all-$1.0$ fitted surface is a rank-degenerate boundary, not survival.  The support condition for a translation claim is a strictly positive controlled entry,
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_{j}} &> 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
under the declared controls and baseline ablations.  If all controlled entries are non-positive, or if the matrix is rank-saturated, the translation-readout claim is refused for this coordinate.

\paragraph{Local scope.}
The chapter therefore records a finite optimality fact: in the powered $B^{*}_{Q6}$ panel, three axes are insufficient and a fourth mechanism is present, with the computed rows and scalar gates stated above.  It does not assert that $f_{3}$-stress changes protein abundance, folded structure, localization, stability, biological function, phenotype, physical admissibility, mechanism, or universality.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched empirical contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present row supports only the finite axis-sufficiency conclusion and the associated no-promotion boundary for translation and higher biological layers.
