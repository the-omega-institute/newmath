\subsection{Rank-powered insufficiency of three optimal axes in the $B^*_Q6$ translation surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-three-axis-power-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This BioReality record concerns the finite coding-sequence residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection and then restricted to the $B^*_Q6$ coordinate family.  The particular row tested here is the $f_3$-stress row: it compares synonymous codon imbalance across third-position $f_3$ pairs only after amino-acid composition has been quotiented away.  The carrier is therefore not a protein-output object and not a structure or phenotype object.  It is a finite table whose columns include synonymous residual coordinates, organism labels, codon-usage summaries, tRNA or wobble-weight readbacks where available, and translation-layer rows only when a matched translation readout is supplied.

The saturated $f_3$ support was finite and explicit.  The computed set $M=\operatorname{Med}_R\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contained
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding saturated $f_3$-$R$ set removed exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and retained
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified $f_3$ boundary set was empty, $\partial_{f_3}R=\varnothing$.  Thus the row is not driven by an unresolved boundary codon in this finite support; any surviving biological readout must enter through a separate organism-matched translation row rather than through a missing codon-topology case.

\paragraph{Three-axis test.}
The rank-powered optimality test used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous design columns.  Its deterministic run, recorded as experiment $\mathrm{b\_star\_q6\_optimality\_axis\_sufficiency\_powered}$ with run id $2436\mathrm{cae}9\mathrm{d}455$, passed with conclusion \emph{fourth\_mechanism\_present}.  In this chapter the conclusion is read narrowly: the first three axes did not close the observed residual optimality surface in the powered finite panel.  It is not read as a claim that the fourth direction has already been identified as a translation mechanism, a protein-abundance mechanism, an RNA-stability mechanism, or a universal biological law.

The neighboring finite rows locate why the three-axis closure is insufficient.  A two-axis span test, using the same $5$-fold and $41$-column design scale with $11$ computed organisms, concluded \emph{third\_axis\_present}; its stated reason was that $d_\perp$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms.  The residual optimality row then reported that the non-tRNA residual direction aligned most with $f_3$ translation selection.  The core direction, by contrast, was already routed through tRNA adaptation in the universal-core test.  Therefore the finite pattern is not merely ``more tRNA''; it separates a tRNA-adaptation component, an $f_3$-linked residual component, and an additional direction left over after those components are admitted.

\paragraph{Axis spectrum.}
The per-organism spectrum was also finite: $11$ organisms were computed from $16$ requested, again under a $5$-fold design with $41$ synonymous design columns and a $500$-protein minimum.  The captured spectra were led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and every computed organism had $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ as its dominant counted axis, giving dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  Domain means were explicitly descriptive rather than balanced, with sample sizes $\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$.  The largest GC3 rank effect reported in that row was only $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$, so the recorded spectrum does not collapse the residual signal into a simple GC3 ranking statement.

The third-axis identity row supplied a provisional finite identification: $d_\perp$ aligned to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The companion mechanism row classified the same direction as selection-not-GC, not wobble-clustered, with CSC or positional identity still requiring a further outside contact.  This is an important narrowing.  The row supports a non-GC residual direction in the controlled finite design, but it does not by itself supply a measured ribosome-occupancy row or a causal mechanism.

\paragraph{Translation readout boundary.}
For the $f_3$-stress coordinate to become a translation-readout claim, one must compute a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ against a translation-layer target such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The intended statistic is bounded by the rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0
\end{aligned}
$$
only after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where applicable, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon baselines.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ three-organism matrix is a null boundary for this purpose, not survival evidence.  Likewise, modeled tAI or stAI weights may supply bounded codon-adaptation readbacks, but they are not direct ribosome footprints and are not protein-output measurements.

The finite mechanism rows sharpen this boundary.  The $R$-sense tRNA-poverty row used $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, and used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$; it passed with the mechanism call that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights.  This supports a tRNA-supply interpretation for $R$ avoidance, but it remains a tRNA-supply row unless it is joined to a controlled translation-readout matrix.  The fourth-mechanism identity checks then excluded or weakened several obvious promotions: the mRNA-stability assay concluded \emph{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}; the codon-pair assay concluded \emph{not\_codon\_pair} with $50$ shuffle nulls; the translation-accuracy assay concluded \emph{not\_accuracy} with $200$ permutations over $11$ computed organisms from $16$ requested; and the elongation-dwell assay in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} concluded only \emph{partial}.  These rows are compatible with a fourth residual direction, but they do not license a downstream biological promotion.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim rule.}
This chapter therefore records a finite rank-powered insufficiency result, not a full biological mechanism.  The passed three-axis test says that the first three modeled axes were not sufficient in the powered $B^*_Q6$ panel, and the surrounding rows constrain the residual direction away from simple GC, mRNA stability, codon-pair identity, and translation accuracy as dominant explanations in the tested assays.  No claim of measured translation survival follows unless a same-organism, same-CDS translation readout is joined and the controlled $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ entry is positive and nondegenerate.  No claim about protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, localization, stability, molecular function, phenotype, causation, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding finite rows and the declared controlled statistic is computed there.
