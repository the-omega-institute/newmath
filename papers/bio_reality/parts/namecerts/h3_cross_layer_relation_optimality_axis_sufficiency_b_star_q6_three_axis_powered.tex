\subsection{Three-axis insufficiency for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ optimality surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-three-axis-insufficiency}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record concerns the finite CDS-by-organism residual coordinate surface obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $f_3$-stress component of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate system and to matched translation-readout rows.  The coordinate is not read as a protein, phenotype, or universal law.  It is a row-wise residual descriptor whose biological question is narrower: after ordinary sequence controls are removed, does the $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance retain measurable association with a bounded translation readout, and is the standard three-axis model sufficient for that association?

The finite $f_3$ saturation calculation fixes the relevant codon support.  The set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains the 20 codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its $f_3$ saturation removes only the two $A C N$ boundary points, giving the 18-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
while the recorded $f_3$ boundary outside this saturation is empty.  Thus the finite object under test is not an unbounded codon universe.  It is a closed finite row surface in which the $f_3$ stress coordinate is available only where the synonymous codon geometry survives amino-acid quotienting.

\paragraph{Powered row test.}
The optimality-axis sufficiency experiment was run as a powered finite-row assay with 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, 41 synonymous design columns, and 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested organisms.  Its recorded status is passed, and the conclusion is that a fourth explanatory component is present: the three-axis surface is insufficient for the observed residual optimality pattern.  In symbols, if $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, $d_{f_3}$, and $d_{\perp}$ denote the first three candidate directions, the finite result rejects the claim that the observed residual surface is exhausted by their span:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{resid}} \not\subseteq \operatorname{span}(d_{\mathrm{tRNA}},d_{f_3},d_{\perp})
\end{aligned}
$$
for the tested design matrix and organism panel.  The statement is deliberately finite: it says that the assayed coordinate table requires another axis under the fixed row construction, not that biology has a unique universal fourth mechanism.

The surrounding powered records locate why the conclusion is not an artifact of a missing second or third axis.  A two-axis closure test over the same 5-fold, 41-column design and 11 computed organisms concluded that a third axis is present, with systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed $d_{\perp}$ directions across organisms.  A residual-axis assay identified the dominant non-tRNA residual direction as $f_3$ translation selection.  The third-axis identity assay then aligned $d_{\perp}$ to usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The corresponding mechanism-separation assay reported that the third axis remains selection-like after GC orthogonalization, is not wobble-clustered, and still needs a separate external positional or CSC contact before any stronger mechanistic interpretation is made.

\paragraph{Axis spectrum.}
The per-organism axis-strength spectrum supplies a consistency check on the finite row geometry.  It was also computed on 11 organisms out of 16 requested organisms with 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous design columns.  The captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and the per-organism dominant-count table assigned all 11 computed organisms to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$.  Domain means were marked descriptive only because the domain counts were imbalanced, with $n=1$ archaeal organism, $n=2$ bacterial organisms, and $n=8$ eukaryotic organisms.  The largest GC3 rank effect in this spectrum was small, $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$, so the result should not be paraphrased as a GC3-only separation.

The boundary-set tRNA-poverty assay supplies a separate finite contact for the $R$-sense part of the same codon region.  Across 40 computed organisms out of 46 requested organisms, using 1000 permutations and $\alpha=0.05$, $R$ sense codons were systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN together with dos Reis wobble weights.  This supports a tRNA-supply interpretation for $R$ avoidance in that bounded assay.  It does not by itself identify the fourth axis, and it does not turn the $f_3$ residual coordinate into a measured ribosome-occupancy row.

\paragraph{Fourth-axis exclusions.}
The fourth-axis follow-up records constrain several candidate interpretations.  The mRNA-stability assay passed with the conclusion that the fourth axis is not mRNA stability in the yeast half-life alignment test, because the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  The codon-pair assay passed with conclusion \emph{not codon pair}, using 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, 41 synonymous design columns, and 50 shuffle-null samples.  The translation-accuracy assay passed with conclusion \emph{not accuracy}, using 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested organisms, 5 folds, 41 synonymous design columns, and 200 permutations.  The elongation-dwell assay passed with a partial conclusion in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Taken together, these rows make the fourth component a real finite-model demand while keeping its identity bounded: it is not licensed as mRNA stability, codon-pair structure, or translation accuracy, and elongation dwell supplies only partial organism-specific support.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The allowed readback is the controlled survival entry $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ for a translation-layer row such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The row is meaningful only after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls have been applied.  A modeled tAI or stAI surface can be used as a bounded codon-adaptation readout; it is not the same thing as observed ribosome occupancy.  Likewise, mRNA abundance is a control covariate for translation-efficiency residualization, not a translation-output measurement.

The present finite evidence therefore supports the following chapter-local statement.  The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ optimality surface, restricted to the $f_3$-stress coordinate and the powered multi-organism panel, is not exhausted by the three named axes already isolated in the preceding finite assays.  The powered optimality-sufficiency run records a passed fourth-axis demand on 11 computed organisms, 5 folds, and 41 synonymous design columns.  This is a finite residual-geometry result plus bounded translation-readout routing.  It does not, without an additional layer-matched contact and statistic, assert realized translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular mechanism, organismal phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Local falsifier.}
The relevant falsifier is not a failure to name the fourth component.  The local falsifier is a controlled readout failure.  If every admissible $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ entry remains non-positive after the declared controls, or if the apparent survival surface is rank-degenerate, then the $f_3$-stress coordinate remains a finite residual coordinate without positive translation-readout survival.  In particular, an all-$1.0$ matrix on a three-organism panel is treated as a saturated null boundary rather than as biological support.  A positive finite-row fourth-axis demand may guide the next assay, but it cannot replace the same-row translation statistic.
