\subsection{Three-axis insufficiency for the powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns the finite coding-sequence surface obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $f_3$-stress coordinate of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ and to matched translation-readout rows.  The coordinate is not treated as a protein, phenotype, or global biological law.  Its admissible readback is the controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-stress},T_j}$ for a bounded translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The finite codon boundary used by the record is explicit: the ambient marked set is
$$
\begin{aligned}

$$
\begin{aligned}

\paragraph{Powered optimality test.}
The primary powered finite-row result is the three-axis sufficiency test with run id $2436cae9d455$.  It passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  Its conclusion is \emph{fourth\_mechanism\_present}.  In this chapter that conclusion has a narrow meaning: the three axes already under consideration are not sufficient for the observed optimality surface in the powered panel.  It does not identify the fourth source, and it does not license any downstream biological interpretation by itself.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite rows.}
The surrounding powered rows make the insufficiency call sharper.  The universal core tRNA-adaptation row passed on the same $11/16$ computed-organism design with $5$ folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  The non-tRNA residual optimality row also passed and reported that the dominant residual direction aligns most with $f_3$ translation selection.  The two-axis closure row then reported \emph{third\_axis\_present}, with the reason that $d_{\perp}$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms.  The third-axis identity row identified $d_{\perp}$ provisionally with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The third-axis mechanism row kept the axis GC-orthogonal, reporting selection rather than GC and no wobble clustering, while leaving the CSC or positional identity in need of a separate contact.

\paragraph{Axis spectrum.}
The per-organism axis-strength spectrum was also computed on $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again with $5$ folds, $500$ minimum proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  Its descriptive spectrum was led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and the per-organism dominant-count table was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  The domain split was imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms, so the domain means are descriptive rather than a balanced phylogenetic law.  The largest GC3 rank effect reported in this row was small, $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$, which is consistent with treating GC3 as a necessary control rather than as the explanation of the residual surface.

\paragraph{Boundary and exclusion rows.}
The $R$-sense tRNA-poverty row passed on $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, with $6$ skipped organisms, $\alpha=0.05$, and $1000$ permutations.  It supports a supply-side pressure on the $R$ sense codons under tRNA gene-copy and wobble-weight readbacks, but it is not by itself an observed ribosome-occupancy row.  The candidate fourth-source exclusions are mixed and bounded.  The mRNA-stability row passed and concluded that the fourth axis is not dominantly mRNA stability in its yeast half-life assay.  The codon-pair row passed with $50$ shuffle nulls and concluded \emph{not\_codon\_pair}.  The translation-accuracy row passed on $11/16$ organisms with $200$ permutations and concluded \emph{not\_accuracy}.  The elongation-dwell row passed in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} and was only partial.  These rows justify the local statement that a fourth source is present in the powered design, while preventing the chapter from naming that source too strongly.

\paragraph{Readout rule.}
The operative survival condition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j} &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where applicable, and simpler codon baselines have been controlled.  If every such entry is non-positive, or if the readout matrix is rank-degenerate, the translation-readout claim fails for this coordinate.  In particular, an all-$1.0$ surface over three organisms is a saturation boundary, not a survival result.  A modeled tAI or stAI row can be used as a bounded codon-adaptation readback, but observed ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency requires matched measured rows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter records a finite powered insufficiency result for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress residual axis.  It does not claim that the coordinate produces translation output, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a named biochemical mechanism, localization, stability, phenotype, or universality.  Each of those assertions would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present result is therefore a bounded statement about the residual-coordinate surface: under the powered organism panel, three axes do not exhaust the optimality geometry, and any biological promotion beyond a translation-readout survival row remains blocked until the corresponding data are joined and estimated.
