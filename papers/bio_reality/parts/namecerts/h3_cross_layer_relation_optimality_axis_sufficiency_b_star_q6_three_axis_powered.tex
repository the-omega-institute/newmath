\subsection{A powered three-axis test for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ optimality surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-three-axis-powered}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns the finite coding-sequence surface obtained after amino-acid quotienting and restriction to the $f_3$-stress coordinate of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$.  The rows are organism-scoped synonymous codon residuals, tRNA or wobble-weight summaries, and matched translation-readout routes.  The coordinate is not a protein, phenotype, function, or structural observable.  Its bounded readout is only a controlled entry of the form $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$, where $T_j$ is a translation-layer row such as a tAI surface, an stAI surface, or a measured ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency row when such a row is actually joined to the same coding sequences.

\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The finite $f_3$ closure used here is explicit.  The ambient set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}
\end{aligned}
$$
contains $20$ listed codons.  The $f_3$ saturation of the $R$ set removes exactly the two codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and leaves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  Thus the chapter does not begin from an unbounded codon universe; it begins from a closed finite row set in which the relevant residual contrast is the synonymous imbalance that remains after amino-acid projection.

\paragraph{Powered sufficiency test.}
The powered optimality-axis assay was run with $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms.  Its finite verdict is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{conclusion}=\mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Accordingly, the three-axis span is not reported as a complete explanatory surface for the observed residual optimality geometry.  The two-axis precursor had already returned $\mathrm{third\_axis\_present}$ on the same scale of $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again using $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The present assay therefore records a further failure of closure: after the tRNA and $f_3$ directions are accounted for, and after the third residual direction is admitted, a fourth mechanism is still detected by the finite design.

\paragraph{Position of the third axis.}
The third residual direction is not left nameless.  In the powered identity run it aligned provisionally with usage frequency within synonymous families, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The companion mechanism run classified the same direction as selection-not-GC and not-wobble-clustered, with CSC or positional identity still requiring an additional contact.  The universal residual-axis run identified the non-tRNA residual optimum most strongly with $f_3$ translation selection.  These rows justify treating the $f_3$ stress direction as a concrete residual coordinate, but they do not by themselves make it a measured ribosome-dynamics claim.

\paragraph{Axis-strength spectrum.}
The per-organism spectrum gives the local geometry of the powered panel.  Across the $11$ computed organisms, the captured spectra were led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and the dominant-axis count was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  Domain summaries were explicitly descriptive because the panel was imbalanced, with counts $\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$.  The largest reported GC3 rank effect was for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$, with $\rho=0.136$.  These finite numbers are consistent with the fourth-mechanism verdict: the dominant residual variation is not exhausted by a three-axis description and is not reduced to a large GC3 rank effect in the recorded spectrum.

\paragraph{Mechanistic exclusions and partial contacts.}
Several bounded mechanism checks constrain what the fourth direction may be.  The R-sense boundary-set assay used $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms and $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$; it found that R sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights, supporting a tRNA-supply route for R avoidance.  That result is compatible with translation-layer pressure, but it is not identical to the fourth mechanism.  The fourth-mechanism identity assays excluded several simple candidates: the mRNA-stability assay returned $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mRNA\_stability}$; the codon-pair assay returned $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ with $50$ shuffle nulls; the translation-accuracy assay returned $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ using $200$ permutations over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms; and the elongation-dwell assay in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} returned only $\mathrm{partial}$.  The finite row therefore supports a narrow conclusion: a fourth mechanism is present in the powered optimality surface, while the named simple alternatives are either excluded in this assay or only partially aligned.

\paragraph{Controlled translation readout.}
For the $f_3$-stress coordinate to become a translation-readout distinction, the asymmetry condition must be real at the contact layer.  In symbols, the coordinate becomes dynamically testable only in rows where a context weight separates paired third-position codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
When that condition is absent, a rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface is a null boundary rather than survival evidence.  When it is present, the relevant statistic is still not the codon topology alone.  It is a controlled survival entry after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density baseline, matched mRNA control where applicable, rank checks, and simpler-codon-baseline ablation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j} > 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The powered rows summarized above do not contain a same-organism and same-CDS joined matrix of codon counts, wobble weights, measured ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency rows, and matched mRNA abundance rows for this exact statistic.  The chapter therefore records a ready finite optimality result and a still-open translation-survival boundary.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
Nothing in this chapter promotes the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate to a downstream biological claim.  Codon-usage tables can support sequence-level residual estimation.  tRNA copy or wobble-weight rows can support bounded adaptation-weight readbacks.  Ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contacts can test translation-layer survival only after the matching and controls are actually present.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control covariate, not a translation readout by itself.  Protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism, and universality each require their own layer-matched contact and their own controlled statistic.  The present finite conclusion is therefore precise: in the powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ optimality panel, three axes do not close the residual surface, a fourth mechanism is present, and any interpretation above the bounded translation row remains blocked until separate evidence supplies it.
