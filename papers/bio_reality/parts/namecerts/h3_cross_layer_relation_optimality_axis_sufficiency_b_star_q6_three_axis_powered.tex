\subsection{Three-axis insufficiency for the powered $B^*_{Q6}$ translation surface}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-three-axis-insufficiency}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record concerns a finite residual coordinate surface over coding sequences, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ third-position stress coordinate and to translation-readout rows that can be matched by organism. The coordinate is not a protein, phenotype, or mechanism claim. It is a controlled row in which synonymous codon imbalance is first quotiented by amino acid, then tested against translation-facing quantities such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The finite $f_3$ saturation record gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\partial_{f_3}R &= \varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the saturated $f_3$ surface has eighteen listed codons inside the tested $M$ set, with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ present in $M$ but absent from the displayed saturated $R$ list. The empty $f_3$ boundary is important: the present claim is not that an exposed boundary codon by itself yields biology, but that a residual coordinate can be asked whether it survives in a controlled translation row when third-position pairing is broken by context-dependent tRNA or wobble weights.

\paragraph{Powered optimality test.}
The powered three-axis sufficiency assay was run with five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms. Its recorded conclusion is $\mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present}$. In the notation of this chapter, the three-axis model is a span of the tRNA adaptation direction, the $f_3$ translation-selection direction, and the residual third axis $d_\perp$. The finite outcome says that this span is not sufficient for the held-out optimality surface:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{span}(d_{\mathrm{tRNA}},d_{f_3},d_\perp) &\not\supseteq \text{the observed powered optimum},\\
\text{computed organisms} &= 11,\\
\text{requested organisms} &= 16,\\
\text{folds} &= 5,\\
\text{synonymous design columns} &= 41.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a sufficiency failure, not a free permission to name the missing factor. It states that the three recorded axes do not close the powered residual optimum under the declared finite design. The conclusion must therefore be read as a demand for an additional translation-facing or coding-sequence-facing coordinate, not as evidence for abundance, structure, localization, stability, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring finite rows.}
The surrounding powered rows make the insufficiency more specific. The two-axis closure test on tRNA plus $f_3$ reported $\mathrm{third\_axis\_present}$ on the same scale of five folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The identity row for $d_\perp$ then provisionally aligned that third axis to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The mechanism row further reported that GC-orthogonalized $d_\perp$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$, with the finite call \emph{selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs external data}. The residual optimal-axis row separately identified the non-tRNA residual optimum with $f_3$ translation selection. Together these records justify the local reading: tRNA adaptation, $f_3$ translation selection, and the usage-frequency-like $d_\perp$ direction are real finite axes in the powered panel, but they still leave a fourth component in the optimality surface.

\paragraph{Axis-strength spectrum.}
The per-organism spectrum gives an additional sanity check on the sufficiency failure. On $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with five folds and $41$ synonymous design columns, the captured spectra were led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$. The per-organism dominant counts were $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$, so the fourth residual direction dominated in every computed organism in that spectrum. Domain means were explicitly descriptive only because the panel was imbalanced, with $n=\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$, and the largest GC3 rank effect was small, $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$. This spectrum is compatible with the optimality conclusion: the observed deficit is not merely a naming artifact of the three-axis basis, since the residual fourth direction appears as the leading per-organism spectral component in the computed panel.

\paragraph{Mechanistic exclusions and bounded candidates.}
The finite follow-up rows constrain what the fourth component is allowed to mean. The boundary-set tRNA-poverty assay, computed on $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested organisms with $1000$ permutations and $\alpha=0.05$, reported that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. That supports a tRNA-supply interpretation for $R$ avoidance, but it does not close the fourth residual mechanism. The tested fourth-mechanism candidates are mixed: mRNA stability was excluded as the dominant fourth-axis identity in the yeast half-life assay; codon-pair structure returned $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ with a shuffle null of $50$; translation accuracy returned $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ after $200$ permutations on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$; elongation dwell returned only $\mathrm{partial}$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Thus the powered result is best stated as a sharply bounded insufficiency theorem for the empirical surface: a fourth mechanism is present in the finite optimality test, while the available candidate rows do not yet identify it as mRNA stability, codon-pair preference, translation accuracy, or a fully closed dwell-time mechanism.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
For the $f_3$ stress coordinate, the only admissible readback in this chapter is a controlled translation row. A positive interpretation requires a non-vacuous entry $S_{f_3,T_j}>0$ after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance, rank, and simpler-baseline controls. A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface over three organisms is a null boundary, not survival. Likewise, modeled tAI or stAI weights may define a bounded translation-facing annotation, but they are not observed ribosome occupancy by themselves. The strict local test is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3,T_j} &> 0 &&\text{permits a bounded translation-readout statement},\\
S_{f_3,T_j} &\leq 0 &&\text{blocks the translation-readout statement},\\
\mathrm{rank\;degenerate} &&&\text{is treated as no evidence of survival}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No translation realization beyond that statistic follows here. No folding, physical admissibility, protein abundance, localization, stability, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows unless another layer-matched contact supplies its own finite rows and controls. The chapter records a powered residual-axis insufficiency and a disciplined next test: find the missing fourth coordinate only through same-organism or same-CDS translation-readout data, not by promoting codon geometry or model weights into biology.
