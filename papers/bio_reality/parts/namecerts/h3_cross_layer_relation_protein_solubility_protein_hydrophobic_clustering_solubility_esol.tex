\subsection{Finite boundary for an $f_3$-stress translation-window test}\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-translation-window-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row under review.}
The record considered here is the claim
\texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol}, with experiment run \texttt{8742a224b538}.  Its verified finite table contains $n=3198$ rows and a recorded runtime of $60.0$ seconds.  These numbers certify only that a finite computational surface exists for the named protein-solubility claim; they do not, by themselves, certify the separate translation-window conjecture described by the $f_3$-stress packet.  The packet asks for a coding-sequence window table whose rows carry an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate, synonymous amino-acid composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry surface, matched mRNA abundance, and a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy readout.  The admissible row type is therefore a same-organism and same-CDS tuple of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r = (&\mathrm{organism},\mathrm{CDS},\mathrm{window},S_{f_3},A,\mathrm{GC3},L,M, \\
      &W_{\mathrm{wobble}},\mathrm{mRNA},\mathrm{RPF},T_{\mathrm{resid}},\mathrm{rank},\mathrm{break}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $S_{f_3}$ is the residual coordinate after amino-acid projection, $A$ denotes amino-acid or position-bin controls, $L$ is CDS length, $M$ is the methionine-density baseline term, $W_{\mathrm{wobble}}$ is the bounded codon-adaptation or wobble-weight row, and $T_{\mathrm{resid}}$ is the translation-window residual after the declared controls are attached.

\paragraph{What the present finite data establish.}
The $3198$-row solubility run is a real finite computation for the named solubility surface, but the supplied finite-row memory does not contain same-window ribosome-footprint rows, the matched mRNA denominator, or a declared $f_3$-stress translation statistic for those rows.  Consequently the present chapter records a boundary result rather than a positive translation survival result.  The intended statistic would compare a controlled model without $S_{f_3}$ to a controlled model with $S_{f_3}$ inside the wobble-asymmetric stratum:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 T_{\mathrm{resid}} &= \mathrm{RPF}_{\mathrm{window}} - F(A,\mathrm{GC3},L,M,\mathrm{codon\ counts},\mathrm{mRNA}),\\
 \Delta_{f_3} &= \mathcal{L}(T_{\mathrm{resid}}\mid Z) - \mathcal{L}(T_{\mathrm{resid}}\mid Z,S_{f_3}),\\
 Z &= (A,\mathrm{GC3},L,M,\mathrm{codon\ counts},\mathrm{mRNA},\mathrm{coverage},\mathrm{mappability}).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive interpretation would require nondegenerate rank, adequate coverage, simpler-baseline ablation, and a break condition excluding the case where codon geometry or wobble weights are mistaken for measured translation.  None of those finite scalar outcomes is present here as a p-value, effect estimate, confidence interval, hit rate, module label, $R$ list, $M$ list, or fitted $\lambda$ value.  The available solubility row count and runtime therefore support only the existence of a finite run for the named solubility claim, not a completed cross-layer translation-window result.

\paragraph{Comparison row used only as a scope check.}
A linked finite record, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_human}, has experiment run \texttt{90ea6b5f7fc7}, $n=1426$, runtime $5.372$ seconds, and an explicit abundance-control flag set to false.  Its memory also states that a yeast rule transfers to human under preregistered gates.  This second row is cited here only to show that the table distinguishes finite biological claims by their own row counts, controls, and gate conditions.  It is not evidence for the $f_3$-stress translation-window conjecture, and its absent abundance control reinforces the local rule: a claim may not be promoted across layers when its required matched control surface is missing.

\paragraph{Local admissibility condition.}
The $f_3$-stress packet becomes a biological translation-window claim only after the same finite row simultaneously contains ordered CDS coordinates, codon counts, wobble-weight asymmetry, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome occupancy.  Wobble asymmetry is necessary but not sufficient: it makes the third-position coordinate eligible for a dynamical test, while the ribosome-occupancy row supplies the actual translation readout.  The support condition is therefore local and finite:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{support}(r)=1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 &\mathrm{joined}(\mathrm{CDS},\mathrm{window},S_{f_3},W_{\mathrm{wobble}},\mathrm{mRNA},\mathrm{RPF})\\
 &\wedge\ \mathrm{rank\ sufficient}(Z,S_{f_3})\\
 &\wedge\ \Delta_{f_3}>0\ \mathrm{under\ the\ declared\ bounded\ test}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If any component of this conjunction fails, the row remains a request for a missing measurement surface rather than an observed translation relation.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon topology, $f_3$-stress residual geometry, modeled tAI or stAI weights, or review eligibility alone.  No protein abundance, folding state, structural order, physical admissibility, localization, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation claim, or universal law follows from the finite solubility run or from the translation-window packet unless a separate matched contact supplies that layer's readout and a finite statistic survives the corresponding controls.  In particular, a modeled codon-adaptation weight may be used as a bounded annotation, but it is not observed ribosome occupancy.  Matched mRNA abundance may serve as a transcription-layer control, but it is not itself a translation measurement.  The present record is therefore scientifically useful as a precise finite boundary: it identifies the row join and controlled statistic required before the $f_3$-stress coordinate can be tested as a translation-window variable.
