\paragraph{Row form.}
A row is read as a bounded tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x = (&\text{organism},\ \text{CDS or protein id},\ \text{codon-position bin},\ s_{f_3},\ a,\\
       &\text{amino-acid controls},\ \text{GC3},\ \text{length},\ \text{M-density},\ m,\ r,\ b),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $s_{f_3}$ is the synonymous residual coordinate after amino-acid projection, $a$ records whether the local wobble-weight surface distinguishes a codon from its $f_3$-mate, $m$ is the matched mRNA control, $r$ is the position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residual, and $b$ is the local break condition.  The row is meaningful only on the matched translation surface: same organism, same CDS, same local window, compatible ordered coding coordinates, codon counts, wobble weights, mRNA abundance, and ribosome-footprint occupancy must all be present before $s_{f_3}$ can be tested as a translation-window covariate.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
The scientific test is not whether third-position codon geometry exists.  It is whether a nonzero $f_3$-stress coordinate improves a bounded position-resolved translation readout after the usual confounders have already been carried by the same finite row.  In local notation the intended comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 s_{f_3} + \beta_2 a + \beta_3(s_{f_3}a) + \Gamma Z + \epsilon,\\
  Z &= (\text{amino-acid bin or position},\ \text{GC3},\ \text{length},\ \text{M-density},\ \text{codon counts},\ m,\ \text{coverage},\ \text{mappability}).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive finite-row survival claim would require that the $s_{f_3}$ term, or the wobble-asymmetric interaction term, passes rank sufficiency, coverage and mappability restrictions, same-window joining, and simpler-baseline ablation.  The supplied audit data provide the row count and run metadata for such a bounded table, but they do not provide coefficient estimates, $p$-values, confidence intervals, module labels, hit rates, $R$ lists, $M$ lists, or lambda values.  Consequently this chapter records the valid finite surface and its admissible query; it does not report a positive effect size.

\paragraph{Wobble asymmetry condition.}
The distinction that matters is between codon windows compressed by the synonymous amino-acid projection and windows where third-position weights break the $f_3$ symmetry.  A context with $w_c = w_{c^{f_3}}$ does not make the third-position coordinate a translation variable by itself.  The coordinate can enter the biological readout only when $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$ is represented on the same row as the measured or explicitly bounded translation readout.  In the absence of that same-row readout, the coordinate remains a codon-topology residual rather than an observed translation-layer quantity.
