\subsection{Protein-Solubility Route for a Position-Resolved $f_3$-Stress Translation Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-protein-solubility-f3-stress-translation-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row and scope.}
The record named by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol} is retained here as a finite BioReality row whose available verified surface is small but concrete: experiment \texttt{8742a224b538} reports $n=3198$ rows and a $60.0$ second runtime, with verification timestamp \texttt{2026-06-24T15:01:47+00:00}.  The row is linked to a second verified finite surface, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_human}, where experiment \texttt{90ea6b5f7fc7} reports $n=1426$, runtime $5.372$ seconds, and an abundance-control flag equal to false.  These numbers are sufficient to identify the row as an executed finite biological table, but they are not sufficient to turn the $f_3$-stress coordinate into a measured translation, folding, solubility, mechanism, or universal-law result.  The chapter therefore treats the solubility-route identifier as a constrained review surface for a translation-window statistic, not as a positive claim about protein solubility.

\paragraph{Coordinate to be tested.}
The finite object is a coding-sequence window table.  Each row is intended to carry an organism key, a CDS or protein identifier, a codon-position bin, a residual $f_3$-stress coordinate, amino-acid-composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry indicator, a matched ribosome-occupancy readout, and a recorded break condition.  The defining comparison is between windows whose third-position codon geometry still has nonzero $f_3$-stress after projection to amino-acid composition and windows whose signal is compressed by the synonymous projection.  The coordinate is only allowed to become dynamical after a bounded wobble surface breaks the $f_3$ symmetry, namely in contexts where $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$, and after a same-window ribosome-footprint statistic has been attached.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
For a window row $i$, the intended test is not a protein-output test.  It is a bounded translation-window residual test in which the response is a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residual, and the admissible covariates include amino-acid bin or position bin, amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, M-density, codon-count controls, matched mRNA abundance, coverage, mappability, and a simpler-baseline ablation.  In schematic form the admissible finite comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_i &= \operatorname{OccResidual}_i,\\
Q_i &= f_3\operatorname{-stress}_i,\\
Z_i &= (\operatorname{AA}_i,\operatorname{bin}_i,\operatorname{GC3}_i,\operatorname{length}_i,M_i,\operatorname{mRNA}_i,\operatorname{coverage}_i,\operatorname{mappability}_i),\\
\Delta_i &= \operatorname{score}(T_i \mid Z_i,Q_i,w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}})-\operatorname{score}(T_i \mid Z_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive row-level interpretation requires that the joined design matrix have adequate rank and that the reported improvement survive the preregistered break conditions.  Without those joined rows, $Q_i$ remains a codon-window residual coordinate rather than a measured translation variable.

\paragraph{Reality contacts.}
Three contacts are required before the statistic can be promoted beyond a request for measurement.  First, position-resolved ribosome occupancy must supply codon-indexed or fixed-window footprint rows keyed to the same organism, same CDS, same codon position, and same profiling sample.  Second, matched mRNA abundance must supply the transcription-layer denominator or control, so that translation-efficiency residuals are not confused with transcript abundance.  Third, tAI or stAI weights may supply bounded codon-adaptation weights, but only as modeled readout annotations; they do not observe ribosome dynamics by themselves.  The known biases are material: ribosome pausing, nuclease bias, fragment-length bias, frame assignment, mappability, start and stop edge effects, normalization, condition specificity, and CDS annotation mismatch can all move a local occupancy estimate.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The row may not be read as evidence for translation realization from codon geometry alone.  It may not be read as protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, protein function, localization, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights are not observed ribosome occupancy.  A review-ready or gate-passed status is not a biological measurement.  The solubility-route name is therefore not used here to assert solubility: no ESOL-level solubility, folding, hydrophobic-clustering, or downstream protein-state claim follows from the available finite numbers $n=3198$ and runtime $60.0$ seconds.  The linked acetylation row likewise contributes only a finite comparison surface, with $n=1426$ and no abundance control, and does not supply a rescue for the missing translation-window matrix.

\paragraph{Break condition and current classification.}
The claim breaks if the narrative treats the contact name, the modeled wobble weights, the $S^{QT}$ notation, the $f_3$-stress coordinate, or the codon-window table as though they were already a computed same-window ribosome-occupancy statistic.  It also breaks if a positive protein-solubility, folding, physical-admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal-law conclusion is drawn before a separate layer-matched contact supplies that layer's readout.  With the present finite facts, the scientifically stable classification is a bounded cross-layer boundary record: the row has an executed finite surface and a concrete sample count, but the decisive same-organism, same-CDS, same-window ribosome-footprint, wobble-weight, codon-count, and matched-mRNA join is still the required measurement surface.
