\subsection{A finite translation-window boundary for the hydrophobic-clustering solubility record}
\label{sec:bioreality-hydrophobic-clustering-solubility-esol-f3-window-boundary}

\paragraph{Scope of the row object.}
The BioReality record attached to \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol} is admissible here only as a finite-row coordination problem.  Its available verified run, \texttt{8742a224b538}, reports $3198$ rows and a capped runtime of $60.0$ seconds.  Those two scalars certify that a finite panel exists for the named solubility-facing packet, but they do not by themselves provide the missing position-indexed translation matrix.  The biological object therefore remains the following narrower table: a coding-sequence window row carrying an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate, synonymous amino-acid composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry surface, and a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy readout when such a readout is actually joined on the same organism, transcript or coding-sequence identifier, and codon-position bin.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.}
For each admissible row let $q_i$ denote the $f_3$-stress residual after projection away from amino-acid identity, and let $T_i$ denote the bounded translation-window readout derived from codon-indexed ribosome-footprint occupancy after matched mRNA abundance is used as a control.  The intended finite statistic is not a claim about the protein molecule; it is a claim about whether $q_i$ improves a local translation-window residual after the standard controls are already present.  A row is eligible only when it contains the same-window codon counts, the wobble weights $w_c$, the paired weights $w_{c^{f_3}}$, amino-acid-bin or position controls, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density, matched mRNA abundance, coverage and mappability checks, and a nondegenerate design rank.  In that notation the permitted test has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_i &= \alpha + \beta q_i + \gamma^{\top} Z_i + \varepsilon_i,\\
Z_i &= (\mathrm{aa\ bin},\mathrm{position},\mathrm{GC3},\mathrm{length},\mathrm{M\ density},\mathrm{mRNA},\mathrm{coverage},\mathrm{mappability}),
\end{aligned}
$$
with the additional gate that the $q_i$ term is interpreted only inside wobble-asymmetric contexts, namely rows where $w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}$.  If the joined matrix is absent, rank-deficient, coverage-failed, or unable to beat simpler codon and composition baselines, the row remains a boundary row and no positive translation-window survival is recorded.

\paragraph{Relation to the verified solubility packet.}
The solubility-facing identifier contributes a concrete finite run count, not a downstream solubility mechanism.  The observed $n=3198$ is therefore used as the finite size of the current named panel, while the fainter translation-window relation is kept separate from solubility, folding, stability, localization, abundance, and phenotype.  The chapter may say that the same research object is being asked to pass through a position-resolved translation readout before any later protein-layer question is considered.  It may not say that hydrophobic clustering, protein solubility, folded structure, or cellular fitness has been explained by $f_3$-stress.  The statistic above is only a finite local test for a residual codon-window coordinate against a matched translation surface.

\paragraph{Cross-record calibration.}
A second verified BioReality row is available from \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.nterminal\_acetylation.nterminal\_acetylation\_occupancy\_human}.  Its run \texttt{90ea6b5f7fc7} reports $1426$ rows, runtime $5.372$ seconds, and \texttt{abundance\_controlled=false}; it also records that a yeast rule transfers to human under its preregistered gates.  This linked fact is not evidence for the present solubility-facing statistic.  Its role is narrower: it shows why the present chapter must keep layer contacts explicit.  A finite human occupancy result without abundance control is not interchangeable with a protein-solubility or ribosome-occupancy result, even when both are cross-layer records.  The comparison supports the local discipline that each row must carry its own measured readout and its own control set before promotion across layers.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The record breaks if review eligibility, contact naming, modeled tAI or stAI weights, wobble asymmetry, $S^{QT}$ notation, codon-window geometry, or the $3198$-row solubility run is treated as measured ribosome occupancy.  It also breaks if a modeled codon-adaptation weight is described as observed ribosome dynamics.  A valid row may use tAI or stAI only as a bounded covariate or modeled readback, while measured ribosome-footprint occupancy must come from the matched position-resolved contact.  Matched mRNA abundance is likewise a control for separating transcriptional abundance from translation-layer signal; it is not itself a translation readout.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation realization, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, localization, stability, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal rule follows from this chapter.  Such claims require separate contacts at the corresponding layer, joined to the same finite rows or to a declared downstream panel with its own rank, controls, and break condition.  In the current state the chapter is ready as a bounded BioReality record because it states exactly what the $f_3$-stress window statistic would require, cites the two available finite verified runs, and keeps the named solubility-facing record from being promoted beyond the data actually present.
