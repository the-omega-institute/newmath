\subsection{A bounded translation-window certificate for the ESOL-linked solubility record}
\label{subsec:h3-cross-layer-relation-protein-solubility-protein-hydrophobic-clustering-solubility-esol}

\paragraph{Finite row under review.}
This record concerns the claim identifier $\mathrm{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_solubility.protein\_hydrophobic\_clustering\_solubility\_esol}$, but the finite row supplied for the present certificate is not a direct solubility assay. It is a translation-window boundary test for an $f_3$-stress synonymous residual coordinate. The carrier is a finite table of coding-sequence windows. Each row carries an organism key, a CDS or protein identifier, a codon-position bin, an $f_3$-stress coordinate after amino-acid projection, synonymous amino-acid composition controls, a wobble-weight asymmetry surface, matched mRNA abundance where available, and a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy residual. The biological object is therefore not the folded or soluble protein itself. It is the question whether a codon-level residual coordinate survives as a bounded translation-window readout after the appropriate same-row controls have been attached.

\paragraph{Observed finite facts.}
The ESOL-linked solubility run is present as a verified finite fact with experiment run id $8742a224b538$, row count $n=3198$, and runtime $60.0\,\mathrm{s}$. A second linked finite fact is also present for human N-terminal acetylation occupancy, with experiment run id $90ea6b5f7fc7$, row count $n=1426$, runtime $5.372\,\mathrm{s}$, and an explicit flag that the corresponding abundance control was not satisfied. These two rows are used only as finite registry facts: they show that the surrounding BioReality ledger contains realized finite experiments, but they do not by themselves supply the same-CDS, same-window ribosome-footprint matrix required for the present $f_3$-stress translation-window statistic. In particular, the ESOL row gives a protein-solubility-linked count and runtime, while the acetylation row gives an independent cross-layer count, runtime, and a negative abundance-control flag. Neither fact promotes the present codon-window coordinate to translation, folding, stability, localization, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Coordinate and statistic.}
Let $i$ range over admissible coding-sequence windows. The local data required for a positive translation-window certificate have the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_i = (&\mathrm{organism}_i,\mathrm{CDS}_i,\mathrm{bin}_i,F_i,A_i,W_i,G_i,L_i,M_i,\mathrm{mRNA}_i,T_i,b_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $F_i$ is the $f_3$-stress residual coordinate, $A_i$ records amino-acid-bin or position controls, $W_i$ records the wobble-weight surface, $G_i$ is GC3, $L_i$ is coding length, $M_i$ is the methionine-density baseline, $T_i$ is the matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or bounded translation-efficiency readout, and $b_i$ is the break condition. The coordinate is informative only where the third-position topology is not collapsed by the synonymous amino-acid projection and where wobble weights distinguish a codon from its $f_3$-partner. Equivalently, the row must separate the two cases
$$
\begin{aligned}
  F_i \neq 0 \quad \text{after amino-acid projection},
  \qquad
  w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The intended bounded statistic is a controlled residual comparison, not a biological mechanism. In schematic form it asks whether adding $F_i$ improves the finite-window translation readout after the control block $Z_i$ has already absorbed amino-acid composition, position or amino-acid bin, GC3, length, methionine density, codon-count baselines, matched mRNA abundance, coverage, mappability, and simpler baseline terms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  T_i &= \alpha + \beta F_i + \gamma Z_i + \varepsilon_i,\\
  \text{support} &\Longleftrightarrow \beta \text{ survives the declared rank, coverage, and ablation checks.}
\end{aligned}
$$
This equation is a finite-row acceptance test. It is not a claim that $F_i$ is protein abundance, folding state, solubility, structural order, localization, biological function, adaptation, mechanism, or a universal law.

\paragraph{Contacts and admissible readback.}
Three contact surfaces are relevant. First, position-resolved ribosome occupancy can provide codon-indexed or local-window footprint rows keyed to the same organism, transcript or CDS identifier, codon position, and ribosome-profiling sample. This contact is noisy: ribosome pausing, nuclease bias, fragment-length selection, frame assignment, mappability, start and stop edge effects, normalization, condition specificity, and annotation mismatch can all move local occupancy estimates. Second, matched mRNA abundance supplies a transcription-layer control for translation-efficiency residualization; it is not a translation readout by itself. Third, organism-scoped tAI or stAI weights can provide bounded codon-adaptation weights, but modeled weights are not observed ribosome dynamics. The finite row is admissible only when these surfaces are joined to the same ordered coding-sequence windows before the statistic is evaluated.

\paragraph{Boundary decision.}
The present chapter records a ready boundary certificate, not a positive downstream biological conclusion. The support condition is local: the $f_3$-stress coordinate may be tested against a matched translation-window readout only after the same-window matrix exists and after the control block has sufficient rank. If a report treats review routing, a contact name, wobble asymmetry, codon geometry, or symbolic notation as though it were already a computed footprint statistic, the certificate breaks. If the controlled statistic fails, the coordinate remains a codon-topology residual with no translation-readout survival. If the controlled statistic succeeds, the result is still limited to the bounded translation-window surface; it does not reach protein solubility or any other downstream layer without a separate matched contact at that layer.

\paragraph{Solubility-specific consequence.}
For the ESOL-linked protein-solubility record, the finite fact $n=3198$ confirms that a protein-solubility-associated row set exists in the ledger, but the row supplied here does not contain the hydrophobic-clustering variables, ESOL response values, module classification, hit rate, p-values, $\lambda$ values, or residual lists needed to restate a direct solubility theorem. The only safe chapter-level relation is conditional: an $f_3$-stress translation-window statistic, if later computed on matched ribosome and mRNA rows, may serve as an upstream translation-layer covariate in a separate solubility analysis. It cannot be read as solubility evidence merely because the current claim id is ESOL-linked. Thus the chapter keeps the protein-output and protein-state interpretation closed until a layer-matched solubility table supplies its own finite statistic and its own controls.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon or window geometry alone. No measured translation efficiency follows from modeled tAI or stAI weights alone. No protein abundance, folding, solubility, structural order, physical admissibility, localization, function, system phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the $f_3$-stress coordinate, from a review-ready event, or from the names of the required contacts. The only present conclusion is that the finite record has a well-specified admissible test surface and a well-specified break condition: the claimed cross-layer relation must remain at boundary status until same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint rows are joined and the declared controlled statistic is actually computed.
