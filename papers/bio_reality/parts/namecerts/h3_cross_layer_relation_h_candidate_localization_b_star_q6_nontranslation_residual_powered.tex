\subsection{Localization Absorption of the Non-Translation Residual}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite localization test for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation abundance residual. The working surface has nine residual coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. Each coordinate is residualized against the same control basis: intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons supported by the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. The control matrix has $24$ columns and observed rank $23$ in every localization organism considered here. The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label one-hot surface parsed from reviewed UniProtKB subcellular annotations.

\paragraph{Rows and joins.}
The localization panel has three organism rows. For $Danio\ rerio$, the localization-restricted join contains $1984$ CDS rows, while the broader CDS-abundance join reports $14410$ rows; the localization source contains $2821$ records. For $Homo\ sapiens$, the corresponding counts are $16008$ joined CDS rows, $19053$ broader CDS-abundance rows, and $16406$ localization records. For $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, the corresponding counts are $4656$, $5815$, and $4831$. The abundance-contact hit rates in the associated direct protein-omics surface are $1.0$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $0.9779294769799313$ for $Homo\ sapiens$, and $0.9156038419146592$ for $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$; these numbers are used only to state coverage, not to replace the localization statistic.

\paragraph{Localization categories.}
The $K=12$ localization dictionary is the same across the three rows: cell membrane, cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, membrane, mitochondrion, nucleus, other, peroxisome, secreted, and vacuole/lysosome. The parsed counts are finite and uneven. In $Danio\ rerio$, the largest categories are cytoplasm $883$, nucleus $845$, and membrane $708$, with smaller entries such as peroxisome $14$ and other $23$. In $Homo\ sapiens$, membrane has $7119$, cytoplasm $6305$, nucleus $5837$, cell membrane $3570$, and peroxisome $109$. In $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, membrane has $1863$, cytoplasm $1736$, nucleus $1733$, mitochondrion $811$, and other $44$. Since the annotations are multi-label and coarse, the statistic is an absorption readout for categorical localization information, not a compartmental mechanism.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $P\_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the modeled-translation complement of the abundance component carried by the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface after the stated controls. For each organism, the tested quantity is the fraction of this complement absorbed by the residualized localization design $L$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A\_{loc} &= \frac{\|\Pi\_{L\mid Z} P\_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2}{\|P\_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A\_{loc}(Danio\ rerio) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A\_{loc}(Homo\ sapiens) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A\_{loc}(Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
A $K$-column degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials gives null means and $95$th percentiles
$$
\begin{aligned}
Danio\ rerio &: 0.0062549946178242525,\quad 0.011321328712802819,\\
Homo\ sapiens &: 0.0008032852113983743,\quad 0.0013758521256775384,\\
Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae &: 0.002689902676583632,\quad 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the localization statistic exceeds the degree-of-freedom matched $95$th percentile in human and yeast, but not in zebrafish. The zebrafish row is still a valid computed row; it is reported as a weaker row because localization availability changes the join strongly and because its observed absorption is below the finite null threshold.

\paragraph{Complement consistency.}
The localization join changes the abundance surface, so the modeled complement must be checked on the restricted rows. The modeled complement value on the localization join is $0.7608723943210729$ in $Danio\ rerio$, compared with $0.874628721049266$ on the broader abundance-CDS reference, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$. In $Homo\ sapiens$, the localization-join value is $0.9020707321191324$ compared with $0.9208949355679455$, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$. In $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, it is $0.1997853864617063$ compared with $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$. The human and yeast restrictions stay within the declared $0.02$ tolerance; the zebrafish restriction does not, so zebrafish is interpreted with the join-shift caveat rather than as a matched complement replication.

\paragraph{Direct protein-omics background.}
The localization test is motivated by a direct protein-abundance association that remains after the same general codon and composition controls. In the direct protein-omics surface, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design has nine surviving coordinates in the three organisms used here, with $R^2\_{QP}=0.04613529807085213$ in $Danio\ rerio$, $R^2\_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ in $Homo\ sapiens$, and $R^2\_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$. These $R^2$ values say that the residual codon surface predicts measured abundance under the stated controls. They do not, by themselves, identify localization as the reason for the association, and they do not create a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a qualified positive H-candidate, not a mechanism. Human and yeast show localization absorption above the matched null threshold, and both preserve the modeled complement within the stated tolerance after the localization join. Zebrafish is a computed but non-supporting row for this H-candidate because its absorption is below the null $95$th percentile and its localization-restricted complement differs substantially from the broader abundance-CDS reference. The chapter therefore records localization as a plausible carrier of part of the non-translation residual in the human and yeast rows, while keeping the zebrafish row as a coverage-sensitive boundary case.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
Nothing in this finite matrix proves translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, a causal mechanism, or a universal biological rule. A positive localization absorption row is a controlled statistical projection of a modeled abundance residual onto coarse annotation columns. Translation requires a separate measured or explicitly bounded translation contact; folding, physical admissibility, function, and phenotype require their own layer-matched measurements. The present chapter only states that, in the finite rows above, localization columns carry more of the non-translation residual than a degree-of-freedom matched null in human and yeast.
