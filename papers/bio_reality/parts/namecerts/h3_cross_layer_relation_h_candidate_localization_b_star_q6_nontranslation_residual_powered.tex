\subsection{Localization as a candidate carrier for the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-localization-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether coarse subcellular localization carries a detectable part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component that remains after the modeled translation readout has already been removed.  The finite carrier is the joined matrix whose rows are coding sequences with protein identifiers and whose columns contain nine residual synonymous coordinates, the control block $Z$, a modeled-translation complement target, and a twelve-column multi-label localization readout.  The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-stress}$.  The control block has $24$ named columns: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the active localization joins the numerical rank of $Z$ is $23$ in all three organisms.

\paragraph{Rows and localization alphabet.}
The experiment uses reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-localization annotations as a coarse, multi-label protein-state readout.  The localization alphabet has $K=12$ columns: Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.  The finite joins are unequal because localization availability filters the protein identifiers.  For \textit{Danio rerio}, $2821$ localization records give $1984$ joined coding-sequence rows out of $14410$ reported coding sequences; the category counts are Cell membrane $230$, Cytoplasm $883$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Golgi $125$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Nucleus $845$, Other $23$, Peroxisome $14$, Secreted $79$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $45$.  For \textit{Homo sapiens}, $16406$ localization records give $16008$ joined rows out of $19053$ reported coding sequences; the counts are Cell membrane $3570$, Cytoplasm $6305$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Golgi $1096$, Membrane $7119$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Nucleus $5837$, Other $161$, Peroxisome $109$, Secreted $1876$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $482$.  For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $4831$ localization records give $4656$ joined rows out of $5815$ reported coding sequences; the counts are Cell membrane $274$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Golgi $224$, Membrane $1863$, Mitochondrion $811$, Nucleus $1733$, Other $44$, Peroxisome $77$, Secreted $91$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $217$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $Q$ be the residualized nine-column $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design, let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ be the modeled tAI translation readout, let $P$ be log protein abundance, and let $L$ be the twelve-column localization matrix.  The tested target is the modeled-translation complement of the $Q$-explained abundance component, written informally as $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$.  Localization is scored by projecting that target onto $L$ after the same $Z$ controls have been removed.  The comparison null is a $K$-column degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian baseline with $200$ trials per organism; therefore the raw absorbed fraction is interpreted only when it exceeds the organism-specific null $95$th percentile.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L &= R^2\bigl(\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}} \sim L \mid Z\bigr),\\
\text{localization signal} &\Longleftrightarrow A_L > \mathrm{Null}_{0.95}(K=12,\,200\ \text{trials}).
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Numerical readback.}
The absorbed fractions are $0.009508864544725801$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $0.009254913329269913$ for human, and $0.011225105608516642$ for yeast.  The corresponding degree-of-freedom-matched null summaries are mean $0.0062549946178242525$ and $95$th percentile $0.011321328712802819$ for \textit{D. rerio}; mean $0.0008032852113983743$ and $95$th percentile $0.0013758521256775384$ for human; and mean $0.002689902676583632$ and $95$th percentile $0.004958116973149795$ for yeast.  Thus the localization projection is below the null threshold in \textit{D. rerio}, but above the null threshold in human and yeast.

\paragraph{Join-shift diagnostic.}
The modeled-complement diagnostic also checks whether the localization-restricted join still resembles the full abundance-CDS complement calculation.  In human, $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ changes from $0.9208949355679455$ on the full $19053$-row abundance-CDS join to $0.9020707321191324$ on the $16008$-row localization join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$, within the $0.02$ tolerance.  In yeast, it changes from $0.21173219572706703$ on the full $5815$-row join to $0.1997853864617063$ on the $4656$-row localization join, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$, also within tolerance.  In \textit{D. rerio}, it changes from $0.874628721049266$ on the full $14410$-row join to $0.7608723943210729$ on the $1984$-row localization join, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$, so the localization availability filter materially changes the analyzed surface.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding protein-abundance and translation surfaces.}
The localization result is interpreted against already measured lower surfaces, not as a standalone mechanism.  Direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance survival was positive for all nine coordinates in the measured PAXdb abundance scan, with $R^2_{QP}$ values including $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, and $0.04613529807085213$ in \textit{D. rerio}; the broader direct scan used $19053$ human rows, $5815$ yeast rows, and $14410$ \textit{D. rerio} rows, with reported abundance-CDS join hit rates $0.9779294769799313$, $0.9156038419146592$, and $1.0$, respectively.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also present on a smaller four-organism translation surface: $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$ for human, $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, and $0.04034217614654355$ for \textit{D. rerio} after the same kind of length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls.  The measured protein-abundance mediation surface reports mediated fractions $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, and $0.0$ for \textit{D. rerio} after clipping the suppressive raw value $-0.04801486048721136$ to the unit interval.  These numbers motivate the localization test as an $H$-candidate search for remaining protein-state structure, not as a replacement for the measured translation step.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite readback supports a restricted statement: on the reviewed localization rows, coarse multi-label localization carries excess signal in the modeled-translation complement for human and yeast, while the \textit{D. rerio} localization subset does not pass the degree-of-freedom-matched null threshold and also fails the join-stability diagnostic.  The result is therefore a two-organism positive localization-candidate signal with one explicit null organism, not a universal biological rule.  The positive entries say that a coarse compartment annotation absorbs part of the remaining abundance component after the modeled translation readout has been accounted for; they do not identify a trafficking mechanism, a compartment-determining codon code, or a causal path.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation claim follows from this localization projection by itself: translation requires a separate measured or explicitly bounded translation readout.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, post-translational mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from the projection either.  Bulk abundance, localization labels, modeled tAI, and ribosome-TE statistics occupy distinct readout surfaces; a claim at any higher layer requires its own matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In this chapter the admissible conclusion is only that human and yeast reviewed localization annotations are non-null candidate carriers for part of the non-translation residual, whereas \textit{D. rerio} remains blocked on the present finite rows.
