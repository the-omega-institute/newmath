\subsection{Localization as a candidate carrier for the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:b_star_q6_localization_nontranslation_residual}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The finite object tested here is a row-matched surface over the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f_3\text{-stress}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each joined coding sequence, the codon residual design is first residualized against a control block $Z$ consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the localization test the control block has $24$ columns and numerical rank $23$ in all three organism panels.  The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-localization encoding with columns for Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.

\paragraph{Complement residual being queried.}
The quantity sent to the localization readout is not raw protein abundance and not a translation-rate readout.  It is the modeled-translation complement of the abundance component: the protein-abundance residual component attributed to $Q$ after removing the part accounted for by the modeled tAI readout.  In symbols, the tested target is the residual vector denoted here by $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, formed after the same $Z$ controls and then projected onto the residualized localization columns.  The descriptive scalar $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ records how much of the $Q$-linked abundance component is already assigned to the modeled translation readout before this localization query is applied.  On the localization joins it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.7608723943210729,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.9020707321191324,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.1997853864617063.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human and yeast localization joins remain close to the corresponding full-abundance complement computations: the absolute differences are $0.018824203448813193$ and $0.011946809265360736$, respectively, both within the stated $0.02$ tolerance.  The zebrafish localization join changes the available protein set more strongly: its difference from the full-abundance reference is $0.11375632672819314$, so the zebrafish localization value is reported as a join-specific diagnostic rather than as a stable complement match.

\paragraph{Rows, ranks, and readout coverage.}
The three computed organism panels have different row availability.  For $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $1984$ coding sequences join the localization table, compared with $14410$ reported CDS-abundance joins and $2821$ parsed localization records.  For $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $16008$ joined rows are available, with $19053$ reported CDS-abundance joins and $16406$ localization records.  For $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, $4656$ joined rows are available, with $5815$ reported CDS-abundance joins and $4831$ localization records.  Thus the test is not a symbolic assertion over all proteins: it is a finite matrix computation on the rows where codon counts, abundance, modeled complement, and localization annotations can be simultaneously keyed.

\paragraph{Localization absorption statistic.}
Let $L$ be the residualized $12$-column localization design on an organism-specific joined panel, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ be the target vector on the same rows.  The reported absorption scalar is the fraction of the target energy projected into the localization span after the control residualization.  The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_L(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_L(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because a multi-column categorical design can absorb target energy mechanically, these numbers are interpreted only against a degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials per organism.  The corresponding null means and $95$th percentiles are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_0(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.0062549946178242525,& q_{0.95}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
\mu_0(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.0008032852113983743,& q_{0.95}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mu_0(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.002689902676583632,& q_{0.95}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the localization carrier is positive relative to the null in human and yeast, where $0.009254913329269913 > 0.0013758521256775384$ and $0.011225105608516642 > 0.004958116973149795$.  It is not positive relative to the null in zebrafish, where $0.009508864544725801 < 0.011321328712802819$.  The organism classification for this finite row object is therefore: human and yeast are localization-positive H-candidate panels; zebrafish is a computed but null-bounded panel under the present join.

\paragraph{Category distribution.}
The localization columns are not uniformly populated.  In human, the parsed counts are Cytoplasm $6305$, Membrane $7119$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Golgi $1096$, Vacuole/Lysosome $482$, Other $161$, and Peroxisome $109$.  In yeast, the largest categories are Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, and Mitochondrion $811$, with smaller counts for Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, Vacuole/Lysosome $217$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Secreted $91$, Peroxisome $77$, and Other $44$.  In zebrafish, the largest categories are Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Cell membrane $230$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Golgi $125$, Secreted $79$, Vacuole/Lysosome $45$, Other $23$, and Peroxisome $14$.  These distributions matter because the absorption statistic is a projection into a coarse multi-label annotation space, not an assay of trafficking dynamics.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance surfaces.}
The localization result sits downstream of two already computed finite surfaces.  The direct protein-abundance survival surface reports positive $Q\to P$ association after controls across $15$ organism panels, with all nine coordinates surviving and examples including $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, and $0.019256877907572044$ in \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}.  A measured-translation mediation surface then conditions the abundance association on ribosome-derived TE in four organisms: the measured mediated fractions are $0$ for zebrafish, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The localization test therefore asks a narrower question: after the modeled-translation component is separated, does a coarse subcellular-localization annotation carry excess energy from the remaining $Q$-linked abundance component?

\paragraph{Readback.}
The finite readback is affirmative only as an H-candidate localization signal in two panels.  Human has $16008$ joined rows, rank-$23$ controls, rank-$12$ localization, and an absorption value about $6.72$ times its null $95$th percentile.  Yeast has $4656$ joined rows, rank-$23$ controls, rank-$12$ localization, and an absorption value about $2.26$ times its null $95$th percentile.  Zebrafish has $1984$ joined rows, the same control and localization ranks, but remains below its null $95$th percentile and also fails the complement-match tolerance against the full-abundance join.  Consequently the localization readout is a finite, organism-scoped carrier for part of the non-translation residual in human and yeast, while zebrafish remains a computed boundary case.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter does not turn localization absorption into a translation mechanism.  The localization annotations are coarse, multi-label UniProt category records, and the projection fraction is descriptive.  No conclusion about translation rate, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from these rows.  Such claims would require their own matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.  In particular, direct $Q\to P$ association and localization absorption do not by themselves show that a synonymous residual coordinate changes protein localization; they only show that, on the stated finite joins and controls, the residual abundance component left after modeled translation has non-null overlap with the localization annotation span in human and yeast.
