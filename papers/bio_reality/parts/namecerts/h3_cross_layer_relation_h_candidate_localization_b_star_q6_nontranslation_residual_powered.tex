\subsection{Localization as an $H$-candidate for the non-translation $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite-row question is whether a coarse protein-localization readout carries any part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation surface has been removed.  The object under test is not localization as a causal route, and it is not a claim about folding, compartmental mechanism, molecular function, physical admissibility, or a universal biological law.  The object is the following finite projection: construct the nine synonymous residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$; residualize them and protein abundance against the same $Z$ controls; remove the modeled translation component from the $Q$-explained abundance direction; then ask how much of the remaining component is absorbed by reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-localization labels.

\paragraph{Finite matrix.}
For each eligible organism, the design used $24$ control columns: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The observed control rank was $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in all three localization organisms.  The localization readout had $K=12$ one-hot columns and residualized localization rank $12$ in each organism.  The joined row counts were:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{Danio rerio}:&\quad n=1984,\quad 2821\ \text{localization records},\quad 14410\ \text{reported CDS-abundance joins},\\
\textit{Homo sapiens}:&\quad n=16008,\quad 16406\ \text{localization records},\quad 19053\ \text{reported CDS-abundance joins},\\
\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}:&\quad n=4656,\quad 4831\ \text{localization records},\quad 5815\ \text{reported CDS-abundance joins}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The twelve localization categories were Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.  For example, the human parsed localization table contained $6305$ Cytoplasm, $7119$ Membrane, $5837$ Nucleus, $3570$ Cell membrane, $1876$ Secreted, $1529$ Cytoskeleton, $1449$ Endoplasmic reticulum, $1370$ Mitochondrion, $1096$ Golgi, $482$ Vacuole/Lysosome, $161$ Other, and $109$ Peroxisome records.  Yeast was similarly broad, with $1736$ Cytoplasm, $1863$ Membrane, $1733$ Nucleus, $811$ Mitochondrion, and smaller counts in the remaining categories.  The danio table was the sparsest of the three, with $883$ Cytoplasm, $845$ Nucleus, $708$ Membrane, $247$ Mitochondrion, and $230$ Cell membrane records.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the modeled-tAI complement of the protein-abundance component explained by the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after the same $Z$ controls.  The localization test projects this finite vector onto the residualized $12$-column localization space $L$ and records the absorbed fraction.  To prevent the multi-column readout from being rewarded merely for having dimension, the same computation was compared with a $K$-column degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials per organism.  The observed absorption and null upper reference were:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{Danio rerio}:&\quad A_L=0.009508864544725801,\quad \mathrm{Null}_{95}=0.011321328712802819,\\
\textit{Homo sapiens}:&\quad A_L=0.009254913329269913,\quad \mathrm{Null}_{95}=0.0013758521256775384,\\
\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}:&\quad A_L=0.011225105608516642,\quad \mathrm{Null}_{95}=0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the human and yeast localization spaces exceed their degree-of-freedom matched null baselines, while the danio localization space does not exceed its own null reference.  The result is therefore not a pan-organism localization law.  It is a finite-row signal that, in two of the three available localization joins, reviewed coarse localization annotations carry measurable information about the residual abundance direction left after the modeled translation component has been removed.

\paragraph{Join sensitivity.}
The complement direction was also checked against the reference full abundance-CDS join.  In human, the current localization join gave $d_{modeled}=0.9020707321191324$ against the full-reference value $0.9208949355679455$, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$, within the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  In yeast, the current value was $0.1997853864617063$ against $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$, also within tolerance.  In danio, however, the localization-available join gave $0.7608723943210729$ against $0.874628721049266$, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$, outside tolerance.  The danio row therefore has two independent cautions: its localization absorption does not clear the null $95$ reference, and its localization-available join changes the modeled complement direction substantially relative to the full abundance-CDS join.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance contacts.}
The localization statistic is downstream of, and weaker than, measured translation mediation.  Separate measured-TE computations on organism-matched rows showed positive $B^{*}_{Q6}$ survival against $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$ in four organisms: $R^2_{QT}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.04034217614654355$ in danio, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.009394266754822262$ in \textit{E. coli}; all nine coordinates survived the positive-threshold screen in those runs.  Separate measured protein-abundance computations showed direct $Q\to P$ survival across a larger organism panel, with examples including $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.10217906758013379$ in \textit{C. elegans}, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, and $0.019256877907572044$ in \textit{Halobacterium salinarum}.  Direct protein-omics association alone is not a mechanism.  In the measured mediation run, the measured mediated fractions were $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.0$ in danio after clamping the negative raw danio fraction.  These values keep the localization chapter in its proper scope: localization is tested as an $H$-candidate for residual abundance left beyond modeled translation, not as evidence that the synonymous coordinate directly controls compartment identity.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is mixed but informative.  Human and yeast support localization as an $H$-candidate readout for the non-translation residual because their observed absorbed fractions exceed their degree-of-freedom matched null $95$ references: $0.009254913329269913>0.0013758521256775384$ in human and $0.011225105608516642>0.004958116973149795$ in yeast.  Danio is reported as a negative or unstable localization join for this statistic because $0.009508864544725801<0.011321328712802819$ and the localization-available join changes the modeled complement by $0.11375632672819314$.  The scientifically admissible statement is therefore narrow: coarse reviewed localization labels absorb a small but non-null portion of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual in human and yeast under the declared controls.  Nothing in this finite matrix establishes translation causality, folding state, physical admissibility, protein function, compartmental mechanism, evolutionary direction, phenotype, or universality.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic at that layer.
