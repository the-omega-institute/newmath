\subsection{Localization as an H-candidate for the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-h-candidate-localization}

\paragraph{Finite matrix under test.}
This record tests whether coarse reviewed subcellular localization can carry part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual left after the modeled translation readout has already been removed.  The finite coordinate surface has nine residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each coordinate is residualized against the same control block used for the protein-abundance and translation-readout comparisons: an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The localization readout is a twelve-column multi-label one-hot matrix extracted from reviewed UniProtKB subcellular annotations.  The tested response is the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$, projected onto the residualized localization columns and compared with a twelve-column Gaussian null of matched degrees of freedom.

\paragraph{Joined rows and ranks.}
The row counts are not treated as interchangeable across organisms, because localization availability changes the protein identifier join.  The danio row set contains $1984$ joined CDS rows from $14410$ reported abundance-joined CDS rows and $2821$ localization records.  Human contains $16008$ joined rows from $19053$ reported abundance-joined CDS rows and $16406$ localization records.  Yeast contains $4656$ joined rows from $5815$ reported abundance-joined CDS rows and $4831$ localization records.  In all three organisms the residual control design has $24$ control columns and rank $23$, while the localization block has $K=12$ columns and residual rank $12$.  Thus the localization projection is not a single-label lookup; it is a finite multi-column residual readout whose apparent absorption must be judged against a matched null.

\paragraph{Observed localization absorption.}
The absorption fractions are small but organism-specific.  For danio, localization absorbs $0.009508864544725801$ of the modeled complement residual, while the matched null has mean $0.0062549946178242525$ and $95$th percentile $0.011321328712802819$ over $200$ trials.  This is below the null cutoff and is read as no positive excess localization signal.  For human, localization absorbs $0.009254913329269913$, whereas the null mean is $0.0008032852113983743$ and the null $95$th percentile is $0.0013758521256775384$ over $200$ trials.  Human therefore shows excess absorption above the null baseline.  For yeast, localization absorbs $0.011225105608516642$, against null mean $0.002689902676583632$ and null $95$th percentile $0.004958116973149795$ over $200$ trials, so yeast also shows excess absorption.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{Danio} &= 0.009508864544725801 < 0.011321328712802819,\\
A_{Human} &= 0.009254913329269913 > 0.0013758521256775384,\\
A_{Yeast} &= 0.011225105608516642 > 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$

The four-case readback for this chapter is therefore not a uniform survival call.  Danio is a null-boundary case for this localization test.  Human and yeast are positive H-candidate cases in the narrow statistical sense that residualized localization columns carry more of the modeled complement abundance residual than a degree-of-freedom matched random readout.

\paragraph{Localization categories.}
The finite localization matrix is biologically coarse and multi-label.  In danio the largest categories are Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Cell membrane $230$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Golgi $125$, Secreted $79$, Vacuole/Lysosome $45$, Other $23$, and Peroxisome $14$.  In human the corresponding counts are Membrane $7119$, Cytoplasm $6305$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Golgi $1096$, Vacuole/Lysosome $482$, Other $161$, and Peroxisome $109$.  In yeast they are Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, Mitochondrion $811$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, Vacuole/Lysosome $217$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Secreted $91$, Peroxisome $77$, and Other $44$.  These counts explain why the statistic is interpreted as categorical residual absorption, not as a mechanistic compartment assignment for any individual coordinate.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation mediation.}
The matched measured-translation experiment provides the necessary contrast.  In that separate finite surface, the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates were tested against measured ribosome-seq translation efficiency and matched protein abundance.  The measured translation and protein-abundance scalars were $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.04034217614654355$, $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$, and mediated fraction $0.0$ for danio; $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.018837911344127926$, $S^{QP}=0.04288464996165745$, and mediated fraction $0.0027966485036786744$ for human; and $S^{QT}_{meas}=0.13681931343730436$, $S^{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, and mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The measured row joins were $13140$ three-way rows for danio, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, with residual degrees of freedom after controls $13117$, $11531$, and $5105$ respectively.  These values show that localization is not being used as a substitute for translation.  It is tested only after a modeled translation complement is formed, and its positive excess in human and yeast points to a remaining H-candidate readout rather than to a completed $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ explanation.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The computation supports a finite residual-localization absorption statement in human and yeast, and a null-boundary statement in danio, under the stated controls and row joins.  It does not show that $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates cause localization, that localization explains a translation mechanism, or that any coordinate determines folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organism phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  Subcellular localization here is a reviewed, coarse, multi-label annotation; multiple columns can absorb residual variance mechanically, which is why the matched random baseline is part of the definition of the readback.  A translation, folding, physical, functional, mechanistic, or universal claim would require a distinct layer-matched measurement and a new controlled statistic at that layer.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is mixed-positive at the H-candidate level.  Human and yeast pass the degree-of-freedom matched localization excess test, while danio does not.  The result is therefore a localized residual-routing observation for $B^*_{Q6}$ complement abundance, not a promotion to translation mediation or higher biological interpretation.
