\subsection{Localization as a Non-translation Residual Candidate}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-localization-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.} The experiment tests whether the part of the protein-abundance signal left after a modeled translation readout has a detectable subcellular-localization component. The finite row object is a matrix of coding-sequence rows, nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, a residualized modeled translation complement, and twelve multi-label UniProtKB reviewed localization columns. The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. The control design has $23$ control rank with $24$ control columns in each organism-specific fit: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Rows and readouts.} The localization contact is not a translation measurement. It is a coarse, reviewed, multi-label protein annotation readout. The joined localization matrix has $K=12$ localization columns in each organism, with $1,984$ joined CDS rows for \textit{Danio rerio}, $16,008$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $4,656$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The corresponding reported CDS joins before the localization restriction are $14,410$, $19,053$, and $5,815$, and the parsed localization-record counts are $2,821$, $16,406$, and $4,831$. The category counts are informative about the finite support: in human, for example, the largest parsed categories are Membrane with $7,119$ records, Cytoplasm with $6,305$, Nucleus with $5,837$, Cell membrane with $3,570$, Secreted with $1,876$, Cytoskeleton with $1,529$, Endoplasmic reticulum with $1,449$, Mitochondrion with $1,370$, Golgi with $1,096$, Vacuole/Lysosome with $482$, Peroxisome with $109$, and Other with $161$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.} Let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual after the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block and the modeled translation readout have been separated under the declared controls. Let $L$ be the residualized $12$-column localization matrix. The reported scalar is the fraction of the residual energy in $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ absorbed by projection onto $L$, compared to a $K$-column degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials. The finite results are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Abs}_{Loc}(\textit{Danio}) &= 0.009508864544725801, & \mathrm{Null}_{95} &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
\mathrm{Abs}_{Loc}(\textit{Human}) &= 0.009254913329269913, & \mathrm{Null}_{95} &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mathrm{Abs}_{Loc}(\textit{Yeast}) &= 0.011225105608516642, & \mathrm{Null}_{95} &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null means are $0.0062549946178242525$, $0.0008032852113983743$, and $0.002689902676583632$ for \textit{Danio}, human, and yeast respectively. Thus human and yeast show localization absorption above the local null threshold, while the zebrafish localization-restricted panel does not clear its own null threshold.

\paragraph{Coverage diagnostic.} The modeled-complement diagnostic is also sensitive to the localization join. The current localization-restricted $d_{modeled}$ values are $0.7608723943210729$ for \textit{Danio}, $0.9020707321191324$ for human, and $0.1997853864617063$ for yeast. The corresponding full abundance-CDS complement references are $0.874628721049266$, $0.9208949355679455$, and $0.21173219572706703$. The absolute differences are $0.11375632672819314$, $0.018824203448813193$, and $0.011946809265360736$. Human and yeast remain within the $0.02$ diagnostic tolerance, whereas \textit{Danio} does not; the finite interpretation is that localization availability changes the protein-id join enough to make the zebrafish localization projection non-comparable to the full abundance-CDS complement surface.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation mediation.} The separate measured-TE mediation run gives the translation-side context for the same nine-coordinate block. Its measured $S^{QT}$ values are $0.04034217614654355$ in \textit{Danio}, $0.009394266754822262$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast. Its protein-abundance survival values $S^{QP}$ are $0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$, with measured mediated fractions $0.0$, $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$. These numbers prevent the localization projection from being read as a primary translation mechanism: in human the measured mediated fraction is small, and in yeast the measured translation mediation is large while the localization projection is only a downstream H-candidate readout on the remaining modeled complement.

\paragraph{Comparison with turnover.} The human turnover candidate is a useful negative-scale comparator. On $8,057$ joined human turnover-abundance-CDS rows, the large human modeled-tAI complement has $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490492302532$ and $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ norm squared $1262.2237678771598$, but log half-life absorbs only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the complement residual. Its one-column degree-of-freedom null has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$, so the turnover candidate does not exceed its null. Against that comparator, the human and yeast localization projections are not merely numerical dust relative to their own $12$-column nulls, but they remain descriptive projections rather than causal explanations.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.} The local four-case interpretation is therefore: case A, no supported translation or protein readout, is not the present situation because measured translation and protein-abundance survival values exist in the companion row set; case B, translation-bounded readout without downstream protein interpretation, is too weak for the human and yeast abundance context; case C, direct protein-omics or post-translation prediction without a demonstrated mediating step, remains admissible for localization because the statistic is computed on the modeled complement and not by perturbing translation; case D, translation-mediated mechanism, is not reached by this localization surface. The finite localization result is a human-and-yeast H-candidate for where non-translation residual abundance signal may sit, not evidence that synonymous residual coordinates cause localization, trafficking, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Local boundary.} The admissible claim is narrow. UniProtKB reviewed localization absorbs more of the modeled translation-complement abundance residual than a matched random $12$-column readout in human and yeast, while zebrafish fails the same threshold and also fails the complement-join comparability diagnostic. No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this projection. Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic showing that the same $q_i$ first survives a measured translation readout and that conditioning on that readout removes or materially reduces the downstream association.
