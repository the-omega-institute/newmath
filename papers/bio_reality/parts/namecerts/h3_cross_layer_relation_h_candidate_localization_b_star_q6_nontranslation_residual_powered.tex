\subsection{Localization as a residual carrier for the $B^*_{Q6}$ complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite matrix surface.}
The localization packet tests a specific residual question: after the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinates have been residualized against sequence-level controls and after the modeled translation component has been removed from protein abundance, does a coarse protein-localization readout absorb any remaining abundance-aligned component?  The coordinate block is the nine-column list
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each tested organism the control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$, while the localization readout has $K=12$ residualized multi-label columns.

\paragraph{Joined rows and localization alphabet.}
The localization rows are finite and organism-specific.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, $1984$ CDS-localization rows are joined against $2821$ localization records, compared with $14410$ reported CDS rows in the abundance-side join.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $16008$ rows are joined against $16406$ localization records, compared with $19053$ reported CDS rows.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $4656$ rows are joined against $4831$ localization records, compared with $5815$ reported CDS rows.  The twelve localization columns are the parsed UniProt categories Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.  The largest category counts are not identical across organisms: human has $7119$ Membrane, $6305$ Cytoplasm, and $5837$ Nucleus annotations; yeast has $1863$ Membrane, $1736$ Cytoplasm, and $1733$ Nucleus annotations; zebrafish has $883$ Cytoplasm, $845$ Nucleus, and $708$ Membrane annotations.  Thus the readout is not a single scalar proxy for abundance; it is a finite categorical surface whose degrees of freedom must be compared to an explicit null.

\paragraph{Residual quantity being tested.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the component of residualized log protein abundance aligned with the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates after the sequence controls above.  Let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modeled translation readout used in the complement experiment, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ be the abundance component remaining after the modeled translation component has been removed.  The localization test projects $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto the residualized localization design $L$ and records the absorbed fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L &= \frac{\|\Pi_L P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}{\|P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed absorbed fractions are $0.0095088645$ for zebrafish, $0.0092549133$ for human, and $0.0112251056$ for yeast.  The matched Gaussian degree-of-freedom nulls, computed with $200$ trials per organism, have $95$th percentiles $0.0113213287$ for zebrafish, $0.0013758521$ for human, and $0.0049581170$ for yeast.  Consequently the localization signal exceeds the finite-column null in human and yeast, while zebrafish remains below its null boundary on the available localization join.

\paragraph{Complement consistency.}
The modeled-complement diagnostic is stable in human and yeast and is not stable in zebrafish under the localization-restricted join.  The full abundance-side complement reference gives $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949356$ for human and the localization-join value is $0.9020707321$, an absolute difference of $0.0188242034$, inside the declared $0.02$ tolerance.  Yeast gives $0.2117321957$ on the reference abundance join and $0.1997853865$ on the localization join, an absolute difference of $0.0119468093$, also inside tolerance.  Zebrafish gives $0.8746287210$ on the reference join and $0.7608723943$ on the localization join, an absolute difference of $0.1137563267$, outside tolerance.  This failure is not hidden: the localization availability filter changes the protein-id join strongly enough that the zebrafish localization projection is recorded as a computed boundary quantity rather than as a stable residual explanation.

\paragraph{Relation to direct protein-omics survival.}
The localization packet sits downstream of a direct protein-omics association surface, not in place of it.  Across the broader protein-abundance panel, all nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates survive the direct $Q\to P$ fit in each listed organism after length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls.  The direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.1076752668$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.2983762499$ for yeast, $0.0445456512$ for human, $0.0498279841$ for mouse, $0.0461352981$ for zebrafish, and $0.0619062459$ for \emph{B. subtilis}.  The direct survival matrix has high join sizes, for example $19053$ human CDS-protein rows with a $0.9779294770$ join hit rate, $5815$ yeast rows with a $0.9156038419$ hit rate, and $14410$ zebrafish rows with a $1.0$ hit rate.  Those direct values justify searching for downstream carriers, but they do not identify a mechanism by themselves.

\paragraph{Translation and non-translation split.}
Measured translation-efficiency mediation supplies the local boundary for deciding what the localization residual is allowed to mean.  In the measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition, the measured mediated fraction is $0.4793069656$ for yeast, $0.0409115834$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485$ for human, and $0.0$ for zebrafish after truncating the negative raw zebrafish value $-0.0480148605$ to the unit interval.  The corresponding $S^{QT}_{\mathrm{meas}}$ values are $0.1368193134$ for yeast, $0.0093942668$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0188379113$ for human, and $0.0403421761$ for zebrafish.  Thus the localization experiment is not a translation proof; it asks whether the component not removed by a modeled translation readout has a coarse post-translational categorical correlate.  In the joint TE/stability decomposition, the yeast joint mediation fraction is $0.5195182226$, with TE-only fraction $0.4697188320$ and stability-only fraction $0.0626618595$; human has joint fraction $0.1689071210$, TE-only $0.0054949086$, and stability-only $0.1531467987$; \emph{E. coli} has joint fraction $0.2404669587$, TE-only $0.0197552134$, and stability-only $0.2305205632$.  These numbers reinforce the same separation: translation, stability, and localization are different finite readout surfaces.

\paragraph{Adjacent reduction checks.}
The yeast readout-dictionary certificate reports $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$ out of $4758$ available joined rows, $5$ folds, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077$, selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.0291490072$, certified coverage $C^*=0.2453821144$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856$.  The $f3\_stress$ dwell mediation check on yeast reports $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1257$, $5$ folds, base $R^2=0.1855578758$, full $R^2=0.2041226152$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0185647394$, permutation null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362$, $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441451$, and approximate $p=7.8739160572\times 10^{-9}$.  The structural-order positional check classifies the structural ramp module as $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$: the primary $50$-codon ramp increment is $0.0011165640$, the body increment is $-0.0002615731$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value is $p=0.05859375$.  These auxiliary numbers keep the localization result in its correct position: it is one candidate residual carrier among several measured or bounded readouts, not a final biological layer closure.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is positive but scoped.  Human and yeast show localization absorption above the degree-of-freedom matched null: human has $0.0092549133>0.0013758521$, and yeast has $0.0112251056>0.0049581170$.  Zebrafish has $0.0095088645<0.0113213287$ and also fails the modeled-complement join-stability tolerance, so it is retained as a computed non-supporting row.  The chapter therefore records localization as an $H$-candidate for the non-translation residual in human and yeast, with a null-boundary outcome in zebrafish.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
No translation law follows from the localization projection.  No folding statement, physical admissibility statement, molecular function statement, trafficking mechanism, causal mediation claim, phenotype claim, or universal biological rule follows from these rows.  The localization readout is a coarse, multi-label UniProt annotation surface; the absorbed fraction is descriptive; and the multi-column design can absorb variation mechanically.  Only excess over the matched null is counted as residual signal, and even that signal says only that localization annotations carry finite information about the modeled-translation complement on the joined rows.  Any stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.
