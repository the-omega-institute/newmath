\subsection{Localization as an H-candidate for the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-localization-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite-row question is whether a coarse protein-state annotation can account for part of the abundance component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual that is not absorbed by the modeled translation readout.  The tested object is not a raw codon count and not a translation-rate measurement.  It is the modeled-tAI complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, formed after projecting measured protein abundance against the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and then removing the modeled-tAI component.  Subcellular localization is then used only as an H-candidate readout: a multi-label categorical surface that may absorb some remaining abundance variance.  The result is therefore a bounded finite-row projection statement, not a statement about trafficking mechanism, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, or universality.

\paragraph{Finite matrix and controls.}
The source design has nine residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\mathrm{\ vs\ }UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\mathrm{\ vs\ }UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\mathrm{\ vs\ }AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\mathrm{\ vs\ }UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\mathrm{\ vs\ }ACY},\ f3_{\mathrm{stress}}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each organism is residualized against the same control family: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control matrix has 24 columns and rank $23$ in each localization run.  The localization readout has $K=12$ columns, with one-hot multi-label classes for Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.

\paragraph{Rows entering the localization test.}
Three organism panels are finite and non-vacuous.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, 2,821 localization records are parsed and 1,984 CDS rows join the codon, abundance, modeled-complement, and localization surfaces, compared with 14,410 CDS rows in the broader abundance join.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, 16,406 localization records are parsed and 16,008 rows join, compared with 19,053 reported CDS-abundance joins.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, 4,831 localization records are parsed and 4,656 rows join, compared with 5,815 reported CDS-abundance joins.  These row counts matter because the projection is evaluated on the localization-available subset, not on the full proteomics panel.

\paragraph{Localization dictionaries.}
The parsed localization dictionaries are broad enough to avoid a single-label artifact.  The human panel contains 6,305 Cytoplasm labels, 7,119 Membrane labels, 5,837 Nucleus labels, 3,570 Cell membrane labels, 1,876 Secreted labels, 1,529 Cytoskeleton labels, 1,449 Endoplasmic reticulum labels, 1,370 Mitochondrion labels, 1,096 Golgi labels, 482 Vacuole/Lysosome labels, 161 Other labels, and 109 Peroxisome labels.  The yeast panel contains 1,736 Cytoplasm labels, 1,863 Membrane labels, 1,733 Nucleus labels, 811 Mitochondrion labels, 426 Endoplasmic reticulum labels, 274 Cell membrane labels, 224 Golgi labels, 217 Vacuole/Lysosome labels, 165 Cytoskeleton labels, 91 Secreted labels, 77 Peroxisome labels, and 44 Other labels.  The danio panel is smaller, with 883 Cytoplasm labels, 845 Nucleus labels, 708 Membrane labels, 247 Mitochondrion labels, 230 Cell membrane labels, 207 Endoplasmic reticulum labels, 182 Cytoskeleton labels, 125 Golgi labels, 79 Secreted labels, 45 Vacuole/Lysosome labels, 23 Other labels, and 14 Peroxisome labels.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
For each organism, the tested statistic is the fraction of the modeled-tAI complement abundance component absorbed by the residualized localization columns.  The finite values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because a twelve-column categorical readout can absorb variance mechanically, the comparison baseline is a degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null with 200 trials per organism.  The corresponding null means and 95th percentiles are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{0,\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.0062549946178242525, & q_{0.95,\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
\mu_{0,\mathrm{human}} &= 0.0008032852113983743, & q_{0.95,\mathrm{human}} &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mu_{0,\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.002689902676583632, & q_{0.95,\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus human and yeast exceed the dof-matched 95th percentile, while danio does not.  The chapter-level readback is consequently mixed: localization carries excess residual signal in human and yeast under this statistic, but the danio localization-available subset is not above its null threshold.

\paragraph{Subset stability of the modeled complement.}
The modeled complement itself changes when the localization join restricts the row set.  Human and yeast are stable under this restriction: human has $d_{\mathrm{mod}}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join versus $0.9208949355679455$ on the full abundance-CDS join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$; yeast has $d_{\mathrm{mod}}=0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$.  Danio is not stable at the same tolerance: $d_{\mathrm{mod}}=0.7608723943210729$ on the localization join versus $0.874628721049266$ on the full abundance-CDS join, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$.  This is reported as a row-availability effect, not hidden as a biological conclusion.

\paragraph{Relation to the direct protein-omics and translation surfaces.}
The localization H-candidate is downstream of a direct abundance association, but it does not replace the translation-mediated test.  In the broad direct protein-omics surface, all fifteen organisms have rank-sufficient $B^*_{Q6}$ residual designs with $9$ residual coordinates and rank $9$ after controls, and all have nine surviving coordinates for $S^{QP}$.  The direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, and $0.04613529807085213$ for danio.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also finite where joined: $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.018837911344127926$ in human, $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.04034217614654355$ in danio, and $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{Escherichia coli}.  The measured TE-mediated abundance decomposition gives mediated fractions $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0$ for danio after clipping a negative raw value $-0.04801486048721136$, and $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}.  These numbers show why localization is treated as a residual H-candidate: it is evaluated after a translation-conditioned abundance decomposition has already left non-translation residual structure.

\paragraph{Turnover contrast.}
Human protein turnover provides a useful negative contrast for the same residual logic.  On 8,057 joined human turnover-abundance-CDS rows, the turnover projection has $R^2_{Q,T_{\mathrm{turn}}}=0.004615408769189838$ but absorbs only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the modeled-tAI complement component.  Its 200-trial dof null has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and 95th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$, so the turnover absorption does not exceed the null.  Localization is therefore not merely a generic consequence of adding any protein-state covariate: in the available finite panels, human and yeast localization exceed their matched nulls, while human turnover does not.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is ready in a bounded sense.  Localization is a credible H-candidate for the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual in human and yeast, because the absorbed fractions $0.009254913329269913$ and $0.011225105608516642$ exceed their respective dof-matched 95th-percentile nulls $0.0013758521256775384$ and $0.004958116973149795$.  Danio remains a non-supporting row subset for this test: its absorbed fraction $0.009508864544725801$ is below the null 95th percentile $0.011321328712802819$, and its modeled-complement value shifts substantially under the localization join.  The result is a finite projection readback over reviewed UniProtKB subcellular localization annotations.  It does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization mechanism, trafficking mechanism, causal mediation, or a universal biological rule.  Any such stronger interpretation would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new statistic at that layer.
