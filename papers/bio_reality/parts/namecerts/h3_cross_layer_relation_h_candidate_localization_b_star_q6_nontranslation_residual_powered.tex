\subsection{Localization as a Residual H-Candidate for the Rank-Powered $B^*_{Q6}$ Surface}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The localization test uses the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate panel
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each coordinate is evaluated after removing the same sibling control block: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates.  The localization readout is not a translation readout and is not treated as a direct mechanism.  It is a coarse UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-location annotation, encoded as a multi-label one-hot matrix with $K=12$ localization columns and compared against a degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null.

\paragraph{Joined rows and residual ranks.}
The finite rows are organism-specific.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, the localization join contains $16008$ CDS rows from $19053$ CDS rows reported in the abundance join, with $16406$ localization records, $24$ control columns, and $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the join contains $4656$ rows from $5815$ reported CDS rows, with $4831$ localization records, $24$ control columns, and $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, the localization join contains $1984$ rows from $14410$ reported CDS rows, with $2821$ localization records, $24$ control columns, and $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$.  The organism-specific category counts show that the readout is broad rather than single-compartment: human has $6305$ cytoplasm, $7119$ membrane, $5837$ nucleus, $1876$ secreted, and $1370$ mitochondrion annotations; yeast has $1736$ cytoplasm, $1863$ membrane, $1733$ nucleus, and $811$ mitochondrion annotations; danio has $883$ cytoplasm, $708$ membrane, $845$ nucleus, and $247$ mitochondrion annotations.

\paragraph{Modeled translation complement.}
The tested target is the modeled-tAI complement of the abundance residual, denoted here as $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$.  The finite diagnostic $d_{modeled}$ compares the current localization join against the full abundance complement calculation.  Human remains within the local tolerance: $d_{modeled}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join versus $0.9208949355679455$ on the reference full abundance-CDS join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$.  Yeast also remains within tolerance: $d_{modeled}=0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$.  Danio is reported as a join-sensitive case rather than silently promoted: $d_{modeled}=0.7608723943210729$ on the localization join versus $0.874628721049266$ on the reference abundance join, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
The localization candidate is measured by projecting $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto the residualized localization matrix and then comparing the absorbed fraction with a $K$-column null of the same effective size.  The resulting finite comparisons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{human:}\quad& A_L=0.009254913329269913,\quad N_{95}=0.0013758521256775384,\quad N_{mean}=0.0008032852113983743,\\
\mathrm{yeast:}\quad& A_L=0.011225105608516642,\quad N_{95}=0.004958116973149795,\quad N_{mean}=0.002689902676583632,\\
\mathrm{danio:}\quad& A_L=0.009508864544725801,\quad N_{95}=0.011321328712802819,\quad N_{mean}=0.0062549946178242525.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus human and yeast exceed their degree-of-freedom-matched $95\%$ null baselines, while danio does not.  The local readback is therefore an organism-qualified H-candidate: localization carries excess residual signal in human and yeast on this finite panel, but the same claim is not made for danio because the absorbed fraction is below the null $95\%$ threshold and the join changes the modeled-complement diagnostic materially.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance surfaces.}
The localization calculation sits downstream of several separate finite surfaces.  Direct protein-omics survival is positive across the larger abundance panel: for example, $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, and $0.04613529807085213$ in danio, with all nine coordinates surviving in each of those direct abundance fits.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also positive on its own contact: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$ in human, $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, and $0.04034217614654355$ in danio.  The measured mediation surface is much more uneven: the measured mediated fraction is $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, and $0.0$ in danio after clipping a negative raw fraction.  This is why localization is treated only as a candidate carrier for residual protein-abundance signal, not as a substitute for the measured translation step.

\paragraph{Reduction context.}
Other finite reductions sharpen the interpretation without replacing the localization test.  In the joint TE-plus-mRNA-stability decomposition, yeast has $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$ and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, giving joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$ with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  Human has $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$ and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$, giving joint mediation fraction $0.16890712099923358$ with the same right-tail permutation value.  The yeast readout-dictionary certificate reports $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, and coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  These numbers show that measured readouts explain part, but not all, of the abundance residual.  The localization calculation is one concrete probe into that remaining component.

\paragraph{Position and dwell checks.}
The $f3\_stress$ dwell experiments provide a separate local mechanism-adjacent readout but do not license a localization mechanism.  In yeast, the dwell mediation test reports $n_{join}=1257$, $\mathrm{base}\ R^2=0.1855578757644517$, $\mathrm{full}\ R^2=0.20412261520502883$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, null $95\%$ value $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$ for the $f3$--dwell correlation.  The positional structural-order check classifies the structural-order result as \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}: with primary ramp size $50$, the weighted ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, the weighted body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, the ramp sign-flip value is $0.0478515625$, and the paired ramp-minus-body value is $0.05859375$.  These finite rows support a narrow statement about a ramp-associated signal, not a folded-structure or compartment mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The localization record is ready as a bounded H-candidate statement.  The positive part is finite and specific: human and yeast localization annotations absorb more of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ than a matched random $K=12$ readout, while danio is kept at null boundary.  The result is descriptive and row-bound.  It says that a coarse localization readout is statistically aligned with part of the non-modeled-translation abundance complement in two tested organisms.  It does not say that $B^*_{Q6}$ causes localization, that localization is the mechanism of translation-mediated protein abundance, or that the residual coordinate system realizes folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal biological rule.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and a new finite statistic showing the corresponding translation, structure, physical, functional, perturbational, or universality condition directly.
