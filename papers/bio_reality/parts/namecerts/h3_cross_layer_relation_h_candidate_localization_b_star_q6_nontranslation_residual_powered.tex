\subsection{Localization absorption of the modeled non-translation residual}\label{sec:bior-h-candidate-localization-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The localization test is a finite cross-layer readout on the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. Each coordinate is first read through the same residualization frame used by the protein-abundance and translation-efficiency panels: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and the M-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons lying in the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The control design has $24$ columns and residualized rank $23$ in the three localization organisms. The measured localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-location surface with categories Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
The target is not the full protein-abundance signal and not a translation mechanism. It is the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, projected onto residualized localization one-hot columns. In symbols, the reported scalar is the localization absorption fraction
$A_L$ of the modeled complement component after the same $Z$ controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(o) &= \frac{\|\Pi_{L_o \mid Z}\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}},o}\|^2}{\|\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}},o}\|^2},\\
L_o &\in \mathbb{R}^{n_o\times 12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A $12$-column degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null is evaluated for each organism; only excess over the organism-specific null $95$th percentile is interpreted as localization carrying part of the residual signal.

\paragraph{Joined rows and localization records.}
The finite joins are organism-specific. For Danio rerio, the full abundance-CDS panel reports $14410$ joined CDS rows, while the localization-limited join contains $1984$ rows from $2821$ localization records. For Homo sapiens, the corresponding numbers are $19053$ full abundance-CDS rows and $16008$ localization-joined rows from $16406$ localization records. For Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the corresponding numbers are $5815$ full abundance-CDS rows and $4656$ localization-joined rows from $4831$ localization records. The localization payloads are fixed local files: Danio rerio has $577472$ bytes with sha256 $491b4557da573cebd4d962c7efc74cbb76b04e228b2b98d9352d8dec9cd2a3eb$, Homo sapiens has $4594821$ bytes with sha256 $4a79e64ed107dac2c89b67e2f6f2a0368a9a3e5850dfcc481f8c5e1f866b44e9$, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $864253$ bytes with sha256 $6a61433a589317e3be7cc94241c806c95b5fbbf186e0af8a38ed9c1c2f4d8380$.

\paragraph{Absorption result.}
The finite localization absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_L(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_L(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched null means and null $95$th percentiles are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_0(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.0062549946178242525,& q_{0.95} &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
\mu_0(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.0008032852113983743,& q_{0.95} &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mu_0(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.002689902676583632,& q_{0.95} &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the human and yeast localization projections exceed their degree-of-freedom matched null boundaries, while Danio rerio does not. The readback is therefore a two-organism positive H-candidate localization absorption result, with a negative Danio rerio localization-limited readback under the declared threshold.

\paragraph{Complement consistency.}
The modeled complement fraction on the localization join is also compared with the full abundance-CDS complement reference. Danio rerio changes from $0.874628721049266$ on the $14410$-row reference join to $0.7608723943210729$ on the $1984$-row localization join, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$, so it fails the $0.02$ stability check. Human changes from $0.9208949355679455$ to $0.9020707321191324$, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$, and passes the $0.02$ check. Yeast changes from $0.21173219572706703$ to $0.1997853864617063$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$, and also passes. This makes the human and yeast absorption calls the clean localization evidence in this finite surface; the Danio rerio value is reported as a localization-availability boundary rather than promoted.

These companion values locate the localization chapter as an H-candidate residual analysis: it asks what remains after a modeled translation readout, not whether localization is itself a translation measurement.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The localization H-candidate test is finite-row ready. It supports the following bounded statement: on the localization-available human and yeast joins, residualized multi-label subcellular localization absorbs more of the modeled non-translation abundance residual than a $12$-column matched null would predict. Danio rerio is not a positive localization absorption case under the same rule, and its localization-limited complement fraction differs substantially from the full abundance-CDS reference. No causal claim follows from these projections. The result does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Any such claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
