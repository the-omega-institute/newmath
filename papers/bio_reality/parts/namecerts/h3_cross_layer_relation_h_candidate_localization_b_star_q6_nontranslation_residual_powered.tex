\subsection{A localization test for the non-translation residue of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance signal}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-residue}

\paragraph{Question.}
The rank-powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ panel leaves a measurable protein-abundance association after the usual sequence controls and after a modelled translation readout have been accounted for. The present finite-row test asks whether that remaining abundance component is carried by a coarse protein-localization readout. The tested object is not the original $q_i \to P$ association, and not a causal route. It is the residual protein-abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ projected onto residualized multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-localization columns. Thus the local question is whether localization annotations carry more of the non-translation abundance residue than a degree-of-freedom matched null would carry mechanically.

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The tested matrix has nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K_{AAA},\quad Arg_{AGR},\quad Ile_{AUA},\quad Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\quad Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\quad Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\quad Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\quad f3_{stress},
\end{aligned}
$$
residualized against an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each localization organism. The localization readout has $K=12$ residualized one-hot columns: Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.

\paragraph{Rows and localization payloads.}
Three organism-level localization joins were computed. For \emph{Danio rerio}, $14410$ CDS rows were available in the abundance join, while the localization join retained $1984$ rows and $2821$ localization records. For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding counts were $19053$ CDS rows, $16008$ joined rows, and $16406$ localization records. For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the counts were $5815$ CDS rows, $4656$ joined rows, and $4831$ localization records. The parsed localization category counts were, respectively, Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, and Mitochondrion $247$ among the larger zebrafish categories; Cytoplasm $6305$, Nucleus $5837$, Membrane $7119$, and Cell membrane $3570$ in human; and Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, and Mitochondrion $811$ in yeast. These counts matter because the readout is multi-label and annotation-density dependent, so a raw projection can absorb variance even when no biological localization explanation is present.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the abundance component attached to the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ design after the sequence controls. Let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modelled tAI-complement translation readout used in the abundance-complement experiment, and let $L$ be the residualized $12$-column localization design. The computed quantity is the fraction of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ absorbed by $L$, compared with a Gaussian null design with the same column count. In schematic form the reported scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L &= \frac{\|\Pi_L\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}{\|\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2},\\
A_L &> A_{\mathrm{null},0.95}\quad \text{is the descriptive excess-localization condition.}
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a descriptive projection test. It is not a perturbation, not temporal ordering, and not a causal mediation analysis.

\paragraph{Observed localization absorption.}
The localization absorption fractions were $0.009508864544725801$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.009254913329269913$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.011225105608516642$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, each with localization rank $12$. The degree-of-freedom matched null used $200$ trials. Its means and $95$th percentiles were $0.0062549946178242525$ and $0.011321328712802819$ for zebrafish, $0.0008032852113983743$ and $0.0013758521256775384$ for human, and $0.002689902676583632$ and $0.004958116973149795$ for yeast. Therefore human and yeast show localization absorption far above the matched-null $95$th percentile, while zebrafish does not exceed its own null cutoff. The strongest finite-row statement is consequently organism-conditional: localization carries a detectable fraction of the non-translation abundance residue in the human and yeast joins, but not in the zebrafish localization join under this statistic.

\paragraph{Compatibility with the translation and stability panels.}
The localization result is downstream of a measured translation-abundance decomposition rather than a substitute for it. In the measured TE panel, the four organism joins had $3415$ rows for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ rows for human, $5128$ rows for yeast, and $13140$ rows for zebrafish. The measured $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance survival values were $S^{QP}=0.10941535951828074$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.04288464996165745$ in human, $0.3146818503437042$ in yeast, and $0.04926578454614652$ in zebrafish. The measured translation-readout survival values were $S^{QT}=0.009394266754822262$, $0.018837911344127926$, $0.13681931343730436$, and $0.04034217614654355$, respectively. The measured mediated fractions were small in \emph{E. coli} and human, $0.04091158340950673$ and $0.0027966485036786744$, large in yeast at $0.4793069656345167$, and clamped to $0$ for zebrafish because the raw value was $-0.04801486048721136$. A separate TE-plus-mRNA-stability partition gave right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in each of \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast, with joint mediation fractions $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$. Its overlap classification was approximately independent in all three organisms. These numbers place localization beside TE and mRNA-stability as an additional residual readout, not above them as an explanatory mechanism.

\paragraph{Turnover comparison.}
The human turnover probe gives a useful negative calibration. On $8057$ human rows, Mathieson-style log half-life absorbed only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ of the large human modelled-tAI-complement abundance residue, below the null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$ from $200$ trials. The same human abundance-complement reference had $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490492302532$, $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ norm squared $1262.2237678771598$, and modelled complement fraction $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455$. Thus turnover does not explain the human residual in this finite test, while localization does exceed its own matched-null threshold on the larger $16008$-row human localization join.

\paragraph{Join sensitivity.}
The localization test changes the available protein-id join, and this is visible in the modelled-complement diagnostic. Human remains close to the full abundance-CDS reference: $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join versus $0.9208949355679455$ on the full reference, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$. Yeast is also close: $0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$. Zebrafish is not close by the fixed $0.02$ tolerance: $0.7608723943210729$ versus $0.874628721049266$, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$. The zebrafish non-excess localization result should therefore be read together with a stronger join-change warning.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row verdict is that the non-translation residue of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association has a localization component in human and yeast, but not in the zebrafish localization join under the same null rule. The result is a coarse categorical projection onto UniProtKB reviewed localization annotations. It does not establish translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, trafficking mechanism, causal mediation, organismal phenotype, or any universal biological law. Such conclusions would require their own layer-matched measurements and a statistic showing that the relevant signal survives after the declared controls.
