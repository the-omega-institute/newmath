\subsection{Localization absorption for the non-translation complement}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-complement}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a coarse protein-localization readout carries part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component that is not accounted for by the modeled translation readout. The finite carrier is a matched matrix whose rows are coding sequences with protein identifiers and whose columns include nine residual synonymous coordinates, a control design $Z$, a modeled-translation complement abundance vector, and a multi-label localization design. The nine residual coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The control side has $24$ listed columns: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. In the three localization joins the effective rank of $Z$ is $23$.

\paragraph{Readout construction.}
The localization contact is the reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-localization annotation, parsed as a $K=12$ multi-label one-hot readout. The tested quantity is not raw abundance and not a translation rate. It is the modeled-translation complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, projected onto residualized localization columns after the same $Z$ controls. The finite readout therefore asks a narrow question: among proteins for which abundance, coding sequence, and localization rows can be joined, does the categorical localization design absorb more of this complement than a degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null would absorb mechanically?

\paragraph{Joined rows and category counts.}
The localization join is organism-specific. For $Danio\ rerio$, $1{,}984$ CDS-protein-localization rows are retained from $14{,}410$ reported CDS joins and $2{,}821$ localization records. The parsed localization counts are Cell membrane $230$, Cytoplasm $883$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Golgi $125$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Nucleus $845$, Other $23$, Peroxisome $14$, Secreted $79$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $45$. For $Homo\ sapiens$, $16{,}008$ rows are retained from $19{,}053$ reported CDS joins and $16{,}406$ localization records; the corresponding counts are Cell membrane $3{,}570$, Cytoplasm $6{,}305$, Cytoskeleton $1{,}529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1{,}449$, Golgi $1{,}096$, Membrane $7{,}119$, Mitochondrion $1{,}370$, Nucleus $5{,}837$, Other $161$, Peroxisome $109$, Secreted $1{,}876$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $482$. For $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, $4{,}656$ rows are retained from $5{,}815$ reported CDS joins and $4{,}831$ localization records; the counts are Cell membrane $274$, Cytoplasm $1{,}736$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Golgi $224$, Membrane $1{,}863$, Mitochondrion $811$, Nucleus $1{,}733$, Other $44$, Peroxisome $77$, Secreted $91$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $217$.

\paragraph{Complement consistency.}
The localization join changes the available protein set, so the modeled complement is checked again on the active rows. The abundance-complement reference values were $0.874628721049266$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $0.9208949355679455$ for $Homo\ sapiens$, and $0.21173219572706703$ for $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$. On the localization join those become $0.7608723943210729$, $0.9020707321191324$, and $0.1997853864617063$, respectively. Thus the absolute differences are $0.11375632672819314$, $0.018824203448813193$, and $0.011946809265360736$. The human and yeast joins remain within the declared $0.02$ tolerance, while the zebrafish localization subset does not. The failed zebrafish match is not hidden: it means localization availability changes the active protein-id subset enough that its complement score must be read as a localization-subset statistic rather than as a full-abundance-join statistic.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
Let $H$ denote the residualized localization design on the active join, with rank $12$, and let $u=\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the residualized complement vector. The reported absorption is the squared-energy fraction of $u$ captured by $H$ after the $Z$ residualization. The dof-matched null uses the same number of localization columns and $200$ deterministic trials per organism. The finite comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(Danio) &= 0.009508864544725801, & N_{95}(Danio) &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(Human) &= 0.009254913329269913, & N_{95}(Human) &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(Yeast) &= 0.011225105608516642, & N_{95}(Yeast) &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding null means are $0.0062549946178242525$, $0.0008032852113983743$, and $0.002689902676583632$. Thus human and yeast show excess localization absorption above the $95\%$ null boundary, whereas zebrafish does not exceed the null boundary and also has the largest complement-subset discrepancy.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding measured readouts.}
The localization test is downstream of a broader $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance and translation matrix, but it is not allowed to replace that matrix. Direct protein-omics survival against PAXdb abundance was positive in the powered panel: for example $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in $E.\ coli$, and all $15$ listed organisms had all nine coordinates surviving at the positive threshold. Measured ribosome-TE survival was also computed separately, with $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, $0.009394266754822262$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast. The measured TE-to-abundance mediation fractions were $0$ for zebrafish after truncating a suppressive raw value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast. These numbers justify treating localization only as an additional candidate readout for the leftover component; they do not turn localization into a translation measurement.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The finite readback has three organism-level outcomes. In zebrafish, the localization absorption is $0.009508864544725801$ against a null $95\%$ value of $0.011321328712802819$, and the active localization join changes the complement score by $0.11375632672819314$ relative to the full abundance join. This is a null localization outcome for this finite row set. In human, localization absorbs $0.009254913329269913$ against a null $95\%$ value of $0.0013758521256775384$, with complement discrepancy $0.018824203448813193$; this is a positive localization-carrying residual signal on the active row set. In yeast, localization absorbs $0.011225105608516642$ against a null $95\%$ value of $0.004958116973149795$, with complement discrepancy $0.011946809265360736$; this is also a positive localization-carrying residual signal on the active row set. The result is therefore not a universal localization law; it is a finite, organism-indexed absorption pattern with two positive excesses and one blocked/null case.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter establishes only a descriptive projection fact. Subcellular localization here is a coarse, reviewed, multi-label annotation; it is not a perturbation, trafficking assay, folding assay, translation-rate measurement, or causal mediator. A positive excess over the dof-matched null says that localization labels carry some of the modeled-translation complement in the active join. It does not by itself imply translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, a mechanism, an organismal phenotype, or a universal biological rule. Any such claim requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
