\subsection{Localization Absorption of the Modeled-Translation Complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-h-localization-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The tested object is a finite row surface over nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, a modeled translation readout, measured protein abundance, and a coarse protein-localization readout. The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}stress$. Each organism-level fit first removes a control space $Z$ containing an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined from the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The recorded control design has $24$ columns and residual rank $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in each of the three localization organisms.

\paragraph{Complement target.}
The response being localized is not the full protein-abundance association. It is the modeled-translation complement $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$, obtained after the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component has been compared with the modeled translation surface. The reference complement sizes show that this residual is organism-specific: the full-abundance-join complement diagnostic is $d_{mod}=0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish, $d_{mod}=0.9208949355679455$ for human, and $d_{mod}=0.21173219572706703$ for yeast. On the localization join itself the corresponding values are $0.7608723943210729$, $0.9020707321191324$, and $0.1997853864617063$. Thus the human and yeast localization joins remain within $0.02$ of the full-abundance reference, with absolute differences $0.018824203448813193$ and $0.011946809265360736$, while the zebrafish localization join differs by $0.11375632672819314$ and must be read as a join-shifted localization surface rather than a direct replica of the full abundance complement.

\paragraph{Localization readout.}
The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label one-hot surface parsed from reviewed UniProtKB subcellular localization annotations. The joined surfaces contain $1984$ zebrafish CDS rows from $2821$ localization records, $16008$ human CDS rows from $16406$ localization records, and $4656$ yeast CDS rows from $4831$ localization records. The twelve retained categories are Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome. The dominant category counts make the row surfaces concrete: human has $7119$ Membrane, $6305$ Cytoplasm, and $5837$ Nucleus records; yeast has $1863$ Membrane, $1736$ Cytoplasm, and $1733$ Nucleus records; zebrafish has $883$ Cytoplasm, $845$ Nucleus, and $708$ Membrane records. These counts define a coarse categorical readout, not a trafficking mechanism or a physical compartment-selection law.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
For each organism, the localized quantity is the fraction of the modeled-translation complement absorbed by the residualized $K=12$ localization design. The computed absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{loc}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_{loc}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_{loc}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
A degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian baseline was computed with $200$ trials per organism. Its $95\%$ reference points are
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{.95}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
N_{.95}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
N_{.95}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore human and yeast exceed the matched-column null, while zebrafish does not. The finite readback is not simply that localization columns absorb some variance: a $12$-column categorical design can absorb mechanically. The informative comparison is the excess over the matched-null threshold, and that comparison is positive for human and yeast only.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein and translation surfaces.}
The localization result sits downstream of two separately recorded finite surfaces. Direct protein-omics survival $S^{QP}$ was positive across a fifteen-organism PAXdb surface after the same family of controls, with examples $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.04613529807085213$ for zebrafish, and $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}. Measured ribosome-TE survival $S^{QT,meas}$ was also positive on a four-organism measured-TE surface, with $R^2_{QT}=0.018837911344127926$ for human, $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, $0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, and $0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{E. coli}. However, the measured protein-abundance mediation surface reports sharply different translation-conditioned fractions: $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0$ after truncating a suppressive raw value $-0.04801486048721136$ for zebrafish, and $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}. The localization surface therefore tests an $H$-candidate for the residual left by the modeled translation route; it is not a substitute for the measured $Q\to T\to P$ mediation statistic.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is a two-organism positive localization absorption on the modeled-translation complement, with one organism-specific failure. Human and yeast support the statement that coarse localization annotations carry non-null information about the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component after modeled translation has been removed. Zebrafish remains below the matched-null threshold and also has a localization join whose complement diagnostic differs substantially from the full abundance reference. The strongest local reading is consequently conditional and descriptive: localization is a plausible measured holder of part of the nontranslation residual in human and yeast under this categorical readout, while the zebrafish surface remains unresolved under the present join.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter. The localization entries are multi-label annotation columns, and the absorbed fraction is a statistical projection of a residual abundance component. A stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as perturbation, rescue, trafficking, compartment-specific abundance, folding, physical-state, or functional assay rows, joined to the same CDS units and evaluated after the translation-readout step and the same control family.
