\subsection{Localization As A Non-Translation Residual Candidate}
\label{subsec:bioq6-localization-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The localization packet tests whether the part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal left after a modeled translation readout has been removed can be carried by coarse protein-location annotation.  The row carrier is a same-protein join among coding-sequence codon counts, measured protein abundance, the modeled-$T$ complement of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance projection, and UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-localization labels.  The $Q$ side has nine residual coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All entries are residualized against the same control family: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $Q$ vectors.  The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each reported organism.  The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label one-hot surface with categories Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.

\paragraph{Joined rows and residual target.}
The tested organisms are zebrafish, human, and budding yeast.  For $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$ the localization join uses $1{,}984$ CDS rows from $14{,}410$ abundance-joined CDS records and $2{,}821$ localization records.  For $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$ it uses $16{,}008$ CDS rows from $19{,}053$ abundance-joined CDS records and $16{,}406$ localization records.  For $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$ it uses $4{,}656$ CDS rows from $5{,}815$ abundance-joined CDS records and $4{,}831$ localization records.  The projected target is the modeled-translation complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, not a direct claim about translation efficiency or a causal downstream state.

\paragraph{Localization absorption statistic.}
For each organism the residualized localization matrix $L$ is compared with a degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null using $200$ trials.  The reported absorption fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad A_L=0.009508864544725801,\quad N_{0.95}=0.011321328712802819,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad A_L=0.009254913329269913,\quad N_{0.95}=0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:&\quad A_L=0.011225105608516642,\quad N_{0.95}=0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus human and yeast exceed their degree-of-freedom-matched $95\%$ null thresholds, while zebrafish does not.  The null means are $0.0062549946178242525$ for zebrafish, $0.0008032852113983743$ for human, and $0.002689902676583632$ for yeast.  The strongest finite statement is therefore not that localization explains the $Q$ residual in general, but that the human and yeast localization surfaces carry more of the modeled-translation-complement component than expected from a same-rank random readout.

\paragraph{Join sensitivity.}
The complement diagnostic changes with localization availability.  The modeled-complement fraction on the localization join is $0.7608723943210729$ in zebrafish versus $0.874628721049266$ on the full abundance-CDS reference join, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$ and therefore outside the $0.02$ agreement band.  Human has $0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join versus $0.9208949355679455$ on the reference join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$.  Yeast has $0.1997853864617063$ on the localization join versus $0.21173219572706703$ on the reference join, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$.  The human and yeast localization tests therefore preserve the modeled-complement scale closely enough for the reported absorption to be read as an $H$-candidate signal on the available rows; the zebrafish failure is reported as a join-dependent boundary rather than hidden as a positive case.

\paragraph{Context from the translation and abundance surfaces.}
The direct protein-abundance surface is real but not mechanistic: after the same style of $Z$ controls, all nine $Q$ coordinates survive against measured PAXdb abundance in fifteen organisms, with $R^2_{QP}$ values ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in $\mathrm{Halobacterium\ salinarum}$ to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also positive on the organisms where TE rows are present, with $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$ in $\mathrm{E.\ coli}$, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish, and $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast.  The measured translation-conditioned abundance decomposition, however, is uneven: the measured mediated fraction is $0$ in zebrafish, $0.04091158340950673$ in $\mathrm{E.\ coli}$, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast.  In the joint TE-and-mRNA-stability surface, the joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ for $\mathrm{E.\ coli}$, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, with right-tail permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$ in each listed organism.  A separate yeast dictionary compression test with $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$ reports $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ coverage and $0.7546178856026946$ unexplained signal, above a selection-aware null $95\%$ level of $0.029149007242956115$.  These surrounding facts make localization a plausible residual readout to test, but they do not turn the localization projection into a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The chapter-level readback is finite and case-sensitive.  In human and yeast, localization is a positive non-translation $H$-candidate for the modeled-$T$ complement because $A_L>N_{0.95}$ on the same-rank null surface.  In zebrafish, localization remains unaccepted on this test because $0.009508864544725801<0.011321328712802819$ and because the localization-available join shifts the modeled-complement diagnostic by more than $0.02$.  The localization labels are coarse, reviewed UniProt CC annotations and may be multi-label; their columns can absorb variance mechanically, which is why the null-matched comparison is part of the statement.  A separate positional structural-order screen classifies the available signal as \emph{ramp positive but not localized}: the primary ramp window has weighted $f3$ ramp held-out increment $0.0011165640103326878$, the body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability is $0.05859375$, so the structural localization gate does not pass.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This record licenses only the following bounded claim: reviewed subcellular-localization labels, after the stated controls and same-rank null, carry excess variance from the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual in human and yeast, but not in the zebrafish join.  It does not by itself establish translation realization, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, a causal mechanism, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or a universal biological rule.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic; direct $q_i\to P_k$ association, modeled tAI complement, and localization absorption are not substitutes for that contact.
