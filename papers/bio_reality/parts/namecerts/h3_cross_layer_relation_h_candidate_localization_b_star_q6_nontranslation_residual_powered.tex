\subsection{Localization of the non-translation abundance remainder}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-remainder}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a coarse protein-location annotation carries any of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance remainder that is left after the modeled translation readout has been projected out. The coordinate side is the same nine-dimensional synonymous residual family
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$. Each row is residualized against an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates. The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-location design with columns for membrane, cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, mitochondrion, nucleus, peroxisome, secreted, vacuole/lysosome, and residual location classes. The tested quantity is not a direct translation score. It is the projection of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ onto the residualized localization columns.

\paragraph{Joined rows and category mass.}
The finite join is present for three organisms. For \emph{Danio rerio}, the abundance-CDS contact reports $14410$ CDS rows, while the localization-restricted join uses $1984$ rows and $2821$ localization records; the residual control matrix has rank $23$ with $24$ control columns. Its parsed localization counts include $883$ cytoplasm records, $845$ nucleus records, $708$ membrane records, $247$ mitochondrion records, and $207$ endoplasmic-reticulum records. For human, the corresponding join uses $16008$ rows out of $19053$ reported CDS rows and $16406$ localization records, again with rank $23$ for the controls; the largest parsed categories are membrane $7119$, cytoplasm $6305$, nucleus $5837$, cell membrane $3570$, secreted $1876$, and cytoskeleton $1529$. For yeast, the localization-restricted join uses $4656$ rows out of $5815$ reported CDS rows and $4831$ localization records; the largest categories are membrane $1863$, cytoplasm $1736$, nucleus $1733$, mitochondrion $811$, and endoplasmic reticulum $426$. These row counts make the localization calculation a finite matched-row statistic rather than a name-only contact.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
Let $D_{L}$ denote the fraction of the modeled-complement abundance component absorbed by the residualized $12$-column localization design, and let $D_{0.95}$ denote the $95$th percentile of a degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials. The computed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_L(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.009508864544725801, & D_{0.95}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
D_L(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.009254913329269913, & D_{0.95}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
D_L(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.011225105608516642, & D_{0.95}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the localization readout exceeds its matched null in human and yeast, but not in the danio localization-restricted join. The null means are $0.0062549946178242525$ for danio, $0.0008032852113983743$ for human, and $0.002689902676583632$ for yeast. The danio failure is not hidden: the localization-restricted modeled-complement fraction is $0.7608723943210729$, whereas the full abundance-CDS complement reference is $0.874628721049266$, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$. Human and yeast preserve the complement fraction within the declared $0.02$ tolerance: human has $0.9020707321191324$ versus $0.9208949355679455$, and yeast has $0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation and protein-abundance rows.}
The localization result is downstream of a broader rank-powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface. Direct protein-abundance survival is positive across the larger PAXdb panel: examples include $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.10217906758013379$ in \emph{C. elegans}, $0.08141786718177903$ in \emph{Dictyostelium}, $0.061906245886660105$ in \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, and $0.04454565118019448$ in human, with all nine coordinates surviving in each listed organism. Measured ribosome-TE survival is also finite-row positive in the four measured-TE organisms, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.04034217614654355$ in danio, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}. The measured abundance mediation calculation, however, is uneven: the measured mediated fractions are $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.0$ in danio after clipping the raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$ to the unit interval. The localization statistic therefore addresses the remainder left after modeled translation, not a completed causal translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Residual dictionary context.}
Other finite readouts show that the same abundance remainder is only partially accounted for by measured low-dimensional candidates. In the joint TE-and-stability decomposition, yeast has $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$ and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, giving a joint mediation fraction of $0.5195182225621592$ with TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$ and stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$. Human has $R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215$ and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993$, giving joint fraction $0.16890712099923358$; \emph{E. coli} has $R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932$ and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036$, giving joint fraction $0.2404669586626661$. The yeast readout-dictionary certificate reports $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, selection-aware coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ on $n=1800$ joined rows. For the $f3\_stress$ component, the dwell-mediated abundance statistic has held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$, with the positional split naming the $5'$ window as the dominant segment. Human turnover, by contrast, does not clear its null: the turnover absorption is $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ against null $95$th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$. These neighboring rows make the localization test interpretable as one concrete H-candidate projection among several finite residual readouts.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The localization packet is ready as a finite, organism-matched descriptive result. Human and yeast show localization absorption above the matched null for the modeled-translation-complement abundance component, while danio does not. The conclusion is therefore narrow: coarse reviewed subcellular localization carries measurable residual abundance information in two of the three tested localization joins, but it is not a translation readout, not a folding or physical-admissibility assay, not a molecular-function assay, not a perturbation mechanism, and not a universal biological law. A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic showing that the relevant $q_i\to T_j$ translation step survives and that conditioning on that translation readout reduces the downstream $q_i\to P_k$ signal in the same matched rows.
