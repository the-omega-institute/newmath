\subsection{Localization as a Residual Carrier for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Protein-Abundance Complement}\label{sec:bq6-localization-nontranslation-residual}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite matrix surface.} The tested object is a finite row panel whose columns contain nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, a sibling control design $Z$, a modeled-translation complement inside protein abundance, and a twelve-column multi-label localization readout.  The residual coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control space has 24 named columns and numerical rank 23: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons lying in the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates.  The localization readout is not a scalar phenotype.  It is a rank-12 multi-label one-hot surface parsed from reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-location annotations.

\paragraph{Rows and modules.} Three organisms enter the localization calculation.  The joined panels contain 1984 Danio rerio CDS rows, 16008 Homo sapiens CDS rows, and 4656 Saccharomyces cerevisiae CDS rows.  The corresponding localization-record counts are 2821, 16406, and 4831.  In each organism the localization module has $K=12$ columns and rank $12$.  The parsed label masses are visibly nonuniform: for human the largest columns are Membrane $7119$, Cytoplasm $6305$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Golgi $1096$, Vacuole/Lysosome $482$, Other $161$, and Peroxisome $109$.  Yeast has Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, Membrane $1863$, Mitochondrion $811$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, Vacuole/Lysosome $217$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Secreted $91$, Peroxisome $77$, and Other $44$.  Danio has Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Cell membrane $230$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Golgi $125$, Secreted $79$, Vacuole/Lysosome $45$, Other $23$, and Peroxisome $14$.

\paragraph{Residual target.} The target projected onto localization is the modeled-translation complement of the protein-abundance component carried by the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates.  On the full abundance-CDS joins, direct protein-omics survival exists as an association surface but is not promoted to a process claim: the $S^{QP}$ calculation used 15 organism panels, all with rank $9$ residualized $Q$ design, rank $23$ control design, and all nine coordinates surviving at the positive threshold.  The direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in E. coli, $0.10217906758013379$ in C. elegans, $0.08141786718177903$ in Dictyostelium, $0.0770952887136557$ in Drosophila, $0.0646471273126996$ in Pseudomonas, $0.06397514251295629$ in Sulfolobus, $0.061906245886660105$ in Bacillus, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, $0.04613529807085213$ in Danio, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.03980793596647631$ in chicken, $0.03209009170649934$ in Mycobacterium, $0.02763998603083845$ in rat, and $0.019256877907572044$ in Halobacterium.  These numbers justify looking for a residual carrier, but by themselves they remain protein-abundance correlations.

\paragraph{Localization absorption test.} The localization computation asks how much of the modeled-translation complement is carried by the residualized localization columns after the same $Z$ controls.  The absorption fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{Danio}} &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because a twelve-column readout can absorb variance mechanically, the calculation includes a degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null with 200 trials per organism.  The corresponding null means and 95th percentiles are
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{Danio}} &= (0.0062549946178242525,\ 0.011321328712802819),\\
N_{\mathrm{human}} &= (0.0008032852113983743,\ 0.0013758521256775384),\\
N_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= (0.002689902676583632,\ 0.004958116973149795).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the localization carrier is positive above the null boundary in human and yeast, while Danio is below the degree-of-freedom matched 95th percentile.  The organism-level module classification is therefore: human positive localization carrier, yeast positive localization carrier, Danio computed but not above-null on the available localization join.  The Danio caveat is material because the localization join has 1984 rows while the abundance reference join has 14410 rows, and its modeled-complement distance changes from $0.874628721049266$ on the full abundance-CDS reference to $0.7608723943210729$ on the localization join, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$.  Human and yeast are stable under this comparison: human changes from $0.9208949355679455$ to $0.9020707321191324$, difference $0.018824203448813193$; yeast changes from $0.21173219572706703$ to $0.1997853864617063$, difference $0.011946809265360736$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation mediation.} The localization result is deliberately placed after, not before, translation-conditioned checks.  In the measured TE mediation panel, the three-way joins contain 13140 Danio rows, 3415 E. coli rows, 11554 human rows, and 5128 yeast rows.  The measured $S^{QT}$ values are $0.04034217614654355$, $0.009394266754822262$, $0.018837911344127926$, and $0.13681931343730436$, respectively.  The measured $S^{QP}$ values on the same mediation calculation are $0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$.  The measured mediation fractions are $0$ for Danio after truncating a negative raw fraction, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  This is important for interpretation: localization is being tested against the residual part left after a modeled translation readout, not as proof that the codon coordinate causes compartmental trafficking.

\paragraph{Compression comparison.} The yeast dictionary certificate gives a second scale for the residual.  On an available join of 4758 rows with $n=1800$ used, five folds, $\lambda_{\mathrm{dl}}=0.01$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, the best selected readout family covers $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ of the source signal, leaving $0.7546178856026946$ unexplained.  Its raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.2745002077400602$, above the selection-aware null 95th percentile $0.029149007242956115$ and the naive fixed-subset null 95th percentile $0.00598374993486675$.  This scale is consistent with the localization result: a positive localization absorption in yeast is a small but non-null carrier of the residual, not a collapse of the whole residual into a single biological annotation.

\paragraph{Verdict.} The chapter records a ready finite-row result for the localization carrier, with the following bounded conclusion.  After residualizing against CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density, and after projecting the modeled-translation complement of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance component onto twelve residualized localization labels, human and yeast exceed a degree-of-freedom matched null threshold, while Danio does not on the available localization join.  This supports localization as an $H$-candidate carrier for part of the non-translation residual in human and yeast.  It does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, compartment-routing mechanism, organismal phenotype, or a universal rule.  Any such stronger conclusion would require its own layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.
