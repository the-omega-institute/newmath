\subsection{Localization as a residual H-candidate for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-localization-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Finite question.}
The experiment asks whether coarse subcellular localization annotations carry any finite-row part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after a modeled translation readout has already been accounted for. The tested object is not the raw codon table and not a claim about trafficking. It is the modeled-tAI complement of the $Q \to P$ protein-abundance projection, written here as $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, after residualizing the coding-sequence rows against an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates. The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{\mathrm{stress}}$.

\paragraph{Joined localization rows.}
The localization readout is a multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-localization table collapsed to $K=12$ category columns. The three joined organisms have the following finite row counts and control ranks:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad n_{\mathrm{joined}}=1984,\quad n_{\mathrm{localization}}=2821,\quad n_{\mathrm{CDS,reported}}=14410,\quad \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad n_{\mathrm{joined}}=16008,\quad n_{\mathrm{localization}}=16406,\quad n_{\mathrm{CDS,reported}}=19053,\quad \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23,\\
\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:&\quad n_{\mathrm{joined}}=4656,\quad n_{\mathrm{localization}}=4831,\quad n_{\mathrm{CDS,reported}}=5815,\quad \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23.
\end{aligned}
$$
The category spectrum is broad enough to make a twelve-column projection meaningful rather than a single-label surrogate. In human, for example, the parsed counts are Cytoplasm $6305$, Membrane $7119$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Golgi $1096$, Vacuole/Lysosome $482$, Peroxisome $109$, and Other $161$. Yeast has Cytoplasm $1736$, Membrane $1863$, Nucleus $1733$, Mitochondrion $811$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, Vacuole/Lysosome $217$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Secreted $91$, Peroxisome $77$, and Other $44$. Zebrafish has Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Cell membrane $230$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Golgi $125$, Secreted $79$, Vacuole/Lysosome $45$, Other $23$, and Peroxisome $14$.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
For each organism, the finite matrix first forms the $Z$-residualized modeled complement $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ on the active localization join. It then projects that complement onto the $Z$-residualized localization one-hot span $L$ and compares the absorbed fraction to a degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials. The computed absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_L(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio})&=0.009508864544725801,\quad &&\mathrm{Null}_{95}=0.011321328712802819,\\
D_L(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens})&=0.009254913329269913,\quad &&\mathrm{Null}_{95}=0.0013758521256775384,\\
D_L(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae})&=0.011225105608516642,\quad &&\mathrm{Null}_{95}=0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding null means are $0.0062549946178242525$ for zebrafish, $0.0008032852113983743$ for human, and $0.002689902676583632$ for yeast. Thus human and yeast exceed their degree-matched null thresholds, while zebrafish does not. The module-level classification is therefore mixed: localization is a positive H-candidate on the human and yeast joins, but not on the zebrafish join under the same finite rule.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement consistency check.}
The localization join changes the available protein set, so the complement magnitude was remeasured rather than assumed from the full abundance matrix. The current localization-join complement fractions are $0.7608723943210729$ for zebrafish, $0.9020707321191324$ for human, and $0.1997853864617063$ for yeast. Against the full abundance-join references $0.874628721049266$, $0.9208949355679455$, and $0.21173219572706703$, the absolute differences are $0.11375632672819314$, $0.018824203448813193$, and $0.011946809265360736$, respectively. Human and yeast are within the stated $0.02$ tolerance; zebrafish is not, which means the zebrafish localization result must be read as a restricted-join diagnostic rather than as a stable estimate of the full abundance complement.

\paragraph{Relation to direct protein abundance and measured translation.}
The localization statistic is not being used to create the original $Q \to P$ signal. Direct protein-omics survival was already positive across a larger matched PAXdb panel after the same family of controls: $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, $0.02763998603083845$ in rat, $0.04613529807085213$ in zebrafish, $0.03980793596647631$ in chicken, $0.10217906758013379$ in \emph{C. elegans}, $0.0770952887136557$ in \emph{Drosophila}, $0.08141786718177903$ in \emph{Dictyostelium}, $0.0646471273126996$ in \emph{Pseudomonas}, $0.061906245886660105$ in \emph{Bacillus}, $0.03209009170649934$ in \emph{Mycobacterium}, $0.019256877907572044$ in \emph{Halobacterium}, and $0.06397514251295629$ in \emph{Sulfolobus}. These are direct abundance associations, not mechanisms.

Measured ribosome-TE survival supplies a separate translation-layer check on four organisms: $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish. The measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition gives mediated fractions $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.0$ for zebrafish after clipping a negative raw value. This places the localization result in the residual-H part of the map: it is evaluated after modeled translation has left a complement, and it is not a substitute for measured TE mediation.

\paragraph{Other residual partitions.}
The same residual field has additional finite-row reductions, which help bound the interpretation. In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition, the joint mediation fractions are $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, and $0.16890712099923358$ for human, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. The single-mediator splits are yeast TE-only $0.4697188319693239$ and stability-only $0.0626618595344251$, \emph{E. coli} TE-only $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only $0.23052056324898387$, and human TE-only $0.00549490864190343$ and stability-only $0.15314679870183337$. A yeast dictionary reduction with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$, and $120$ permutations gives selection-aware coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ against a selection-aware null $95$th percentile of $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. Separately, the yeast $f3_{\mathrm{stress}}$ dwell test gives held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$; its positional version identifies the $5'$ segment as dominant, with the $5'$ window passing and the $3'$ window not passing. A structural-order ramp check is classified as \emph{ramp-positive but not localized}: at primary ramp length $50$, the weighted ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, the weighted body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value is $0.05859375$.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The localization finite-row record is ready as a bounded H-candidate statement. Human and yeast show localization absorption above the degree-matched null on the modeled-translation complement, while zebrafish does not and also fails the complement-matching tolerance because the localization join is much smaller than the full abundance join. The admissible conclusion is therefore narrow: coarse multi-label localization carries a measurable part of the non-modeled translation complement in two of the three tested organisms, with one organism reported as a null or join-sensitive case.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation process, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this localization projection. The localization columns are coarse annotations; their absorption of residual abundance variance is descriptive. A stronger claim would require its own matched measurement at the relevant layer and a statistic showing that the relevant $q_i\to T_j$ step survives and that conditioning on that measured readout changes the downstream association.
