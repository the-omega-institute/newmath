\subsection{Localization as a Candidate Readout for the Non-Translation Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Question.}
The localization experiment asks whether the abundance component left after the modeled translation readout still has a detectable, layer-matched correlate in coarse protein localization annotations.  The row object is a finite CDS-indexed surface built from nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, the sibling control matrix $Z$, a modeled-tAI complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, and a twelve-column multi-label localization matrix $L$.  The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control side contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline, giving $24$ control columns and rank $23$ for every reported organism.

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The localization join is organism-specific and is not treated as a universal matrix.  For Danio rerio, $1984$ CDS rows join to $2821$ localization records, from a reported codon-protein abundance join of $14410$ CDS rows.  For Homo sapiens, $16008$ rows join to $16406$ localization records, from $19053$ reported CDS rows.  For Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $4656$ rows join to $4831$ localization records, from $5815$ reported CDS rows.  In each case $K=12$ localization columns are retained, so the readout being tested is not a single compartment label but the rank-$12$ span of reviewed UniProtKB subcellular annotations.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
The measured quantity is the fraction of the modeled-translation-complement abundance component absorbed by the residualized localization span.  With $R_Z$ denoting residualization against the control matrix and $\Pi_{R_Z L}$ denoting projection onto the residualized localization columns, the finite statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L &= \frac{\|\Pi_{R_Z L} R_Z\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}{\|R_Z\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The computed values are $A_L=0.009508864544725801$ for Danio rerio, $A_L=0.009254913329269913$ for Homo sapiens, and $A_L=0.011225105608516642$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null uses $200$ trials per organism.  Its $95\%$ cutoffs are $0.011321328712802819$ for Danio rerio, $0.0013758521256775384$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.004958116973149795$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with null means $0.0062549946178242525$, $0.0008032852113983743$, and $0.002689902676583632$, respectively.

\paragraph{Readback.}
On this finite surface, Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae exceed their degree-of-freedom matched null thresholds, while Danio rerio does not.  Thus the localization span carries excess signal for the modeled-translation-complement abundance component in the human and yeast joins, but the zebrafish localization join is reported as below its own null boundary.  The complement diagnostic agrees with the full abundance-CDS reference within $0.02$ in Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae: the human value changes from $0.9208949355679455$ on the full complement reference to $0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$; yeast changes from $0.21173219572706703$ to $0.1997853864617063$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$.  Danio rerio changes from $0.874628721049266$ to $0.7608723943210729$, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$, so its localization availability changes the row geometry enough that the null result should be read locally rather than pooled with the other two organisms.

\paragraph{Localization categories.}
The twelve localization columns are biologically coarse and multi-label.  In Homo sapiens the largest parsed categories are Membrane $7119$, Cytoplasm $6305$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Golgi $1096$, Vacuole/Lysosome $482$, Peroxisome $109$, and Other $161$.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae the largest categories are Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, Mitochondrion $811$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, Vacuole/Lysosome $217$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Secreted $91$, Peroxisome $77$, and Other $44$.  In Danio rerio the largest categories are Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Cell membrane $230$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Golgi $125$, Secreted $79$, Vacuole/Lysosome $45$, Other $23$, and Peroxisome $14$.  These counts identify the measured annotation span; they do not isolate a compartment-specific mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to the cross-layer residual chain.}
The localization result is an $H$-candidate statistic, not the primary translation mediation statistic.  The broader finite-row chain shows why this distinction matters.  Direct $Q\to P$ protein-omics survival is positive in the powered PAXdb surface across the reported organisms, with $R^2_{QP}$ ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in Halobacterium salinarum to $0.29837624994140827$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and with all nine residual coordinates surviving in each listed organism.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also nonzero on the four measured-TE joins: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.04034217614654355$ for Danio rerio, $0.009394266754822262$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.018837911344127926$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.13681931343730436$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  However, measured TE absorbs only a fraction of the direct abundance signal: the measured mediation fractions are $0$ for Danio rerio after clipping a suppressive raw value, $0.04091158340950673$ for Escherichia coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.4793069656345167$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The localization readout therefore belongs after the modeled-translation complement has been formed; it is evidence about where residual abundance signal may reside, not evidence that localization caused the codon residual.

\paragraph{Auxiliary finite checks.}
Other measured readout dictionaries support the same conservative interpretation.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability partition, the joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ for Escherichia coli, $0.16890712099923358$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.5195182225621592$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in all three organisms.  Yeast also has a partial readout dictionary certificate with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, selection-aware null $95\%$ cutoff $0.029149007242956115$, and retained coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  For the $f3\_stress$ dwell readout in yeast, $n_{join}=1257$, base $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, permutation null $95\%$ cutoff $0.0013115362189692937$, and approximate $f3$--dwell correlation $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  These numbers show that the residual can be partly associated with measured post-translation or translation-adjacent readouts, while still leaving a substantial unexplained component.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The chapter-level verdict is that localization is a positive $H$-candidate in the human and yeast localization joins and a nonpositive local readback in the zebrafish join under its own null threshold.  This is a projection result over finite rows with explicit rank and null controls.  It does not license claims about translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, organismal phenotype, or universality.  Such claims would require a separate layer-specific measurement contact and a statistic showing the relevant same-row survival or attenuation step.
