\subsection{Localization as a finite candidate for the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-h-candidate-localization}

\paragraph{Finite question.}
The record tested here is not a claim that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates cause localization, translation, folding, protein function, or an organism-level phenotype.  It asks a narrower finite-row question.  After codon residual coordinates $Q$, modeled translation readout $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$, and protein abundance $P$ are residualized against the same covariate surface, does a coarse localization readout absorb a non-negligible part of the abundance component that remains outside the modeled-translation direction?  The coordinate block has nine entries,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f_{3}\text{-stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each localization join the control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Rows and localization modules.}
The localization contact is the reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-location annotation, parsed as a twelve-column multi-label readout.  The three organisms with joined rows are zebrafish, human, and budding yeast.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the computation joins $1{,}984$ CDS rows from $14{,}410$ reported CDS-abundance rows to $2{,}821$ localization records, with $K=12$ localization columns.  The category counts are Cell membrane $230$, Cytoplasm $883$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Golgi $125$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Nucleus $845$, Other $23$, Peroxisome $14$, Secreted $79$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $45$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the join has $16{,}008$ rows from $19{,}053$ reported CDS-abundance rows and $16{,}406$ localization records; the corresponding counts are Cell membrane $3{,}570$, Cytoplasm $6{,}305$, Cytoskeleton $1{,}529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1{,}449$, Golgi $1{,}096$, Membrane $7{,}119$, Mitochondrion $1{,}370$, Nucleus $5{,}837$, Other $161$, Peroxisome $109$, Secreted $1{,}876$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $482$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the join has $4{,}656$ rows from $5{,}815$ reported CDS-abundance rows and $4{,}831$ localization records; the category counts are Cell membrane $274$, Cytoplasm $1{,}736$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Golgi $224$, Membrane $1{,}863$, Mitochondrion $811$, Nucleus $1{,}733$, Other $44$, Peroxisome $77$, Secreted $91$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $217$.

\paragraph{Residual target.}
The abundance target is the modeled-tAI-complement component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ on the current localization join.  The diagnostic comparison with the full abundance complement gives $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.7608723943$ in zebrafish versus the full-join reference $0.8746287210$, an absolute difference $0.1137563267$.  The same diagnostic is much closer in human, where $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321$ on the localization join and $0.9208949356$ on the full abundance join, with difference $0.0188242034$.  Yeast gives $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.1997853865$ on the localization join and $0.2117321957$ on the full abundance join, with difference $0.0119468093$.  Thus the zebrafish localization-available subset changes the complement appreciably, while the human and yeast localization joins preserve the full-join complement to within $0.02$.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
The tested statistic is the fraction of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ absorbed by the residualized localization column space, compared with a $K$-column degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian baseline.  The observed absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.0095088645,\\
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0092549133,\\
A_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.0112251056.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding null means and $95$th percentiles, computed over $200$ trials, are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{0},q_{0.95}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.0062549946,\ 0.0113213287,\\
\mu_{0},q_{0.95}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0008032852,\ 0.0013758521,\\
\mu_{0},q_{0.95}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.0026899027,\ 0.0049581170.
\end{aligned}
$$
Human and yeast exceed their degree-of-freedom matched $95$th-percentile baselines; zebrafish does not.  The finite interpretation is therefore organism-resolved: human and yeast localization annotations carry measurable excess information about the modeled-translation-complement abundance component on these joined rows, while the zebrafish subset is reported as a non-excess case under the stated null.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance readouts.}
The localization result is downstream of several finite surfaces, but it does not replace them.  Direct protein-omics survival against PAXdb abundance is positive across a broad organism panel, with $R^2_{QP}$ values including $0.1076752668$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.2983762499$ in yeast, $0.0445456512$ in human, $0.0498279841$ in mouse, $0.0461352981$ in zebrafish, and $0.0639751425$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}; in each reported organism all nine coordinates survive the positive-threshold screen.  Measured ribosome-TE survival gives $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.0093942668$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.1368193134$ in yeast, $0.0188379113$ in human, and $0.0403421761$ in zebrafish, again with nine surviving coordinates in each measured-TE organism.  The measured translation-conditioned abundance decomposition gives mediated fractions $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.0409115834$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.4793069656$ for yeast, $0.0027966485$ for human, and $0$ for zebrafish after clamping the suppressive raw value $-0.0480148605$.  These numbers show why localization is treated only as an $H$-candidate for residual signal: the localization column space is tested after a modeled translation complement has been formed, not as evidence that translation itself has been mechanistically identified.

\paragraph{Reduction context.}
The larger readout dictionary gives a useful scale for the residual.  In yeast, the joint TE-plus-mRNA-stability partition has $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989546$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.2545361225$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.1304758265$, with joint mediation fraction $0.5195182226$, TE-only fraction $0.4697188320$, stability-only fraction $0.0626618595$, and right-tail permutation value $0.0009990010$.  The selected-readout certificate on the same yeast context uses $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1{,}800$, five folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, and $120$ permutations; its selection-aware coverage is $C^{*}=0.2453821144$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856$ after the selected family.  The $f_{3}$ dwell test also gives a positive held-out increment, with base $R^2=0.1855578758$, full $R^2=0.2041226152$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0185647394$, null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362$, and approximate $p=7.8739160572\cdot 10^{-9}$.  A separate ramp/body structural-order screen classifies the primary result as ramp-positive but not localized: at ramp length $50$, weighted ramp $\Delta R^2=0.0011165640$, weighted body $\Delta R^2=-0.0002615731$, paired ramp-minus-body $\Delta R^2=0.0013781371$, ramp sign-flip $p=0.0478515625$, and paired ramp-minus-body $p=0.05859375$.  These companion finite-row results support a conservative reading: several measured readout families reduce or localize pieces of the residual, but none is promoted to a global mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The chapter claim is only that the localization projection was computed and that human and yeast show absorption above the matched null while zebrafish does not.  Coarse multi-label localization annotations are not perturbations, trafficking mechanisms, folding measurements, physical-admissibility assays, molecular-function assays, phenotype assays, or universal biological laws.  A direct $Q\to P$ association, a positive $Q\to T$ statistic, or an excess localization absorption score does not by itself authorize a translation mechanism, folded-structure conclusion, physical-admissibility conclusion, biological-function conclusion, causal mechanism, or universality statement.  Such statements would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  The admissible conclusion is therefore a bounded one: localization is a supported residual carrier in human and yeast for the modeled-translation-complement abundance component, remains null in the zebrafish localization subset under the declared baseline, and leaves the non-translation residual open beyond this particular categorical readout.
