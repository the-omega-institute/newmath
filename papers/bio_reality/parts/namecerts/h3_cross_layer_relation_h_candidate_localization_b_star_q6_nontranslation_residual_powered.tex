\subsection{Localization Absorption of the Translation-Complement Residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite test asks whether the residual part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal that is not absorbed by a modeled translation readout has any measurable alignment with coarse protein localization annotations.  The tested object is not a new localization mechanism.  It is a finite projection problem: start from the nine residual synonymous coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$; residualize them and protein abundance against the same covariate surface; remove the modeled translation component; then ask how much of the remaining abundance projection is absorbed by a residualized multi-label localization matrix.

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
For each joined coding-sequence row, the design contains the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates, a control matrix $Z$, and a localization matrix $L$.  The control matrix has $24$ columns before rank reduction: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism reported here, the residualized control rank is $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.

The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label one-hot surface from reviewed UniProtKB subcellular localization records.  The twelve columns are Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome.  The finite joins are: Danio rerio has $n=1984$ joined rows from $2821$ localization records, against $14410$ reported CDS-abundance rows; Homo sapiens has $n=16008$ joined rows from $16406$ localization records, against $19053$ reported CDS-abundance rows; Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $n=4656$ joined rows from $4831$ localization records, against $5815$ reported CDS-abundance rows.  The localization matrix has residual rank $12$ in all three organisms.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P$ denote log protein abundance after the $Z$ controls, and let $T_{mod}$ denote the modeled translation readout used to remove the translation-aligned part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance projection.  The tested residual is the modeled-complement target
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}
  &= \Pi_{Q\mid Z}P - \Pi_{T_{mod}\mid Z}\Pi_{Q\mid Z}P,
\end{aligned}
$$
read only as a descriptive projection.  The reported absorption number is the fraction of this target captured by the residualized localization columns:
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{loc}
  &= \frac{\|\Pi_{L\mid Z}P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^{2}}{\|P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}\|^{2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because a $12$-column readout can absorb variance mechanically, the comparison baseline is a degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null with $200$ deterministic trials per organism.  A localization carrying excess residual signal is reported only when the observed $d_{loc}$ exceeds the organism-specific null $95$th percentile.

\paragraph{Observed localization counts.}
The finite category counts are not uniform, so the interpretation is explicitly row-local and organism-local.  In Danio rerio, the localization records contain Cell membrane $230$, Cytoplasm $883$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Golgi $125$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Nucleus $845$, Other $23$, Peroxisome $14$, Secreted $79$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $45$.  In Homo sapiens, the corresponding counts are $3570$, $6305$, $1529$, $1449$, $1096$, $7119$, $1370$, $5837$, $161$, $109$, $1876$, and $482$.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, they are $274$, $1736$, $165$, $426$, $224$, $1863$, $811$, $1733$, $44$, $77$, $91$, and $217$.

\paragraph{Result.}
The localization absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{loc}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
 d_{loc}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
 d_{loc}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
The degree-of-freedom-matched null $95$th percentiles are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{0.95}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
 n_{0.95}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
 n_{0.95}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the localization readout exceeds the matched null in human and yeast, but not in zebrafish.  The finite-row readback is therefore a two-organism positive localization-absorption signal for the modeled translation-complement abundance residual, with a zebrafish non-excess outcome under the same rule.

\paragraph{Compatibility with the modeled-complement target.}
The same computation records how closely the localization join preserves the modeled-complement quantity from the full abundance-CDS join.  The current localization-join complement values are $0.7608723943210729$ for Danio rerio, $0.9020707321191324$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.1997853864617063$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The corresponding full abundance-CDS reference values are $0.874628721049266$, $0.9208949355679455$, and $0.21173219572706703$.  Human and yeast match within the declared $0.02$ tolerance, with absolute differences $0.018824203448813193$ and $0.011946809265360736$.  Zebrafish differs by $0.11375632672819314$, so its null result is reported as a localization-availability-sensitive join outcome rather than as evidence against a general localization residual.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance contacts.}
The localization calculation is downstream of a broader finite surface in which direct protein-omics survival is positive across the rank-powered abundance panel.  For example, the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance residual $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, and $0.04613529807085213$ in zebrafish, with all nine coordinates surviving the direct abundance screen in those organisms.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also finite on the same coordinate family, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$ in human, $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, and $0.04034217614654355$ in zebrafish.  However, the measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance mediation fractions are sharply different: $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, and $0$ in zebrafish after truncating the suppressive raw fraction $-0.04801486048721136$ to the unit interval.  The localization test therefore sits in the residual part of the cross-layer analysis: it does not replace the measured $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ mediation check.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The finite readback has three cases.  Yeast is the cleanest positive case: $d_{loc}=0.011225105608516642$ is above the null $95$th percentile $0.004958116973149795$, and the localization join preserves the modeled-complement target within $0.011946809265360736$.  Human is also positive: $d_{loc}=0.009254913329269913$ is far above $0.0013758521256775384$, and the complement target matches within $0.018824203448813193$.  Zebrafish is not positive under this finite rule: $d_{loc}=0.009508864544725801$ is below $0.011321328712802819$, and the complement target changes substantially under the localization join.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter only establishes a finite statistical absorption statement for coarse multi-label localization annotations.  It does not say that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates cause localization, encode compartment identity, determine trafficking, or create a molecular mechanism.  It also does not infer translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or a universal biological law from the localization projection.  Those conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.  In particular, a direct $q_i\to P_k$ association and a localization absorption excess do not by themselves constitute a translation-mediated mechanism; the measured $q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ step remains the admissible route for any translation-conditioned protein-abundance claim.
