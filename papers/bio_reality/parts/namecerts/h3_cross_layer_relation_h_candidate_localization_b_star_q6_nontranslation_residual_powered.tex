\subsection{Localization absorption of the non-translation residual}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record tests subcellular localization as a finite readout for the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal that is not accounted for by the modeled translation surface.  The row surface has nine synonymous residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Each coordinate is residualized against the same $Z$ family: intercept, $\log({\rm cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline formed from the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each reported localization organism.  The localization readout is a $K=12$ multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-category matrix, residualized before projection.

\paragraph{Joined panels.}
The finite panels are not abstract examples.  They are three organism-specific joins between CDS rows, protein abundance, modeled translation complement, and localization annotations.  Danio rerio has $n=1984$ joined localization rows from $2821$ localization records, with $14410$ CDS rows reported in the abundance join.  Homo sapiens has $n=16008$ joined rows from $16406$ localization records, with $19053$ CDS rows reported.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $n=4656$ joined rows from $4831$ localization records, with $5815$ CDS rows reported.  The localization category counts are broad rather than mechanistic: in human, for example, Cytoplasm has $6305$ annotations, Membrane $7119$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, and the remaining categories fill a twelve-column categorical surface.  Yeast similarly has Cytoplasm $1736$, Membrane $1863$, Nucleus $1733$, and Mitochondrion $811$ annotations.  These counts define a coarse annotation surface, not a compartmental transport model.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement residual being tested.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the $Z$-residualized protein-abundance component explained by the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ design, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ denote the part left after removing the modeled translation readout.  The localization experiment reports the modeled complement fraction $d_{mod}$ on the active localization join, then compares it with the full abundance-CDS complement experiment.  The values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{mod}^{Danio} &= 0.7608723943, & d_{ref}^{Danio} &= 0.8746287210, & |\Delta| &= 0.1137563267,\\
 d_{mod}^{Human} &= 0.9020707321, & d_{ref}^{Human} &= 0.9208949356, & |\Delta| &= 0.0188242034,\\
 d_{mod}^{Yeast} &= 0.1997853865, & d_{ref}^{Yeast} &= 0.2117321957, & |\Delta| &= 0.0119468093.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the human and yeast localization joins preserve the modeled-complement residual within the stated $0.02$ tolerance, while the Danio join does not.  The Danio discrepancy is not hidden: it means localization availability materially changes the protein-ID slice being tested, so the Danio absorption value is descriptive but not a stable localization witness for the same residual surface.

\paragraph{Localization absorption statistic.}
The tested quantity is the fraction of $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ absorbed by the residualized twelve-column localization matrix $L$.  A $K$-column degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null is computed per organism with $200$ trials, so a positive reading requires more than the mechanical absorption expected from adding a twelve-dimensional readout.  The observed and null values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{loc}^{Danio} &= 0.0095088645, & N_{95}^{Danio} &= 0.0113213287, & N_{mean}^{Danio} &= 0.0062549946,\\
 A_{loc}^{Human} &= 0.0092549133, & N_{95}^{Human} &= 0.0013758521, & N_{mean}^{Human} &= 0.0008032852,\\
 A_{loc}^{Yeast} &= 0.0112251056, & N_{95}^{Yeast} &= 0.0049581170, & N_{mean}^{Yeast} &= 0.0026899027.
\end{aligned}
$$
Human and yeast exceed their null $95$th percentiles, so localization carries a measurable part of the non-modeled abundance residual on those two finite row surfaces.  Danio falls below its own null threshold and also fails the complement-matching diagnostic, so it is not counted as positive support.  The finite verdict is therefore two positive localization-absorption panels and one reported null panel.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance rows.}
The same coordinate family has direct protein-abundance survival in a larger PAXdb surface, but that fact alone does not explain the non-translation residual.  Across the direct $Q\to P$ protein-omics panels, all nine coordinates survive in each listed organism, with representative $R^2_{QP}$ values $0.2983762499$ in yeast, $0.1076752668$ in Escherichia coli, $0.1021790676$ in Caenorhabditis elegans, $0.0646471273$ in Pseudomonas aeruginosa, $0.0498279841$ in mouse, $0.0445456512$ in human, and $0.0192568779$ in Halobacterium salinarum.  Those direct associations are bounded protein-abundance correlations after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  They do not by themselves identify translation, localization, turnover, folding, or function.

\paragraph{Measured translation context.}
Measured ribosome-based translation efficiency supplies a separate finite check.  The measured TE join rates are $0.8818572657$ for yeast with $5128$ genes used, $0.9133458144$ for E. coli with $3415$ genes used, $0.6064136881$ for human with $11554$ genes used, and $0.9118667592$ for Danio with $13140$ genes used.  The measured $Q\to T$ readouts are positive but heterogeneous:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 R^2_{QT,meas}(Yeast) &= 0.1368193134,\\
 R^2_{QT,meas}(Danio) &= 0.0403421761,\\
 R^2_{QT,meas}(Human) &= 0.0188379113,\\
 R^2_{QT,meas}(E.coli) &= 0.0093942668.
\end{aligned}
$$
The measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition is similarly uneven.  The measured mediated fractions are $0.4793069656$ in yeast, $0.0409115834$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485$ in human, and $0$ in Danio after suppressive raw mediation is clipped at the reported boundary.  These numbers matter locally because the localization test is aimed at the residual left after modeled translation, not at a claim that translation has been causally established.

\paragraph{Neighboring residual explanations.}
Other finite rows show that the residual is not a single-layer phenomenon.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition, the joint mediation fractions are $0.5195182226$ in yeast, $0.1689071210$ in human, and $0.2404669587$ in E. coli, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In yeast, the single-mediator split is dominated by measured TE at $0.4697188320$ with stability-only $0.0626618595$; in human, stability-only is $0.1531467987$ while TE-only is $0.0054949086$; in E. coli, stability-only is $0.2305205632$ while TE-only is $0.0197552134$.  A separate yeast $f_3$-stress dwell row reports $n_{join}=1257$, base $R^2=0.1855578758$, full $R^2=0.2041226152$, held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0185647394$, $f_3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441451$, approximate $p=7.8739160572\times 10^{-9}$, null $95$th percentile $0.0013115362$, and $120$ deterministic permutations.  The partial mediation certificate on a unified yeast context has $n_{join}=1800$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077$, selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.0291490072$, accepted coverage $C^*=0.2453821144$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856$.  These surrounding rows support a restrained reading: localization can absorb part of the modeled-translation complement in human and yeast, but it is one candidate readout among several finite residual routes.

\paragraph{Structural and positional boundary.}
The structural-order ramp row is also bounded.  It reports conclusion $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$ with weighted $f_3$ ramp held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0011165640$, body held-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0002615731$, ramp-minus-body $0.0013781371$, ramp sign-flip $p=0.0478515625$, and paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip $p=0.05859375$.  This is a positive ramp signal but not a passed localization gate.  It is therefore not evidence that the localization absorption result is a folded-structure or physical-admissibility result.  The localization statistic is a coarse subcellular-annotation projection of an abundance residual.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The ready finite verdict is the following.  On the human and yeast panels, residualized UniProt localization columns absorb more of $P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ than expected from a twelve-column matched null, and the active joins preserve the modeled-complement residual within the $0.02$ diagnostic tolerance.  On the Danio panel, the absorption is below the null $95$th percentile and the modeled-complement diagnostic differs by $0.1137563267$, so it is carried as a reported null.  The chapter-level claim is therefore not that localization explains all non-translation residual power, but that localization is a finite, measured H-candidate for part of that residual in two rank-powered panels.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal pathway, or universal biological law follows from this row.  The localization readout is a coarse, multi-label annotation matrix; the absorption fraction is descriptive; the null comparison only protects against the obvious degrees-of-freedom artifact.  A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact, such as perturbation, rescue, compartment-specific quantitative localization, turnover, folding, physical assay, or functional assay rows, evaluated after the relevant translation and abundance controls on the same finite units.
