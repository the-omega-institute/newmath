\subsection{Localization absorption of the non-translation residual}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether a coarse protein-localization annotation can account for part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation readout has already been removed.  The coordinate side is the same nine-dimensional synonymous residual basis
$\{\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\mathrm{f3\_stress}\}$.
The nuisance block $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline, giving 24 control columns and observed rank $23$ in each localization fit.  The response being projected is not raw protein abundance: it is the modeled-tAI complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ on the rows where CDS, protein abundance, and localization annotations all join.

\paragraph{Localization rows and categories.}
The localization readout is a 12-column multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-location encoding, residualized against the same control block.  The three finite joins have very different sizes.  Danio rerio contributes $n=1984$ joined CDS rows from $2821$ localization records, Homo sapiens contributes $n=16008$ joined rows from $16406$ localization records, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae contributes $n=4656$ joined rows from $4831$ localization records.  In all three cases the localization matrix has $K=12$ columns and rank $12$ after the stated parsing.  The largest human categories are Membrane $7119$, Cytoplasm $6305$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, and Mitochondrion $1370$.  The yeast categories are also broad rather than mechanistic: Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, Mitochondrion $811$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $217$.  These counts identify the finite readout surface; they do not identify trafficking mechanisms.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
Let $L$ denote the residualized localization design and let $H=\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the non-translation residual component on the active join.  The reported statistic is the fraction of $\|H\|^2$ absorbed by the column span of $L$, compared with a $K$-column degrees-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null.  Numerically,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Abs}_{\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.009508864544725801, & \mathrm{Null95}_{\mathrm{danio}} &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
\mathrm{Abs}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.009254913329269913, & \mathrm{Null95}_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mathrm{Abs}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.011225105608516642, & \mathrm{Null95}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each null was computed with 200 trials.  Thus human and yeast show localization absorption above the matched null boundary, while Danio rerio does not exceed the null boundary on the available localization join.

\paragraph{Join-sensitive complement check.}
The same computation records whether the modeled complement on the localization join matches the reference full abundance-CDS join.  Human is stable within the stated $0.02$ tolerance: $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join versus $0.9208949355679455$ on the full complement reference, absolute difference $0.018824203448813193$.  Yeast is also stable: $0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, absolute difference $0.011946809265360736$.  Danio is not stable under this join restriction: $0.7608723943210729$ versus $0.874628721049266$, absolute difference $0.11375632672819314$.  The Danio localization result is therefore reported as a computed projection on a changed row population, not as evidence that localization explains the residual.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance surfaces.}
The localization test sits downstream of already measured finite surfaces.  Direct protein-omics survival of the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates against PAXdb abundance is positive across the broad panel, with $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in E. coli, $0.10217906758013379$ in C. elegans, $0.0646471273126996$ in Pseudomonas, $0.061906245886660105$ in Bacillus, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, $0.04613529807085213$ in zebrafish, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, and smaller positive entries down to $0.019256877907572044$ in Halobacterium.  Measured ribosome-TE survival is also present on the four joined TE panels: yeast has $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, Danio $0.04034217614654355$, human $0.018837911344127926$, and E. coli $0.009394266754822262$.  However, the measured mediation fractions remain organism-specific rather than universal: yeast has $M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{meas}}=0.4793069656345167$, E. coli $0.04091158340950673$, human $0.0027966485036786744$, and Danio is clipped to $0$ from a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$.

\paragraph{What the localization result says.}
The finite conclusion is a bounded H-candidate statement.  On the human and yeast joins, a 12-column localization annotation absorbs more of the modeled-translation complement than a rank-matched random readout would absorb.  On the Danio join, the absorption is below the null 95th percentile and the complement itself changes materially because the localization availability restricts the row set.  The result therefore supports the following local reading: part of the non-translation residual abundance component is aligned with coarse localization annotation in human and yeast, but this alignment is descriptive and row-dependent.

\paragraph{Other reductions do not close the residual.}
Several neighboring finite tests keep the same caution.  Joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability conditioning explains a sizeable fraction of the abundance association in yeast, with joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$ and right-tail permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$, while human and E. coli show smaller joint fractions $0.16890712099923358$ and $0.2404669586626661$ with the same reported right-tail value.  A compact readout dictionary in yeast covers $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ at $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$ after a selection-aware null95 of $0.029149007242956115$.  The $\mathrm{f3\_stress}$ dwell test has held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ against null95 $0.0013115362189692937$, with $\mathrm{corr}(\mathrm{f3},\mathrm{dwell})=0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  Its positional split is led by the 5-prime window, and the separate structural-order ramp test is classified as $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$: the primary 50-codon ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, the body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value is $0.05859375$.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folding conclusion, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, system phenotype, causal path, or universal biological rule follows from this localization projection.  The localization columns are coarse, multi-label annotations; their span can absorb variance mechanically, which is why the matched-null comparison is part of the finite statement.  A stronger biological interpretation would require a separate layer-appropriate contact and a statistic showing that the same $q_i$ first survives against a measured translation readout and that the downstream readout is reduced when that measured translation readout is conditioned on.  Without that extra row-matched contact, the present chapter records a finite descriptive absorption result only.
