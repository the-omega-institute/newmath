\subsection{Localization absorption of the non-translation abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Claim and finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-layer test for the conjecture that the residual part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal, after removing a modeled translation readout, may still be partly organized by protein localization. The carrier is a finite matrix surface over nine synonymous residual coordinates, a residualized protein-abundance response, and a twelve-column multi-label localization readout. The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$. The control basis has $24$ columns before rank reduction and rank $23$ after residualization: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.

The localization readout is not a mechanism assertion. It is a coarse UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-localization annotation parsed into $K=12$ one-hot columns: Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome. For each organism, the computed object is the projection of the modeled-translation complement abundance residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$ onto the residualized localization columns, compared with a degree-of-freedom matched Gaussian null using $200$ trials.

\paragraph{Rows, joins, and localization counts.}
Three organisms have the localization join needed for this test. In Danio rerio the reported CDS abundance join contains $14410$ CDS rows, while the localization-restricted join contains $1984$ rows and $2821$ localization records. The category counts are Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Cell membrane $230$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Golgi $125$, Secreted $79$, Vacuole/Lysosome $45$, Other $23$, and Peroxisome $14$. In Homo sapiens the reported CDS abundance join contains $19053$ CDS rows, the localization-restricted join contains $16008$ rows, and the localization file contributes $16406$ records. The largest human categories are Membrane $7119$, Cytoplasm $6305$, Nucleus $5837$, Cell membrane $3570$, Secreted $1876$, Cytoskeleton $1529$, Endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, Mitochondrion $1370$, Golgi $1096$, Vacuole/Lysosome $482$, Other $161$, and Peroxisome $109$. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae the reported CDS abundance join contains $5815$ CDS rows, the localization-restricted join contains $4656$ rows, and the localization file contributes $4831$ records. Its largest categories are Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, Nucleus $1733$, Mitochondrion $811$, Endoplasmic reticulum $426$, Cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, Vacuole/Lysosome $217$, Cytoskeleton $165$, Secreted $91$, Peroxisome $77$, and Other $44$.

\paragraph{Primary localization statistic.}
The finite statistic is an absorbed fraction, not a causal coefficient. The computed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{loc}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_{loc}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_{loc}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched null baselines are
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{0.95}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.011321328712802819,\\
N_{0.95}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0013758521256775384,\\
N_{0.95}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the human and yeast localization projections exceed the degree-of-freedom matched $95$th percentile, while the danio value does not. The corresponding null means are $0.0062549946178242525$ for danio, $0.0008032852113983743$ for human, and $0.002689902676583632$ for yeast. The finite-row verdict is therefore not that localization globally explains the residual, but that localization is a live $H$-candidate in the human and yeast rows and is not supported by this statistic in the danio row.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement consistency check.}
The localization experiment uses the modeled-translation complement abundance residual. On the localization-restricted rows, the modeled-complement fraction is $0.7608723943210729$ in danio, $0.9020707321191324$ in human, and $0.1997853864617063$ in yeast. The full abundance-CDS reference values from the complement experiment are $0.874628721049266$ in danio, $0.9208949355679455$ in human, and $0.21173219572706703$ in yeast. Therefore the absolute differences are $0.11375632672819314$, $0.018824203448813193$, and $0.011946809265360736$, respectively. Human and yeast match the complement reference within the declared $0.02$ tolerance; danio does not, and the danio localization result must be read with the additional caveat that localization availability changes the protein-id join enough to move the modeled-complement diagnostic.

\paragraph{Relation to direct protein abundance and translation readouts.}
The localization calculation is downstream of a stronger direct protein-omics surface. Across the measured PAXdb abundance panel, all nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates survive the direct abundance regression after the same sibling controls. The direct $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.10217906758013379$ for Caenorhabditis elegans, $0.08141786718177903$ for Dictyostelium discoideum, $0.0770952887136557$ for Drosophila melanogaster, $0.0646471273126996$ for Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, $0.06397514251295629$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus, $0.061906245886660105$ for Bacillus subtilis 168, $0.049827984062539195$ for Mus musculus, $0.04613529807085213$ for danio, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.03980793596647631$ for Gallus gallus, $0.03209009170649934$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, $0.02763998603083845$ for Rattus norvegicus, and $0.019256877907572044$ for Halobacterium salinarum. These values are direct protein-abundance associations, not translation mechanisms.

Measured ribosome-derived translation efficiency supplies a separate translation readout in four organisms. The measured $R^2_{QT}$ values are $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.04034217614654355$ in danio, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.009394266754822262$ in E. coli, again with all nine coordinates counted as surviving at the positive threshold. When protein abundance is conditioned on measured TE, the statistical mediated fractions are $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.0$ for danio after truncating a raw suppressive value of $-0.04801486048721136$ into the unit interval. These numbers justify keeping the localization analysis separate: human and yeast both show localization absorption above the null, but they sit in different translation-conditioned regimes.

\paragraph{Other measured residual routes.}
Several companion finite surfaces constrain how the localization result should be read. A joint TE-and-mRNA-stability decomposition gives joint mediation fractions $0.5195182225621592$ in yeast, $0.16890712099923358$ in human, and $0.2404669586626661$ in E. coli, with right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in all three rows. In yeast, a small readout dictionary with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$ covers $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ of the residual signal after selection-aware correction, with selection-aware null $95$th percentile $0.029149007242956115$, naive fixed-subset null $95$th percentile $0.00598374993486675$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. The $f3\_stress$ dwell test in yeast gives held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ over a null $95$th percentile of $0.0013115362189692937$, with $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. Its positional version classifies the leading segment as the $5'$ ramp: the $5'$ window passes, while the $3'$ body window does not. These data make the yeast row a structured residual regime, not a single localization-only story.

The human turnover check is also informative as a negative comparator. On $8057$ joined human turnover-abundance-CDS rows, the half-life absorption is only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, below a degree-of-freedom null $95$th percentile of $0.0004342285066808464$, even though the full human complement diagnostic remains $d_{modeled}=0.9208949355679455$. Thus the same human residual that is localization-positive in this chapter is not explained by the tested median half-life projection.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The readback is a four-case local classification over residualized $Q$, translation $T$, and protein abundance $P$. Case A is translation-null and protein-null. Case B is translation-positive but protein-null. Case C is translation-null but protein-positive, which is direct protein-omics prediction or confounding unless a measured translation readout is found. Case D is translation-positive and protein-positive with attenuation of the direct $Q\to P$ signal after conditioning on the translation readout. The localization statistic reported here is not itself one of these four cases. It is an $H$-candidate projection placed after the direct $Q\to P$ signal and after the modeled-translation complement. Its legitimate interpretation is narrower: in human and yeast, the residual component left after the modeled translation readout has more alignment with localization columns than expected from a twelve-column null design; in danio, the corresponding alignment is below the local null threshold and the join diagnostic is less stable.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation mechanism follows from the localization absorption numbers. No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, trafficking mechanism, compartment-causation claim, or universal biological law follows from the direct protein-omics $R^2$, the measured TE survival values, the modeled-translation complement, the localization one-hot projection, or the $f3\_stress$ dwell statistics. The valid conclusion is finite and row-specific: UniProtKB reviewed localization annotations carry excess residual signal for the human and yeast localization joins under the declared controls and null model, while danio does not pass that local excess test. A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic designed for that layer, for example perturbation, imaging, fractionation, protein-state, structural, functional, or phenotype data joined to the same CDS units and conditioned on the appropriate translation and transcript controls.
