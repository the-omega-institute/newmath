\subsection{Localization as a bounded non-translation residual readout}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-h-candidate}\origin{ai}
\paragraph{Finite carrier.} The finite surface for this record consists of nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, the same $Z$ controls used in the protein-abundance residual experiments, and a twelve-column multi-label localization readout. The coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}stress$. The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates. In each organism the recorded control-column count is $24$ and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.
\paragraph{Projection target.} The tested quantity is not a translation effect. First, measured protein abundance is residualized against $Z$ and the modeled translation component is removed, yielding the modeled-tAI complement abundance component $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}$. Second, that residual component is projected onto residualized UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-localization indicators. The localization readout has $K=12$ columns in each tested organism, and the interpreted statistic is the excess localization absorption above a $K$-column degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null. In symbols, the recorded comparison has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(o) &= R^2\bigl(\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{mod}}(o), L(o)\mid Z(o)\bigr),\\
\mathrm{signal}(o) &\Longleftrightarrow A_L(o)>Q_{0.95}^{null}(o),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $L(o)$ is the residualized twelve-column localization design for organism $o$. This definition makes the null subtraction essential: a twelve-column categorical readout can absorb variance mechanically, so only excess above the matched null is counted as localization-carried residual signal.
\paragraph{Joined localization rows.} Three organism-level joins were computed. In \emph{Danio rerio}, $1984$ CDS rows joined the localization matrix, compared with $14410$ CDS rows reported in the full abundance join; $2821$ localization records were parsed. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $16008$ joined rows were used, with $19053$ full abundance CDS rows reported and $16406$ localization records parsed. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $4656$ joined rows were used, with $5815$ full abundance CDS rows reported and $4831$ localization records parsed. The localization categories are the same twelve labels in all three cases: cell membrane, cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, membrane, mitochondrion, nucleus, other, peroxisome, secreted, and vacuole/lysosome.
\paragraph{Category counts.} The parsed category counts show that the readout is neither a single binary annotation nor an evenly balanced design. For \emph{D. rerio}, the largest categories are cytoplasm $883$, nucleus $845$, membrane $708$, mitochondrion $247$, and cell membrane $230$, with smaller counts including peroxisome $14$ and other $23$. For human, the largest categories are membrane $7119$, cytoplasm $6305$, nucleus $5837$, cell membrane $3570$, secreted $1876$, cytoskeleton $1529$, endoplasmic reticulum $1449$, and mitochondrion $1370$. For yeast, membrane $1863$, cytoplasm $1736$, nucleus $1733$, mitochondrion $811$, endoplasmic reticulum $426$, cell membrane $274$, Golgi $224$, and vacuole/lysosome $217$ dominate the readout. These finite counts are part of the readback: the localization matrix is a coarse multi-label annotation surface, not a compartment-mechanism assay.
\paragraph{Absorption values.} The computed absorption fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_L(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_L(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding degree-of-freedom-matched null summaries from $200$ trials are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{0.95}^{null}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.011321328712802819, & \mu^{null}(\mathrm{danio}) &= 0.0062549946178242525,\\
Q_{0.95}^{null}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0013758521256775384, & \mu^{null}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.0008032852113983743,\\
Q_{0.95}^{null}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.004958116973149795, & \mu^{null}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.002689902676583632.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the localization readout is above the matched null in human and yeast, while the danio value is below its $95\%$ null boundary. The finite verdict is therefore organism-specific: human and yeast carry a positive localization H-candidate signal for the modeled non-translation residual, and danio remains a computed but non-excess case.
\paragraph{Join-stability check.} The modeled complement fraction was also checked against the full abundance-complement experiment. Human has $d_{modeled}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join versus $0.9208949355679455$ on the full abundance CDS join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$, within the $0.02$ reporting tolerance. Yeast has $d_{modeled}=0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$, also within tolerance. Danio has $d_{modeled}=0.7608723943210729$ versus $0.874628721049266$, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$, outside tolerance. This reinforces the same interpretation: the human and yeast localization joins preserve the modeled complement closely enough for the H-candidate readback, whereas the danio localization availability changes the protein-id join and should not be promoted.
\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding measured-readout facts.} The localization result sits downstream of several measured surfaces but does not replace them. Direct $Q\to P$ survival against PAXdb abundance was positive across the powered abundance panel, with examples including $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, and $0.04613529807085213$ in danio, each using nine surviving coordinates. Measured ribosome-TE survival was also computed separately, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.04034217614654355$ in danio. Measured mediation of protein abundance by measured TE was strongest in yeast, where $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$, and the measured mediated fraction was $0.4793069656345167$; it was much smaller in \emph{E. coli} at $0.04091158340950673$ and human at $0.0027966485036786744$, while danio was clipped to $0$ from a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$. These numbers locate the localization chapter after the translation and abundance surfaces rather than inside them.
\paragraph{Residual dictionary context.} The same yeast abundance setting also has a partial readout-dictionary certificate: with $n_{join}=1800$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, five folds, and $120$ permutations, the selected readout family $\{\mathrm{measured\ TE},\mathrm{mRNA\ stability},\mathrm{turnover}\}$ explains $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ of the residual target, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. The raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.2745002077400602$, the selection-aware null $95\%$ value is $0.029149007242956115$, and the naive fixed-subset null $95\%$ value is $0.00598374993486675$. This matters for the present localization result because the localization signal is not being used to close all residual abundance; it is one bounded H-candidate surface among several measured or annotated readouts.
\paragraph{Dwell and positional comparison.} A separate yeast $f_3$ dwell experiment gives an additional non-localization comparator. On $n_{join}=1257$ rows with five folds and $120$ row-label permutations, the base model has held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, the dwell-alone readout has $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, the full model has $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, and the held-out increment is $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$, above a null $95\%$ value of $0.0013115362189692937$. The same target has $\|P_{f3}\|^2=44.20095750389323$, $P_{f3}$ fraction $0.1999063916718794$, and $f_3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. The structural-order positional run classifies the ramp result as $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$: at primary ramp length $50$, weighted ramp $\Delta R^2=0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body $\Delta R^2=-0.00026157313923957926$, and paired ramp-minus-body $\Delta R^2=0.001378137149572267$, with ramp sign-flip $p=0.0478515625$ and paired ramp-minus-body $p=0.05859375$. These finite facts show that localization, dwell, and position are distinct residual readouts; none is a substitute for a causal mechanism.
\paragraph{Local verdict.} The chapter-level verdict is a bounded H-candidate localization result. Human and yeast show localization absorption above the degree-of-freedom-matched null while preserving the modeled complement join within the stated tolerance. Danio is computed but does not pass either the null-excess test or the complement join-stability check. Therefore the admissible conclusion is: in human and yeast, coarse reviewed subcellular-localization annotations carry a small but non-null fraction of the modeled non-translation $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual. This does not say that the residual causes localization, that localization explains the whole residual, or that the same effect holds universally across organisms.
\paragraph{Local boundary.} No translation claim follows from this localization projection; measured translation must come from a translation readout such as ribosome-footprint-derived TE or an explicitly bounded modeled adaptation surface. No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, evolutionary direction, or universal biological rule follows from the localization absorption values. Protein abundance, translation efficiency, mRNA stability, turnover, dwell, positional occupancy, localization, structure, and function are separate readout layers. A positive localization excess only licenses the narrow statement that a finite, residualized, coarse categorical localization matrix absorbs more of the modeled non-translation residual than a matched random design in the organisms where the reported inequality holds.
