\subsection{Localization Readback for the Non-translation $B^*_{Q6}$ Complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-h-candidate-localization-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests subcellular localization as an $H$-candidate for the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance signal that is not absorbed by a modeled translation readout. The finite row set consists of three organism-specific joins, each carrying nine synonymous residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The residualization controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected coordinates. Each localization readout is a twelve-column multi-label UniProtKB reviewed subcellular-location surface, residualized against the same control basis and compared with a twelve-degree-of-freedom Gaussian null.

\paragraph{Joined rows and category ranks.}
The three evaluated joins are not interchangeable. In Danio rerio, the CDS/protein-abundance surface reports $14410$ joined CDS rows, but the localization intersection contains $1984$ joined rows and $2821$ localization records; the control rank is $23$ and the localization rank is $12$. In Homo sapiens, the corresponding numbers are $19053$ reported CDS joins, $16008$ localization-intersection rows, $16406$ localization records, control rank $23$, and localization rank $12$. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the finite surface contains $5815$ reported CDS joins, $4656$ localization-intersection rows, $4831$ localization records, control rank $23$, and localization rank $12$. The twelve parsed categories are Cell membrane, Cytoplasm, Cytoskeleton, Endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi, Membrane, Mitochondrion, Nucleus, Other, Peroxisome, Secreted, and Vacuole/Lysosome. The largest human categories are Membrane with $7119$ annotations, Cytoplasm with $6305$, and Nucleus with $5837$; yeast has Membrane $1863$, Cytoplasm $1736$, and Nucleus $1733$; danio has Cytoplasm $883$, Nucleus $845$, and Membrane $708$.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement alignment.}
The readout being tested is not the whole $Q \to P$ surface. It is the modeled-tAI complement abundance component, denoted here by $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$, restricted to the localization join. The complement diagnostic is stable for human and yeast but not for danio. Human gives $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join, compared with $0.9208949355679455$ on the full abundance-CDS reference join; the absolute difference is $0.018824203448813193$, inside the $0.02$ tolerance. Yeast gives $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.1997853864617063$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, with absolute difference $0.011946809265360736$, also inside tolerance. Danio gives $0.7608723943210729$ versus $0.874628721049266$, with absolute difference $0.11375632672819314$, so its localization-restricted join changes the complement enough that the danio value is reported as a separate surface rather than treated as a matched replication of the full abundance complement.

\paragraph{Localization absorption statistic.}
The finite statistic is the fraction of the modeled complement absorbed by the residualized twelve-column localization design, compared with a degree-of-freedom-matched null. The observed absorbed fractions are $0.009508864544725801$ for danio, $0.009254913329269913$ for human, and $0.011225105608516642$ for yeast. The corresponding null means are $0.0062549946178242525$, $0.0008032852113983743$, and $0.002689902676583632$, with null $95\%$ thresholds $0.011321328712802819$, $0.0013758521256775384$, and $0.004958116973149795$ over $200$ trials in each organism. Thus human and yeast exceed the finite null threshold, while danio does not:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{human}:&\quad 0.009254913329269913 > 0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mathrm{yeast}:&\quad 0.011225105608516642 > 0.004958116973149795,\\
\mathrm{danio}:&\quad 0.009508864544725801 < 0.011321328712802819.
\end{aligned}
$$
The chapter-level readback is therefore a two-organism positive localization absorption signal with one non-supporting organism whose localization join also fails the modeled-complement match diagnostic.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance surfaces.}
The localization statistic sits downstream of several already measured finite surfaces. Direct protein-omics survival against PAXdb abundance is positive across the larger rank-powered panel: for example, $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, and $0.04613529807085213$ in danio, with all nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates surviving by the stated threshold in each listed organism. Measured ribosome-TE survival is also finite on the four measured-TE organisms, with $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$ in yeast, $0.009394266754822262$ in E. coli, $0.018837911344127926$ in human, and $0.04034217614654355$ in danio. The measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance decomposition gives mediated fractions $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.0$ in danio after clipping the raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$. These values show why localization is treated here as an $H$-candidate for remaining protein-abundance residual structure, not as a replacement for the translation step.

\paragraph{Residual dictionary and related reductions.}
Other finite probes identify partial, not complete, explanations of the same abundance-side residual. On the unified yeast context join, the partial-mediation certificate uses $n=1800$ rows out of $4758$ available, five folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, and $120$ deterministic permutations. Its best selection-aware readout dictionary covers $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ of the residual signal, leaving $0.7546178856026946$ unexplained, with a selection-aware null $95\%$ threshold of $0.029149007242956115$ and a naive fixed-subset threshold of $0.00598374993486675$. The joint TE and mRNA-stability variance partition reports joint mediation fractions $0.5195182225621592$ in yeast, $0.2404669586626661$ in E. coli, and $0.16890712099923358$ in human, each with right-tail permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$. For the yeast $f_3$-stress component, the dwell readout gives held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ over a null $95\%$ threshold of $0.0013115362189692937$, with $f_3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. The structural-order ramp probe is classified as ramp-positive-but-not-localized: the primary $50$-codon ramp has weighted $f_3$ ramp held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0011165640103326878$, body $\Delta R^2=-0.00026157313923957926$, ramp sign-flip probability $0.0478515625$, and paired ramp-minus-body probability $0.05859375$. These companion surfaces constrain interpretation: localization is one finite absorber among several measured readout families, not a global account of the residual.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The positive content of this chapter is narrow. Human and yeast subcellular localization annotations absorb more of $\widehat P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ than a matched-rank null, while danio does not pass the same null comparison and also changes the modeled-complement diagnostic under the localization join. The finite statement is therefore: coarse multi-label localization carries detectable residual signal in two evaluated organisms after the declared controls, but the result is descriptive and row-set dependent. It does not establish translation, ribosome mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causality, evolutionary direction, or universality. Any such stronger claim requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled finite statistic.
