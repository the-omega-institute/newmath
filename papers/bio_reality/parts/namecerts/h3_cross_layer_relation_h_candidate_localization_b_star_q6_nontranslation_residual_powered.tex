\subsection{Localization absorption of the non-translation abundance residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite object tested here is not the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ association with protein abundance, and it is not a claim that codon residual geometry produces a localization mechanism.  The tested object is the remaining abundance component after a modeled translation readout has already been used as a bounded translation coordinate.  For each coding sequence row, the nine-dimensional residual coordinate block
$Q=(K_{AAA},Arg_{AGR},Ile_{AUA},Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},f_3\text{-stress})$
is residualized against the same baseline covariates used in the protein-abundance experiments: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  This gives $24$ control columns and rank $23$ for the control design in every localization panel below.

\paragraph{Finite matrix surface.}
The localization readout is the reviewed UniProt subcellular-localization annotation, parsed as a $K=12$ multi-label one-hot surface with categories
$Cell\_membrane$, $Cytoplasm$, $Cytoskeleton$, $Endoplasmic\_reticulum$, $Golgi$, $Membrane$, $Mitochondrion$, $Nucleus$, $Other$, $Peroxisome$, $Secreted$, and $Vacuole\_Lysosome$.
The three evaluated organisms have finite joined panels
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{danio\_rerio}:&\quad n=1984,\ K=12,\ \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23,\ n_{\mathrm{loc}}=2821,\\
\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}:&\quad n=16008,\ K=12,\ \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23,\ n_{\mathrm{loc}}=16406,\\
\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}:&\quad n=4656,\ K=12,\ \operatorname{rank}(Z)=23,\ n_{\mathrm{loc}}=4831.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding full CDS abundance joins reported by the upstream abundance surface are $14410$ for zebrafish, $19053$ for human, and $5815$ for yeast.  On the larger direct protein-abundance experiment, the same abundance resource had join hit rates $1.0000$ for zebrafish, $0.9779294769799313$ for human, and $0.9156038419146592$ for yeast, with direct $S^{QP}$-style partial-abundance readbacks $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529807085213$, $0.04454565118019448$, and $0.29837624994140827$, respectively.  Those direct values are used only as finite context for the downstream abundance surface; they are not interpreted as localization or mechanism.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement residual.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the $Z$-residualized protein-abundance component explained by the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates.  Let $T_{\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the modeled translation readout used in the companion abundance decomposition, and let $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ be the part of $P_Q$ not absorbed by that modeled translation coordinate.  The localization test projects this non-translation abundance component onto the residualized $12$-column localization matrix $L$.
The recorded complement fractions on the localization joins are
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{danio\_rerio}) &= 0.7608723943210729,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}) &= 0.9020707321191324,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}) &= 0.1997853864617063.
\end{aligned}
$$
For human and yeast these agree with the full abundance-complement references within the declared $0.02$ tolerance: human differs by $0.018824203448813193$ from the reference value $0.9208949355679455$, and yeast differs by $0.011946809265360736$ from the reference value $0.21173219572706703$.  Zebrafish differs by $0.11375632672819314$ from the reference value $0.874628721049266$, so the zebrafish localization join is reported as a changed-row-surface case rather than as a matched complement reproduction.

\paragraph{Observed localization absorption.}
The finite statistic is the fraction of $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ absorbed by the residualized localization columns.  The computed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(\mathrm{danio\_rerio}) &= 0.009508864544725801,\\
A_L(\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}) &= 0.009254913329269913,\\
A_L(\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}) &= 0.011225105608516642.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because a $12$-column multi-label readout can absorb variance mechanically, the comparison is made against a degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null with $200$ trials per organism.  The null means and $95$th percentiles are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{danio\_rerio}:&\quad \mu_0=0.0062549946178242525,\quad q_{0.95}=0.011321328712802819,\\
\mathrm{homo\_sapiens}:&\quad \mu_0=0.0008032852113983743,\quad q_{0.95}=0.0013758521256775384,\\
\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}:&\quad \mu_0=0.002689902676583632,\quad q_{0.95}=0.004958116973149795.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row readback has a two-positive, one-null pattern: human and yeast exceed their matched-null $95$th-percentile thresholds, while zebrafish does not.  In compact form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L(\mathrm{danio\_rerio})-q_{0.95} &= -0.001812464168077018,\\
A_L(\mathrm{homo\_sapiens})-q_{0.95} &= 0.007879061203592374,\\
A_L(\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae})-q_{0.95} &= 0.006266988635366847.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human and yeast rows therefore support localization as an $H$-candidate for carrying part of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual on these finite joined panels.  The zebrafish row remains below the finite null threshold and also fails the complement-match tolerance, so it is not used as a positive localization-carrying row.

\paragraph{Readout composition.}
The parsed localization surfaces show that the positive rows are not single-category artifacts.  Human has $16406$ reviewed localization records, including $6305$ cytoplasm, $7119$ membrane, $5837$ nucleus, $3570$ cell-membrane, $1876$ secreted, $1529$ cytoskeleton, $1449$ endoplasmic-reticulum, $1370$ mitochondrion, $1096$ Golgi, $482$ vacuole-lysosome, $161$ other, and $109$ peroxisome records.  Yeast has $4831$ records, including $1736$ cytoplasm, $1863$ membrane, $1733$ nucleus, $811$ mitochondrion, $426$ endoplasmic-reticulum, $274$ cell-membrane, $224$ Golgi, $217$ vacuole-lysosome, $165$ cytoskeleton, $91$ secreted, $77$ peroxisome, and $44$ other records.  Zebrafish has $2821$ records, including $883$ cytoplasm, $845$ nucleus, $708$ membrane, $247$ mitochondrion, $230$ cell-membrane, $207$ endoplasmic-reticulum, $182$ cytoskeleton, $125$ Golgi, $79$ secreted, $45$ vacuole-lysosome, $23$ other, and $14$ peroxisome records.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation rows.}
The same residual coordinate block has measured ribosome-TE survival rows in a separate finite test: $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.04034217614654355$ for zebrafish, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving under the positive threshold.  The measured translation-mediated abundance decomposition reports mediated fractions $0.0$ for zebrafish after truncating the raw negative fraction $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  These numbers explain why the localization experiment is deliberately phrased as an $H$-candidate test on the residual complement: the measured or modeled translation surfaces do not exhaust every downstream abundance component on every organism, and the remaining component can be interrogated by a separate finite readout such as localization.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The chapter-level conclusion is a bounded finite-row statement.  On the human and yeast localization joins, residualized multi-label subcellular localization absorbs more of $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ than expected from a degree-of-freedom-matched null, with margins $0.007879061203592374$ and $0.006266988635366847$.  On the zebrafish join, the observed absorption $0.009508864544725801$ is below the null $95$th percentile $0.011321328712802819$, and the localization-filtered complement fraction does not match the full abundance-complement reference.  The finite evidence therefore supports localization as a descriptive carrier of residual abundance signal in human and yeast only, not as a universal statement across the three organisms.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This test does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  The localization annotations are coarse, reviewed, multi-label category records; their projection onto $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ is a statistical absorption calculation.  A separate layer-matched contact would be required for claims about ribosome dynamics, folded structure, compartmental trafficking mechanism, physical feasibility, biochemical function, perturbation response, or organism-level consequence.  In particular, a positive localization absorption row says only that the residual abundance component has finite covariance with the localization category surface after the stated controls and matched-null comparison.
