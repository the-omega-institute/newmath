\subsection{Localization absorption of the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-localization-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record tested here is the finite matrix surface obtained after the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are residualized against the same ordinary sequence controls used in the protein-abundance and translation-readout runs.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}stress$.  The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus the localization experiment is not a raw codon-count comparison: it is a projection test on the residual abundance component left after the modeled translation readout has been separated from the $Q$-associated protein-abundance component.

\paragraph{Rows and localization modules.}
The localization contact is a reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-location table converted to a twelve-column multi-label readout.  The three organism joins are finite and non-vacuous: Danio rerio has $1984$ joined CDS rows against $2821$ localization records, Homo sapiens has $16008$ joined CDS rows against $16406$ localization records, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $4656$ joined CDS rows against $4831$ localization records.  Each organism uses $K=12$ localization columns, a $24$-column control design, and a control rank $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$.

For Danio rerio the twelve localization counts are Cell membrane $230$, Cytoplasm $883$, Cytoskeleton $182$, Endoplasmic reticulum $207$, Golgi $125$, Membrane $708$, Mitochondrion $247$, Nucleus $845$, Other $23$, Peroxisome $14$, Secreted $79$, and Vacuole/Lysosome $45$.  For Homo sapiens the corresponding counts are $3570$, $6305$, $1529$, $1449$, $1096$, $7119$, $1370$, $5837$, $161$, $109$, $1876$, and $482$.  For Saccharomyces cerevisiae they are $274$, $1736$, $165$, $426$, $224$, $1863$, $811$, $1733$, $44$, $77$, $91$, and $217$.  These numbers make the readout a coarse compartmental annotation matrix, not a mechanistic trafficking assay.

\paragraph{Absorption statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the protein-abundance component explained by the residual coordinate block after the sequence controls.  Let $T_{mod}$ denote the modeled tAI-style translation readout used in the complementary abundance decomposition, and let $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ be the part of the $Q$-associated protein-abundance component not absorbed by that modeled translation readout.  The localization test projects this residual target onto the residualized twelve-column localization matrix $L$ and reports the absorbed fraction together with a degree-of-freedom-matched Gaussian null using $200$ trials per organism:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_L &= \frac{\|\Pi_{L\mid Z} P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2}{\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2},\\
\text{signal gate} &:= A_L > \operatorname{Null}_{0.95}(K=12).
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed absorption fractions are $0.0095088645$ for Danio rerio, $0.0092549133$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.0112251056$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The matched null $95$th percentiles are respectively $0.0113213287$, $0.0013758521$, and $0.0049581170$, with null means $0.0062549946$, $0.0008032852$, and $0.0026899027$.  Therefore the localization contact exceeds its degree-of-freedom null in Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, but not in Danio rerio.  The resulting finite classification is a two-of-three positive localization absorption for the modeled-translation-complement residual, with the Danio row reported as below-null despite a positive raw absorbed fraction.

\paragraph{Compatibility with the protein-abundance surface.}
The direct protein-omics run establishes that the nine-coordinate residual block has nonzero protein-abundance association after the same type of sequence controls across a larger organism panel.  In the three organisms used here, the direct $R^2_{QP}$ values from measured PAXdb abundance are $0.0461352981$ for Danio rerio, $0.0445456512$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.2983762499$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with all nine residual coordinates surviving the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold in each case.  The larger direct panel also includes $R^2_{QP}=0.0619062459$ for Bacillus subtilis, $0.1021790676$ for Caenorhabditis elegans, $0.0814178672$ for Dictyostelium discoideum, $0.0770952887$ for Drosophila melanogaster, $0.1076752668$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.0398079360$ for Gallus gallus, $0.0192568779$ for Halobacterium salinarum, $0.0498279841$ for Mus musculus, $0.0320900917$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, $0.0646471273$ for Pseudomonas aeruginosa, $0.0276399860$ for Rattus norvegicus, and $0.0639751425$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus.  The join rates in that protein-abundance panel range from $0.8231586752$ in Rattus norvegicus to $1.0$ in Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Halobacterium salinarum, Mycobacterium smegmatis, and Sulfolobus solfataricus; the corresponding Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae rates are $0.9779294770$ and $0.9156038419$.

The localization rows are not identical to the full abundance rows.  The modeled-complement scalar $d_{modeled}$ changes from $0.8746287210$ on the full Danio abundance-CDS join to $0.7608723943$ on the localization join, an absolute difference of $0.1137563267$.  In Homo sapiens it changes from $0.9208949356$ to $0.9020707321$, a difference of $0.0188242034$, and in Saccharomyces cerevisiae from $0.2117321957$ to $0.1997853865$, a difference of $0.0119468093$.  Thus the human and yeast localization joins preserve the complement diagnostic within $0.02$, while the Danio localization availability changes the active row set enough that its negative gate should be interpreted with that sampling fact in view.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation readouts.}
Measured ribosome-profiling TE supplies a separate translation-layer statistic.  The measured $S^{QT}$ values are $0.0403421761$ for Danio rerio, $0.0093942668$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $0.0188379113$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.1368193134$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, again with all nine coordinates surviving in each organism.  In the matched measured-TE mediation decomposition, the measured mediated fractions are $0.0$ for Danio rerio after the raw value $-0.0480148605$ is clipped to the unit interval, $0.0409115834$ for Escherichia coli, $0.0027966485$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.4793069656$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  These values show why the localization test is specifically an $H$-candidate test: it asks where the residual abundance component may reside after modeled or measured translation readouts have failed to absorb the whole $Q\to P$ association.

The joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability partition gives a second check on this interpretation.  The joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669587$ in Escherichia coli, $0.1689071210$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.5195182226$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with right-tail permutation values $0.0009990010$ in all three cases.  The single-mediator components are approximately additive in the recorded overlap classification: in Homo sapiens the TE-only fraction is $0.0054949086$ and the stability-only fraction is $0.1531467987$; in yeast the TE-only fraction is $0.4697188320$ and the stability-only fraction is $0.0626618595$.  These numbers do not turn localization into a causal mediator.  They only show that the non-translation part of the abundance signal is large enough to motivate finite readout dictionaries beyond the translation coordinate.

\paragraph{Other residual dictionaries.}
The yeast residual has independent finite witnesses that it is not exhausted by one translation readout.  In the $f_3\text{-}stress$ dwell run, the active join has $n=1257$ rows, the base sample size is $1800$, the base model has held-out $R^2=0.1855578758$, the full model has held-out $R^2=0.2041226152$, and the dwell increment is $0.0185647394$.  The deterministic row-label null has mean $-0.0032911643$, maximum $0.0050013500$, and $95$th percentile $0.0013115362$, while the approximate $f_3$--dwell correlation $p$-value is $7.8739160572\times 10^{-9}$.  The partial mediation dictionary run on yeast uses $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, and $120$ permutations; it records selection-aware coverage $C^{\ast}=0.2453821144$ by the readout family $\{\text{measured TE},\text{mRNA stability},\text{turnover}\}$, leaving unexplained mass $0.7546178856$ above a selection-aware null $95$th percentile of $0.0291490072$.  A structural-order positional screen classifies the primary result as $\text{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$: the weighted $f_3$ ramp increment is $0.0011165640$, the weighted body increment is $-0.0002615731$, the ramp sign-flip probability is $0.0478515625$, and the paired ramp-minus-body probability is $0.05859375$.  These side records constrain interpretation: the localization absorption is one finite readout in a larger residual dictionary, not a final explanation of the residual.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The localization chapter is ready as a finite cross-layer readout statement.  The supported claim is narrow: after ordinary sequence controls and after separating the modeled translation-readout component, reviewed multi-label subcellular localization absorbs an above-null fraction of the remaining $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$-associated protein-abundance component in Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, while Danio rerio is below the degree-of-freedom-matched null and has a shifted localization join.  No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, causal localization mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this projection.  Any such stronger assertion would require its own layer-matched measurement and a separately recorded statistic.
