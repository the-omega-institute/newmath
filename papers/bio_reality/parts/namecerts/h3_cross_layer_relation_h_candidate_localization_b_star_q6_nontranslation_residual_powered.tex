\subsection{Localization as a non-translation residual candidate for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-localization-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a coarse protein-localization readout carries part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after a modeled translation layer has already been separated. The rows are coding sequences joined to protein identifiers, abundance readouts, codon-count controls, and reviewed UniProtKB subcellular-localization annotations. The residual coordinate block has nine columns,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The control block has 24 columns: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$ computed from real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. In each organism the recorded control rank is $23$, while the localization matrix has $K=12$ columns.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
Let $P\_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ denote the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual after the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate projection has been formed and the modeled translation readout has been removed. Let $L$ denote the residualized multi-label localization one-hot matrix. The reported scalar is the fraction of the residual energy of $P\_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ absorbed by the column span of $L$, compared to a Gaussian null with the same number of localization columns. The calculation is descriptive: the localization columns are coarse, multi-label UniProt comment categories, and a multi-column readout can absorb energy mechanically. Therefore only excess over the degree-of-freedom-matched null is treated as evidence that localization carries residual signal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{L} &= \frac{\|\Pi_{L} P\_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^{2}}{\|P\_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}\|^{2}},\\
\mathrm{excess}_{0.95} &\equiv A_{L} > A^{\mathrm{null}}_{L,0.95}.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Localization absorption readback.}
The absorption values separate the organisms into one non-excess case and two excess cases under the recorded null. In danio rerio, the localization absorption is $0.009508864544725801$, while the matched-null mean is $0.0062549946178242525$ and the 95th percentile is $0.011321328712802819$ over $200$ trials. Thus the danio localization projection does not clear the null boundary. In homo sapiens, the localization absorption is $0.009254913329269913$, while the null mean is $0.0008032852113983743$ and the 95th percentile is $0.0013758521256775384$ over $200$ trials; this is a positive excess over the matched null. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the localization absorption is $0.011225105608516642$, while the null mean is $0.002689902676583632$ and the 95th percentile is $0.004958116973149795$ over $200$ trials; this is also a positive excess over the matched null.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{danio\ rerio}:&\quad 0.0095088645 < 0.0113213287,\\
\mathrm{homo\ sapiens}:&\quad 0.0092549133 > 0.0013758521,\\
\mathrm{saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:&\quad 0.0112251056 > 0.0049581170.
\end{aligned}
$$

The modeled-complement diagnostic is consistent with the full abundance-CDS reference in two of the three localization joins. Homo sapiens has $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9020707321191324$ on the localization join, compared with $0.9208949355679455$ on the full abundance-CDS join, an absolute difference of $0.018824203448813193$. Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.1997853864617063$, compared with $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011946809265360736$. Danio rerio has $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.7608723943210729$, compared with $0.874628721049266$, an absolute difference of $0.11375632672819314$; this larger discrepancy is recorded as a join-availability change rather than interpreted away.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation mediation.}
The localization readout is not itself a translation readout. The same claim family also contains a measured translation-efficiency decomposition using $\log_{10}(TE)$, where $TE$ is ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance, and $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ is the protein-abundance readout. That decomposition was computed on four joined panels: danio rerio with $13{,}140$ three-way joined rows, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 with $3{,}415$ rows, homo sapiens with $11{,}554$ rows, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae with $5{,}128$ rows. The measured $S^{QT}$ values are $0.04034217614654355$ for danio, $0.009394266754822262$ for E. coli, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast. The corresponding $S^{QP}$ values are $0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$. The measured mediated fractions are $0.0$ for danio after clamping a raw value of $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.

Those numbers matter because they prevent the localization result from being promoted into a translation mechanism. The localization projection begins after a modeled translation complement residual has been formed. It says that, in human and yeast, coarse localization categories absorb more of that remaining abundance residual than a matched random multi-column basis would. It does not say that $q_i$ changes translation, and it does not say that translation causes localization. Any such reading would require a same-row measured chain in which $q_i \to T_j$ survives first and conditioning on $T_j$ reduces the downstream association.

\paragraph{Turnover contrast.}
A separate human turnover projection gives a useful negative contrast. The human turnover panel joins $8{,}057$ turnover-abundance-CDS rows, from $8{,}164$ valid turnover proteins and $19{,}483$ valid abundance proteins. Its modeled complement reference has $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490492302532$, $P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ norm squared $1262.2237678771598$, and $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.9208949355679455$ on the full human abundance-CDS join. The turnover absorption is only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, while the degree-of-freedom null has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and 95th percentile $0.0004342285066808464$ over $200$ trials. The recorded null comparison therefore does not support turnover as the carrier of the large human modeled-tAI complement residual. The combined diagnostic for that turnover test is $d_{\mathrm{combined}}=0.8901589776585174$, with $R^2_{Q,Tturn}=0.004615408769189838$.

\paragraph{Four-case interpretation.}
For this finite surface the readback is case-specific. If a coordinate has neither translation survival nor downstream protein survival, it remains null for this packet. If it has translation survival but no protein survival, it is bounded to a translation-readout association. If it has protein survival but no surviving translation step, it is only a direct or confounded protein-omics predictor. If it has both and the direct $q_i \to P_k$ signal is reduced after conditioning on a measured $T_j$, it may be reported as a translation-conditioned statistical mediation pattern. The present localization calculation occupies a different position: it asks whether a non-translation readout, measured after the modeled translation complement residual is formed, carries remaining abundance-residual energy. Human and yeast clear that descriptive localization threshold; danio does not.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow. Reviewed subcellular-localization annotations carry detectable residual signal above a matched 12-column null in homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, but not in the danio rerio localization join. This is a finite projection fact about joined rows, residualized controls, and coarse multi-label categories. It does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, organismal phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality. Those claims require separate measurements at the relevant layer and a statistic showing that the relevant layer-specific readout survives under the same control discipline.
