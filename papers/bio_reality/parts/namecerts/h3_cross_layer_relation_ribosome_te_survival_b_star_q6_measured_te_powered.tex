\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-ribosome-te-survival-b-star-q6-measured-te-powered}

\paragraph{Finite row and readout.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface leaves a measurable trace in ribosome-derived translation efficiency after the ordinary compositional explanations have been removed.  The tested coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The response is $\log_{10}(TE)$, where the local ribosome files define $TE$ as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance.  Thus the translation readout is already normalized by the transcript denominator before the residual regression is fit.

\paragraph{Residual design.}
For each organism, the $Q$ matrix and the TE response are residualized against the same control family: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  The control design has $24$ columns with effective rank $23$.  The residualized $B^{*}_{Q6}$ design has rank $9$ in every tested organism, so the computation is not an all-fit rank artifact.  The rank tolerance is $10^{-10}$, the degeneracy cutoff is $0.999999$, and the positive-entry threshold is $10^{-12}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(Danio\ rerio) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(Escherichia\ coli\ K12) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(Homo\ sapiens) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$

Every row has all $9$ residual coordinates counted as surviving coordinates under the stated threshold.  The strongest measured-TE row is yeast, with $R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$; the weakest is $E.\ coli$ K12, with $R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$.  The result is therefore a positive finite-row association between the residual coordinate surface and measured ribosome TE, not merely a modeled codon-adaptation comparison.

\paragraph{Joined organism rows.}
The measured TE contact is powered by four same-organism joins.  In $Danio\ rerio$, $13140$ genes with TE are used out of $14410$ CDS rows, with join hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  In $E.\ coli$ K12 MG1655, $3415$ genes with TE are used out of $3739$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  In human, $11554$ TE genes are used out of $19053$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  In yeast, $5128$ TE genes are used out of $5815$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.

The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $13117$ for $Danio\ rerio$, $3392$ for $E.\ coli$ K12, $11531$ for human, and $5105$ for yeast.  The corresponding residual response energies are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.  The finite matrix is therefore row-supported at both the identifier level and the linear-algebra level: the TE joins are nontrivial, the control rank is stable, and the residual $Q$ rank remains full at $9$.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance.}
This TE-survival row should not be read as a protein-output statement by itself.  A separate direct protein-abundance computation reports positive $Q \to P$ associations on PAXdb abundance for $15$ organisms, with examples including $R^{2}_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, $0.10767526677985896$ in $E.\ coli$ K12, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, and $0.04613529807085213$ in $Danio\ rerio$.  A further measured-mediation computation joins measured TE and protein abundance and reports measured fractions $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in $E.\ coli$ K12, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.0$ in $Danio\ rerio$ after clipping a negative raw fraction.  Those facts show why the present TE row is useful for downstream comparison, but they are not collapsed into the present verdict.  The present verdict is only that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured ribosome TE under the stated controls.

\paragraph{Four-case reading.}
At the level of this finite row, a positive $R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$ means that the codon residual surface has a detectable association with measured TE after residualizing the declared covariates.  It does not by itself decide whether a protein-abundance association is mediated by TE, remains direct after TE conditioning, is confounded by another readout, or disappears under a stricter panel.  The correct downstream reading requires the same row units to carry $Q$, measured $T$, matched $P$, and matched transcript controls, followed by an explicit comparison of $Q \to P$ before and after conditioning on $T$.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter licenses a bounded statement: measured ribosome TE retains positive association with the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate surface in the four rank-powered organism rows listed above.  It does not assert a causal translation mechanism, a universal translation-efficiency law, elongation speed, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or evolutionary direction.  Protein abundance, folding, stability, localization, function, physical assay behavior, and universality each require their own layer-matched contact and their own controlled statistic.  A direct $Q \to P$ signal is therefore not promoted to mechanism unless a measured $Q \to T \to P$ analysis shows that the same measured translation readout absorbs or reduces the direct protein-abundance association on matched rows.
