\subsection{Rank-powered measured-TE survival for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-measured-te-survival}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite per-CDS matrix whose rows join coding-sequence codon counts to a measured translation-efficiency readout. The readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$, with $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{footprint}/\mathrm{mRNA}$ in the local ribosome-profiling files, so the translation measurement is already normalized by the matched transcript denominator before the residual calculation is made. The tested coordinate family has nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. Each organism-specific fit residualizes the translation readout and the coordinate matrix against the same control family: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline. The control matrix has $24$ columns and observed rank $23$ in every powered organism, while the residualized $B^{*}_{Q6}$ design has rank $9$ in every organism.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
The four measured-TE joins are nondegenerate and organism matched. In \textit{Danio rerio}, $13140$ genes with TE are used against $14410$ joined CDS entries, with a ribosome-TE join hit rate of $0.9118667591950035$. In \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $3415$ TE genes are used against $3739$ joined CDS entries, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$. In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $11554$ TE genes are used against $19053$ joined CDS entries, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$. In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5128$ TE genes are used against $5815$ joined CDS entries, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$. These joins are large enough to rule out the rank-degenerate three-organism boundary: after controls, the residual degrees of freedom are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$ respectively.

\paragraph{Residual survival calculation.}
The measured-TE survival statistic is the partial explanatory power of the residualized nine-coordinate matrix for the residualized TE vector. The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{Danio rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{Homo sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^{2}_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold is $10^{-12}$, and each organism reports $9$ surviving coordinates. The residual-energy diagnostics are also finite-row checks rather than symbolic assertions: the residual TE energies after control removal are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$ for danio, \textit{E. coli}, human, and yeast respectively. The explained energies from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates on the measured TE readout are $144.76301201119642$, $8.637891149939946$, $38.66659710161269$, and $103.10404057290246$ in the same order. Thus this chapter records a positive measured translation-readout association in all four joined panels, with the strongest observed measured-TE value in yeast and the weakest in \textit{E. coli}.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance.}
The same coordinate family also has direct protein-abundance survival in a larger PAXdb panel, but that fact has a different status. For the four organisms with measured TE mediation rows, the direct protein-abundance associations in the measured mediation run are $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ for danio, $0.10941535951828074$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.04288464996165745$ for human, and $0.3146818503437042$ for yeast. Conditioning on measured TE gives measured mediation fractions $0.0$, $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$ respectively, with the raw danio decomposition suppressive at $-0.04801486048721136$ and therefore clipped to zero for the bounded fraction. These numbers show why the present TE-survival result should not be read as a protein-output mechanism by itself: the measured translation readout is a real finite row in the matrix, but attenuation of the protein-abundance association is a separate decomposition.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
At the level of this chapter, the module is a cross-layer relation between codon residual coordinates and a measured translation readout. The ready claim is not that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ causes translation, nor that it determines protein abundance. The ready claim is narrower: after removing amino-acid composition, length, GC3, and $M$-density controls, the nine-coordinate residual surface retains positive explanatory power for measured ribosome-profiling TE in four rank-powered organism panels. The per-entry readback is therefore translation-readout survival, not folding, localization, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folded structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, causal pathway, or universality follows from these rows alone. The TE files are single-study or small-study, condition-specific, cross-sectional measurements, and agreement or disagreement with modeled tAI is only a coordinate-pattern comparison. Any stronger statement must attach its own layer-matched contact: protein abundance requires the protein readout and mediation fit; folding requires structure-level evidence; function and phenotype require their own assays; causality requires perturbation or rescue design. Within those limits, the measured-TE survival surface is no longer a null boundary: it is a finite, controlled, rank-powered readback of $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates against measured translation efficiency.
