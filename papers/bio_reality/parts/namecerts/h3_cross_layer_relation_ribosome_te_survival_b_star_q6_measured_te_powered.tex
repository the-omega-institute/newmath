\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bq6-measured-ribosome-te-survival}

\paragraph{Finite matrix being tested.}
The measured ribosome-TE experiment evaluates a finite row surface whose columns are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates and whose response is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$, with $\mathrm{TE}$ read from local ribosome-profiling files as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance.  The nine residual coordinates are \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}.  The rows are real CDS-linked genes in four organisms, not symbolic examples: \textit{Danio rerio}, \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The control design residualizes both the coordinate matrix and the TE readout against CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the M-density baseline.  In each organism the control block has 24 columns, rank $23$, and the residual coordinate matrix has rank $9$, so the positive scores are not produced by a rank-degenerate all-fit surface.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
The organism-specific joins are large enough to make the survival readout a finite empirical statistic rather than a routing claim.  The TE contact uses 13,140 genes in \textit{D. rerio}, with 14,410 CDS rows reported and a ribosome-TE join hit rate of $0.9118667591950035$.  The \textit{E. coli} contact uses 3,415 genes, with 3,739 CDS rows reported and hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  The human contact uses 11,554 genes, with 19,053 CDS rows reported and hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  The yeast contact uses 5,128 genes, with 5,815 CDS rows reported and hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  These joins define the finite panel on which $S^{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$ is read, with the measured TE response already mRNA-normalized by construction.

\paragraph{Residual rank and survival values.}
After the controls are projected out, all four organism blocks retain the full nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate rank.  The residual degrees of freedom after the control block are $13{,}117$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $3{,}392$ for \textit{E. coli}, $11{,}531$ for human, and $5{,}105$ for yeast.  The response residual energies are respectively $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.  The measured TE survival scores are positive in all four blocks:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each organism reports nine surviving coordinates at the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The largest measured TE survival is in yeast, where the coordinate block explains $103.10404057290246$ units of TE residual energy on a TE residual energy of $753.5781168800304$.  The smallest positive block is \textit{E. coli}, where the explained energy is $8.637891149939946$ on residual energy $919.4854026798789$; this remains a positive measured TE association, but it is much weaker than the yeast surface.

\paragraph{Comparison with measured protein abundance mediation.}
The matched measured-TE/protein-abundance mediation run uses the same nine-coordinate source and the same kind of control residualization, but changes the response from TE alone to the pair consisting of measured TE and PAXdb protein abundance.  On the three-way joined rows, the measured mediation fractions are $0.0$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $0.04091158340950673$ for \textit{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  Thus the measured TE survival surface is not by itself a protein-output mechanism claim: the same $Q \to T$ signal can be strong while its measured contribution to $Q \to P$ attenuation is absent, weak, or large, depending on the organism and joined protein-abundance surface.  In yeast, the measured TE result is consistent with a substantial statistical attenuation of the codon-to-protein association, while in human the measured TE survival remains positive but its measured protein-abundance mediation fraction is only about $0.0028$.

\paragraph{Joint TE and mRNA-stability context.}
A separate abundance-decomposition surface adds mRNA stability to measured TE and evaluates how much of the $B^*_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association is reduced by conditioning on the two readouts jointly.  On the four-way joins, the joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ in human, and $0.5195182225621592$ in yeast, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ under the fixed pair-permutation audit.  The single-readout split is organism-specific: TE-only fractions are $0.01975521340963268$, $0.00549490864190343$, and $0.4697188319693239$, while stability-only fractions are $0.23052056324898387$, $0.15314679870183337$, and $0.0626618595344251$ for \textit{E. coli}, human, and yeast respectively.  This comparison places the measured ribosome-TE survival result in the correct layer: it certifies a finite $Q \to T$ association after controls, and it motivates translation-conditioned decompositions, but it does not collapse all downstream protein-abundance variation into TE.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
For this chapter, the local readback is: positive measured translation-readout survival is present in every powered organism block; protein-abundance mediation by measured TE is heterogeneous; and joint TE-plus-stability conditioning shows that yeast has the strongest TE-linked protein-abundance attenuation among the available organisms.  The finite row evidence therefore supports a measured translation-layer survival statement for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  It also marks the exact boundary of the result: the survival statistic is cross-sectional, condition-specific, and fitted on ribosome-profiling TE rows that come from one or a small number of local studies.  It is not a perturbation, rescue, causal mediation experiment, elongation-speed measurement, folding measurement, molecular-function assay, physical-admissibility assay, phenotype readout, or universal biological law.  Translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and universality remain outside the claim unless another layer-matched contact supplies rows and a separate controlled statistic for that layer.
