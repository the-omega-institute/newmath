\subsection{Rank-powered measured-TE survival for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-ribosome-te-survival-b-star-q6-measured-te-powered}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The finite object in this chapter is a four-organism matrix whose rows are coding sequences with matched codon counts and measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readouts.  The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Each coordinate is evaluated after residualizing against a local control design with $24$ columns, rank $23$, amino-acid composition, coding-sequence length, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline.  The measured readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ computed in the local data files as ribosome footprint divided by mRNA.  Thus the mRNA denominator is already built into the translation-efficiency measurement, while matched transcript information remains part of the control interpretation rather than a downstream protein or mechanism readout.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
The measured-TE contact is nonvacuous in all four organisms.  For Danio rerio, $13140$ genes with measured TE are joined against $14410$ reported coding-sequence joins, giving hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  For Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $3415$ genes are used from $3739$ coding-sequence joins, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  For Homo sapiens, $11554$ genes are used from $19053$ coding-sequence joins, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  For Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $5128$ genes are used from $5815$ coding-sequence joins, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  These counts are large enough to avoid the rank-saturated three-row artifact: the residual degrees of freedom after the $Z$ controls are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$, respectively, and the residualized $Q$ design has rank $9$ in each organism.

\paragraph{Measured survival surface.}
The fitted statistic is the controlled association between the residualized $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate matrix and measured $\log_{10}(TE)$.  The resulting organism-level measured-TE survival values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{E.\ coli\ K12\ MG1655}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
All four rows retain all nine coordinates above the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The residual energy of the measured-TE readout after controls is $3588.378859021949$ in Danio, $919.4854026798789$ in E. coli, $2052.594706241978$ in human, and $753.5781168800304$ in yeast.  The corresponding explained energies of the $Q$ surface on measured TE are $144.76301201119642$, $8.637891149939946$, $38.66659710161269$, and $103.10404057290246$.  This is a measured translation-readout survival result: it says that the residual codon-coordinate surface carries finite information about ribosome-footprint-over-mRNA translation efficiency after the declared controls.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance boundary.}
The same coordinates also have direct protein-abundance associations in a separate proteomics readout, with representative controlled $R^2_{QP}$ values $0.10767526677985896$ for E. coli, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, and $0.04613529807085213$ for Danio.  Those direct $Q \to P$ values are not, by themselves, translation mechanisms.  A measured-TE conditioning fit gives measured mediation fractions $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, and $0.0$ in Danio after truncating the raw suppressive Danio value $-0.04801486048721136$ to the unit interval.  The corresponding measured $S_{QP}$ values in that three-way fit are $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, $0.3146818503437042$, and $0.04926578454614652$.  Therefore the present chapter supports measured-TE survival for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, and it permits downstream comparison with protein abundance only where the same-row conditioning calculation is explicitly reported.

\paragraph{Readout dictionary context.}
Additional finite-row checks sharpen the interpretation without enlarging the claim.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition, the right-tail permutation value is $0.000999000999000999$ for E. coli, human, and yeast; the joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, respectively.  In the yeast readout-dictionary compression record, the source design uses $n=1800$ rows from $4758$ available joins, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $\rho_{join}=0.05$; its selected coverage is $0.24538211439730545$ against a selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  These numbers show that the measured-TE signal is one component of a broader finite readout decomposition, not a complete account of protein abundance.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter status is ready for the measured translation-efficiency layer.  The supported statement is: in a rank-powered, organism-matched, measured-ribo-seq panel, the nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface survives against $\log_{10}(TE)$ after amino-acid composition, length, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls in Danio rerio, E. coli K12 MG1655, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The strongest measured-TE row is yeast with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$, and the weakest is E. coli with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$, but all four rows are positive and rank-sufficient.
