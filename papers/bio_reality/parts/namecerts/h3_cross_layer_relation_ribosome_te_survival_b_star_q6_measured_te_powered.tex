\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{sec:biorality-q6-measured-ribosome-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record tests whether the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates retain measurable signal against organism-matched ribosome-profiling translation efficiency after the usual sequence-composition controls have been removed.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  For each coding sequence row, the translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ read from local ribosome-profiling files as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA.  Thus the readout is already mRNA-normalized at the data-source level, and the statistical question is not whether mRNA abundance exists, but whether codon residual coordinates still explain the measured TE surface after residualization.

\paragraph{Residualization design.}
For each organism the design residualizes both the $Q$ matrix and the measured TE vector against a control basis consisting of an intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The fitted residual coordinate matrix keeps full rank in all four measured-TE organisms: $rank(\widetilde X)=9$ and $rank(Z)=23$ with $24$ control columns.  The finite-row ranks and residual degrees of freedom are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Danio\ rerio &: n_{TE}=13140,\ hit=0.9118667591950035,\ df_Z=13117,\ E_Y=3588.378859021949,\\
E.\ coli\ K12 &: n_{TE}=3415,\ hit=0.9133458143888741,\ df_Z=3392,\ E_Y=919.4854026798789,\\
Homo\ sapiens &: n_{TE}=11554,\ hit=0.6064136881331024,\ df_Z=11531,\ E_Y=2052.594706241978,\\
S.\ cerevisiae &: n_{TE}=5128,\ hit=0.8818572656921754,\ df_Z=5105,\ E_Y=753.5781168800304.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding reported CDS joins are $14410$ for zebrafish, $3739$ for $E.$ coli K12 MG1655, $19053$ for human, and $5815$ for budding yeast.  The rank tolerance is $10^{-10}$ and the degenerate-$R^2$ guard is $0.999999$, so the four surfaces are not the rank-degenerate all-one boundary that can appear in underpowered organism panels.

\paragraph{Measured TE survival.}
The measured-TE survival statistic is the partial $R^2$ of the residualized $Q$ matrix against residualized $\log_{10}(TE)$.  With positivity threshold $10^{-12}$, all nine residual coordinates survive in each measured organism.  The finite-row measured-TE values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(Danio\ rerio) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(E.\ coli\ K12) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(Homo\ sapiens) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(S.\ cerevisiae) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
Yeast has the largest measured-TE surface among these rows, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ on $5128$ TE genes and $5105$ residual degrees of freedom after the control basis.  Zebrafish and human remain positive at larger row counts, and $E.$ coli remains positive despite a smaller residual TE energy.  The empirical statement supported here is therefore narrow but non-vacuous: the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate surface is visible in measured ribosome-profiling TE after sequence-composition residualization in four organism-matched panels.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance.}
The measured-TE result is not, by itself, a protein-abundance mechanism.  A separate protein-abundance surface reports direct $Q\to P$ survival against PAXdb abundance across a larger organism panel, with $R^2_{QP}=0.061906245886660105$ in $B.$ subtilis, $0.10767526677985896$ in $E.$ coli, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, and $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, again with all nine coordinates positive under the same style of sequence controls.  The measured mediation fit then conditions the protein-abundance association on measured TE for the four organisms with matched TE rows.  Its finite-row fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}_{Danio} &= 0,\quad S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652,\quad S^{meas}_{QT}=0.04034217614654355,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{E.coli} &= 0.04091158340950673,\quad S_{QP}=0.10941535951828074,\quad S^{meas}_{QT}=0.009394266754822262,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{Human} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\quad S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745,\quad S^{meas}_{QT}=0.018837911344127926,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{Yeast} &= 0.4793069656345167,\quad S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042,\quad S^{meas}_{QT}=0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
This places the present chapter at the measured-translation boundary.  It supports positive $Q\to T$ survival in all four measured-TE panels and shows that a downstream $Q\to P$ association can be compared with TE-conditioned attenuation where matched abundance is present.  The yeast row is the strongest translation-conditioned protein-abundance case in this finite set, while human and $E.$ coli show much smaller measured-TE mediation fractions, and zebrafish gives a suppressive raw fraction clipped to zero in the reported unit interval.

\paragraph{Four-case readback.}
The relevant per-row interpretation is a four-way distinction over residualized $Q$, translation readout $T$, and protein abundance $P$.  The present TE computation establishes the positive $Q\to T$ side of the readback for all four measured organisms.  Direct $Q\to P$ survival is separately positive in the protein-omics panel, but direct protein survival is not promoted to a mechanism claim unless conditioning on measured $T$ removes or materially reduces the $Q\to P$ signal.  In this finite evidence set, the measured mediation fractions are organism-specific: yeast has a large TE-conditioned reduction, $E.$ coli and human have small reductions, and zebrafish has no positive measured reduction after clipping.  Consequently the chapter records measured TE survival as ready, while downstream mechanism language remains tied to the explicit mediation numbers rather than to the existence of either $S^{QT,meas}$ or $S^{QP}$ alone.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result is a cross-sectional association over finite joined rows.  It does not establish a causal translation mechanism, universal elongation law, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or evolutionary direction.  The ribosome-profiling TE values come from one or a small number of condition-specific studies per organism, and TE itself is a footprint-over-mRNA readout with its own measurement and normalization biases.  Protein abundance is a separate steady-state omics layer affected by turnover, localization, post-translational state, measurement platform, and condition.  Any claim about folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality requires its own layer-matched measurement and its own finite-row statistic.  Within those limits, the measured conclusion is that rank-powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured ribosome-profiling TE in zebrafish, $E.$ coli, human, and budding yeast after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, and $M$-density controls.
