\subsection{Measured ribosome-efficiency survival of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-survival}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched matrix whose rows are coding sequences and whose columns combine three kinds of data: nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, a measured ribosome-efficiency readout, and the covariates used to remove ordinary compositional explanations.  The nine residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The measured readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$, so the ribosome-footprint signal is already normalized by the matched transcript signal in the local source files.  The covariate block has 24 columns before rank reduction: intercept, coding-sequence length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the support of the nine projected residual coordinates.

\paragraph{Powered matched panels.}
Four organisms have nondegenerate joins between coding-sequence codon counts and measured ribosome-efficiency rows.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, 13,140 genes with measured $TE$ are joined against 14,410 reported coding-sequence rows, giving hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, 3,415 measured-$TE$ genes join against 3,739 reported coding-sequence rows, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, 11,554 measured-$TE$ genes join against 19,053 reported coding-sequence rows, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, 5,128 measured-$TE$ genes join against 5,815 reported coding-sequence rows, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  These joins are not three-organism rank artifacts: each organism has $rank(\widetilde X)=9$ for the residual-coordinate design and $rank(Z)=23$ after the control block is formed, with rank tolerance $10^{-10}$ and degeneracy threshold $0.999999$.

\paragraph{Controlled survival statistic.}
For each organism, the computation residualizes both the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate matrix and $\log_{10}(TE)$ against the same control block $Z$, then fits the remaining $TE$ variation against the remaining nine-dimensional residual-coordinate surface.  The measured survival values are all positive above the declared partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{D. rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{H. sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
In each case all nine residual coordinates have positive measured-$TE$ survival under the implemented threshold.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are 13,117 for \emph{D. rerio}, 3,392 for \emph{E. coli}, 11,531 for \emph{H. sapiens}, and 5,105 for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  The corresponding residual $TE$ energies are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.  Thus the finite-row conclusion is not merely that codon usage and modeled adaptation weights can be routed to a translation layer; it is that a measured ribosome-footprint-over-mRNA readout retains a positive association with the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface after length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and $M$-density have been removed.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance mediation.}
The same four organism route also has a separate measured-$TE$ to protein-abundance decomposition.  In that downstream calculation the measured mediated fractions are $0.0$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.4793069656345167$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  These values show why the present record must be read as a measured-$TE$ survival statement, not as a blanket protein-output mechanism.  Positive $R^2_{QT,meas}$ is necessary for a measured translation-readout route, but downstream absorption of a $Q\to P$ association is a second statistic.  Yeast is the strongest finite row in that second statistic, while human and \emph{E. coli} show only small measured fractions and \emph{D. rerio} gives no positive clipped mediated fraction in that decomposition.

\paragraph{Comparison with other reduction rows.}
The broader finite-row context is consistent with a partial, not total, reduction picture.  In the joint measured-$TE$ and mRNA-stability decomposition, the joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.16890712099923358$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.5195182225621592$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ under the recorded pair-permutation null.  In the yeast readout-dictionary compression row, the best selected coverage after the selection-aware correction is $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n_{join}=1800$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  A positional structural-order row classifies the ramp result as \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}: for the primary 50-codon window the weighted $f3$ ramp increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, the weighted body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value is $0.05859375$.  These companion rows are not used to reinterpret the present measured-$TE$ statistic as structure or function; they only locate the measured-$TE$ result among other finite reductions already computed on related joins.

\paragraph{Local admissible conclusion.}
The admissible statement is therefore narrow and finite.  On the four matched panels, the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface survives against measured ribosome-efficiency readouts after the stated controls, with all nine coordinates positive in each organism and with organism-level $R^2_{QT,meas}$ ranging from $0.009394266754822262$ in \emph{E. coli} to $0.13681931343730436$ in yeast.  The result is an observed association between codon-residual geometry and a measured $RPF/mRNA$ translation-efficiency readout.  It does not by itself assert elongation mechanism, causal mediation, universal translation law, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or evolutionary direction.  Any such stronger statement requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  Protein abundance, localization, stability, structural order, and function remain separate readout layers unless their corresponding rows are joined and evaluated after the measured translation-readout step.
