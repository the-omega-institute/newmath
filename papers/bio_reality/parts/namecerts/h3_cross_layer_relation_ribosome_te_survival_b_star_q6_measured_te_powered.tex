\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{subsec:bioreality-bstar-q6-measured-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The measured ribosome-TE record is a four-organism finite matrix whose rows are matched CDS units and whose columns are the nine residual coordinates $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$.  The readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ recorded in the local ribosome files as footprint abundance divided by matched mRNA abundance.  Thus the measured translation-efficiency column is already normalized by mRNA within its source construction; it is not a raw transcript-abundance proxy and it is not a protein-abundance measurement.

The fitted panel is rank-powered in every organism in which the measured TE contact is used.  Danio rerio contributes $13140$ TE-matched genes from $14410$ reported CDS joins, with join hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 contributes $3415$ genes from $3739$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  Homo sapiens contributes $11554$ genes from $19053$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae contributes $5128$ genes from $5815$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.

\paragraph{Residualization and rank.}
For each organism, the nine-coordinate design is residualized against the same control family: CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The retained control matrix has $24$ columns, numerical rank $23$, and uses the degeneracy threshold $0.999999$ with rank tolerance $10^{-10}$.  After projecting out the controls, the $B^*_{Q6}$ matrix retains rank $9$ in every organism.  The organism-level residual degrees of freedom and TE residual energies are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{df}_{Danio} &= 13117, & E_{TE,Danio} &= 3588.378859021949,\\
\mathrm{df}_{E.coli} &= 3392, & E_{TE,E.coli} &= 919.4854026798789,\\
\mathrm{df}_{Human} &= 11531, & E_{TE,Human} &= 2052.594706241978,\\
\mathrm{df}_{Yeast} &= 5105, & E_{TE,Yeast} &= 753.5781168800304.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding $Q$-explained TE energies after the same controls are $144.76301201119642$ for Danio, $8.637891149939946$ for E. coli, $38.66659710161269$ for human, and $103.10404057290246$ for yeast.  These numbers are the finite-row certificate that the measured-TE test is not the rank-degenerate three-row boundary: the residual coordinate rank is full, the residual degrees of freedom are large, and all four fitted matrices pass the powered-rank check.

\paragraph{Measured TE survival.}
The measured-TE survival statistic is positive in all four fitted organisms.  The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(Danio) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(E.coli) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(Human) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(Yeast) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each organism has $9$ surviving coordinates at the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The strongest measured-TE survival is the yeast entry, $0.13681931343730436$, and the weakest is the E. coli entry, $0.009394266754822262$; both remain strictly positive after the declared controls.  The chapter claim is therefore ready at the bounded translation-readout level: measured ribosome-derived TE contains a detectable $B^*_{Q6}$ residual signal after the local control projection.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance.}
The measured-TE result is not by itself a protein-output or mechanism result.  A separate protein-abundance surface gives direct $Q \to P$ association after the sibling controls, including $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529807085213$ for Danio, $0.10767526677985896$ for E. coli, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, and $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast in the broader direct proteomics panel.  A more specific measured-TE conditioning run on the same four organisms reports $S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ and clipped measured mediation fraction $0.0$ for Danio, $S_{QP}=0.10941535951828074$ and mediation fraction $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$ and mediation fraction $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$ and mediation fraction $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  These entries show why the measured-TE survival statement must be kept narrower than a universal mediation statement: yeast has a large measured-TE attenuation fraction, E. coli and human have small positive fractions, and Danio has no positive clipped fraction despite positive measured-TE survival.

\paragraph{Dictionary context.}
A selection-aware readout-dictionary check on the yeast context gives $n_{join}=1800$ from $4758$ available joined rows, five folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, raw best coverage $0.2745002077400602$, selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, certified coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  This places the measured-TE result in a partial-reduction regime rather than a complete explanatory regime.  The finite surface detects translation-readout survival, but most of the yeast abundance residual remains outside the measured readout dictionary used in that check.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The admissible conclusion is a controlled finite-row association from $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates to measured ribosome-derived TE.  No claim about elongation mechanism, causal translation control, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality follows from this chapter.  No direct $Q \to P$ association is promoted to mechanism unless a separately joined and controlled translation readout both survives as $Q \to T$ and reduces the corresponding $Q \to P$ signal when conditioned upon.  The present chapter supplies the measured $Q \to T$ side of that route and records its organism-specific strength; it does not close the higher biological layers.
