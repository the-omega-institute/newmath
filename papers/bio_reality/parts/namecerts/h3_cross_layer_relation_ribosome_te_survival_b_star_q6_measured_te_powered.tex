\subsection{Rank-powered measured ribosome-TE survival for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-ribosome-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates leave a measurable trace in per-CDS ribosome-derived translation efficiency after the ordinary coding-sequence covariates have been removed.  The coordinates are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each joined CDS row, the measured translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, where $TE$ is the local ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance.  Thus the readout has already divided by mRNA at construction time; matched transcript abundance is not a downstream biological conclusion, but part of the finite readout definition.

\paragraph{Residualization design.}
For each organism the design removes length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline before testing the residual coordinates.  The control table has $24$ columns and residualized rank $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in every organism.  The nine-coordinate residual design stays full rank in all four measured-TE panels:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)&=9,\\
\tau_{rank}&=10^{-10},\\
\theta_{degenerate\ R^2}&=0.999999.
\end{aligned}
$$
The powered rows are therefore not the rank-degenerate three-organism surface that produces all-one artifacts.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $13117$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $3392$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $11531$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $5105$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding residual TE energies are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
The finite panels are organism-matched and CDS-matched.  \textit{Danio rerio} contributes $13140$ genes with measured TE out of $14410$ CDS rows, for a ribosome-TE join hit rate of $0.9118667591950035$.  \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 contributes $3415$ measured-TE genes out of $3739$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  \textit{Homo sapiens} contributes $11554$ measured-TE genes out of $19053$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  \textit{S. cerevisiae} contributes $5128$ measured-TE genes out of $5815$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  These row counts are large enough for the nine-coordinate residual matrix to be estimated after the $23$-rank control surface, rather than fitted by saturation.

\paragraph{Measured-TE survival values.}
The measured-TE survival surface is positive in every tested organism.  The controlled partial coefficient of determination for $Q\to T_{meas}$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{D. rerio})&=0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{E. coli}\ K12)&=0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{H. sapiens})&=0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\textit{S. cerevisiae})&=0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive-entry threshold is $10^{-12}$, and each panel reports all nine residual coordinates as surviving under this threshold.  The strongest measured-TE association in this finite set is yeast, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ after the same controls.  The weakest positive measured-TE association is \textit{E. coli}, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$; it is still strictly above the declared positive threshold and is estimated on $3415$ measured-TE genes.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance comparison.}
A separate PAXdb protein-abundance surface shows that direct $Q\to P$ associations exist after the same style of coding controls, but those direct associations do not by themselves explain a translation route.  The direct $Q\to P$ surface reports, for example, $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ in \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, and $0.04613529807085213$ in \textit{D. rerio}; all four have nine surviving coordinates.  The broader direct protein panel contains $15$ organisms and likewise reports full nine-coordinate survival in each organism, with values ranging from $0.019256877907572044$ in \textit{Halobacterium salinarum} to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast.  This comparison matters because a positive $Q\to P$ entry is only a protein-omics association unless a measured translation readout is explicitly inserted and tested.

\paragraph{Measured mediation readback.}
The same four measured-TE organisms also have a finite $Q\to T_{meas}\to P$ decomposition against local PAXdb protein abundance.  The three-way joins contain $13140$ rows for \textit{D. rerio}, $3415$ rows for \textit{E. coli}, $11554$ rows for human, and $5128$ rows for yeast.  On these joined rows the measured translation-conditioned fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{D. rerio})&=0.0\quad\text{from raw }-0.04801486048721136,\\
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{E. coli}\ K12)&=0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{H. sapiens})&=0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP,meas}(\textit{S. cerevisiae})&=0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The paired survival values in that decomposition are $S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ and $S_{QT,meas}=0.04034217614654355$ for \textit{D. rerio}; $S_{QP}=0.10941535951828074$ and $S_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ for \textit{E. coli}; $S_{QP}=0.04288464996165745$ and $S_{QT,meas}=0.018837911344127926$ for human; and $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$ and $S_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$ for yeast.  The yeast row is therefore the clearest measured translation-conditioned protein-abundance case in this finite table: the measured TE readout survives, the protein-abundance association survives, and conditioning through measured TE absorbs a substantial fraction of the direct association.  The human and \textit{E. coli} rows show much smaller measured fractions, and the \textit{D. rerio} row is clipped to zero because the raw fraction is suppressive.

\paragraph{Four-case interpretation.}
The appropriate readback for this chapter is a finite four-way classification rather than a biological law.  A coordinate-organism entry with positive $Q\to T_{meas}$ and no positive $Q\to P$ remains translation-readout evidence only.  A positive $Q\to P$ entry without a surviving measured translation step is direct protein-omics prediction, not a translation-mediated result.  A positive pair $Q\to T_{meas}$ and $Q\to P$ becomes a measured mediation candidate only when conditioning on $T_{meas}$ reduces the direct $Q\to P$ signal.  In the present finite data, measured-TE survival is positive in all four organisms, direct protein-abundance survival is also positive in those organisms, but the measured mediation strength differs sharply: yeast is large, \textit{E. coli} is small, human is very small, and \textit{D. rerio} is suppressive under the declared scalar.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter supports a bounded statement: the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates have rank-powered, Z-controlled association with measured ribosome-derived TE in four organism-matched CDS panels, and those rows can be compared to direct protein abundance and measured-TE conditioning when the same CDS units are joined.  It does not assert universal translation efficiency, elongation speed, causal mediation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a general biological law.  The ribosome-derived TE contact supplies the measured translation readout used here; every stronger layer would need its own matched measurement and its own finite statistic.  Protein abundance, structure, localization, stability, function, perturbation response, and evolutionary direction are therefore not inferred from the TE survival values alone.
