\subsection{Rank-powered measured translation-efficiency survival for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-te-survival}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The measured translation-efficiency test is a finite row experiment on nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The response is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ defined in the local files as ribosome footprint divided by mRNA.  Thus the measured readout has already used matched transcript abundance as its denominator, and the reported statistic asks only whether the codon residual coordinates retain finite explanatory power for measured translation efficiency after the declared controls.  The controls are CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; the residualized control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each measured organism.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank diagnostics.}
Four organism panels are nondegenerate for this test.  In zebrafish, $13{,}140$ genes with measured TE are used against $14{,}410$ reported CDS joins, giving a TE join hit rate $0.9118667591950035$; the residualized design has $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$, residual degrees of freedom $13{,}117$, and TE residual energy $3588.378859021949$.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $3{,}415$ measured-TE genes are used against $3{,}739$ reported CDS joins, hit rate $0.9133458143888741$, residual degrees of freedom $3{,}392$, and TE residual energy $919.4854026798789$.  In human, $11{,}554$ measured-TE genes are used against $19{,}053$ reported CDS joins, hit rate $0.6064136881331024$, residual degrees of freedom $11{,}531$, and TE residual energy $2052.594706241978$.  In budding yeast, $5{,}128$ measured-TE genes are used against $5{,}815$ reported CDS joins, hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, residual degrees of freedom $5{,}105$, and TE residual energy $753.5781168800304$.  All four panels have $\operatorname{rank}(X\_{\sim Z})=9$ for the nine-coordinate residual surface, with rank tolerance $10^{-10}$ and degenerate-$R^2$ threshold $0.999999$, so the row counts are not the three-organism saturated boundary.

\paragraph{Measured $S^{QT}$ values.}
The finite statistic is the controlled partial translation-readout survival score $S^{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$, read as the $R^2$ contribution of the residualized nine-coordinate block against residualized $\log_{10}(TE)$.  The measured list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2\_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{danio\ rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2\_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{E.\ coli\ K12}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2\_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2\_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each organism reports $9$ surviving coordinates at the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The largest measured TE survival in this finite row set is yeast, where $13.681931343730436\%$ of the residualized TE variation is assigned to the nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ block after the controls.  Zebrafish follows at $4.034217614654355\%$, human at $1.8837911344127926\%$, and \emph{E. coli} at $0.9394266754822262\%$.  This is enough for a bounded measured translation-readout survival statement: the finite panel contains positive $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual signal against measured ribosome-derived TE in all four rank-powered rows.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance mediation.}
A separate same-family finite run joined measured TE to PAXdb protein abundance and reported measured mediation fractions, again without causal promotion.  Its three-way row counts were $13{,}140$ for zebrafish, $3{,}415$ for \emph{E. coli}, $11{,}554$ for human, and $5{,}128$ for yeast.  The measured $S^{QP}$ values in that run were $0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$, respectively.  The corresponding raw measured mediation fractions $M^{QTP}$ were $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$, $0.0027966485036786744$, and $0.4793069656345167$; after interval clipping, zebrafish is reported as $0.0$, while \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast retain positive measured fractions.  This comparison shows why the measured-TE survival claim is narrower than a protein-output mechanism claim: positive $S^{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$ is the translation-readout leg, while protein abundance mediation requires the additional $P$ readout and an attenuation calculation.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The ready claim is therefore precise: the rank-powered measured TE panel contains a positive, controlled $B^{*}_{Q6}\to TE$ finite-row signal in zebrafish, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast, with all nine residual coordinates surviving under the stated threshold in each organism.  The strongest measured-TE row is yeast, and the weakest is \emph{E. coli}; none of the four rows is a rank-saturated all-$1.0$ artifact.  The chapter may say that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are observed to survive into measured ribosome-derived translation efficiency on these finite panels after the declared controls.  It may not say that translation efficiency is universal, that elongation speed has been isolated, that protein abundance follows mechanically, or that any folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or biological law has been established.  Those stronger statements require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
