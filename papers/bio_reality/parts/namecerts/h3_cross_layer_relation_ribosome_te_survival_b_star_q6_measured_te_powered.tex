\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-ribosome-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record tests a finite matrix whose rows are coding-sequence entries and whose columns are the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.
The response column is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ read from organism-local ribosome-profiling files as footprint divided by matched mRNA.  Thus the translation-efficiency response is already normalized by transcript abundance before the regression is formed.  The residualization covariates are the intercept, coding-sequence length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine coordinates.  Each organism keeps the same nine-coordinate surface after residualization, and every computed organism reports $rank(X_{\perp Z})=9$ and $rank(Z)=23$.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
Four organism-specific TE contacts were joined to coding-sequence codon counts.  The joined panels are not small-rank boundary artifacts: Danio rerio uses $13140$ genes from $14410$ CDS rows with hit rate $0.9118667591950035$; Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 uses $3415$ genes from $3739$ CDS rows with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; Homo sapiens uses $11554$ genes from $19053$ CDS rows with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; and Saccharomyces cerevisiae uses $5128$ genes from $5815$ CDS rows with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.
After the $Z$ controls, the residual degrees of freedom are respectively $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$.  The corresponding measured-TE residual energies are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.

\paragraph{Measured survival values.}
The computed $B^*_{Q6}\to TE$ survival values are strictly positive in all four powered panels:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(Danio\ rerio) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(E.\ coli\ K12) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(Homo\ sapiens) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(S.\ cerevisiae) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
In each case the surviving-coordinate count is $9$, using the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The strongest measured-TE survival in this finite set is yeast, where the nine-coordinate surface explains $13.681931343730436\%$ of the $Z$-residualized measured-TE energy.  The weakest is E. coli, but it remains positive at $0.9394266754822262\%$ and is computed on $3415$ matched genes rather than on a rank-degenerate panel.

\paragraph{Rank and residualization audit.}
The four panels have the same control-column count, namely $24$, with numerical rank $23$ for the control basis and rank tolerance $10^{-10}$.  The explained energies of the nine-coordinate surface on measured TE after the controls are $144.76301201119642$ in Danio rerio, $8.637891149939946$ in E. coli, $38.66659710161269$ in human, and $103.10404057290246$ in yeast.  The degenerate-$R^2$ threshold is $0.999999$, and every organism is marked powered-rank sufficient.  Consequently the positive measured-TE entries are finite-row regression outcomes over organism-matched coding sequences; they are not the all-$1.0$ shape that appears when too few rows saturate the design.

\paragraph{Downstream abundance comparison.}
The measured-TE survival result should be read together with the measured abundance decomposition, not as a stand-alone causal path.  When protein abundance is joined to the same measured-TE readout, the measured mediation fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP,meas}_{Danio\ rerio} &= 0,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{E.\ coli\ K12} &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{Homo\ sapiens} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP,meas}_{S.\ cerevisiae} &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding direct protein-abundance surfaces in that same measured-mediation run are $S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ for Danio rerio, $0.10941535951828074$ for E. coli, $0.04288464996165745$ for human, and $0.3146818503437042$ for yeast.  Thus yeast supplies the clearest finite-row instance in which a positive measured-TE coordinate surface is paired with a large reduction of the downstream abundance association after conditioning on measured TE.  Human and E. coli have positive measured-TE survival but small measured mediation fractions, and Danio rerio has a suppressive raw fraction clipped to zero.  These outcomes are reported as readout-specific decompositions, not as universal biological mechanisms.

\paragraph{Four-case discrimination and direct protein-omics contrast.}
A separate modeled-tAI four-case discriminant gives all nine yeast coordinates case $D$, while E. coli gives seven case-$D$ coordinates and two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop.  That classifier uses modeled tAI rather than ribosome-profiling TE, so it is a bounded contrast rather than an observed ribosome substitute.  The direct protein-omics survival surface is much broader: across fifteen PAXdb organism joins, $R^2_{QP}$ ranges from $0.019256877907572044$ in Halobacterium salinarum to $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving in each direct protein-abundance panel.  Direct $Q\to P$ survival therefore cannot by itself certify a translation-mediated route.  The measured-TE result supplies the needed $Q\to T$ contact; the measured-mediation fractions decide how much of a downstream $Q\to P$ association is absorbed by that contact in the organisms where all rows are joined.

\paragraph{Stability, dictionary, and dwell side conditions.}
The joint TE and mRNA-stability decomposition sharpens the readout boundary.  In yeast, the joint mediation fraction is $0.5195182225621592$, with TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$, stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$, and right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  In E. coli the joint mediation fraction is $0.2404669586626661$, with TE-only $0.01975521340963268$ and stability-only $0.23052056324898387$; in human it is $0.16890712099923358$, with TE-only $0.00549490864190343$ and stability-only $0.15314679870183337$.  The partial readout dictionary on yeast reports coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ at $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, with selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$ and unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The $f3_{stress}$ dwell analysis gives held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ over a permutation null with $95\%$ value $0.0013115362189692937$, $p\approx 7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$ for the $f3$--dwell correlation, and $n_{join}=1257$.  These side computations say that measured TE is a real finite readout, but it is not the only downstream axis carrying the residual abundance surface.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The measured ribosome-TE contact is positive and powered for all four tested organisms.  The finite claim supported here is therefore: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface survives against measured $\log_{10}(TE)$ after amino-acid composition, length, $GC3$, and $M$-density controls, with all nine coordinates present and nonzero in each powered organism-specific panel.  The strongest organism-level instance is yeast; the weakest positive instance is E. coli.  This chapter does not infer elongation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, or a universal biological law from those entries.  Translation-rate mechanism, mature protein-output mediation, structural order, physical admissibility, and functional consequence each require their own layer-matched contact and a separate finite statistic.  In the present matrix, positive $S^{QT,meas}$ means only that the codon residual coordinates retain measured-TE predictive content after the declared controls.
