\subsection{Rank-powered ribosome-TE survival for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-ribosome-te-survival}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The measured-translation-efficiency row tests a nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface against per-CDS ribosome-profiling translation efficiency.  The coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  For each organism, the response is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ taken from local ribosome-profiling files as footprint divided by mRNA.  Thus the translation readout is already normalized by the matched transcript denominator before the $Q$ coordinates are fitted.

\paragraph{Controls and rank.}
Each organism is residualized against a fixed $Z$ design consisting of length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The recorded control-column count is $24$, with $rank(Z)=23$ in all four measured-TE organisms.  The residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ design has full coordinate rank, $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, in danio, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $13117$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $3392$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $11531$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $5105$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding response residual energies are $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.  The rank tolerance is $10^{-10}$ and the degeneracy cutoff is $0.999999$, so the all-coordinate survival calls are not the rank-saturated $n=3$ boundary case.

\paragraph{Joined measured rows.}
The four finite joins are powered at the CDS level.  Danio contributes $13140$ genes with measured TE out of $14410$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  \emph{E. coli} contributes $3415$ measured-TE genes out of $3739$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  Human contributes $11554$ measured-TE genes out of $19053$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  Yeast contributes $5128$ measured-TE genes out of $5815$ reported CDS joins, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  These are finite matched rows, not organism-level summaries alone.

\paragraph{Measured $S^{QT}$ values.}
The residual survival statistic is positive in every measured-TE organism, using the registered positivity threshold $10^{-12}$.  The measured translation-efficiency survival values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\text{danio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\text{E. coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\text{human}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\text{yeast}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
In each organism the surviving-coordinate count is $9$, matching the full $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate count.  The largest measured-TE survival in this row is yeast, while the smallest is \emph{E. coli}; both remain strictly positive after the same $Z$ residualization scheme.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance comparison.}
The measured-TE result should be read together with the direct protein-abundance row, because the latter shows that $Q$-to-protein association exists but does not by itself identify a translation mechanism.  In the direct PAXdb protein-abundance surface, $R^2_{QP}$ is $0.061906245886660105$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, and remains positive across all fifteen listed organisms, with $9$ surviving coordinates in every organism.  That row is a direct protein-omics association after controls, not an explanation of how the signal reaches protein abundance.

\paragraph{Measured mediation boundary.}
A separate measured $Q\to T\to P$ row decomposes the protein-abundance association by conditioning on measured ribosome TE.  Its finite mediation fractions are organism-specific:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{danio}) &= 0,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{E. coli}) &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{human}) &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{yeast}) &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same mediation row records $S^{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ for danio, $0.10941535951828074$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04288464996165745$ for human, and $0.3146818503437042$ for yeast.  Thus the measured-TE survival row is a necessary translation-layer contact, but the amount of protein-abundance absorption by measured TE is uneven: large in yeast, modest in \emph{E. coli}, small in human, and clipped to zero in danio because the raw fitted fraction is negative.

\paragraph{Joint-readout context.}
The broader finite panel also shows why the measured-TE row should not be promoted into a single universal mechanism.  In the joint TE plus mRNA-stability decomposition, yeast has a TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$, stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$, and joint mediation fraction $0.5195182225621592$.  Human instead has TE-only fraction $0.00549490864190343$, stability-only fraction $0.15314679870183337$, and joint mediation fraction $0.16890712099923358$.  \emph{E. coli} has TE-only fraction $0.01975521340963268$, stability-only fraction $0.23052056324898387$, and joint mediation fraction $0.2404669586626661$.  The reported right-tail permutation probability is $0.000999000999000999$ in each of those three organisms.  A dictionary-style partial-mediation certificate on the yeast context uses $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $120$ permutations, $n_{join}=1800$, and gives selected-readout coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  The yeast $f3\_stress$ dwell row further records $f3$--dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$, approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$, and held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ against a null $95\%$ level of $0.0013115362189692937$.  These nearby rows support a layered finite readout dictionary rather than a one-step causal story.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The row therefore has the following finite interpretation.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured ribosome-profiling TE after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls in four rank-powered organism panels.  This is a positive translation-readout association, because the response is $\log_{10}(\text{footprint}/\text{mRNA})$ and the joined CDS counts are large enough to avoid the rank-degenerate refusal boundary.  The statement is not that $B^*_{Q6}$ determines translation in general; it is that this finite panel contains a nonzero controlled $Q\to T$ surface.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No stronger biological conclusion is licensed by this row alone.  A positive $S^{QT,meas}$ entry is not a claim of universal translation efficiency, elongation speed, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causation, or a general biological law.  A direct $Q\to P$ association may be reported only as a protein-omics association unless a measured translation readout survives and conditioning on that readout reduces the $Q\to P$ signal.  Folding, physical admissibility, function, localization, turnover mechanism, and universality require their own layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
