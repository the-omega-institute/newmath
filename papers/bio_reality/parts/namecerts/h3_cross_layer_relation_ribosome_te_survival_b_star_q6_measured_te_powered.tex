\subsection{Rank-powered measured-TE survival of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-measured-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the nine-dimensional synonymous residual surface $B^*_{Q6}$ has a nonzero measured translation-efficiency readback after ordinary coding-sequence covariates have been removed.  The finite rows are coding sequences whose codon counts are joined to organism-local ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency files.  The measured readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$, with $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{footprint}/\mathrm{mRNA}$ in the local files, so the transcript denominator is already part of the readout construction.  The tested residual coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\; Arg\_AGR,\; Ile\_AUA,\; Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\; Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\; Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\; Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\; f3\_stress .
\end{aligned}
$$
For each organism the design residualizes both the $Q$ coordinates and the measured-TE target against a 24-column control design of rank $23$: intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the support of the nine coordinates.  The $Q$ residual design has rank $9$ in every reported organism, so the positive entries are not the rank-degenerate all-ones boundary rejected by the rank-refusal rule.

\paragraph{Joined measured-TE rows.}
The measured TE join is rank-powered in four organisms.  Danio rerio contributes $13{,}140$ TE-matched genes from $14{,}410$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.9118667591950035$.  Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 contributes $3{,}415$ TE-matched genes from $3{,}739$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  Homo sapiens contributes $11{,}554$ TE-matched genes from $19{,}053$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae contributes $5{,}128$ TE-matched genes from $5{,}815$ CDS rows, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  The lower human hit rate is an availability limit of the TE study, not a negative biological result.

\paragraph{Residual rank and target energy.}
After control residualization the remaining degrees of freedom and TE residual energies are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{danio}:&\quad df=13117,\quad \|T_Z\|^2=3588.378859021949,\quad E_Q=144.76301201119642,\\
\mathrm{E. coli}:&\quad df=3392,\quad \|T_Z\|^2=919.4854026798789,\quad E_Q=8.637891149939946,\\
\mathrm{human}:&\quad df=11531,\quad \|T_Z\|^2=2052.594706241978,\quad E_Q=38.66659710161269,\\
\mathrm{yeast}:&\quad df=5105,\quad \|T_Z\|^2=753.5781168800304,\quad E_Q=103.10404057290246 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive threshold is $10^{-12}$ for partial $R^2$, and every organism has the full nine surviving coordinates above that threshold.  The measured survival values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{danio})&=0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{E. coli})&=0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{human})&=0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}(\mathrm{yeast})&=0.13681931343730436 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row test supports measured translation-readout survival for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface in all four joined organisms, with yeast giving the largest measured-TE association and E. coli the smallest but still positive association.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance mediation is a separate statistic.}
The TE survival result above is not by itself a protein-output or mechanism claim.  A separate measured-TE to abundance decomposition joined the same kind of codon residuals, measured TE, and PAXdb abundance.  In that statistic the measured mediated fractions were $0.0$ in danio after truncating a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast.  The corresponding direct abundance survival values in that decomposition were $S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652$ for danio, $0.10941535951828074$ for E. coli, $0.04288464996165745$ for human, and $0.3146818503437042$ for yeast.  This shows that measured TE can absorb a substantial part of the codon-residual to abundance association in yeast, a small part in E. coli and human, and none in danio under this scalar decomposition.  It does not turn the measured-TE survival table into a causal pathway.

\paragraph{Joint readout context.}
A third finite statistic conditions codon-residual to abundance association on both measured TE and mRNA stability in yeast, E. coli, and human.  Its single and joint residual $R^2$ values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{E. coli}:&\quad R^2_{QP}=0.1181743523064932,\; R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.11583979275713331,\; R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.09093273405121653,\; R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036,\\
\mathrm{human}:&\quad R^2_{QP}=0.04274178488511215,\; R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04250692268197657,\; R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.036196017359154814,\; R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.0355223930537993,\\
\mathrm{yeast}:&\quad R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803,\; R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314,\; R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165,\; R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$ for E. coli, $0.16890712099923358$ for human, and $0.5195182225621592$ for yeast, each with deterministic pair-permutation right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$.  The TE-only fractions in the same table are $0.01975521340963268$, $0.00549490864190343$, and $0.4697188319693239$, respectively; the stability-only fractions are $0.23052056324898387$, $0.15314679870183337$, and $0.0626618595344251$.  This places the measured-TE survival result inside a broader variance-partition picture: yeast has strong TE-linked abundance attenuation, while E. coli and human show larger mRNA-stability fractions than TE fractions in that particular joined panel.

\paragraph{Dictionary compression boundary.}
A yeast readout-dictionary test gives a compactness check on the residual abundance signal.  On an available join of $4{,}758$ rows, the powered run used $n=1800$, five folds, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $\rho_{join}=0.05$.  Its selected readout family had coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ against a selection-aware null95 value $0.029149007242956115$; the raw best coverage over the candidate family was $0.2745002077400602$, and the unexplained fraction remained $0.7546178856026946$.  The selected family was the three-readout set $\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{turnover}\}$.  This confirms partial reducibility of the yeast abundance residual to measured readouts, while preserving a large residual component outside those readouts.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The measured-TE survival claim is supported: four organism-matched panels have nondegenerate rank, full nine-coordinate survival, and positive $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$ after amino-acid composition, length, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  The strongest finite signal is yeast, with $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$ and a measured-TE mediated abundance fraction $0.4793069656345167$ in the separate abundance decomposition.  The weakest measured-TE survival is E. coli, with $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.009394266754822262$, still above the declared positive threshold and with all nine coordinates surviving.

\paragraph{Scope of interpretation.}
These rows show codon-residual association with a measured translation-efficiency readout.  They do not show a universal translation law, do not identify elongation speed or initiation as the operative variable, and do not establish causation, perturbation rescue, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or evolutionary direction.  Protein abundance mediation, mRNA-stability attenuation, turnover, localization, structure, and function each require their own layer-matched finite rows and controls.  A direct $q_i\to P_k$ association remains only a downstream association unless the corresponding measured $q_i\to T_j$ step survives and conditioning on $T_j$ reduces the direct abundance signal in the same controlled panel.
