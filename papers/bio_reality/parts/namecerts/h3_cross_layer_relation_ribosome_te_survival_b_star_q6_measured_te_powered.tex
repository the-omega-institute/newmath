\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-ribosome-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns the finite matrix whose rows are coding sequences with joined codon counts and measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and whose columns are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The measured readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ defined in the local files as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA. Thus the readout is already mRNA-normalized before the codon residual fit is evaluated. Each organism fit removes length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline before asking whether the residual coordinate block retains positive explanatory power for the measured TE readout.

\paragraph{Rank and row admissibility.}
Four organism panels are rank-powered rather than three-organism saturated artifacts. Danio rerio joins $13140$ measured-TE genes against $14410$ reported CDS rows, giving hit rate $0.9118667591950035$; Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 joins $3415$ genes against $3739$ CDS rows, hit rate $0.9133458143888741$; Homo sapiens joins $11554$ genes against $19053$ CDS rows, hit rate $0.6064136881331024$; Saccharomyces cerevisiae joins $5128$ genes against $5815$ CDS rows, hit rate $0.8818572656921754$. In all four cases the residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$ with $9$ coordinates, while the control design has rank $23$ from $24$ columns. The residual degrees of freedom after controls are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$, respectively, so no all-one fitted matrix or rank-degenerate boundary is being promoted as biological survival.

\paragraph{Measured survival values.}
The controlled measured-TE survival statistic is positive in every powered organism panel. The finite values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{E.\ coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Human}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Yeast}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each panel reports $9$ surviving coordinates at positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$. The strongest measured-TE survival in this finite set is yeast, while the weakest is E. coli. The values should be read as per-gene codon-residual associations with a measured TE surface after the stated controls, not as a universal law of translation efficiency.

\paragraph{Connection to protein abundance.}
A separate measured protein-abundance mediation fit uses the same nine-coordinate residual family and conditions the $Q\to P$ association on measured ribosome TE. Its measured mediated fractions are $0$ for Danio rerio after clipping a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast. The corresponding direct abundance survivals in that same measured-TE mediation panel are $S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$. This makes the measured-TE result biologically sharper in yeast than in the other panels: yeast has both the largest $R^2_{QT,meas}$ and the largest measured TE-conditioned abundance attenuation. The other organisms still supply positive measured-TE survival, but their abundance mediation fractions are small or null on this finite readout.

\paragraph{Stability and dictionary context.}
The same cross-layer surface was also decomposed against measured TE and mRNA stability where all three available organisms had computed four-way joins: $2517$ E. coli rows, $11322$ human rows, and $4759$ yeast rows. The joint mediation fractions were $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$ in each organism. In yeast, a separate readout-dictionary certificate on $1800$ joined rows used $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$ and found selection-aware coverage $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ above selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. These numbers support a partial-reduction picture: measured TE and allied readouts carry real finite signal, but they do not exhaust the abundance residual.

\paragraph{The $f_3$-stress subcoordinate.}
For the $f_3$-stress component in yeast, the dwell mediation panel used $1257$ joined rows and found held-out $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ against a permutation null $95\%$ value $0.0013115362189692937$, with $f_3$-dwell correlation $0.1615441450901653$ and approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. Its positional refinement identified the leading segment as $5'$ and passed the five-prime ramp gate while the three-prime body gate did not pass. A separate structural-order ramp panel classified the structural readout as \emph{ramp-positive but not localized}: weighted ramp increment $0.0011165640103326878$, weighted body increment $-0.00026157313923957926$, and paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip value $0.05859375$. This places the positional observation below a full structural-localization claim.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is ready for a measured translation-readout claim: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block survives against measured ribosome TE in four rank-powered organism panels after the declared sequence and composition controls. The statement is deliberately narrower than a mechanism claim. Positive $Q\to TE$ survival does not by itself establish translation causation, elongation speed, protein abundance realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality. Any such assertion needs its own layer-matched measurement surface and its own controlled statistic. Direct $Q\to P$ survival is therefore only direct protein-omics prediction unless a measured $Q\to T\to P$ conditioning step reduces the direct abundance signal on the same joined rows.
