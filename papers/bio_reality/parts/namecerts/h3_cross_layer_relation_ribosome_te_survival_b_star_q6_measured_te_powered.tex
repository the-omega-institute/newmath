\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-ribosome-te-survival}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The finite object tested here is a row-matched matrix whose columns are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and whose readout is measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-}stress$.  For each coding sequence row, the translation readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ defined in the local ribosome files as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance.  Thus the tested readout is already normalized by transcript abundance before the regression is performed.

\paragraph{Control geometry and rank sufficiency.}
Each organism-specific design residualizes both the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate matrix and the measured TE vector against a shared control block $Z$: intercept, coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The control block has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every measured-TE organism.  After this residualization the $B^*_{Q6}$ block keeps full residual rank $9$, so the positive entries below are not the all-saturated rank-degenerate boundary.  The powered residual degrees of freedom are $13117$ for zebrafish, $3392$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11531$ for human, and $5105$ for budding yeast.  The residual TE energies after control removal are respectively $3588.378859021949$, $919.4854026798789$, $2052.594706241978$, and $753.5781168800304$.

\paragraph{Matched ribosome-TE rows.}
The row joins are large enough for a finite powered readback rather than a three-organism or low-rank artifact.  Zebrafish contributes $13140$ genes with measured TE out of $14410$ reported CDS joins, for a TE join hit rate of $0.9118667591950035$.  \emph{E. coli} contributes $3415$ TE genes out of $3739$ CDS joins, with hit rate $0.9133458143888741$.  Human contributes $11554$ TE genes out of $19053$ CDS joins, with hit rate $0.6064136881331024$.  Budding yeast contributes $5128$ TE genes out of $5815$ CDS joins, with hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  These are same-organism codon and measured-TE joins, not merely contact names or modelled codon-adaptation weights.

\paragraph{Measured survival values.}
Let $X_Q$ denote the residualized nine-column $B^*_{Q6}$ matrix and let $T$ denote the residualized $\log_{10}(TE)$ vector.  The measured survival statistic is the controlled partial coefficient of determination for $T$ against $X_Q$, with a positivity threshold of $10^{-12}$.  The four organism rows give
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{E.\ coli\ K12}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
All four organisms report $9$ surviving coordinates.  The strongest measured-TE surface is yeast, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.13681931343730436$; the weakest among these powered rows is \emph{E. coli}, with $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$.  Since every value is strictly above $10^{-12}$ and every residualized coordinate block has rank $9$, the finite row verdict for the measured-TE layer is positive.

\paragraph{Relation to protein abundance mediation.}
The same measured-TE readout was also used in a downstream protein-abundance decomposition over zebrafish, \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast.  In that three-way codon--TE--protein join, the measured mediated fractions were $0.0$ for zebrafish, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  These values show why measured TE survival and protein mediation must be kept distinct.  Positive $S^{QT,meas}$ says that the codon residual surface predicts measured TE after the stated controls; it does not by itself say that TE carries the protein-abundance association.  In yeast the measured-TE mediation fraction is large, while in zebrafish the measured mediation fit is clipped to $0.0$ despite positive $S^{QT,meas}=0.04034217614654355$.

\paragraph{Relation to four-case classification and stability mediation.}
A separate four-case discriminant over modelled tAI and measured abundance classified the tested \emph{E. coli} coordinates as $7$ case-D coordinates plus $2$ positive-positive entries without mediation drop, and the yeast coordinates as $9$ case-D coordinates.  That modelled-readout result is not substituted for the measured ribosome-TE result above; it is a comparison surface.  In the joint measured-TE and mRNA-stability decomposition, the organism count was $3$, with direct $R^2_{QP}$ values $0.1181743523064932$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.04274178488511215$ for human, and $0.2715520808117803$ for yeast.  The corresponding joint mediation fractions were $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, each with right-tail permutation value $0.000999000999000999$.  These numbers support a bounded variance-partition reading of downstream abundance, not a causal pathway claim.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The measured ribosome-TE layer is ready: the finite surface contains four powered same-organism TE joins, full residual $B^*_{Q6}$ rank, and positive controlled $R^2_{QT,meas}$ in every tested organism.  The appropriate readback is narrow.  It is a codon-residual association with measured ribosome-profiling TE after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  It is not a claim of universal translation efficiency, elongation mechanism, protein abundance realization, folded structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causality, or a general biological law.  Any such higher-layer conclusion requires a separate row-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
