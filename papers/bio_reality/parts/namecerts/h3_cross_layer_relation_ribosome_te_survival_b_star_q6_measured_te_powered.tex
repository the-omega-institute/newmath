\subsection{Measured ribosome-TE survival for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-measured-ribosome-te-survival}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The finite object tested here is a per-CDS matrix whose columns are the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates and whose response is a measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readout.  The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$, with $TE$ defined in the local ribosome-profiling files as ribosome footprint divided by matched mRNA abundance.  Thus the translation readout is already normalized by transcript abundance at the source level, while the regression design separately residualizes against CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.

\paragraph{Rank and join audit.}
Four organisms have same-row codon and measured-TE joins for this test.  The joined panels contain 13,140 genes for \emph{Danio rerio}, 3,415 genes for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, 11,554 genes for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and 5,128 genes for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The corresponding ribosome-TE join hit rates are $0.9118667591950035$, $0.9133458143888741$, $0.6064136881331024$, and $0.8818572656921754$.  In every organism the residualized $Q$ design has rank $9$ against a control rank of $23$ from $24$ control columns, so the matrix is not a rank-degenerate all-fit surface.  The residual degrees of freedom after the control projection are 13,117, 3,392, 11,531, and 5,105 respectively, with rank tolerance $10^{-10}$ and degenerate-$R^2$ threshold $0.999999$.

\paragraph{Measured translation survival.}
The powered measured-TE survival statistic is positive in all four joined panels.  The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{D. rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{E. coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{H. sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold used by the finite-row calculation is $10^{-12}$, and each organism reports all nine residual coordinates as surviving coordinates against measured TE.  The largest measured-TE survival occurs in yeast, where the residualized codon surface explains about $13.68\%$ of the measured-TE residual energy after the stated controls.  The smallest positive case is \emph{E. coli}, where the same controlled design still gives $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394266754822262$ on 3,415 joined genes.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance comparison.}
The measured-TE result is a translation-readout result, not by itself a protein-abundance mechanism.  A separate measured-protein mediation calculation joins the same kind of $Q$ residual surface to protein abundance and measured TE.  In that calculation the measured mediated fractions are $0$ for \emph{D. rerio}, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for \emph{H. sapiens}, and $0.4793069656345167$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  The corresponding direct codon-to-protein survival scores in the same measured-mediation calculation are $S_{QP}=0.04926578454614652$, $0.10941535951828074$, $0.04288464996165745$, and $0.3146818503437042$, while the measured translation scores are $S_{QT,meas}=0.04034217614654355$, $0.009394266754822262$, $0.018837911344127926$, and $0.13681931343730436$.  This separates the robust claim, namely survival of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates against measured TE, from the stronger and organism-dependent statement that measured TE absorbs a protein-abundance association.

\paragraph{Direct protein-omics boundary.}
A broader protein-omics pass reports direct $Q \to P$ associations across fifteen organisms after length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  Examples include $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse, $0.0646471273126996$ in \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, and $0.29837624994140827$ in yeast, again with nine surviving coordinates in each listed organism.  Those direct protein-omics numbers are useful downstream readouts, but they are not substitutes for the measured-TE contact.  A direct $Q \to P$ association may reflect translation, mRNA abundance, turnover, localization, condition, selection, measurement platform, or other covariates unless a measured translation readout is explicitly joined and conditioned on.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
For this chapter the finite-row classification is translation-surviving at the measured-TE layer.  It is not translation-null, because every powered measured-TE panel has positive $R^2_{QT,meas}$ above the declared threshold and full residual rank for the nine-coordinate $Q$ surface.  It is not a universal translation law, because the four organisms are condition-specific ribosome-profiling panels and the observed magnitudes differ substantially across taxa and source studies.  It is not a protein mechanism claim by itself, because protein mediation requires the separate $Q \to T \to P$ join and attenuation calculation.  Yeast is the strongest measured-mediation case in the available finite rows, with mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$; human and \emph{E. coli} are much smaller, and \emph{D. rerio} is clipped to zero in the measured-mediation scalar.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
The allowed conclusion is narrow: the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates survive against measured ribosome-profiling TE in four rank-powered, same-row panels after amino-acid composition, CDS length, GC3, and $M$-density controls.  No translation mechanism, elongation mechanism, protein folding statement, physical admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, phenotype statement, causal statement, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter alone.  Protein abundance, localization, stability, structural order, and function each require their own layer-matched contact and their own controlled finite-row statistic.  The measured-TE contact can carry a bounded translation-readout claim; it cannot promote codon residual geometry into protein output, structure, function, or mechanism without the corresponding joined measurements.
