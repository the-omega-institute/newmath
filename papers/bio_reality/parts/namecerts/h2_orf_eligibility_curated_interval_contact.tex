\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record isolates the layer named \texttt{orf\_eligibility}.  The biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by start--stop codon interval enumeration, not by a translation assay, a folded-protein observation, a physical-admissibility model, a functional annotation, or a general mechanism.  The finite claim is therefore deliberately narrow: after a separately supplied curated interval contact is attached, the geometry-only classifier may be evaluated against that contact, while all stronger biological readings remain outside the conclusion.  The run reports scope \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}; hence the observed interval information is a curated biological input to the test and is not kernel content of the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
The contact run loaded \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json} with $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The required input classes were all present: \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}.  The protocol was frozen before the external observation: the protocol time was $2026$-$05$-$29$T$19{:}11{:}13$+00:00, whereas the external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30$T$10{:}29{:}31$+00:00.  The report states that the classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were fixed before that observation, and that the analysis used no translation or structure input.

\paragraph{Classifier and controls.}
The classifier identifier was \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified during the contact run.  The earlier start--stop enumeration check had one external nucleotide window and classified that one window, with the source described as a curated finite nucleotide-window manifest rather than a fact derived from the codon geometry alone.  In the larger curated interval contact, all $72$ positive curated intervals were accepted, giving accept rate $1.0$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail probability $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched non-ORF decoys were all rejected: $72$ of $72$, reject rate $1.0$, with the same one-sided probability $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison gave $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, again with one-sided probability $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The boundary cases were not promoted: $24$ boundary cases were tested, $0$ were promoted, and the promoted fraction was $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The split-stability check used three held-out folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$.  The same counts and rates held for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  Thus the finite evidence is not merely a single aggregate count: each of the three predeclared held-out locus folds preserved the positive acceptance and matched-decoy rejection behavior.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The contact is sufficient for the following restricted statement: within the $168$-row curated RefSeq interval dataset and the frozen start--stop-window classifier, the positive curated ORF intervals and the matched non-ORF decoys separate perfectly under the recorded controls.  This establishes an ORF-eligibility contact for this dataset-level task only.  It does not establish translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, species-wide law, or universality.  The matched decoy and boundary controls also do not classify windows outside the recorded dataset.  The self-check remains consistent with this limitation: it records a BioReality self-check with $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
