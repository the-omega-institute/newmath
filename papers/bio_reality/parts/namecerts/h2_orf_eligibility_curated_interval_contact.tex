\subsection{Curated Interval Contact for ORF Eligibility}
\label{subsec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Object and scope.}
This record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by a curated interval contact. The biological object is not protein production, molecular folding, energetic feasibility, cellular role, or evolutionary conservation. It is the narrower predicate that a finite nucleotide interval is accepted by a frozen start--stop window classifier when the interval is supplied by an external curated annotation source. The contact is therefore a reality-facing test of interval eligibility only. It does not turn codon-window geometry into translation, structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

The external contact used here is a finite NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS-interval extraction from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30. The loaded dataset path is \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}, and the row count is $168$. The declared scope is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}. The required input classes are all present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs.

The predeclared paired baseline comparison records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, again with upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.

The boundary-class cap is also finite and explicit: $24$ boundary cases were inspected, $0$ were promoted, and the promoted fraction was $0.0$. Thus the contact does not merely report successful positive classification; it also records that the packet did not use ambiguous boundary material as additional biological support.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The contact supports an ORF-eligibility statement for this finite curated interval dataset: under the frozen geometry-only classifier, the $72$ positive curated intervals are accepted, the $72$ matched decoys are rejected, the $24$ boundary cases are not promoted, and all three held-out splits pass with positive accept rate and decoy reject rate equal to $1.0$. The conclusion is local to the 2026-05-30 RefSeq-derived finite table and to the declared classifier. It does not assert that the accepted intervals are translated in vivo, that their products fold, that they are physically admissible as molecules, that they have biological function, that the classifier explains a biochemical mechanism, or that the behavior extends beyond the curated dataset. Any such stronger biological layer would require a separate contact capable of testing that stronger layer directly.
