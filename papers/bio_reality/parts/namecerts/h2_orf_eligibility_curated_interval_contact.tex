\paragraph{Biological object.}
This record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by start and stop codon enumeration against a curated interval contact. The biological object is not a translated protein and not a folded molecule. It is the narrower question of whether a finite nucleotide interval, already supplied by a curated biological annotation source, is accepted by a frozen start--stop classifier while matched non-ORF decoys and boundary cases are rejected or left unpromoted. The scope is therefore external ORF interval contact only. The contact used here is the NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval annotation for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within this finite row set, the curated interval contact supports the following narrow assertion: a frozen geometry-only start--stop classifier separates the supplied curated ORF intervals from matched non-ORF decoys, remains stable across three held-out locus folds, and does not promote boundary controls. The result is a contact-dependent eligibility statement for the supplied interval dataset, not a claim about every possible nucleotide window. In particular, no translation event, protein folding fact, physical admissibility claim, molecular mechanism, biological function, or universal genomic law follows from these rows. Any such stronger assertion would require a different contact carrying the relevant biological evidence.
