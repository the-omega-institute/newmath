\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF-boundary eligibility}
\label{subsec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Question isolated by the record.}
This record concerns a narrow biological layer: whether a finite start--stop codon enumeration can be treated as an eligibility test for an open-reading-frame interval after contact with curated sequence annotation. The underlying geometric packet by itself is not an observed transcript, not a translated peptide, and not a protein-level object. Its role is to define a finite classifier whose only biological question is interval eligibility under an attached curated annotation source. The contact used here is a RefSeq GenBank XML CDS-interval manifest for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30. The protocol was fixed before the annotation evidence was read: the protocol timestamp is 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the external observation timestamp is 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00. The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the audit records that its rules were not modified after the evidence was observed.

\paragraph{Contact dataset and input classes.}
The curated interval dataset contains $168$ rows loaded from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}. The required input classes are all present: \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}. The row count decomposes into $72$ positive curated ORF intervals, $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, and $24$ boundary cases. This is a contact-level test, not a genome-wide biological law: the recorded scope is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}. A separate earlier start--stop-window check used one input window and classified one window, but that earlier result remained only start/stop enumeration over an external manifest and did not supply translation or structure information.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
The result established here is only that the frozen start/stop-window eligibility classifier separates the recorded positive curated intervals from the matched non-ORF decoys in the $168$-row contact dataset, with the boundary rows left unpromoted. It does not establish translation, ribosomal realization, folded protein structure, energetic admissibility, molecular mechanism, organismal function, or universality across genomes. The RefSeq annotation remains a curated biological input rather than content generated by the combinatorial kernel. Any promotion to translation, folding, physical admissibility, functional annotation, mechanistic explanation, or global biological law would require a distinct contact carrying data appropriate to that higher layer.
