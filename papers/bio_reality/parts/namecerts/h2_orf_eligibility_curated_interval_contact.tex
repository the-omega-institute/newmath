\subsection{Curated interval contact for open-reading-frame eligibility}
\label{sec:bior-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Layer addressed.}
This record concerns the layer named \emph{open-reading-frame eligibility}: the finite question of whether an externally supplied nucleotide interval is accepted by a start--stop enumeration test.  The biological object is not protein production and not molecular action.  It is only the eligibility of a curated interval under a rule that looks for a start codon, a compatible terminal stop codon, and the corresponding finite window structure.  The contact used here is an external curated annotation source: NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals from \emph{Escherichia coli} K--12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.

\paragraph{Finite contact table.}
The curated interval table contains 168 rows at \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  Its required input classes are exactly the three classes \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}.  The tested scope is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}.  The classifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were fixed before the external observation, and the analysis records that no translation or protein-structure input was used.  The protocol time stamps are concrete: the rule package was fixed at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the external evidence was observed at 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00.

\paragraph{Primary finite result.}
Among the curated positives, $72$ of $72$ intervals were accepted by the enumeration classifier, for accept rate $1.0$.  The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value recorded for this positive interval statistic is $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Among the matched non-ORF decoys, $72$ of $72$ were rejected, again with rate $1.0$ and the same exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired comparison gives $72$ wins in $72$ positive--decoy pairs, hence win rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the contact.}
This contact upgrades the ORF-eligibility packet from a mere start--stop boundary question to an externally checked interval-eligibility record for the named RefSeq snapshot.  The inference is deliberately narrow.  The data show that the fixed enumeration rule accepts the curated CDS intervals in this finite table, rejects the matched non-ORF decoys, keeps boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable across the three recorded held-out locus folds.  The result is therefore a finite contact between a codon-window eligibility rule and curated biological annotation.

\paragraph{Local limit of the claim.}
Nothing in this record says that any accepted interval is translated in a cell, folds into a protein, has physically admissible dynamics, carries a biological function, implements a mechanism, or supports a universal biological law.  The coordinate facts for $R$, $M$, $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$, $\pi_M$, and the quotient $f$-vectors are finite combinatorial data; the RefSeq table is an external curated biological contact.  The two are joined only for this layer and this dataset.  Any claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require another contact with evidence appropriate to that layer.
