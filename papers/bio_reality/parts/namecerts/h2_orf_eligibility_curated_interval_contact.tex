\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}\label{sec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by a curated interval contact, not by codon geometry alone. The biological object is the finite decision problem obtained from start and stop codon enumeration once a separate annotated nucleotide interval is supplied. The claim is therefore deliberately narrow: the packet may say that the supplied interval passes the start--stop eligibility classifier under the recorded protocol, and it may compare that behavior with matched non-ORF decoys and boundary cases. It may not infer translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law. Those stronger assertions require their own contacts and their own tests.

\paragraph{Start--stop contact predecessor.}
The predecessor start--stop-window row loaded one external window dataset with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$. Its scope was external-window start--stop enumeration only, with $1$ input window and $1$ classified window. The row explicitly labels the result as $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$, which is the correct layer for the present contact. Thus the finite classifier may decide whether a supplied window has the required start--stop form, but the existence and biological standing of the interval must come from the curated annotation row.

\paragraph{Curated interval data.}
The decisive contact row loaded $168$ rows from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}. The source is NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval annotation for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$. The required input classes are present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs. The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; the row records that its rules were not modified during the analysis, and that the analysis used no translation or structure input.

\paragraph{Positive and decoy statistics.}
Among positive curated intervals, $72$ out of $72$ were accepted, giving accept rate $1.0$. The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail probability recorded for this positive interval statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Among matched non-ORF decoys, $72$ out of $72$ were rejected, giving rejection rate $1.0$, with the same recorded one-sided binomial upper-tail probability
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired comparison likewise reports $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, and binomial upper-tail probability $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. These numbers support a finite interval-contact result: on this curated dataset and its matched controls, the start--stop eligibility classifier separates positives from decoys exactly.

\paragraph{Held-out split stability.}
The held-out stability check used $3$ splits. In the left-locus split, $24$ positives had accept rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys had rejection rate $1.0$. The middle-locus split had the same $24$ positive and $24$ decoy counts, again with accept and rejection rates $1.0$. The right-locus split also had $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, with both rates $1.0$. All three splits passed. The boundary-control row reports $24$ boundary cases, $0$ promotions, and promoted fraction $0.0$. This prevents the curated-interval result from spilling into windows outside the declared contact class.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The self-check row records $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. The present contact therefore has the following precise meaning. A curated interval annotation from the stated RefSeq source supplies the missing biological interval; the fixed start--stop classifier then accepts all $72$ positive curated intervals, rejects all $72$ matched decoys, leaves all $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable across $3$ held-out locus splits. That is an ORF-eligibility contact result for this finite row set. It is not a translation assay, not a folding assay, not a physical admissibility proof, not a functional annotation, not a mechanistic model, and not a claim of universality beyond the recorded dataset.
