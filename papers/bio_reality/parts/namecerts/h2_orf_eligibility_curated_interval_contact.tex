\subsection{Curated Interval Contact for ORF Eligibility}
\label{subsec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as a finite contact problem rather than as a claim about translation or biological function.  The biological object is the eligibility of nucleotide intervals under start and stop codon enumeration.  The observed contact is a curated interval manifest derived from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS annotations for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  The verified scope is only \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}.  Within that scope, the dataset has 168 rows and contains exactly the required input classes \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.  The classifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were held fixed before the curated intervals were observed, and the analysis used no translation or protein-structure input.

\paragraph{Finite codon substrate.}
The finite codon data used by the contact packet remain explicit.  The motif-compressed set $R$ is
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$,
with no missing and no extra entries relative to the recorded motif union.  The larger finite set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The $f_3$ saturation check records
$\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$ and an empty $f_3$ boundary.  The quotient geometry associated to $M$ has $f$-vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, while the recorded quotient has $f$-vector $(10,14,6,1)$ and quotient size $10$.  These finite values specify the codon-level enumeration substrate; by themselves they do not constitute a biological interval observation.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The contact step attaches curated CDS intervals to the start/stop-window classifier.  The earlier window enumeration check loaded one external nucleotide-window manifest, classified one input window, and explicitly marked the scope as external start/stop enumeration only.  The present curated-interval contact is stronger: it loads 168 curated rows, compares positive intervals with matched non-ORF decoys, retains boundary cases as non-claims, and checks stability across three held-out locus folds.  The contact is therefore not merely an enumeration of possible codon windows; it is a finite comparison against curated biological interval annotations.

\paragraph{Statistical certificate.}
Among $72$ positive curated ORF intervals, all $72$ were accepted by the classifier, giving accept rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Among $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, all $72$ were rejected, giving reject rate $1.0$ with the same exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired comparison also records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, hence win rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The three held-out splits were \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold}, and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  Each split had $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, positive accept rate $1.0$, decoy reject rate $1.0$, and passed the split criterion.  The boundary set contained $24$ cases, of which $0$ were elevated to ORF claims, so the boundary elevation fraction was $0.0$.

\paragraph{Layer boundary.}
The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite evidence supports the statement that, for this curated interval dataset and this fixed start/stop-window classifier, positive curated ORF intervals and matched non-ORF decoys are separated exactly in the recorded rows, with stable behavior on the three listed held-out folds and with no boundary-case elevation.  It does not show that any interval is translated into protein, that a folded molecular structure exists, that a physical admissibility criterion is satisfied, that a biological function is present, that a mechanism has been identified, or that a universal biological law has been obtained.  The RefSeq annotation remains a curated biological contact supplied from outside the codon geometry.  Any later claim about translation, folding, physical realization, function, mechanism, or generality would require a separate contact suited to that layer.

\paragraph{Self-check.}
The self-check records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is carried across biological layers by the BioReality check itself.  Thus the packet is accepted only as an ORF-eligibility contact for the 168-row curated interval dataset, not as a promotion from finite codon enumeration to higher biological content.
