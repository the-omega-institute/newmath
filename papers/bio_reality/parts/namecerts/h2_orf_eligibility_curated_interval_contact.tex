\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}\label{subsec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This record concerns the narrow biological layer called \emph{ORF eligibility}: whether a finite nucleotide interval supplied by an outside curated annotation passes a start--stop-window eligibility classifier.  The object is not a translated protein, not a folded molecule, not a physical admissibility assertion, and not a functional biological claim.  The only promoted statement is that a predeclared start--stop enumeration rule can distinguish curated coding intervals from matched non-ORF decoys inside the finite dataset described below.  The layer is therefore local to interval eligibility, with all stronger biological readings kept out of scope unless a further contact supplies the corresponding observation.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The contact used here is a curated interval table derived from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}.  The snapshot date is $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The loaded table has $168$ rows at \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  Its required classes are exactly the three finite input types needed by the test: \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}.  The scope string is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}, which is read literally: the curated annotation is an observed biological input to the experiment, while the codon geometry remains a symbolic classifier input.

\paragraph{Protocol separation.}
The protocol records that the classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were fixed before the curated evidence was observed.  The freeze time was $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, while the external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified after observation.  The analysis explicitly used no translation or protein-structure input.  Thus the contact tests the predeclared interval-eligibility rule rather than fitting a rule to the observed annotation table.

\paragraph{Finite acceptance and rejection counts.}
The positive curated intervals give $72$ accepts out of $72$, so the positive accept rate is $1.0$.  The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value recorded for this positive statistic is $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched non-ORF decoys give $72$ rejections out of $72$, again with rate $1.0$ and the same exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, with win rate $1.0$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  In compact form, the three principal finite tests are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{positive accepts} &= 72/72,\\
\text{matched decoy rejections} &= 72/72,\\
\text{paired baseline wins} &= 72/72.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary cases add a non-promotion check: $24$ boundary rows were examined, $0$ were promoted, and the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out split stability.}
The held-out split audit contains three folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$.  The same counts and rates hold for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  All three splits passed.  The split check is therefore not a single aggregate coincidence: it repeats the same $24$-positive and $24$-decoy behavior across left, middle, and right locus partitions of the finite contact table.

\paragraph{Local mathematical verdict.}
For this finite contact, the start--stop-window eligibility rule has a clean interval-level separation: all curated positive intervals are accepted, all matched non-ORF decoys are rejected, and all boundary cases remain unpromoted.  The statement is intentionally weaker than a biological mechanism.  It says that a frozen symbolic ORF-window classifier agrees with this curated RefSeq interval contact under the listed finite controls.  It does not say that the windows were translated, that a peptide folded, that any molecular state is physically admissible, that any gene product has function, that the rule is universal, or that windows outside this finite contact table are classified.  Those stronger claims require separate contacts addressed to those layers.
