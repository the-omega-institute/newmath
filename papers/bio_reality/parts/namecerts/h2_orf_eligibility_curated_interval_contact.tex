\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record concerns the layer $\mathsf{orf\_eligibility}$ and only that layer.  The biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by start/stop codon enumeration against an externally curated interval manifest.  The finite packet does not identify translated protein, folded conformation, biochemical admissibility, cellular function, causal mechanism, or a universal genomic rule.  It asks a narrower question: after the classifier has been fixed as the geometry-only rule $\mathsf{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}$, can externally supplied curated intervals be separated from matched non-ORF decoys while boundary cases remain unpromoted?

\paragraph{Finite codon background.}
The codon-level background used by the row is completely finite.  The motif-compressed set $R$ has the thirteen entries
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, with no missing and no extra entries in the recorded comparison.  The larger finite set $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ has cardinality $20$ and is listed as
$\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The saturation check records $\mathrm{Sat}_{f3}(R)=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$ and an empty $f3$ boundary, so the start/stop-window classifier is being applied over a documented finite codon substrate rather than over an unbounded informal class.  The quotient geometry check records an $M$ face vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, quotient size $10$, and quotient face vector $(10,14,6,1)$.  These numbers are background coordinates for the classifier; by themselves they are not biological observations.

\paragraph{External interval row.}
The actual contact row is the curated interval dataset at snapshot date $\mathrm{2026\mbox{-}05\mbox{-}30}$, sourced from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$.  The loaded dataset contains $168$ rows and the required input classes are all present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORF intervals.  The protocol records that classifier rules, input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were fixed at $\mathrm{2026\mbox{-}05\mbox{-}29T19:11:13+00:00}$, before external evidence was observed at $\mathrm{2026\mbox{-}05\mbox{-}30T10:29:31+00:00}$.  The analysis row also records that no translation or structure input was used.

\paragraph{Observed finite separation.}
The positive class contains $72$ curated intervals, and all $72$ are accepted by the fixed start/stop-window eligibility classifier.  The matched decoy class contains $72$ non-ORF decoys, and all $72$ are rejected.  The exact one-sided binomial tail reported for the positive acceptance statistic is $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$, and the same tail probability is reported for the matched decoy rejection statistic.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with one-sided binomial tail $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  In compact form the row states
$$\begin{aligned}
 n_{+} &= 72, & a_{+} &= 72, & a_{+}/n_{+} &= 1.0,\\
 n_{-} &= 72, & r_{-} &= 72, & r_{-}/n_{-} &= 1.0,\\
 n_{\mathrm{pair}} &= 72, & w_{\mathrm{pair}} &= 72, & w_{\mathrm{pair}}/n_{\mathrm{pair}} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}$$
The boundary controls contain $24$ cases and promote none of them, so
$$\begin{aligned}
 n_{\partial} &= 24, & p_{\partial} &= 0, & p_{\partial}/n_{\partial} &= 0.0.
\end{aligned}$$
The result is therefore not merely a positive hit count.  It is a finite three-way pattern: curated positives are accepted, matched decoys are rejected, and boundary cases are held at the boundary.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The held-out split check has three folds.  In the left-locus fold, $24$ positives are accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys are rejected at rate $1.0$.  The middle-locus fold has the same counts and rates.  The right-locus fold also has $24$ positives at acceptance rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys at rejection rate $1.0$.  Thus the row records split count $3$ and reports each split as passed:
$$\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{left}: &(24,1.0;\,24,1.0),\\
 \mathrm{middle}: &(24,1.0;\,24,1.0),\\
 \mathrm{right}: &(24,1.0;\,24,1.0).
\end{aligned}$$
This is a finite stability statement over the curated manifest.  It does not say that all genomes, all transcripts, or all possible nucleotide windows are classified.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier window manifest.}
A separate start/stop-window eligibility row records one external window dataset loaded at snapshot date $\mathrm{2026\mbox{-}05\mbox{-}25}$, with $1$ input window and $1$ classified window.  That earlier row is explicitly scoped to external window start/stop enumeration only.  The present curated-interval row is stronger because it supplies $168$ interval-contact rows, balanced positive and decoy testing, boundary controls, held-out folds, and a predeclared paired comparison.  Both rows keep the same layer discipline: geometry can evaluate eligibility under a fixed rule, but it cannot become translation, structure, physical admissibility, or function without an additional observation of that kind.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The finite result supports the following limited statement: within this externally supplied $168$-row curated interval contact, the fixed geometry-only start/stop classifier separates $72$ curated ORF intervals from $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, leaves $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable across three held-out locus folds.  The row does not establish that an accepted interval is translated in vivo, that a peptide folds, that a molecule is physically admissible, that a gene has biological function, that the classifier gives a mechanism, or that the pattern is universal outside the manifest.  The NCBI RefSeq annotation is treated as the biological contact, not as content generated by the finite codon geometry.  The self-check row is consistent with this restriction: it records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
