\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF-window eligibility}\label{sec:bior-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim frame.} This record concerns only the finite eligibility question determined by start and stop codon enumeration against an externally supplied interval table.  The biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility, not realized translation.  The layer is therefore kept at \emph{orf eligibility}: a window may be accepted as compatible with a curated coding interval when the finite classifier and the curated interval row agree, but the same acceptance is not evidence that a protein was translated, folded, physically admissible, functional, mechanistic, conserved, or universal.  Those conclusions require separate observations with their own contact data.

The required row classes are all present: \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}. \paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.} The result established here is narrow.  It says that, for this finite RefSeq-derived interval table and these matched controls, a pre-specified start/stop-window eligibility classifier separates curated positive intervals from matched non-ORF decoys and refuses to promote boundary cases.  It does not establish translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, genome-wide universality, or a global law about ORFs.  It also does not license classification of windows outside this dataset.  The NCBI RefSeq interval table remains an external curated biological contact, while the codon geometry remains a finite classifier substrate.  The self-check records the same boundary: three gates were checked, and no codon geometry was promoted across biological layers.
