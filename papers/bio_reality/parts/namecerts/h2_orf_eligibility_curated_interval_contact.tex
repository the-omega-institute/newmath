\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{subsec:bior-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The object studied here is the eligibility of a nucleotide interval to be treated as an open-reading-frame candidate when the only internal classifier is start--stop enumeration.  The local claim is deliberately narrow.  A window may be accepted by the finite classifier only as an interval-level eligibility record against a curated annotation contact.  This does not assert translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Each of those stronger biological layers would require its own contact term and its own test.  The present packet therefore separates a finite start--stop rule from the biological acts that could occur downstream of it.

\paragraph{Internal finite coordinates.}
The codon-level background used by the packet is finite and explicitly enumerated.  The motif-compression row identifies
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,\\
&UCA,UGA,UUA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing and no extra elements relative to the recorded motif union.  The companion row gives the twenty-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
&CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
It is recorded as $M=WNR\cup CUN$ with cardinality $20$.  The saturation row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so that $\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)$ is $M$ with $ACA$ and $ACG$ absent, and the $f3$ boundary is empty.  These finite coordinates are useful because they define the classifier's combinatorial input, but they do not by themselves turn a nucleotide window into an observed biological interval.

The quotient audit supplies a second finite control on the same coordinate system.  The row for $M$ has $f$-vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$, while the expected quotient has ten points and $f$-vector $(10,14,6,1)$.  The recorded quotient image consists of the ten binary coordinates

This geometry is retained only as finite bookkeeping for the start--stop classifier.  It is not read as a translation assay, a structural assay, or a physical model.

\paragraph{First interval contact.}
The earlier start--stop-window row loaded one external window manifest with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$ and classified all supplied windows: $1$ of $1$ input windows was assigned by start--stop enumeration.  Its recorded scope was external window start--stop enumeration only, and its non-promotion field was $\texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  The present row is the stronger curated interval contact.  It loads $168$ rows from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The source is NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval annotation from the complete \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 genome, accession NC\_000913.3.  The three required input classes are present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs.

\paragraph{Frozen protocol.}
The protocol was frozen before the external observation.  The recorded freeze time is $2026$-$05$-$29T19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, while the external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30T10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified after freezing.  The same row records that classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were all predeclared, and that the analysis used no translation or structure input.  Thus the test is an interval-contact test against curated annotation, not a post hoc biological-layer promotion.

\paragraph{Positive and decoy performance.}
There are $72$ positive curated ORF intervals and $72$ matched non-ORF decoys in the strong binomial comparisons.  The positives were accepted at rate $1.0$: $72$ accepted out of $72$, with one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$: $72$ rejected out of $72$, with the same recorded one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared pairwise baseline comparison also has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, and one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  These numbers support the finite interval-contact claim: under the frozen classifier and this curated dataset, the start--stop eligibility rule separates the curated positives from matched decoys.

\paragraph{Held-out stability and boundary cap.}
The held-out split check has three folds, each passing with $24$ positives and $24$ decoys.  The left-locus fold has positive accept rate $1.0$ and decoy reject rate $1.0$; the middle-locus fold has the same two rates; the right-locus fold also has the same two rates.  Boundary cases are not promoted: $0$ of $24$ boundary cases were promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$.  This boundary cap is essential to the claim.  It prevents the finite classifier from being interpreted as a rule for all possible nucleotide windows, and it keeps ambiguous or deliberately marginal cases outside the accepted interval-contact class.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The self-check row records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Combining the finite codon coordinates, the quotient audit, the frozen start--stop classifier, and the curated RefSeq interval contact yields a scoped ORF-eligibility result for this dataset: $168$ curated rows were loaded; $72$ positive intervals were accepted; $72$ matched decoys were rejected; $24$ boundary cases were not promoted; and all three held-out splits passed.  The result remains exactly at interval eligibility.  It does not establish that any accepted interval is translated, folded, physically admissible, functional, mechanistically explained, or representative of a universal biological law.  Those would be different claims requiring different observations.
