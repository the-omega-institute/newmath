\paragraph{Layer and question.}
This record concerns the layer $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ for open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by start and stop codon enumeration against an externally curated interval table. The object under examination is not translation, not protein production, not folding, not physical admissibility, and not biological function. It is only the finite question whether a frozen start/stop-window classifier, using no translation or structure input, separates curated coding intervals from matched non-ORF decoys while leaving boundary cases unpromoted.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite contact is therefore an interval-level support record for $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ on the stated RefSeq-derived table: $168$ rows, $72$ positive curated ORFs, $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, and $24$ boundary cases. The supported statement is that a frozen start/stop-window eligibility classifier, using no translation or structure inputs, accepts every curated positive interval, rejects every matched decoy, wins every predeclared pairwise comparison, and promotes no boundary case in this dataset.

\paragraph{Scope limits.}
No stronger biological statement follows from this record. It does not establish that translation occurs, that a protein folds, that a molecular state is physically admissible, that a gene product has function, that a mechanism has been identified, or that a universal biological law holds. It also does not classify windows outside the loaded dataset. The NCBI RefSeq annotation remains the biological contact used for this test; the finite codon geometry and the frozen classifier are not themselves a source of genomic reality.
