\paragraph{Finite claim under test.}
This record concerns only open-reading-frame eligibility as obtained from start and stop codon enumeration against a curated interval annotation.  The biological object is not translation, protein production, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, mechanism, phenotype, or function.  It is the narrower finite question of whether a frozen start/stop-window classifier, using no translation or structural input, agrees with a curated collection of coding-sequence intervals and rejects matched non-ORF decoys under the declared protocol.

The contact packet has claim identifier \texttt{h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact}.  The curated interval dataset was loaded from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json} with $168$ rows.  Its declared biological source is NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval annotation from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The protocol was fixed before the external annotation was observed: the protocol timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$29$T$19{:}11{:}13{+}00{:}00$, while the external evidence observation timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$30$T$10{:}29{:}31{+}00{:}00$.  The input classes were fixed, the held-out splits were fixed, the classifier rules were fixed, and the baselines were predeclared.  The analysis flag records that no translation or structure input was used.

\paragraph{Enumeration-only baseline.}
The preceding start/stop-window record loaded one external nucleotide-window manifest with $1$ input window, and all $1$ windows were classified by start/stop enumeration.  Its scope was explicitly limited to external window start/stop enumeration, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$.  The recorded output string for layer promotion was \texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only}.  Thus the classifier may say that a window satisfies the finite start/stop eligibility rule, but it may not say that a transcript was present, that a ribosome translated it, that a peptide folded, that a molecular state is physically admissible, or that any function follows.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
The later curated-interval packet supplies the separate annotation contact that the boundary probes demanded for the ORF-eligibility layer.  The required input classes are present: \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the record states that the classifier rules were not modified after freezing.

On the $72$ positive curated ORF intervals, the classifier accepted all $72$, giving accept rate $1.0$.  The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value recorded for this positive interval statistic is $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  On the $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, the classifier rejected all $72$, giving reject rate $1.0$, with the same exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired comparison has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, hence win rate $1.0$, again with binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.

The boundary controls are not promoted: $24$ boundary cases were examined, $0$ were promoted, and the promoted fraction is $0.0$.  This is the finite check that the classifier does not turn boundary windows into asserted ORF intervals merely because they resemble the enumerated geometry.

The interval contact includes $3$ held-out splits. The finite result is therefore not just a pooled $72$-pair count; it is stable across the three declared locus folds, each carrying the same $24$-positive and $24$-decoy balance.

\paragraph{What the contact establishes.}
Within the stated scope \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}, the packet establishes a finite agreement pattern between a frozen geometry-only ORF-window eligibility classifier and a curated RefSeq CDS interval contact.  The concrete evidence is the $168$-row dataset, the $72/72$ positive acceptance result, the $72/72$ matched-decoy rejection result, the $72/72$ paired advantage result, the $0/24$ boundary-promotion result, and the three held-out folds that each pass with $24/24$ positive acceptance and $24/24$ decoy rejection.

This is enough to move the ORF-eligibility seed from a contact-gap boundary probe to a finite curated-interval agreement statement for this dataset and this frozen classifier.  It is not enough to infer any stronger biological layer.  In particular, no translation event, folded protein structure, physical admissibility claim, biological function, molecular mechanism, global genomic law, or universal rule over unobserved windows follows from these rows.  Any such stronger claim would require a distinct observation channel or curated record aimed at that stronger layer.

The packet would fail its own declared interpretation if the start/stop-window classifier were used to assert ORF eligibility without the curated interval contact, if classifier rules had been changed after observing the RefSeq annotation, if boundary cases were promoted as positives, if matched decoys were not rejected, or if the finite codon geometry were treated as a substitute for transcript, translation, structural, physical, mechanistic, or functional evidence.  The self-check ledger records the same separation: $3$ gates were checked, and no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  The final reading is therefore deliberately narrow: the curated RefSeq interval rows support ORF-window eligibility agreement on the declared finite contact, and nothing above that layer is claimed.
