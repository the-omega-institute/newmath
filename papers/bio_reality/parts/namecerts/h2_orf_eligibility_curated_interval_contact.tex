\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite record.}
This record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as a deliberately narrow biological layer. The object under test is not protein production, folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, or biological role. It is the weaker question whether a start--stop codon interval, when compared against a curated nucleotide annotation, passes a finite eligibility check. The finite row therefore separates a geometric codon-window predicate from any stronger biological conclusion. The claim identifier is \texttt{h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact}, and its scope is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}. The contact is a curated RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval source for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within the finite contact, the start--stop-window classifier cleanly separates the supplied curated ORF intervals from the supplied matched non-ORF decoys and leaves the boundary cases unpromoted. The result is therefore an ORF-eligibility contact for this dataset and protocol. It does not establish translation realization, folded protein structure, thermodynamic or cellular admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, or universality across genomes or annotation regimes. Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact capable of measuring that stronger layer. The RefSeq annotation remains curated biological input to this finite test; it is not converted into kernel content by the codon-coordinate construction.
