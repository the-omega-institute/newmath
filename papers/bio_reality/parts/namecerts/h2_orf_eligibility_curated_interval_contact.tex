\subsection{Curated Interval Contact for ORF Eligibility}\label{sec:biorality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question being tested.}
This record concerns a deliberately narrow biological question: whether a frozen start/stop-window classifier can recognize externally curated open-reading-frame interval annotations in a finite nucleotide-window dataset, while refusing to promote nearby boundary cases and matched non-ORF decoys. The biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration. The claim is not that the underlying coordinate geometry discovers translation, protein folding, admissible molecular physics, biological function, mechanism, or a general law. It is only that, after an independently supplied curated interval contact is attached, the finite classifier separates the contacted positive intervals from the paired finite controls in this dataset.

\paragraph{Finite codon geometry used by the classifier.}
The classifier is tied to a fixed finite codon vocabulary. The motif union $R$ contains exactly the thirteen codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\\
&\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The larger set $M$, verified as $WNR\cup CUN$, has cardinality $20$ and is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_3$ saturation check gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the reported $f_3$ boundary is empty. The same finite geometry reports an $M$ face vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and a quotient face vector $(10,14,6,1)$, with quotient size $10$. These numbers are used only to identify the frozen enumeration substrate for the window classifier; they are not biological observations by themselves.

\paragraph{Reality contact and frozen protocol.}
The attached contact is a curated interval dataset derived from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$. The snapshot date is $2026$-$05$-$30$. The loaded file is recorded as \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}, with $168$ rows. The input classes present in the dataset are \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}. Before the outside annotation was observed, the protocol records the classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines as fixed. The protocol freeze time is $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, and the external evidence observation time is $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$. The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; the row also records that the classifier rules were not modified and that the analysis used no translation or structure input.

\paragraph{Positive intervals, decoys, and boundary cases.}
The finite test separates three roles. Among $72$ curated positive intervals, all $72$ are accepted, so the reported accept rate is $1.0$. The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Among $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, all $72$ are rejected, again with reported rate $1.0$ and the same exact one-sided binomial value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired baseline comparison has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, hence win rate $1.0$, with the same reported exact upper-tail value. The boundary-control cap records $24$ boundary cases, $0$ promotions, and promoted fraction $0.0$. Thus the tested object is not merely successful acceptance of positives; it also includes matched decoy rejection and explicit non-promotion at the boundary.

\paragraph{Held-out split stability.}
The dataset is partitioned into three held-out folds. In the left-locus fold \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, the classifier has $24$ positives with positive accept rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys with decoy reject rate $1.0$. The middle-locus fold \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} has the same counts and rates. The right-locus fold \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold} also has $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$. All three folds are marked as passed. The split evidence therefore supports finite stability across the three predeclared locus partitions, not an unrestricted genome-wide theorem.

\paragraph{Boundary of the conclusion.}
The accepted conclusion is confined to \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}: under the frozen start/stop-window classifier, the curated RefSeq interval contact separates the $72$ positive intervals from the $72$ matched decoys, leaves $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable over three held-out folds. Nothing in this row establishes translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, evolutionary explanation, or universality. The RefSeq annotation remains an outside curated biological contact rather than content generated by the finite codon geometry. The earlier self-check also records the same kind of limit: three gates are checked, and no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. Any later claim about translation, structure, physics, function, mechanism, or broad biological law would require a separate contact designed for that higher layer.
