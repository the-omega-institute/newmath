\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{subsec:bioreality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Object and layer.}
This record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as a finite question about start and stop codon windows after an observed interval has been supplied by curated biological annotation. The layer is only $\mathsf{orf\_eligibility}$. It is not a claim that the interval is translated, that a protein folds, that a physical conformation is admissible, that a function exists, that a mechanism has been identified, or that a universal biological rule has been obtained. The contact used here is an NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval source for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$. The dataset contains $168$ finite rows, located at \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}. Its row classes are exactly the three required input classes: positive curated ORF intervals, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary cases.

\paragraph{Frozen protocol and classifier.}
The classifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}. Its rules were frozen before the curated interval observation and were not modified afterward. The protocol was frozen at $2026$-$05$-$29T19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, while the external curated evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30T10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$. The input classes, held-out splits, baseline comparisons, and classifier rules were all fixed in advance. The analysis used no translation input and no protein-structure input. Thus the contact tests whether the frozen start/stop-window eligibility rule agrees with curated interval status inside this dataset, not whether molecular translation or downstream biology has occurred.

The matched non-ORF decoy block has $72$ rejected rows out of $72$, so its observed rejection rate is $1.0$. The held-out split check is likewise finite and row-explicit: the left-locus fold has $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$; the middle-locus fold has the same $24/24$ positive and $24/24$ decoy outcome; the right-locus fold also has the same $24/24$ positive and $24/24$ decoy outcome. All three held-out split records are marked as passed.

\paragraph{Boundary rows and non-promotion.}
The boundary control contains $24$ cases and promotes $0$ of them, giving promoted fraction $0.0$. This is essential to the claim: the contact is not allowed to classify outside the curated interval dataset merely because the codon geometry has a start/stop shape. The earlier start/stop-window enumeration loaded one external window and classified that one input window, with scope restricted to external window start/stop enumeration only; it also recorded that no geometry-to-translation promotion was made. The self-check records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. The present contact preserves that same restriction at the larger curated-interval scale.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within the $168$ rows of the curated interval contact, the frozen geometry-only classifier separates the $72$ curated ORF intervals from the $72$ matched decoys, remains stable across three held-out folds, beats the predeclared paired baseline in all $72$ pairs, and leaves all $24$ boundary rows unpromoted. Therefore this chapter may record a finite ORF-eligibility contact for this dataset and this snapshot. The conclusion stops there. It does not assert translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, genome-wide completeness, or species-independent universality. Any such stronger assertion would require a different contact whose measured object is that stronger biological layer.
