\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{subsec:orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the record.}
This BioReality record concerns only open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by finite start--stop codon interval data.  The biological object is not protein production, not folded structure, not chemical admissibility, and not organismal function.  The record asks a narrower question: after the start--stop window rule has been fixed without using translation or structure information, does a curated interval contact separate annotated coding intervals from matched non-ORF decoys, while leaving boundary cases unpromoted?  The answer recorded here is affirmative for the finite dataset described below, and no wider biological conclusion is licensed by that answer.

The background finite geometry supplies a codon set $M$ of cardinality $20$ and a motif subfamily $R$ of cardinality $13$.  The motif family is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The larger enumerated set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA, AAG, ACA, ACG, AGA, AGG, AUA, AUG, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UCG, UGA, UGG, UUA, UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite computation records $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ with size $20$.  A saturation check gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f3}(R)=\{&AAA, AAG, AGA, AGG, AUA, AUG, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UCG, UGA, UGG, UUA, UUG\},

The quotient computation has $|\pi_M|=10$, with $M$-vector $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$.  These finite facts describe the start--stop geometry that the classifier may inspect; they do not by themselves assert that any interval is realized in a genome.

\paragraph{Protocol and contact.}
The contact used here is a curated interval dataset loaded from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  It contains $168$ rows, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$, and is described as NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-$12$ MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3.  The required input classes are present: \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.  The protocol was frozen at $2026$-$05$-$29$T$19$:$11$:$13$+00:$00$, before the external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30$T$10$:$29$:$31$+00:$00$.  The finite row also records that classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were fixed in advance, and that the analysis used no translation or protein-structure input.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the rule file is recorded as unmodified during the contact.

All $72$ positives were accepted, giving accept rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  All $72$ matched decoys were rejected, giving rejection rate $1.0$ and the same exact one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  In the predeclared paired comparison, the positive member beat its matched decoy in all $72$ pairs, so the paired win rate is $1.0$ with $72$ wins out of $72$ and the same recorded one-sided binomial value.  The boundary control promoted no cases: $0$ promoted out of $24$, hence promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
Within this chapter, ORF eligibility means only successful finite agreement between a predeclared start--stop interval rule and a curated interval annotation contact.  The result is a dataset-scoped separation: positives in this contact are accepted, matched decoys in this contact are rejected, boundary cases are not promoted, and the result is stable across the three frozen locus splits.  The self-check row records $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  This is the intended reading of the contact: curated annotation supplies the biological interval being tested, while the finite codon geometry supplies only the classifier's permitted input.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  The curated RefSeq annotation remains biological input to the experiment, not kernel content and not a derivation from codon geometry.  The matched decoy and boundary controls do not classify arbitrary windows outside the $168$-row dataset.  A higher-layer assertion would require another contact whose data are appropriate to that layer: ribosome or transcript evidence for translation, structural evidence for folding, physical evidence for admissibility, functional assays for biological role, mechanistic perturbation for causal explanation, and broader sampling for universality.  Without such additional contact, this record remains a finite, dataset-scoped ORF-eligibility contact and no more.
