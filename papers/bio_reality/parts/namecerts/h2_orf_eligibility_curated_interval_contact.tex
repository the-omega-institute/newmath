\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{subsec:orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate data before biological contact.}
The record concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as a finite start--stop interval question, not as a claim about expression or protein production. The coordinate layer used by the packet is the same finite codon geometry that had already been measured in the lower-order checks. The motif set $R$ is exactly
$\{AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU, UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}$,
with no missing and no extra elements in the motif-compression check. The ambient set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is
$\{AAA, AAG, ACA, ACG, AGA, AGG, AUA, AUG, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU, UAA, UAG, UCA, UCG, UGA, UGG, UUA, UUG\}$.
The third saturation test gives
$\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=M\setminus\{ACA,ACG\}$, while the $f_3$ boundary is empty. The quotient-geometry audit reports the finite vector $f(M)=(20,38,26,8,1)$ and the quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$, with $|\pi(M)|=10$. These numbers fix the combinatorial input used by the interval classifier; they do not, by themselves, report a biological interval.

\paragraph{Contacted interval dataset.}
The present packet attaches a curated interval annotation contact to the start--stop enumeration layer. The loaded dataset is recorded at \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json} and contains $168$ rows. Its source is the NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval annotation for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30. The three declared input classes are present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs. Thus the observation is not an inference from codon geometry alone; it is a comparison between a fixed start--stop-window classifier and a curated biological interval table.

\paragraph{Frozen protocol and classifier scope.}
The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the row audit says that its rules were not modified during the contact. The protocol was frozen at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, before the external evidence observation time 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00. The recorded guards say that the baselines, input classes, and held-out splits were fixed before observation, and that the analysis used no translation or structure input. The one-window precursor check also remains narrow: it loaded an external nucleotide-window manifest with $1$ input window and classified $1$ window by start/stop enumeration only. Its own scope was already limited to external-window start/stop enumeration, with the explicit value \texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only} for the non-promotion field.

\paragraph{Positive, decoy, and boundary counts.}
The curated interval contact separates the $168$ rows into $72$ positive curated intervals, $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, and $24$ boundary cases. Every positive interval was accepted, and every matched decoy was rejected. The corresponding one-sided exact binomial values are the same in the positive, decoy, and paired baseline tests:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{+} &= 72, & a_{+} &= 72, & \widehat p_{+} &= 1.0,\\
  n_{d} &= 72, & r_{d} &= 72, & \widehat p_{d} &= 1.0,\\
  n_{pair} &= 72, & w_{pair} &= 72, & \widehat p_{pair} &= 1.0,\\
  P_{\mathrm{tail}} &= 2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary control is also explicit: $24$ boundary cases were tested and $0$ were promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$. This is a crucial part of the result, because it says that the contacted interval test did not convert nearby or ambiguous windows into positive biological claims.

\paragraph{Held-out split stability.}
The split audit contains three held-out folds. In the left-locus fold, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$. The same finite outcome holds for the middle-locus fold and the right-locus fold. Hence the split table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{left}:&\quad (24/24)\ \mathrm{positive\ acceptance},\quad (24/24)\ \mathrm{decoy\ rejection},\\
  \mathrm{middle}:&\quad (24/24)\ \mathrm{positive\ acceptance},\quad (24/24)\ \mathrm{decoy\ rejection},\\
  \mathrm{right}:&\quad (24/24)\ \mathrm{positive\ acceptance},\quad (24/24)\ \mathrm{decoy\ rejection}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All three folds passed. The table therefore supports a finite contacted eligibility statement for this curated interval sample, rather than only a single aggregate count.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The supported statement is deliberately small. A fixed start--stop-window eligibility classifier, using only the finite coordinate information above, agrees with the contacted RefSeq CDS interval rows on this $168$-row sample, rejects the matched decoys in the same count, and leaves all $24$ boundary cases unpromoted. This is enough to turn the earlier contact-gap seed into an ORF-eligibility interval contact for the given dataset. It is not enough to say that the sequence is translated, that a folded protein exists, that a physical conformation is admissible, that a cellular function is present, that a mechanism has been identified, or that a universal biological rule has been established. Each of those stronger assertions would require its own contact of the appropriate kind. The self-check agrees with this reading: it reports $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
