\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{sec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Question isolated by the packet.}
This record concerns only the eligibility of open-reading-frame intervals under start--stop codon enumeration.  The biological object is not a translated product, not a folded molecule, and not a functional annotation.  It is the much narrower question whether a finite nucleotide interval, after an independently supplied curated contact, is accepted by a frozen start--stop-window classifier.  Before that contact is attached, the start--stop enumeration is only a boundary test: it says what kind of interval would be examined, but it does not itself observe a biological interval.

\paragraph{Reality contact used by this record.}
The contact for this packet is a curated interval dataset loaded from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  The dataset contains $168$ rows and is described as NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals from \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The required input classes are present: \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.  The scope is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}; thus the input is a curated biological interval contact, while the codon geometry remains a finite classifier coordinate.

\paragraph{Protocol and classifier freeze.}
The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}.  Its rules were recorded as unmodified, and the protocol states that classifier rules, input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were fixed before observation of the curated evidence.  The protocol freeze time was $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, while the external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The analysis flag records that no translation or structure input was used.  This ordering is the central finite-row protection: acceptance is measured against a predeclared interval task rather than fitted after seeing the curated rows.

\paragraph{Finite test outcomes.}
The positive curated intervals were accepted in all $72$ cases, giving accept rate $1.0$.  The corresponding exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched non-ORF decoys were rejected in all $72$ cases, giving reject rate $1.0$ with the same recorded upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, hence win rate $1.0$, again with upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Boundary cases were not promoted: among $24$ boundary rows, $0$ were promoted and the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The split-stability check contains three folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$.  The same finite counts and rates hold for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  All three splits are marked as passed.  These split results support stability of this particular curated interval contact; they do not assert that every nucleotide window outside the dataset is classified.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
Within this chapter, an ORF-eligibility acceptance means only this: under the frozen start--stop classifier, a curated interval row from the stated RefSeq contact lands on the accepted side of the finite eligibility test.  The result is supported by $72/72$ positive acceptances, $72/72$ matched decoy rejections, $0/24$ boundary promotions, three stable held-out splits, and $72/72$ paired baseline wins.  The codon geometry supplies the finite vocabulary and quotient counts used by the classifier; the curated RefSeq interval supplies the biological contact.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No conclusion about translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, organism-wide law, or universality follows from this packet.  In particular, start--stop geometry plus this curated interval contact does not show that a transcript is translated, that a polypeptide has a stable structure, that the product is physically admissible in vivo, that it performs a function, or that the same rule classifies windows outside the recorded dataset.  Any such assertion requires a separate contact whose measurement directly addresses that higher layer.
