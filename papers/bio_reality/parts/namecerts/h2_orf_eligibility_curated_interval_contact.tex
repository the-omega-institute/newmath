\paragraph{Contact protocol.}
The separate reality contact is a curated interval table loaded from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  It contains $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K--12 MG1655 complete genome accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  The input classes present in the table are \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}.  The analysis protocol was frozen at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, before the external evidence time 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00.  The input classes, held-out splits, baselines, and classifier rules were fixed before observation, and the analysis used no translation or protein-structure input.  The classifier was \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified during the contact check.  An earlier finite start--stop-window check loaded one external window dataset with one input window and classified that one window by start--stop enumeration alone, under the scope \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}.  The present record is narrower and stronger: it tests curated intervals against matched decoys and boundary cases under \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}.

\paragraph{Observed finite outcomes.}
Among the curated positives, the classifier accepted $72$ of $72$, for an acceptance rate of $1.0$.  The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value recorded for this positive-interval statistic is $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Among the matched non-ORF decoys, the classifier rejected $72$ of $72$, again with rate $1.0$ and the same exact one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison had $72$ wins in $72$ positive--decoy pairs, so its win rate was $1.0$ and its one-sided binomial value was also $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The $24$ boundary cases were not promoted: the promoted count was $0$, and the promoted fraction was $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The held-out split check used three named folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, there were $24$ positives and $24$ decoys; the positive acceptance rate was $1.0$, the decoy rejection rate was $1.0$, and the split passed.  The same finite pattern held for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold}: $24$ positives, $24$ decoys, acceptance rate $1.0$, rejection rate $1.0$, and pass status true.  It also held for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}: $24$ positives, $24$ decoys, acceptance rate $1.0$, rejection rate $1.0$, and pass status true.  Thus the contact is not a single unsplit tally; the same finite classification behavior is present across three predeclared locus folds.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The contact upgrades the ORF-eligibility seed from a mere boundary question to a finite external-annotation contact for this dataset: under the frozen start--stop-window classifier, curated CDS intervals from the stated RefSeq source are separated from matched non-ORF decoys in all $72$ paired cases, and boundary cases are capped at zero promotion.  The self-check attached to the record reports three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Consequently, the result is an eligibility statement about this curated interval table, not a claim about translation taking place, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, or universal genomic law.  Any such higher biological assertion would need its own independent contact supplying the relevant observed evidence; it is not obtained from the codon coordinate data, the quotient geometry, the start--stop enumeration, or the RefSeq interval contact recorded here.
