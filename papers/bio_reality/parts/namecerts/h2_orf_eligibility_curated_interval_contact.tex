The required input classes are present: \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}. The frozen classifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the row states that its rules were not modified. The protocol row records that input classes, held-out splits, classifier rules, and baselines were fixed before the external observation; the protocol timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, and the external evidence timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
This chapter therefore licenses a narrow claim: for the curated RefSeq interval rows in this finite manifest, the frozen start--stop window classifier separates curated positive ORF intervals from matched non-ORF decoys, while leaving boundary cases unpromoted. The previously recorded single-window enumeration row had only $1$ input window and $1$ classified window under the scope \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}; the present $168$-row contact is the first row here that attaches the start--stop geometry to curated interval annotation. The self-check row is consistent with this reading: it records $3$ checked gates and explicitly keeps codon geometry from crossing biological layers by itself.

\paragraph{Non-promoted boundary.}
Nothing in the finite row asserts translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, or a universal law about ORFs. The RefSeq interval annotation is an external curated biological input, not content generated by the codon geometry. The matched decoy and boundary controls do not classify arbitrary windows outside the manifest, and the positive rows do not show that the annotated intervals are translated, folded, stable, active, or mechanistically explained. Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact carrying the relevant experimental or curated biological evidence.
