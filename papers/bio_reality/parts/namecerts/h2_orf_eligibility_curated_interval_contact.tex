\subsection{Curated interval contact for open-reading-frame eligibility}
\label{subsec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record concerns the layer named \emph{open-reading-frame eligibility}: the finite question whether a nucleotide interval is accepted by a fixed start--stop window enumeration after a curated interval has been supplied.  The biological object is not translation, protein production, folded molecular form, thermodynamic admissibility, regulatory role, or biological function.  The claim is therefore deliberately narrow.  It says that, for a specified curated interval dataset, a frozen geometry-only classifier separates curated ORF intervals from matched non-ORF decoys and keeps boundary examples from promotion.  The observation is attached to an external curated annotation contact, and the underlying BEDC coordinate by itself does not make an ORF claim.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
The contact used here is a curated RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval source for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  The loaded dataset path was \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  The run loaded $168$ rows.  The required input classes were present: positive curated ORF intervals, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary cases.  The protocol record states that the classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were fixed before the external observation: the protocol timestamp was $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, and the external evidence timestamp was $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The analysis also records that no translation or structure input was used.

\paragraph{Observed finite separation.}
The positive curated intervals were accepted at rate $1.0$: $72$ accepted out of $72$.  The matched non-ORF decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$: $72$ rejected out of $72$.  The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value recorded for each of these two finite tests is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired baseline comparison also reports $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, with win rate $1.0$ and the same one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Boundary examples were not elevated: $0$ of $24$ boundary cases were promoted, for promoted fraction $0.0$.

The split stability check used three held-out folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, $24$ positive intervals had positive accept rate $1.0$, while $24$ decoys had decoy reject rate $1.0$. This is a finite stability statement about the supplied dataset and frozen classifier; it is not a universal law about all genomes or all possible annotation regimes.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter records a successful contact at the ORF-eligibility layer and no higher biological layer.  The curated interval contact does not establish translation, protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a molecular mechanism, or universality.  The matched decoy and boundary controls do not classify windows outside the dataset.  The NCBI RefSeq annotation remains curated biological input to the experiment, not kernel content of the formal coordinate system.  The self-check for this layer counted $3$ checked gates and explicitly kept codon geometry from being promoted across biological layers.  Any later assertion about translation, folding, mechanism, physical feasibility, function, or genome-wide generality would require a separate contact carrying evidence for that layer.
