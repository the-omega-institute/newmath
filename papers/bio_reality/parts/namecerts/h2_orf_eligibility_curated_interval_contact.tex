\paragraph{Layer and object.}
This record concerns the layer named \emph{orf eligibility}: the finite question of whether a nucleotide interval is accepted by a start/stop-codon enumeration rule after a curated biological interval has been supplied. The biological object is not a protein, a folded state, a cellular role, or a mechanism. It is the narrower contact question in which externally curated coding-sequence intervals from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession NC\_000913.3 are compared with matched non-ORF decoys and boundary cases under a predeclared start/stop-window classifier. The snapshot date is 2026-05-30, the contact dataset contains $168$ rows, and the scope is explicitly restricted to external ORF interval contact.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The result supports only the following local claim: after an external curated interval table is supplied, the frozen start/stop-window classifier separates the positive curated ORF intervals from the matched non-ORF decoys in this finite dataset, while refusing to promote boundary cases. This is stronger than a contact-gap seed because the interval table is present and the finite checks are populated. It is still not a claim about translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, or universality. Those layers require their own contacts and tests. The self-check records the same restriction: three gates were checked, and no codon geometry was promoted across biological layers.
