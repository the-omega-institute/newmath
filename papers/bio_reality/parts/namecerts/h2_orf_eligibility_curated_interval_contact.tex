\subsection{Curated Interval Contact for ORF Eligibility}
\label{subsec:bioreality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record isolates the claim that start--stop codon enumeration may be tested against externally curated coding-sequence intervals, without allowing the geometric test to become a claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality. The biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility: a finite nucleotide window is accepted only when its boundary pattern is compatible with the frozen start--stop classifier and with a curated interval contact. The test is therefore deliberately narrow. It asks whether the predeclared boundary classifier separates curated coding intervals from matched non-ORF decoys in the supplied finite table, while keeping boundary cases outside promotion.

A window that has start--stop boundary geometry but lacks the curated interval status needed by this contact remains outside the accepted ORF-eligibility class. The classifier is therefore not being used as a general biological oracle; it is a finite discriminator over the supplied contact table.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within this finite dataset, the curated interval contact supports the narrow statement that the frozen start--stop eligibility classifier separates the $72$ curated positive intervals from the $72$ matched decoys, leaves $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable over three held-out folds. The contact upgrades the earlier boundary seed only to dataset-scoped ORF eligibility for the specified RefSeq-derived interval table. It does not assert translation, protein folding, thermodynamic or cellular admissibility, molecular function, regulatory role, organism-level effect, mechanism, or a universal law about all nucleotide windows. Any such stronger claim would require an additional observation layer designed for that target.
