\paragraph{Question fixed by the row.}
This record asks a deliberately narrow biological question. A start--stop enumeration can mark a nucleotide window as syntactically eligible for open-reading-frame testing, but that enumeration is not itself an observation of a biological interval. The claim assessed here is therefore only the following: after a separately curated interval annotation is supplied, the frozen start--stop-window classifier separates the curated positive intervals from matched non-ORF decoys and leaves boundary cases unpromoted. The layer is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$, and the scope is the finite external interval contact recorded on snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.

\paragraph{External interval contact and frozen protocol.}
The present contact loads $168$ rows from the curated interval dataset at \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}. The source is NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$. The protocol was fixed at $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, before the external evidence time $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$. The frozen settings state that classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were predeclared, and that the analysis used no translation or protein-structure input. The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the finite row explicitly records that the classifier rules were not modified after contact.

Among the curated positives, $72$ of $72$ were accepted, for accept rate $1.0$ and one-sided exact binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. \paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The contact also records three held-out folds. In the left-locus fold, $24$ positives had acceptance rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys had rejection rate $1.0$. The middle-locus fold has the same counts and rates, and the right-locus fold again has $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$. All three folds passed. This makes the finite result stronger than a single pooled count: the separation survives the declared partition into left, middle, and right locus folds.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The supported conclusion is that the curated RefSeq interval contact, under the frozen geometry-only classifier, realizes an ORF-eligibility separation on this finite dataset: positives are accepted, matched decoys are rejected, and boundary rows are not promoted. The conclusion is intentionally local. It does not assert translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality. None of those claims follows from codon geometry, start--stop enumeration, or the curated interval contact unless another independently specified contact supplies evidence for that higher layer. The NCBI annotation remains an external curated biological input to the experiment; it is not converted into kernel content or into a global biological law.
