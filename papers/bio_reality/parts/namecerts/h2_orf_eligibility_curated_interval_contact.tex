\subsection{Curated interval contact for open-reading-frame eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Biological question.}
The record \texttt{h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact} tests a narrow biological assertion: whether a frozen start--stop window classifier can separate externally curated coding intervals from matched non-ORF decoys in a finite annotation table. The object under test is not translation, protein folding, molecular stability, mechanism, or biological function. It is only interval eligibility at the level of start and stop codon enumeration, once an external curated interval contact has been supplied. The relevant contact is a RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval manifest for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, with snapshot date \texttt{2026-05-30}. The contact table contains $168$ rows and includes the three declared row classes
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{boundary\_case},\quad \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy},\quad \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite biological datum that changes the earlier boundary seed into an interval-contact test. Without such a curated interval table, start--stop enumeration remains only a proposed test condition and carries no ORF eligibility assertion.

\paragraph{Frozen classifier and row scope.}
The classifier used in the contact row is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}. Its rules were fixed before the external observation: the protocol was frozen at \texttt{2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00}, while the external evidence was observed at \texttt{2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00}. The row records \texttt{classifier\_rules\_modified = false}, with input classes, baselines, and held-out splits all fixed in advance. The analysis also records that no translation or structure input was used. Thus the test is deliberately limited to geometry of nucleotide windows plus the external curated interval contact. In the adjacent finite-row record \texttt{h2.orf.start\_stop.window\_eligibility}, the external window dataset has $1$ input window, $1$ classified window, and scope \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}; that predecessor row does not by itself establish an observed ORF. The present row supplies the stronger contact table, but its scope is still only \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}.

These codon rows describe the finite coordinate system used by the classifier; they do not themselves supply biological observation.

\paragraph{Observed finite outcomes.}
The curated interval contact row reports $72$ accepted positive curated ORF intervals out of $72$, so the positive accept rate is $1.0$. It also reports $72$ rejected matched non-ORF decoys out of $72$, so the matched decoy rejection rate is $1.0$. The exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value attached to each of these two tests is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p = 2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired baseline comparison gives $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with win rate $1.0$ and the same one-sided binomial upper-tail value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p = 2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary-control part of the row records $24$ boundary cases and $0$ boundary cases moved into the eligible class, hence promoted fraction $0.0$. These are finite-row facts, not asymptotic estimates and not universal claims about all nucleotide windows.

\paragraph{Held-out split stability.}
The contact row includes three held-out folds. In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, there are $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, with positive accept rate $1.0$ and decoy reject rate $1.0$. The same counts and rates hold for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}. Each split is marked as passed. Thus the contact record is not merely a single pooled tally: it also records stability across three finite locus folds, each with balanced positive and decoy counts.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The row supports only the following restricted conclusion: under a pre-fixed start--stop-window classifier, the supplied RefSeq CDS interval contact separates the $72$ positive curated intervals from $72$ matched non-ORF decoys in this finite table, while leaving $24$ boundary cases outside eligibility. Nothing in this chapter says that the classifier observes translation, confirms translated products, determines folded structure, proves physical admissibility, assigns biological function, identifies a mechanism, or establishes a universal biological law. Those stronger layers would require separate contacts of the appropriate kind, such as translation evidence, protein-structure evidence, physicochemical admissibility evidence, functional assay evidence, or mechanistic intervention evidence. The self-check row \texttt{self.claim} records this same local gate with checked gate count $3$ and with the boundary statement that no codon geometry is carried across biological layers. The scientific content of the present chapter is therefore finite and contact-indexed: a curated interval table can support ORF interval eligibility for its own rows, while the rest of biology remains outside the asserted layer.
