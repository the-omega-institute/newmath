\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
The present contact supplies the missing biological interval layer through a finite curated dataset, not through the codon geometry alone.  The dataset path is \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}; it contains $168$ rows and is sourced from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K--$12$ MG$1655$, complete genome accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}.  The snapshot date is $2026$--$05$--$30$, and the declared scope is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}.  The required input classes are exactly \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified during the contact test.

\paragraph{Frozen protocol and row-level outcome.}
The protocol was frozen at $2026$--$05$--$29T19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, before the external evidence was observed at $2026$--$05$--$30T10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The input classes, baselines, held-out splits, and classifier rules were fixed in advance, and the analysis used no translation or structure input.  On the positive curated intervals, $72$ of $72$ were accepted, giving acceptance rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the matched non-ORF decoys, $72$ of $72$ were rejected, giving rejection rate $1.0$ with the same upper-tail value.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison also has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, and the same exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value.  Boundary controls did not cross the eligibility gate: among $24$ boundary cases, $0$ were promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The split check used three held-out folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, there were $24$ positives and $24$ decoys; the positive acceptance rate was $1.0$, the decoy rejection rate was $1.0$, and the split passed.  The same numbers hold for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  Thus the finite result is not a single aggregate tally hiding one unstable locus partition; each of the three declared folds satisfies the same positive-versus-decoy separation.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The contact upgrades only the narrow claim that the frozen start--stop-window classifier can separate the supplied curated ORF intervals from matched non-ORF decoys and can keep boundary cases unpromoted in this dataset.  It does not establish translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  It also does not classify windows outside the $168$ supplied rows.  The RefSeq contact remains curated biological input to the experiment rather than content generated by the finite codon kernel.  The self-check records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Therefore the proper reading is: this packet has a finite, externally anchored ORF-eligibility contact for the listed dataset, while every higher biological layer remains unavailable unless a separate contact supplies evidence for that layer.
