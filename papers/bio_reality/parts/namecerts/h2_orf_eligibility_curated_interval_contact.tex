\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}\label{sec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record concerns the biological layer called ORF eligibility.  The biological object is the finite question whether a nucleotide interval passes a start--stop codon enumeration test after a curated interval contact has supplied the interval to be tested.  The claim is deliberately narrow: an interval may be accepted as eligible for this layer only because a separate curated annotation provides the interval and a frozen start--stop classifier evaluates that supplied interval.  The kernel-side coordinate by itself is not an observed ORF, and a start--stop window by itself is not evidence of translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Those stronger assertions require distinct contacts whose measurements address those layers directly.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The interval contact used here is a curated RefSeq GenBank XML contact for CDS intervals from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  The dataset path recorded by the finite run is \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}, and the dataset contains $168$ rows.  Its required input classes are exactly the three finite classes \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified during the contact run.

\paragraph{Protocol order.}
The protocol row records that the classifier rules, input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were fixed before the external observation.  The protocol was frozen at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the external evidence was observed at 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00.  The analysis row also records that no translation or structure input was used.  Thus the contact is a finite test of interval eligibility under a predeclared start--stop rule, not an after-the-fact fit to protein outcome data.

\paragraph{Observed finite performance.}
The positive curated interval statistic contains $72$ accepted positives out of $72$, with accept rate $1.0$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched decoy statistic contains $72$ rejected decoys out of $72$, with reject rate $1.0$ and the same one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared pairwise comparison contains $72$ wins out of $72$ pairs, again with one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Boundary cases are capped rather than promoted: among $24$ boundary cases, $0$ were promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The held-out split row contains three folds.  In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$.  The same $24$-positive and $24$-decoy counts, with rates $1.0$ and $1.0$, are recorded for \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold} and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.  All three folds passed.  The earlier one-window enumeration check is compatible with this interpretation: it loaded an external finite nucleotide-window manifest dated 2026-05-25, classified $1$ input window out of $1$, and kept the scope at external start--stop enumeration only.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The contact upgrades the seed from a mere gap record to a finite externally tested ORF-eligibility interval record for this dataset.  It does not establish that any accepted interval is translated, that any translated product folds, that any fold is physically admissible, that any product has biological function, that the classifier explains a mechanism, or that the finite result is universal outside the curated contact.  The matched decoys and boundary controls also do not classify windows beyond this dataset.  The RefSeq annotation remains an outside biological contact attached to the packet rather than content generated by the codon geometry itself.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For claim \texttt{h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact}, the finite evidence supports the following bounded assertion: under a frozen start--stop eligibility classifier and a predeclared curated RefSeq interval contact, $72$ positive intervals are accepted, $72$ matched decoys are rejected, $24$ boundary cases are not promoted, and three held-out folds each preserve $24/24$ positive acceptance and $24/24$ decoy rejection.  The statement is therefore ready as an ORF-eligibility contact claim, with all stronger biological layers left outside the claim.
