\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Layer and claim.}
This record concerns the layer $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ for open-reading-frame eligibility obtained from finite start and stop codon enumeration, then tested against a curated interval contact. The object under examination is not translation, protein production, folded molecular structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological regularity. It is the narrower question of whether a frozen start/stop-window classifier, using only boundary information, can separate curated coding intervals from matched non-ORF decoys in a finite external annotation table. The seed packet before contact was deliberately empty on the biological side: its carrier, distinctions, and readback were all recorded as awaiting a contact with reality. The self-check also records the same boundary discipline, with checked gate count $3$ and the statement that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.

\paragraph{External interval contact.}

Its source is NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals from \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$. The required input classes were all present: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs. The classifier was \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified during the contact test. The protocol records that classifier rules, held-out splits, baselines, and input classes were frozen before the external observation: the protocol time was $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, and the external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$. The analysis also records that no translation or structure input was used.

\begin{aligned}
p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired baseline comparison gives $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with win rate $1.0$ and the same one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The boundary control contains $24$ boundary cases, with $0$ promoted and promoted fraction $0.0$. \paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
The conclusion is restricted to $\mathrm{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}$. The contact establishes neither translation realization nor protein structure, neither physical admissibility nor biological function, and neither mechanism nor universality. It also does not classify windows outside the loaded dataset. The RefSeq annotation is an external curated biological input, not content produced by the codon geometry itself. Therefore the only ready claim is the following finite-row contact claim: for the $168$-row curated interval dataset, the frozen geometry-only start/stop-window classifier separates $72$ curated ORF intervals from $72$ matched decoys with exact one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$, leaves $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains confined to ORF eligibility rather than any higher biological layer.
