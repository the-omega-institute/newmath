\paragraph{Layer and finite scope.}
This record concerns the layer $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ only.  The biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility as tested by start and stop codon interval enumeration, not by translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, cellular role, biochemical mechanism, or a universal biological rule.  The finite row is therefore interpreted as a contact with curated interval annotation data for a bounded classifier, with scope
$\mathrm{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}$.
The classifier is recorded as $\mathrm{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}$, and its rules were not modified after the protocol was fixed.  The protocol freeze time was $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, while the external evidence was first observed at $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  Thus the finite observation is separated from classifier design in time: the input classes, held-out splits, baselines, and classifier rules were all fixed before the curated interval evidence was read.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The curated interval contact supports an ORF-eligibility assertion for this finite RefSeq-derived interval table under the frozen start--stop-window classifier and its matched controls.  It does not establish that any interval is translated in vivo; it does not establish protein folding, structural stability, energetic admissibility, molecular function, regulatory mechanism, organism-level role, or a general law of biology.  It also does not make BEDC codon geometry into curated biological annotation: the RefSeq contact remains an outside curated biological input to this finite experiment.  The self-check row records the same separation as a gate count of $3$ and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Consequently the only local claim licensed here is: within the $168$-row curated interval contact, the frozen ORF-eligibility classifier accepts the $72$ curated positive intervals, rejects the $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, leaves the $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable across the three specified held-out splits.
