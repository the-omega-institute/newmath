\subsection{Curated interval contact for open-reading-frame eligibility}
\label{sec:bioreality-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Biological question.}
This record concerns a deliberately narrow biological layer: eligibility of a nucleotide interval as an open-reading-frame candidate under start and stop codon enumeration. The object is not translation, protein formation, molecular stability, phenotype, or function. It is the weaker question of whether a finite interval, when compared with a curated sequence annotation, passes the start/stop-window rule that had been fixed before the curated observation was inspected. The contact used here is a curated RefSeq GenBank CDS-interval source for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.

\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.

$$

$$
\begin{aligned}

These values describe the enumeration context only; they do not, by themselves, make any biological interval real.

\paragraph{Reality contact and protocol scope.}
The curated interval contact loaded $168$ finite rows from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}. The required input classes were all present: \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}. The classifier identifier was \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified during the contact audit. The protocol timestamps separate the rule from the observation: the rule package was fixed at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the curated evidence was observed at 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00. The analysis explicitly used no translation input and no structure input. Thus the contact tests whether the previously fixed start/stop-window classifier distinguishes curated intervals from matched non-ORF decoys inside this dataset.

\paragraph{Observed finite-row outcome.}
The positive interval test accepted $72$ of $72$ curated positives, for accept rate $1.0$, with exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The matched decoy test rejected $72$ of $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, for reject rate $1.0$, with the same exact one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The predeclared paired comparison recorded $72$ wins in $72$ positive/decoy pairs, hence win rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The boundary controls were not promoted: $24$ boundary cases yielded $0$ promotions and promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The finite split check used three held-out folds. In the left-locus fold, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$. In the middle-locus fold, the same counts held: $24$ positives at accept rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys at reject rate $1.0$. In the right-locus fold, again $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$. All three folds passed. A prior window-enumeration check also reports one external start/stop-window record loaded, one input window, and one classified window, with scope restricted to \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}. The self-check gate count is $3$, and its recorded boundary says that codon geometry is not promoted across biological layers.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The contact supports a narrow statement: on the $168$-row curated interval dataset, a fixed start/stop-window eligibility classifier separates $72$ curated positives from $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, leaves $24$ boundary cases unpromoted, and remains stable across three held-out locus folds. This is a finite contact with curated annotation data, not an inference from codon geometry alone.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this record establishes that any interval is translated in a cell. Nothing establishes folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical mechanism, biological function, phenotype, organism-level effect, or a universal law about all genomes. The RefSeq annotation is a curated biological input to the audit, not content generated by the finite codon geometry. The matched decoy and boundary controls classify only the rows in this contact package and do not license claims about windows outside the dataset unless another contact supplies the relevant biological observation.
