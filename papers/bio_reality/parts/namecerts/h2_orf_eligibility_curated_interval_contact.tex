\subsection{Curated Interval Contact for ORF Eligibility}\label{sec:bior-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether a finite start--stop codon enumeration can be attached to an observed biological interval without turning that attachment into a claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  The object under test is open-reading-frame eligibility in the narrow sense of interval compatibility with a curated coding-sequence annotation.  The contact is therefore not a protein claim.  It is a finite check that a predeclared start--stop-window classifier agrees with an external curated interval table on this dataset, while matched non-ORF decoys and boundary cases remain outside the accepted class.

These data explain the combinatorial coordinate used by the classifier, but by themselves they do not observe an ORF interval in a genome.

\paragraph{External interval contact.}
The separate biological contact is a curated interval dataset at \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}.  It contains $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  The required input classes are exactly the three recorded classes: positive curated ORFs, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary cases.  The protocol was frozen before the external observation: classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were fixed at 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the external evidence was observed at 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00.  The classifier identifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, and the row records that the classifier rules were not modified after contact.

\paragraph{Observed finite outcomes.}
The contact row has $72$ positive curated intervals, $72$ matched decoys, and $24$ boundary cases.  All $72$ positive intervals are accepted, giving acceptance rate $1.0$ with one-sided exact binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  All $72$ matched decoys are rejected, giving rejection rate $1.0$ with the same recorded one-sided value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired comparison has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, hence win rate $1.0$ and the same exact sign-test value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The boundary cap is also explicit: among $24$ boundary cases, $0$ are promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The split stability check is finite and threefold.  In the left-locus fold, $24$ positives are accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys are rejected at rate $1.0$.  The middle-locus fold has the same $24$ positive and $24$ decoy counts with the same rates.  The right-locus fold again has $24$ positives and $24$ decoys with acceptance and rejection rates $1.0$.  All three held-out split rows are marked as passed.  This is stronger than a single aggregate count because the same decision pattern survives the three predeclared locus folds, but it is still a finite dataset statement.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The conclusion licensed here is only interval-level ORF eligibility relative to this curated contact and this frozen classifier.  The start--stop-window coordinate does not establish translation, protein structure, thermodynamic or cellular admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  The matched decoy and boundary controls also do not classify arbitrary windows outside the $168$-row contact table.  The RefSeq annotation remains an external curated biological input rather than content generated by the codon geometry.  The self-check row records the same boundary as a three-gate check: no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Therefore this chapter closes only the following finite claim: in the stated RefSeq interval contact, under the frozen geometry-only ORF-window classifier, the positive, decoy, boundary, and held-out rows behave exactly as recorded above.
