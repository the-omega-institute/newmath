\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF eligibility}\label{sec:h2-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite contact under test.}
This record concerns the layer named \emph{orf eligibility}: the narrow question whether a nucleotide interval is admitted by a start--stop codon enumeration rule after a curated interval has been supplied. The biological object is not a protein, a transcript, a fold, or a function. It is the eligibility of a finite interval under a classifier whose inputs are boundary positions and codon symbols. The finite row therefore has two parts. First, the codon-side coordinate system gives a closed enumeration on which the classifier can operate. Second, an outside curated interval table supplies the biological contact needed to test whether the enumeration is being applied to annotated intervals rather than to geometry alone.

The upstream codon table used by the row contains the finite motif union
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The larger marker set has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The saturation fact records that the $f_3$ boundary is empty, and that saturating $R$ gives $M$ with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ absent from the saturated subcollection. In the same finite geometry, the $M$ face vector is $(20,38,26,8,1)$, the quotient has $10$ classes, and the quotient face vector is $(10,14,6,1)$. These numbers are background coordinates for the enumeration rule. They do not by themselves say that a biological interval exists.

\paragraph{Curated interval rows.}
The actual contact row uses a curated ORF interval dataset loaded from \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/curated\_orf\_interval\_contact\_dataset.json}. The loaded table has $168$ rows, drawn from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K--12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$. The row explicitly records three required input classes: \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{boundary\_case}. Thus the test is not merely a scan over codon strings; it is a comparison among curated positive intervals, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary controls under one frozen rule.

The classifier is identified as \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}. Its rules were not modified during the contact check. The protocol was fixed at $2026$-$05$-$29$T$19{:}11{:}13$+00:00, before the outside evidence timestamp $2026$-$05$-$30$T$10{:}29{:}31$+00:00. The row also records that the analysis used no translation or structure input, that baselines were predeclared, that held-out splits were fixed, and that input classes were fixed. This matters because the reported contact is an interval-level test of a frozen start--stop-window rule, not a fit performed after reading the curated positives.

\paragraph{Observed finite separation.}
The finite contact is strong in the limited sense appropriate to this layer. Among $72$ positive curated intervals, $72$ were accepted, giving acceptance rate $1.0$ and an exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. Among $72$ matched decoys, $72$ were rejected, giving rejection rate $1.0$ with the same reported upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The predeclared paired baseline comparison also has $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, and upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.

The split check repeats the same finite pattern across three held-out folds. In \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$. The middle fold has the same $24/24$ positive acceptance and $24/24$ decoy rejection. The right fold also has $24/24$ positive acceptance and $24/24$ decoy rejection. Boundary cases were not promoted: among $24$ boundary inputs, $0$ were promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Layer verdict.}
The finite row supports a local statement only: after attaching the curated RefSeq CDS-interval contact, the frozen start--stop-window classifier separates the supplied positive intervals from the supplied matched decoys, while leaving supplied boundary cases unpromoted. The scope field is \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only}; the earlier single-window start--stop enumeration row likewise had scope \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only} and classified $1$ input window as $1$ classified window. Together these rows say that the contact requirement has been met for this curated interval experiment, not that geometry alone has become a biological observation.

No stronger biological conclusion is licensed here. This chapter does not infer translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, or universality from the interval test. It also does not classify windows outside the supplied table. The RefSeq annotation remains a curated biological input to the experiment rather than content generated by the codon-coordinate kernel. Any claim about realized translation, folded protein structure, physical viability, biological activity, causal mechanism, or a general law across organisms would require another contact designed for that layer.
