\subsection{Curated interval contact for ORF-window eligibility}
\label{sec:bior-orf-eligibility-curated-interval-contact}

\paragraph{Question fixed by the finite record.}
The biological object considered here is the weakest open-reading-frame eligibility predicate obtained from start and stop codon enumeration.  The predicate is not a claim that a transcript is translated, that a protein folds, that a molecule is physically admissible, or that a biological role is present.  It is only the question whether an externally supplied nucleotide interval belongs to the finite class recognized by the predeclared start--stop-window classifier.  The finite contact used for this chapter is a curated interval table derived from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3, with snapshot date 2026-05-30.  The table contains $168$ rows and has exactly the required input classes
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{boundary\_case},\quad \text{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy},\quad \text{positive\_curated\_orf}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classifier is identified as \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}.  Its rules were fixed before the external biological intervals were observed: the protocol timestamp is 2026-05-29T19:11:13+00:00, while the outside evidence timestamp is 2026-05-30T10:29:31+00:00.  The recorded flags state that the baselines, input classes, held-out splits, and classifier rules were all fixed in advance, and that the analysis used no translation or structure input.

\paragraph{Curated interval test.}
The curated interval table supplies the missing contact for this weak layer.  Among the $168$ rows, the strong statistics are arranged as $72$ positive curated ORF rows, $72$ matched non-ORF decoy rows, and $24$ boundary cases.  The positive interval test accepted $72$ of $72$ positive rows, with accept rate $1.0$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The matched decoy test rejected $72$ of $72$ decoys, with reject rate $1.0$ and the same recorded one-sided binomial value
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared pairwise baseline comparison gives $72$ wins in $72$ positive--decoy pairs, hence win rate $1.0$ and again
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary-control cap is also explicit: $24$ boundary cases were examined, $0$ were promoted, and the promoted fraction was $0.0$.

The interval record contains $3$ held-out splits.  In the left-locus fold, $24$ positives were accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys were rejected at rate $1.0$.  The middle-locus fold has the same counts and rates, and the right-locus fold has the same counts and rates.  Each split is marked as passed.  Therefore the finite result is not merely a pooled count: it is also stable across the three named partitions \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_left\_locus\_fold}, \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_middle\_locus\_fold}, and \texttt{refseq\_nc\_000913\_right\_locus\_fold}.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
Within the finite table and the fixed classifier, the claim is ready at the \texttt{external\_orf\_interval\_contact\_only} scope.  The evidence consists of the $168$-row curated interval contact, perfect acceptance of the $72$ positive curated intervals, perfect rejection of the $72$ matched decoys, non-promotion of all $24$ boundary cases, three stable held-out splits, and the predeclared paired advantage over the matched baseline.  The result is therefore an externally contacted ORF-window eligibility result for this finite dataset, not a purely coordinate-internal assertion.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No conclusion about translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter.  The finite contact does not assert that the accepted intervals are translated in a cell, that a protein product is structurally realized, that any molecule is physically permitted, that any function is present, or that the classifier is a global law for unobserved windows.  The NCBI RefSeq contact remains curated biological input outside the finite codon kernel, and the matched decoy and boundary controls do not classify windows outside this dataset.  Any stronger biological statement requires a separate contact whose own rows test that stronger layer.
