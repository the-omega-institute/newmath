\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life RBP Bridge at the Q6 Residual Coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA panel in which each admissible row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls for abundance, composition, transcript architecture, and perturbation state. The relevant row shape is not a generic codon-usage table. It is a joined panel in which the coordinate, translation readout, RNA half-life measurement, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss arm, wild-type rescue arm, and binding-defective or non-target rescue arm are all keyed to the same biological window or to a declared same-transcript aggregation rule. The available finite translation contact for the linked mTOR translation run contains $n=6494$ rows, with run identifier $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, runtime $9.469$ seconds, and readout source \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}. This row count is sufficient to name a translation-side finite surface, but it is not by itself a decay-factor RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Statistic to be read.}
The proposed bridge asks whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels differ in RNA half-life after the translation readout and all declared nuisance variables have been attached. A finite statistic for this question may be written as a contrast of residual half-life after conditioning on the matched row data:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6,H\mid T,Z}
  &= E\bigl[H \mid q=\mathrm{high}, T, Z, O, L, R_{wt}, R_{bd}\bigr] \\
  &\quad - E\bigl[H \mid q=\mathrm{low}, T, Z, O, L, R_{wt}, R_{bd}\bigr],
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ is RNA half-life, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $Z$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline terms, $O$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L$ is target-RBP loss, $R_{wt}$ is wild-type rescue, and $R_{bd}$ is binding-defective or non-target rescue. The bridge is allowed only when the contrast follows measured local occupancy, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue. If any one of these arms is absent, the row panel can still describe a bounded RNA half-life association, but it does not identify an RBP-mediated decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Available finite contacts.}
The mTOR translation surface supplies the strongest directly cited finite translation readout here: $6494$ rows from the Thoreen-linked run. A separate RBP-binding calibration fact is present in the corpus with run identifier $b225890251c2$, $900$ total peaks, and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases. That second number demonstrates that finite RBP-binding rows exist elsewhere in the evidence table, but it is not the same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue panel demanded by this claim. A third finite cross-layer contact, the RNA $m^6A$ stoichiometry exon-architecture run, reports $n=60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance true, coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$, and runtime $78.316$ seconds. This additional contact is useful as a scale comparison for finite transcript-layer readouts, but it also does not supply the RBP perturbation structure required here. These facts keep the chapter grounded in concrete finite data while preventing an unsupported promotion from translation or generic RNA-binding measurements to the specific RNA half-life RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Null and acceptance rule.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after controlling amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout. Under this null, the coordinate may correlate with finite RNA stability only as a bounded descriptor. A positive bridge requires a stricter pattern: the half-life contrast must be paired with experimentally decoupled measured RBP occupancy at the editable RNA window, must disappear when the target RBP is removed or disabled, must reappear under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and must not reappear under binding-defective or non-target rescue. Thus the readout is not merely $q \mapsto H$ and not merely $q \mapsto T \mapsto H$; it is a finite intervention-sensitive relation among coordinate, translation, half-life, occupancy, loss, and rescue rows.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter licenses no claim about protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the specified RBP-decay bridge, or universality. The $6494$-row translation run is a finite translation-side contact, not an RNA half-life mechanism. The $900$-peak RBP-binding calibration fact is a finite binding-side contact, not a same-window perturbation and rescue panel for this coordinate. The $60000$-row RNA-modification contact shows that large transcript-layer finite surfaces can be handled, but it is not an RNA half-life bridge for the present subject. Therefore the present record should be read as a bounded, reviewable specification for a finite cross-layer test. It becomes a positive BioReality bridge only after the missing same-organism, same-window RBP occupancy, target-loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target rescue, and declared half-life statistic are joined to the translation and RNA half-life rows.
