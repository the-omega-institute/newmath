\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With an RBP Occupancy Gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel whose rows are coding-transcript windows carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  Each row is required to carry the coordinate label, the coding transcript or CDS identifier, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, transcription and abundance controls, local sequence and structure controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy measurement at the editable RNA window.  The intended contrast is not a generic codon-usage contrast.  It is the bounded contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after the amino-acid sequence has already been held fixed.

The currently verified translation contact for the named mTOR translation surface is finite: run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ parsed $6494$ rows from the Thoreen-associated readout source
$\mathrm{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}$
in $9.469$ seconds.  That number is used here only as evidence that a same-study translation table can be represented as a finite row set.  It is not, by itself, an RNA half-life table and it is not an RBP perturbation table.  A separate verified RBP-binding contact exists at another layer: run $b225890251c2$ reported $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds for the RBNS/eCLIP binding surface.  Those numbers show that finite RBP occupancy-style rows can be audited as rows, but they do not yet identify the decay-factor RBP, the same transcript windows, or the perturbation and rescue rows required by this chapter.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
For a row $r$, let $Q(r)$ be the high-versus-low residual coordinate class, $T(r)$ the attached translation readout, $H(r)$ the RNA half-life readout, and $O(r)$ the measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the same RNA window.  Let $Z(r)$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the matched mRNA control.  The positive bridge requires a finite residual half-life contrast that remains visible after translation conditioning and controls,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \widehat{E}\bigl[H(r) \mid Q(r)=\mathrm{high},T(r),Z(r)\bigr]
   - \widehat{E}\bigl[H(r) \mid Q(r)=\mathrm{low},T(r),Z(r)\bigr],
\end{aligned}
$$
with rank sufficiency in both coordinate classes.  This contrast alone is not the bridge.  It becomes an RBP-mediated bridge only when the same finite panel also shows the ordered occupancy and perturbation pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q \rightsquigarrow O, \qquad
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{RBP\ loss}} \approx 0, \qquad
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} \approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{baseline}}, \qquad
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}} \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The RBP identity must be chosen before inspecting the coordinate effect.  A coordinate-associated half-life difference cannot select its own RBP after the fact.

\paragraph{Readback discipline.}
The translation table with $6494$ rows supplies only the translation side of the required row join.  The RBNS/eCLIP surface with $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases supplies only a separate binding-readout precedent.  The chapter therefore treats the named conjecture as a sharply bounded finite-row criterion: a positive result would require all required columns to be present on the same organism, same transcript or CDS, and same editable window.  The readback is the five-part pattern consisting of translation-conditioned half-life effect, measured local RBP occupancy change, collapse under target-RBP loss, restoration under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

If the half-life contrast is present but local RBP occupancy is unchanged, the result remains an RNA-stability association.  If occupancy differs but the target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, the result remains a coordinate/occupancy correlation.  If loss collapses the effect but wild-type rescue does not restore it, the perturbation is not specific enough.  If binding-defective or non-target rescue restores the effect, the bridge is rejected because the claimed binding-dependent mechanism is not isolated.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null hypothesis is row exchangeability of high and low residual coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on $T$ and $Z$.  Equivalently, the coordinate label may index a finite transcript descriptor without licensing a cross-layer explanation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(r) \perp Q(r) \mid T(r), Z(r)
\end{aligned}
$$
unless the prespecified RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue sequence is observed on the same finite panel.  The null is also retained when a half-life association can be absorbed by transcription rate, transcript abundance, isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, amino-acid composition, or the same-row translation readout.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA decay mechanism follows from codon-window geometry or from the existence of a translation table.  No RBP mechanism follows from a binding dataset unless the measured occupancy is same-window, prespecified, experimentally decoupled from the coordinate where needed, and paired with target-RBP loss plus wild-type and defective-control rescue.  The finite mTOR translation run and the finite RBP-binding run are useful row-level contacts, but neither supplies protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those claims would require separate layer-matched measurements and their own finite statistics.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The chapter is ready as a bounded BioReality criterion, not as a positive biological discovery claim.  The available verified facts establish finite translation rows and a separate finite RBP-binding surface; they do not yet establish the joined RNA half-life, same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss, rescue, and control panel.  Thus the scientific content is the admissibility rule for when a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate can be promoted from a translation-conditioned RNA half-life descriptor to an RBP-bridged cross-layer relation.
