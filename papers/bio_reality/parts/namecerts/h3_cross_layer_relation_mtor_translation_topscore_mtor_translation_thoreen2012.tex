\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP-bridge test}
\label{sec:bioreality-mtor-translation-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record named \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} contributes a finite translation-side panel with $6494$ rows, parsed from the GEO payload \texttt{GSE36892\_RAW.tar}.  The run identifier is \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, and the recorded execution time is $9.469$ seconds.  In this chapter the panel is not treated as a completed RNA-decay mechanism.  It is used only as the translation-readout side of a stricter finite-row test: each admissible row must carry a coding-transcript or CDS identifier, a synonymous residual coordinate label of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ kind after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, a matched RNA half-life readout, matched abundance and transcription controls, and an externally prespecified decay-factor RBP measurement at the editable RNA window.

\paragraph{Coordinate split.}
Let $I$ be the finite set of joined transcript/CDS rows that survive identifier matching and quality filters.  For each $i \in I$, write $q_i \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ for the synonymous residual-coordinate class after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented out, $T_i$ for the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ for the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ for measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and $Z_i$ for the collected control vector.  The intended finite statistic is not a naked association $q_i \mapsto H_i$.  It is the residual contrast that remains after translation and the declared controls are attached:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{Contr}\bigl(H_i, q_i \mid T_i, O_i, Z_i\bigr),\\
Z_i
  &= \{\text{transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC/dinucleotide composition,}\\
  &\qquad \text{codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

A positive chapter-level bridge would require the high-coordinate and low-coordinate groups to differ in translation-conditioned RNA half-life only in the rows where the prespecified RBP occupancy changes in the expected direction.  The same signal must collapse when the target RBP is lost, return under wild-type rescue, and fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Without that finite perturbation and rescue pattern, the row panel remains an RNA-stability association or a translation-conditioned descriptor, not an RBP-mediated bridge.

\paragraph{Reality contacts used as boundaries.}
The translation panel supplies the largest row count directly relevant to this chapter, $n=6494$, but it does not measure decay-factor RBP occupancy or rescue.  A separate RBP binding benchmark, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode}, records $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with run time $223.648043$ seconds.  That benchmark shows that RBP affinity or binding rows can be finitely represented, but it does not by itself join the Thoreen translation rows to same-window RNA half-life, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective controls.  As a second calibration point for row-scale molecular readbacks, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6am\_methylation.m6am\_flanking\_identity\_stoichiometry\_crownseq} records $31886$ total rows, $19406$ distinct sites, $20942$ rows in the variable subset, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, a saturated fraction $0.343223$ above $0.95$, and cross-cell concordance marked true.  Those numbers demonstrate that a molecular layer can be represented with explicit continuous stoichiometry and cross-condition checks, but they are not RNA half-life or RBP perturbation data for the present coordinate.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null hypothesis is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the mandatory conditioning variables are attached.  In finite-row terms, permutation of $q_i$ within matched strata should leave the translation-conditioned half-life contrast unchanged up to the declared statistic.  A half-life difference that survives only before translation conditioning is not sufficient.  A half-life difference that survives translation conditioning but lacks measured local RBP occupancy remains a bounded stability association.  A half-life and occupancy association that lacks coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue remains an unclosed mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Acceptance rule.}
A ready positive bridge would have to satisfy all of the following finite tests on the same joined panel.  First, the row set must contain the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, RNA half-life, matched mRNA abundance, and all listed controls.  Second, the decay-factor RBP must be named before testing the coordinate contrast, and its local occupancy must be measured at the relevant RNA window.  Third, the half-life contrast must track experimentally separated occupancy rather than merely motif density, RNA structure, GC composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, or batch.  Fourth, target-RBP loss must remove the coordinate-associated half-life effect.  Fifth, wild-type RBP rescue must restore the effect, while binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail to restore it.  These conditions are intentionally stronger than a translation panel alone, because the proposed object is a cross-layer relation from synonymous residual coordinate through translation-conditioned RNA half-life into a specific RBP-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The present finite data do not license a claim that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate causes translation, RNA decay, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, phenotype, or a universal sequence law.  The $6494$-row Thoreen-derived translation panel can support a bounded translation-readout surface after matched controls, and the separate RBP and stoichiometry rows show that other molecular readouts can be finite and auditable.  They do not supply the same-organism, same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, rescue, and non-target control rows required here.  Therefore the chapter records a precise testable boundary: the coordinate may enter an RNA half-life analysis only after same-row translation conditioning, and it may enter an RBP-decay bridge only after the specified occupancy-loss-rescue pattern is observed on the same finite matched panel.
