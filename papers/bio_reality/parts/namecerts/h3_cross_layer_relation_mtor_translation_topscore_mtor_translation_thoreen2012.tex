\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP bridge test}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record treats the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate as a finite label attached to matched transcript or coding-sequence rows.  The intended panel is not merely a codon-usage table and not merely a half-life table: a row must carry the high-versus-low residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP loss, wild-type target-RBP rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.  The available translation contact for the linked mTOR translation surface is finite and concrete: run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ parsed $n=6494$ rows from $\mathrm{GSE36892\_RAW.tar}$, with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  This supplies a translation-readout surface for the chapter, but it does not by itself supply RNA half-life, local RBP occupancy, perturbation, or rescue rows.

\paragraph{Claim tested here.}
The positive claim would be a bounded cross-layer relation in which the coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual survives the required transcription, abundance, composition, codon-context, isoform, batch, and translation-readout controls, and then follows the experimentally separated RBP bridge.  In row notation, the admissible positive pattern has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
& q_i \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\},\quad T_i=\mathrm{same\mbox{-}row\ translation\ readout},\quad H_i=\mathrm{RNA\ half\mbox{-}life},\\
& O_i=\mathrm{local\ decay\mbox{-}factor\ RBP\ occupancy},\quad L_i=\mathrm{target\mbox{-}RBP\ loss},\quad W_i=\mathrm{wild\mbox{-}type\ rescue},\\
& B_i=\mathrm{binding\mbox{-}defective\ or\ non\mbox{-}target\ rescue},\\
& H_i \not\perp q_i \mid Z_i,T_i,\quad O_i \not\perp q_i \mid Z_i,T_i,\\
& \Delta_H(q_i \mid Z_i,T_i)\ \mathrm{collapses\ under}\ L_i,\quad
\Delta_H(q_i \mid Z_i,T_i)\ \mathrm{returns\ under}\ W_i,\\
& \Delta_H(q_i \mid Z_i,T_i)\ \mathrm{does\ not\ return\ under}\ B_i .
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_i$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and rank-sufficiency controls.  The same-row translation readout is mandatory because the chapter is translation-conditioned; RNA half-life alone is not promoted to a translation-layer result.

\paragraph{What the finite facts currently establish.}
The linked mTOR translation run establishes only that a finite translation-readout table exists at the required scale for this subject class: $6494$ rows were read from the declared GEO raw archive.  A separate RBP-binding contact in the same evidence ledger is also finite, with run $b225890251c2$ reporting $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in $223.648043$ seconds.  That second fact is useful as a reminder that RBP evidence can be represented as finite rows, but it is not the same organism, same window, same transcript, same perturbation, or same rescue panel required for the decay-factor bridge here.  A further RNA-modification control surface, run $eb689b7dc73c$, contains $n=31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct sites, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, and a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at the $0.95$ threshold; it shows that row-level stoichiometric RNA surfaces can be audited in this corpus, but it is not an RNA half-life RBP-loss/rescue experiment for this coordinate.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The operative null is exchangeability of the high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after all required controls and the same-row translation readout are attached:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid Z_i,T_i .
\end{aligned}
$$
Even if this null is rejected for half-life, the RBP mechanism is still not licensed unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy changes in the predicted direction, the coordinate and occupancy terms are experimentally decoupled, target-RBP loss removes the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  A half-life association without this occupancy-loss-rescue pattern remains a bounded RNA-stability association, not an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter therefore records a ready negative-to-conditional result: the mTOR translation table gives a finite translation-readout surface, and other ledger entries show finite RBP-binding and RNA-row readouts, but the present payload does not contain the joined panel needed for a positive RNA half-life RBP bridge.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA-decay mechanism follows from a same-row translation readout alone.  No measured RBP mechanism follows from a contact name, an RBP peak table, motif density, RNA structure, transcript abundance, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, or batch correction.  No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law is claimed here unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that measurement and the finite statistic is recomputed on the joined rows.
