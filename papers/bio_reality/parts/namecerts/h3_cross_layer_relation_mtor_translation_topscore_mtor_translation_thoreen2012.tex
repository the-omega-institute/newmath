\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP occupancy gate}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Claim surface.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^{*}_{Q6}$ is evaluated only after quotienting by amino-acid identity. The intended biological object is not the coordinate itself, but a joined panel whose rows carry the coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy of a prespecified RNA decay factor, perturbation of that factor, rescue by the wild-type factor, and negative rescue controls. The coordinate contrast is therefore high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label at fixed amino-acid content, with the readout restricted to translation-conditioned RNA half-life.

\paragraph{Finite contacts already present.}
The translation-side contact is anchored by the Thoreen mTOR translation dataset used for the present claim family. The finite run records $n=6494$ rows, read from the GEO raw archive \texttt{GSE36892\_RAW.tar}, with runtime $9.469$ seconds and run id \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}. This supplies a row-level translation-efficiency boundary for the claim, not a protein-output or mechanism result. A separate RBP contact family is represented by the RBNS/eCLIP binding comparison record, whose finite summary reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run \texttt{b225890251c2}. That record is useful as evidence that finite RBP occupancy tables can be carried as row-level contacts, but it is not yet the required same-window decay-factor occupancy panel for this particular RNA half-life bridge. A nearby RNA-output control family is represented by the poly(A)-tail contact, with $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows and a non-concordant cross-species flag; this illustrates why RNA-layer readouts must remain row-, organism-, and assay-specific rather than being promoted to a general RNA-stability law.

\paragraph{Required finite statistic.}
Let each eligible row $i$ contain a residual coordinate class $q_i \in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$, a same-row translation readout $T_i$, an RNA half-life residual $H_i$, measured local occupancy $O_i$ for the nominated decay-factor RBP, target-factor loss indicator $L_i$, wild-type rescue indicator $W_i$, and binding-defective or non-target rescue indicator $D_i$. The admissible bridge is a finite contrast in which the coordinate-associated half-life residual appears only after the translation boundary is attached and then follows the RBP intervention pattern:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\leadsto H_i \mid T_i, Z_i,\\
q_i &\leadsto O_i \text{ at the editable RNA window},\\
q_i \leadsto H_i &\text{ collapses under } L_i,\\
q_i \leadsto H_i &\text{ returns under } W_i,\\
q_i \leadsto H_i &\text{ does not return under } D_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_i$ denotes the declared control block: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler-baseline controls. The row statistic is bounded: it may certify only a translation-conditioned RNA half-life association mediated through the specified RBP contact pattern.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the same-row translation readout and the control block are attached. A measured half-life difference alone is insufficient. The effect must be coupled to measured local decay-factor occupancy, must disappear when the target RBP is lost, must return under wild-type rescue, and must fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue. If any one of these finite conditions is absent, the record remains an RNA half-life association or a coordinate descriptor, not an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Why the current evidence is bounded.}
The Thoreen-derived translation contact gives a concrete translation readout surface with $6494$ rows, and the RBNS/eCLIP record shows a separate finite occupancy-style surface with $900$ peak rows. These are not yet the same rows and not yet the same RNA windows. The RNA half-life contacts likewise provide organism-scoped half-life estimates, but they do not by themselves contain decay-factor occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or defective-rescue controls. Thus the present record is scientifically meaningful as a design-level cross-layer requirement: it specifies exactly what finite data would be needed to turn a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual into a bounded RBP-mediated claim.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone, and no RNA decay mechanism follows from the presence of a translation readout alone. The coordinate label, codon-window geometry, and same-row translation readout do not imply ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality. Each of those stronger statements would require its own layer-matched measurement and its own finite statistic. In this chapter the strongest licensed positive statement is conditional: if a future same-organism, same-window panel supplies translation readout, RNA half-life, local decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue on the same matched rows, then the declared statistic can test whether the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate participates in a bounded translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge.
