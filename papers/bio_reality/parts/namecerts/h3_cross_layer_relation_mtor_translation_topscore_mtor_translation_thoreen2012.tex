\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life boundary with a decay-factor RBP gate}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-mtor-translation-topscore-mtor-translation-thoreen2012}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA panel in which each admissible row carries a coding-transcript or CDS identifier, a synonymous residual coordinate label of type $B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched abundance and transcription controls, and an explicitly named decay-factor RBP route.  The available finite translation contact for the mTOR-linked readout is the Thoreen-style translation payload with $n=6494$ rows, stored under run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ and sourced from \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}; the recorded runtime for that extraction is $9.469$ seconds.  These numbers establish that the translation side is not merely a verbal route: it has a finite row count and a named experimental payload.  They do not, by themselves, establish an RNA-decay mechanism or an RBP-mediated half-life effect.

\paragraph{Coordinate split and required readout.}
The coordinate comparison is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after amino-acid quotienting.  A row is eligible for the present boundary only if the translation readout is attached on the same row before the RNA half-life term is interpreted.  In notation, the intended finite statistic has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i,T_i,H_i,Z_i) \longmapsto \Delta H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is the RNA half-life readout, and $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline controls.  A half-life contrast that is not conditioned on $T_i$ remains a bounded RNA-stability association at most.  It is not promoted to a translation-realized result simply because a translation dataset exists elsewhere in the project memory.

\paragraph{RBP bridge test.}
The stronger bridge requires an experimentally decoupled decay-factor RBP measurement at the editable RNA window.  The local rule is stricter than an occupancy correlation.  The same finite panel must distinguish measured target-RBP occupancy present versus absent or reduced, target-RBP loss versus wild-type target-RBP rescue, and wild-type rescue versus binding-defective or non-target rescue.  In finite-row terms, the positive bridge is allowed only if the following pattern is present on matched rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(q_i \mid Z_i) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{RBP\ loss}) \approx 0,\\
&\Delta H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \approx \Delta H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i),\\
&\Delta H(q_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}) \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ denotes measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The RBP route is not supplied by the mTOR translation payload.  As a separate calibration surface, the RBP-binding memory contains an RBNS/eCLIP-style finite contact with $n_{\mathrm{peaks}}=900$, cross-RBP concordance equal to $3$, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds under run $b225890251c2$.  That contact shows that finite RBP-binding readouts exist in the surrounding evidence system, but it does not fill the exact same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue rows needed here.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null hypothesis is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after all declared controls and the same-row translation readout are attached.  Equivalently, for matched rows with the same amino-acid sequence class and comparable control vector $Z$, the coordinate label should not predict $H_i$ once translation and transcript-level covariates are accounted for:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i,Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
If this null is broken for half-life but the occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern is absent, the result remains a finite RNA half-life association.  It is not an RBP bridge.  If occupancy changes but target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life term, the RBP reading fails.  If wild-type rescue restores the term but a binding-defective or non-target rescue also restores it, the bridge again fails because the rescue is not binding-specific.

\paragraph{Comparator evidence.}
A useful comparator for scale is the m6Am flanking-identity stoichiometry run $eb689b7dc73c$, which contains $n=31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, and $20942$ variable-subset rows.  That run records a GC-matched null $p=0.000833$, partial correlations $0.268094$ for the full set, $0.098666$ for the low-tail subset, and $0.168335$ for the variable subset, with a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$ and cross-cell concordance marked true.  Those scalar values illustrate the kind of finite statistic that would be needed for this chapter: row counts, controls, a declared null, and a bounded readout statistic.  They do not transfer biological support to the mTOR translation/RNA-half-life/RBP claim; they only clarify the evidential standard demanded by the present boundary.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The present chapter is therefore a bounded cross-layer gate, not a positive mechanistic result.  The finite mTOR translation payload licenses discussion of a same-row translation-readout boundary with $6494$ rows.  The separate RBP-binding contact licenses discussion of finite RBP-binding surfaces in general, with $900$ peaks and cross-RBP concordance $3$.  Neither source supplies the complete joined panel containing $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Consequently no translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, causal mechanism, adaptation, or universal biological law follows from this record.  A positive claim would require the missing joined rows and the finite bridge statistic above; without them, the coordinate remains a controlled descriptor or, at most, a translation-conditioned RNA half-life association.
