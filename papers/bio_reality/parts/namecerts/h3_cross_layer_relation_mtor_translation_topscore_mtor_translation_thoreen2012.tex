\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP-decay boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns finite coding-transcript rows carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and a proposed decay-factor RNA-binding-protein bridge.  The coordinate label is the high-versus-low $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual class after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The biological question is not whether codon geometry alone predicts an outcome.  It is whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual can be promoted to a bounded cross-layer statement only after the translation readout and a measured RBP-decay panel have both been attached on the same finite rows.

The available finite translation contact for the mTOR translation surface is a Thoreen-style readout table with $n=6494$ rows, fetched from the recorded GEO raw archive source and parsed in run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$ with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  In the notation of this chapter, those rows supply the translation boundary $T$ and allow the coordinate-to-translation part of a joined panel to be declared as finite rather than merely verbal.  The separate RBP-binding survey contact has $n_{\mathrm{peaks}}=900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run $b225890251c2$, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  That second contact is useful only as evidence that finite RBP occupancy surfaces can be represented in the same audit language; it is not, by itself, the required same-window decay-factor RBP perturbation panel for this conjecture.

\paragraph{Statistic to be read.}
For each matched row $i$, let $q_i\in\{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\}$ denote the residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, let $T_i$ denote the attached translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  Let $L_i$ encode target-RBP loss, $W_i$ wild-type rescue, and $D_i$ binding-defective or non-target rescue.  A positive bridge is allowed only for a finite statistic of the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
&= \widehat{E}\bigl[H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,D_i\bigr]
 - \widehat{E}\bigl[H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i,O_i,L_i,W_i,D_i\bigr],\\
Z_i
&=(\mathrm{transcription\ rate},\mathrm{local\ RNA\ structure},\mathrm{motif\ density},\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{batch}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The statistic is bridge-positive only if the coordinate-associated half-life difference follows the measured local occupancy change, collapses under loss of the named target RBP, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under the binding-defective or non-target control.  If any one of these four requirements is absent, the finite observation remains an RNA-stability association or a translation-conditioned descriptor, not an RBP-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Matched null.}
The null model keeps the high and low coordinate labels exchangeable after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout.  In finite-row terms, the null is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i,Z_i
\end{aligned}
$$
unless the occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows produce the specified pattern.  The current mTOR translation contact establishes that a translation readout table of $6494$ rows is available for the broader translation surface, but the supplied memory does not contain the same-organism, same-window RNA half-life plus RBP occupancy plus perturbation/rescue matrix needed to evaluate this bridge statistic.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The finite translation fact cited here is the mTOR translation row set with $n=6494$ and source \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}.  It is suitable for recording a translation-readout boundary, not for recording RNA decay, local RBP occupancy, or rescue behavior.  The finite RBP-binding fact cited here is the RBNS/eCLIP-style binding contact with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  It is a binding-surface contact at the RBP layer, but it is not declared as the prespecified decay-factor RBP, does not provide the same transcript windows as the mTOR translation rows, and does not include target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter therefore permits only a bounded formulation of the requested bridge.  The coordinate label may be joined to translation rows and may be tested against RNA half-life rows when such rows are available.  It may not be called translation realization from half-life alone.  It may not be called an RNA decay mechanism from translation conditioning alone.  It may not be called an RBP bridge unless measured same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control behavior are all present in the finite panel.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the stated RBP-decay bridge, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate geometry, the mTOR translation row count, the RBP binding contact, or their names.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite evidence is sufficient to state the exact test and its failure modes, and it is sufficient to cite two concrete finite contacts: a $6494$-row mTOR translation surface and a $900$-peak RBP-binding surface.  It is not sufficient to report a positive translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge.  The present record is therefore a ready negative-boundary chapter: the bridge is mathematically specified, the finite contacts are named with row counts, and the promotion rule is blocked until the missing same-window occupancy and perturbation/rescue rows are attached.
