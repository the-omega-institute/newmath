\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP-bridge target}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record studied here is a finite matched-panel target for coding transcripts.  Each admissible row carries a synonymous residual-coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid identity, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance and transcription controls, and a proposed decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy surface at the editable RNA window.  The intended contrast is not merely high-coordinate versus low-coordinate codon geometry.  The contrast is a stricter cross-layer pattern: a coordinate-associated half-life residual may be reported as an RBP bridge only when it remains visible after the same-row translation readout is attached, tracks measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, disappears under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and is not restored by a binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The available translation-side finite row evidence is the Thoreen mTOR translation readout attached to the claim id \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012}.  Its verified payload contains $6494$ rows, read from the archived source \texttt{GSE36892\_RAW.tar}, with a recorded local runtime of $9.469$ seconds.  This number is used only as a finite translation-readout boundary: it gives a row scale at which a same-gene or same-CDS translation covariate can be joined, not an RNA-decay mechanism by itself.  The nearby RBP-binding reality contact has a different shape: the RBNS/eCLIP binding check reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  That evidence shows that finite RBP-binding surfaces can be audited in the same framework, but it is not yet the required same-organism, same-window decay-factor occupancy and perturbation panel for the present claim.  Two additional finite contacts mark the scale of adjacent layer checks: the CPE/poly(A)-tail record has $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows and is not cross-species concordant, while the N-terminal acetylation record has $657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, and AUROC $0.942081$ on $622$ high/low rows.  These figures are cited only to anchor finite-row discipline across neighboring readout layers; they do not supply the missing RNA half-life RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual-coordinate label for transcript or CDS row $i$, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the relevant RNA window.  Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and identifier-mapping controls.  The admissible bridge statistic is not the marginal association $q_i \to H_i$.  It is a finite controlled pattern of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
& q_i \not\perp H_i \mid T_i, Z_i, \\
& q_i \not\perp O_i \mid T_i, Z_i, \\
& q_i \to H_i \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss}, \\
& q_i \to H_i \text{ returns under wild-type target-RBP rescue}, \\
& q_i \to H_i \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The present finite data establish only the translation-readout side of this display at the $6494$-row Thoreen scale.  They do not establish the occupancy, loss, rescue, or binding-defective-control lines.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The matched null says that, after conditioning on translation readout and the declared transcript, sequence, isoform, abundance, and batch controls, high- and low-coordinate residual labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  A half-life difference without the occupancy/loss/rescue pattern remains an RNA-stability association.  A translation-conditioned half-life difference without measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy remains a bounded cross-layer candidate.  A measured RBP-binding association without coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls remains a binding correlation.  None of these weaker outcomes licenses an RBP-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter therefore records a ready negative-boundary result rather than a positive mechanistic bridge.  The Thoreen mTOR translation rows can be used to prevent promotion from RNA half-life alone to translation-conditioned interpretation, because the same-row translation covariate has a concrete finite source.  The record does not show translation realization from codon geometry, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality.  It also does not show measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue failure, or non-target rescue failure for the same windows.  A positive version of this claim requires a same-organism, same-window panel containing the residual-coordinate labels, translation readouts, mRNA abundance controls, RNA half-life measurements, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls, all evaluated under the finite controlled statistic above.
