\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life bridge for the Thoreen mTOR translation panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-mtor-translation-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
This record treats the Thoreen mTOR translation surface as a finite matched-row boundary for a proposed $B^{*}_{Q6}$ cross-layer RNA-stability question.  The available verified run for the mTOR translation claim contains $6494$ rows from the GEO payload at \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}, with a recorded runtime of $9.469$ seconds.  Those rows are useful as a translation readout surface: they can attach a same-gene or same-CDS translation-efficiency coordinate to a synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotienting.  They do not, by themselves, measure RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, or binding-defective control behavior.  The chapter therefore records a bounded panel design rather than a positive mechanistic result.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.}
The carrier is a finite table whose intended row has a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate, amino-acid identity already quotiented out, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life value, a measured local occupancy value for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, and matched control columns.  The required comparison is not merely high-coordinate versus low-coordinate half-life.  It is the stricter comparison in which any residual half-life contrast is conditioned on the translation readout and then checked against the RBP bridge columns.  In schematic form, the admissible statistic has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta H(q \mid T,Z) \quad \text{with}\quad Z=\{\text{transcription},\ \text{abundance},\ \text{RNA structure},\ \text{motif density},\ \text{GC/dinucleotide},\ \text{codon-pair},\ \text{isoform},\ \text{batch}\},\\
&\Delta H(q \mid T,Z) \text{ is bridge-readable only when } O_{\mathrm{RBP}},\ L_{\mathrm{RBP}},\ R_{\mathrm{WT}},\ R_{\mathrm{defect}} \text{ have the required finite pattern.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $H$ is RNA half-life, $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is measured local occupancy of the chosen decay-factor RBP, $L_{\mathrm{RBP}}$ is the target-RBP loss condition, $R_{\mathrm{WT}}$ is wild-type rescue, and $R_{\mathrm{defect}}$ denotes binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.  The mTOR translation run supplies only the $T$ side of this display for $6494$ rows; the other columns remain unfilled for this subject.

\paragraph{What the verified rows support.}
The verified mTOR run supports a translation-readout boundary for a finite panel.  It licenses the statement that a same-row translation covariate can be attached before any RNA half-life claim is interpreted.  It does not license an RNA-decay mechanism.  A separate RBP-binding verification in the ledger records $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases for an RBNS/eCLIP binding route, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  That aggregate binding fact is relevant as a sanity check on the kind of contact needed, but it is not the missing same-window decay-factor occupancy table for the Thoreen mTOR rows.  It also lacks target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue columns.  Thus the RBP-binding fact can only illustrate that binding contacts are a distinct layer; it cannot complete the bridge for this chapter.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is finite and row-exchangeable.  After conditioning on amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout, high- and low-$B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  A half-life difference that survives these controls is still not an RBP bridge unless measured local occupancy changes at the editable RNA window, target-RBP loss collapses the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails.  The null therefore separates three outcomes: a coordinate descriptor, a bounded RNA-stability association, and a true RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.  The current finite facts reach only the first translation-conditioned boundary and do not reach the third outcome.

\paragraph{Comparison with another finite cross-layer surface.}
A separate m6A stoichiometry run in the ledger contains $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites, cross-condition concordance marked true, and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$, with runtime $78.316$ seconds.  That record shows what a larger finite biochemical readout can look like when a layer-specific statistic is actually attached.  It is not evidence for the present mTOR/RBP bridge, but it helps fix the evidentiary standard: row count alone is not enough; the required layer-specific measurement must be present in the same analytic object.  For the mTOR translation subject, the verified $6494$ translation rows are therefore a necessary boundary column, not a sufficient RNA half-life or RBP perturbation panel.

\paragraph{Required bridge pattern.}
A positive chapter-specific bridge would require a same-organism, same-window table in which each row carries the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label, the Thoreen-style translation readout or an explicitly joined equivalent, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The permitted readback is narrow: translation-conditioned RNA half-life effect, local occupancy change, collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Without all of these finite columns, a half-life association remains an RNA-stability association, and an occupancy association remains an occupancy association; neither becomes a decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA half-life difference becomes a decay-factor RBP mechanism without measured same-window occupancy and perturbation-rescue behavior.  No binding-defective rescue, non-target control, RNA-structure-only account, motif-density-only account, transcription-rate account, isoform-state account, transcript-abundance account, GC or dinucleotide account, codon-pair-context account, or batch account may be counted as support for the RBP bridge.  The present record also makes no claim about protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite statistics.

\paragraph{Verdict for this chapter.}
The chapter is ready as a bounded BioReality record because the finite mTOR translation run supplies a concrete same-row translation surface with $6494$ rows and because adjacent verified facts clarify that binding or stoichiometry evidence must remain layer-specific.  The positive biological bridge is not yet established.  The present result is a disciplined negative boundary: the available mTOR translation rows can condition a future RNA half-life analysis, but they do not themselves contain the RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective control columns needed for a cross-layer RBP-mediated RNA half-life claim.
