\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life Requires an RBP Perturbation Panel}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a bounded cross-layer question, not a completed mechanistic claim. The carrier is a finite matched RNA table whose intended rows contain a synonymous residual coordinate label $q$, a coding-transcript or CDS identifier, a same-row translation readout $T$, a matched mRNA abundance or transcriptional control, an RNA half-life readout $H$, local composition and structure controls $Z$, and, for any positive RBP bridge claim, measured occupancy and perturbation rows for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein. The observed translation readout currently attached to the mTOR translation surface is finite: the verified run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} reports $n=6494$ rows, a runtime of $9.469$ seconds, and the readout source \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}. This establishes a concrete translation-side contact size for the chapter. It does not, by itself, attach RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or rescue rows.

\paragraph{Controlled statement.}
Let $i$ range over the finite joined rows that survive identifier matching. Each eligible row must carry a high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotienting, a translation readout $T_i$, an RNA half-life readout $H_i$, and control fields including transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and rank diagnostics. The admissible test is not the marginal association $q_i \mapsto H_i$. It is the conditioned and perturbation-aware comparison
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
&= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i)
   - \widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}}
&= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i,T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{target\ RBP\ loss}),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}
&= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i,T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{wild\ type\ RBP\ rescue}),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{bad\ rescue}}
&= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i,T_i,Z_i,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive RBP bridge is licensed only when the same finite table shows local measured occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, the half-life effect follows the experimentally decoupled occupancy contrast, the effect collapses under target-RBP loss, the wild-type target-RBP rescue restores the signal, and the binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it. Without those rows, the coordinate may remain a bounded descriptor or an RNA-stability association, but not an RBP-mediated mechanism.

\paragraph{Null model and break condition.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to $H$ after conditioning on $T$ and the declared controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i,Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
The stronger bridge null remains in force unless all RBP rows are present. A half-life difference without measured occupancy, occupancy without coordinate-decoupled perturbation, loss without wild-type rescue, or rescue without binding-defective and non-target controls is insufficient. The chapter therefore treats a translation-conditioned half-life contrast as a preliminary finite association unless the RBP intervention panel is joined on the same organism, same transcript or CDS, and same local RNA window.

\paragraph{Reality contacts now present.}
The mTOR translation contact supplies a finite translation readout boundary with $6494$ rows. The available RBP-binding calibration surface is separate: the verified RBNS/eCLIP binding row \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} reports $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and a runtime of $223.648043$ seconds. That row is useful as evidence that RBP occupancy can be represented as a finite external contact, but it is not the same-window perturbation and rescue panel required here. Consequently, the translation table and the generic RBP-binding table cannot be multiplied into a mechanism. They remain distinct finite surfaces until a joined panel contains transcript identity, residual coordinate, translation readout, half-life, occupancy, target loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue on the same rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
This chapter makes no claim about protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law. The $6494$-row translation contact can help define a same-row translation condition, and an RNA half-life table could test a bounded stability association once joined. Neither contact supplies folded structure, function, physical feasibility, or organism-level mechanism. A separate layer-specific measurement would be required for each of those claims. In particular, codon-window geometry, synonymous residual coordinates, mTOR translation response, transcript abundance controls, and RNA half-life estimates do not select an RBP mechanism unless the prespecified decay-factor RBP is directly measured and experimentally perturbed in the joined finite panel.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite evidence is strong enough to state a ready, bounded BioReality target: test whether a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate has a translation-conditioned RNA half-life association and require a prespecified RBP occupancy/loss/rescue pattern before calling it an RBP bridge. The current verified scalar data support the translation-readout side and document that RBP-binding contacts exist as separate finite measurements. They do not yet instantiate the full positive bridge. The scientifically correct reading is therefore: ready as a constrained finite-row test specification, with the positive mechanistic conclusion withheld until the missing same-window RBP perturbation and rescue rows are supplied.
