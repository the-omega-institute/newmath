The record considered here is the cross-layer claim

The concrete translation contact is the Thoreen mTOR translation readout surface, verified under run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}. These numbers fix only the finite translation panel used as the same-row conditioning surface.  They do not, by themselves, provide RNA half-life rows, local decay-factor RBP occupancy rows, perturbation rows, or rescue rows.

\paragraph{Coordinate statement.}
For a coding transcript row $r$, let $Q_6(r)$ denote the synonymous residual coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The admissible comparison is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels inside matched transcript or CDS rows.  A row may enter the proposed bridge test only when it also carries a same-row translation readout $T(r)$, an RNA half-life readout $H(r)$, matched transcript-abundance and transcription controls, local sequence-composition controls, local RNA-structure controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy measurement $O(r)$ at the editable RNA window.  The Thoreen panel supplies the finite translation side of this row type; it does not supply the remaining RBP-decay coordinates.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
The intended finite statistic is not the marginal association between $Q_6$ and RNA half-life.  It is a translation-conditioned RNA-stability test with a mechanistic veto.  A positive bridge requires that the half-life residual follow experimentally separated local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, that the effect collapse under target-RBP loss, that wild-type target-RBP rescue restore the effect, and that binding-defective or non-target rescue fail to restore it.  In compact notation, the claim would need a joined finite table supporting all of the following checks:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&Q_6(r) \;\text{contrasted after amino-acid quotient},\\
&H(r) \;\text{tested after conditioning on}\; T(r) \;\text{and transcript controls},\\
&O(r) \;\text{decoupled experimentally from coordinate-only explanations},\\
&\Delta H_{Q_6} \;\text{lost under target-RBP depletion},\\
&\Delta H_{Q_6} \;\text{returned by wild-type rescue},\\
&\Delta H_{Q_6} \;\text{not returned by binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same-row translation readout is therefore a gate on interpretation, not evidence of RNA decay mechanism by itself.

\paragraph{Controls and null.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after the declared controls are attached.  The control set includes amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, transcript abundance, splice or isoform state, batch, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, and the mandatory same-row translation readout.  Under that null, a measured half-life difference remains only an RNA-stability association unless the RBP occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and negative rescue pattern is present.  Conversely, an RBP occupancy difference remains only an occupancy association unless it is tied to the same finite rows and passes the perturbation and rescue checks.

\paragraph{Current verdict.}
The present record is ready as a bounded negative-claim chapter: it fixes the finite translation panel and the exact conditions under which a future RNA half-life RBP bridge would be allowed.  It is not a positive RBP-mediated RNA half-life result.  The current data do not contain same-organism same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type target-RBP rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue for the $6494$ translation rows.  Therefore the coordinate may be discussed only as a descriptor joined to a translation readout boundary, or at most as a candidate RNA-stability association if half-life rows are later joined.  No translation realization, protein output, folding statement, physical admissibility statement, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causal mechanism, or universal sequence law follows from the present finite rows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant measurement and statistic.
