\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the decay-factor RBP test}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA panel whose rows carry a coding-transcript identifier, an amino-acid-controlled $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched control covariates. The intended contrast is not a free comparison of codons or transcripts. It is the bounded contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after quotienting by amino-acid sequence and after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation measurement.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The translation-side contact currently attached to this record is the Thoreen mTOR translation table, with $6494$ finite rows and repository source \texttt{https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE36nnn/GSE36892/suppl/GSE36892\_RAW.tar}. The recorded run identifier is \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, and the computation recorded a runtime of $9.469$ seconds. This is a useful translation readout surface, but it is not by itself an RNA half-life, RBP occupancy, perturbation, or rescue surface. The RBP-contact comparison available elsewhere in the finite ledger has $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run \texttt{b225890251c2}, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds. That contact establishes that RBP measurements can be represented as finite rows, but it does not supply the same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss, or rescue rows required for this specific RNA half-life bridge. A separate polyadenylation-tail contact records $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance marked false and runtime $31.5212$ seconds; this reinforces the local nature of RNA-layer readouts and prevents importing a general RNA-processing law into the present claim.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive result would require a finite statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}\mid T,RBP}
  &= \widehat{\mathrm{HL}}(Q6_{\mathrm{high}} \mid T,Z,RBP_{\mathrm{local}})
   - \widehat{\mathrm{HL}}(Q6_{\mathrm{low}} \mid T,Z,RBP_{\mathrm{local}}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\mathrm{HL}$ is RNA half-life, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, and $Z$ contains the declared transcriptional, sequence-composition, structure, isoform, abundance, and batch controls. The sign or magnitude of this statistic is not enough. The local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must change in the editable RNA window independently of the coordinate label, the half-life effect must collapse under target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target controls must fail to restore it. Without those rows, the statistic remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Null exchangeability.}
The matched null is that, after the declared controls and the mandatory translation readout are attached, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q6_{\mathrm{high}} \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathrm{HL}
  \;\big|\; Q6_{\mathrm{low}},T,Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
A departure from this null can be reported only at the RNA-stability level unless the same finite panel also contains the RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue pattern. A half-life difference with unchanged local occupancy, no collapse after target-RBP loss, no wild-type rescue return, or restoration by a binding-defective or non-target construct is not evidence for the claimed decay-factor RBP route.

\paragraph{Readback and refusal boundary.}
The present finite rows therefore license a bounded research target rather than a completed biological mechanism. The mTOR translation table supplies $6494$ translation readout rows, and the separate RBP-binding ledger shows finite RBP-row handling, but the joined same-organism, same-window RNA half-life, local decay-factor occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and defective-control matrix is absent for this subject. Consequently no translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone; no RNA decay mechanism follows from the presence of a translation readout; and no protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry. Each such assertion would require its own layer-matched measurement surface and its own declared finite statistic.

\paragraph{Name certificate.}
This section certifies the name \texttt{q6.translation-conditioned.rna-half-life-rbp-bridge.cross-layer} as a finite-row, translation-conditioned RNA half-life target with a stringent decay-factor RBP gate. Its current status is not a positive RBP bridge. It is a precisely bounded conjectural site: the translation readout is represented by the $6494$-row Thoreen table, related finite RBP-row machinery is represented by the $900$-peak RBP contact, and the decisive same-window occupancy/loss/rescue matrix remains missing. The proper readback is therefore conditional: if the full joined panel is later attached and satisfies the occupancy, loss, rescue, and defective-control requirements under the stated controls, the record may be promoted to a bounded RBP-mediated RNA half-life relation; otherwise it remains a finite RNA-stability descriptor conditioned on translation.
