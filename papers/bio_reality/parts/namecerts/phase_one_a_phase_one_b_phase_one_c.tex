\subsection{Phase-one codon geometry and the translation-mediated protein-abundance boundary}\label{subsec:phase-one-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This subsection records a bounded BioReality claim about the finite surface on which the phase-one codon-geometry result may be compared with translation and protein-abundance measurements.  The carrier is the finite collection of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ in which $q_i$ ranges over the nine computed $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, $t_j$ is a translation readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout.  The nine residual coordinates used in the downstream tests are
$$\begin{aligned}
&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress.
\end{aligned}$$
The phase-one external check was run against the NCBI genetic-code table readback with target claims for the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, leave-one-code-out family behavior, and leave-one-codon-out module classification.  The locked confirmatory record contains $40$ successes in $40$ independent-replication trials and $40$ successes in $40$ boundary-probe trials; both one-sided binomial upper-tail probabilities are $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$.  The negative-control arm contains $20$ rows with $0$ unexpected successes, and the critical-ablation arm likewise contains $20$ rows with $0$ unexpected successes.  This establishes a finite genetic-code-table contact for the phase-one codon geometry only; it does not by itself measure translation, protein output, molecular function, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Translation readout surface.}
A separate modeled-translation contact computes tAI weights from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The modeled tAI surface was evaluated for $3$ organisms: \textit{Escherichia coli} K12, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Each organism supplied all $20$ standard amino-acid families; the matched tRNA FASTA header counts were $87$ of $89$ for \textit{E. coli} K12, $432$ of $432$ for human, and $275$ of $275$ for yeast.  The modeled readout covers $61$ sense codons in each organism, with zero raw-weight fallback count $0$ in all three cases.  The bounded coupling audit reports $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute coupling
$$\begin{aligned}
\max |S_{ij}|=3801961.031607896.
\end{aligned}$$
This is not a powered measured-ribosome result: the same record states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that at least $40$ matched species would be required for the powered survival route.  Therefore the tAI contact is a bounded translation-layer proxy surface, not an observed ribosome-occupancy or proteomics validation.

MC2 155, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  In every organism the control matrix has $24$ columns, the residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$, the translation block has rank $1$, and the combined $(Q,T)$ block has rank $10$ after control residualization.  Thus the finite matrix has enough rank for the declared joint nine-coordinate decomposition in these rows.

\paragraph{Control structure and yeast decomposition.}
The control set used in the yeast structure-mediated audit consists of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an M-density baseline.  The five-way yeast join contains $4759$ rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and overall join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  The yeast decomposition reports
$$\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}&=0.2715520808117803,\\
R^2_{QP\mid S}&=0.2706601505000995,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M}&=0.13773967696311287,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}&=0.13759011208318542.
\end{aligned}$$
The structure-only fraction is $0.003284564452662019$, the structure-incremental fraction is $0.0005507778820193332$, and $R^2(S\sim Q)=0.003774248455422586$.  The permutation right-tail probability for $S\sim Q$ is $0.027972027972027972$, whereas the structure-incremental right-tail probability is $0.1258741258741259$.  Thus the finite yeast rows show that structure-related covariates can be audited as controls, but they do not convert the translation-mediated abundance surface into a folded-structure claim.

\paragraph{Axis identity and perturbation contact.}
The structural-order axis audit identifies $f3\_stress$ as the only coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate.  Its weighted held-out increment is $0.0031744502294756994$, its unit loading is $0.5109586606724208$, and its sign-flip probability is $0.03125$.  The other eight axes do not pass that gate: for example, $Arg\_AGR$ has sign-flip probability $0.142578125$, $Ile\_AUA$ has $0.0693359375$, and $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$ has $0.529296875$.  A separate synonymous-perturbation protein-output audit in the Nieuwkoop \textit{E. coli} mRFP dataset contains $1459$ variants and reports $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta DL=19.089327473282296$ bits, matched and within-null probabilities both equal to $0.004975124378109453$, with real-CAI robustness true and real-CAI absorption false.  This perturbation result is useful as a layer-specific protein-output contact, but it remains dataset-specific and does not promote the cross-sectional mediation matrix to a global law.

\paragraph{Negative and bounded rows.}
Several linked audits keep the boundary sharp.  The evolutionary-selection-imprint audit has $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; it reports $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$, null $95\%$ maximum $0.05427157066522958$, and a negative positive-selection-imprint gate.  The mass-balance audit on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals reports $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628201518571$, $R^2_{L1}=0.996410628851051$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443377743347$, $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$, $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, and $\Delta DL=-668.8046070381928$ bits.  The turnover-residual audit on $3290$ genes reports $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta DL=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  These rows prevent over-reading: the finite evidence supports a controlled translation-mediated protein-abundance matrix, while selection imprint, dynamic mass balance, and turnover prediction remain bounded or negative under their registered tests.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite rows support the following local statement.  Phase-one codon geometry has an external genetic-code-table contact with exact finite success counts; the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be placed on a translation-readout surface; and, in the twelve-organism protein-abundance audit, the controlled matrix $Q\to T\to P$ has nonzero mediated fractions after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched abundance controls are included.  The admissible readback is only
$$\begin{aligned}
q_i\longrightarrow t_j\longrightarrow p_k,
\end{aligned}$$
with $t_j$ restricted to the declared translation readout and $p_k$ restricted to matched protein abundance.  A direct $q_i\to p_k$ association is not treated as protein realization unless the $q_i\to t_j$ step is present in the same controlled row system.  No translation mechanism, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter unless another layer-matched contact supplies that measurement and the corresponding controlled statistic.
