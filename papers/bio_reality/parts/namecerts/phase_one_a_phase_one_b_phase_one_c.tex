\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a finite BioReality surface for the claim id \texttt{phase.one.a+phase.one.b+phase.one.c} and for its attached $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation-mediated protein-abundance question.  The row carrier is the finite family of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ in which $q_i$ is a synonymous residual coordinate, $t_j$ is a translation readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout.  The intended readback is not a direct residual-to-protein assertion.  A protein-abundance row is admissible only after the same residual coordinate first has an attached translation-readout row and after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, the $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance have remained controls rather than promoted coordinates.

The residual coordinate list used by the translation and protein-abundance surfaces has nine coordinates:

That number is a bounded modeled-readout contact, not an observed ribosome-occupancy measurement.

\paragraph{Modeled translation surface.}
The modeled translation readout is deliberately weaker than a ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.  Three organisms were present in the bounded modeled-tAI computation: \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Each organism supplied all $20$ standard amino-acid tRNA families.  The tRNA FASTA header counts were $89$ for \textit{E. coli} K-12, $432$ for human, and $275$ for yeast; matched header counts were $87$, $432$, and $275$, respectively.  The modeled tAI readout had zero raw-weight fallback count $0$ in each of those three organisms.

The matrix therefore has finite contact with organism-scoped codon-adaptation weights, but it remains underpowered for a strong survival claim because only $3$ organisms are present and the registered powered route requires at least $40$ matched species.  In local notation, the modeled surface may be summarized as a bounded coupling table rather than as a measured survival table:

$$
\begin{aligned}
 |Q| &= 9,\\
 |C_{\mathrm{sense}}| &= 61,\\
 n_{\mathrm{organism}} &= 3,\\
 \#\{(i,j): |S_{ij}|>0.01\} &= 81,\\
 \max_{i,j}|S_{ij}| &= 3801961.031607896.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is enough to justify keeping the translation-readout row open and finite.  It is not enough to say that ribosomes executed the residual coordinate, that the coordinate controlled protein abundance, or that the residual has a mechanism-level interpretation.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite record is ready as a chapter-level BioReality entry because it contains a completed phase-one code-table contact, an explicitly bounded modeled translation matrix, and a matched protein-abundance mediation matrix with concrete organism-specific row counts and scalar decompositions.  The scientific statement is therefore narrow: $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be carried into a controlled finite matrix where modeled translation readouts reduce portions of residual-to-protein abundance association.  The strongest protein-abundance row is yeast, where the modeled mediation fraction is $0.9491551041216973$ on $5815$ joined proteins.  The broadest row set is human and mouse, with $19053$ and $19395$ joined proteins, respectively.  The phase-one genetic-code contact has two $40/40$ exact-binomial panels with $p=9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$ and two $20$-row zero-unexpected control panels.

The local boundary is part of the result.  A direct $q_i\to p_k$ association is not admissible as protein realization unless the $q_i\to t_j$ row is attached and survives the stated controls.  A modeled tAI or stAI contact is not a ribosome-footprint observation.  PAXdb abundance is not folding, localization, turnover, function, physical admissibility, phenotype, or mechanism.  The chapter therefore supports a finite controlled mediation readback and refuses promotion beyond that layer unless a separate matched contact supplies the missing measurement.
