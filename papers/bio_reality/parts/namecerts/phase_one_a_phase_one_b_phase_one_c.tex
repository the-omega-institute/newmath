\paragraph{Finite surface.}

Against the NCBI genetic-code table readback, the confirmatory panel reports $40$ successes in $40$ boundary trials and $40$ successes in $40$ independent replication trials.  In both cases the exact one-sided binomial upper-tail probability is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  p_{\mathrm{binom}} &= 9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13},\\
  n &= 40,\\
  \widehat r &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The negative-control panel has $20$ trials and $0$ unexpected successes, and the critical-ablation panel also has $20$ trials and $0$ unexpected successes.  Thus the phase-one object is not a floating narrative claim: it is a finite pass--fail surface whose positive rows, null rows, and ablation rows are all counted.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter therefore has a two-level verdict.  First, the phase-one genetic-code table contact is strongly positive on its own locked finite surface: $40/40$ boundary successes, $40/40$ independent successes, and no unexpected negative-control or ablation successes in $20+20$ null trials.  Second, the downstream $B^*_{Q6}$ readback is allowed only where an attached finite contact supplies the relevant layer: modeled tAI supports a bounded adaptation-weight calculation; joined protein-abundance rows support a statistical $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ decomposition; separate perturbation rows may support a specific protein-output result.  No translation realization, folding statement, physical admissibility statement, molecular function, causal mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from the phase-one table, from codon-window geometry, from modeled tAI, or from a direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies that claim and the declared controls survive.
