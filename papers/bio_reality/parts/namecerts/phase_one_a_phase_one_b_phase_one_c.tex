\paragraph{Finite record.}

Its represented targets are \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving}, \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}, and \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_codon\_out.module\_classification}. The observed table-level result is exact rather than asymptotic: the independent replication statistic has $40$ successes in $40$ trials, rate $1.0$, and one-sided binomial upper-tail probability $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$; the boundary probe has the same $40/40$ success count, the same rate $1.0$, and the same probability. The negative controls report $0$ unexpected successes across $20$ trials, and the critical ablations report $0$ unexpected successes across $20$ trials. The finite source is the NCBI genetic-code table \texttt{gc.prt} read against the locked codon-geometry claims, with preregistration time \texttt{2026-05-29T20:11:31+00:00} and snapshot time \texttt{2026-06-16T18:30:05+00:00}. Thus the phase-one contact certifies a sharply bounded codon-table geometry fact, not a biological realization above the genetic-code table.

\paragraph{Coordinate surface.}
For the translation-gated protein-abundance question, the carrier is the finite surface of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$. The $q_i$ are the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}

$$
The $t_j$ coordinate is a bounded translation readout, here including modeled tAI surfaces, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency when such rows are actually attached. The $p_k$ coordinate is a matched protein-abundance readout. The admissible readback is the mediated matrix $q_i\to t_j\to p_k$ after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, the $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance have been held in the control set. A direct $q_i\to p_k$ association is therefore not promoted to protein realization unless the corresponding $q_i\to t_j$ step survives as a translation readout.

\paragraph{Modeled translation-contact evidence.}
The modeled tAI survival calculation used $61$ sense codons and three organisms: \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. The local tRNA payload covered all $20$ standard amino-acid families in each of the three organisms; the FASTA header counts were $89$ for \textit{E. coli} K-12, $432$ for human, and $275$ for yeast, with matched header counts $87$, $432$, and $275$, respectively. The bounded-coupling scan reported $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}$ equal to $3801961.031607896$ and the same value on the largest diagonal entry. This is useful as a finite modeled-contact surface, but the organism count is only $3$, so the record itself states that powered survival is not available without at least $40$ matched species. The correct local interpretation is bounded modeled coupling, not measured ribosome occupancy, not protein output, and not a universal translation law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter therefore records three separate facts. First, the phase-one codon geometry contact is strongly supported at the genetic-code-table layer, with two independent $40/40$ binomial contacts and zero unexpected successes in both $20$-row control panels. Second, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ translation-mediated protein-abundance matrix is numerically populated and rank-admissible across $12$ organism-level panels, with explicit $M^{QTP}$ fractions and controlled direct remainders. Third, the interpretation remains bounded: modeled tAI is not observed ribosome dynamics, protein abundance is not folding or molecular function, and a residual-to-protein association is not accepted unless the residual first passes through the translation-readout coordinate. No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, adaptation law, or universality follows from this chapter unless a distinct layer-matched contact supplies the relevant rows and the corresponding controlled statistic is computed on the same finite surface.
