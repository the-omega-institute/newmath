\subsection{Phase-one codon geometry and the translation-mediated protein-abundance boundary}
\label{sec:phase-one-q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record treats the phase-one codon-geometry result as a finite empirical interface, not as a license to promote codon geometry into all biological layers.  The named claim is \texttt{phase.one.a+phase.one.b+phase.one.c}.  Its finite support consists of the three represented targets \texttt{h0.null.model.aa\_class\_preserving}, \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_code\_out.by\_family}, and \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_codon\_out.module\_classification}, read against the NCBI genetic-code table \texttt{gc.prt} under a locked confirmatory falsification scope.  The positive phase-one contact is sharp: both the boundary probe and the independent replication have $40$ successes in $40$ trials, with exact one-sided binomial upper-tail probability $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$ in each case.  The two null-side guards are equally finite: the negative-control panel has $20$ trials with $0$ unexpected successes, and the critical-ablation panel also has $20$ trials with $0$ unexpected successes.  Thus the local phase-one statement is a codon-table readback with doubled minimum independent trial counts and zero-tolerance controls, not a claim about translation, protein structure, function, or a biological law.

\paragraph{Residual coordinates and admissible readouts.}
The cross-layer object considered here is the controlled surface of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$.  The $q_i$ coordinates are the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i\in\{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.

The modeled translation contact computed a $61$-codon sense space for three organisms using GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  It found $81$ bounded-coupling entries above threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  This is useful as a modeled codon-adaptation readout, but it is explicitly underpowered for a broad survival claim: the organism count is $3$, while the recorded powered-survival condition asks for at least $40$ matched species.  The local rule is therefore strict.  A residual-to-protein association is admissible only after the same residual coordinate has first passed through a translation readout such as modeled tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency; direct $q_i\to p_k$ evidence is not enough.

\paragraph{Readback theorem for this chapter.}
The chapter-level assertion is therefore the following finite statement.  Phase-one codon geometry is confirmed at the genetic-code-table layer by two $40/40$ success panels with exact probability $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$ and by two $20$-row zero-failure controls.  The same paper may then examine the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates against translation and protein-abundance contacts, but only through the controlled matrix $q_i\to t_j\to p_k$.  In the supplied protein-abundance rows, modeled tAI supplies a bounded translation readout and the mediation fraction is positive in all $12$ organisms, with the strongest yeast value $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  This licenses a statistical cross-layer relation under the stated controls.  It does not license translation realization from the phase-one table alone, folded structure from protein abundance, physical admissibility from codon coordinates, biological function from residual survival, a molecular mechanism from mediation coefficients, or a universal law from any single organism or sign.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
