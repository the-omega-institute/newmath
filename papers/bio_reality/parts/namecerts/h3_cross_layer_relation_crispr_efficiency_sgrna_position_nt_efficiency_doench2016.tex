\subsection{Position-resolved sgRNA nucleotide efficiency in the Doench panel}
\label{subsec:bioreality-crispr-efficiency-sgrna-position-nt-efficiency-doench2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite table.}
The Doench sgRNA-efficiency record is a bounded finite-row contact between guide-sequence coordinates and a measured CRISPR efficiency readout.  The verified run for this contact used $n=5310$ guide rows and completed in $24.6506$ seconds.  Its reported marginal GC association was small and negative, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_{\mathrm{GC},\mathrm{main}} &= -0.0582917,\\
  n_{\mathrm{Doench}} &= 5310.
\end{aligned}
$$
This row count and scalar readout are enough to treat the contact as a real finite measurement surface for position-resolved guide features, but not enough to enlarge it into a general sequence law.  The biological object in this chapter is therefore the guide-position nucleotide table itself: a finite panel in which position-indexed nucleotide indicators, local sequence summaries, and the measured efficiency response are co-present in the same row system.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset anchor.}
A separate CRISPR-efficiency verification route records a Luo cross-protocol panel with $n_{\mathrm{Luo}}=10543$, cross-protocol status equal to true, and a source marked as the Doench2016 repository.  That run completed in $33.705459$ seconds.  These numbers are not pooled with the Doench statistic in this chapter.  They serve a narrower role: they show that the same class of position-resolved sgRNA nucleotide features has a second finite-row route available for cross-protocol checking.  The two finite contacts therefore occupy different audit positions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{Doench}} &= 5310,\\
  n_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= 10543,\\
  \mathrm{crossProtocol}_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The Doench contact is the primary row system for this chapter, while the Luo route is evidence that the coordinate family is not merely a single-file naming artifact.

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The admissible claim is only that a finite CRISPR guide table can test whether position-resolved nucleotide features carry efficiency information after the chosen controls and baselines are applied.  The verified scalar $r_{\mathrm{GC},\mathrm{main}}=-0.0582917$ is especially useful as a guardrail: GC content is present, but its marginal value is weak, so the chapter should not present GC composition alone as the explanatory object.  The operative surface is the finite guide-position design matrix together with the measured efficiency readout.  Any stronger statement would need the actual fitted position terms, held-out improvement, permutation or resampling evidence, and baseline-ablation outputs for the same rows.

\paragraph{Relation to the f\_3-stress translation packet.}
The input conjecture also names an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate and a desired same-window translation or ribosome-occupancy survival statistic.  The CRISPR Doench table does not supply that translation layer.  It has guide efficiency rows, not same-CDS codon-window rows with wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and codon-indexed ribosome-footprint occupancy.  Thus the CRISPR contact can be used as a separate finite example of a position-resolved biological readout, but it cannot certify $S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$ and cannot promote an $f_3$ residual coordinate into a translation-realization claim.  The required translation statistic would need a joined table containing the residual coordinate, codon counts, wobble-weight context, mRNA control, position or amino-acid-bin controls, and the matched ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout in the same finite row system.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
No conclusion about translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from the Doench sgRNA-efficiency rows.  The measured object is CRISPR guide efficiency under a position-indexed nucleotide representation.  Even inside the CRISPR layer, the available scalar summary does not by itself establish a complete mechanistic model of Cas9 cutting, DNA repair, chromatin accessibility, guide loading, or cell-state dependence.  Those would require separate contacts at their own measurement layers.  In the present chapter, the finite result is deliberately smaller: a real $5310$-row Doench surface with a weak marginal GC association, accompanied by a $10543$-row cross-protocol route, sufficient for a bounded position-resolved sgRNA-efficiency record and insufficient for any downstream biological promotion.
