\subsection{CRISPR Position-Efficiency Rows as a Boundary for the $f_3$-Stress Translation Question}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-position-efficiency-f3-stress-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The finite table attached to this record is a CRISPR guide-efficiency surface, not a ribosome-footprint surface.  Its primary checked row count is $n=5310$ for the Doench-style position-nucleotide efficiency analysis, with run id $\mathrm{b6c6363256e0}$, runtime $24.6506$ seconds, and reported GC main correlation
$\mathrm{gc\_main\_r}=-0.0582917$.  A second CRISPR contact in the same claim family gives a cross-protocol Luo panel with $n=10543$, run id $\mathrm{2e9cce255c01}$, runtime $33.705459$ seconds, and $\mathrm{cross\_protocol}=\mathrm{true}$.  These two finite surfaces support a narrow statement: position-resolved nucleotide features can be joined to guide activity rows, and a small GC-axis association is recorded in the Doench surface.  They do not supply coding-sequence windows, synonymous codon residuals, wobble-weight rows, mRNA controls, or codon-indexed ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Coordinate requested by the conjecture.}
The proposed cross-layer coordinate is the $f_3$-stress residual after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  A row capable of testing it would have to contain an ordered coding-window key, local synonymous codon counts, a context-dependent wobble-weight surface with $w_c \ne w_c^{f_3}$, a matched translation readout $T_{\mathrm{position}}$, mRNA abundance for the same molecule and condition, and controls for amino-acid composition or position bins.  In such a table, the intended survival entry is a controlled finite statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) &> 0,\\
Z &= \{\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{position},\mathrm{GC3},\mathrm{length},M\text{-}\mathrm{density},\mathrm{mRNA},\mathrm{baseline}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The present CRISPR guide-efficiency rows do not instantiate that matrix.  They therefore cannot be promoted into evidence for $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$, even though they are useful finite biological rows for a different cross-layer question about guide sequence and editing efficiency.

\paragraph{What the verified CRISPR facts do show.}
The Doench surface records a finite guide panel of size $5310$ and a weak negative GC-main correlation.  The Luo cross-protocol surface records a larger guide panel of size $10543$ and confirms that the guide-efficiency family is not confined to one protocol snapshot.  Taken together, the rows justify keeping the claim as a finite biological contact for CRISPR guide efficiency, with position-nucleotide information and GC covariates.  They do not contain codon-window translation measurements.  The distinction matters because an sgRNA position effect is a nucleotide-design readout for a guide molecule, whereas $f_3$-stress survival asks whether synonymous coding-window residuals improve a translation-layer readout after a demanding control set.

\paragraph{Comparison with powered translation-adjacent rows.}
Other verified rows show what a stronger translation-adjacent claim looks like.  The structural-order boundary run $\mathrm{1b97387835f7}$ reports $5$ folds, joined structural-order counts $3412$ for $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12\ MG1655}$ and $5119$ for yeast, a positive weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$, and a sign-flip probability $p=0.0029296875$.  That record also reports two positive organism calls for the full-control statistic.  A separate stop-readthrough crossdataset row, run $\mathrm{7c85e126c888}$, has $n=55886$ and untreated $\rho=0.032718$.  These facts are cited only as calibration examples of finite-row survival or crossdataset readback: they do not transfer their positive statistics to the CRISPR guide-efficiency rows and do not fill the missing $f_3$ translation-window matrix.

\paragraph{Rank and ablation condition.}
A positive chapter-level claim would require held-out improvement over simpler alternatives.  In finite-row terms, the admissible test is not merely that the $f_3$ coordinate can be computed.  The required inequality is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}} &= \mathcal{L}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z,\mathrm{baseline})
 - \mathcal{L}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z,\mathrm{baseline},f_3),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}} &> 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with enough nondegenerate rank after amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The Doench row count $5310$ and Luo row count $10543$ are large enough to make the CRISPR guide-efficiency question empirically meaningful, but row count alone is not the same as the required coding-window rank.  The needed rows must be same-organism, same-CDS, and same-window rows; sgRNA activity rows do not automatically provide those keys.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter therefore records a bounded negative boundary for the requested $f_3$-stress translation interpretation.  The verified CRISPR facts may support guide-efficiency statements about position-nucleotide features and GC covariates.  They may not be used to infer translation realization, ribosome pausing, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such inference would require a separate layer-matched contact: codon-indexed ribosome footprints or translation-efficiency rows, matched mRNA controls, and the declared survival statistic computed on the same coding windows.  Until that table exists, the safe conclusion is that the CRISPR finite rows are real and useful, while the $f_3$-stress translation-survival claim remains uninstantiated for this subject.
