\subsection{Doench position-nucleotide efficiency as a finite CRISPR readout surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-doench-position-nucleotide-efficiency}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The claim indexed by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.crispr\_efficiency.sgrna\_position\_nt\_efficiency\_doench2016} is not a translation claim.  Its directly verified contact is a finite CRISPR guide-efficiency table whose rows encode position-resolved nucleotide features for guide RNAs and an experimentally reported efficiency response.  The recorded Doench-row run has $n = 5310$ usable guide rows, runtime $24.6506$ seconds, and a main GC association of $r = -0.0582917$.  In this chapter the row table is used only as a bounded biological readout for guide-efficiency variation, not as a coding-sequence translation measurement.

\paragraph{Local statistic.}
Let $G$ be the finite set of guide rows retained by the Doench run.  For each $g \in G$, let $Y_g$ denote the guide-efficiency readout, let $X_g$ denote the position-nucleotide feature vector, and let $C_g$ denote the GC summary coordinate recorded by the run.  The verified scalar contact supplies the following row-level boundary:
$$
\begin{aligned}
|G| &= 5310,\\
r_{GC,Y} &= -0.0582917,\\
t_{run} &= 24.6506\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign and magnitude of $r_{GC,Y}$ are interpreted narrowly: within the retained Doench table, the coarse GC coordinate has a small negative marginal association with the guide-efficiency readout.  This is a finite empirical coordinate on a CRISPR guide panel.  It does not identify a causal editing mechanism, does not isolate position-specific nucleotides by itself, and does not license any statement about translation, protein output, folding, physical feasibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset routing check.}
A second finite CRISPR contact records a cross-protocol Luo-route table with $n_{Luo} = 10543$, runtime $33.705459$ seconds, \texttt{cross\_protocol = true}, and \texttt{frozen\_source = Doench2016 repo}.  This companion row set matters only as a routing and transport check for guide-efficiency features across CRISPR assay surfaces.  It does not turn the Doench association into a general law, and it does not add a ribosome, mRNA, codon-usage, translation-efficiency, protein-abundance, localization, folding, or phenotype readout.  Its role is to show that the CRISPR guide-efficiency family has a second finite table against which feature transport can be asked, not that the present Doench scalar has already survived all such transport.

\paragraph{Relation to the proposed $f_3$-stress boundary.}
The broader conjectural packet mentions an $f_3$-stress synonymous residual coordinate, local synonymous codon counts, wobble-weight context, matched mRNA control, and position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency rows.  None of those translation-layer rows is supplied by the Doench CRISPR guide-efficiency run.  The Doench finite table therefore cannot be used as the same-CDS, same-window matrix required for a statistic of the form $S_{f3}(T_{position}\mid Z)$.  The admissible statement is only that a finite CRISPR efficiency surface exists and has the recorded row count and GC association.  A translation-survival statement would require a different joined panel whose rows contain coding-sequence windows, synonymous codon counts, wobble-weight values, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency observations under the same row keys.

\paragraph{Controlled claim.}
For the Doench surface, the local finite-row assertion is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{R}_{Doench}
&= \{(X_g, C_g, Y_g) : g \in G\},\\
|G| &= 5310,\\
\operatorname{corr}(C_g,Y_g) &= -0.0582917.
\end{aligned}
$$
This supports a bounded CRISPR guide-efficiency contact: position-nucleotide and GC-derived guide features can be compared with a measured guide-efficiency response on a finite panel.  It does not support a claim that codon geometry, $f_3$-stress, wobble asymmetry, or a modeled codon-adaptation weight has been read out by ribosomes.  It also does not support protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological rule.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.

\paragraph{Null boundary for promotion.}
The break condition for this chapter is any inference that treats the Doench CRISPR guide table as if it were a coding-sequence translation table.  The verified Doench scalar is compatible with a guide-efficiency analysis and with later cross-dataset CRISPR checks.  It is not compatible with promoting the record to translation realization or to downstream biological layers.  In particular, the presence of $n = 5310$ guide rows and $r_{GC,Y} = -0.0582917$ does not create the missing same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon, wobble-weight, matched mRNA, and ribosome-footprint matrix.  Until such a matrix is separately attached and evaluated, the $f_3$-stress translation-survival packet remains uninstantiated at the ribosome-readout layer, while the Doench record remains a finite CRISPR guide-efficiency readout.
