\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record concerns the finite CRISPR guide-efficiency surface indexed by the claim identifier \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.crispr\_efficiency.sgrna\_position\_nt\_efficiency\_doench2016}.  The observed table used for this contact has $n=5310$ guide rows in the Doench-2016 surface, with the recorded main GC association
$\mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{GC}}=-0.0582917$
and runtime $24.6506$ seconds for the finite audit run.  A separate cross-protocol route is recorded for the Luo-2020 comparison, with $n=10543$ rows, runtime $33.705459$ seconds, and \texttt{cross\_protocol=true} against the stated Doench-2016 source.  These two finite surfaces are useful as guide-efficiency contact checks, but they do not by themselves contain coding-window ribosome-footprint rows, matched mRNA controls, wobble-weight rows, or synonymous codon-window counts.

\paragraph{Local mathematical object.}
For this chapter, the CRISPR table is treated as a finite family
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{CRISPR}}=\{(g_i,y_i,x_i):1\leq i\leq 5310\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g_i$ is a guide identity, $y_i$ is the measured guide-efficiency response, and $x_i$ contains the guide-position and nucleotide features used by the Doench-2016 contact.  The stored scalar $\mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{GC}}=-0.0582917$ is only a weak finite association inside this CRISPR guide table.  It is not a translation readout, not a ribosome-density estimate, and not a synonymously controlled coding-window statistic.  The cross-protocol Luo route supplies an additional finite guide-efficiency comparison table, but it remains in the guide-design layer rather than the coding-window translation layer.

\paragraph{Requested f\_3-stress statistic.}
The proposed $f_3$-stress cross-layer statistic would require a different finite table.  Its row must include a coding-sequence window, an $f_3$-stress residual after amino-acid quotienting, local synonymous codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight context, a same-window ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, a matched mRNA control, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The desired survival entry has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)>0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes amino-acid composition or position bins, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density, codon-count controls, matched mRNA abundance, coverage and mappability limits, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The supplied CRISPR guide table does not instantiate this row type.  Therefore the Doench-2016 guide-efficiency result may be cited as a finite, verified contact in the CRISPR-efficiency family, but not as evidence that $f_3$-stress survives against position-resolved ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Comparison to a powered translation-adjacent result.}
A separate verified translation-conditioned structural-order audit records a passed status with $5$ folds, minimum joined row count $300$, joined structural-order rows $3412$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Its weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ is $0.004764742175882462$, with aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ ranging from $0.002688284523492457$ to $0.007880037229394898$, and sign-flip probability $p=0.0029296875$.  Those numbers show what a powered, joined, controlled translation-conditioned record looks like when it is actually present.  The CRISPR guide-position contact here lacks the analogous joined matrix for $f_3$-stress and position-resolved translation readout.  The distinction is material: finite verification in one assay layer does not license promotion into another assay layer without the corresponding rows and controls.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
This chapter records a negative promotion boundary.  The verified Doench-2016 and Luo-2020 guide-efficiency facts support only CRISPR guide-position and nucleotide-efficiency analysis.  They do not imply translation realization, ribosome pausing, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  A positive $f_3$-stress translation-survival claim would require a separate organism-matched and coding-window-matched contact table with codon counts, wobble weights, ribosome-footprint or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency rows, matched mRNA abundance, and the declared controlled statistic.  Until such a table is attached, the admissible conclusion is only that the CRISPR guide-efficiency contact is finite and verified, while the proposed $f_3$-stress position-resolved translation claim remains unpromoted.
