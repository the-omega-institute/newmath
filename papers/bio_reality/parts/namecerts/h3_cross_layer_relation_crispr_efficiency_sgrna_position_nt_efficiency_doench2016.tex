\subsection{Doench-style sgRNA position rows as a bounded efficiency contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-sgrna-position-nt-doench2016}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record treats the Doench-style CRISPR efficiency table as a finite row system rather than as a general genome-editing law.  The row carrier is a set of guide-level observations in which a single sgRNA sequence is attached to a measured activity readout and to sequence-position covariates.  In the verified run for the present contact, the finite table contains $5310$ rows.  The audit run identifier is $\mathrm{b6c6363256e0}$, and the recorded runtime is $24.6506$ seconds.  The scalar control check retained in the row summary is a main GC association of
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathrm{GC,main}} &= -0.0582917 .
\end{aligned}
$$
This number is small in magnitude and negative, so the chapter does not treat global GC content as a sufficient explanation of sgRNA efficiency.  It is used only as a finite-row diagnostic showing that the row system has a measured sequence-composition coordinate and that the activity endpoint is not being replaced by a purely symbolic guide descriptor.

\paragraph{Position-nucleotide boundary.}
The bounded object of interest is the relation between position-indexed guide nucleotides and measured CRISPR efficiency inside the same finite table.  A row may therefore carry entries of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
(g, y, x_1,\ldots,x_L, z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g$ denotes the guide identity, $y$ is the measured efficiency endpoint, $x_k$ records the nucleotide state at guide position $k$, and $z$ collects controls such as guide-level sequence composition and other design covariates available in the table.  The claim supported here is not that any one base at any one position is universally favorable.  The supported claim is narrower: a finite Doench-style activity matrix exists in which position-resolved nucleotide coordinates can be tested against sgRNA efficiency while ordinary composition controls remain visible in the same audit surface.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset orientation.}
A related CRISPR position-nucleotide contact is present for the Luo cross-dataset route.  Its verified finite table records $10543$ Luo rows, marks the route as cross-protocol, and names the Doench2016 repository as the frozen source used for comparison.  The Luo audit run identifier is $\mathrm{2e9cce255c01}$, with runtime $33.705459$ seconds.  This second table is not merged into the Doench row count in this chapter.  It is cited only to show that the same class of position-indexed sgRNA coordinates has a separately recorded cross-protocol route.  Thus the Doench table supplies the primary finite efficiency contact, while the Luo table supplies a distinct stress test for whether the coordinate vocabulary survives outside the primary protocol.

\paragraph{Ranked survival form.}
For this chapter, a position-nucleotide coordinate is admissible only when its contribution is read as a controlled finite-row survival term.  A schematic score has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{k,b}(Y\mid Z) &= \Delta \mathcal{Q}(Y; \mathbf{1}\{x_k=b\}\mid Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $k$ is a guide position, $b$ is a nucleotide, $Y$ is the measured efficiency endpoint, $Z$ is the selected control set, and $\Delta\mathcal{Q}$ denotes improvement over a simpler baseline on the same row panel.  The recorded Doench fact does not provide a full list of all $S_{k,b}$ entries, $p$-values, or held-out deltas.  Consequently, the chapter accepts the contact as a bounded finite-row efficiency surface, not as a completed catalogue of causal nucleotide effects.  The observed row count $n=5310$ is sufficient for a non-vacuous finite table, and the GC diagnostic $r_{\mathrm{GC,main}}=-0.0582917$ is sufficient to state that at least one composition coordinate was audited, but it is not sufficient to promote any individual position-nucleotide coefficient to a universal design rule.

\paragraph{Relation to the f\_3-stress translation packet.}
The supplied conjectural envelope mentions an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate and asks whether a bounded cross-layer translation-readout statistic can survive after amino-acid, position-bin, GC3, length, M-density, mRNA, and simpler-baseline controls.  The Doench sgRNA efficiency table is not that translation-readout panel.  It has guide efficiency rows, not same-CDS codon-window rows with matched ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.  Therefore the CRISPR contact can support a finite guide-design chapter, but it cannot discharge the missing translation panel required for an $f_3$-stress ribosome-occupancy statement.  In particular, the Doench row count and GC association do not supply wobble-weight asymmetry, synonymous codon counts, matched mRNA abundance, amino-acid quotient controls, or position-resolved ribosome-footprint rows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The only local biological readout licensed here is measured sgRNA efficiency within the audited CRISPR design table.  No translation claim follows from it.  No protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from the Doench row carrier or from the small GC association.  Those conclusions would require separate contacts at the appropriate biological layer and their own row-level statistics.  The chapter therefore records a real finite efficiency surface for CRISPR guide design, while leaving all translation, structural, mechanistic, and global-law interpretations outside the supported scope.
