\subsection{Position-Resolved CRISPR Efficiency as a Boundary Check for Translation-Layer Promotion}\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-efficiency-sgrna-position-nt-efficiency-doench2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The finite surface considered here is the Doench-style sgRNA efficiency table identified by the claim id \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.crispr\_efficiency.sgrna\_position\_nt\_efficiency\_doench2016}.  Its verified row count is $n=5310$, with recorded run id \texttt{b6c6363256e0} and runtime $24.6506$ seconds.  The only scalar association explicitly available for this surface is the GC main correlation $r=-0.0582917$.  Thus the row table is suitable for a bounded position/nucleotide-efficiency check, but it is not by itself a translation-readout table.  A related cross-protocol CRISPR surface, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.crispr\_efficiency.sgrna\_position\_nt\_crossdataset\_luo2020}, records $n=10543$ Luo rows, run id \texttt{2e9cce255c01}, runtime $33.705459$ seconds, and \texttt{cross\_protocol=true} against a frozen Doench2016 source.  These two finite facts jointly certify that the CRISPR part of the record has real row mass and an external cross-dataset route, but they do not certify any ribosome-footprint, translation-efficiency, protein-output, or codon-window survival statistic.

\paragraph{Claim-local coordinate.}
The proposed biological coordinate is an $f_3$-stress synonymous residual coordinate on finite coding-sequence windows.  In the intended test, every row would have to carry the residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting, local synonymous codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight context, a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, an mRNA control, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The row-level statistic is therefore not a generic CRISPR score.  It has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition or position-bin terms, GC3, coding-sequence length, an $M$-density baseline, matched mRNA abundance, codon-count controls, coverage and mappability limits, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The Doench finite row fact gives an efficiency table and a weak GC association, but it does not instantiate $T_{\mathrm{position}}$ and does not attach the same-window translation readback required by this formula.

\paragraph{What the CRISPR rows can support.}
The Doench surface can be used as a finite example of a position-sensitive biological assay in which nucleotide context is measured against an efficiency endpoint.  The verified scalar $r=-0.0582917$ for the GC main term, over $5310$ rows, is evidence that the table has a computable covariate-to-endpoint relation rather than merely a named contact.  The Luo cross-protocol surface adds a second row-bearing check with $10543$ rows and a declared Doench2016 source, so the CRISPR efficiency route is not a single isolated identifier.  These facts support a local statement: position-indexed sequence features can be evaluated against finite experimental efficiency readouts under explicit row counts.  They do not support a statement that $f_3$-stress survives against ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Why this does not promote to translation.}
The intended $f_3$-stress packet requires a same-organism, same-CDS, same-window join between codon geometry and translation readout.  The available CRISPR facts instead concern sgRNA efficiency.  Even when an sgRNA table is position-resolved, it is not a codon-indexed ribosome-footprint matrix and it is not a matched translation-efficiency residual.  The boundary is numerical as well as semantic: the available values are $n=5310$, $r=-0.0582917$, and runtime $24.6506$ seconds for Doench, plus $n=10543$ and runtime $33.705459$ seconds for the Luo cross-protocol route.  None of these fields is a held-out improvement for $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$; none is a rank-powered survival entry after the required translation controls; and none records wobble-weight asymmetry as an observed row-level translation phenomenon.

\paragraph{Contrast with an actually translation-conditioned finite result.}
A separate verified translation-conditioned structural-order boundary, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.translation\_conditioned\_structural\_order\_boundary.b\_star\_q6\_powered}, records two joined organisms and a passed verdict for its own experiment.  Its structural-order joined counts are $3412$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Its aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ summary has $n=2$, minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, and two positive organism calls, with full-control sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.  Those numbers show what a row-joined, control-bearing cross-layer report looks like.  They cannot be transferred back to the Doench sgRNA table, but they give a local standard for the missing object: the CRISPR claim would need its own joined translation matrix and its own held-out, controlled positive statistic.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The proposed $f_3$-stress translation-survival claim breaks if the chapter treats review routing, a contact name, modeled tAI or stAI weights, sgRNA efficiency rows, GC association, wobble notation, or codon/window coordinates as though they were a measured same-window ribosome-occupancy statistic.  It also breaks if a positive CRISPR efficiency association is described as protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  The admissible statement is narrower: the finite CRISPR surfaces give row-counted position/nucleotide-efficiency assays, while the $f_3$ translation-survival packet remains pending until a same-CDS, same-window translation readout is joined and tested under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter therefore records a boundary-positive but promotion-negative result.  The CRISPR efficiency data are finite and concrete: $5310$ Doench rows with GC main $r=-0.0582917$, and $10543$ Luo rows on a cross-protocol route to the Doench2016 source.  These values are sufficient to write a non-generic BioReality entry about position-sensitive sequence assays and their limits.  They are not sufficient to claim translation realization for the $f_3$-stress coordinate.  Translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, and universal biological law remain outside the claim unless separate layer-matched contacts supply the corresponding finite rows and controlled statistics.
