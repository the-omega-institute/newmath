\subsection{Position-resolved CRISPR efficiency as a boundary test for an $f_3$-stress translation coordinate}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-efficiency-sgrna-position-nt-efficiency-doench2016}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record considered here is a finite-row CRISPR efficiency surface rather than a direct translation experiment.  Its primary row set is the Doench-style guide-efficiency table with $n=5310$ scored guide rows, experiment run identifier \texttt{b6c6363256e0}, and a reported scalar GC main correlation
$gc_{\mathrm{main}}=-0.0582917$ computed in $24.6506$ seconds.  A second finite surface is available as a cross-protocol check: the Luo-linked table has $n_{\mathrm{Luo}}=10543$ rows, \texttt{cross\_protocol}=\texttt{true}, source tag \texttt{Doench2016 repo}, and runtime $33.705459$ seconds.  These two finite surfaces support a guide-position and nucleotide-context analysis of CRISPR efficiency, but they do not by themselves instantiate a same-CDS ribosome-footprint matrix.

\paragraph{Coordinate being audited.}
The proposed coordinate is the $f_3$-stress synonymous residual direction after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The intended row carrier is not merely a guide-efficiency table.  It is a CDS-window table in which every row contains an $f_3$ residual coordinate, local synonymous codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight context, a matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, matched mRNA abundance, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The finite CRISPR tables can therefore be used only as nearby position-resolved evidence about guide sequence effects, not as the translation-layer readout required by the $f_3$ coordinate.

\paragraph{Observed finite facts.}
The Doench finite surface has enough rows to make guide-position nucleotide effects estimable within its own assay.  The weak negative GC main correlation is summarized by
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{Doench}} &= 5310,\\
 gc_{\mathrm{main}} &= -0.0582917,\\
 t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 24.6506\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cross-protocol Luo-linked surface records
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= 10543,\\
 \mathrm{cross\_protocol} &= \mathrm{true},\\
 t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 33.705459\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values are real finite-row anchors for CRISPR guide-efficiency modeling.  They justify treating position-resolved guide sequence effects as a legitimate finite assay surface.  They do not supply codon-indexed ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, wobble-weight asymmetry measured in the same CDS windows, or the matched mRNA denominator needed for a translation survival statistic.

\paragraph{Required survival statistic.}
The admissible translation claim would require a controlled held-out improvement for the same CDS windows.  In local notation, the statistic has the form $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$, where $T_{\mathrm{position}}$ is a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency readout, and where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, position-bin controls, $GC3$, CDS length, methionine-density baseline, local codon-count controls, matched mRNA abundance, coverage and mappability filters, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The positive case is not the existence of a CRISPR guide model.  It is the inequality
$$
\begin{aligned}
 S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
after the declared controls and after held-out evaluation.  The present finite CRISPR facts do not compute this quantity.

\paragraph{Wobble asymmetry condition.}
The conjectural mechanism requires a context in which wobble weights break $f_3$ symmetry.  This is a row-level condition, not a naming convention.  For a codon context $c$, the necessary asymmetry can be written as $w_c\neq w_c^{f_3}$, but this inequality is admissible only when the relevant wobble-weight surface is attached to the same organism, the same CDS or transcript identifiers, and the same local windows used for the translation readout.  A modeled tAI or stAI table can serve as a bounded codon-adaptation contact, but it is not an observed ribosome-footprint measurement and cannot replace the missing position-resolved readout.

\paragraph{Relation to the CRISPR assay.}
The Doench and Luo finite surfaces are useful because they show that position-resolved nucleotide information can carry predictive content in a controlled biological assay.  The target here, however, is stricter.  A guide-position nucleotide model is not a synonymous-codon translation model, and a CRISPR efficiency endpoint is not ribosome occupancy.  Thus the Doench value $gc_{\mathrm{main}}=-0.0582917$ is recorded only as a finite scalar from the guide-efficiency analysis.  It is not evidence for $f_3$-stress translation survival, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or any universal biological law.

\paragraph{Local null boundary.}
The present record is ready as a boundary certificate: it states exactly what the finite CRISPR tables can and cannot support.  They support a finite guide-efficiency surface with $5310$ Doench rows and a cross-protocol Luo-linked surface with $10543$ rows.  They do not contain the same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon counts, wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA controls, and codon-indexed ribosome-footprint rows needed to evaluate $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$.  Consequently, no translation realization follows here.  No claim about protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, structural order, or universality is licensed unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the controlled statistic is positive.

\paragraph{Admissible next finite table.}
The next table required for promotion is a joined CDS-window matrix.  Each row must be keyed by organism, transcript or CDS identifier, codon position or fixed local window, and experimental condition.  The row must include the $f_3$ residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting, synonymous codon counts, $GC3$, length and methionine-density covariates, wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, coverage and mappability fields, and a position-resolved ribosome-footprint or explicitly bounded translation-efficiency entry.  Only after this matrix exists can the rank-powered survival test distinguish a genuine cross-layer signal from guide-efficiency position effects, codon-composition baselines, transcript abundance, and rank saturation.
