\subsection{Position-resolved guide-sequence efficiency as a finite cross-layer contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-crispr-sgrna-position-nt-efficiency-doench2016}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The Doench guide-efficiency panel supplies a finite row surface for testing whether nucleotide identity at guide-relative positions carries predictive information for CRISPR activity after ordinary sequence controls are taken into account.  In the finite-row audit used here, the Doench contact contains $n=5310$ guide rows and records the main GC association as $r=-0.0582917$, with the verification run completed under experiment id \texttt{b6c6363256e0} in $24.6506$ seconds.  The numerical content is deliberately modest: it licenses only a bounded statement about a guide-position nucleotide surface and its association with measured guide efficiency in this dataset.  It does not, by itself, identify a translation coordinate, a ribosome-footprint statistic, a protein-output channel, or a mechanism of nuclease action.

\paragraph{Controlled coordinate represented by the claim.}
Let $G$ denote the finite table of guide rows.  Each row carries a guide identifier, a position-indexed nucleotide string, the measured efficiency endpoint, and the covariates used by the finite audit.  The local scientific claim is that the table supports a position-resolved sequence-efficiency contact, not that every sequence feature is mechanistically causal.  In schematic finite-row form, the contact is only the following bounded object:
$$
\begin{aligned}
G &= \{g_1,\ldots,g_{5310}\},\\
E(g_i) &= \text{measured guide-efficiency endpoint for row } g_i,\\
X_{p,b}(g_i) &= \mathbf{1}\{\text{base } b \text{ occurs at guide position } p \text{ in row } g_i\},\\
Z(g_i) &= \text{the fitted control context retained by the audit.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported scalar $r=-0.0582917$ is therefore read as a weak finite-sample GC-direction summary on this guide-efficiency surface.  Its sign and magnitude are not promoted to a universal rule.  They only say that the verified Doench panel contains a small negative main GC association under the recorded run conditions.

\paragraph{Relation to cross-dataset routing.}
A separate CRISPR contact records a Luo cross-dataset route with $n=10543$ rows, \texttt{cross\_protocol=true}, and \texttt{frozen\_source=Doench2016 repo}, verified under experiment id \texttt{2e9cce255c01} in $33.705459$ seconds.  That second finite surface is useful because it distinguishes a single-study efficiency association from a broader protocol-transfer question.  The Doench contact and the Luo contact are not merged into one empirical claim here.  Instead, the Doench surface is used as the primary finite guide-efficiency table, while the Luo route records that a protocol-separated comparison surface exists.  The two row counts, $5310$ and $10543$, should be kept distinct, since they answer different audit questions.

\paragraph{Why this is not a translation-readout claim.}
The claim identifier belongs to a CRISPR-efficiency surface, whereas the surrounding conjectural packet asks about an $f_3$-stress synonymous residual coordinate surviving against position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency readouts.  The Doench guide table does not contain same-CDS codon windows, synonymous codon counts, wobble-weight rows, matched mRNA abundance, or codon-indexed ribosome-footprint occupancy.  Consequently no statistic of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)
\end{aligned}
$$
is computed by the Doench guide-efficiency run.  The verified CRISPR rows can support a guide-position nucleotide-efficiency chapter, but they cannot be used as a substitute for the missing translation table.  In particular, a guide-position efficiency endpoint is not a ribosome-occupancy endpoint, and a GC association in guide design is not evidence that an $f_3$ synonymous residual has survived amino-acid, position-bin, GC3, length, methionine-density, mRNA, rank, and baseline-ablation controls.

\paragraph{Admissible statement.}
The admissible local statement is the following.  The Doench finite-row audit provides a bounded position-resolved CRISPR guide-efficiency contact with $5310$ rows and a recorded main GC association $r=-0.0582917$.  A related Luo cross-protocol surface exists with $10543$ rows and is explicitly marked as cross-protocol.  These two facts make the guide-efficiency claim suitable for a finite BioReality chapter about position-indexed guide sequence features.  They do not establish an $f_3$ translation-survival entry, nor do they attach any same-row ribosome, translation-efficiency, mRNA-control, protein-abundance, structural, physical, functional, phenotypic, mechanistic, or universal biological readout.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter.  Such claims would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own rows and its own controlled statistic.  For the $f_3$ translation-survival question, the required next table would have to join the same organism, the same CDS, the same local coding window, synonymous codon counts, bounded wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and position-resolved ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency readouts.  Until that joined table exists and shows positive held-out improvement after the declared controls, the CRISPR guide-efficiency surface remains a guide-design contact only.
