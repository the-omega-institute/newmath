\subsection{Position nucleotide efficiency as a finite CRISPR readout}\label{subsec:bioreality-crispr-sgrna-position-nt-efficiency-doench2016}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite sgRNA-efficiency table, not a direct translation-readout table.  The available finite contact is the Doench-style position-nucleotide CRISPR efficiency panel with $n=5310$ scored rows in the verified analysis run $\mathrm{b6c6363256e0}$.  Each row is interpreted as a guide-level observation whose measurable endpoint is guide efficiency under a position-resolved nucleotide design surface.  The reported scalar control readback for this panel is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{Doench}} &= 5310,\\
 r_{\mathrm{GC,main}} &= -0.0582917,\\
 t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 24.6506\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign and small magnitude of $r_{\mathrm{GC,main}}$ are used here only as a finite-row diagnostic for the guide-efficiency surface.  They are not promoted to a statement about codon translation, ribosome occupancy, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular mechanism, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Cross-protocol anchoring.}
A second finite contact is available through the Luo cross-dataset route, with run identifier $\mathrm{2e9cce255c01}$.  That route records a cross-protocol comparison against a Luo panel with
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= 10543,\\
 \mathrm{cross\_protocol} &= \mathrm{true},\\
 t_{\mathrm{Luo}} &= 33.705459\ \mathrm{s},\\
 \mathrm{source} &= \text{Doench2016 repository}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This second contact is useful because it shows that the guide-design readout is not being described solely from a single finite table.  The cross-protocol flag supplies a limited robustness check on sgRNA position-nucleotide behavior, while the source label keeps the comparison tied to the Doench repository lineage.  It still remains a CRISPR-efficiency readout.  It does not attach codon-indexed ribosome footprints, matched mRNA abundance, amino-acid-bin controls, wobble-weight rows, or a coding-window translation statistic.

\paragraph{Claim-local mathematical form.}
Let $G$ be the finite set of guide rows in the Doench panel and let $Y_g$ denote the measured guide-efficiency endpoint for row $g\in G$.  Let $X_g$ denote the position-resolved nucleotide design vector for that same row, and let $C_g$ collect available guide-level controls such as GC summaries and other design-surface covariates.  The finite readout actually supported by the cited row set has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 G &= \{g_1,\ldots,g_{5310}\},\\
 Y_g &= \mathrm{CRISPR\ guide\ efficiency}(g),\\
 X_g &= \mathrm{position\ nucleotide\ design\ features}(g),\\
 C_g &= \mathrm{guide\ level\ controls}(g).
\end{aligned}
$$
The row table can therefore support a bounded statement about whether a position-nucleotide design surface has finite predictive contact with CRISPR guide efficiency.  The scalar $r_{\mathrm{GC,main}}=-0.0582917$ is one observed component of that contact.  The Luo route supplies a second finite count, $n_{\mathrm{Luo}}=10543$, for a cross-protocol design comparison.  Neither table contains the tuple needed for an $f_3$-stress translation-survival statistic.

\paragraph{Why this is not yet an $f_3$ translation-survival result.}
The proposed $f_3$-stress survival statement would require a different finite carrier.  A row sufficient for that claim would have to join, on the same organism, coding sequence, and local coding window, an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting, synonymous codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight surface, matched mRNA abundance, and a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout.  It would also need controls for amino-acid composition or position bins, GC3, coding-sequence length, methionine-density baseline, mRNA, codon-count alternatives, coverage, mappability, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The Doench and Luo CRISPR panels do not supply those joined coding-window rows.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The finite evidence can be summarized as follows.  The Doench guide-efficiency contact contributes $5310$ measured sgRNA rows and a recorded main GC correlation of $-0.0582917$.  The Luo cross-dataset route contributes $10543$ additional guide rows under a cross-protocol comparison sourced from the Doench2016 repository.  These facts are enough to write a local BioReality chapter about a finite CRISPR sgRNA position-nucleotide efficiency readout.  They are not enough to claim that $f_3$-stress survives into translation, ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, or a general biological law.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a computed controlled statistic on the joined coding-window panel.
