\subsection{Position-resolved CRISPR rows as a boundary test for an unrelated translation-coordinate packet}
\label{subsec:biorreality-crispr-doench-position-f3-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite assay surface.}
The finite table attached to this record is a CRISPR guide-efficiency surface, not a ribosome-footprint surface.  The Doench guide-position analysis contributes $n=5310$ finite rows with a reported main GC association $r=-0.0582917$ and runtime $24.6506$ seconds under run id \texttt{b6c6363256e0}.  A linked cross-protocol check contributes a second CRISPR guide panel with $n=10543$ Luo rows, \texttt{cross\_protocol=true}, source tag \texttt{Doench2016 repo}, and runtime $33.705459$ seconds under run id \texttt{2e9cce255c01}.  These two facts certify that the chapter is grounded in finite assay rows and not in an uninstantiated biological narrative.  They also determine the scope of the present record: the measured layer is guide-sequence efficiency, with position-resolved nucleotide and GC features available for CRISPR activity, whereas the conjectural packet asks about an $f_3$-stress synonymous residual coordinate in coding-sequence windows and a same-window translation readout.

\paragraph{Coordinate requested by the packet.}
The proposed coordinate is a residual $f_3$ quantity after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  Its intended row has an ordered CDS window, local synonymous codon counts, a wobble-weight context with $w_c \neq w_c^{f_3}$, a matched mRNA control, and a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readback.  In the notation of this chapter, a valid row would have the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x &= (\mathrm{organism},\mathrm{CDS},\mathrm{window},q_{f_3},w, T_{\mathrm{pos}},m,Z),\\
  S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z) &> 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_{f_3}$ is the amino-acid-quotiented residual coordinate, $w$ is the bounded wobble-weight surface, $T_{\mathrm{pos}}$ is the same-window translation readout, $m$ is the matched mRNA control, and $Z$ includes amino-acid or position-bin controls, GC3, CDS length, M-density, codon-count controls, coverage, mappability, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The supplied Doench and Luo tables do not contain such rows.  Their finite counts are therefore used here as reality anchors for what has actually been measured, not as a substitute for the missing translation panel.

\paragraph{Observed support and non-support.}
The Doench record supplies a concrete finite guide-efficiency analysis with $5310$ rows and a small negative GC main association, $r=-0.0582917$.  The Luo-linked record supplies a larger $10543$-row cross-protocol guide-efficiency contact.  Together they support the statement that position-resolved nucleotide features can be audited in finite biological assay tables across CRISPR guide-efficiency panels.  They do not support the stronger statement that an $f_3$ synonymous residual coordinate survives against ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.  A separate linked translation-conditioned structural-order run reports status \texttt{passed}, two joined organisms, fold count $5$, structural-order joined sizes $3412$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and a weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$ with sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.  That linked result shows that some translation-conditioned BEDC-derived coordinates can be evaluated on finite controlled panels, but it is not the same-row $f_3$ position-resolved ribosome-occupancy statistic required here.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The local survival claim would require a held-out improvement after controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}} &= R^2(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\sim Z+q_{f_3}+w)-R^2(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\sim Z+\mathrm{simpler\ baselines}),\\
  \Delta_{\mathrm{heldout}} &> 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The break condition is triggered whenever review eligibility, a CRISPR guide-position table, a modeled tAI or stAI surface, a contact name, or the notation $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z)$ is treated as though this inequality had already been computed.  In the supplied finite data, no same-organism, same-CDS, same-window ribosome-footprint matrix is present; no matched mRNA denominator is joined to those windows; no wobble-weight asymmetry is observed on the same rows; and no rank-powered survival estimate is reported for the requested $f_3$ coordinate.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter therefore admits only a bounded boundary statement.  The CRISPR rows show finite guide-efficiency measurements and allow position-nucleotide audit language for Doench and Luo-style guide panels.  They do not establish translation realization, ribosome pausing, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality for the $f_3$ coordinate.  A modeled wobble-weight table, even if later attached, would still be only a codon-adaptation readback unless the same finite rows also contain measured or explicitly bounded translation output.  Any downstream claim about protein abundance, structural order, physical viability, function, mechanism, or a general biological law must be supplied by a separate layer-matched contact and a separately computed statistic.

\paragraph{Admissible current statement.}
The current BioReality statement is consequently negative but informative: the record is ready as a finite-row boundary chapter because it identifies exactly where the measured CRISPR guide-position facts stop and where the requested $f_3$ translation-readout statistic would have to begin.  The finite facts cited here are real assay entries, but they certify only guide-efficiency surfaces and linked finite cross-protocol routing.  The $f_3$ translation-survival conjecture remains open until a CDS-window table joins codon counts, amino-acid quotienting, wobble weights, mRNA controls, and position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency on the same rows, with positive held-out improvement after the declared controls.
