\subsection{CROWN-seq m6Am flanking identity as a finite stoichiometry record}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6am-flanking-identity-crownseq}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The m6Am packet is read as a finite row system rather than as a sequence law.  The active CROWN-seq panel contains $31886$ assayed rows, of which $19406$ have distinct coordinate identities after the row-level parsing and flank construction.  The variable subset contains $20942$ rows, and the low-tail stratum with stoichiometry below $0.8$ contains $3230$ rows.  The high-saturation part is also nontrivial: the fraction of rows with measured value at least $0.95$ is $0.343223$.  These counts are enough to make the chapter a finite stoichiometry record: the object under discussion is not an unconstrained transcriptome-wide principle, but a parsed table of m6Am sites with local sequence identity, coverage and GC controls, cross-cell comparison, and a declared empirical statistic.

\paragraph{Observed flank signal.}
The main finite statistic reports a nonzero flanking-identity association after GC-aware controls.  In the full panel the partial rank correlation is $0.268094$, while the variable subset retains partial rank correlation $0.168335$.  The low-tail subset remains weaker but still directionally positive, with partial rank correlation $0.098666$.  The matched-null computation used $1200$ null draws and returned $p=0.000833$; the same value is reported for the full statistic, the low-tail statistic, and the GC-matched null.  The null means are small relative to the observed signal: $0.003666$ for the full null, $0.001263$ for the low-tail null, and $0.002173$ for the variable-subset null.  In compact form, the finite comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n&=31886, & d&=19406, & n_{\mathrm{var}}&=20942, & n_{<0.8}&=3230,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{full}}&=0.268094, & \rho_{\mathrm{var}}&=0.168335, & \rho_{<0.8}&=0.098666, & p&=0.000833.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the empirical reason for treating the CROWN-seq object as crossing a finite readout boundary: local nucleotide identity around the m6Am site is not behaving like an exchangeable decoration once the declared controls are applied.

\paragraph{Positive controls.}
The same run carries internal calibration checks for known m6Am features.  The $+2G$ group has mean level $0.901842$ across $14263$ rows, while the $+2A$ group has mean level $0.837479$ across $6902$ rows, giving a $+2G$ versus $+2A$ separation of $0.064362$.  The minus-one cytosine contrast is $0.046475$.  The PCIF1-knockout control contains $31886$ rows, with mean $0.009592$ and median $0.0$, consistent with the expected collapse of the m6Am signal under loss of the writer context.  A second sequence-control comparison reports $0.957884$ for the SSCA GC class on $707$ rows and $0.71252$ for the VA/RR class on $1086$ rows, a difference of $0.245364$.  These controls do not prove a mechanism by themselves, but they keep the finite statistic anchored to the biochemical assay rather than to an arbitrary coordinate label.

\paragraph{Cross-cell reproducibility.}
The run reports cross-cell concordance as true.  The relevant point is not merely that the panel is large; it is that the parsed CROWN-seq table passes the stated checks for continuous m6Am measurement, position identity, chromwise hg38 flank recovery, GC-coverage control, GC-matched null comparison, positive-control reproduction, and cross-cell agreement.  The chapter therefore records a bounded m6Am stoichiometry result: local flanking identity carries information about measured m6Am level in this finite assay, and the observed association survives the finite null route supplied with the run.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring RNA contacts.}
The RBP-binding comparison is deliberately kept separate.  The RBNS/eCLIP binding record contains $900$ total peak rows and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but those rows are not the same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation, or rescue panel for this CROWN-seq m6Am statistic.  Likewise, the separate m6A stoichiometry exon-architecture record contains $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct entries, true cross-condition concordance, and coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$; it supports another finite RNA-modification surface, not a direct proof about m6Am flanking identity.  These neighboring contacts are useful guards against over-reading: they show that RNA-modification and RBP rows can be organized as finite empirical panels, while preserving the fact that each panel has its own assay boundary, controls, and admissible conclusion.

\paragraph{Null model and allowed reading.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to m6Am stoichiometry after the declared GC, coverage, positional, and cross-cell controls.  The observed CROWN-seq statistic rejects that finite null at the reported resolution.  The allowed conclusion is therefore narrow: in this finite panel, flanking identity is associated with m6Am stoichiometry, and the association remains visible in full, variable, and low-tail strata under the provided controls.  The record may be used as a bounded cross-layer RNA-modification readout because it joins sequence-window identity to measured RNA methylation stoichiometry on the same finite rows.

\paragraph{Boundary of non-claims.}
No translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, causation, or universal sequence law follows from this chapter.  In particular, the CROWN-seq table does not by itself supply ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, RNA half-life, target decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue rows.  A decay-factor RBP bridge would require a separate same-window panel in which measured local RBP occupancy changes with the editable RNA window, the half-life effect collapses under target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  Without that extra perturbation and rescue contact, the present chapter remains a finite m6Am flanking-identity stoichiometry result, not an RNA-decay mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite evidence supports a ready bounded readout for m6Am flanking identity in CROWN-seq: $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct coordinate identities, true cross-cell concordance, a high-saturation fraction of $0.343223$, full-panel partial correlation $0.268094$, and GC-matched null probability $0.000833$.  The empirical signal is strong enough to record the m6Am stoichiometry association, while the missing same-window translation, half-life, RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue rows prevent promotion to translation realization, RNA-decay mechanism, protein-level biology, or general biological law.
