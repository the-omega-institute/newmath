\subsection{CROWN-seq m6Am identity rows as a bounded RNA-layer contact}\label{sec:bioreality-m6am-crownseq-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The row set for the CROWN-seq m6Am contact is a finite transcript-window table, not an unrestricted biological law.  The verified packet h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6am\_methylation.m6am\_flanking\_identity\_stoichiometry\_crownseq records $n=31886$ assayed rows and $19406$ distinct sites or site-like identifiers.  Among these rows, $20942$ belong to the variable subset, $3230$ lie in the low-tail stratum below $0.8$, and the saturated fraction at threshold $0.95$ is $0.343223$.  The cross-cell concordance flag is true, so the observed signal is not merely a one-cell parsing artifact.  The same run records the positive-control checks as passing: the $+2G$ mean is $0.901842$ over $14263$ rows, the $+2A$ mean is $0.837479$ over $6902$ rows, and the $+2G$ versus $+2A$ contrast is $0.064362$.  It also records $707$ SSCA/GC rows at level $0.957884$ and $1086$ VA/RR rows at level $0.71252$, with an SSCA/GC minus VA/RR readout of $0.245364$.  The PCIF1-knockout control has $31886$ rows, mean $0.009592$, and median $0.0$.

\paragraph{Statistic readout.}
The principal finite statistic is an identity-and-flank m6Am stoichiometry contact after GC and coverage checks.  The same verified packet reports a full-sample partial correlation $0.268094$, a variable-subset partial correlation $0.168335$, and a low-tail partial correlation $0.098666$.  The matched null is not treated as a verbal control; it is a finite permutation-style comparison with $1200$ realized null draws and $p=0.000833$ for the main statistic.  The GC-matched null also gives $p=0.000833$, with the full and low-tail GC-matched null values both equal to $0.000833$.  The corresponding null means are $0.003666$ for the full comparison, $0.002173$ for the variable subset, and $0.001263$ for the low-tail comparison.  In compact form, the observed contact is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 31886,\qquad d=19406,\qquad n_{\mathrm{var}}=20942,\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{full}} &= 0.268094,\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{var}}=0.168335,\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{lowtail}}=0.098666,\\
 p_{\mathrm{main}} &= p_{\mathrm{GC}}=0.000833,\qquad f_{\geq 0.95}=0.343223.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers justify a bounded RNA-modification contact: local nucleotide identity and flanking context carry finite information about measured m6Am stoichiometry in the CROWN-seq rows.

\paragraph{What the rows do not yet supply.}
The proposed cross-layer claim is stricter than the m6Am result.  It asks whether a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate, after amino-acid quotienting and after same-row translation conditioning, can be connected to RNA half-life through a prespecified decay-factor RBP bridge.  The CROWN-seq rows do not contain the required RNA half-life column, the same-row translation-efficiency boundary, the local decay-factor RBP occupancy measurement, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective and non-target rescue controls.  Therefore the m6Am CROWN-seq fact can be used only as a neighboring finite RNA-layer example: it demonstrates that a chemical RNA readout can cross a finite identity-context boundary, but it does not by itself instantiate an RNA half-life mechanism or an RBP-mediated decay bridge.

\paragraph{Translation and RBP comparison contacts.}
Two separate verified packets show why the requested bridge cannot be collapsed into the m6Am statistic.  The mTOR translation packet h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012 has $n=6494$ rows and supplies a translation-layer readout surface, while the RBP-binding packet h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode has $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  These are useful comparison contacts because they sit on different biological surfaces: translation readout and RBP binding.  They are not joined here to the CROWN-seq m6Am rows or to the proposed $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate windows.  Without a same-organism, same-transcript, same-window join, their presence cannot be read as evidence that the CROWN-seq m6Am identity statistic is translation-conditioned or decay-factor-RBP mediated.

\paragraph{Local null model.}
The null for this chapter is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after transcription rate, transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, batch, amino-acid composition, and same-row translation readout have been controlled.  A finite half-life difference would still not be enough.  The bridge requires a measured local occupancy change for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapse of the half-life effect after target-RBP loss, restoration by wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.  If any one of these rows is absent, the result remains an RNA-stability association or an RNA-modification contact rather than a cross-layer RBP-decay relation.

\paragraph{Permitted conclusion.}
The CROWN-seq packet is ready as a bounded BioReality record for m6Am flanking identity and stoichiometry: it has $31886$ rows, true cross-cell concordance, a saturated high-stoichiometry fraction of $0.343223$, positive-control recovery, and a GC-matched finite null with $p=0.000833$.  The stronger $B^*_{Q6}$ RNA half-life RBP-bridge claim is not licensed by these rows.  No translation realization, ribosome mechanism, protein output, folding statement, physical admissibility claim, functional interpretation, phenotype claim, causal adaptation claim, or universal biological rule follows from the CROWN-seq table.  Those conclusions would require a separate joined panel carrying the same residual coordinate labels together with translation readout, RNA half-life, decay-factor RBP occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and negative-control rows.
