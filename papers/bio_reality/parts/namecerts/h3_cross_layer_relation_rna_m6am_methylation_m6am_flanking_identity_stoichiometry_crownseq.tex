\subsection{CROWN-seq m6Am flanking identity and the RNA-decay boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-m6am-flanking-identity-crownseq}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}  This record concerns a finite CROWN-seq panel in which transcript-associated cap-adjacent methylation rows carry local sequence identity, methylation stoichiometry, cell-context replication, and GC-aware controls.  The realized CROWN-seq surface contains $31886$ rows and $19406$ distinct sites.  Within that panel, $20942$ rows belong to the variable subset, $3230$ rows lie in the low-tail slice below $0.8$, and the saturated mass at or above $0.95$ is $0.343223$.  These numbers make the contact finite and inspectable: the claim is not a universal law about RNA methylation, but a bounded observation that a position-identity coordinate survives as a stoichiometric readout in the declared table.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}  The main CROWN-seq statistic reports a permutation count $B=1200$ and a matched-null probability $p=0.000833$.  The same value appears for the full GC-matched null and for the low-tail GC-matched null, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
  p_{\mathrm{main}} &= 0.000833,\\
  p_{\mathrm{full}} &= 0.000833,\\
  p_{\mathrm{lowtail}} &= 0.000833.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding null means are separated by subset: $0.003666$ for the full null, $0.001263$ for the low-tail null, and $0.002173$ for the variable subset.  The partial rank associations are also finite-row quantities: $0.268094$ for the full surface, $0.098666$ in the low-tail slice, and $0.168335$ in the variable subset.  The internal verdict recorded for this CROWN-seq surface is that the finite statistic crosses the boundary for a methylation-stoichiometry readout, and the cross-cell concordance flag is true.

\paragraph{Positive controls and positional identity.}  The control block is not a decorative appendix; it is part of the reason the row surface can be read as methylation-specific rather than merely as a parsing artifact.  The positive-control rows include $31886$ PCIF1-knockout entries with mean $0.009592$ and median $0.0$.  The plus-two nucleotide comparison has $6902$ plus-two-A rows with mean $0.837479$ and $14263$ plus-two-G rows with mean $0.901842$, giving a plus-two-G versus plus-two-A contrast of $0.064362$.  A minus-one-C versus non-C contrast of $0.046475$ is also present.  The SSCA-GC and VA-RR controls have $707$ and $1086$ rows respectively, with levels $0.957884$ and $0.71252$ and a difference of $0.245364$.  These controls support a narrow reading: local positional identity is attached to measured cap-adjacent methylation stoichiometry in this finite CROWN-seq table.

\paragraph{Comparison to nearby RNA contacts.}  The nearby m6A exon-architecture surface is useful as a calibration point, not as a source of extra authority for the present chapter.  That independent m6A contact reports $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance true, and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$.  The comparison shows that the BioReality ledger can hold separate RNA-modification row surfaces with their own row counts, concordance flags, and confound diagnostics.  It does not merge m6A and m6Am into one biological mechanism.  Likewise, the RBP-binding contact contains $900$ total peaks and only $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases; those rows can warn about RBP-contact scarcity, but they do not by themselves measure decay-factor occupancy at the m6Am editable window.  The mTOR translation contact gives $6494$ rows for a translation readout source, yet that row count does not make the CROWN-seq m6Am table a translation experiment.

\paragraph{RNA half-life and RBP bridge status.}  The stronger RNA-half-life conjecture requires a different finite object from the one actually realized here.  A positive bridge would need same-organism and same-window rows carrying the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label, a same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  The present CROWN-seq table supplies methylation stoichiometry and flanking-identity structure, but not that perturbation-and-rescue matrix.  Therefore the finite statement supported here is a methylation-stoichiometry statement, not an RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Local null.}  The null model is row exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to the measured readout after the declared controls.  For the CROWN-seq surface, the GC-matched null is rejected at $p=0.000833$ under the finite permutation budget.  For the RNA-half-life RBP bridge, however, the null remains in force because the decisive rows are absent.  In symbols, the verified methylation contact supports only the bounded assertion
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{position\ identity} + \mathrm{GC\ controls}
  &\longrightarrow \mathrm{m6Am\ stoichiometry\ readout},\\
  \mathrm{position\ identity} + \mathrm{m6Am\ readout}
  &\not\longrightarrow \mathrm{decay\ factor\ RBP\ mechanism}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The second line is a boundary condition, not a failure of the first line.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}  This chapter licenses no translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law.  It also licenses no RNA-decay mechanism and no decay-factor RBP bridge without a separate finite contact measuring local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls on the same relevant window.  The CROWN-seq evidence is strong enough for a bounded flanking-identity stoichiometry statement because the row counts, concordance flag, null probabilities, positive controls, and scalar contrasts are present.  It is not strong enough to promote the coordinate into a cross-layer RNA-half-life mechanism.
