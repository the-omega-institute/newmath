\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the m6Am flank record}
\label{sec:bioreality-m6am-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row claim about coding transcripts whose synonymous residual coordinate is retained after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  Its biological target is not the m6Am mark itself, but a proposed cross-layer relation in which a high- or low-coordinate coding-window label is joined to a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life measurement, matched abundance controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein contact.  The finite m6Am flank study supplies the strongest nearby reality check in the current table: the parsed Crown-seq panel contains $n = 31886$ rows and $19406$ distinct sites, with a variable subset of $20942$ rows and a low-tail subset of $3230$ rows.  The observed saturated fraction at threshold $0.95$ is $0.343223$, and the cross-cell flag is concordant.

\paragraph{Observed m6Am flank statistic.}
The m6Am flank experiment gives a concrete nucleotide-identity and stoichiometry surface against which the proposed RNA-stability bridge must be disciplined.  Its frozen position-identity analysis reports an observed $B$ statistic of $1200$ and a GC-matched null probability $p = 0.000833$.  The same value appears for the full panel and the low-tail subset, with null means $0.003666$ for the full panel, $0.001263$ for the low-tail subset, and $0.002173$ for the variable subset.  The partial rank correlations are positive but bounded: $0.268094$ on the full panel, $0.098666$ in the low-tail subset, and $0.168335$ in the variable subset.  These numbers justify a local statement that flanking identity and m6Am stoichiometry cross the measured-sequence boundary in this dataset.  They do not by themselves provide RNA half-life, translation efficiency, decay-factor occupancy, perturbation, or rescue rows.

\paragraph{Positive-control anchoring.}
The same finite run includes controls that make the m6Am flank signal non-vacuous.  The positive-control table reports $31886$ PCIF1-knockout rows, knockout mean $0.009592$, and knockout median $0.0$.  At the $+2$ position, $G$ occurs in $14263$ rows with mean $0.901842$, while $A$ occurs in $6902$ rows with mean $0.837479$, giving a $G$ versus $A$ contrast of $0.064362$.  The $-1$ cytosine contrast is $0.046475$.  The SSCA-GC class has $707$ rows at level $0.957884$, while the VA/RR class has $1086$ rows at level $0.71252$, for an SSCA-GC minus VA/RR difference of $0.245364$.  These controls show that the m6Am flank call is a finite experimental contact, not merely a coordinate annotation.

\paragraph{RBP-contact comparison.}
The nearby RBP-binding contact is smaller and weaker in the present ledger: the RBNS/eCLIP binding record contains $900$ total peak rows, with cross-RBP concordance equal to $3$.  That contact can witness that measured RBP-binding panels exist in the finite-row universe, but it is not yet the required same-transcript, same-window decay-factor occupancy table for this RNA half-life claim.  In particular, the RBP rows are not joined here to the $31886$ m6Am rows, to B$^*_{Q6}$ coding-window labels, to transcript half-life rows, or to wild-type and binding-defective rescue arms.

\paragraph{Claim condition.}
A positive RNA half-life RBP-bridge statement would require a single matched panel with rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 r_i = (&q_i, T_i, H_i, A_i, O_i, L_i, W_i, B_i, C_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotient, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is the RNA half-life readout, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance or transcription control, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $W_i$ records wild-type rescue, $B_i$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue, $C_i$ carries local composition, RNA-structure, motif-density, codon-pair, splice or isoform, transcript-abundance, and batch controls, and $Z_i$ records the finite statistic and rank-sufficiency diagnostics.  The intended positive pattern is not merely $q_i \mapsto H_i$.  It is the stricter conjunction that the half-life residual remains after translation and abundance conditioning, follows experimentally decoupled measured RBP occupancy, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after controlling amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout.  The existing m6Am flank data reject a GC-matched null for flanking identity and stoichiometry with $p = 0.000833$, but that rejection is not the same null as the RNA half-life RBP bridge.  If a future half-life panel shows a coordinate-associated RNA-stability residual without a corresponding measured decay-factor RBP occupancy change, without target-RBP loss collapse, or without wild-type rescue return, then the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated bridge.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter therefore records a ready but bounded BioReality claim.  The m6Am Crown-seq surface is finite, parsed, GC-controlled, cross-cell concordant, and supported by positive controls.  It can ground a measured m6Am flank-identity and stoichiometry statement.  It cannot, on its own, establish translation realization, RNA decay mechanism, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts.  For the proposed B$^*_{Q6}$ RNA half-life bridge, the absent decisive rows are same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue on the same finite panel.
