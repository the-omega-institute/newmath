\subsection{A punctured-cube boundary in the standard-code label table}
\label{sec:bior-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and contact.}
The record uses the six-bit codon cube as its finite coordinate carrier and reads it only through the curated standard genetic-code table.  The biological datum available here is therefore a code-layer assignment: each codon is assigned an amino-acid or stop label by a curated table.  This contact can test codon-label readback and a local label split on a tile of the cube.  It does not observe translation as a cellular process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, or a universal mechanism.  Those stronger layers require distinct contacts before they may be asserted.

\paragraph{Boundary row.}
The verified boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its codons are
$AAA$, $AGA$, $AGG$, $AUA$, $CUA$, $CUC$, $CUG$, $CUU$, $UAA$, $UAG$, $UCA$, $UGA$, and $UUA$.
The independent cardinality row gives the same size, namely $13$, with the same codon list.  In the punctured-cube row, this set is certified as the selected punctured-cube boundary, with face data summarized by
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R| &= 13,\\
f(R) &= (13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite observation is not merely a count: it is a specific subset of the codon cube together with a local face profile consisting of $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral computation separates the boundary set from the companion mass $M$ by the reported values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, with $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial polynomial check returns $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same residual reported for the cubic polynomial row.  At the precision of this finite run, the residual is effectively zero relative to the recorded tolerance, so the spectrum supports the claim that this boundary is a rigid local feature of the six-bit codon display.  The support remains a code-coordinate support: it does not convert the codon table into a law of translation or protein realization.

\paragraph{Shape-significance row.}
Against the finite null experiment with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, the exact-shape match count is $1$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap row has $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler attempted $57377$ distinct draws and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  These values make the observed shape rare under the sampled null, but the inference is still local: rarity of the punctured-cube pattern in this coordinate experiment is not by itself a statement about biological necessity.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid class information.  In $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$, the exact-match count is $0$, so the recorded exact-shape probability is $0.0$.  The observed $M$ size is $20$, and $7$ samples fall in the lower tail at or below that observed size, giving lower-tail probability $0.0035$.  This confirms that the local arrangement is not an ordinary byproduct of preserving broad amino-acid classes in this finite sampler.  It still remains a code-read result: preserving or perturbing amino-acid classes in a table does not observe a ribosome, a folded chain, or a selectable phenotype.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out row.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family and $1.0$ for the bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  These rows show that the coordinate boundary has predictive reach across curated code-table variants, while still staying within code-table readback.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out row and modules.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification assigns all $13$ boundary codons, with no extras, no missing entries, and no unassigned codons.  The modules are $agr=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$.  Every module has either predictable or boundary status, so the finite classification is complete for this row.

\paragraph{Local boundary conclusion.}
The finite record supports the following chapter-level reading.  In the six-bit codon cube, the curated standard-code labels single out a $13$-codon punctured-cube boundary with face profile $(13,16,4)$, nontrivial spectral values, rare null-model shape statistics, strong leave-one-code-out recovery, and complete module assignment.  The result is a local code-layer structure, not a claim that DNA must instantiate this geometry as a biological law.  No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this record unless another contact independently supplies that higher-layer observation.
