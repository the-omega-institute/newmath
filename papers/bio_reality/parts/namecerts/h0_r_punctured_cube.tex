\subsection{Punctured-cube boundary packet for the standard codon table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This packet records a finite code-layer pattern in the standard genetic-code table.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon treated as a six-bit display after the usual two-bit encoding of the four nucleotide symbols.  The observed object is not a biochemical mechanism; it is the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Within that finite table, the distinguished boundary region $R$ is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, and the finite cubical incidence count for this region is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the table-level assertion is precise: the selected codons form a punctured-cube boundary pattern with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces in the window-six coordinate system.

\paragraph{Finite readout.}
The same thirteen codons are recovered in the independent cardinality record and in the punctured-cube record, so the set identity and the size assertion agree.  The construction uses four local distinctions: a six-bit codon display, a local tile face, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  These distinctions are enough to identify the finite row pattern, but they do not replace the curated code table.  The code table is the contact that supplies the codon-to-label assignments; the coordinate construction supplies the local geometry in which the listed rows are compared.

$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}

Its reported values are $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  The two null checks agree that the local shape is uncommon under the tested finite shuffles.

The packet is therefore ready as a code-read claim: a curated standard-code label table, when viewed through the six-coordinate codon display, contains a thirteen-row punctured-cube boundary pattern with the incidence vector $(13,16,4)$, the listed spectral values, uncommon null-model shape scores, and complete module assignment.  Nothing in this finite row record says that translation has been realized, that a folded protein structure has been observed, that a physical admissibility condition has been tested, that biological function follows, that a mechanism has been identified, or that a universal biological law has been proved.  Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate contact: for example, an experimental translation assay for translation, structural evidence for folding, physical measurements for admissibility, functional assays for biological role, or a cross-system mechanistic study for universality.  The present record remains at the codon-label layer.
