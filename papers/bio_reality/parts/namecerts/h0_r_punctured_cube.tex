\subsection{A Punctured-Cube Boundary in the Standard Codon Table}
\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record isolates a finite code-layer pattern inside the standard codon table. The object under inspection is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read through a curated standard-code label table. The verified row is the subset $R$ of codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records $|R|=13$ and identifies this thirteen-codon set with a punctured local cube. Its cell count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is not merely a list of labels: it carries a finite face structure with thirteen displayed vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local square faces. The biological contact used here is deliberately narrow. It is the curated standard genetic-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels, and it is used only to test codon-label readback and the associated local split.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate content.}
The punctured-cube row is supported by two independent finite checks. The cardinality check reports the same thirteen codons as the expected set, and the shape check reports the same thirteen-codon set together with the face-vector $(13,16,4)$. The codons concentrate in a recognizable boundary region of the six-bit display: the CUN block contributes $CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU$; the stop and tryptophan-adjacent block contributes $UAA, UAG, UGA$; the AGR pair contributes $AGA, AGG$; and the remaining singleton or small boundary entries are $AAA, AUA, UCA, UUA$. This makes the row suitable as a local boundary tile: the record sees a finite neighborhood in codon-coordinate space, not a complete account of translation.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The same finite object has a numerical spectral signature. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, matching the recorded cubic-polynomial residual to numerical tolerance. The spectral-radius entry is $0.6752479308830213$. These numbers do not add a biological mechanism. They state that the finite coordinate object has a stable algebraic fingerprint under the computation that produced the row.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The shape is not treated as obvious background. In the random comparison with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. The same run found $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the test also exposes the finite search pressure created by the constraint. In the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed comparison size was $20$, exact matches were $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$. The corresponding values were $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}=0.0035$. These tests support the statement that the local shape is statistically sharp under the specified finite nulls, while still leaving its biological interpretation at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Leave-out behavior.}
Predictive probes also stay within the same narrow scope. In the leave-one-code-out experiment, $23$ tables and $63$ targets were evaluated. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. In the leave-one-codon-out experiment, the recovery rate was $1.0$, with $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $0$ anchor cases. These values say that the finite tile and its surrounding closure recover code-level entries unusually well under the stated probes. They do not say that a ribosome, a fold, a phenotype, or an evolutionary law has been reconstructed.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification is complete for the thirteen-codon row. The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons. Each module has a predictable-or-boundary status. At the family level, the leave-one-code-out probe records $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, and bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline. This is strong finite evidence for a local code-table phenomenon, especially around reassignment-prone codon families, but it is still evidence about label recovery in curated tables.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The contact table assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. From that contact one may report a punctured-cube row, a thirteen-codon boundary, a face vector, spectral values, null-model sharpness, leave-out recovery, and complete module accounting. One may not infer translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism of origin, or a universal biological law. Those stronger claims require separate observations of the relevant kind. Amino-acid label readback is a code-layer readout; it is not a measurement of folding, structure, molecular feasibility, organismal function, or necessity across biology.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The ready claim is therefore limited and concrete: in the standard-code table, the codon subset $R=\{AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU, UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}$ forms a verified thirteen-point punctured-cube boundary with $f(R)=(13,16,4)$. It has a recorded spectral signature, survives finite null comparisons with small empirical tail probabilities, and supports high code-layer recovery under leave-out tests. The result is a local codon-coordinate fact tied to a curated label table. It is not promoted beyond that layer without an additional contact designed for the higher biological claim.
