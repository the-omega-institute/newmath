\subsection{The punctured codon cube row}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed boundary.}
This BioReality row records a local six-coordinate codon display whose readout is tested only against the curated standard-code label table. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the distinguished local region $R$ is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The direct cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen codons appearing in the punctured-cube equality record. Its face-count summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is not merely a label list: it also carries a finite local tile shape with vertices, edges, and square faces in the six-bit codon coordinate system. The singleton-label corner and the three-point label class are therefore treated as local coordinate phenomena inside the code table, not as independent biological mechanisms.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling evidence.}
The associated spectral row reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, matching the recorded tolerance for the spectral radius. A separate shape test over $N=2000$ sampled controls used seed $42$ and observed one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. The high-overlap statistic recorded $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. These values support the claim that the displayed local shape is a sharp finite-row pattern relative to the reported null comparison, while still remaining at the code-label layer.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
The amino-acid-class preserving control was also run with $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It reports observed $M$ size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$. The finite-row probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row separates the observed local codon-tile behavior from a class-preserving randomization in the finite experiment that was actually recorded. The row does not include an explicit member list for $M$, so the scientifically safe statement is the scalar one: $|M|=20$ was the observed size in this null comparison, and the exact-shape event did not occur in the sampled class-preserving controls.

\paragraph{Leave-one tests.}
The leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out probe reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, closure coverage high $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. These tests make the local boundary useful for code-layer prediction, but they do not convert the local geometry into a claim about translation being realized in cells.

Every module has either predictable or boundary status in the recorded classification. This is the row's strongest finite organization statement: the punctured-cube set is partitioned without remainder into named code-level modules.

\paragraph{Local contact boundary.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact can test code readback and local label splitting; it cannot test translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism. Consequently, this row supports a local punctured-cube pattern in six-bit codon coordinates and a finite set of code-layer recovery statistics. It does not show that DNA must obey this geometry as a biological necessity, that amino-acid label strings determine protein structure, or that the local tile is a global law. Any promotion beyond codon-to-label readback requires a distinct higher-layer contact with its own observations.
