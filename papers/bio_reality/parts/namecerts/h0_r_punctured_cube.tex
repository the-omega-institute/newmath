\subsection{A punctured cube in the standard-code codon boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite display.}
The packet studies the standard-code codon table only at the code-label layer.  Codons are placed in a six-coordinate window, so that the sixty-four codons form a finite cube $Q_6$.  The distinguished boundary set $R$ is the following thirteen-codon row:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen displayed codons.  In the local cubical cell count, this row has $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed finite shape is not merely a set of labels: it has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four square faces in the selected window-six adjacency.  The biological readout used here is the curated standard genetic-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  That table supplies label contact for the standard code, not a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary claim.}
The claim is that $R$ is the punctured-cube boundary row singled out by the window-six coordinate display.  The word ``punctured'' is used in the finite combinatorial sense recorded by the data: a local cubical tile is present with the above $f$-vector, but the row is not promoted to a complete law for genetic translation.  The contact supports codon-to-label readback and local split checks.  It does not by itself observe ribosomal realization, folded protein geometry, admissible chemistry, organismal function, or a universal rule across biological contexts.

Thus the packet contains a numerical shape signature tied to the same finite codon window.  This spectral evidence is still internal to the displayed codon-coordinate and label-row construction; it does not add a higher biological layer.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Against a shape null with $N=2000$ sampled rows, the exact punctured-cube shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run recorded $25$ high-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite sampling frame was heavily constrained by repeated draws.  A second amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$, recorded no exact shape match, an observed module size $|M|=20$, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below that size:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the claim that the displayed row is uncommon under the tested finite shuffles.  They do not identify an evolutionary cause or a molecular selection pressure.

\paragraph{Leave-one recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the packet recorded
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ above\ baseline}&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in\ }M\setminus T&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit\ rate\ in\ }M\setminus T\mathrm{\ plus\ boundary}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
By family, the hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, $1.0$ for candidate-other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate.  The leave-one-codon-out test recorded $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, no anchor cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These are code-table recovery statistics; they are not measurements of translation dynamics.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen boundary codons are completely assigned to six named modules.  The recorded partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $13$ assigned codons, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Every module contains a predictable or boundary member.  This completes the finite row bookkeeping for the punctured-cube claim.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  The standard-code table, read through the six-coordinate codon window, exposes a thirteen-codon boundary row with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, a stable module partition, uncommon null-shuffle behavior, and strong leave-one recovery at the code-label layer.  The same evidence blocks any stronger sentence unless another contact is supplied.  In particular, the packet does not establish translation realization, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Amino-acid labels are labels in this record; they are not folded structures.
