\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The packet works in the six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$, with each codon represented by its window-six coordinate and then read only through the curated standard-code label table.  The distinguished set $R$ is the following thirteen-codon row:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality row gives $|R|=13$, and the punctured-cube row identifies the same thirteen codons with the same ordered support.  The cell-count signature attached to this finite shape is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed object is not merely a label subset: it has a local two-dimensional cubical boundary profile inside the six-bit codon cube.  The term ``punctured cube'' is used here in this finite combinatorial sense only.  It names the local cubical incidence pattern carried by $R$ and does not assert a global biological principle.

These rows say that the displayed local shape is rare under the supplied finite comparisons.  They do not say that the genetic code is forced by the cube, nor that a codon-tile pattern is sufficient to derive molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Therefore the chapter closes at the code-read layer.  The contact can test codon-label readback and the local label split of the tile; it cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence obtained from the table is not a folded protein structure, and a CUN/UUR or tRNA-Leu wording route is not structural evidence unless it is tied to a separate organism-matched structure or mechanism contact.  The finite evidence supports a rare and recoverable local codon-window pattern, not a claim that biology must obey this geometry.
