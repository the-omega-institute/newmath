\subsection{The punctured cube tile in the standard codon table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object tested here is the standard-code codon table, viewed only at the code-read layer.  The six-window codon display supplies a finite coordinate frame on codons, while the curated standard table supplies the amino-acid or stop labels.  In this packet the observed boundary set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The direct row audit gives $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons occur in the independent cardinality check.  The shape computation identifies this set with a punctured-cube tile whose finite face data are
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the recorded local object has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the chosen codon-coordinate complex.  The statement is intentionally local: it says that this finite label-side subset has a cube-like boundary form after one reads the standard table through the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape measurements.}
The finite spectral calculation separates the observed tile from a generic label reshuffling background.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the radial cubic residual reported as $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$.  This residual is at numerical tolerance and functions here only as a finite consistency check on the row computation; it is not a biochemical mechanism.  In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, the exact shape count was $1$, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap threshold had $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The large duplicate-retry count, $55377$, is part of the finite sampling record and should be read as a warning that the tested space is heavily constrained by the row-preserving setup.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class control.}
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null model tested whether the same tile appears merely because the labels were sampled inside broad amino-acid classes.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact match count was $0$, so the reported exact-shape probability was $0.0$.  The observed companion set size was $20$, and only $7$ null samples fell in the lower tail at or below that size, giving a lower-tail value $0.0035$.  These controls support the narrow claim that the observed standard-code row has a non-generic local boundary pattern under the tested finite null models.  They do not identify the evolutionary origin of the table, the molecular dynamics of translation, or any protein-level consequence.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out checks.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ genetic-code tables and $63$ target events.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary class was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-stratified record contains five families: alternative yeast at $0.8333333333333334$, bacterial plastid at $1.0$, candidate other at $1.0$, mitochondrial at $1.0$, and nuclear ciliate at $1.0$.  These numbers show that the local row pattern has strong code-table readback stability across the finite panel tested.  They still remain table-level facts: a table-level recovery event is not an observation of ribosomal execution, folded structure, physical feasibility, cellular fitness, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Codon-level recovery and module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out run recorded $0$ anchor cases, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification accounts for all thirteen members of $R$ with no extras, no omissions, and no unassigned codons.  The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification completeness record is $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with empty extra-module, missing-from-module, and unassigned lists.  Thus every codon in the punctured-cube boundary belongs to one of the named finite modules, and every named module contributes predictable or boundary behavior in the local test.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table as a codon-to-label record.  It supports checks about label readback, local codon-tile membership, module coverage, finite recovery rates, and null-model separation.  It does not contain an assay of translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, cellular function, organism-level fitness, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently the result may be stated as follows: in the six-coordinate codon display, the standard-code label table contains a thirteen-codon boundary set $R$ with punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, non-generic behavior under the recorded finite null models, and complete module-level accounting.  No stronger biological claim follows from this packet alone.  Any move from code-read geometry to translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires a separate observation designed for that higher layer.
