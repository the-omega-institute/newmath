\subsection{A punctured-cube row for the standard-code boundary set}
\label{subsec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record concerns a window-six encoding of the standard genetic-code table.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read as a six-bit display for the three nucleotide positions of a codon.  The finite row isolates a boundary set $R$ whose members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality row gives $|R|=13$ and returns exactly the same thirteen codons.  The punctured-cube row further reports the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the object under discussion is not an arbitrary amino-acid list: it is a finite local tile in the six-coordinate codon cube, with thirteen occupied corners, sixteen local edges, and four two-dimensional faces in the tested representation.

\paragraph{Observed label contact.}
The only reality contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  This contact is sufficient for code-layer readback: it lets the finite coordinate set be compared with the conventional labels attached to $\mathrm{AAA}$, $\mathrm{AGA}$, $\mathrm{AGG}$, $\mathrm{AUA}$, $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, $\mathrm{CUU}$, $\mathrm{UAA}$, $\mathrm{UAG}$, $\mathrm{UCA}$, $\mathrm{UGA}$, and $\mathrm{UUA}$.  It is not a contact with translation dynamics, protein folding, structural order, thermodynamic admissibility, biological function, evolutionary mechanism, or a universal law.  Those stronger claims require separate observations of the corresponding layer; they are not licensed by a codon-label table.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The finite spectrum row reports two distinguished radii and an auxiliary scalar:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records a cubic residual of $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, equivalently the listed radial polynomial value, so the numerical certificate is at roundoff scale rather than a visible violation.  A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and twenty-five high-overlap matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  That run also reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which is important because the null search was finite, collision-heavy, and explicitly counted.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed comparison set has size $20$, the exact-shape count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$, giving a lower-tail value $0.0035$ and an exact-shape value $0.0$ in the sampled table.

\paragraph{Leave-out recovery.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and adding the boundary raises the value to $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers support a local recoverability statement for code labels in the tested window, not a biological mechanism statement.  They say that the coordinate-and-boundary description is highly predictive for the coded labels under the supplied leave-out tests; they do not say that a ribosome, a folded protein, a cellular phenotype, or a physical pathway has been reconstructed.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module row assigns all thirteen members of $R$ with no extras, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.  The six reported modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The summary fields give $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and assigned count $13$.  Thus the row closes a finite bookkeeping problem: every boundary codon belongs to one named module, and the module list neither omits nor adds codons.  The by-family code-out row reports five families, with hit rates above baseline $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  This family breakdown is useful as a robustness check for the code-read layer, but it still remains label-level evidence.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The claim certified by these rows is local and finite: in the six-coordinate codon cube, the listed set $R$ behaves as a punctured-cube boundary tile with vector $(13,16,4)$, nontrivial spectral values, low sampled null frequencies, complete module assignment, and high leave-out recovery against curated code labels.  The result therefore records a reproducible code-read pattern for the standard-code table.  It does not establish translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal mechanism, or universality.  A future claim crossing any of those boundaries must attach an additional contact that observes that layer directly, rather than promoting the present codon-label pattern beyond its measured scope.
