\subsection{Window-six punctured-cube boundary for the standard code}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The packet uses the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ as its finite display.  A codon is read as a six-bit window after the ordinary four-letter alphabet has been represented by two binary coordinates per nucleotide.  The biological contact is only the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Within that contact, the distinguished row set $R$ is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count is $|R|=13$.  The same computation reports the cell counts
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed set is not being used merely as a list of exceptional codons.  It is treated as a local face-and-corner pattern inside $Q_6$: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces survive in the finite cubical incidence structure.  The phrase ``punctured cube'' in this chapter means only this finite cubical shape, together with the listed codons and their standard-code labels.

\paragraph{Local label geometry.}
The row set contains a singleton label corner and several multi-codon label classes.  The singleton row visible in the finite data is $\mathrm{AAA}$, while the three-codon stop-related class is represented by $\mathrm{UAA}$, $\mathrm{UAG}$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$.  The complete module assignment accounts for all thirteen rows and introduces no extra module codons: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, the missing list is empty, and the unassigned list is empty.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the finite label split under study.  It is a code-level observation: the table supplies labels, and the coordinate display supplies adjacency and face data.  Nothing in this row set observes ribosomal translation, chemical viability, folded structure, organismal fitness, or a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The same finite packet records a scalar spectral profile.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the same number is reported as the cubic-polynomial residual.  Thus the numerical witness is internally coherent at the stated tolerance: the observed finite operator has spectral radius $0.6752479308830213$, and the residual is at roundoff scale.  This spectral datum is used only as a finite shape diagnostic.  It is not evidence that the genetic code is forced by geometry, nor that the window-six display determines a biological process.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Against the shape randomization with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, the exact shape appeared once.  The resulting exact-shape probability estimate is $p=0.0005$.  The high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p=0.0125$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite sampling space is being hit repeatedly rather than casually traversed.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, the observed value was $|M|=20$, the exact-shape estimate was $0.0$, and the lower-tail estimate was $0.0035$.  These finite numbers support the claim that the observed local shape is not typical under the stated null procedures.  They do not replace a mechanistic model.

\paragraph{Leave-one behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out check evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$; the same $0.9841269841269841$ value is reported for performance above baseline.  By family, the hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  The leave-one-codon-out check reports boundary count $5$, anchor count $0$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These row-level tests say that the finite local tile has predictive stability inside the code-label contact.  They do not say that an omitted codon is physically reconstructed, translated, folded, or functionally selected by the coordinate geometry.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  The standard-code table gives a finite label readback, and the six-bit codon cube exposes a local punctured-cube pattern with $13$ codons, $16$ edges, and $4$ square faces.  The null tests and leave-one checks make this pattern nontrivial as a code-layer finite observation.  However, the contact is a codon-to-label table, not a translation experiment, not a protein-structure measurement, not a physical-admissibility test, not a functional assay, and not evidence for universality across all biological contexts.  Any step from this local code tile to translation, folding, chemical possibility, organismal function, mechanism, or global biological law would require a separate contact that directly observes that higher layer.
