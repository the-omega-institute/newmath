\subsection{The punctured-cube codon boundary packet}\label{subsec:h0-r-punctured-cube}\origin{ai}
\paragraph{Finite object and readback level.}
This packet records a finite codon-window observation at the code-read level.  The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is represented by a six-bit display and the curated standard-code table supplies only codon-to-label readback.  The distinguished boundary set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons are returned by the punctured-cube computation.  The face-count signature attached to this finite object is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the packet is not merely a list of labels: it also carries a local cubical shape with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-dimensional faces inside the window-six coordinate system.
\paragraph{Shape significance and null comparisons.}
The finite shape was compared against randomized codon-label arrangements.  In the shape test with $N=2000$ samples, one exact shape match occurred after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold also remained uncommon: $25$ samples had Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second null model preserving amino-acid class structure evaluated $N=2000$ samples.  The observed comparison object had size $20$; there were $0$ exact matches, while $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below the observed size.  Thus the reported probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the narrower statement that the standard-code labels produce a rare local boundary pattern under the tested finite randomizations.  They do not by themselves identify a biochemical cause, a universal coding principle, or a physical constraint.
\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The same finite packet carries a spectral summary.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is also the recorded value of the cubic-polynomial check.  The spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers are used here as finite coordinates of the packet: they summarize the observed local relation between the larger comparison object $M$ and the punctured-cube boundary set $R$ without asserting a continuous dynamics or a molecular energy model.
\paragraph{Predictive checks at the code-read level.}
Two leave-one-out probes were attached to the same boundary packet.  The code-table leave-one-out check evaluated $23$ tables with $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the reported hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The codon-level leave-one-out check reported $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These probes test whether the finite local code pattern can recover held-out label positions under the stated coordinate and boundary constraints; they still remain tests of codon-label readback, not tests of translation realization or protein behavior.
\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family-level code-out analysis used five families.  The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  The module classification accounts for all thirteen codons of $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The six reported modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\texttt{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\texttt{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification summary gives $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$, with empty lists for extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons.  This supplies a finite internal decomposition of the punctured-cube boundary into recognizable code-table modules, while keeping the biological interpretation limited to curated codon labels.
\paragraph{Local scope of the packet.}
The admissible conclusion is that the standard-code table, when read through the six-coordinate codon display, contains a rare and reproducible local boundary-tile pattern.  The support comes from the exact thirteen-codon boundary set, its cubical signature $(13,16,4)$, the spectral coordinates, the randomized shape comparisons, the leave-one-out recovery statistics, and the complete module assignment.  The contact is a curated code table, so it observes codon-to-label assignments only.  No statement about translation machinery, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality is licensed by this packet unless an independent contact at that higher level is supplied.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence obtained from codon readback is not a folded structure, and cross-organism naming or tRNA-related wording does not convert a code-layer pattern into a structure-layer or function-layer result.
