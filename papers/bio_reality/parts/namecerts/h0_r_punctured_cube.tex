\subsection{Punctured-cube row in the standard codon display}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and contact.}
The object tested here is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The empirical contact is only the curated codon-to-label assignment: each codon is read as an amino-acid label or as a stop label, and no claim is made that this table by itself realizes translation dynamics, molecular folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a causal mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Within that limited contact, the row under audit is the distinguished label-boundary subset $R$ of the codon cube $Q_6$.  The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation separately records $|R|=13$, with the expected-codon list equal to the displayed set.  Thus the data row is not a vague codon-family description: it is a concrete thirteen-codon subset of the finite codon table.

\paragraph{Local geometry.}
The verified cell count for this subset is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Interpreted as a local subcomplex of the six-bit codon cube, this says that the row contains thirteen codon vertices, sixteen one-coordinate adjacencies, and four square two-faces.  The phrase ``punctured cube'' is used only for this finite local shape: the computation identifies a cube-like tile with a missing part inside the codon-coordinate display.  It does not assert that the genetic code is generated by cube geometry, nor that nearby codons must obey this pattern outside the observed standard-code labels.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The same finite run assigns the row a spectral signature.  The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial check gives $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the cubic-polynomial residual is the same number to the recorded precision.  These values are numerical certificates for the coordinate construction, not biochemical laws.  Their role is to show that the finite row has a reproducible local spectral profile in the codon display.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the sampled null used in the finite audit, the row is uncommon.  With $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, the run reports $55377$ duplicate retries, one exact shape match, and twenty-five matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
An amino-acid-class-preserving null gives a separate check: again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact-match count is $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$, and the observed $M$-size is $20$.  The recorded probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite tests support the claim that the observed tile is not a routine outcome of the sampled label-preserving alternatives.  They still remain tests of label geometry, not tests of translation, fold, energy, fitness, or organism-level mechanism.

\paragraph{Prediction checks.}
The leave-one-code-out audit evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the recorded hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  A family-level breakdown over $5$ families records hit rates above baseline of $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other rows, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast row.  The leave-one-codon-out audit records boundary count $5$, anchor count $0$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module classification covers all thirteen codons in $R$ with no extras, no missing module codons, and no unassigned codons.  The assigned modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded summary is $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and assigned count $13$, with empty extra, missing, and unassigned lists.  This is the strongest local reading supported by the row: the standard code exposes a thirteen-codon boundary tile whose modules are completely accounted for under the audited classification.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The row is accepted as a finite code-layer observation: in the standard codon table, the displayed thirteen-codon subset has the verified punctured-cube cell vector $(13,16,4)$, the stated spectral profile, significant rarity under the sampled nulls, strong leave-one-code-out behavior, perfect leave-one-codon-out recovery for the recorded predictable set, and complete module assignment.  The result stops at codon-label readback.  A separate translation experiment would be required for translation realization; a separate structural contact would be required for folding or structural order; separate physical and functional contacts would be required for admissibility or function; and a broad comparative mechanism study would be required before any universality statement.
