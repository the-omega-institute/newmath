\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed row.}
The packet records a finite code-layer phenomenon in the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The biological contact is the curated standard-code table, used only as a table of codon labels.  Within that finite contact, the distinguished row $R$ has cardinality $13$ and consists exactly of
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this row as a punctured-cube tile with face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite observation is not merely that thirteen codons were named.  It is that the named thirteen-codon row occupies a local cubical boundary pattern: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four square faces in the induced six-bit display.  The singleton-corner and three-point-class distinctions are therefore read at the level of coordinate incidence together with the curated codon-to-label table, not at any higher biological layer.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table.  It supplies codon-to-label readback and allows the local split in $Q_6$ to be tested against labels.  It does not observe translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, a molecular mechanism, or universality.  Therefore the chapter records a coordinate-and-label fact: the standard-code row $R$ is a thirteen-codon punctured-cube tile with the stated incidence vector, spectrum, null-model rarity, recovery behavior, and module partition.  Any claim about actual translation, protein folding, structural order, physical feasibility, organismal function, or a universal biological principle would require a separate contact of the appropriate kind.
