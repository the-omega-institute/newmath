\subsection{Window-six punctured cube record}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The claim concerns the standard-code codon table after codons are placed in a six-bit cube $Q_6$ and read only at the code-label layer.  In this representation the distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified face data for this set is the finite vector $(13,16,4)$, recording $13$ vertices, $16$ edges, and $4$ local square faces in the induced tile.  Thus the observed object is not merely a count anomaly: it is a small punctured-cube pattern with a specific codon inventory and a specific low-dimensional incidence profile.

\paragraph{Code-layer contact.}
The biological contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is sufficient to test codon-label readback and local label splitting in the six-bit display.  It is not a contact for translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  The chapter therefore treats the table as a label source for a finite codon object, not as evidence that the local geometry is a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Spectral measurement.}
The associated finite operators give two reported label-side scales, with $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$.  The radial parameter is $\mu=3.051487585298128$, and the cubic residual is numerically negligible at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,\\
P(\mu)&=-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This gives a reproducible numerical signature for the finite row.  The same data do not identify a protein structure or a biochemical pathway; they identify a finite coordinate-and-label regularity inside the code table.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
Against the recorded randomization test with $N=2000$, the exact shape occurred once, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  High-overlap shapes with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling log records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the comparison is tied to a finite search trace rather than an informal visual impression.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, the observed $M$ size was $20$, exact shape matches were $0$ out of $2000$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving $p=0.0035$ for that lower-tail event.

\paragraph{Prediction checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and adding the boundary lifted the reported rate to $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row separately reports $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, no anchor cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These checks support the local boundary-tile reading: the codons in $R$ behave like a stable finite boundary under these held-out tests.

\paragraph{Family and module resolution.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out split contains $5$ families.  The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast.  The module classification assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This partition is the strongest chapter-local evidence that the punctured-cube row is not only a set of isolated codons.  It also carries a finite module structure aligned with the code-label table.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The ready claim is therefore narrow.  The six-bit codon display exposes a verified $13$-codon boundary set whose induced face vector is $(13,16,4)$, whose spectral row has the recorded $\lambda_M$, $\lambda_R$, and $\mu$ values, and whose held-out tests recover the same local boundary behavior at high rates.  The claim stops at code-layer readback.  No translation process, folded protein geometry, physical feasibility statement, functional explanation, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from this chapter unless an additional contact supplies that higher-layer evidence.
