\subsection{Window-Six Punctured Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table only at the code-read layer. The carrier is the six-bit codon coordinate cube $Q_6$, equipped with the curated codon-to-label table as a separate observed contact. Inside this display the finite row isolates a subset $R$ of thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row verifies both the cardinality and the named elements: $|R|=13$, and the same thirteen codons occur in the independent cardinality record. The punctured-cube certificate reports the cubical face count
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed local pattern has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the finite coordinate complex used by the row. This is a local tile statement about the codon display. It is not a claim that this coordinate geometry is a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Readback contact.}
The biological contact is the curated standard genetic-code table. Its role is narrow: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or a stop label. The contact therefore permits code-label readback and local split checks, but it does not observe translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Amino-acid label strings obtained from this table remain label strings. They do not become protein structures without a distinct structural contact.

\paragraph{Null checks.}
The finite-row significance checks compare the observed shape against random alternatives. In the shape-significance run, $N=2000$ sampled tables were used with seed $42$. The sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. There was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. In the amino-acid-class-preserving null run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$. The exact-shape count was $0$, so the reported exact-shape value is $0.0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $0.0035$. These rows make the local codon-tile geometry statistically non-generic under the tested finite null procedures, but they do not change the layer of the claim.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon module row.}
The codon-withholding check reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ codons with high closure coverage, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$. The module classification is complete for the thirteen elements of $R$: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the missing, extra, and unassigned lists are all empty. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\

Every listed module contains a predictable codon or a boundary codon in the row. This gives a finite internal organization of the punctured-cube set, with the singleton corner $AAA$, the three-point stop--tryptophan class, the two-codon arginine class, the four-codon $CUN$ block, and the remaining one- and two-codon modules all accounted for.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The claim is ready at the finite code-read level. The evidence identifies a thirteen-codon subset $R$ in the six-bit codon cube, verifies its punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, records spectral numbers for the associated finite row, and supplies null-model and leave-one-out tests that are specific to codon-label readback. The result should be stated as a local boundary-tile phenomenon in the standard-code table. No translation process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility condition, functional biological role, universal mechanism, or cross-organism law follows from these data unless a separate observed contact is added for that higher layer.
