\paragraph{Object and coordinate frame.}
This record studies a window-six representation of the standard-code codon table.  The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, used only as a coordinate display for codon labels.  A codon is read as a point in this cube, and the observed table supplies the amino-acid or stop label attached to that point.  The packet isolates a distinguished boundary set $R$ whose members are
$AAA$, $AGA$, $AGG$, $AUA$, $CUA$, $CUC$, $CUG$, $CUU$, $UAA$, $UAG$, $UCA$, $UGA$, and $UUA$.
The verified row count is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R| &= 13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies $R$ with the punctured-cube pattern in the window-six coordinate system.  Its recorded face data are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2) &= (13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local object has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four two-dimensional faces in the finite tile detected by the computation.

\paragraph{Local tile content.}
The detected set is not a random list of codons.  It is concentrated in recognizable label modules: $\{AGA,AGG\}$ for the $AGR$ pair, $\{AAA\}$ for the arginine--lysine boundary, $\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$ for the $CUN$ quartet, $\{AUA\}$ for the isoleucine--methionine site, $\{UCA,UUA\}$ for the serine--leucine edge, and $\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$ for the stop--tryptophan region.  The module audit assigns all thirteen codons and leaves no extras: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and both the missing-from-module and unassigned lists are empty.  Equivalently, the classification covers the whole observed boundary set:
$$
\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{missing} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{unassigned} &= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable or boundary status in the leave-one-codon audit, which is the finite reason this section treats the pattern as a local label tile rather than as an isolated enumeration.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The shape signal is tested against two finite null regimes.  In the unrestricted shape comparison with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, there is one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-Jaccard threshold has $25$ matches at threshold at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and produced $55377$ duplicate retries, which should be read as a warning that the comparison space is finite and highly constrained.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving comparison, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed module size is $20$, the exact-match count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$.  The recorded probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the claim that the detected local shape is not typical under the tested finite randomizations.  They do not identify a biochemical cause.

The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and adding the boundary raises it to $0.9841269841269841$. \paragraph{Interpretation.}
The supported statement is deliberately local.  The window-six cube exposes a punctured-cube boundary tile for the curated standard-code labels, and the finite tests show that this tile is numerically structured, module-complete for the thirteen listed codons, and predictive in the recorded leave-one-out tasks.  The biological contact used here is only the curated codon-to-label table.  It observes label assignment, not translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, realized function, a molecular mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Any claim at those higher layers would require a separate contact designed for that layer.  In particular, reading an amino-acid label sequence from codons is not evidence of protein folding or function.  This section therefore records a code-layer finite pattern: a punctured local cube in codon-label coordinates, bounded by the curated table from which its labels are read.
