\subsection{The punctured codon cube as a code-layer boundary packet}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The claim \texttt{h0.R.punctured.cube} concerns a finite pattern inside the standard-code codon table.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as a coordinate display for the sixty-four codons.  The relevant distinctions are the codon display itself, local tile faces in the cube, a singleton labelled corner, and a three-point labelled class.  The statement is deliberately placed at the code-read layer: it concerns codon-to-label readback from a curated standard-code table and the finite geometry induced by that readback.  It does not assert translation in a cell, folded structure, physical admissibility, realized function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact with the corresponding biological level.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The verified row isolates a set $R$ of thirteen codons.  The row gives the set explicitly as
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate cardinality check reports the same list and records $|R|=13$.  The punctured-cube check records the face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the finite object has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional edges, and four square faces in the selected local tile complex.  The row therefore supplies more than a label inventory: it gives a small cubical complex whose boundary can be inspected directly inside $Q_6$.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The spectral row gives three scalar coordinates for the same finite structure.  The reported values are

\begin{aligned}

The radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is numerically zero at the displayed tolerance.  The same row records the spectral-radius value $0.6752479308830213$.  In this chapter these numbers are treated as finite diagnostics for the code-layer tile, not as physical energy, fitness, kinetics, or folding data.

\paragraph{Shape significance against finite nulls.}
The shape-significance row used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} = 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap test found $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9} = 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A stricter amino-acid-class-preserving null also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It observed $0$ exact matches for the shape, an observed $M$-size of $20$, and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed size.  Its reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite null rows support the claim that the selected local code-layer tile is not a routine outcome under the sampled constraints.  They do not license promotion from codon labels to organismal mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  It records a baseline hit rate of $0.19821739130434785$, a hit rate in $M\setminus T$ of $0.9365079365079365$, and a hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary of $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate above baseline is also $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Thus, under these finite deletions, the local code-layer construction remains recoverable by its boundary and closure pattern.  The recovery statement is still a statement about codon labels and table rows.

The family-level leave-one-code-out row records five families.  Their hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate-other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables. Every listed module has either a predictable case or a boundary case.  This is the local biological readback supported by the finite rows: a punctured-cube tile, its label classes, and its deletion stability within the code table.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The only reality contact used here is a curated standard-code table that assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for code-layer readback and local split checks.  It is not a measurement of ribosomal translation, molecular dynamics, protein folding, structural order, biochemical admissibility, function, or evolutionary universality.  The local tile may be a sharp and reproducible finite signature of the standard-code table, but it remains a code-layer signature until another independently specified contact is attached.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence is not a folded protein structure, and the CUN, UUR, AGR, or stop-to-tryptophan terminology in the modules is not by itself evidence for folding, function, or a global biological rule.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The claim is ready as a finite BioReality record at the code-read layer.  The verified data identify $R$ exactly, certify $|R|=13$, record the punctured-cube face-vector $(13,16,4)$, provide spectral diagnostics, test shape rarity under finite null models, and show leave-one stability across codon and code-table probes.  The accepted conclusion is therefore narrow: the standard-code label table contains a finite local boundary-tile pattern in six-bit codon coordinates.  The rejected conclusion is any upward transfer from that pattern to translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality without a separate contact designed for that level.
