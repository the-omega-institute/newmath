\subsection{The punctured codon cube boundary row}
\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The row concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  Each codon is represented by its three nucleotide positions, with each nucleotide displayed as a two-bit symbol.  The construction does not treat this coordinate system as a biochemical mechanism.  It is a finite display on which one can ask whether the codons participating in a local label split occupy a recognizable face-and-corner pattern.  The label readout is supplied by the curated standard genetic-code table: a codon is assigned to an amino-acid label or to a stop label, and no further biological realization is part of this contact.

\paragraph{Observed row.}
The distinguished set $R$ has thirteen codons.  In the recorded row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathtt{AAA},\mathtt{AGA},\mathtt{AGG},\mathtt{AUA},\mathtt{CUA},\mathtt{CUC},\mathtt{CUG},\mathtt{CUU},\\
&\mathtt{UAA},\mathtt{UAG},\mathtt{UCA},\mathtt{UGA},\mathtt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation records $|R|=13$.  The cubical incidence summary of this row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is not merely a list of labels: it has a finite two-dimensional boundary profile inside the six-bit cube, with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local square faces.  The row therefore supports the narrower mathematical statement that these thirteen codons form the punctured-cube boundary pattern detected by the coordinate probe.

\paragraph{Local label geometry.}
The codons in $R$ split into modules that match the recorded finite classification.  The complete module assignment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathtt{AGA},\mathtt{AGG}\},&
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathtt{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathtt{CUA},\mathtt{CUC},\mathtt{CUG},\mathtt{CUU}\},&

\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathtt{UCA},\mathtt{UUA}\},&

$$
The module audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  Thus the punctured-cube row and the module partition have the same finite support.  The statement is deliberately local: it identifies a boundary-tile organization of codon labels, not a rule of translation or protein behavior.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The same packet records a numerical spectral profile.  The two reported boundary radii are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,
\end{aligned}
$$
with radial parameter $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The cubic relation residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the radial polynomial value is the same residual to the recorded precision.  This gives a numerical consistency check on the row: the observed coordinate pattern is accompanied by a stable finite-spectrum signature rather than being only a named subset.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Against the shape null with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, the exact shape was matched once, so the exact-shape probability is $p=0.0005$.  The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The same run attempted $57377$ distinct samples and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  A second null that preserves amino-acid class structure also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It found zero exact matches, giving exact-shape probability $0.0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p=0.0035$.  These finite comparisons support the claim that the row is not typical under the tested rearrangements.  They do not identify a causal process.

\paragraph{Prediction rows.}
Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon tests add finite stress tests for the same local geometry.  In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ tables were evaluated over $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Family-resolved leave-one-code-out data report five families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count is $0$, boundary count is $5$, closure coverage high is $8$, predictable count is $8$, and the recovery rate is $1.0$.  These numbers are evidence for finite local recoverability of codon labels inside the tested rows.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard-code table.  It gives a code-layer observation: codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels.  It does not include ribosomal translation dynamics, molecular folding, thermodynamic admissibility, protein function, organism-level fitness, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently the mathematical row may be read as a local coordinate-and-label pattern in the standard code, but it may not be promoted beyond that layer by this chapter alone.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The supported statement is that the thirteen codons listed above form the recorded punctured-cube row in $Q_6$, with $f(R)=(13,16,4)$, the displayed module partition, the spectral values $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and the finite null and leave-one-out summaries stated here.  Nothing in this row entails that the local tile is a global biological law.  Nothing here establishes translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanistic necessity, or universality.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer in question.
