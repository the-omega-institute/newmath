\paragraph{Coordinate object.}
The claim recorded here concerns a finite window-six codon coordinate on the standard-code label table.  The relevant carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read only as a display coordinate for the sixty-four codon strings.  The distinguished row is the thirteen-codon subset

&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.

Thus the object is not merely a set of label changes: it is a thirteen-vertex cubical boundary pattern with sixteen displayed edges and four displayed square faces inside the codon-coordinate cube.

\paragraph{Local table contact.}
The only observational contact used in this record is the curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  That contact is enough to ask whether the coordinate row is visible at the code-reading layer, because the table supplies codon-to-label readback.  It is not enough to assert translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or universality.  Those stronger claims would require separate observations at the corresponding layer.  In this chapter the row $R$ is therefore treated as a local label-boundary pattern in a codon display, not as a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The record supports the following restricted conclusion.  In the six-bit codon display of the curated standard-code table, the row $R$ forms a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary with face vector $(13,16,4)$, nontrivial spectral diagnostics, significant shape behavior under the recorded finite null tests, and complete module accounting.  The same record blocks a larger biological reading. Any future structural, translational, functional, or organism-wide assertion must enter through a distinct empirical contact rather than through this codon-label table alone.
