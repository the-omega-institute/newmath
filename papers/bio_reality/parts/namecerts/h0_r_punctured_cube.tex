\subsection{A punctured-cube boundary row in the standard-code table}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate row.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite display of the $64$ codons by binary coordinates for the three nucleotide positions.  The distinguished row $R$ is not asserted to be a biological mechanism.  It is a finite boundary subset selected by the coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation probes, and then compared with the curated codon-to-label table.  The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with cardinality
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R|&=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run verifies that this row has the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)&=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is being used here as a local tile with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces.  The phrase ``punctured cube'' is therefore a finite combinatorial description of the displayed codon subset, not a statement about molecular necessity.

\paragraph{Label contact.}
The external contact used by this record is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact supplies codon labels: each codon is assigned an amino-acid label or a stop label.  It does not supply translation dynamics, ribosomal realization, folding, energetic admissibility, organismal function, or a universal law.  In this chapter the table is used only to ask whether the finite row $R$ and its local boundary behavior align with the observed code labels.  The singleton-corner and three-point label-class language therefore remains at the level of label readback.  A codon label sequence is not a folded protein, and a local codon tile is not a global biological rule.

\paragraph{Spectral row check.}
The spectral probe records two local scales and one radial parameter.  The verified values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic and radial residuals are both $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at tolerance for the finite computation.  The point of this row is not that the spectrum explains the genetic code.  The point is narrower: after the codon cube is displayed in the chosen coordinates, the same finite subset that gives the punctured-cube face vector also sits inside a reproducible spectral calculation with a stable radius value $0.6752479308830213$ for the $M$ side and $0.4758352843197002$ for the $R$ side.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Two randomization checks give the row its empirical pressure.  In the shape-significance comparison with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, the exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and high-Jaccard matches with threshold $0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite sampling space is tight enough that duplicate management is part of the observation.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  Thus the finite-run values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $0.0$ is the observed finite count divided by $2000$, not a proof of impossibility outside the sampled ensemble.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the row-based recovery rates were much higher:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T)&=0.9365079365079365,\\
\mathrm{hit}(M\setminus T+\partial)&=0.9841269841269841,\\
\mathrm{hit}_{\mathrm{above\ baseline}}&=0.9841269841269841.
\end{aligned}
$$
The leave-one-codon-out row has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers support a local boundary-reading claim: the row is useful for recovering code-layer labels in the tested finite setting.  They do not promote the row into an account of translation or function.

The family split covers $5$ families.  The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables. Every module has either predictable or boundary status in the finite classification.  This is the strongest local conclusion licensed by the present data: the punctured-cube row organizes a specific standard-code boundary subset and its module split.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The row is a code-layer record.  It supports the statement that window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern whose labels can be checked against the curated standard-code table.  It does not show that DNA must obey this geometry, that ribosomes realize the geometry, that the corresponding amino-acid labels fold into any particular structure, that a protein is physically admissible, that a biological function follows, or that the pattern is universal across life.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact that actually measures translation, structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Until such a contact is supplied, the scientifically disciplined reading is finite and local: $R$ is the thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary row in the standard-code label table, with verified face vector $(13,16,4)$, stable numerical spectrum, nontrivial null-model pressure, and leave-one-out recovery behavior at the label layer only.
