\subsection{Punctured-Cube Readback in the Standard Codon Table}\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The claim records a local window-six codon pattern in the standard-code table.  The ambient finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read as a six-bit display of the $64$ codon positions.  Inside this carrier, the distinguished set $R$ is exactly the thirteen-codon row
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit identifies this same row in two independent ways: its cardinality is $13$, and its cubical incidence profile is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the observed object is not merely a list of exceptional codons; it is a punctured local cubical patch with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces in the selected coordinate display.  The relevant biological readback is only the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.

\paragraph{Label modules inside the row.}
The row is completely partitioned by the module audit.  The six reported modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification check reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and assigned count $13$, with no extra module codons, no missing row codons, and no unassigned codons.  This is the local label split carried by the standard table: a singleton corner, a three-point stop-or-tryptophan class, a four-codon CUN block, and the remaining small classes all sit inside the same punctured-cube row.  The construction does not require the row to be a law of biology; it only says that, after the standard labels are supplied by the curated table, this finite coordinate patch has a sharply specified module decomposition.

\paragraph{Spectral and randomization checks.}
The spectral audit attaches the scalars
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical relation is satisfied to the stated tolerance.  In the shape randomization run with $N=2000$, the exact-shape count is $1$ and the exact-shape probability is $0.0005$.  The high-overlap threshold gives $25$ matches at Jaccard at least $0.9$, with probability $0.0125$.  The sampling log also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the result should be read as a finite combinatorial comparison under the stated sampling procedure, not as an asymptotic theorem.

A second null route preserves amino-acid class information.  In that run, the observed $M$-size is $20$, exact match count is $0$ among $2000$ samples, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$.  The corresponding probabilities are $0.0$ for exact shape and $0.0035$ for the lower tail.  These figures strengthen the local readback claim because the punctured row and its surrounding class geometry are not reproduced by the sampled controls at ordinary rates under either reported null experiment.

\paragraph{Leave-out probes.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is again $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline hit rates are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These numbers indicate that the local coordinate rule has predictive grip on code-table label changes, subject to the finite family split in the audit.

The leave-one-codon-out experiment reports anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, closure-coverage-high count $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module-level audit also states that every module has a predictable or boundary status.  Consequently the finite row is not just recovered as a static list; its local boundary behavior is visible when codons are removed and reintroduced through the reported probe.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  It supplies codon-to-label assignments and allows code-layer tests of the local tile split.  It does not observe translation dynamics, ribosomal realization, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Therefore the correct reading is narrow: window-six coordinates expose a reproducible local boundary tile in the standard-code label table, and the finite audits quantify its shape, spectrum, null behavior, leave-out recovery, and module partition.  No claim about folded protein structure or biological necessity follows from these data unless a separate contact measuring that higher layer is supplied.
