\subsection{Punctured codon-cube boundary tile}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The record \texttt{h0.R.punctured.cube} concerns a local six-coordinate codon display for the standard genetic-code label table. The biological contact is deliberately narrow: a curated table assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop. The finite construction may therefore be tested against codon-label readback and against local tile membership, but it is not a claim about translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Those higher layers would require separate observations.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records the cubical face count $f(R)=(13,16,4)$. Thus the row is not merely a list of exceptional labels: it is a thirteen-vertex local object with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces in the six-bit codon display. The companion cardinality check reports the same set and the same size, so the punctured-cube assertion and the cardinality assertion agree at the finite-data level.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinates.}
The spectral row supplies a numerical consistency check for the same finite object. It records $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The cubic relation is numerically satisfied with residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, and the recorded spectral-radius value is $0.6752479308830213$. These values are used only as finite coordinates of the codon-window construction. They do not identify a biochemical energy, a folding potential, or a physical law.

\paragraph{Shape rarity against shuffled label controls.}
The generic label-shuffle comparison used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and twenty-five samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampler attempted $57377$ distinct rows and rejected $55377$ duplicates. This supports a finite combinatorial statement: the observed local shape is uncommon under that sampling protocol. It does not convert the local codon geometry into a universal biological principle.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A stricter null model preserving amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. The observed $M$-side size was $20$. The run found no exact shape match, so the recorded exact-shape rate is $0.0$, and it found seven rows in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size, giving lower-tail value $0.0035$. This second comparison keeps more label information fixed than the generic shuffle and still treats the punctured-cube row as unusual. The evidence remains a finite code-read statistic, not a mechanism of assignment.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$; the hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$; and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the same type of check reports five families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline. These rows say that the local boundary description is predictive for code-table label perturbations under the tested protocol. They do not say that the codon table is forced by the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records recovery rate $1.0$, predictable count $8$, boundary count $5$, closure coverage high $8$, and anchor count $0$. The module classification accounts for all thirteen $R$ codons with no missing, extra, or unassigned codons. The six finite modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The module count therefore matches the full $R$ row: $R$ has size $13$, assigned count $13$, and empty residual lists for missing, extra, and unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table. It supplies codon-to-label assignments at code-read resolution. Within that contact, the punctured-cube row is allowed to say that a local six-bit codon pattern isolates a thirteen-codon boundary tile whose label behavior is statistically non-generic under the recorded controls. The same contact blocks promotion beyond the code layer. In particular, amino-acid label readback is still only label readback: it is not a folded protein, not a structure observation, not a physical-admissibility test, not a function assay, and not a general law of biological realization.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite evidence is internally coherent for the claim \texttt{h0.R.punctured.cube}. The verified $R$ list has size $13$ and cubical count $(13,16,4)$; the spectral coordinates satisfy their recorded relation to numerical tolerance; the generic and amino-acid-class preserving controls give small empirical tail values; and the leave-one-code and leave-one-codon tests recover the boundary behavior at high rates. The chapter therefore records a ready code-layer claim: the standard-code labels exhibit a punctured local codon-cube boundary pattern in the supplied finite rows. The conclusion stops there unless a new, independent contact is added for translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
