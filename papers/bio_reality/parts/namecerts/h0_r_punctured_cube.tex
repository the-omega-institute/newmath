\subsection{A punctured local codon cube}
\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-label layer.  Each codon is placed in a six-bit coordinate display over the codon cube $Q_6$, and the only permitted readout is the curated assignment of a codon to an amino-acid label or to a stop label.  The finite row isolates a distinguished set $R$ of thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality row gives $|R|=13$ and lists exactly the same codons.  Thus the displayed set is not an inferred verbal summary: it is the finite carrier reported by the run with experiment id $e19917012c60$ and cross-checked by the cardinality run with experiment id $5d4a3459997d$.

\paragraph{Local tile statement.}
Within the six-bit display, the same run reports that $R$ has the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
The interpretation is local.  The thirteen codons form a punctured cubical tile: it has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional boundary adjacencies, and four square faces in the induced coordinate geometry.  This is a statement about a finite coordinate pattern attached to codon labels.  It is not a claim that the coordinate geometry itself causes translation, folding, biochemical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal rule of life.

\subsection{Spectral and sampling evidence}
\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube-evidence}

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectral row gives two local radii and one radial parameter:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that the cubic radial polynomial is numerically satisfied to residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$.  At this precision the spectral datum behaves as a stable finite calculation rather than as a qualitative description.  The row also records the same value $0.6752479308830213$ as the spectral-radius-in-tolerance value for $M$.

\paragraph{Shape significance.}
A sampling run with $N=2000$ trials and seed $42$ found one exact shape match and twenty-five samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The corresponding probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The run needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact-match count therefore marks the punctured-cube shape as rare under that sampling design, while the high-Jaccard count shows that near-neighbor shapes are present but still uncommon.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null row.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid classes.  It again uses $N=2000$ and seed $42$, reports observed $M$-size $20$, and records zero exact matches.  Its lower-tail count is $7$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a stronger finite check against the possibility that the punctured-cube observation is merely a generic consequence of preserving broad amino-acid label classes.  The row does not supply the member list for $M$; it supplies the size, null counts, and probabilities used here.

\subsection{Reality contact and scope}
\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube-scope}

\paragraph{Permitted contact.}
The only empirical contact used in this chapter is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact tests the code-read layer and the local split of labels across the finite tile.  It does not test translation dynamics, tertiary or quaternary structure, physical admissibility of a molecule, biological function, or a universal generative mechanism.

\paragraph{Blocked promotions.}
The finite rows support the following bounded conclusion: in the six-bit codon display, the listed thirteen codons form a punctured cubical tile with $f(R)=(13,16,4)$, rare shape statistics under the reported null designs, stable spectral numerics, complete module classification, and high code-label recovery in the reported leave-one-out tests.  No stronger biological conclusion follows from these rows.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence read from the standard code is not a folded protein, a physical molecule, a functional assay, or a mechanism.  Any such assertion would require an additional contact whose observable content lies at that higher layer.
