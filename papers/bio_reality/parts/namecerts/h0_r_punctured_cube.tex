\subsection{Punctured Codon Cube Record}
\label{subsec:h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record uses the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$ as its finite display carrier. Each standard-code codon is read as a six-bit window coordinate, and the tested boundary class $R$ is the following thirteen-codon subset:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with the same ordered codon set: $AAA$, $AGA$, $AGG$, $AUA$, $CUA$, $CUC$, $CUG$, $CUU$, $UAA$, $UAG$, $UCA$, $UGA$, and $UUA$. The cubical incidence audit returns the face-vector signature $(13,16,4)$, so this row is not merely a label list: it has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local tile faces inside the window-six coordinate geometry.

\paragraph{Local boundary interpretation.}
The geometric claim is intentionally local. The thirteen codons mark a punctured cube pattern in the codon-coordinate display, with singleton-corner and three-point label-class behavior visible only after contact with the curated standard-code label table. The label table supplies codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop readback; it does not supply ribosomal realization, kinetic feasibility, folded structure, organismal use, or biological function. Thus the object tested here is a code-layer split: a local face-and-boundary pattern in $Q_6$ together with standard-code labels. No claim of translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row unless a separate experiment or curated contact supplies that higher-layer information.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral audit supplies three scalars attached to the same finite display. The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also recorded as the cubic-polynomial residual, and the spectral-radius entry is $0.6752479308830213$. In finite-row terms this says that the observed matrix or adjacency-derived spectrum is numerically coherent with the stored cubic relation to near floating-point tolerance; it does not by itself convert the codon table into a mechanistic biological law.

\paragraph{Shape significance against randomized code-label rows.}
The shape-significance experiment used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Exactly one sampled row matched the observed shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. Twenty-five sampled rows had Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. These values support the statement that the punctured-cube boundary is unusually sharp under the tested finite null model. The amino-acid-class-preserving null gives a complementary check: with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$-size is $20$, the exact-shape count is $0$, the exact-shape probability is $0.0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$, hence $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$. The row therefore remains stable under a stricter label-class-preserving shuffle, while still remaining a code-read observation rather than a structural or functional result.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon probes.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out row has anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. These probe rows make the finite pattern operational: deleting a code table or deleting a codon still leaves a strong recovery signal in the coordinate-boundary organization, but the recovery target is the code label class, not a translated molecule or a fold.

\paragraph{Family and module decomposition.}
The family-level leave-one-code-out row partitions the signal into $5$ families. The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate. The module audit assigns all thirteen $R$ codons with no extras, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons: $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$. The modules are $agr=\{AGA,AGG\}$, $ar\_lys=\{AAA\}$, $cun=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\}$, $ile\_met=\{AUA\}$, $ssn=\{UCA,UUA\}$, and $stop\_trp=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}$. Every module has either predictable or boundary status in the supplied row, which is the finite reason that the punctured cube can be described as a coordinated local tile rather than as thirteen unrelated codon labels.

\paragraph{Reality contact and refusal of overreach.}
The only reality contact used in this record is the curated standard genetic-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels. This contact can test code-read behavior and local codon-tile label splits. It cannot test translation realization, protein structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or global mechanism. The folded-structure question is therefore blocked at this stage: amino-acid label readback gives a label sequence at the code layer, while folding, function, and organismal realization require independent contacts. The finite-row verdict is consequently narrow and positive: the standard-code codon table supports a thirteen-codon punctured-cube boundary with face-vector $(13,16,4)$, strong finite-null evidence, coherent spectral scalars, complete module assignment, and high leave-one-out recovery, but only as a local code-layer pattern.
