\subsection{Punctured-cube boundary in the standard codon table}\label{sec:h0-r-punctured-cube}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The packet studies a six-coordinate codon display for the standard genetic-code label table.  The carrier is the finite cube of length-three codons over the four RNA letters, read as a six-bit coordinate object after each base is expanded into the chosen binary display.  The observed boundary class $R$ is not introduced as a biological mechanism.  It is a finite row set whose members are visible in the curated standard-code table and whose geometric role can be checked inside the coordinate carrier.  The verified boundary row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen codons in the same finite class.  The punctured-cube check records the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the local object is a thirteen-vertex tile with sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies and four two-dimensional faces in the chosen cube presentation.  This is the content of the claim: the code-label rows contain a small local boundary tile, not a law of translation or a statement about protein structure.

\paragraph{Label contact and permitted readback.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact has resolution at the codon-label level.  It permits the packet to ask whether the coordinate tile agrees with the visible label split in the standard table, and it permits leave-one-row checks against the same code-layer labels.  It does not observe a ribosome, a folded chain, an energetic feasibility condition, a cellular phenotype, or a cross-organism mechanism.  Therefore every statement in this section is a statement about code readback and local tile geometry.  No conclusion about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this packet unless a separate observation supplies that higher layer.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model checks.}
The spectrum run attaches scalar structure to the same finite carrier.  The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check returned $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically zero at the reported tolerance, and the spectral-radius entry repeats $0.6752479308830213$.  These numbers support the claim that the tile is not merely a named list: it carries a reproducible finite spectral signature in the coordinate representation.

A shape-significance run with $N=2000$ sampled comparators reported $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$.  Thus the reported exact-shape probability is $p=0.0005$, while the high-Jaccard probability is $p=0.0125$.  A second null model preserving amino-acid class used $N=2000$, observed $|M|=20$, found $0$ exact shape matches, and counted $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  Its reported probabilities are $p=0.0$ for exact shape and $p=0.0035$ for the lower tail.  These finite experiments make the local tile statistically sharp relative to the tested comparison families, while still leaving all mechanistic biology outside the present contact.

\paragraph{Leave-one evidence.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ alternative code tables and $63$ target rows.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level split covered $5$ families.  The reported family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  These rows say that the local code-layer tile is stable under the tested leave-one table perturbations, except that the alternative-yeast family is not perfect in this statistic.

The leave-one-codon-out run recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Its module classification assigns all thirteen $R$ codons with no extras and no missing entries.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, $extra\_module\_codons=\varnothing$, $missing\_from\_modules=\varnothing$, and $unassigned\_codons=\varnothing$.  Thus the module decomposition covers exactly the punctured-cube boundary set and nothing else.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the narrow claim that the standard-code label table contains a thirteen-codon boundary tile in the six-coordinate codon carrier, with face vector $(13,16,4)$, reproducible spectral scalars, significant tested shape statistics, and complete module coverage.  The packet remains deliberately local.  The standard-code table is enough to validate codon-to-label readback and boundary membership, but it is not enough to infer translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, a universal biological principle, or any organism-level mechanism.
