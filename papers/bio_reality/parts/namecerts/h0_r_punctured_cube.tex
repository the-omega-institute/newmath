\subsection{Punctured Local Cube in the Standard Codon Table}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Record.}
This BioReality record concerns the claim $\mathrm{h0.R.punctured.cube}$ for the standard-code codon table. The working carrier is the six-coordinate codon window $Q_6$, read as a finite codon display whose labels are supplied by a curated standard-code table. The contact available here is deliberately narrow: it assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels, so it can test code-layer label readback and local tile separation. It does not observe ribosomal realization, folding, energetic admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of origin, or a universal law. Any assertion across those layers requires a distinct contact, not a stronger reading of this table.

\paragraph{Finite subset.}
The verified row isolates a set $R$ of thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check gives $|R|=13$ and returns the same thirteen codons: $\mathrm{AAA}$, $\mathrm{AGA}$, $\mathrm{AGG}$, $\mathrm{AUA}$, $\mathrm{CUA}$, $\mathrm{CUC}$, $\mathrm{CUG}$, $\mathrm{CUU}$, $\mathrm{UAA}$, $\mathrm{UAG}$, $\mathrm{UCA}$, $\mathrm{UGA}$, and $\mathrm{UUA}$. This agreement is important because the shape claim is not stated over a vague amino-acid class; it is pinned to this explicit codon subset.

\paragraph{Cubical shape.}
The row verifies that $R$ has the local incidence profile of a punctured cube inside the six-bit codon display. Its face-vector data are
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed object has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four local square faces. The phrase \emph{punctured cube} is used here only for this finite combinatorial profile in the codon-coordinate presentation. It is not a biological necessity claim and it is not a statement that DNA, translation, or proteins are governed by cubical geometry. The table supplies labels; the coordinate construction supplies a local shape test; neither one by itself supplies a mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectrum check supplies three scalar values: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The recorded cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, with the same value reported as the radial polynomial residual, and the spectral-radius entry is $0.6752479308830213$. Thus the finite row is not only a membership list plus an incidence count; it also carries a stable numerical signature for the surrounding local geometry. The available data report the size of the comparison set $M$ as $20$, but they do not include the full codon list for $M$ in this row.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. Exactly one sampled table matched the exact observed shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$. Twenty-five samples reached Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$. A second null preserving amino-acid classes also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. In that comparison the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class,lower}}=0.0035$. These numbers support a finite-code-layer anomaly claim: the local shape is uncommon under the sampled alternatives. They do not turn the local pattern into translation, structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Readback tests.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary codons was again $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the reported hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast. The leave-one-codon-out row reported anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. These results say that the finite local coordinate and boundary apparatus recovers many code-label positions under the tested deletion regimes. They still remain at code readback level.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ without leftovers. The six modules are $\mathrm{agr}=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$, $\mathrm{ar\_lys}=\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$, $\mathrm{cun}=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$, $\mathrm{ile\_met}=\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$, $\mathrm{ssn}=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$. The completeness check reports $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from modules, and no unassigned codons. This supplies a closed finite bookkeeping statement for the local tile: every codon in the punctured-cube row has a named module position, and every named module position belongs to the displayed row.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The verdict for $\mathrm{h0.R.punctured.cube}$ is ready as a code-layer record. The curated table contact supports codon-label readback and local boundary testing for the thirteen displayed codons. The verified rows support the punctured-cube incidence vector $(13,16,4)$, the cardinality $13$, the spectral scalars, the low sampled null probabilities, the leave-one recovery statistics, and the complete module assignment. The same evidence explicitly stops before higher biological layers: it does not demonstrate translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, evolutionary mechanism, or a universal biological law. Those claims must remain absent unless a separate contact supplies observations at the relevant layer.
