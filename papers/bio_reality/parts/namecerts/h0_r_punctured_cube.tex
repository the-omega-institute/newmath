\subsection{A punctured local cube in the standard-code readout}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
The record is a code-layer observation about the standard genetic-code table under a six-coordinate codon display.  It does not begin from a biochemical mechanism.  It begins from the finite table of codon labels and asks whether the codons selected by the local boundary rule form a small, reproducible shape inside the $6$-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  In the verified row for claim $h0.R.punctured.cube$, the selected set is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The companion cardinality row records $|R|=13$ and gives the same thirteen codons.  Thus the finite object is not a verbal description of a region: the row identifies the exact codons and the exact size.  The shape test records the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed boundary object has thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces in the induced local cube geometry.  This is the sense in which the local tile is punctured: it is a finite cubical pattern with missing ambient cube volume, not a complete biological system.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The same finite row family gives a numerical spectral signature.  The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, reported also as the cubic-polynomial residual, which places the computed value inside numerical tolerance rather than at a visibly drifting approximation.  The shape-significance experiment used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  Among those samples there was one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and there were $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite comparison was performed in a constrained combinatorial space rather than in an unconstrained continuum.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null row preserves amino-acid class information.  It again used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  In that run the observed $M$-region size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The reported values were $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These numbers are useful because they test against a label-aware randomization rather than merely against arbitrary codon subsets.  They do not turn the local cube into a causal theory of translation; they only say that, under the supplied finite null model, the observed code-layer pattern is uncommon in the sampled comparison family.

\paragraph{Leave-one readback tests.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$; the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$; and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-split row reports $5$ families.  Their hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  The leave-one-codon-out row records recovery rate $1.0$, predictable count $8$, boundary count $5$, anchor count $0$, and high closure coverage $8$.  These are code-read recovery statistics.  They support the claim that the local coordinate and boundary data carry predictive information for label recovery within the finite table regime.

\paragraph{Module decomposition.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ without extras or omissions.  It records $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no unassigned codons, and no extra module codons.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable or boundary role in the supplied classification.  The decomposition is therefore exact at the row level: it partitions the thirteen-codon boundary set into named code-read modules and leaves no codon outside the accounting.

\paragraph{Reality contact and limitation.}
The reality contact is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for codon-label readback and for checking a local label split.  It is not an observation of ribosomal translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, evolutionary necessity, or a universal law of molecular biology.  The finite cube shape, the spectral values, the low sampled tail probabilities, and the recovery statistics remain at the code-read layer.  Any assertion about realized protein structure, biochemical mechanism, organism-level function, or cross-context universality would require a separate empirical contact carrying that higher-layer information.  In this chapter the verified conclusion is deliberately narrower: the standard-code labels contain a thirteen-codon punctured local cubical boundary with face vector $(13,16,4)$, nontrivial spectral readings, uncommon sampled shape behavior, and strong finite readback performance under the supplied leave-one protocols.
