\subsection{The punctured-cube codon row}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The finite object studied here is the window-six codon cube $Q_6$ equipped with the ordinary standard-code codon labels.  The selected row $R$ is not introduced as a biochemical mechanism; it is a finite codon subset whose shape can be tested against a curated codon-to-label table.  In the verified row, the selected codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row-count check gives $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen codons in the cardinality audit and in the punctured-cube audit.  Its cubical face data are recorded by the $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the object is a thirteen-vertex local tile with sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies and four two-dimensional faces in the chosen six-bit display.  The claim is therefore a finite shape claim about a distinguished row in a codon cube, not a statement that the cell, ribosome, or organism is governed by this geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The associated spectral measurements give $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check is numerically closed at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial residual, so the finite spectral summary is stable at the reported precision.  In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$ sampled comparators and seed $42$, exactly one sample matched the full shape, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  The high-overlap count was $25$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which is important because the quoted probabilities describe the realized distinct-comparator experiment rather than an uninspected continuum of possible models.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.}
A second null model preserves amino-acid classes.  In that run $N=2000$, the observed comparison size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and only $7$ samples fell in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size.  The resulting exact-shape estimate is $p=0.0$ at the displayed sampling resolution, while the lower-tail estimate is $p=0.0035$.  These numbers support the limited conclusion that the observed codon-row shape is not a frequent outcome under the tested label-class preserving randomization.  They do not identify an evolutionary cause, a translation apparatus, a folding constraint, or a physicochemical law.

\paragraph{Code-read recovery tests.}
The leave-one-code-out test evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ together with boundary codons was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level split contained $5$ families: alternative yeast had hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families each had hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.  The leave-one-codon-out test separately reported $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchors, closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These are code-layer recovery statements: a missing codon label is often recoverable from the local row-and-boundary pattern, but the experiment still observes labels in code tables rather than translation events or protein phenotypes.

\paragraph{Module accounting.}
The thirteen codons in $R$ are completely assigned by the reported module decomposition.  The modules are $\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\}$ for $\mathrm{agr}$, $\{\mathrm{AAA}\}$ for $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$, $\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\}$ for $\mathrm{cun}$, $\{\mathrm{AUA}\}$ for $\mathrm{ile\_met}$, $\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$ for $\mathrm{ssn}$, and $\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}$ for $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$.  The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons, no unassigned codons, and no extra module codons.  Thus every codon in the punctured-cube row is accounted for by the listed modules, and every module has predictable or boundary status in the leave-one-codon-out classification.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of interpretation.}
The only outside contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code-read claims and local label splits.  It cannot test whether translation is realized in a cell, whether an amino-acid sequence folds, whether a molecule is physically admissible, whether a biological function occurs, whether a mechanism is universal, or whether a local tile is a global law of biology.  Consequently the ready claim is narrow: in a six-bit codon display, the verified set $R$ is a thirteen-codon punctured-cube row with $f(R)=(13,16,4)$, nontrivial spectral and randomization signatures, complete module accounting, and strong code-label recovery behavior against curated code tables.  Any claim about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality requires a separate observation channel and is not licensed by this finite row.
