\subsection{A punctured local tile in the six-bit codon cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite row and code-level contact.}
This record concerns a finite row in the six-coordinate codon display $Q_6$, where codons are read as window-six binary-coordinate positions and then compared with the curated standard-code label table. The biological contact used here is only the standard code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and it therefore supports code-label readback and local split tests. It does not itself observe translation in a cell, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.

The verified row $R$ is the following thirteen-codon set:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with the same listed codons. The local cubical count attached to this row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is not merely a label list: it carries a finite face incidence profile inside the codon cube. The phrase ``punctured cube'' in this record refers to that finite local face pattern, not to a claim that the whole genetic code is geometrically forced.

\paragraph{Null-model comparison.}
The finite sampling test for the shape of $M$ used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$. It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. The exact shape was matched once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the high-Jaccard threshold $\geq 0.9$ was met $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model separately reports observed $|M|=20$, no exact shape matches among $2000$ trials, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size. In that constrained null, the exact-shape estimate is $0.0$ in the finite sample and the lower-tail estimate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite comparisons support the claim that the observed local tile is rare under the tested randomizations. They do not, by themselves, supply a causal pathway or a biological necessity principle.

The hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.

Thus the row behaves as a strong code-label recovery pattern under the stated finite probes. The result is still a code-layer result: it concerns label recovery in codon tables, not the downstream realization of a protein molecule.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The supported conclusion is narrow. The six-bit codon cube exposes a thirteen-point punctured local tile with face count $(13,16,4)$, a stable spectral summary, rare sampled shape behavior, strong leave-one-out recovery, and a complete module partition. The curated standard code table is the only biological contact invoked here, so the chapter may speak about codon labels and local boundary behavior in the code-read layer.
