\subsection{The punctured window-six codon cube}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier and observed row.}
The record uses the six-coordinate codon display as its finite carrier.  A codon is read as a point of the window-six cube, while the biological label attached to that point is supplied by the curated standard-code table.  The claim tested here is deliberately local: the geometry isolates a distinguished boundary row $R$ inside the codon cube, and the table contact says which amino-acid or stop labels are attached to those codons.  The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with exactly the same thirteen codons.  The punctured-cube check gives the same row and records the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the finite object is not merely a list of codons: it has a specific local tile profile with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces in the tested window-six representation.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape measurements.}
The spectral computation gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The associated radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is numerically zero at the tolerance used by the finite run.  Thus the distinguished row is tied to a stable spectral reading rather than only to a hand-selected display.  The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled controls, with $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and twenty-five high-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  These numbers support the statement that the row has a low-frequency local shape among the sampled alternatives, but they do not promote the row to a general biological law.

\paragraph{Class-preserving null comparison.}
A second null run preserved amino-acid class information and again used $N=2000$.  In that test the observed module size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$.  The recorded values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This comparison matters because it asks whether the local codon-tile shape survives a stricter label-aware randomization.  The answer in this finite row is yes at the recorded resolution: no exact class-preserving replicate appeared among the $2000$ sampled controls, and only seven controls landed in the lower tail for the observed module size.  The conclusion remains a code-layer conclusion, because the contact contains codon-to-label assignments and not a translation apparatus or physical model.

\paragraph{Prediction checks within the code layer.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the recorded rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  The leave-one-codon-out run found $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closures, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These figures say that the local row is useful for code-label recovery tests under the specified finite protocol.  They do not say that the codons physically translate, fold, bind, stabilize, or implement a function by virtue of this geometry.

\paragraph{Module partition of the row.}
The row also closes into six named modules with no unassigned or extra codons.  The complete finite partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, assigned count $13$, no missing codons from the modules, no extra module codons, and no unassigned codons.  This is the strongest finite bookkeeping point in the record: every codon in the punctured row belongs to exactly the listed module accounting used by the test.

\paragraph{Local scope of the biological contact.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code label table.  It licenses a codon-label readback at the code layer: codons may be compared by their assigned amino-acid or stop labels, and local label splits may be tested against the window-six tile.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, tRNA abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, organism-level selection, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently the chapter records a finite code-read phenomenon: the thirteen-codon row $R$ forms a punctured local cube with $f(R)=(13,16,4)$, stable spectral measurements, low-frequency sampled shape statistics, strong leave-one-out recovery inside the tested code layer, and a complete six-module partition.  Any stronger biological assertion requires a separate empirical contact aimed at that stronger layer.
