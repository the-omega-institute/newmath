\subsection{Punctured codon cube at the code-read layer}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube obtained from three nucleotide positions with two displayed binary coordinates per position.  In this record, the distinguished row set $R$ is not introduced as a biochemical mechanism.  It is the finite subset selected by the window-six coordinate construction and then checked against the curated standard-code label table at the level of codon-label readback.  The verified $R$ rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check gives $|R|=13$, with the same thirteen codons.  The punctured-cube check reports the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed row set is a thirteen-vertex local tile with sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies and four two-dimensional faces in the induced codon-cube geometry.

\paragraph{Local label classes.}
The module classification assigns all thirteen rows and leaves no unassigned or extra codons.  The six displayed classes are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification completeness row reports $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with empty missing, extra, and unassigned lists.  Thus the local tile is not merely a cardinality coincidence: every row in the punctured cube is accounted for by one of the finite label modules that the code-read experiment tracks.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The spectral row supplies three scalars for the induced finite operator comparison: $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, matching the radial polynomial residual to numerical tolerance.  A separate shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and saw $55377$ duplicate retries, which is consistent with a constrained finite sampling space.  In that run there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The reported values are $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Code-table contact.}
The curated standard-code table supplies the reality contact used here: codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  This contact is sufficient to ask whether the finite codon tile lines up with a code-layer label split, and it is not sufficient to infer a process that realizes translation.  The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate in $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary rows was $0.9841269841269841$.  Broken down by five code families, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.

\paragraph{Single-codon removal.}
The leave-one-codon-out row gives a second local stability check.  It reports $\mathrm{anchor\_count}=0$, $\mathrm{boundary\_count}=5$, $\mathrm{predictable\_count}=8$, and $\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}=8$.  The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the recovery-above-threshold value is also $1.0$.  Read together with the module classification, this means that the local tile has a recoverable boundary-and-predictable structure inside the code-read experiment: rows can be removed one at a time without destroying the finite local account of the observed module split.

\paragraph{Scientific scope.}
The claim supported by these rows is deliberately narrow.  The window-six codon coordinates expose a local punctured-cube pattern in a thirteen-codon row set, and the curated standard-code table lets that pattern be read against codon labels.  No conclusion about translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality follows from these rows alone.  Amino-acid label readback is still label readback.  Any move from this local code-layer pattern to structure, function, or organism-level mechanism would require a different contact that directly observes that higher layer.  The present chapter therefore records a finite code-read alignment: a punctured local codon cube with a complete thirteen-row module assignment, significant finite-sampling behavior under the supplied null checks, and explicit refusal to promote the local tile into a global biological law.
