\subsection{Punctured Codon Cube as a Local Code-Read Record}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-punctured-cube}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table only as a curated assignment of codon words to amino-acid or stop labels. The working carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is represented by its finite window-six display. The claim isolated here is not that the biological system is generated by this geometry. It is the narrower observation that a distinguished set $R$ of reassignment-sensitive or boundary codons occupies a punctured-cube shaped local tile inside the six-coordinate display, while label readback is supplied by the curated code table.

The verified row for $R$ gives the following thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},\\
|R| &= 13.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row reports the cell count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2) &= (13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the observed object is a thirteen-vertex tile with sixteen local one-dimensional adjacencies and four local two-dimensional faces. In this chapter, the phrase punctured cube means exactly this finite cell-profile together with the listed codon support; it does not assert a biological conservation law.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The same finite data package includes a spectral summary for the local split. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at tolerance for the recorded polynomial relation. This supplies a compact numerical fingerprint for the local tile: the measured radius for $M$ is larger than the radius for $R$, and the auxiliary scalar $\mu$ records the associated radial normalization used by the row audit.

The shape was also tested against randomized alternatives. In the unrestricted shape trial with $N=2000$, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, the audit observed $55377$ duplicate retries. There was $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$. The exact-shape count was $0$, hence the recorded exact-shape probability is $0.0$, while the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving a lower-tail probability of $0.0035$. These numbers support a finite local enrichment statement: the tile is uncommon under the tested shuffles. They do not convert the tile into an explanation of translation, folding, chemical admissibility, phenotype, or universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Readback through the curated label table.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table. It assigns codon words to amino-acid or stop labels and thereby permits code-layer readback. It does not observe ribosomal realization, tertiary structure, energetic feasibility, or function. Consequently, a label sequence read from codons remains a code-level object in this chapter, not a protein-structure result.

Leave-one-code-out testing evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The family split records $5$ families. The family hit rates above baseline were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families. These figures say that the local tile is useful for code-label recovery in the tested table family. They do not say that the same geometry is a general law of biology.

\paragraph{Codon deletion and module accounting.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchors, and $8$ cases with high closure coverage. Its recovery rate is $1.0$, matching the threshold row value of $1.0$. The module classification is complete for the same thirteen-codon support: $R$ has size $13$, all $13$ codons are assigned, and there are no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.

The modules are explicitly:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has either a predictable or boundary status in the deletion audit. This gives a finite module-level account of the punctured-cube row: the thirteen codons are not merely counted, but partitioned into named local classes whose deletion behavior remains within the code-read scope.

\paragraph{Local boundary of the claim.}
The result recorded here is a finite code-read pattern: the thirteen-codon set $R$ forms the recorded punctured-cube tile with cell vector $(13,16,4)$, has the stated spectral fingerprint, is rare under the sampled null models, and supports high-rate recovery in the listed code-table probes. The contact is still only a codon-to-label table. No claim about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from these rows. Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate observation at the relevant biological layer, not another restatement of the codon-label table.
