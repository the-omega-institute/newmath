\subsection{The punctured codon cube as a local code-layer packet}
\label{sec:bior-punctured-codon-cube}

\paragraph{Finite display.}
The packet begins with the six-coordinate codon display obtained by writing each standard-code codon as a point in the codon cube $Q_6$.  Its selected boundary set is the thirteen-codon row
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The independent cardinality check reports $|R|=13$ and gives exactly the same ordered codon list.  The punctured-cube check then identifies this same $R$ with a finite cubical boundary object whose face data have
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a counted subset of the sixty-four codon vertices; it is a thirteen-vertex local tile with sixteen edge incidences and four square faces after the missing corner is removed.  This is the geometric content of the claim: the codons listed above form a finite local pattern in the six-bit coordinate display, while their amino-acid or stop labels are read only through the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling checks.}
The same finite object has a recorded spectral signature.  The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, matching the reported tolerance for the spectral-radius calculation.  The finite sampling comparison for the ambient $M$-shape used $N=2000$ trials with seed $42$.  Among $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ were duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second amino-acid-class-preserving null run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, found observed $M$-size $20$, no exact class-preserving shape matches, and $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed size.  Its reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the local-row distinctiveness claim inside the tested finite models.  They do not by themselves identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Code-table readback.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table: a table assigning codons to amino-acid labels or stop labels.  In this chapter the table is used only at the code-reading level.  It allows the thirteen vertices of $R$ to be named as codon labels and compared with standard-code assignments, but it does not observe translation in a cell, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal generative law.  The tile can therefore be described as a local code-layer boundary pattern, not as a global biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one checks.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The reported hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, $5$ families were evaluated: bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each had family hit rate $1.0$ above baseline, while alternative-yeast had $0.8333333333333334$.  The leave-one-codon-out run records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases, with recovery rate $1.0$.

\paragraph{Module partition of the row.}
The row $R$ is also exhausted by six named modules, with no missing or extra module codons.  The complete partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit reports $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Every module therefore contains at least one codon that is classified as predictable or as a boundary case in the finite row analysis.  This gives a precise local organization of the punctured cube: the row is not only a thirteen-codon set, but a fully assigned module-level decomposition of the same thirteen vertices.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The claim is ready at the code-read layer.  The verified finite row is the set $R$ displayed above, it has the punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, it carries the reported spectral values, and it survives the listed sampling and leave-one checks with the recorded rates and probabilities.  The scientific scope remains narrow.  Nothing in this packet licenses promotion from codon-label geometry to translation realization, protein folding, physical feasibility, biological function, mechanistic necessity, or universality.  Such stronger claims would require separate observations aimed at those layers, not merely reuse of the standard-code label table.
