\subsection{The punctured codon cube as a code-layer record}
\label{subsec:h0-r-punctured-cube}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object considered here is the six-coordinate codon cube obtained by displaying each codon as a finite word over the standard nucleotide alphabet and reading local adjacency through coordinate changes. The distinguished row set $R$ is not a free biological postulate; it is the finite set selected by the window-six computation and then compared with the curated standard-code label table. In the verified row, the selected set has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records the face data of this selected region by the vector $(13,16,4)$: thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies, and four square faces. Thus the row is a punctured local cube rather than a complete codon block. The phrase ``punctured'' is used in this finite combinatorial sense only: it reports the missing part of a local cube-shaped adjacency pattern and does not assert a physical gap in DNA, RNA, translation, or protein structure.

\paragraph{Code labels and local split.}
The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label readback at the code layer. It lets the selected codons be compared against amino-acid and stop labels, but it does not supply a translation experiment, a folded structure, a thermodynamic admissibility test, a functional assay, or a universal mechanism. The relevant biological contact is therefore deliberately narrow: a standard-code table assigns labels to codons, and the window-six construction asks whether the internal row selection has a stable local boundary pattern when these labels are inspected. The contact supports code-label comparison; it does not convert local geometry into a law of biology.

\paragraph{Spectral row signature.}
The finite adjacency computation also gives a numerical spectral signature. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated cubic check is numerically stable at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the radial-polynomial residual is the same number. This is small enough to be treated as a floating-point zero for the finite computation reported here. The point of the spectral record is not to claim a hidden biochemical force; it is to distinguish the selected row from a generic finite subset by a reproducible adjacency invariant.

\paragraph{Shape significance against random relabeling.}
The finite null comparison used $N=2000$ sampled controls with seed $42$. Because duplicate controls were rejected, the sampler made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries. Under that sampling, the exact selected shape occurred once, so the exact-shape estimate is $p=0.0005$. A looser high-overlap comparison with Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving $p=0.0125$. These values do not prove necessity of the genetic code. They say that, under this stated finite control procedure, the observed punctured-cube row is uncommon as an exact shape and remains uncommon under a high-overlap relaxation.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving control.}
A second control preserves amino-acid class information while testing whether the same local form appears. In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed comparison size was $20$. The exact selected shape appeared $0$ times, so the recorded exact-shape estimate is $0.0$. The lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving a lower-tail value of $0.0035$. This is stronger than a purely unconstrained row shuffle in one respect: it keeps a label-class constraint while still failing to reproduce the exact punctured-cube selection in the sampled trials. The finite row data supplied here do not include the full member list of the comparison set $M$; only its size, spectral value, and control statistics are available in this packet.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The row also has predictive behavior under a code-table deletion test. Across $23$ evaluated tables and $63$ total targets, the baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. Family-level subdivision records $5$ families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have rate $1.0$. This supports a code-layer regularity in how the local row and its boundary help recover missing code-table labels. It does not show that organisms implement the geometry as a causal rule.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The codon deletion test gives a complementary finite-row check. It records anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$. The threshold comparison also reports $1.0$. These numbers mean that the observed row has no separate anchor codons in the reported classification, while its predictable cases are recovered through closure and boundary behavior. The result remains a code-read statement: it concerns codon labels and local adjacency under deletion, not ribosomal realization, protein folding, physical stability, or biological function.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The selected row is completely assigned by the module table. The module classification records $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing selected codons, and no unassigned selected codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has predictable or boundary status in the finite classification. This is the most concrete form of the local tile claim: the selected thirteen codons are not an unstructured list, but a fully assigned boundary-sensitive row with codon modules visible in the standard-code readback.

\paragraph{Local verdict and scope boundary.}
The verified packet supports the following limited claim. In the six-coordinate codon display, the standard-code label table exposes a thirteen-codon row $R$ with punctured-cube face data $(13,16,4)$, a reproducible spectral signature, low sampled occurrence under two finite null procedures, strong leave-one-code-out recovery, full leave-one-codon-out recovery for the reported predictable class, and complete module assignment. The same packet also blocks all stronger interpretations. No translation realization follows from these rows. No folded structure follows from amino-acid labels. No physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from the local tile. Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer rather than inferring it from codon-label readback.
