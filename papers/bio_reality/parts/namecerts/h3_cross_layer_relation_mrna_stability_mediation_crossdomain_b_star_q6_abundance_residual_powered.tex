\subsection{B-star Q6 mRNA-stability mediation across bacterial and human abundance panels}\label{sec:b-star-q6-mrna-stability-mediation-crossdomain}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.} This record concerns a finite cross-layer panel in which nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are tested against protein-abundance residuals after an mRNA half-life or stability readout is introduced as a mediator. The tested coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. The record is classified at the $cross\_layer\_relation$ level, but only for the observed statistical decomposition from residual codon-coordinate geometry to abundance after conditioning on mRNA stability. It is not a direct translation-efficiency survival claim, and it is not a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Rows and controls.} The finite rows are organism-matched coding-sequence records carrying real codon counts, a protein-abundance readout, and a matched mRNA half-life or stability readout. The control matrix $Z$ has $24$ declared columns before rank loss: an intercept, $\log(CDS\ length\ in\ nt)$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. In the two computed panels the realized control rank is $rank(Z)=23$. The joint $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ block has $9$ columns, the residualized mediator block has rank $1$, and the combined residualized block has rank $10$.

\paragraph{Identifier joins.} The bacterial panel uses the Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 join in which Esquerre GeneId $b$-numbers are matched to CDS locus tags at growth rate $0.40\ h^{-1}$. It reports $3739$ CDS rows, $2947$ half-life records, $2720$ joined rows, $2947$ valid half-life records by gene id, a CDS join hit rate of $0.9978649586335735$, and $1019$ CDS records skipped for lack of a half-life match. The human panel uses Agarwal Ensembl Gene Ids without version against CDS header $gene{:}ENSG$ values, with unique gene-symbol fallback. It reports $19053$ CDS rows, $19480$ consensus records, $16165$ joined rows, $16150$ Ensembl-id joins, $15$ symbol-fallback joins, $0$ ambiguous fallback keys, a CDS join hit rate of $0.9779294769799313$, and $2876$ CDS records skipped for lack of a half-life match. These joins define the finite row support of the chapter; rows outside these supports do not contribute to the numerical claim.

The corresponding checks are $R^2_{M\mid X}=0.12750047680284674$, $R^2_{QM,path\ a}=0.02298556640056076$, and $b_{P\leftarrow M\mid X}=-1.2432793356009886$. For the human panel, the residual degrees of freedom are $16142$, the $p$-residual energy is $22233.302987773684$, the mediator residual energy is $1422.0291144063049$, $X$-explained energy on protein abundance is $910.5016829711035$, mediator-explained energy on protein abundance is $948.3971104494655$, $X$-explained energy on the mediator is $14.936525062963877$, mediator improvement given $X$ is $819.8980203765552$, and direct $X$ improvement given mediator is $782.0025928981933$. \paragraph{Computed mediation readback.} The mediation scalar is a joint nine-dimensional variance partition: first estimate the residual association from $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates to protein abundance, then recompute it after adding the residualized mRNA stability mediator. The numerical summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(E.\ coli) &= 0.11632311854858134,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M}(E.\ coli) &= 0.0875945621412319,\\
\mu_{M}(E.\ coli) &= 0.24697202727892137,\\
p_{perm}(E.\ coli) &= 0.000999000999000999,\\
R^2_{QP}(human) &= 0.04095215557813418,\\
R^2_{QP\mid M}(human) &= 0.03517257842112907,\\
\mu_{M}(human) &= 0.1411299863319291,\\
p_{perm}(human) &= 0.000999000999000999.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\mu_M$ denotes the reported mediation fraction, not a causal coefficient. In the bacterial panel, conditioning on mRNA half-life removes about $24.697202727892137\%$ of the joint $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance residual association. In the human panel, conditioning on the consensus mRNA-stability readout removes about $14.11299863319291\%$. The deterministic permutation audit uses a right-tail value of $0.000999000999000999$ in both panels, so the finite-row statistic is not being presented as a saturated fit. The rank-degeneracy threshold recorded for the run is $0.999999$, while the observed $R^2$ quantities are far below that boundary.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier mRNA-abundance projection.} The same coordinate family had a separate descriptive test in which residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA abundance was used as an $H$-candidate for the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual. That earlier calculation used four organisms and the same $24$-column control design with realized rank $23$: Danio rerio had $13140$ joined rows, Escherichia coli had $3415$, Homo sapiens had $11554$, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae had $5128$. The modeled complement values were $0.8424312412078521$, $0.6628211177352172$, $0.8566190269974241$, and $0.22386566043976477$, respectively, and the mRNA absorption fractions were $0.011973033879985792$, $0.0017706954554908886$, $0.007858222847072218$, and $0.0018976054721605224$. Those numbers are useful as a consistency background for transcript-layer covariates, but they do not by themselves establish the mRNA-stability mediation statistic reported in this chapter.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.} The supported statement is narrow: in two finite panels, the joint $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block retains a protein-abundance association after the standard controls, and a measurable portion of that association is partitioned through an mRNA half-life or stability readout. The bacterial row support gives the larger mediation fraction, while the human row support gives the smaller one. The sign difference in $b_{P\leftarrow M\mid X}$ between Escherichia coli and human is part of the finite readback and should not be smoothed into a universal direction.

\paragraph{Non-promotion rule.} No translation-efficiency survival, ribosome-occupancy claim, protein folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, biological-function claim, mechanistic pathway claim, phenotype claim, or universal law follows from these rows. The statistic is observational and cross-sectional with per-organism readout limitations: Escherichia coli uses one half-life dataset at the stated growth rate, and the human panel uses a consensus stability readout from the Agarwal data columns. Abundance and half-life measurements are not guaranteed to share platform, condition, or perturbation structure across organisms. A separate layer-matched contact would be required before promoting the result to translation dynamics, protein state, molecular function, or causal biology.
