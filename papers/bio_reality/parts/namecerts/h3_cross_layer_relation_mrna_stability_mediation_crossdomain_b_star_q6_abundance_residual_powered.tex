\subsection{Cross-domain RNA-stability mediation of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual}
\label{sec:biorReality-q6-mrna-stability-mediation-crossdomain}

\paragraph{Finite panel and coordinate boundary.}
This record tests a finite cross-layer panel in which each coding sequence row carries an organism label, a residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate vector, a protein-abundance readout, and an RNA-stability mediator.  The coordinate block has nine entries,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_{3}$-stress.  These coordinates are interpreted only after quotienting away the amino-acid sequence component and after residualizing against the declared control matrix $Z$: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $q$ vectors.  The design therefore separates a synonymous residual coordinate from the amino-acid composition and from a pure $M$-density explanation before any abundance or stability statistic is read.

\paragraph{Joined rows.}
Two organism-specific three-way joins were computed.  For \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, the Esquerre half-life table was joined to CDS and abundance rows by matching GeneId b-number to the CDS locus tag.  The half-life table contributed $2947$ valid gene records, the CDS-side report contained $3739$ joined CDS records, and the final three-way panel contained $2720$ rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9978649586335735$.  The readout condition was growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$.  For human, the Agarwal consensus half-life table was joined by Ensembl gene identifier without version, with fallback through unique gene symbol.  The final human panel contained $16165$ rows; $16150$ came from Ensembl identifiers and $15$ from the gene-symbol fallback.  The human consensus table supplied $19480$ valid records, the CDS-side report contained $19053$ joined CDS records, and the reported CDS join hit rate was $0.9779294769799313$.  The human readout was the unlogged mean normalized relative mRNA half-life consensus across $54$ dataset columns.

\paragraph{Rank diagnostics.}
The residualized design was nondegenerate in both computed panels.  For \emph{E. coli}, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, $\operatorname{rank}(X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(M)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(X,M)=10$, with $2697$ residual degrees of freedom after $Z$.  For human, the corresponding ranks were $23$, $9$, $1$, and $10$, with $16142$ residual degrees of freedom.  Thus the mediator is not merely reusing the full coordinate row space.  The recorded degeneracy cutoff was $0.999999$, while the observed $R^{2}$ values of the mediator on the coordinate block after controls were much smaller:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{M\mid X}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.12750047680284674,\\
R^{2}_{M\mid X}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.036877022762988716.
\end{aligned}
$$
The auxiliary joint check for the $Q\to M$ path also remained small,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{QM}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.02298556640056076,\\
R^{2}_{QM}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.010503670362051522.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite variance-partition calculation rather than a saturated row-space fit.

\paragraph{Protein residual decomposition.}
Let $X$ denote the residualized nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate block, $M$ the residualized mRNA half-life or stability readout, and $P$ the residualized protein-abundance readout.  The computed association of $X$ with $P$ was reduced after conditioning on $M$ in both organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{QP}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.11632311854858134, & R^{2}_{QP\mid M}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.0875945621412319,\\
R^{2}_{QP}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.04095215557813418, & R^{2}_{QP\mid M}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.03517257842112907.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding mediation fractions were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.24697202727892137,\\
\mu_{\mathrm{human}} &= 0.1411299863319291.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both right-tail permutation tests used deterministic residual-mediator permutations and both reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 0.000999000999000999.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw energy diagnostics agree with the same direction.  In \emph{E. coli}, $X$ explained $296.8250528743585$ units of energy on $P$, while $M$ added $325.3466399519295$ units given $X$ and $X$ added $223.51756781880516$ units given $M$.  In human, the corresponding energies were $910.5016829711035$, $819.8980203765552$, and $782.0025928981933$.  The fitted coefficient of $P$ on $M$ given $X$ was $-1.2432793356009886$ in \emph{E. coli} and $0.7633409850900272$ in human, so even the mediator direction is organism- and assay-dependent rather than a universal biological sign law.

\paragraph{Relation to the mRNA-abundance control surface.}
A separate abundance-control computation treated matched mRNA abundance as an $H$-candidate by projecting the modeled-tAI complement abundance residual onto residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA.  That four-organism calculation used the same nine coordinates and the same $24$ declared control columns, with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in every organism.  The joined row counts were $13140$ in zebrafish, $3415$ in \emph{E. coli}, $11554$ in human, and $5128$ in yeast.  The modeled complement distances on the all-readout join were
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.8424312412078521,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{E.coli}) &= 0.6628211177352172,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.8566190269974241,\\
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.22386566043976477.
\end{aligned}
$$
The additional absorption by mRNA abundance was small in that computation: $0.011973033879985792$ in zebrafish, $0.0017706954554908886$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.007858222847072218$ in human, and $0.0018976054721605224$ in yeast.  This comparison matters because it keeps transcript abundance, transcript stability, translation readout, and protein abundance in distinct roles.  The current half-life calculation concerns a statistical mediator $M$, not a direct translation-survival entry $S^{QT}$.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row evidence supports a bounded descriptive statement: after the stated controls, a joint nine-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual has a measurable protein-abundance association in \emph{E. coli} and human, and conditioning on a matched mRNA half-life or stability readout removes a positive fraction of that association.  The supported quantities are the row counts, ranks, residual energies, $R^{2}$ reductions, mediation fractions, and permutation $p$-values listed above.  They do not establish a causal route from synonymous coordinate to RNA decay to protein abundance.  They also do not supply a measured translation-readout survival matrix $S^{QT}$.  Consequently no translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and its own controlled statistic.  Protein abundance here remains a downstream residual readout, and RNA stability remains a statistical mediator in an observational finite panel.
