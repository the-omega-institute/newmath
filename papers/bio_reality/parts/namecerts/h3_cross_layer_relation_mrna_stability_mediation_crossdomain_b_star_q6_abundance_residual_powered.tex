\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record treats the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates as a finite nine-coordinate panel tested against protein-abundance residuals and mRNA half-life or stability readouts. The rows are coding-sequence records joined by organism-specific identifiers, with one residualized protein-abundance readout $P$, one residualized mRNA stability readout $M$, and the nine-dimensional residual coordinate block $X$. The coordinate block is
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.

The control rank is $23$ in both organisms, so the downstream quantities are read as residual finite-row statistics rather than as raw codon-composition associations.

The corresponding variance-partition fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\hbox{E. coli}} &= 0.24697202727892137,\\

These values support a descriptive statement: in each joined organism, a nonzero portion of the finite $B^{*}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance residual association is accounted for by the residualized mRNA stability readout. The result is cross-domain in the narrow sense that the same nine-coordinate residual block is evaluated in a bacterial half-life panel and in a human consensus half-life panel.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter-level claim is therefore a controlled finite-row mediation readback, not a causal pathway. The observed statistic says that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual block retains a protein-abundance residual association in both organisms and that conditioning on mRNA half-life or stability removes a finite fraction of that association: about $24.6972\%$ in \emph{E. coli} and about $14.1130\%$ in human. The two panels differ in source, condition, organism, identifier system, and biological domain, so the comparison is descriptive rather than phylogenetically controlled. The statistic also does not establish that codon choice causes mRNA stability change, that mRNA stability causes protein abundance change, or that the same biochemical process is responsible in both organisms.

\paragraph{Promotion boundary.}
This finite-row result may be used only as a bounded cross-layer abundance residual record. It does not by itself supply a translation-readout survival matrix $S^{QT}$, and it does not turn a modeled tAI or stAI coupling entry into observed translation survival. It also does not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, protein-complex participation, localization, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality. Any such statement requires a separate layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic. In particular, a downstream protein-omics interpretation remains ordered: a translation-readout survival entry must first be estimated under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and mRNA controls before a protein-abundance mediation interpretation is promoted beyond this variance-partition record.
