The deterministic permutation seeds are organism-specific and keyed by \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_mrna\_stability\_mediation\_crossdomain\_powered}, the organism label, the residual-mediator permutation mode, permutation index, row index, and row count. The module classification is therefore a cross-layer statistical mediation decomposition: it is not a translation-survival matrix, not a protein-mechanism proof, and not a causal perturbation result.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The E. coli panel gives the stronger finite-row mediation fraction, about $24.6972\%$ of the controlled $B^{*}_{Q6}$ to protein-abundance residual association, while the human panel gives about $14.1130\%$. Both right-tail permutation values equal $0.000999000999000999$ under the recorded deterministic permutation design. The comparison is descriptive across domains: one organism uses the Esquerre half-life table at a specified growth rate, and the other uses the Agarwal consensus half-life readout. The row counts, identifiers, platforms, and conditions are not identical, so the two values should be read as two organism-scoped finite panels rather than as a phylogenetically controlled law.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
This chapter supports only the bounded statement that, on the two joined finite panels, conditioning on residualized mRNA half-life or stability reduces the controlled association between the nine-dimensional $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate block and protein abundance by the reported fractions. It does not establish that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ controls translation, because no measured $S^{QT}$ translation-readout survival matrix is computed here. It does not establish folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality. Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact, row join, controls, rank audit, and statistic. The present result is a variance-partition record for $X \to P$ after adding $M$, not evidence that codon geometry itself is a biological mechanism.
