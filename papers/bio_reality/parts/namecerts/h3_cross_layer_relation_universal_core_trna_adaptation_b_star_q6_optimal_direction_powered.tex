\subsection{Universal Core tRNA Adaptation Along the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ Direction}
\label{sec:bioreality-universal-core-trna-adaptation-b-star-q6-optimal-direction-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite coding-sequence residual surface indexed by organism and by synonymous codon coordinates after amino-acid projection.  The coordinate of interest is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ optimal direction, with special attention to the $f_{3}$-stress component: a third-position-paired synonymous imbalance that can become biologically testable only when the corresponding codon pair is not symmetric under the organismal tRNA or wobble-weight surface.  The finite codon support used by the upstream saturation calculation is explicit.  The set $M=\mathrm{Med}_{R}\cup\mathrm{ACA}$ has $20$ codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_{3}$ saturation of $R$ removes $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ and leaves $18$ codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_{3}}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_{3}$ boundary is empty.  Thus the combinatorial coordinate is not being introduced from an unbounded codon universe; it is a finite residual surface with a named $20$-row parent list, an $18$-row saturated list, and no residual boundary row in this saturation run.

\paragraph{Powered finite computation.}
The central powered run, \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_universal\_core\_is\_trna\_adaptation\_powered}, passed on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  Its deterministic seed was \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_universal\_core\_is\_trna\_adaptation\_powered:deterministic}.  This supports the local finite statement that the leading $B^{*}_{Q6}$ direction in the available powered panel is compatible with a core tRNA-adaptation axis.  The statement is deliberately narrower than a biological law: it says that the controlled residual coordinate behaves like a tRNA-adaptation direction in this finite panel, not that every organism, every codon family, or every translation readout must follow the same direction.

\paragraph{Supporting spectrum.}
The companion axis-spectrum run also used $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  Its recorded summary says that $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ leads the captured spectra on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and that all $11$ per-organism dominant counts were assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$.  The domain breakdown is descriptive only because the panel is imbalanced, with $n=1$ archaeal organism, $n=2$ bacterial organisms, and $n=8$ eukaryotic organisms.  The largest GC3 rank effect recorded in that run was small, $\rho=0.136$, for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$.  These finite rows make the tRNA-adaptation reading more specific: the core direction is not presented as a pure GC3 artifact, but the available panel is also not balanced enough to promote the domain means into a cross-domain theorem.

\paragraph{Boundary-set mechanism row.}
The boundary-set tRNA-poverty run gives an independent finite mechanism-facing check.  It passed on $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, with $6$ skipped organisms, $1000$ permutations, and $\alpha=0.05$.  Its mechanism call states that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy numbers combined with dos Reis wobble weights, supporting a tRNA-supply explanation for $R$ avoidance.  This row is useful because it names the finite direction of the supply pressure, but it remains a supply-side contact.  By itself it does not measure ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, or any downstream phenotype.

\paragraph{Residual and closure checks.}
The non-tRNA residual optimality run passed on $11$ of $16$ requested organisms and identified $\mathrm{f3\_translation\_selection}$ as the dominant non-tRNA residual axis.  The two-axis closure run, with the same $11$ of $16$ organism count, reported \texttt{third\_axis\_present}, with systematically positive held-out $R^{2}$ for $d_{\perp}$ and same-signed $d_{\perp}$ directions across organisms.  The third-axis identity run assigned $d_{\perp}$ provisionally to usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  The third-axis mechanism run retained significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^{2}$ after GC orthogonalization, and summarized the outcome as selection-not-GC, not wobble-clustered, with the CSC or positional identity still needing additional contact.  The three-axis sufficiency run reported \texttt{fourth\_mechanism\_present}.  The fourth-mechanism probes then separated several candidate explanations: mRNA stability was not the dominant fourth-axis identity in the yeast half-life assay, codon-pair signal was rejected as \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}, translation accuracy was rejected as \texttt{not\_accuracy}, and elongation dwell was only \texttt{partial} in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The result is a finite, layered picture rather than a single-mechanism closure: tRNA adaptation is supported for the core direction, an $f_{3}$-selection residual appears, a third axis is present, and the fourth mechanism remains only partially localized.

\paragraph{Translation-readout condition.}
The $f_{3}$-stress coordinate becomes a translation-layer test only under a broken-pair condition.  For a synonymous pair $c,c^{f_{3}}$, the finite row must include a context in which the tRNA or wobble weights differ, $w_{c}\ne w_{c^{f_{3}}}$, and the paired coding rows must be joined to a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The bounded survival quantity is therefore not the raw codon imbalance.  It is the controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_{j}}$ after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, mRNA abundance where relevant, rank, and simpler-baseline controls.  A positive statement requires a non-vacuous, non-rank-degenerate entry in that controlled surface.  An all-$1.0$ three-organism matrix is treated here as a rank-saturated boundary, not as survival evidence.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
The present row supports a finite tRNA-adaptation reading for the powered $B^{*}_{Q6}$ optimal direction and supplies a controlled design target for the $f_{3}$-stress translation readout.  It does not, without another matched measurement layer, establish measured translation efficiency, ribosome occupancy, mature protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, cellular localization, system phenotype, causal mechanism, or universality.  Codon-usage rows, tRNA gene-copy rows, and modeled tAI or stAI weights may bound a translation-adaptation surface, but they do not replace a same-organism and same-CDS measured translation matrix.  Any downstream claim must bring its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic before it can be read as biological realization beyond this finite coordinate record.
