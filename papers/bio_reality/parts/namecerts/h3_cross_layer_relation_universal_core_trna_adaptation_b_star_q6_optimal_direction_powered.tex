\subsection{Rank-powered tRNA adaptation along the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-universal-core-trna-adaptation}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate under test.}
The finite biological record considered here is the coding-sequence residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate whose visible stress is the third-position $f_3$ pairing.  The coordinate is not a protein-level variable.  It is a finite synonymous-codon residual coordinate that can be compared with organism-scoped codon usage, tRNA copy or wobble-weight summaries, and bounded translation-readout rows when such rows are actually joined to the same organisms or coding sequences.  The $f_3$ saturation audit supplies the finite codon boundary used by the chapter.  The marked set $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ has $20$ codons,
$$\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The $f_3$-saturated $R$ surface removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, leaving $18$ codons,
$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}$$
and the recorded $f_3$ boundary set is empty.  Thus the chapter starts from a closed finite codon surface rather than from an open-ended sequence pattern.

\paragraph{Observed rank-powered core.}
The powered tRNA-adaptation assay for the universal core was run with $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous-design columns, and $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms.  Its deterministic seed was recorded as \texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_universal\_core\_is\_trna\_adaptation\_powered:deterministic}, and the assay status was \texttt{passed}.  The finite interpretation is narrow: within the computed rank-powered panel, the optimal $B^*_{Q6}$ direction is supported as a tRNA-adaptation direction on the available synonymous residual design.  This is a cross-layer relation between a finite codon residual surface and tRNA or wobble-weight readbacks; it is not, by itself, an assertion about mature protein abundance, folded structure, organismal phenotype, or a universal law of translation.

\paragraph{Boundary support from tRNA poverty.}
The separate $R$-sense tRNA-poverty assay gives the mechanistic pressure behind the direction without promoting it beyond the measured layer.  That assay computed $40$ organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipped $6$, used $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, and reported that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy numbers combined with dos Reis wobble weights.  The status was \texttt{passed}.  This supplies a finite support relation: the residual direction selected by the $B^*_{Q6}$ surface is compatible with an asymmetric tRNA-supply pressure.  It does not say that every organism realizes the same direction with the same strength, and it does not replace measured ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency rows.

\paragraph{Axis geometry around the core.}
The surrounding powered assays show that the tRNA direction is not the whole residual geometry.  In the axis-strength spectrum, $11$ organisms were computed out of $16$ requested organisms, again with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The captured spectra were led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ and then by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; all $11$ per-organism dominant calls were assigned to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$.  The domain split in that run was imbalanced, with $n=1$ archaea, $n=2$ bacteria, and $n=8$ eukaryotes, so domain means are only descriptive.  The largest recorded GC3 rank effect was the $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ effect with $\rho=0.136$.  A paired residual-axis assay identified the non-tRNA residual optimality direction most strongly with $f_3$ translation selection, while the two-axis closure assay still reported a third axis: $d_\perp$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms.  The identity assay for $d_\perp$ aligned it with usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$.  Its mechanism audit then classified the signal as selection-not-GC, not wobble-clustered, with the CSC or positional identity still needing an additional external contact.

\paragraph{Fourth-axis boundary.}
The finite row record also prevents overcompression of the result into a single tRNA story.  The three-axis sufficiency assay concluded that a fourth mechanism is present.  Follow-up identity tests classified the codon-pair explanation as \texttt{not\_codon\_pair}, the translation-accuracy explanation as \texttt{not\_accuracy}, and the mRNA-stability explanation as \texttt{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}; the yeast elongation-dwell assay was only \texttt{partial}.  These rows keep the tRNA-adaptation core from being read as an exhaustive account of all residual directions.  The supported claim is that a rank-powered $B^*_{Q6}$ optimal direction has a tRNA-adaptation core on the computed panel, not that the remaining residual axes have been reduced to tRNA copy, wobble weights, codon pairs, translation accuracy, mRNA stability, or elongation dwell alone.

\paragraph{Translation-readout gate.}
For the $f_3$-stress interpretation, the decisive readback would be a joined row of the form $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ for a bounded translation readout $T_j$, such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where applicable, rank, and simpler codon baselines.  The present row set supports the tRNA-adaptation direction and the wobble-breaking motivation, but it does not yet contain a same-organism and same-CDS measured ribosome or translation-efficiency matrix for the exact $f_3$-stress packet.  The three-organism all-$1.0$ rank-degenerate surface is therefore a null boundary rather than survival evidence.  A positive translation claim would require a nondegenerate finite statistic with $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}>0$ under the declared controls; a non-positive or rank-saturated matrix would falsify the translation-readout survival claim without erasing the finite codon-coordinate construction.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter therefore has a bounded conclusion.  The finite evidence supports a rank-powered cross-layer relation from the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface to a tRNA-adaptation direction, with additional finite pressure from $R$-sense tRNA poverty and with explicit residual axes left outside the core.  No claim about observed ribosome dynamics follows unless a measured or explicitly bounded translation contact is joined to the same finite rows and survives the stated controls.  No claim about protein abundance follows unless a separate protein-level contact is evaluated after the translation step.  No claim about folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism in the stronger causal sense, or universality follows from the codon geometry, the tRNA-weight surface, the modeled adaptation weights, or the passed rank-powered assays alone.  Each higher layer requires its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
