\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ optimal direction as a bounded translation-adaptation record}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-trna-adaptation}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This chapter records a finite cross-layer claim about the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate after amino-acid projection. The object under test is not an unrestricted codon-usage law. It is the finite CDS-by-organism residual surface obtained after amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and simpler codon baselines have been kept in the conditioning set. Within that surface, the specific row of interest is the $f_3$-stress direction: a paired synonymous imbalance that can matter only when the paired codons are not equivalent under context-specific tRNA supply or wobble weights. The relevant readout layer is translation-facing: tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. No protein abundance, folded structure, localization, stability, biological function, phenotype, physical admissibility, mechanism, or universal biological law is inferred from this row unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the translation row is first non-vacuous under the stated controls.

\paragraph{Saturation row.}
The finite saturation data fix the codon domain on which the $f_3$ residual question is allowed to be asked. The recorded $M$ row has twenty codons, while the $f_3$-saturated $R$ row removes exactly the two ACA/ACG boundary terms. The verified row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},\\
\partial_{f_3}(R) &= \varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the residual test is not driven by an unresolved $f_3$ boundary term: the boundary set is empty in the recorded saturation row. The two omitted codons, ACA and ACG, remain visible as the finite difference between the twenty-codon $M$ row and the eighteen-codon $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ row, rather than being silently absorbed into the residual coordinate.

\paragraph{Powered adaptation row.}
The central powered row for this chapter is the claim that the universal core optimal direction is a tRNA-adaptation direction. Its run is recorded as experiment run 373843e81379, with status passed, five folds, a minimum of 500 proteins per organism, 11 organisms computed out of 16 requested, and 41 synonymous-design columns. The deterministic seed is recorded for the powered assay, and the finite row therefore supports a scoped statement: inside the computed panel, the optimal $B^*_{Q6}$ direction survives the declared design as a tRNA-adaptation-aligned residual direction. The row does not by itself say that all organisms obey a universal dynamical rule, and it does not replace measured ribosome or translation-efficiency data.

\paragraph{Residual axis context.}
The neighboring powered rows constrain how this optimal direction should be read. The non-tRNA residual optimality row reports that the dominant non-tRNA axis is $f_3$ translation selection, again on 11 computed organisms out of 16 requested, using five folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous-design columns. The two-axis closure row reports a third-axis-present outcome: $d_\perp$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and mutually same-signed directions across organisms. The third-axis identity row identifies $d_\perp$ provisionally with usage frequency within synonymous family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The mechanism row keeps that third axis GC-orthogonal: the recorded conclusion is selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, with CSC or positional identity still requiring a further contact. These rows make the chapter's reading narrower rather than broader: $B^*_{Q6}$ has a finite translation-adaptation signature, but the residual geometry still needs a matched translation readout before it becomes an observed translation-survival statistic.

\paragraph{Spectrum and insufficiency.}
The axis-strength spectrum row gives an additional finite check on scale. The captured spectra are led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; the per-organism dominant count is $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$. Domain means are descriptive only because the panel is imbalanced, with $n=1$ archaeon, $n=2$ bacteria, and $n=8$ eukaryotes. The largest GC3 rank effect is $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$. The three-axis sufficiency row reports fourth-mechanism-present, so the tRNA and $f_3$ components should not be treated as a closed explanation of every residual direction. They form a bounded core for the tested optimal direction, not a complete biological account.

\paragraph{Boundary-set mechanism.}
The separate boundary-set tRNA-poverty assay gives a mechanistic pressure at the codon-supply layer. It was computed for 40 organisms out of 46 requested, with 6 skipped, permutation count 1000, and $\alpha=0.05$. The recorded mechanism call is that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN together with dos Reis wobble weights, supporting a tRNA-supply explanation for $R$ avoidance. This is relevant to the $B^*_{Q6}$ optimal direction because it supplies a finite tRNA-supply asymmetry against which the residual direction can be compared. It still remains a supply-side and modeled-weight contact: it is not an observed ribosome-footprint row, not a protein-output measurement, and not a functional or structural assay.

\paragraph{Translation-readout condition.}
For the $f_3$-stress row, the admissible positive readback is a controlled survival entry $S_{f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ for a translation-facing readout $T_j$. The intended test is finite and falsifiable: after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA where applicable, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline controls, at least one nondegenerate entry must satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j} &> 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
If every such entry is non-positive, or if the design collapses to a rank-degenerate all-1.0 surface on a three-organism panel, then the translation-readout claim fails for this packet. Modeled tAI or stAI weights may be used as bounded codon-adaptation readbacks, but they are not the same thing as observed ribosome occupancy. A positive modeled-weight association is therefore not promoted to measured translation survival unless the relevant row has the matched readout and controls.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter's positive content is the finite alignment of the $B^*_{Q6}$ optimal direction with a tRNA-adaptation axis, together with the $f_3$ residual pressure that becomes biologically interpretable only where wobble or tRNA weights break the paired-codon symmetry. The chapter's negative content is equally important. Codon topology, tRNA-copy summaries, tAI or stAI weights, and residual-coordinate geometry do not by themselves establish translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, folded structure, localization, stability, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, causation, or a universal law. Those stronger claims require their own finite contacts and their own controlled statistics. The present row is therefore ready as a scoped translation-adaptation record, while the measured $S_{f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ translation-readout row remains the required next boundary for any stronger biological interpretation.
