\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-by-organism residual coordinate surface after amino-acid projection.  The coordinate under inspection is the $B^*_{Q6}$ third-position $f_3$-stress direction, paired only with translation-layer readouts such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. $$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}

The verified $f_3$-saturated $R$ set removes $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from that list:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R) = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\

\paragraph{Rank-powered core observation.}
The powered core experiment for $B^*_{Q6}$ tRNA adaptation passed with run id $373843\mathrm{e}81379$.  It used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous design columns.  Its deterministic seed was $\mathrm{sha256{:}b\_star\_q6\_universal\_core\_is\_trna\_adaptation\_powered{:}deterministic}$.  This supports a finite, rank-powered claim that the universal core direction is organized by tRNA-adaptation structure within the assayed residual coordinate surface.  The support is deliberately narrower than a biological mechanism statement: it is a controlled coordinate-readout statement, not a claim about mature protein abundance, folding, localization, stability, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Two-axis and residual-axis checks.}
The companion residual-axis run computed $11$ of $16$ requested organisms with $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns.  Its conclusion was that the non-tRNA residual optimality direction aligns most with $f_3$ translation selection, with dominant axis $\mathrm{f3\_translation\_selection}$.  The two-axis closure run used the same organism count, fold count, protein threshold, and synonymous design dimension, and concluded that a third axis is present: $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$, and the $d_{\perp}$ directions are mutually same-signed across organisms.  These facts place the $f_3$-stress route in a finite cross-layer geometry: the first tRNA-adaptation direction is not sufficient to exhaust the observed residual organization, and a further same-signed direction remains after the two-axis span is tested.

\paragraph{Identity and mechanism pressure on the third direction.}
The third-axis identity run computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and the $500$-protein inclusion threshold.  It identified $d_{\perp}$ provisionally with usage frequency within codon family, reporting mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p = 0.000488$.  The third-axis mechanism run kept significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$ after GC orthogonalization, with the finite conclusion
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{third-axis mechanism} = \text{selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs external contact}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a useful constraint on interpretation.  The observed residual direction cannot be reduced to a GC-only explanation in the tested panel, but the positional or codon-context identity is not closed by these finite rows alone.

\paragraph{Per-organism axis spectrum.}
The axis-strength spectrum run computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms using $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and the same $500$-protein minimum.  The captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, and the per-organism dominant count was $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$.  Domain means were explicitly descriptive under the imbalanced panel size $\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$.  The largest GC3 rank effect was reported for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho = 0.136$.  These numbers make the record stronger as a finite panel statement and weaker as a universal biological assertion: the same rows show a coherent axis hierarchy, but the taxonomic imbalance and finite organism count block any unrestricted domain-wide conclusion.

\paragraph{Boundary-set tRNA poverty.}
The boundary-set tRNA-poverty experiment passed on $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, skipping $6$, with $1000$ permutations and significance level $\alpha = 0.05$.  Its finite mechanism call was that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights, supporting a tRNA-supply explanation for $R$ avoidance.  This supplies an organism-level supply-side contact for the codon set, but it still does not by itself produce a same-CDS ribosome-occupancy row, a translation-efficiency row, or a protein-output row.

\paragraph{Fourth-axis exclusions and partial contact.}
The three-axis sufficiency run computed $11$ out of $16$ requested organisms with $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and the $500$-protein minimum, and concluded that a fourth mechanism is present.  Follow-up finite mechanism tests constrain what that fourth mechanism is not.  The mRNA-stability run concluded $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  The codon-pair run concluded $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ with $50$ shuffle-null samples.  The translation-accuracy run concluded $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$ after computing $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms with $200$ permutations.  The elongation-dwell run, restricted to $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$, returned $\mathrm{partial}$.  The fourth direction is therefore not freely interpretable: several candidate mechanisms are excluded or weakened, while elongation dwell remains only partially aligned in the named yeast row.

\paragraph{Translation-readout rule for the $f_3$-stress coordinate.}
The local support condition for this chapter is not that codon topology alone causes translation.  The admissible readout is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$, where $T_j$ is tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  The coordinate may count as translation-layer evidence only when the fitted row remains non-vacuous after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, matched mRNA abundance where relevant, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon baselines.  In schematic form, the finite survival question is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j} > 0
\quad\text{after controlling for}\quad
Z = (\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{organism},\mathrm{GC3},\mathrm{length},M\text{-}\mathrm{density},\mathrm{mRNA},\mathrm{rank},\mathrm{baseline}).
\end{aligned}
$$
If the panel is rank-degenerate, or if all such controlled entries are non-positive, the $f_3$-stress coordinate remains a bounded residual coordinate without positive translation-readout survival.  In particular, a three-organism all-$1.0$ fitted surface is treated as a saturation boundary rather than as evidence for survival.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion boundary.}
The finite rows above justify a controlled codon-coordinate and tRNA-adaptation record for the $B^*_{Q6}$ panel.  They do not license promotion to translation realization unless a same-organism or same-CDS translation-readout statistic is attached and positive under the declared controls.  They also do not license claims about ribosome dynamics from modeled tAI or stAI weights alone; do not license protein abundance from translation scores alone; and do not license folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism, causation, or universal biological law without separate layer-matched measurements.  The chapter therefore records a finite, rank-powered $B^*_{Q6}$ tRNA-adaptation and $f_3$-stress boundary: the coordinate is mathematically and empirically organized enough to be tested, but every downstream biological interpretation remains conditional on an explicit contact at that layer.
