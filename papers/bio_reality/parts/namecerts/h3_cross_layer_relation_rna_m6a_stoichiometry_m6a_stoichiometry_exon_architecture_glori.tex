\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the decay-factor RBP bridge}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel in which coding-transcript rows carry a synonymous residual-coordinate label, an amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a proposed decay-factor RBP bridge.  The coordinate is not itself a biological measurement.  It is a finite label used to compare high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows after amino-acid sequence has been held fixed.  A row can therefore enter the bridge test only when it has all of the following fields on the same organism and the same transcript or coding interval: the residual label $q$, the translation readout $T$, the RNA half-life readout $H$, matched abundance and composition controls $Z$, local RBP occupancy $O$, and perturbation/rescue indicators $P$.

The finite GLORI-derived m6A stoichiometry surface supplies the largest concrete row count presently attached to this subject: the run reports $n=60000$ assayed rows and $49668$ distinct sites, with cross-condition concordance recorded as true and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$.  These values are useful because they show that the broader RNA-modification layer has a nontrivial finite stoichiometry table rather than only a symbolic claim.  They do not, by themselves, provide the RNA half-life, same-row translation, or perturbation fields required for the RBP bridge.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{GLORI}} &= 60000,\\
 d_{\mathrm{GLORI}} &= 49668,\\
 r_{\mathrm{coverage}} &= -0.135922814,\\
 C_{\mathrm{condition}} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Bridge statistic.}
For the bridge to be counted as a positive cross-layer relation, the finite panel must estimate a translation-conditioned RNA half-life contrast while conditioning on transcription rate, transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline codon models.  A schematic row-level statistic has the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H
 &= \widehat{H}(q=\mathrm{high}\mid T,Z,O,P)
    - \widehat{H}(q=\mathrm{low}\mid T,Z,O,P),\\
 \Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}}
 &= \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{target\ loss}}(q=\mathrm{high}\mid T,Z,O,P)
    - \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{target\ loss}}(q=\mathrm{low}\mid T,Z,O,P),\\
 \Delta_H^{\mathrm{rescue}}
 &= \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}(q=\mathrm{high}\mid T,Z,O,P)
    - \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}}(q=\mathrm{low}\mid T,Z,O,P).
\end{aligned}
$$

The positive pattern is narrow.  The half-life contrast must follow measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, collapse under loss of that target RBP, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.  A half-life difference without this occupancy-loss-rescue shape remains an RNA-stability association.  An occupancy difference without decoupling from coordinate, composition, structure, isoform state, abundance, and batch remains an RBP-association surface.  Neither is enough to claim an RBP-mediated decay bridge.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The translation readout is mandatory because the claim is conditioned on same-row translation rather than on transcript abundance alone.  The attached mTOR translation run gives a concrete translation-layer scale, with $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds for the TopScore mTOR translation surface.  That row count is not joined here to the GLORI m6A rows or to an RNA half-life panel, so it functions only as evidence that a finite translation readout route exists elsewhere in the BioReality memory.  It does not authorize a translation realization for this RNA half-life claim.

The RBP binding surface supplies a separate finite RBP contact: $900$ total peaks were parsed in the RBNS/eCLIP bridge run, with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases recorded.  Those numbers show that RBP occupancy and binding contacts are tracked as finite tables, but they are not the same as measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the target RNA window in this claim.  The bridge therefore remains conditional until the RBP rows are joined to the same transcript windows as $q$, $T$, and $H$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{translation}} &= 6494,\\
 n_{\mathrm{RBP\ peaks}} &= 900,\\
 c_{\mathrm{cross\ RBP}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the required controls and the same-row translation readout are attached.  Equivalently, after conditioning on $T$ and $Z$, the coordinate label should add no reproducible half-life information unless the measured decay-factor RBP bridge is also present.  If a half-life contrast appears but measured local RBP occupancy is unchanged, the target-RBP loss does not erase the contrast, wild-type rescue does not restore it, or binding-defective and non-target rescue arms behave like wild type, the result stays outside the bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a bounded finite-row test for a translation-conditioned RNA half-life relation with an explicitly measured RBP bridge.  It does not infer protein output, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence law.  It also does not treat codon-window geometry, synonymous residual labels, m6A stoichiometry rows, ribosome-profiling routes, mRNA abundance controls, or RBP contact names as substitutes for the missing same-window occupancy and perturbation/rescue panel.  Each higher biological layer would require its own matched contact and its own declared statistic.

\paragraph{Readback status.}
The present finite data support a precise negative discipline: the m6A GLORI surface is large and condition-concordant, the translation and RBP routes have finite row counts, and the proposed bridge has an exact finite-row shape.  The positive bridge is not established until a single joined panel contains residual-coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Until that joined panel is present, the record is a bounded BioReality target with a falsifiable bridge condition, not a completed mechanism claim.
