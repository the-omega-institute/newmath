\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP boundary for Q6 residual coordinates}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA panel whose intended rows have transcript or coding-sequence identifiers, a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and an externally fixed decay-factor RNA-binding-protein readout at the editable RNA window.  The coordinate part is only a row label: a high-coordinate and low-coordinate contrast may be computed after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented away, but the label itself is not a biological assay.  The biological question is narrower: whether a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual can be read as a bounded cross-layer decay-factor RBP bridge after translation conditioning and after an independent occupancy, perturbation, and rescue panel has been attached.

The finite reality rows currently available for the associated methylation surface are GLORI-derived m6A stoichiometry rows with $n=60000$ assayed rows and $49668$ distinct sites.  The run identifier is $2e8583415e99$, and the recorded cross-condition flag is positive.  The measured coverage-confound scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r_{\mathrm{coverage}} &= -0.135922814,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is small enough to be reported as a finite nuisance diagnostic, not as a mechanism.  The paired comparison ledger also contains a CROWN-seq m6Am surface with $n=31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct sites, cross-cell concordance recorded as true, a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$, and a variable-subset count of $20942$.  A separate RBP-binding reality surface has $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  These additional finite surfaces are cited here only to calibrate the kind of row-level evidence required for a cross-layer claim: they do not supply same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or rescue rows for the present Q6 half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Controlled null.}
Let $Q_i$ denote the residual synonymous coordinate label of row $i$, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ denote measured occupancy of the externally prespecified decay-factor RBP at the relevant RNA window.  Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and amino-acid composition.  The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels for half-life once the same-row translation readout and the controls are fixed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i \perp Q_i \mid T_i, Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
A finite association between $Q_i$ and $H_i$ is therefore only an RNA-stability association unless the RBP bridge also appears.  In particular, a half-life contrast without a measured local occupancy contrast, without collapse after target-RBP loss, or without restoration by wild-type rescue remains below the mechanism boundary.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
The bridge predicate is conjunctive rather than cumulative.  A positive row-level bridge would require all of the following finite readbacks in the same organism and at the same RNA window: a translation-conditioned half-life residual, a measured local occupancy change for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, experimental decoupling showing that the coordinate label is not merely a proxy for occupancy or motif density, collapse of the half-life effect after loss of the target RBP, return of the effect under wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Formally, the permitted positive interpretation has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
  &\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
  &\Delta O(Q \mid T,Z) \neq 0,\\
  &\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{target\ loss}) \approx 0,\\
  &\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \approx \Delta H(Q \mid T,Z),\\
  &\Delta H(Q \mid T,Z,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}) \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The present finite packet does not contain those same-window RBP perturbation and rescue rows.  The GLORI m6A stoichiometry matrix supplies a large finite methylation surface with cross-condition concordance and a coverage diagnostic; it does not, by itself, establish RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP dependence, or rescue behavior.

\paragraph{Readback status.}
The verified m6A surface is therefore usable as a bounded finite-row contact for RNA modification stoichiometry and exon-architecture-style site accounting.  Its row count, distinct-site count, cross-condition flag, and coverage-confound scalar are concrete enough to support a chapter-level reality record.  They are not enough to promote the record to a translation-conditioned RNA half-life mechanism.  The m6Am comparison surface and the RBP-binding comparison surface show that finite stoichiometry and finite binding readouts can be recorded in this framework, but they remain separate assays unless joined by same-row identifiers and by the declared statistic.

Thus the local verdict is a bounded descriptor with an explicit route to a stronger claim.  A future positive bridge would need a joined matrix containing $Q_i$, $T_i$, matched mRNA abundance, $H_i$, $O_i$, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Until that matrix exists, no translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate geometry, the methylation rows, the half-life contact name, or the translation-readout boundary.
