\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the m6A exon-architecture panel}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-m6a-glori}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row claim about coding-transcript windows carrying a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and a separately measured decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy field. The motivating verified surface is the GLORI-derived m6A stoichiometry and exon-architecture panel. In that panel the parsed finite table contains $60000$ rows and $49668$ distinct sites. The run reports cross-condition concordance as true, and the measured coverage-confounding correlation is $-0.135922814$. These numbers are not interpreted as a translation or decay result by themselves; they only establish that a large finite RNA-modification table with a nontrivial site inventory and a recorded coverage diagnostic is available for bounded comparison.

\paragraph{Coordinate split.}
Each row may be read as carrying a high- or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after quotienting away amino-acid identity. Let $q_i$ denote the residual-coordinate class, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout when present, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ denote the local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window. The intended statistic is not the unconditioned association $q_i \to H_i$. The admissible object is the finite comparison
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i,T_i,H_i,O_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^{N},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and the same-row translation boundary. A half-life difference after these controls remains only an RNA-stability association unless the RBP occupancy and perturbation fields are also present.

\paragraph{Available reality contacts.}
The attached half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with curated mRNA half-life estimates or normalized consensus half-life readouts. The matched mRNA contact supplies the transcription-layer denominator needed to avoid mistaking abundance for translation efficiency. The ribosome-profiling contact supplies a possible route to $T_i$, but only when ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency is joined to the same organism and same CDS or transcript rows. None of these contacts is an RBP occupancy assay. None is a target-RBP loss experiment. None is a wild-type rescue or binding-defective rescue panel.

\paragraph{RBP bridge requirement.}
The finite bridge therefore has a stricter form than a site-level methylation or half-life association. A positive bridge requires the half-life residual to follow experimentally decoupled local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, to collapse under target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and to fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue. The RBP-binding reference surface currently available in the research memory is an RBNS/eCLIP-style binding panel with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases. That contact can support the existence of finite RBP-binding readout surfaces, but it does not supply the same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue rows required for this particular RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Comparison with a nearby RNA-modification surface.}
The m6Am flanking-identity stoichiometry surface gives a useful scale check for finite RNA-modification claims without closing the present bridge. Its verified run contains $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, cross-cell concordance recorded as true, a saturated $\geq 0.95$ fraction of $0.343223$, a variable-subset size of $20942$, and a low-tail count below $0.8$ of $3230$. The embedded finite statistics include a GC-matched null $p$-value of $0.000833$, partial correlations $0.268094$ for the full set, $0.098666$ for the low-tail subset, and $0.168335$ for the variable subset. These values show that RNA-modification stoichiometry can be represented as a bounded finite-row statistical surface, but they still do not provide translation-conditioned RNA half-life mediation or an RBP perturbation/rescue bridge for the GLORI m6A exon-architecture panel.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on the declared controls. In symbols, the operational null is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, Z_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the additional rule that a residual half-life difference is not promoted to an RBP-decay bridge unless $O_i$ and the target-RBP perturbation and rescue fields satisfy the prespecified pattern. A site table may be large, concordant across conditions, and low in coverage confounding, while still failing this bridge if the occupancy and intervention rows are absent.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The finite readback allowed by the present data is a bounded RNA-modification and exon-architecture surface with an explicit open boundary toward translation-conditioned RNA half-life. The GLORI panel contributes $N=60000$, $49668$ distinct sites, true cross-condition concordance, and coverage-confounding correlation $r=-0.135922814$. The RBNS/eCLIP binding surface contributes only a separate RBP-binding scale reference, not a joined decay-factor occupancy experiment. The m6Am surface contributes a nearby finite RNA-modification comparison with concrete row counts and null diagnostics, not a substitute for the missing bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No measured half-life difference may be interpreted as a decay-factor RBP result unless same-window RBP occupancy changes with the coordinate, target-RBP loss collapses the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails. RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform state, transcript abundance, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, coverage, and batch cannot be treated as positive support for the RBP bridge unless they are included as controls and the bridge pattern remains. The chapter also makes no claim about protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law. Those layers require their own matched contacts and finite statistics.
