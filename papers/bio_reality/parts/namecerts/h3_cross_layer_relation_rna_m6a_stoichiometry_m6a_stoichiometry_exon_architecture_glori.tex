\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the m6A stoichiometry boundary}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-glori}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched transcript panel, not a universal statement about coding sequence.  A row carries a coding-transcript identifier, a synonymous residual coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid identity, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, matched abundance and transcription controls, and a possible local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein.  The intended positive bridge is therefore narrow: a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate contrast may only be read as an RNA half-life relation after conditioning on the translation readout and the ordinary sequence and expression controls; it may only be read as an RBP-mediated decay relation after the same local window also supplies measured occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and a binding-defective or non-target control.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The GLORI m6A stoichiometry run supplies the largest concrete RNA-modification panel in this packet.  Its verified table contains $n=60000$ finite rows and $49668$ distinct mapped sites, with cross-condition concordance recorded as true.  The measured coverage confounding scalar is negative and small in magnitude, $r=-0.135922814$, so the record is suitable as a bounded stoichiometry-and-context surface rather than a coverage-only artifact.  The corresponding audit tuple is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{GLORI}} &= 60000,\\
 d_{\mathrm{GLORI}} &= 49668,\\
 r_{\mathrm{coverage}} &= -0.135922814,\\
 c_{\mathrm{condition}} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This row set is useful for calibrating how a transcript-local chemical mark can be held as a finite readout, but it does not by itself contain the decay-factor RBP perturbation columns required for the proposed RNA-half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Adjacent RNA-contact checks.}
Two neighboring finite contacts help delimit the claim.  The RBNS/eCLIP RBP-binding run reports $900$ total peak rows and only $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries.  That observation is not a target-decay-factor rescue experiment, but it records that RBP occupancy evidence must be handled as a separate measured surface rather than inferred from sequence geometry.  The m6Am Crown-seq run gives an independent RNA-stoichiometry comparison with $n=31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct sites, cross-cell concordance true, a saturated $\geq 0.95$ fraction of $0.343223$, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, and $20942$ rows in the variable subset.  These values show that finite RNA-modification panels can carry condition-stable scalar structure, while still remaining distinct from translation-conditioned RNA half-life and RBP loss-rescue evidence.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual synonymous coordinate label on row $i$, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline terms.  The finite association allowed at the first boundary has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H
 &= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i)
    -\widehat{E}(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
A nonzero $\Delta_H$ is only an RNA-half-life association unless the RBP columns are present.  For the stronger bridge, the same rows must also include local occupancy $O_i$ for a named decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss $L_i$, wild-type rescue $W_i$, and binding-defective or non-target rescue $B_i$.  The bridge pattern is then a finite conjunction: occupancy differs at the editable window, the half-life contrast collapses under target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue restores the contrast, and the defective or non-target rescue does not restore it.

\paragraph{Null model and break condition.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$.  The stronger RBP null is that any remaining half-life contrast is not assigned to a decay-factor RBP unless the occupancy, loss, rescue, and defective-control pattern is observed on the same finite panel.  Thus the GLORI row count and concordance support an RNA-stoichiometry context surface, not a completed RNA-decay mechanism.  The packet breaks if one treats B*$_{Q6}$ residual geometry, codon-window position, m6A stoichiometry, transcript abundance, or translation conditioning as direct evidence for RBP-mediated decay without the local RBP experiment.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It also does not license protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality from the finite rows listed above.  Those claims require their own layer-matched measurements.  In the present record, the correct conclusion is bounded: $60000$ GLORI rows with $49668$ distinct m6A sites and a small negative coverage-confounding scalar can anchor a finite RNA-stoichiometry context; the RBP-binding and m6Am neighboring checks show how separate RNA-contact surfaces are counted; none of these rows yet supplies the complete same-window target-RBP loss and rescue panel needed to promote the RNA half-life association to a decay-factor RBP bridge.
