\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and a decay-factor RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite matched RNA panel in which a synonymous residual coordinate window is allowed to enter an RNA half-life question only after the amino-acid quotient has already removed protein-sequence content.  The coordinate side is a high-versus-low $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label on a coding transcript window.  The biological side is not a bare transcript-stability observation: it requires the same row to carry a translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcription and abundance controls, local sequence and structure controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP route.  The operative finite carrier is therefore a table of rows
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r &= (q, T, H, O, L, W, C),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q$ is the residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotient, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $H$ is RNA half-life, $O$ is measured local occupancy of the named decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, $L$ records target-RBP loss, $W$ records wild-type rescue, and $C$ records binding-defective or non-target controls together with transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch covariates.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The available GLORI m6A stoichiometry run supplies a real finite RNA-modification surface, not the full RBP-perturbation panel required for this claim.  Its run identifier is $2e8583415e99$; the parsed table contains $60000$ rows and $49668$ distinct sites, with cross-condition concordance recorded as true.  The reported coverage-confounding scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \rho_{\mathrm{coverage}} &= -0.135922814,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the computation time recorded for the run is $78.316$ seconds.  These numbers license the existence of a finite stoichiometry-grade RNA surface with a measured coverage diagnostic, but they do not by themselves measure translation-conditioned RNA decay, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss-of-RBP collapse, or rescue behavior.

A separate RBP-binding anchor is also present: the RBNS/eCLIP contact run $b225890251c2$ reports $900$ total peaks, with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases and runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  This second finite anchor shows that an RBP-binding-style table can be represented in the same evidence ledger, but it is not yet joined to the GLORI rows, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ windows, RNA half-life rows, translation rows, and perturbation/rescue rows needed here.  Consequently the present chapter treats the RBP component as a required bridge condition rather than as an already measured positive result.

\paragraph{Tested relation.}
The admissible statistic is a translation-conditioned RNA half-life contrast, stratified by residual-coordinate label and guarded by the RBP bridge.  A positive bridge would require the coordinate-associated half-life contrast to track experimentally separated local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, disappear under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.  In schematic finite-row form, the allowed comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H
    &= \widehat{H}(q=\mathrm{high}\mid T,Z)
       - \widehat{H}(q=\mathrm{low}\mid T,Z),\\
  Z
    &= (\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motifs},\mathrm{GC},
        \mathrm{dinucleotides},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},
        \mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{amino\ acids},\mathrm{batch}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The contrast may be called an RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge only when the same finite panel also shows the occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_H \neq 0,
  \quad \Delta_O \neq 0,
  \quad \Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} \approx 0,
  \quad \Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} \approx \Delta_H,
  \quad \Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
If the half-life contrast remains but these RBP conditions are absent, the result is only a bounded RNA-stability association.  If the half-life contrast vanishes after the declared controls, high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable for this readout.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on translation readout and the control vector $Z$.  The GLORI anchor gives a finite RNA stoichiometry surface with $60000$ measured rows and a weak negative coverage diagnostic, while the RBP-binding anchor gives a finite binding surface with $900$ peaks.  Neither anchor is sufficient to reject the null for this RNA half-life bridge, because the current finite payload does not contain the same-row join
$$
\begin{aligned}
  (B^{*}_{Q6}\ \mathrm{window})
  \times (\mathrm{translation})
  \times (\mathrm{RNA\ half\ life})
  \times (\mathrm{local\ RBP\ occupancy})
  \times (\mathrm{loss/rescue\ controls}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The chapter therefore records a ready finite specification and a bounded negative boundary: the bridge is well-defined, but the available rows do not yet instantiate the decisive RBP-loss and rescue test.

\paragraph{Local scope.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA decay mechanism follows from a coordinate-associated half-life contrast unless measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP changes in the required direction, target-RBP loss collapses the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  No protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the stated RBP-decay test, or universal biological law is licensed by this finite record.  Those claims require their own matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
