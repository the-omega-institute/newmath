\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life and RBP Occupancy at a Q6 Residual Window}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-glori}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel in which each row would carry a coding-transcript or CDS identifier, a synonymous residual-coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcription, abundance, composition, isoform state, batch, local RNA structure, motif density, and codon-pair context. The present empirical anchor for the chapter is the GLORI m6A stoichiometry table identified by the claim id \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6a\_stoichiometry.m6a\_stoichiometry\_exon\_architecture\_glori}. Its verified run \texttt{2e8583415e99} contains $60000$ finite rows, $49668$ distinct sites or row identities after the declared parsing, a cross-condition concordance flag equal to true, and a measured coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$. These numbers are not treated as an RNA-decay result. They delimit the finite stoichiometry surface on which any stronger RNA-half-life or RBP-bridge assertion would have to be joined row by row.

\paragraph{Coordinate contrast and null model.}
Let $q_i$ denote the high-coordinate or low-coordinate synonymous residual label after amino-acid quotienting, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and $Z_i$ the matched control vector. The relevant finite comparison is not a marginal stoichiometry contrast and not a motif-density contrast. It is the residual question whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable with respect to $H_i$ after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$, and whether any remaining half-life effect follows the measured RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i ={}& (\mathrm{transcription}_i,\mathrm{abundance}_i,\mathrm{structure}_i,\mathrm{motif}_i,\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide}_i,\\
&\mathrm{codon\ pair}_i,\mathrm{isoform}_i,\mathrm{batch}_i,\mathrm{amino\ acid}_i),\\
H_i \perp q_i \mid (T_i,Z_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

is the matched null. A departure from this null is still only an RNA half-life association unless the occupancy and perturbation terms are observed on the same finite surface. In particular, a half-life difference without a local RBP-occupancy change, without target-RBP loss collapse, or without restoration by wild-type rescue remains outside the RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Positive bridge condition.}
A positive bridge would require the following rowwise pattern. First, the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate contrast must survive the same-row translation boundary, so that $T_i$ is represented and conditioned rather than merely named. Second, the half-life residual must track experimentally separated local occupancy of the chosen decay-factor RBP at the relevant RNA window. Third, loss of the target RBP must collapse the half-life effect. Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect. Fifth, binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to restore it. The finite bridge statistic therefore has the logical shape

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_O(q;Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z,\mathrm{target\ loss}) \approx 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z,\mathrm{wildtype\ rescue}) \approx \Delta_H(q;T,Z),\\
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}) \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The GLORI table supplies finite m6A stoichiometry and exon-architecture information, not this five-part perturbation panel. Its $60000$ rows and $49668$ distinct entries are therefore used here as a bounded stoichiometry contact, while the RNA half-life and RBP-causal rows remain required joins rather than inferred consequences.

\paragraph{Cross-checks from neighboring finite contacts.}
The RBP-binding contact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} records run \texttt{b225890251c2}, with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases. This establishes that finite RBP occupancy or binding surfaces can be represented as separate row sets, but it does not identify the decay-factor RBP for the present GLORI-linked RNA window, and it does not provide loss or rescue rows. The translation contact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} records run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, with $6494$ translation-readout rows from the declared GEO source. This confirms that a translation surface can be finite and externally keyed, but it does not by itself become an RNA half-life measurement or an RBP perturbation experiment. A related RNA methylation contact, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6am\_methylation.m6am\_flanking\_identity\_stoichiometry\_crownseq}, contains $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, cross-cell concordance true, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$, and partial correlations $0.268094$, $0.098666$, and $0.168335$ in the recorded subsets. These scalar checks show that RNA-modification stoichiometry can carry reproducible finite structure, but they do not substitute for the GLORI-specific half-life, occupancy, loss, and rescue joins.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter therefore records a constrained cross-layer target rather than a completed mechanism. The finite GLORI data support a row-bounded m6A stoichiometry and exon-architecture surface with weak negative coverage association $r=-0.135922814$ and cross-condition concordance. They do not by themselves show translation realization, RNA decay causation, RBP-mediated half-life control, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, adaptation, phenotype, or universality. A future positive claim would require the same organism and same editable window to carry B*$_{Q6}$ residual labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows, followed by the finite statistic described above. Until those rows are present, any observed stoichiometry or half-life difference must remain a bounded descriptor or a finite RNA-stability association, not an RBP-decay bridge.
