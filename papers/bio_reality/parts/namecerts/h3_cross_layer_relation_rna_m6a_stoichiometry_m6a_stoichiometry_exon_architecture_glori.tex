\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and RBP-bridge boundary for the GLORI m6A stoichiometry panel}\label{sec:bioreality-rna-m6a-stoichiometry-glori-rbp-half-life-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The finite carrier for this record is a matched transcript or coding-sequence table in which each row may carry a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout when available, local mRNA abundance controls, and a possible decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation readout.  The GLORI m6A stoichiometry run supplies a large methylation-stoichiometry contact rather than the full RNA-decay/RBP intervention panel: the audited table contains $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites or row identities, a coverage-control correlation of $-0.135922814$, and a positive cross-condition concordance flag.  These numbers make the panel a useful finite methylation-stoichiometry surface, but they do not by themselves supply RNA half-life, decay-factor RBP occupancy, RBP loss, rescue, or binding-defective control rows.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistics.}
The row-level GLORI statistic is a bounded cross-condition m6A stoichiometry readout.  In the notation of this chapter, let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, let $a_i$ denote the local m6A stoichiometry measurement, let $c_i$ collect coverage and sequencing controls, and let $e_i$ record the exon or local transcript architecture variable.  The finite GLORI audit supports only the claim that the measured methylation surface is nonempty, row-indexed, and reproducibly present across the declared conditions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{GLORI}} &= 60000,\\
D_{\mathrm{GLORI}} &= 49668,\\
r_{\mathrm{coverage}} &= -0.135922814,\\
\mathrm{crossConditionConcordant} &= \mathrm{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The negative coverage correlation is small enough to be reported as a finite confound diagnostic rather than as a mechanism.  It says that the stoichiometry signal has been checked against coverage variation in this run; it does not say that coverage has no remaining effect in every downstream conditioning regime.

\paragraph{Methylation-neighbor calibration.}
A second RNA-methylation finite-row contact helps calibrate the scale of this kind of claim without converting one assay into another.  The m6Am Crown-seq audit contains $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct rows, a cross-cell concordance flag equal to true, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, and a saturated fraction $0.343223$ for rows at or above $0.95$.  Its internal finite test reports a GC-matched null probability $p=0.000833$, a full-set partial rank correlation $0.268094$, a low-tail partial rank correlation $0.098666$, and a variable-subset partial rank correlation $0.168335$ on $20942$ rows.  These scalar values show that RNA methylation stoichiometry contacts can carry controlled finite signals, but the m6Am assay is not evidence for the GLORI m6A exon-architecture mechanism.  It is used here only as a same-family scale check: finite methylation stoichiometry can be measured, controlled, and bounded, while layer promotion still requires the layer-specific contact.

\paragraph{Required bridge for an RNA half-life claim.}
The proposed cross-layer statement is stronger than a GLORI m6A stoichiometry association.  It asks whether a high-versus-low $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, after amino-acid quotienting and after transcriptional and compositional controls, is associated with a translation-conditioned RNA half-life effect that is mediated through a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The finite statistic needed for that bridge must join the following row fields in the same organism and compatible transcript window: residual coordinate, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance or transcription control, RNA half-life, measured local occupancy of the named decay-factor RBP, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue control.  The GLORI run does not contain that joined intervention panel.  Therefore the present chapter records a bounded methylation-stoichiometry and architecture contact, plus a precise failure mode for the stronger RBP-decay interpretation.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null states that, after controlling for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout, high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  The null is not broken by a methylation-stoichiometry table alone.  It is also not broken by an RNA half-life contrast alone if the measured RBP occupancy and intervention fields are absent.  A positive bridge would require the half-life effect to track the experimentally decoupled local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, disappear after loss of that RBP, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Decision boundary.}
This chapter therefore permits the following finite statement: the GLORI panel supplies a row-indexed m6A stoichiometry contact with $60000$ audited rows, $49668$ distinct row identities, cross-condition concordance, and a recorded coverage diagnostic $r=-0.135922814$.  It does not permit a translation-realization claim, a ribosome-occupancy claim, an RNA-decay mechanism claim, an RBP-mediated half-life claim, a protein-output claim, a folding claim, a physical-admissibility claim, a function claim, a phenotype claim, an adaptation claim, a causal mechanism claim, or a universal biological law.  Any such statement must be supplied by a separate layer-matched contact and a declared finite statistic at that layer.  For the RBP bridge specifically, the missing evidence is not cosmetic: the required same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are the distinction between a bounded RNA methylation or stability association and a mechanistic RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite GLORI data are ready for use as a bounded m6A stoichiometry and exon-architecture contact.  The translation-conditioned RNA half-life/RBP bridge remains null under the present payload because the data do not include the required RNA half-life, same-row translation, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss, rescue, and control rows.  The correct reading is therefore a scoped positive contact for methylation stoichiometry, paired with an explicit non-promotion rule for translation, RNA decay mechanism, protein state, structure, physical admissibility, function, and universality.
