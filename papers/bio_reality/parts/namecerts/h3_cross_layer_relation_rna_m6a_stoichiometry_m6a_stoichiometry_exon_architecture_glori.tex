\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the m6A exon-architecture panel}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-m6a-glori}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The claim records a finite matched-panel question rather than a general molecular law.  The available audited row surface is the GLORI-derived m6A stoichiometry and exon-architecture panel indexed by the claim id \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6a\_stoichiometry.m6a\_stoichiometry\_exon\_architecture\_glori}.  Its verified run is \texttt{2e8583415e99}, with $n=60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct row identities, a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$, and a cross-condition concordance flag equal to true.  The audit timestamp is \texttt{2026-06-27T19:56:13+00:00}, and the recorded runtime is $78.316$ seconds.  These numbers license a finite statement about a measured m6A stoichiometry surface under an exon-architecture readout; they do not by themselves license an RNA-decay mechanism, an RBP mechanism, or a translation-output conclusion.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.}
The biological object is a set of synonymous residual coordinate windows, denoted here by $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, carried on coding transcript rows after the amino-acid quotient has removed direct amino-acid identity as the explanation.  The intended crossed table has one row per matched transcript or CDS window and contains the residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript abundance controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation record.  The verified GLORI table supplies the m6A stoichiometry and exon-architecture side of this finite record.  It does not yet supply the full same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target rescue columns required for a positive RBP-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Declared finite statistic.}
Let $q_i$ be the high- or low-coordinate residual label for row $i$, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, let $A_i$ be matched transcript abundance, and let $O_i$ be measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler baseline descriptors.  A positive bridge would require the coordinate-associated half-life term to follow the measured occupancy term and to disappear under target-RBP loss while returning under wild-type rescue but not under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  In compact form, the intended finite readout is:
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \widehat{E}(H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,A_i,Z_i)-\widehat{E}(H_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,A_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_O &= \widehat{E}(O_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,A_i,Z_i)-\widehat{E}(O_i\mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,A_i,Z_i),\\
\mathrm{Bridge} &= (\Delta_H\neq 0)\wedge(\Delta_O\neq 0)\wedge\mathrm{loss\ collapse}\wedge\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}\wedge\neg\mathrm{defective\ rescue}.
\end{aligned}$$
This is a finite-panel criterion, not an asymptotic claim.  If any of the required occupancy, loss, rescue, or control arms is absent, the coordinate can remain a descriptor of an RNA-stability association, but it cannot be promoted to an RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{What the verified m6A run contributes.}
The GLORI-side row counts are useful because they show that the m6A exon-architecture readout is not an empty or purely schematic surface.  The run has $60000$ measured rows and $49668$ distinct row identities, so the finite carrier is large enough to support matched filtering and nuisance adjustment in principle.  The recorded coverage-confound correlation, $r=-0.135922814$, is small in magnitude but not zero; therefore coverage must remain a live control rather than being silently ignored.  The cross-condition concordance flag is true, so the m6A stoichiometry signal has a finite reproducibility indication inside the audited GLORI surface.  This is still only a stoichiometry and architecture result.  It does not supply the same-row translation-conditioned RNA half-life statistic or the RBP perturbation logic required by the bridge criterion above.

\paragraph{Comparison surfaces that remain separate.}
Two neighboring finite audits clarify the boundary.  The RBNS/eCLIP RBP-binding audit \texttt{b225890251c2} records $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  This confirms that finite RBP-binding surfaces exist elsewhere in the BioReality ledger, but it is not the same matched transcript window panel as the GLORI m6A stoichiometry table and therefore cannot be spliced into this claim as local decay-factor occupancy.  The m6Am CROWN-seq audit \texttt{eb689b7dc73c} records $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct identities, cross-cell concordance true, a saturated $\geq 0.95$ fraction of $0.343223$, and a variable-subset size of $20942$.  That record is another finite methylation-stoichiometry surface; it helps calibrate the style of row-level methylation evidence but does not provide the missing target-RBP loss or rescue arms for the present m6A exon-architecture claim.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and after conditioning on same-row translation readout.  More explicitly, the null does not say that m6A stoichiometry is unmeasured; the GLORI table has a measured finite surface.  It says that, for this cross-layer bridge, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels remain indistinguishable for translation-conditioned RNA half-life unless the finite row panel contains the RBP occupancy and perturbation pattern.  A half-life difference without measured occupancy change is not enough.  An occupancy association without decoupling from coordinate and nuisance composition is not enough.  Target-RBP loss without wild-type rescue is not enough.  Wild-type rescue without failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue is not enough.

\paragraph{Support condition and failure condition.}
The support condition is narrow.  A positive record would have the GLORI-like coordinate and methylation rows joined to same-organism, same-window translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target controls.  The effect must survive transcription-rate, local-structure, motif-density, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, splice or isoform, abundance, amino-acid, batch, rank-sufficiency, and simpler-baseline controls.  The failure condition is equally concrete: if the observed RNA half-life contrast remains after translation conditioning but the measured RBP occupancy does not move, or if target-RBP loss does not collapse the contrast, or if binding-defective rescue restores it, then the result is not an RBP bridge.  It is at most a bounded RNA-stability association on a finite panel.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not infer translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It does not infer ribosome occupancy from m6A stoichiometry, transcript abundance, or codon-window geometry.  It does not infer protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality from the GLORI row counts.  Those conclusions require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite rows and declared statistics.  The present record is therefore ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because the measured finite m6A surface is concrete and the missing bridge columns are explicitly localized, while the positive RBP-mediated RNA half-life claim remains blocked until the required same-window occupancy, perturbation, and rescue panel exists.
