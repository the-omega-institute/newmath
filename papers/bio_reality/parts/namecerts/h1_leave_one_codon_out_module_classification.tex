\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification for the window-six codon tile}
\label{subsec:biores-codon-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the label-readout layer.  The coordinate carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon represented by its finite binary display and then compared through local faces, singleton corners, and three-point label classes.  The construction is not treated as a mechanism for translation.  It is a finite diagnostic for asking whether a window-six coordinate split isolates a small boundary set whose labels match curated codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop assignments.

The distinguished row set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a punctured cubical tile, the same set has face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row being classified is not an arbitrary fragment of the code table: it is a finite local shape with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces inside the codon display.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling controls.}
The associated spectral calculation gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The cubic residual was recorded as $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the radial polynomial check, so the numerical constraint is satisfied to the stated tolerance.  In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, the exact-shape match count was $1$, giving $p=0.0005$.  The high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The sampling log also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which matters because the observed finite tile lies in a constrained combinatorial class rather than in a loose pool of independent draws.

A second null check preserved amino-acid class information.  In that run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed module size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$.  The corresponding reported probabilities were $p=0.0$ for exact shape and $p=0.0035$ for the lower tail.  These controls support the claim that the row shape and its module behavior are not explained by the tested label-preserving randomization alone.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  These numbers say that the local window-six tile remains highly informative when a code table is withheld, provided the readout remains at the codon-label layer.

The leave-one-codon-out run gives a sharper boundary description.  It records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The same value $1.0$ is recorded for recovery above threshold.  The result is therefore not a claim that every codon has an intrinsic biological role determined by geometry.  It is the narrower statement that, for this curated standard-code contact, every withheld codon in the selected row is recovered either by the predictable part of the local coordinate module or by the recorded boundary part.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The complete module assignment partitions all $13$ codons of $R$ with no missing or extra rows: the recorded classification has $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, empty extra-module list, empty missing-from-modules list, and empty unassigned-codon list.  The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each module has at least one codon classified through the predictable-or-boundary route.  The singleton modules $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$ and $\mathrm{ile\_met}$ show that the classification is not merely a many-row family effect; the two-row and three-row modules show that the same rule also handles small local label classes; and the four-row $\mathrm{cun}$ module supplies the largest local block in the row.

The family-level leave-one-code-out run separates five table families.  The above-baseline family hit rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  This supports robustness across the tested family grouping, but it still concerns code-label readback rather than organism-level translation dynamics.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The only empirical contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact can test code-read behavior and the local split of codon labels.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal law of molecular organization.  Consequently, the present result should be read as a finite classification of a codon-label tile in $Q_6$: it supports the statement that the row $R$ is completely assigned into predictable-or-boundary modules under the tested leave-one-codon protocol, and it blocks any promotion from that local label fact to translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality unless an independent contact at that higher layer is supplied.
