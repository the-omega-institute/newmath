\paragraph{Finite codon carrier.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with codons represented as six-bit positions grouped by the usual three-letter RNA notation.  The observed exceptional row $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a cubical subobject, this same row is recorded with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the data do not merely name thirteen codons: they also specify a finite local shape in the codon cube.  The role of the curated standard-code table is to attach amino-acid or stop labels to these codon positions; it is not used here as evidence for translation chemistry, folded structure, physical viability, function, or a universal biological mechanism.

The sampling run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported small tail is attached to a visibly constrained finite search rather than to an unconstrained continuum argument.

\paragraph{Class-preserving comparison.}
A second null comparison preserved amino-acid classes.  In that experiment $N=2000$, the observed module-size statistic was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
This comparison matters because it tests whether the row could be dismissed as a trivial consequence of label-class preservation.  The answer supplied by the finite run is negative at the reported resolution: preserving the label classes did not reproduce the same local shape, and only seven of the two thousand samples fell at or below the observed module-size statistic.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out probe evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, whereas the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate after adjoining the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level decomposition had five families.  The reported above-baseline hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  The leave-one-codon-out probe reported no anchors, five boundary cases, eight high-coverage closure cases, eight predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers support a local code-label recovery pattern: when a single codon is withheld, the surrounding finite tile and boundary information are sufficient to recover the recorded label behavior in the tested cases.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module-classification row is complete on $R$.  It assigns all $13$ codons, leaves no extra module codons, and reports no missing or unassigned entries.  The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_LYS}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\

\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{STOP\_TRP}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness statement is therefore concrete: $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, codons missing from modules, and unassigned codons are all empty.  Each module has at least one predictable or boundary-supported member, so the local classification is not a loose annotation layered onto the table after the fact; it is a finite partition of the thirteen observed positions.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, whose observed content is a codon-to-label assignment.  Consequently, the supported conclusion is confined to the code-reading layer: window-six coordinates expose a local boundary-tile organization in the standard-code labels, and the leave-one probes show strong finite recovery behavior inside that layer.  No statement about actual translation realization follows from these rows.  No statement about folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, or organism-independent law follows either.  Any such stronger assertion would require a separate contact whose measured object is translation, structure, physics, function, or cross-organism mechanism rather than a codon-label table.  The present packet therefore records a finite, code-layer module classification with explicit refusal of higher-layer promotion.
