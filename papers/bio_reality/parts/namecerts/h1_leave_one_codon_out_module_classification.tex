\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}
This record concerns a code-read object: the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-coordinate codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, while the observable labels are supplied by a curated standard genetic-code table.  The construction is intentionally local.  It tests whether the codons singled out by the window-six boundary calculation are organized by finite codon modules whose members are either directly predictable or attached to a boundary face.  The contact is therefore a table-level contact: it reports codon-to-label assignments, not translation dynamics, folded structure, biochemical admissibility, organismal function, or a universal biological mechanism.

The distinguished residual set has cardinality $13$.  In alphabetical codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
   &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube calculation gives the same set and has face-vector data $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row does not merely count thirteen exceptional codons; it identifies the same thirteen codons as a finite punctured-cube boundary pattern with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces.

The completeness row records $R\_size=13$ and $assigned\_count=13$, with $extra\_module\_codons=\emptyset$, $missing\_from\_modules=\emptyset$, and $unassigned\_codons=\emptyset$.  The biological reading is narrow but concrete: every codon in the residual tile is accounted for by one of the six local modules, and each module has at least one predictable or boundary-supported member.  This is a finite module statement about the codon-label table, not a claim that these modules are causal units in a ribosome or physical folding constraints in a protein.
