\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object considered here is the standard-code codon table after it has been displayed as a six-coordinate codon cube.  The cube coordinate is not treated as a biochemical mechanism.  It is a finite display on which one may ask whether the codons singled out by leave-one-codon probes fall into stable local modules.  The row under audit identifies the relevant residual set as a thirteen-codon subset
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $|R|=13$.  The same finite row records that this set has the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the support is not merely a list of codons but a small local cubical pattern with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces.  The amino-acid side is represented in the available rows by the observed size $|M|=20$, not by a separate explicit element list in this packet.

\paragraph{Spectral coordinate.}
The spectrum row supplies a numerical check on the same finite shape.  Its recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The residual polynomial check is numerically stable at $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is effectively zero at the precision of the computation.  These numbers do not claim a physical eigenmode of a molecule.  They say only that the finite codon display has a reproducible internal spectral reading compatible with the punctured-cube row.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The leave-one-codon-out classification partitions the thirteen residual codons into six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{IleMet}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{StopTrp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification-completeness row reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Thus the module statement is exact at the level of the finite codon rows: every codon in $R$ is accounted for exactly once by the displayed module inventory.  The stronger local condition is also recorded: every module contains a codon that is predictable or boundary-supported under the leave-one-codon-out probe.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon and leave-one-code support.}
The direct leave-one-codon-out row has $anchor\_count=0$, $boundary\_count=5$, $predictable\_count=8$, $closure\_coverage\_high=8$, and $recovery\_rate=1.0$.  Hence the row does not say that an anchor alone reconstructs the codon.  Instead, it says that the observable recovery in this packet is carried by a mixture of predictable positions and boundary positions, with all recovered cases meeting the recorded threshold.  The related leave-one-code-out row gives $23$ evaluated code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$ and the rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary is $0.9841269841269841$.  The row also records the above-baseline hit rate as $0.9841269841269841$.  These values support the narrow code-read statement: in the curated label table, the local codon geometry is strongly aligned with recoverable label-side positions once the relevant boundary is admitted.

\paragraph{Null-model checks.}
Two finite null comparisons constrain the interpretation.  The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts with $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p\_exact=0.0005$, and $25$ high-Jaccard matches with $p\_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The amino-acid-class-preserving null row also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$; it found $0$ exact matches and $7$ lower-tail cases at or below the observed $M$ size, with lower-tail probability $0.0035$.  These rows make the local pattern nontrivial relative to the sampled finite alternatives.  They do not make it inevitable in biology, and they do not replace a laboratory contact.

\paragraph{Family-level row.}
The by-family leave-one-code-out row reports five families.  Their above-baseline hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate.  This supports the statement that the finite code-read pattern is not confined to a single table row in the sampled family grouping.  The weaker alternative-yeast value is kept visible because it is part of the finite record and prevents the result from being described as uniform perfection across all family labels.

\paragraph{Reality contact and local boundary.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, which assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  Consequently the supported readback is a code-layer readback: codons are compared with their curated labels, and local module behavior is checked inside that finite table.  The contact does not observe ribosomal translation, folding, admissible chemistry, biological function, a generative mechanism, or a universal rule of life.  No such higher-layer conclusion follows from the module classification unless a separate contact for that layer is supplied.  In particular, reading an amino-acid label sequence from codons is not evidence of folded protein structure, and the CUN, AGR, StopTrp, IleMet, AR-Lys, and SSN modules should be kept as code-table modules rather than promoted to structural or functional modules.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The finite evidence is sufficient for the stated module-classification claim.  The residual codon set has cardinality $13$, matches the listed codons exactly, has punctured-cube face vector $(13,16,4)$, satisfies the recorded spectral checks, and is completely assigned into six modules.  The leave-one-codon-out row records full recovery at the threshold level through predictable and boundary cases, while the leave-one-code-out and null-model rows show that the observed pattern is substantially above the recorded baselines.  The result is therefore ready as a finite code-read claim about the standard-code label table, with its scope explicitly bounded to local codon-tile classification.
