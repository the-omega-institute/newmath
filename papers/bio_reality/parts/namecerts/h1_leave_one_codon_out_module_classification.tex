\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table at the code-read layer. The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary window presentation of codons together with a curated codon-to-label table. The claim is not that the cube geometry generates biology. It is narrower: when the locally distinguished codon region is tested by leave-one-codon probes, its codons decompose into named modules, and every codon in the distinguished region is accounted for by a predictable-or-boundary module assignment.

\paragraph{Distinguished region.}
The finite region $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object under discussion is not a vague family of codons: it is a concrete thirteen-vertex region with sixteen one-dimensional adjacencies and four two-dimensional faces in the window-six display.

\paragraph{Module assignment.}
The leave-one-codon module classifier partitions the thirteen codons of $R$ into six named modules. The assignments are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completion audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. In particular, the local classification is exact on this finite row: every codon in $R$ belongs to one of the six modules above, and no codon outside $R$ is smuggled into the module inventory.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon behavior.}
The direct leave-one-codon probe reports recovery rate $1.0$ and recovery rate above threshold $1.0$. It classifies $8$ codons as predictable, $5$ as boundary, and $0$ as anchor, with $8$ cases meeting the high closure-coverage condition. This is the finite meaning of the module result: the modules are not merely labels placed after the fact, since each module has at least one codon whose leave-one-out behavior is either predictable or boundary-visible under the same local tile test.

\paragraph{Code-table comparison.}
Across leave-one-code-out evaluation, $23$ code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate in $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$ and the hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The reported hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. By family, the test counted $5$ families. The hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast. These row counts and rates support the local code-read interpretation: the window-six construction is strongly aligned with curated label changes in the tested finite tables, while retaining a separate boundary class.

\paragraph{Shape and spectral checks.}
The associated shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ high-Jaccard matches at threshold $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$. The sampling process required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, the observed $M$ size was $20$, exact shape matches were $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, the exact-shape $p$-value was $0.0$, and the lower-tail $p$-value was $0.0035$. The spectral row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic, or radial, polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical spectral check is at tolerance scale rather than a visible failure of the finite computation.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The biological contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table. Its observed fact is simply that codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels. This contact is adequate for codon label readback and for checking whether a local split of codon labels agrees with the finite module computation. It is not an observation of ribosomal translation dynamics, molecular structure, energetic admissibility, organismal function, or evolutionary universality.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result therefore stays at the code-read layer. The finite row supports the following chapter-specific statement: the thirteen-codon region $R$ is exactly covered by six modules, every module contains predictable-or-boundary leave-one-codon behavior, and the surrounding finite comparisons show non-random alignment with curated codon labels. Nothing in this row upgrades that statement into a claim about translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law. Any such upgrade would require an additional contact that directly measures the higher layer rather than reusing the codon-label table.
