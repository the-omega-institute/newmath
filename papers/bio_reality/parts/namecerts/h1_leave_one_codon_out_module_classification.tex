\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object.  Codons are displayed in a six-bit window, so the working carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$ with local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The claim is not that the cube geometry is a biological law.  The claim is narrower: after one codon is withheld, the finite codon set singled out by the local window geometry can be classified into named modules, and every codon in that set is accounted for by a predictable or boundary role against the curated standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Finite row under test.}
The row to be classified is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality audit gives $|R|=13$.  The punctured-cube audit gives the same list and records the face-count vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not being treated as an open-ended biological class: the exact codon inventory, its size, and its local face data are finite observations of the computational packet.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module partition is complete.  The assigned modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completion row reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  In particular, the classification is exact at the finite-row level: the six modules neither omit nor introduce codons relative to $R$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon recovery signal.}
The leave-one-codon-out audit reports $\mathrm{predictable\_count}=8$, $\mathrm{boundary\_count}=5$, $\mathrm{anchor\_count}=0$, and $\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}=8$.  The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison also returns $1.0$.  These numbers justify the local statement that each module has a predictable or boundary explanation within this finite codon row.  They do not justify moving from codon labels to translation dynamics, molecular folding, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal law.

\paragraph{Code-table contact.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  This contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for checking how the local tile split sits against standard-code labels.  It is not an observation of ribosomal translation, protein structure, stability, activity, organismal usage, or biochemical necessity.  Therefore amino-acid label sequences remain code-layer outputs in this chapter; they are not folded proteins and are not evidence of function.

\paragraph{Boundary statement.}
The chapter records a finite local classification of the thirteen codons in $R$ and its agreement with predictable or boundary module roles.  The curated standard-code table supplies the label contact, and the verified rows supply the codon inventory, face vector, spectrum, null comparisons, leave-one-code behavior, and module coverage.  No claim is made here about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, biological mechanism, or universality.  Any such claim would require a separate contact designed for that higher layer.
