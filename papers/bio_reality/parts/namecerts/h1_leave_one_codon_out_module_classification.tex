\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Standard-Code Window}
\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object on the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The coordinate display is not used as a biological law; it is used as a finite address system in which codons, local faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes can be compared against a curated codon-to-label table.  The tested residue set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check returns the same set and the finite face summary $f=(13,16,4)$.  Thus the local object under study is a specific thirteen-codon boundary pattern, not the full translation system and not a claim about protein realization.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The associated spectral row reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic residual is numerically small, with value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the computed spectral row is internally stable at the recorded tolerance.  The shape-randomization row used $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The same run reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which makes the finite sampling pressure visible rather than hidden.  A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, records observed $M$ size $20$, zero exact matches, $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed size, $p_{\mathrm{class,exact}}=0.0$, and $p_{\mathrm{class,tail}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluates $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the observed hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the corresponding rate after adding boundary codons is $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level split has $5$ families: alternative yeast has rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial/plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have rate $1.0$.  The leave-one-codon-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, predictable count $8$, high closure coverage $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  These numbers support a local code-read pattern: the omitted codon is recovered either through predictable closure behavior or by sitting on the recorded boundary.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module-classification row assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ and reports no missing, extra, or unassigned codons.  The complete partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
AR_{\mathrm{Lys}}&=\{AAA\},\\
CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
Ile/Met&=\{AUA\},\\
SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
Stop/Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit row records $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$.  Consequently every codon in the local residue set is accounted for by one of the named modules, and every named module contains codons that are predictable or boundary-supported in the leave-one-codon-out classification.  This is the finite content of the claim: the standard-code table, when read through the six-bit local window, exposes a closed module accounting for the thirteen-codon boundary set.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only empirical contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, interpreted as codon-label readback.  That contact licenses statements about codon labels, local tile splits, module membership, finite recovery rates, and the comparison of the observed pattern with randomized label-preserving alternatives.  It does not observe translation in a ribosome, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, organism-level mechanism, or universal necessity.  Amino-acid label sequences are therefore kept at the code layer.  Any later statement about folding, structure, function, physical feasibility, or a general biological law would require a separate contact that directly measures or otherwise constrains that higher layer.
