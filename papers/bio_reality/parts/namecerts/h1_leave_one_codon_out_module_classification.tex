\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}
This record studies the standard-code codon table through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is displayed as a six-bit object and the inspected signal is a local tile boundary rather than a biochemical mechanism. The carrier is finite: the code-layer readout is the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels, while the geometric side keeps only coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data. The conjectural content is therefore deliberately narrow. It asks whether a window-six representation exposes a stable local split around the leave-one-codon-out rows; it does not identify that split with translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal law.

\paragraph{Residual codon set.}
The verified residual set has cardinality $13$. The codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check returns the same list and has face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object being classified is not an unspecified biological population. It is the thirteen-row residual codon set above, viewed inside the local six-bit cube and compared against the curated standard-code labels.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The spectral run records $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The reported cubic residual is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is also the radial polynomial value in the finite computation. The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples, with $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}&=0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null run also used $N=2000$. It records observed $M$ size $20$, exact match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support a finite code-layer regularity claim: the observed local shape is uncommon under the recorded randomizations. They do not, by themselves, supply a molecular dynamics, a ribosomal process, a protein fold, or an organism-level function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level summary has $5$ families. The above-baseline family hit rates are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.

For the leave-one-codon-out check, the run reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary rows, $8$ predictable rows, $8$ high-coverage closure rows, and recovery rate $1.0$. This is the operational reason the present row is classified as a local boundary-tile result. The recovery is complete in the finite table experiment, but the classification remains at the code-read layer because the only contact used here is the curated standard-code label table.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module partition is complete over the residual set. It has $R$ size $13$, assigned count $13$, and no extra module codons, no missing module entries, and no unassigned codons. The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
AR_{\mathrm{Lys}}&=\{AAA\},\\
CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
Ile/Met&=\{AUA\},\\
SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
Stop/Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has either predictable or boundary support in the finite classification output. Since the assigned count equals $13$ and the residual set also has size $13$, the classification closes over exactly the rows it claims to classify. There is no hidden surplus codon and no omitted codon within the stated residual set.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary of interpretation.}
The reality contact is a curated standard genetic-code table: it assigns codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for checking whether the listed local split is aligned with the standard-code rows. It is not a translation experiment, not a folding observation, not a measurement of physical admissibility, not a functional assay, and not evidence for a universal mechanism. In particular, an amino-acid label sequence obtained from the table is still only a label sequence. No protein structure, structural order, or biological role follows unless a separate structure or function contact is attached.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the following restricted statement: in the standard-code table, the thirteen residual codons listed above form a complete six-module leave-one-codon-out classification, and each module is covered by predictable or boundary behavior under the recorded window-six checks. The spectral values, punctured-cube face vector, null-model p-values, and hit-rate summaries all point to a nontrivial finite code-layer pattern. The result should be read as a local codon-table regularity with curated label readback, not as a claim about translation realization, folded protein structure, physical possibility, biological function, organismal universality, or a general law of biology.
