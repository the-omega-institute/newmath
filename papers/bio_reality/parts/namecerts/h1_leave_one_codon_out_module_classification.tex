\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The codon object is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read with the usual four-letter codon alphabet and then compared with the curated standard-code label table. The test does not ask whether the cube generates biology. It asks a finite classification question: after the window-six construction isolates the codon set $R$, do the codons left exposed by the leave-one-codon experiment fall into named local modules, and does every member of $R$ receive exactly one such module assignment?

The verified residue set has cardinality $13$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this set with the punctured-cube row, with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the local shape being classified is not a free verbal grouping: it is the thirteen-vertex boundary row already detected by the cube computation.

\paragraph{Module decomposition.}
The module audit partitions the thirteen codons into six named classes. The complete assignment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR/Lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile/Met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop/Trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification check reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons. In set-theoretic terms, the displayed six rows are an exact finite cover of $R$ and are disjoint at the codon-token level. The claim is therefore a finite row classification theorem for the observed residue set, not an extrapolation from a few examples.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon readback.}
The leave-one-codon probe supplies the local predictive status attached to the module cover. It records $8$ predictable cases, $5$ boundary cases, $0$ anchor cases, and $8$ high-closure-coverage cases, with recovery rate $1.0$ and recovery rate above threshold $1.0$. The module-level statement is correspondingly modest: each displayed class contains codons that are either predictable under the local leave-one rule or sit on the boundary recognized by the same finite geometry. The row does not assert that a codon is biologically forced by the geometry; it asserts that the leave-one procedure does not leave any of the thirteen residue codons outside the predictable-or-boundary accounting.

\paragraph{Global-code comparison.}
The related leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary is $0.9841269841269841$. By code-family aggregation, the above-baseline rates are $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear ciliate, and candidate other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, across $5$ families. These numbers support the interpretation that the same local residue geometry is repeatedly visible in curated code-label rows, while still leaving the biological labels themselves as observed table entries rather than consequences of the coordinate system.

\paragraph{Shape and spectral checks.}
The shape audit compares the observed row against random draws. In $2000$ samples with seed $42$, there is $1$ exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$. A class-preserving null model gives $0$ exact matches out of $2000$ and a lower-tail count of $7$ at observed $M$-size $20$, with lower-tail probability $0.0035$. These finite comparisons make the row unlikely under the tested null procedures, but they do not convert the row into a law of molecular implementation.

The spectral row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with radial cubic residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$ and the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial residual. The spectral data are used here only as a numerical invariant of the finite codon-row construction. They help identify the row as a stable coordinate object, but they do not by themselves identify a mechanism in translation, folding, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Biological contact and scope.}
The biological contact is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact is sufficient for code-layer label readback and for checking whether the six displayed modules align with local codon-label splits. It is not a measurement of translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, protein function, or universal mechanism. In particular, amino-acid label sequences obtained from the table are still label sequences; no fold, activity, kinetic pathway, organism-specific realization, or necessity theorem follows from this chapter without an additional contact designed to observe that higher layer.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The finite conclusion is that the thirteen codons in $R$ form a completely assigned leave-one-codon module row: $13$ codons are assigned, none are missing, none are extra, and every module participates in the predictable-or-boundary classification. The supporting rows include the punctured-cube $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$, the leave-one-codon recovery rate $1.0$, the leave-one-code-out above-baseline rate $0.9841269841269841$, the family-level rate table, the shape-comparison probabilities $0.0005$ and $0.0125$, the class-preserving lower-tail probability $0.0035$, and the spectral values $(\lambda_M,\lambda_R,\mu)=(0.6752479308830213,0.4758352843197002,3.051487585298128)$. The chapter therefore establishes a finite code-read classification result for the standard-code contact and explicitly stops before translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.
