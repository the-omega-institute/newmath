\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The object in this record is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-read layer.  A codon is displayed as a six-bit coordinate in the codon cube $Q_6$, and the readout checked here is the finite label table assigning each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop.  The distinguished residual set $R$ has exactly $13$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is certified as a punctured-cube pattern with face vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite geometry being discussed is not the whole genetic code and not a cellular mechanism; it is a local coordinate pattern inside the codon-label table.

\paragraph{Module partition.}

The completion check reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, $extra\_module\_codons=\emptyset$, $missing\_from\_modules=\emptyset$, and $unassigned\_codons=\emptyset$.  The six modules are:
$$
\begin{aligned}

$$
The classification statement is therefore finite and exhaustive: every codon in the residual punctured-cube row belongs to one of these six named modules, and each module has a predictable-or-boundary witness in the leave-one-codon probe.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The external contact for this record is a curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact can test codon-label readback and the local codon-tile label split.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, function, or a universal mechanism.  Amino-acid label-sequence readback remains a code-layer observation; it is not evidence that a protein folds, functions, is physically admissible, or is realized by a particular organismal process.
