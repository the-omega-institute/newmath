\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record tests whether the window-six codon coordinate system gives a stable local account of the codons recovered by the leave-one-codon-out probe.  The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read only as a finite display for standard-code labels.  The claim is not that codon geometry determines biology.  It is the narrower assertion that the recovered codons form named local modules, and that every codon assigned to those modules is either directly predictable by the leave-one-codon-out closure rule or sits on the boundary needed by that rule.

\paragraph{Finite codon set.}
The recovered set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its observed members are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same set is identified as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  In this chapter the word ``module'' refers only to a finite partition of this $13$-codon set by label-neighborhood behavior.  It does not refer to a biochemical module, a translation apparatus, or a protein-domain unit.

\paragraph{Module assignment.}
The module classification is complete at the finite-row level.  The assigned count is $13$, the declared $R$ size is $13$, and the lists of missing, extra, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The six observed modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every codon in $R$ is accounted for exactly once by the displayed module list.  The classification is therefore a finite certificate of local coverage, not a statement about how organisms realize or regulate these labels.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
The direct leave-one-codon-out run reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, and $8$ high-coverage closure codons.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, above the stated threshold.  The module table agrees with that outcome: every module contains only codons that are predictable or boundary-supported in the local coordinate computation.  The relevant finite statement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R|&=13,\\
13&=8+5,\\
\mathrm{assigned}(R)&=13,\\
\mathrm{unassigned}(R)&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the chapter's main finite result.  It says that the local codon tile has no leftover codon after module classification and boundary accounting.

\paragraph{Code-table comparison.}
A separate leave-one-code-out comparison evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The observed hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the above-baseline hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family.  These numbers support the limited reading that the local coordinate rule recovers code-label neighborhoods unusually well relative to the recorded baseline, while still depending on a curated standard-code table for the biological labels themselves.

\paragraph{Shape and null checks.}
The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries.  It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  The corresponding values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,exact}}&=0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class,lower\ tail}}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These checks make the module result less likely to be a trivial artifact of preserving amino-acid class sizes, but they do not turn the coordinate pattern into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Spectral signature.}
The associated spectrum reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The cubic residual, also reported as the radial polynomial value, is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical certificate is effectively at the recorded tolerance scale.  This spectral information is used only as an internal finite diagnostic of the codon-coordinate object.  It is not evidence of folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or evolutionary necessity.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code codon table.  That contact supplies codon-to-amino-acid and codon-to-stop labels.  It supports code-layer readback and local label-split checks.  It does not observe translation events, protein structures, thermodynamic admissibility, organism-level function, or a universal biological law.  Consequently, amino-acid label sequences obtained from this table remain code labels.  They are not folded proteins and are not structure measurements.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The permitted conclusion is therefore sharply bounded.  The window-six codon cube exposes a finite local tile in which the $13$ recovered codons are completely classified into the six modules displayed above, and each module is covered by predictable or boundary codons.  No claim follows here about translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Any such stronger claim would require a separate contact that directly measures the stronger layer.
