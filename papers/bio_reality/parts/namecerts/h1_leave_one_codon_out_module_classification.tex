\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table after it has been placed on a six-bit codon display. The coordinate carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon read as a finite six-coordinate word over the binary expansion of the three nucleotide positions. The contact used in this record is only the curated standard-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. It therefore supports code-layer label readback and local split tests, but it does not by itself observe translation, folded molecular structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, a mechanism of realization, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Residual row set.}
The residual codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$. The verified row list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation identifies $R$ as a punctured-cube row pattern with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite display is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it carries a local tile shape with thirteen vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional faces inside the six-bit coordinate cube.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The shape test against the sampled comparison class used $N=2000$ sampled tables with seed $42$. It made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries. In that run, exact shape agreement occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
A high-overlap criterion with Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It observed $0$ exact-shape matches. Its lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, with observed $M$-size $20$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite counts support the claim that the observed local pattern is not a routine outcome of the tested row-preserving or amino-acid-class-preserving shuffles. They do not license promotion beyond the code-label layer, because the comparison data still concern codon-label tables rather than ribosomal realization, molecular dynamics, folding, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
Across the leave-one-code-out test, $23$ genetic-code tables supplied $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary rows was $0.9841269841269841$. The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is reported as the hit rate above baseline. By family, the hit rate above baseline was $1.0$ for the bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family, over $5$ families total. These numbers make the local codon tile useful as a code-read classifier, but they still remain finite table behavior.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
The leave-one-codon-out row reports $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases. The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison also records $1.0$. In this setting, a codon is not being predicted as a physical molecule or as a functional residue in a folded protein. It is being recovered as a label-bearing row of the curated standard-code table under the specified local coordinate and boundary tests.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module classification is complete: the residual set has $R$-size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons are all empty. The verified partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{\texttt{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{\texttt{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has at least one row classified through the predictable-or-boundary route, and together the six modules exhaust the thirteen codons in $R$. The conclusion is therefore a finite module statement: the leave-one-codon-out residual rows decompose into six named local modules without leftovers.

\paragraph{Readback boundary.}
The scientific readback is deliberately narrow. The computation finds a six-bit codon-coordinate pattern whose residual row set has $13$ codons, punctured-cube incidence data $(13,16,4)$, a stable spectral scale, significant finite null behavior, strong leave-one-code-out recovery, perfect leave-one-codon-out recovery in the reported run, and a complete six-module classification. The only external contact here is the curated standard genetic-code table. Consequently, the result may be stated as a code-read result about local codon labels and boundary modules. No claim about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this chapter unless a separate observation at that higher layer is supplied.
