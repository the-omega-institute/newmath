\paragraph{Claim.}
The record \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_codon\_out.module\_classification} concerns a window-six codon coordinate experiment on the standard-code label table. The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, read as a six-bit display of codon positions together with a curated codon-to-label assignment. The finite observation is not a mechanism of translation. It is a code-layer classification statement: the codons in the distinguished region $R$ can be grouped into named local modules, and every such module contains only codons accounted for by the leave-one-codon predictable-or-boundary criterion. The biological object touched by the row is therefore the standard-code codon table at the label-readout layer, not a protein, fold, biochemical pathway, or organism-level law.

\paragraph{Finite carrier and distinguished region.}
The distinguished set has cardinality $13$. The row reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube object with face-vector $(13,16,4)$. This supplies the finite local shape used by the later module classification. It does not say that the codon table is forced by cube geometry; it says that this particular curated label table, when placed in the window-six coordinate display, exposes a small boundary-like region with these thirteen codons.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module row partitions the thirteen codons of $R$ without omissions or extras. The completeness check reports $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, $extra\_module\_codons=\emptyset$, $missing\_from\_modules=\emptyset$, and $unassigned\_codons=\emptyset$. The named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\

\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central finite assertion of the chapter. Every codon in the distinguished region is assigned to exactly one of these six modules, and every listed module is covered by the predictable-or-boundary condition supplied by the leave-one-codon row. The grouping is therefore a compact description of how the local tile boundary decomposes in the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table. Its observed content is the assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. This contact can test the code-read layer and the local split of codon labels across the tile, but it cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, or biological function. The record is therefore intentionally narrow: the standard table supplies label readback, and the window-six coordinate system supplies the finite local geometry in which the module statement is evaluated.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
No conclusion in this chapter promotes the local codon tile into a biological law. No translation process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility condition, functional phenotype, universal mechanism, or cross-organism structural claim follows from the rows above. Amino-acid label sequences remain labels in a code table unless a separate observation supplies molecular structure or function. In particular, CUN/UUR wording, tRNA-Leu notation, or a cross-organism memory token cannot convert this code-layer module classification into evidence about folding or biochemical action. The finite result is exactly the following: within the curated standard-code table, the thirteen-codon region $R$ forms a punctured-cube-like local boundary, has the recorded spectral and sampling statistics, and admits the six-module predictable-or-boundary classification with complete assignment of all thirteen codons.
