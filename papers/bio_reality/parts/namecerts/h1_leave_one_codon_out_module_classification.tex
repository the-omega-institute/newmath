\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}\label{sec:bior-eality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table after it has been read as a finite labelled subset of the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The six coordinates are not treated as a biochemical mechanism.  They are a finite display in which a codon can be placed at a cube vertex, its local faces can be counted, and its label can be compared with the curated amino-acid or stop assignment.  The reality contact is therefore narrow: the table supplies codon-to-label data, while the cube supplies a coordinate frame for finite probes.

The residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its verified codon list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies $R$ as the punctured-cube residue with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the local finite shape has $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ square faces in the induced residue geometry.  This is the geometric object whose label behavior is being classified; it is not a claim about translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, function, organismal mechanism, or universality.

The radial cubic check returns $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at tolerance for the reported polynomial constraint.  These numbers are used only as finite diagnostics of the displayed codon geometry.  They do not promote the coordinate picture into a biological law.

The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled comparators with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, so the exact empirical probability was
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
Under the amino-acid-class-preserving comparator, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$.  The corresponding finite probabilities were $p=0.0$ for exact shape in the sampled batch and $p=0.0035$ for the lower-tail event.  These tests support the statement that the observed cube residue is not a common outcome of the tested finite shuffles.  They still remain code-layer probes of a labelled table.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon readback.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family breakdown contained $5$ families, with above-baseline hit rates
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate} &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The leave-one-codon-out experiment reported $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  Its threshold comparison was also $1.0$.  The relevant interpretation is modest: after one codon is withheld, the finite coordinate-and-label pattern often places the missing codon back into a predictable or boundary-controlled position.  That is a code-read result, not a translation experiment.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module classification is complete on the residual set.  The verified bookkeeping reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
|R| &= 13,\\
\mathrm{assigned\_count} &= 13,\\
\mathrm{missing\_from\_modules} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{extra\_module\_codons} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{unassigned\_codons} &= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has at least one member covered by the predictable-or-boundary condition.  Since the module list has no missing, extra, or unassigned codons, the classification accounts for all $13$ elements of $R$ exactly once.  The result is therefore a finite classification theorem about the standard-code label table as seen through the window-six coordinate display: the residual codons decompose into six named local modules, and each module touches the recoverable or boundary-controlled part of the leave-one-codon-out probe.

\paragraph{Local biological scope.}
The curated standard-code table is the only biological contact used here.  It observes that codons have amino-acid or stop labels.  It does not observe ribosomal realization, molecular kinetics, folded protein structure, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal function, or a universal generative rule.  Consequently the chapter records a local label-table phenomenon: window-six geometry exposes a boundary-tile organization of the residual codons, and the leave-one-codon-out module classifier is complete on the finite row data.  Any stronger biological assertion would require an additional contact whose measurements are about that stronger layer.
