\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite setting.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with codons read as finite coordinate words and labels supplied by a curated standard-code table.  The question is not whether the cube explains biology as a law.  The question is narrower: after a local window-six split has selected the boundary set $R$, do the leave-one-codon probes fall into a complete finite module classification, and does every module contain codons that are either predictable from the local closure test or lie on the boundary route?

The verified boundary set has exactly thirteen codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite count check records $|R|=13$, and the punctured-cube check records the same set with face vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the local object is not an informal visual pattern: it is a finite row of codons, a row count, and a small cubical incidence signature.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape controls.}
The same finite run records a spectral check for the local split.  The two displayed eigenvalue scales are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is also the recorded cubic-polynomial residual.  This supports the claim that the finite computation is internally consistent at numerical tolerance, but it does not change the biological scope of the observation.

The shape-control run used $N=2000$ random samples with seed $42$.  It found one exact shape match, giving $p=0.0005$, and twenty-five samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The distinct-sample accounting is also finite: $57377$ attempts were needed, with $55377$ duplicate retries.  A second amino-acid-class-preserving null run, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, recorded observed $|M|=20$, zero exact matches, and seven lower-tail samples at or below the observed $M$ size.  Its reported probabilities were $p=0.0$ for exact shape and $p=0.0035$ for the lower-tail count.  The data therefore support a local non-randomness statement for this coordinate representation, while still leaving all higher biological layers outside the tested contact.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The leave-one-codon-out module packet assigns all thirteen codons of $R$ to six named modules.  The complete classification is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row-level completion check records $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons, and no unassigned codons.  Hence the module partition is exhaustive for the observed boundary set.  The additional module condition says that each module has a predictable-or-boundary representative, so no module is left as a purely decorative bin after the leave-one-codon-out test.

\paragraph{Leave-one performance.}
The codon-level leave-one run reports zero anchors, five boundary codons, eight predictable codons, eight high-coverage closure cases, and recovery rate $1.0$.  In symbols, the recorded row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{anchor\_count}&=0,\\
\mathrm{boundary\_count}&=5,\\
\mathrm{predictable\_count}&=8,\\
\mathrm{closure\_coverage\_high}&=8,\\
\mathrm{recovery\_rate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the immediate finite support for the module-classification claim: the thirteen boundary codons are not merely listed, but are recovered through a leave-one procedure that separates boundary support from predictable closure support.

The broader leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  It recorded baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate in $M-T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$.  The same value, $0.9841269841269841$, is reported as the hit rate above baseline.  Family-level rows give five families.  Their above-baseline rates were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families.  These rows place the module result in a wider code-table perturbation setting without promoting the window-six pattern beyond code-label readback.

\paragraph{Biological contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is strong enough to test code-layer label readback and local codon-tile label splits.  It is not an observation of translation machinery, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  In particular, amino-acid label sequence is not a folded structure, and a CUN/UUR or tRNA-Leu wording route does not by itself supply a structural experiment.

The resulting claim is therefore deliberately local.  The finite rows support the statement that the window-six representation exposes a complete leave-one-codon-out module classification for the thirteen-codon boundary set $R$, with every listed module covered by predictable or boundary behavior.  They do not license any claim that DNA must follow this geometry, that the geometry performs translation, that it predicts folds, that it establishes physical viability, or that it explains biological function.  Crossing any of those boundaries would require a separate contact at the corresponding layer.
