\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The finite observation in this chapter is a code-layer statement about the standard genetic-code table after one codon is held out. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read through the usual codon labels $A,C,G,U$ and then through the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The claim is not that the cube geometry explains translation. It is that the finite rows supplied here organize the thirteen distinguished codons into six named local modules, with no residue left outside the module accounting.

The codon set detected by the row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded cardinality is $|R|=13$. The same row reports that this set is the punctured-cube boundary object used by the local tile calculation, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is a finite thirteen-vertex pattern with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces, not an unrestricted biological principle.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module-classification row assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ to six modules. The assignment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness counters are exact: $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the module list, and no unassigned codons. In finite-row terms,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{extra\_module\_codons}&=\varnothing,\\
\mathrm{missing\_from\_modules}&=\varnothing,\\
\mathrm{unassigned\_codons}&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each listed module contains at least one codon marked predictable or boundary by the leave-one-codon-out calculation. This is the local classification content: the thirteen codon positions do not merely appear as isolated names, but are exhausted by a small family of boundary-sensitive label classes.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model controls.}
The spectral row records three numerical invariants for the local pattern:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated radial cubic check has value $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the radial-polynomial and cubic-polynomial residuals. At the precision of the row, this is a numerical zero check for the recorded spectral relation. It gives a finite algebraic consistency check on the local object; it does not promote the object to a mechanism of translation or to a law of molecular behavior.

The shape-significance row compares the observed local shape against $N=2000$ sampled alternatives using seed $42$. It records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, with $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard score at least $0.9$. The corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid-class-preserving null model also uses $N=2000$ and seed $42$. It records observed $M$-size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$ at or below the observed $M$-size. The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact\ shape}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
Together these controls make the finite pattern difficult to dismiss as a generic random relabeling artifact in the recorded sampling regimes. They still remain controls for a code-readback calculation, not observations of a folded structure or biological function.

\paragraph{Reality contact and boundary.}
The empirical contact used here is a curated standard genetic-code table. That contact observes only that codons have amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code. It can test code-level readback and local codon-tile label splits. It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, function realization, universal mechanism, or any statement that a protein fold follows from an amino-acid label sequence.

Consequently the accepted content of the chapter is deliberately narrow. The row supports the statement that the thirteen codons in $R$ are completely classified into the six displayed modules and that every module has predictable-or-boundary support under the leave-one-codon-out computation. No claim follows here about ribosomal realization, molecular dynamics, admissible chemistry, protein structure, organismal fitness, or universality across biological systems unless another contact, designed for that higher layer, is supplied separately.
