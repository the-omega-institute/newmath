\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification in the standard-code tile}
\label{sec:bior-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record concerns the standard genetic-code label table viewed through a six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  The object under test is not a molecular mechanism; it is the finite codon-to-label readout supplied by the curated standard-code table.  The working distinction is between a local tile face in the six-bit codon display, a singleton label corner, and a three-point label class.  The observed exceptional set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite set is reported as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row is not merely a count: it records a specific local boundary shape inside $Q_6$, together with the complete list of codons occupying that shape.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out classification divides the thirteen codons of $R$ into six named modules, and the classification check reports no missing codons, no extra codons, and no unassigned codons.  The complete partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys} &= \{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met} &= \{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp} &= \{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The module audit gives $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{missing\_from\_modules} &= \varnothing,\\
\mathrm{extra\_module\_codons} &= \varnothing,\\
\mathrm{unassigned\_codons} &= \varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$
For this finite row, every module has either a predictable member or a boundary member.  The scientific content is therefore a local classification fact: the thirteen-codon punctured tile is exhausted by six biologically named codon families, and the leave-one-codon-out probe does not leave a residue outside those families.

\paragraph{Recovery and code-table readout.}
The leave-one-codon-out probe reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, $0$ anchor codons, and $8$ high-coverage closure codons.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, with the above-threshold recovery flag also equal to $1.0$.  At the related code-table level, $23$ code tables were evaluated against $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The reported excess over baseline is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9841269841269841 - 0.19821739130434785 &= 0.7859095928226363.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite code-read statement: when codons or code-table rows are held out, the local window-six construction recovers the observed label-side pattern at a rate far above the baseline reported for the same row.

\paragraph{Family-level replication.}
The family split contains $5$ families.  The reported hit-rate-above-baseline values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast} &= 0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial} &= 1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate} &= 1.0.

$$
The weakest of these five family rows is still above $0.8$, and four of the five are exactly $1.0$.  This supports the interpretation that the local module classification is not a single accidental label placement inside one row of the table.  It is still a code-layer result: the test compares codon labels and family partitions, not ribosomal realization or protein behavior.

\paragraph{Shape and spectral checks.}
The shape-significance null run used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  It required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and incurred $55377$ duplicate retries.  The exact shape appeared once, giving $p=0.0005$, and the high-overlap threshold $J\geq 0.9$ appeared $25$ times, giving $p=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with the same $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$.  Hence the reported probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class, lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial check, so the finite spectral computation is numerically closed to the stated tolerance.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The data justify the following narrow reading.  In the standard-code table, the six-coordinate codon display isolates a thirteen-codon punctured local tile; that tile decomposes completely into six named modules; and leave-one-codon-out tests recover predictable-or-boundary membership across all modules.  The curated table supplies the contact with observed labels, while the coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation entries describe the finite structure found around those labels.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No claim about translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows from this row.  Codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop readback is a label-layer observation.  It does not observe ribosome dynamics, tRNA availability, organism-specific regulation, folding trajectories, thermodynamic admissibility, or downstream phenotype.  Any promotion from this local code-read result to one of those higher layers requires a separate empirical contact of the appropriate kind.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences are not folded proteins, and a local window-six boundary is not a law of biology.
