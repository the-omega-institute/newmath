\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The finite record attached to \texttt{h1.leave\_one\_codon\_out.module\_classification} concerns a code-layer pattern in the standard-code codon table. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, read as a binary display of codon positions. The relevant distinctions are local: a codon may sit on a tile face, may occupy a singleton label corner, or may belong to a three-point label class. These distinctions are organized by coordinates, closure, spectrum, and relation data, then compared with the curated standard-code label table. The comparison is deliberately modest: it tests codon-to-label readback and local boundary structure only.

\paragraph{Finite residual set.}
The residual codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified row it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite row records that this set has the punctured-cube face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the tested object is not an unconstrained codon list. It is a thirteen-point residual subset whose incidence profile supplies a concrete local tile shape inside the six-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The associated spectral data record the values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic check leaves residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the cubic-polynomial check. This is small at the precision of the finite computation and functions here as numerical consistency evidence for the coordinate-and-closure calculation. It is not a biological mechanism; it is a reproducible numerical signature of the finite codon display used in the test.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ sampled null trials with seed $42$. The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005,
\end{aligned}
$$
and it found $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A separate amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, recorded observed $M$-size $20$, no exact-shape matches, and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size. The corresponding finite-row values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers say that the local residual shape is rare under the stated finite nulls. They do not say that the geometry is a law of molecular biology.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon checks.}
The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets. It recorded a baseline hit rate of $0.19821739130434785$, a hit rate in $M-T$ of $0.9365079365079365$, and a hit rate in $M-T$ plus boundary of $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate above baseline was also $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level breakdown used $5$ families and gave above-baseline rates $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. The leave-one-codon-out row recorded $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, $8$ predictable cases, and recovery rate $1.0$. These checks support the narrow reading that, once the finite codon coordinate system and curated labels are fixed, local residual positions are strongly constrained by the tested closure-and-boundary rule.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module-classification row assigns all $13$ codons of $R$ into six named modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
AGR &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
AR_{\mathrm{Lys}} &= \{AAA\},\\
CUN &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
IleMet &= \{AUA\},\\
SSN &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
StopTrp &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The classification row is complete in the finite sense: $R$-size is $13$, assigned count is $13$, the extra-module-codon list is empty, the missing-from-modules list is empty, and the unassigned-codon list is empty. Moreover, every listed module contains at least one codon classified through the predictable-or-boundary route. This is the central finite observation for the claim: the leave-one-codon-out residual set is not merely counted; it is partitioned into biologically named code modules without leftover codons under the recorded local rule.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used here is the curated standard-code table, treated as a codon-label readback source. It supplies assignments from codons to amino-acid or stop labels. It does not supply a translation process, a folded structure, a physical-admissibility experiment, a functional assay, or a universal evolutionary principle. Consequently, the observed alignment is a code-layer statement: the window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-and-module pattern whose labels are checked against the curated standard code.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The result must not be promoted beyond its contact. From this row alone one may say that the finite codon cube representation, the thirteen-codon residual set, the punctured-cube incidence data, the spectral values, the null comparisons, and the six-module classification jointly support a local code-read pattern. One may not infer that translation has been realized, that a protein fold has been predicted, that the pattern is physically admissible in a cell, that it has biological function, that it provides a mechanism, or that it is universal across life. Each such stronger statement would require a separate empirical contact aimed at that layer.
