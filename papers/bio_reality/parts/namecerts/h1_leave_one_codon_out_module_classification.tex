\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}

The contact is therefore a code-label contact: it can check codon-label readback and local label splits, but it does not observe translation as a realized cellular process, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Null comparisons and row specificity.}
The shape-significance experiment used $N=2000$ sampled rows with seed $42$.  It reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, with $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches at Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The corresponding empirical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{exact}&=0.0005,\\
p_{Jaccard\geq 0.9}&=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, records observed $M$-size $20$, exact-match count $0$, and lower-tail count $7$.  Its empirical values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{class\ exact}&=0.0,\\
p_{class\ lower\ tail}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
The available row therefore has finite evidence against two simple random explanations: exact shape agreement is rare in the first sampling regime, and the amino-acid-class-preserving comparison gives no exact matches in the recorded sample.  This does not establish necessity; it only says that the recorded module row is a nontrivial finite object under the supplied null tests.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The chapter's conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The curated standard-code table supplies codon-to-label assignments, and the six-coordinate display supplies a finite geometry in which a thirteen-codon row has a complete six-module classification.  The data support a code-layer statement: in this row, each codon is assigned to one of the recorded modules, and each module is accounted for by predictable-or-boundary status under the leave-one-codon-out test.  The data do not show that cells implement the coordinate geometry, that translation is caused by the local tile, that a label sequence folds into a structure, that the sequence is physically admissible, that it has a function, or that the pattern is universal across biology.  Any such statement would require a separate contact measuring that higher layer directly.  Within the present finite record, the result is a local codon-label classification theorem for the standard-code table and nothing stronger.
