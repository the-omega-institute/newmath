\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The experiment is carried out on the six-bit codon cube, where each standard-code codon is represented as a finite coordinate row and the readout is the curated standard codon-to-label table.  The distinguished residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a finite cubical object, this residual set has face data $(13,16,4)$: thirteen vertices, sixteen local edges, and four local two-faces.  The point of the row is not that this shape is a biological law.  It is that the standard code, when restricted to the leave-one-codon window-six residual coordinate, exposes a small finite tile whose label classes can be tested against the curated code table.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model scale.}
The same finite object is accompanied by a numerical spectral signature.  The measured values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the radial cubic check returning $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, effectively zero at the recorded tolerance.  Against $2000$ sampled comparison shapes, only one exact shape match occurred, giving $p=0.0005$; allowing Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$ produced $25$ matches and $p=0.0125$.  The sampling record reports $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the small p-values are not being read as an exhaustive biological proof, but as finite evidence that this coordinate shape is uncommon under the stated comparison procedure.  In the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, the observed $M$ size is $20$; among $2000$ samples there were no exact shape matches, and only $7$ samples lay in the lower tail at or below the observed size, giving lower-tail $p=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Module partition of the residual codons.}
The module-classification row is complete: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the recorded lists of extra module codons, missing residual codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The resulting module partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module contains at least one codon recorded as predictable or boundary-controlled by the leave-one-codon procedure.  This matters because the row is not merely a count of recovered codons; it also records that the whole residual region is covered by named biochemical-label modules without leftover codons.  The classification therefore reads as a finite local organization of the standard-code labels around the residual window.

\paragraph{Leave-one recovery evidence.}
The leave-one-codon row records $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, and no anchors, with closure coverage high at $8$ and recovery rate $1.0$.  The leave-one-code comparison supplies a broader table-level check: $23$ code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and adding the boundary raises it to $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit rate above baseline is $0.8333333333333334$ for the alternative-yeast family and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families, across $5$ recorded families.  These figures support the claim that the module split is not an isolated visual coincidence of the standard table alone; it persists as a finite leave-one readout pattern under the recorded code-family comparisons.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only biological contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, namely the finite assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is enough to test code-layer label readback and local tile splitting, but it is not a contact for translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality.  In particular, an amino-acid label sequence obtained from the table is not a folded protein, and a local codon-tile boundary is not evidence that DNA is forced to obey this coordinate geometry as a biological necessity.  Any claim about translation, structure, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universal biological law would require a separate observation at that layer.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
For the claim $h1.leave\_one\_codon\_out.module\_classification$, the finite-row evidence is internally ready at the code-read layer.  The residual codon set is exactly identified, its cubical face data and spectral scale are recorded, its module classification assigns all $13$ residual codons with no extras or omissions, and its recovery statistics agree with the boundary-or-predictable interpretation.  The scientifically appropriate reading is therefore narrow but nontrivial: window-six codon coordinates expose a local module structure in the curated standard-code label table, while all higher biological interpretations remain outside the present contact.
