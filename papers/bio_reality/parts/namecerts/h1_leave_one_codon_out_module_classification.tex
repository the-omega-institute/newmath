\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}
The record is a code-read experiment on the standard genetic-code table, represented as a six-bit codon cube $Q_6$ with codons read as finite coordinate words.  The object under test is not translation dynamics and not protein structure; it is the codon-to-label table after one codon or one code-row is withheld.  The relevant internal distinctions are coordinate position, closure membership, spectral scale, and label relation.  In this finite packet the exceptional region $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube computation reports the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the row is not being summarized by a single codon count alone: the local shape contains vertices, edges, and square faces inside the codon cube.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out classifier assigns all $13$ codons in $R$ to named modules, with no surplus and no omission.  The completed module accounting is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{size}&=13,\\
\operatorname{assigned}&=13,\\
\operatorname{extra}&=\varnothing,\\
\operatorname{missing}&=\varnothing,\\
\operatorname{unassigned}&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$
The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{AGR}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\operatorname{AR\_Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\operatorname{CUN}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\operatorname{Ile\_Met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\operatorname{SSN}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\operatorname{Stop\_Trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module contains at least one codon class marked predictable or boundary-bearing by the finite classifier.  This is the main row-level conclusion: the local codon tile does not merely collect isolated codons, but covers the full exceptional set by biologically named code-read modules.

\paragraph{Spectral scale.}
The same finite row includes a spectral diagnostic.  The observed values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is reported as $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the corresponding cubic-polynomial check gives the same residual.  At the precision of the run, the spectral constraint is therefore numerically closed for this finite table.  These numbers are used only as code-layer coordinates: they help identify the local tile and its boundary behavior, but they do not by themselves assert biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape significance against finite nulls.}
The shape test used $N=2000$ null samples with seed $42$.  The run required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, indicating that the finite null space was being sampled under a distinctness constraint rather than by accepting repeated tables.  Exact shape agreement occurred once, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{exact}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
High-overlap agreement at Jaccard score at least $0.9$ occurred $25$ times, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
A second amino-acid-class-preserving null test also used $N=2000$ and seed $42$.  It reported observed $|M|=20$, exact-match count $0$, and $7$ lower-tail samples no larger than the observed $M$ size.  Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{aa,exact}&=0,\\
p_{aa,lower}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These finite nulls support the claim that the local tile is not a routine outcome of the tested randomizations.  They still remain code-table tests, not measurements of translation, folding, or cellular function.

\paragraph{Leave-one performance.}
For leave-one-code-out evaluation, $23$ code tables and $63$ targets were evaluated.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The reported hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ together with boundary codons was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the number of families was $5$, with above-baseline hit rates
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{alternative\_yeast}&=0.8333333333333334,\\
\operatorname{bacterial\_plastid}&=1.0,\\
\operatorname{candidate\_other}&=1.0,\\
\operatorname{mitochondrial}&=1.0,\\
\operatorname{nuclear\_ciliate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
For leave-one-codon-out evaluation, the classifier reported anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The threshold comparison also returned $1.0$.  The module classification row is therefore consistent with the broader leave-one behavior: missing codon labels are recovered through local boundary and closure structure rather than through a claimed global biological rule.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The reality contact is the curated standard-code table, whose observation is only that codons are assigned amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for checking the local split described above.  It is not evidence that DNA must obey this coordinate geometry as a biological necessity, and it is not evidence for protein realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a translation mechanism, or universality across organisms.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate contact at the relevant layer, for example a translation assay for translation, a structural experiment for folding, a physical model for admissibility, or a functional assay for biological role.  Within the present finite row, the correct conclusion is narrower: the six-bit codon representation exposes a statistically nontrivial local boundary tile, and the standard-code label table supplies the external labels needed to read that tile at the code layer only.
