\paragraph{Finite codon coordinate record.}
This record studies a standard-code codon table through the window-six codon cube $Q_6$.  The biological contact is a curated codon-to-label table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid or stop label, and nothing in the contact itself observes translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or biological universality.  The finite computation isolates a distinguished codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality check gives $|R|=13$.  The punctured-cube check gives the same thirteen codons and records the face-count vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the coordinate record is not merely a list of labels; it also carries a local combinatorial shape in the six-bit cube.

The sampling log also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, so the small exact-shape count is reported together with the finite search burden rather than as an unqualified law.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The resulting claim is therefore narrow.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a local boundary-tile pattern for the standard-code label table, with a thirteen-codon punctured-cube set, a closed module assignment, spectral values in tolerance, and nontrivial finite null comparisons.  The table contact reads labels only.
