\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification for the window-six codon tile}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
This record concerns the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon display.  The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the only biological readback used here is the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The observed exceptional region $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies the punctured-cube face data by the $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite object under discussion is not an unrestricted codon-table narrative: it is the particular local tile carried by these thirteen codons, together with the label classes read from the standard code.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out module classification assigns all thirteen codons of $R$ and leaves no residue.  The assigned modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\},& AR{\_}Lys&=\{AAA\},& CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
Ile{\_}Met&=\{AUA\},& SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\},& Stop{\_}Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit row reports $R{\_}size=13$, $assigned{\_}count=13$, $extra{\_}module{\_}codons=\varnothing$, $missing{\_}from{\_}modules=\varnothing$, and $unassigned{\_}codons=\varnothing$.  It also reports that every listed module contains a codon that is either predictable by the leave-one-codon-out procedure or lies on the boundary class detected by the local tile.  This is the chapter's main finite claim: the local window-six region has a complete module-level accounting, not merely a codon-by-codon hit list.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out readback.}
The code-level leave-one-out rows give two complementary tests.  Across alternative code tables, $23$ tables were evaluated against $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, whereas the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  At the codon level, the leave-one-codon-out row gives anchor count $0$, boundary count $5$, high closure coverage $8$, predictable count $8$, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The recovery rate is therefore exactly at the reported threshold-satisfying value:
$$
\begin{aligned}
recovery=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Grouped by code-table family, the finite row reports $5$ families.  The family hit rates above baseline are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, and $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families.  The module classification should therefore be read as a code-read phenomenon with strong finite recovery, rather than as a claim about a downstream biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table.  That contact observes codon-to-label assignments; it does not observe ribosomal translation as a process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanistic law.  Consequently the local tile may be used to discuss standard-code label readback and local boundary splitting, but it does not license promotion to translation, folding, structure, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  A separate experiment or independently curated contact would be required before any such higher-layer assertion could enter the paper.  In particular, amino-acid label sequences obtained from the table are still code-layer objects; they are not folded structures and they are not functional proteins.
