\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The claim tested here is that a window-six representation of the standard-code codon table exposes a finite local module structure on the exceptional region, while the amino-acid and stop labels themselves remain supplied by the curated codon table. The carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, with codons displayed as six-bit words. The relevant distinctions are the local tile face, the singleton label corner, and the three-point label class. The internal record uses coordinate, closure, spectrum, and relation data, and its readout is only the standard-code label table. It does not assert that the geometry of $Q_6$ is a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Exceptional codon region.}
The finite region under classification has cardinality $13$. The verified codon list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run identifies this region as a punctured-cube shape with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite object being analyzed is not an arbitrary list of labels: it is a thirteen-vertex codon subset with sixteen local edges and four two-dimensional faces in the tested cube geometry.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out module classification assigns all thirteen codons in $R$ to six named modules and leaves no residue. The verified partition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{AGR} &= \{AGA,AGG\},\\
R_{AR\_LYS} &= \{AAA\},\\
R_{CUN} &= \{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
R_{ILE\_MET} &= \{AUA\},\\
R_{SSN} &= \{UCA,UUA\},\\
R_{STOP\_TRP} &= \{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness check reports $R\_size=13$ and $assigned\_count=13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons. Each module contains at least one codon classified as predictable or boundary-supported under the leave-one-codon-out test. This is the finite content of the classification claim: the exceptional codon region is fully covered by the six displayed modules, and the coverage is exact at the row level.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The codon-level deletion probe reports $predictable\_count=8$, $boundary\_count=5$, and $anchor\_count=0$. The high-coverage closure count is $8$, and the reported recovery rate is $1.0$, meeting the stated recovery threshold. In finite terms, the thirteen codons split into eight closure-predictable cases and five boundary cases, with no anchor-only codons required for this packet. This supports the narrower reading that the local cube representation can organize the exceptional region into recoverable or boundary-detected rows.

\paragraph{Cross-table stress check.}
A separate leave-one-code-out stress test evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The same rate, $0.9841269841269841$, was obtained for the $M-T$ plus boundary criterion, and the rate inside $M-T$ alone was $0.9365079365079365$. By family, five table families were reported. Their hit rates above baseline were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast. These numbers are used here only as stability checks for code-label readout across curated table variants, not as evidence of a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Shape and null comparisons.}
The shape-significance probe used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$. It reported one exact shape match, twenty-five matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, $p\_{exact}=0.0005$, and $p\_{Jaccard\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The distinct-sample attempts were $57377$, with $55377$ duplicate retries. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, the observed $M$ size was $20$; the exact-match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, the exact-shape $p$-value was $0.0$, and the lower-tail $p$-value was $0.0035$ for $N=2000$ with seed $42$. These finite comparisons indicate that the tested geometry is not typical under the sampled null procedures, while still leaving all biological interpretation constrained to the measured code-label layer.

\paragraph{Spectral record.}
The spectrum run gives the scalar values
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported radial polynomial value is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the cubic polynomial residual is the same value, and the spectral-radius entry in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$. These numbers identify a stable numerical signature for the finite codon geometry, but the signature is still a code-table signature rather than a claim about protein realization.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The only external contact used by this packet is the curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels. That contact has codon-label resolution. It can test code-layer readback and local codon-tile label splits. It cannot test translation realization, structural order, physical admissibility, folded structure, function realization, or any universal biological law. Therefore the module classification is reality-bound only in the sense that its labels are checked against the curated codon table.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The accepted conclusion is that the thirteen-codon exceptional region in the six-bit codon cube is exactly partitioned into the six displayed modules, and that the leave-one-codon-out rows are covered by predictable or boundary-supported behavior. No stronger biological conclusion follows from these rows. In particular, this chapter does not infer translation dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or a global law of DNA from the local tile geometry. Any such claim would require a separate experimental or curated contact at the corresponding biological layer.
