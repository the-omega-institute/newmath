\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite code-layer analysis on the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is displayed as a six-bit object through the usual two-bit nucleotide expansion, and the only biological readback used here is the curated standard-code assignment from codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The distinguished residual set is the thirteen-codon subset
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit records $|R|=13$.  In the punctured-cube computation the same codon list has face-vector data $(13,16,4)$, so the object being classified is not an informal collection of genetic-code exceptions but a concrete finite subcomplex inside the codon-coordinate display.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The associated spectral row reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic check has residual $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, which is numerically at the recorded tolerance scale for the computation.  The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled controls with seed $42$.  It observed $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$.  The distinct-sample log also matters: the run needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the quoted probabilities are attached to a highly constrained finite sampling space rather than to a loose visual comparison.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed module size was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{class, exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{class, lower}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module classification is complete on the thirteen-codon residual set.  The six recorded modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\

\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row gives $R_{\mathrm{size}}=13$ and assigned count $13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Thus every codon in $R$ is accounted for exactly once by this module partition.  The row also states that every module has at least predictable or boundary support: no module is left as a merely named residue class.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The only external contact used in this chapter is the curated standard-code table, treated as a codon-label source.  It licenses statements about label readback, local tile faces, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  It does not observe translation in a cell, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, or a universal biological mechanism.  Consequently the chapter's positive conclusion is deliberately narrow: the finite row supports a complete module classification of the thirteen-codon residual set inside the window-six codon cube, with strong local recovery and nontrivial null-model separation.  Any assertion about translation realization, protein folding, structural order, physical feasibility, function, or universality would require an additional contact of the appropriate kind and is not supplied by these rows.
