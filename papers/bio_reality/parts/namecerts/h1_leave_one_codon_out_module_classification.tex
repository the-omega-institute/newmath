\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite codon-coordinate object.}
The record studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed only at the code-label layer.  The finite carrier is the six-coordinate codon cube, with each codon displayed as a word over the four nucleotide letters and treated as a finite coordinate address.  The biological contact is the curated standard-code table assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The claim does not identify this coordinate object with translation chemistry, ribosomal realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  It asks a narrower question: after one codon is held out from the standard-code label table, do the remaining local coordinates classify the exceptional codons into a small set of predictable or boundary modules?

\paragraph{Exceptional set.}
The finite residual set $R$ has cardinality $13$.  Its elements are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same set is also observed as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the record is not merely a count: it contains $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ local two-dimensional faces in the finite codon-coordinate complex.  This is the row-level geometric datum on which the module classification rests.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out module row gives a complete assignment of the $13$ codons in $R$.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{CUN} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{SSN} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completion audit reports $R\_size=13$ and $assigned\_count=13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Therefore the classification is exact at the finite-row level: every codon in the exceptional set belongs to one of the six named modules, and the union of the modules is precisely $R$.

\paragraph{Predictable-or-boundary status.}
The decisive row is the map stating that every module has predictable or boundary content.  The AGR module contains $AGA$ and $AGG$; the AR-Lys module contains $AAA$; the CUN module contains $CUA, CUC, CUG,$ and $CUU$; the Ile-Met module contains $AUA$; the SSN module contains $UCA$ and $UUA$; and the Stop-Trp module contains $UAA, UAG,$ and $UGA$.  Since these six modules cover all $13$ codons in $R$, the leave-one-codon-out classification has no residual exceptional codon outside the predictable-or-boundary ledger.
