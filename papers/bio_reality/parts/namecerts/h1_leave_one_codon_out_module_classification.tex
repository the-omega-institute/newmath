\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Question.}
The finite object studied here is the standard-code codon table read through a six-bit codon coordinate system. The biological input is only the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. The mathematical question is whether the codons singled out by the local window-six construction form a classified boundary set whose members are accounted for by named label modules when one codon is withheld. This is a code-layer question: it concerns codon labels and finite coordinate neighborhoods, not ribosomal translation, molecular folding, thermodynamic admissibility, organismal function, mechanism, or biological universality.

\paragraph{Coordinate carrier and observed boundary set.}
The carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, with vertices represented by codon words and labels read from the curated standard code. The distinguished boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite run verifies that this set is the punctured-cube pattern detected by the local construction, with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row-level evidence is not merely that thirteen codons were selected; it also records a small two-dimensional tile structure inside the six-bit codon display.

\paragraph{Spectral check.}
The finite shape has an accompanying spectral summary. The recorded values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cubic or radial polynomial residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the reported spectral relation is numerically satisfied at the precision of the run. This spectral datum is used only as an internal consistency check on the finite coordinate object. It does not turn the local tile into a law of biology.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
Two finite randomization checks constrain the interpretation. In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$, seed $42$, and $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, the sampler required $55377$ duplicate retries. It observed $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 0.0005,\\
p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\geq 0.9} &= 0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed comparison used $|M|=20$, found $0$ exact matches, and placed only $7$ samples in the lower tail at or below the observed $M$ size. The corresponding reported probabilities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}} &= 0.0,\\
p_{\mathrm{class\ lower\ tail}} &= 0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support the claim that the observed finite shape is not typical under the supplied shuffles. They do not by themselves identify a physical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. Inside $M-T$ the hit rate was $0.9365079365079365$, and inside $M-T$ together with the boundary it rose to $0.9841269841269841$. The leave-one-codon-out run separately reported $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closures, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$. These numbers are the immediate empirical context for the module classification: withheld codons are not recovered from biochemical mechanism, but from finite label geometry plus the curated code table.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The classification run assigns all thirteen members of $R$ to six named modules. The verified module rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row records $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons. Hence the finite conclusion is exact at the row level: every codon in the distinguished boundary set belongs to one of the six module classes, and the union of those classes is precisely the observed $R$.

\paragraph{Family-level support.}
The broader leave-one-code-out family summary reports $5$ families. The hit-rate-above-baseline values are $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast and $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families. This family table is not used to enlarge the biological claim. It only shows that the same finite code-read heuristic remains strong across the supplied family grouping.

\paragraph{Scope of the result.}
The ready claim is therefore a finite code-read statement. In the supplied standard-code row, the window-six boundary set $R$ has a thirteen-codon punctured-cube shape, passes the recorded spectral check, is rare under the supplied null models, and is completely partitioned by the six module classes listed above. The supporting contact is the curated standard genetic-code table, which supplies codon-to-label assignments. No inference is made here to translation in vivo, folded protein structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, a universal mechanism, or any necessity claim about DNA. Any such assertion would require a separate observation at that higher layer.
