\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite codon domain.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label readback problem over the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The row under audit is the leave-one-codon-out module classification for the local boundary tile.  The coordinate carrier is not a biological mechanism: it is a finite display in which codons occupy cube positions, local faces and corners can be queried, and curated amino-acid or stop labels are attached only through the standard-code table contact.  The exceptional set recovered by the finite computation has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$R=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}$.
The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube shape with face data $f=(13,16,4)$, so the finite object being classified is not an arbitrary codon list but a local cube-derived boundary pattern with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-faces in the reported complex.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape calibration.}
The finite spectral row gives $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The associated radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic residual, so the numerical certificate is at machine-zero scale for the recorded finite spectrum.  Shape significance was tested against $N=2000$ sampled alternatives with seed $42$.  Among $57377$ distinct-sample attempts, $55377$ were duplicate retries, $1$ sample matched the exact shape, and $25$ samples had Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$.  The resulting empirical probabilities are $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$ and $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, the observed module-size statistic was $20$, the exact-shape count was $0$ out of $2000$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.  These figures support the claim that the local tile is a structured finite pattern relative to the chosen code-label table, while leaving its biological interpretation bounded by the available contact.

Thus the classification is complete at the level of the finite row: every codon in the thirteen-codon boundary set belongs to exactly one named module, and every named module is connected to the predictable-or-boundary criterion used by the probe.

\paragraph{Code-table readback.}
The only reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, whose observed fact is that codons are assigned to amino-acid or stop labels.  At the code-read layer this is enough to test whether the local codon-tile split aligns with standard-code labels.  The leave-one-code-out row evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  It reports baseline hit rate $0.19821739130434785$, hit rate above baseline $0.9841269841269841$, hit rate inside $M-T$ equal to $0.9365079365079365$, and hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary equal to $0.9841269841269841$.  Family-level aggregation used $5$ families and reported hit rates above baseline of $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial or plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate tables.  These numbers say that, once the curated label table is admitted as the contact, the finite module geometry has strong leave-one-out readback behavior across the recorded table family classes.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The conclusion is deliberately local.  The six-bit coordinate construction, the punctured-cube set $R$, the spectral figures, and the module partition jointly support a code-layer statement: the standard-code label table exhibits a finite local boundary-tile organization, and the leave-one-codon-out module row classifies the thirteen exceptional codons without leftover cases.  Nothing in this record establishes translation as a realized cellular process, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism of code origin, or a universal biological law.  Amino-acid label sequences remain labels read from the curated table unless a separate observation supplies structure or function.  Cross-organism wording, tRNA-copy questions, and folded-structure claims require their own organism-matched or structure-bearing contacts before they can be promoted beyond this code-read layer.
