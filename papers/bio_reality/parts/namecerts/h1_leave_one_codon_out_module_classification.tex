\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification in the codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Coordinate object and readback level.}
This record treats the standard-code codon table as a finite label readback on the codon cube $Q_6$.  A codon is displayed as a six-bit coordinate word, and the observable row is not a protein, a mechanism, or a physical process; it is only the curated assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  The local geometry used here distinguishes a tile face, singleton label corners, and three-point label classes.  The point of the computation is therefore narrow: it asks whether the leave-one-codon-out rows inside the distinguished set $R$ are organized into complete local modules whose members are either predictable from the closure calculation or lie on the boundary of that calculation.

\paragraph{Distinguished punctured cube row.}
The finite row for $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row is recorded as a punctured-cube shape with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the module calculation is not being run on an open-ended biological universe.  It is a thirteen-codon finite subconfiguration whose label readback is checked against the curated standard-code table.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The coordinate computation also supplies a spectral row.  The reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, so the numerical witness is at the tolerance scale rather than at a visible residual scale.  Shape significance was tested against $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$.  The exact-shape count was $1$, giving $p=0.0005$, while the high-overlap count at Jaccard threshold $0.9$ was $25$, giving $p=0.0125$.  The sampling required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which is a useful warning that the null ensemble is combinatorially tight.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, the observed $M$ size is $20$, the exact-shape count is $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size is $7$ out of $2000$, giving lower-tail value $0.0035$.  These figures support a nonrandom local row shape, but they do not by themselves identify a biochemical cause.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out support.}
The broader code-table leave-one-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ together with the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  Family stratification used $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, while bacterial or plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate families each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline.  These rows say that the local classifier is robust across the tested table family partition, not that it explains translation or organismal adaptation.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out counts.}
For the codon-level leave-one-out row, the anchor count is $0$, the boundary count is $5$, and the predictable count is $8$.  The high-coverage closure count is also $8$, and the recorded recovery rate is $1.0$, equal to the thresholded recovery value $1.0$.  This is the appropriate scale for the claim: the computation recovers the thirteen codons in $R$ by predictable or boundary status, with no separate anchor class contributing to the count.

\paragraph{Complete module assignment.}
The module classification is complete because the row reports $R\_size=13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
AR\_Lys&=\{AAA\},\\
CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
Ile\_Met&=\{AUA\},\\
SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
Stop\_Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module contains only codons from $R$, and every codon of $R$ appears in one of these six displayed classes.  The classification therefore closes the finite bookkeeping problem: the leave-one-codon-out distinguished row is exhausted by the predictable-or-boundary module partition.

\paragraph{Reality contact and blocked promotions.}
The reality contact used here is the curated standard-code table, understood only as a codon-to-label source.  It can test code-layer readback and local codon-tile label splitting.  It cannot test translation realization, folded structure, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, or any universal mechanism.  Consequently, amino-acid label readback remains a code-level observation.  A separate structure experiment, translation measurement, physical model, or functional assay would be needed before any claim crossed those boundaries.  The present row is aligned with the standard-code labels precisely because it refuses that promotion: a local codon tile may organize the thirteen-codon boundary row, but no folding, function, mechanism, or universality follows from the tile geometry alone.
