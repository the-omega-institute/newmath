\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out modules in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate setting.}
The experiment places the standard-code codon table inside the six-coordinate codon cube $Q_6$, where each codon is read as a finite coordinate word and then compared with the curated amino-acid or stop label assigned by the standard code.  The object under test is not translation itself, and it is not a model of folded molecular structure.  It is a code-layer question: whether the window-six coordinates isolate a small local boundary tile whose codons are recoverable or classifiable after one codon has been withheld.  The curated table is used only as the label contact for codons, so every numerical claim below remains tied to finite table rows and their local coordinate relations.

The distinguished boundary set has cardinality $13$.  In row order it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube computation gives the same codon set and records the face data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local object is a finite tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional adjacencies, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.  This is the finite support on which the leave-one-codon module classification is evaluated.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-model checks.}
The coordinate tile is not treated as a visual coincidence.  The spectral computation reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$ and $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, with radial parameter $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The reported cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also recorded as the radial polynomial value, so the numerical certificate is effectively at floating-point zero for this run.  The observed $M$ size is $20$, and the finite randomization tests give two independent ways to see that the same shape is not typical under the tested nulls.

For the unrestricted shape randomization with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$.  There were $25$ samples with Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampling required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, which records that the finite sample was drawn from a constrained combinatorial space rather than from a free continuous parameter.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the exact shape count was $0$, giving $p=0.0$ in the finite run, while the lower tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, giving $p=0.0035$.  These numbers support a local code-read regularity; they do not identify a biochemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out recovery.}
At the code-table level, the leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  The family-level split contains $5$ families.  The above-baseline family hit rates were $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear-ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These rows say that the coordinate tile has strong code-read recovery behavior across the tested family grouping, while still being measured against curated labels rather than against ribosomal realization, organismal expression, or protein activity.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out module classification.}
The codon-level leave-one-out run reports no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, $8$ predictable codons, and recovery rate $1.0$.  The module classification assigns every codon in $R$ to one of six named finite modules:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The completeness row states $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and assigned count $13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons from the module list, and no unassigned codons.  Equivalently, the six modules partition exactly the observed boundary set:
$$
\begin{aligned}
2+1+4+1+2+3=13.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module contains codons classified as predictable or boundary-supported in the run.  The result is therefore a finite module-level refinement of the boundary tile: the local coordinate object is not merely a set of exceptional codons, but a completely assigned set of codon modules whose rows are all accounted for by the experiment.

\paragraph{Scope of the contact.}
The biological contact used here is a curated standard-code table assigning codons to amino-acid or stop labels.  That contact is adequate for code-label readback and for checking whether a local coordinate tile separates singleton label corners from three-point label classes.  It is not a contact with translation dynamics, folded structure, thermodynamic admissibility, cellular function, or universality across all biological regimes.  Consequently, the positive verdict is deliberately local: window-six codon coordinates expose a finite boundary-module pattern in the standard-code label table.  No claim about translation realization, protein folding, physical viability, biological function, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this packet unless a separate experiment supplies that higher-layer observation.
