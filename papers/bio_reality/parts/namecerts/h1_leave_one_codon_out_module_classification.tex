\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification in the window-six codon cube}\label{subsec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table only at the code-read layer.  A codon is represented in the window-six coordinate display, and the observed label table is used as a curated contact assigning each codon to an amino-acid or stop label.  The finite distinguished set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same run identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with face-vector data $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the row is not merely a list of exceptional codons: it also carries a local combinatorial shape inside the codon-coordinate cube.  The complementary label readback remains empirical, because the amino-acid and stop assignments are supplied by the curated standard-code table rather than by the coordinate geometry alone.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape checks.}
The coordinate construction has a measured spectral profile.  The recorded values are $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$.  The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, the same value reported for the cubic-polynomial check, so the finite numerical witness is within the recorded tolerance.  In the shape-significance experiment with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  The resulting exact-shape probability was $p=0.0005$.  The high-overlap comparison recorded $25$ samples with Jaccard similarity at least $0.9$, giving $p=0.0125$.  A second null model preserving amino-acid class structure observed $M$-size $20$, found $0$ exact matches in $N=2000$ trials, and gave a lower-tail count of $7$, hence lower-tail probability $0.0035$.  These finite numbers support the claim that the displayed local shape is uncommon under the tested controls, while not turning the shape into a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon probes.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary codons was again $0.9841269841269841$.  The leave-one-codon-out experiment reported $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable codons, with recovery rate $1.0$.  These figures are read as finite recovery evidence for a local code-label pattern: when one codon is withheld, the coordinate-and-boundary bookkeeping is often sufficient to place it back into the observed code-read neighborhood.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module classification partitions all $13$ codons of $R$ into named local modules, with no missing or extra codons.  The complete assignment is
$$\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
AR_{Lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
Ile/Met&=\{AUA\},\\
SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
Stop/Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The completion audit records $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no codons missing from the modules, and no unassigned codons.  Each named module has at least one codon classified as predictable or boundary-supported.  This is the central finite-row assertion for the claim: the local leave-one-codon behavior is not scattered across unrelated entries, but is organized by the six displayed modules.  The family-level leave-one-code-out table supplies an additional comparison across $5$ families: bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate families each have hit rate $1.0$ above baseline, while alternative-yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The result should be stated narrowly.  The window-six codon display, the punctured-cube set $R$, the spectral values, and the leave-one-out recovery counts together describe a finite code-read regularity in the curated standard-code table.  They do not show that codons are translated by this geometry, that a protein structure is formed, that a molecule is physically admissible, that biological function follows, or that the local tile is a universal law.  Amino-acid label readback is still only label readback.  Any claim about translation realization, folding, molecular stability, function, or organism-level universality would require a separate observation with the appropriate resolution.  Within the data supplied here, the correct conclusion is that the standard-code labels expose a compact local boundary-module pattern in the window-six codon cube, and that the $13$-codon module assignment is complete for the distinguished set $R$.
