\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification in the window-six codon cube}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record concerns the standard-code codon table as a code-layer object: each codon is read as a six-bit coordinate in the codon cube $Q_6$, and the only external contact used here is a curated codon-to-label table. The experiment does not treat the table as a dynamical model of translation. It treats it as a finite labelled display against which local codon-window structure can be compared.

The distinguished residual set is the following $13$-element set:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cardinality check reports $|R|=13$, and the punctured-cube check gives the same codon list with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the codons under discussion are not an informal subset selected after inspection; they are the finite row-level support on which the module classification is evaluated.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The associated spectral row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also reported as the cubic-polynomial residual, so the numerical check is at tolerance scale rather than at visual-inspection scale. The shape comparison over $N=2000$ trials gives one exact shape match, hence $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ high-overlap matches at Jaccard threshold $0.9$, hence $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The finite sampling audit also records $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries at seed $42$. In the amino-acid-class-preserving null model, the observed size of $M$ is $20$; among $2000$ trials there are $0$ exact shape matches and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size, giving $p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0$ and $p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and family-level checks.}
The leave-one-code experiment evaluates $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. Its baseline hit rate is $0.19821739130434785$, while the reported hit rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ is $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary class is again $0.9841269841269841$. At family resolution the run groups the cases into $5$ families. The family hit rates above baseline are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alternative\_yeast}&=0.8333333333333334,\\
\mathrm{bacterial\_plastid}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{candidate\_other}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{mitochondrial}&=1.0,\\
\mathrm{nuclear\_ciliate}&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
These values support a local code-read classification claim: the six-bit window and its boundary classes recover much of the label-level variation seen when one code table or one codon is withheld.

\paragraph{Codon-module classification.}
The leave-one-codon module row is complete on $R$. It reports $R\_size=13$ and $assigned\_count=13$, with no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. The modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has at least one codon in the predictable-or-boundary class. In the separate leave-one-codon row, the predictable count is $8$, the boundary count is $5$, the anchor count is $0$, and the closure-high coverage count is $8$. The recovery rate is $1.0$, matching the recorded threshold comparison $1.0$. Therefore the finite conclusion is precise: within this $13$-codon residual set, the six module blocks exhaust the row and each block is touched by the local predictable-or-boundary mechanism.

\paragraph{Scientific scope.}
The contact used here is the curated standard genetic-code table, whose observed fact is that codons carry amino-acid or stop labels in the standard code. That contact is enough to test codon-label readback and local label splitting. It is not enough to infer translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law. A sequence of amino-acid labels remains a code-layer readout until a distinct structure, translation, or function contact is supplied. Consequently, the present result should be read as a finite local classification of codon labels in $Q_6$, not as a claim that the codon cube by itself explains protein structure or organism-level biology.
