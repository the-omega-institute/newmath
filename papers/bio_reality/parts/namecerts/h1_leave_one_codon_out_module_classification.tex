\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification for the standard-code window}
\label{sec:bior-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite object under test.}
The record studies the standard-code codon table through a six-coordinate codon display.  The finite carrier is the codon cube $Q_6$, and the readout layer is limited to the assignment of codon strings to amino-acid or stop labels in the curated standard-code table.  The distinguished residual set has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
     &UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
As a punctured cubical pattern this same residual set has face-vector $(13,16,4)$, so the finite geometry is not only a list of exceptional codons but also a small local tile with $13$ vertices, $16$ one-dimensional incidences, and $4$ two-dimensional faces.  The chapter treats this tile as a code-read object: it may organize label changes in a codon table, but it is not by itself a model of translation, folding, physical viability, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Spectral and shape evidence.}
The associated spectral calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M &= 0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R &= 0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu &= 3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported cubic relation has numerical residual $-6.224354365258478\cdot 10^{-12}$, the same value recorded for the radial polynomial check, so the spectral row is internally coherent to numerical tolerance.  A shape-significance comparison with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$ found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{\mathrm{Jaccard}\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  The sampler needed $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and logged $55377$ duplicate retries, which is relevant because the conclusion is a finite-row enrichment statement rather than an asymptotic population theorem.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null model with $N=2000$ and the same seed, the exact shape count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, the observed $M$-size was $20$, and the corresponding reported probabilities were $0.0$ for exact class-preserving shape recovery and $0.0035$ for the lower tail.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.}
The leave-one-code experiment evaluated $23$ codon tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ after adjoining the boundary was again $0.9841269841269841$.  In the leave-one-codon experiment the anchor count was $0$, the boundary count was $5$, the high-coverage closure count was $8$, the predictable count was $8$, and the recovery rate was $1.0$.  These values justify reading the local tile as a strong code-label recovery pattern for the supplied finite rows.  They do not justify promoting the pattern to a statement about ribosomal realization, protein conformation, energetic admissibility, organismal fitness, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module classification is complete on the residual set: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The six reported modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr} &= \{AGA, AGG\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys} &= \{AAA\},\\
\mathrm{cun} &= \{CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met} &= \{AUA\},\\
\mathrm{ssn} &= \{UCA, UUA\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp} &= \{UAA, UAG, UGA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every listed module has predictable or boundary support in the finite record.  This is the central claim of the chapter: the residual codons left by the window-six construction are not scattered arbitrarily across the code-read layer, but are exhausted by a small family of named modules whose codons are all accounted for by the predictable-or-boundary classification.

\paragraph{Family-level check.}
The family summary records $5$ families.  The hit rates above baseline are $1.0$ for bacterial-plastid tables, $1.0$ for candidate-other tables, $1.0$ for mitochondrial tables, $1.0$ for nuclear-ciliate tables, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative-yeast tables.  Thus the finite row does not merely identify a standard-table residual list; it also reports that the same recovery criterion remains high across the supplied table-family grouping.  The alternative-yeast family is the least saturated of the five in this row, but it remains far above the baseline rate $0.19821739130434785$ reported for the leave-one-code comparison.

\paragraph{Reality contact and scope.}
The observational contact is the curated standard genetic-code table.  It supplies codon-to-label assignments and therefore can test code-read claims, local codon-tile splits, and module-level label recovery.  It does not observe translation as a molecular event, folded protein structure, physical admissibility of a sequence, biological function, or a universal mechanism.  Consequently an amino-acid label sequence read from the table remains a label sequence.  A separate structure or experiment contact would be needed before any statement about folding or structural order could be made, and a separate translation or functional contact would be needed before the local codon geometry could be connected to mechanism or organismal role.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
For the finite rows supplied here, the claim is ready at the code-read layer.  The set $R$ is exactly the $13$-codon punctured tile listed above, the module assignment covers all $13$ residual codons with no extras and no omissions, every module has predictable-or-boundary support, the leave-one-codon recovery rate is $1.0$, and the leave-one-code rate above baseline is $0.9841269841269841$ across $63$ targets from $23$ evaluated tables.  The result should be cited as a finite local classification of standard-code label behavior, not as evidence that six-coordinate codon geometry alone determines translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or biological universality.
