\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Standard Code}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The record considered here is a window-six codon display for the standard-code table. The finite carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with each codon read as a length-three word over the four nucleotide letters after binary expansion of the three sites. The reality contact used in this subsection is only the curated standard-code assignment of codons to amino-acid or stop labels. It is not a contact with ribosomal translation, folding, chemical admissibility, organismal function, or a universal mechanism. The question is therefore deliberately narrow: after one codon label is hidden, does the local coordinate-and-closure geometry place the missing codon in a small boundary or predictable class, and do the exceptional codons split into named modules without residue?

\paragraph{The residual set.}
The residual codon set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In the verified row, it is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports that this set is the punctured-cube pattern detected by the coordinate construction, with face-vector data
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(R)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the combinatorial object is not an informal visual cluster: it is a finite subcomplex with thirteen vertices, sixteen edges, and four two-faces in the supplied computation. The table contact supplies the labels attached to these vertices; the cube geometry supplies only the finite coordinate arrangement in which the labels are inspected.

These values are used only as finite numerical descriptors of the observed row. They do not, by themselves, identify a biochemical force or a dynamical law.

\paragraph{Shape rarity under finite null checks.}
Two null comparisons are attached to the row. In the shape null with $N=2000$ sampled tables and seed $42$, there was $1$ exact shape match after $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}
$$
The high-overlap threshold had $25$ matches, giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the amino-acid-class-preserving null, the observed module size was $20$, with $0$ exact matches among $2000$ trials and $7$ lower-tail samples at or below the observed size. The corresponding reported values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{class\ exact}}=0.0,\qquad p_{\mathrm{lower\ tail}}=0.0035.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers support the claim that the finite row is not a routine occurrence under the sampled controls. They do not upgrade the row into a claim about translation or protein structure.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out readback.}
At the code-table level, the leave-one-code-out computation evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ targets. Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$. The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, and the same value was obtained for $M-T$ with boundary included. Without the boundary supplement, the hit rate in $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$. By family, the row reports $5$ families: bacterial-plastid, candidate-other, mitochondrial, and nuclear-ciliate each have family hit rate $1.0$ above baseline, while alternative-yeast has $0.8333333333333334$. This is a code-read statistic: it says that the curated labels interact strongly with the finite coordinate split under this leave-one-out protocol.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
For the one-codon deletion task, the row records $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary cases, $8$ high-coverage closure cases, and $8$ predictable cases. The recovery rate is $1.0$, and the threshold comparison is also $1.0$. This is the local operational content of the packet: every tested missing codon is recovered by the finite predictable-or-boundary criterion supplied by the coordinate closure. The absence of anchors is important, because the row is not claiming that a fixed codon alone determines the table. The recovery is mediated by the surrounding finite arrangement and the curated labels.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module classification is complete. The residual set has $R$-size $13$, assigned count $13$, no extra module codons, no missing codons from the modules, and no unassigned codons. The named modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the row proves a finite bookkeeping fact for this experiment: the six modules form an exact assignment of the thirteen residual codons, and every module contains codons that are predictable, boundary-located, or both under the supplied classification. The module labels are descriptive names for the finite codon groups; they are not asserted to be evolutionary causes.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The ready claim is therefore a code-layer claim. The standard-code table, used as a curated label source, aligns with a six-bit local tile in which the residual codons form a thirteen-point punctured-cube pattern, exhibit the reported spectral scale, pass the sampled rarity checks, and split into six completely assigned modules. The finite row supports local codon-label readback and leave-one-codon-out classification. It does not support any conclusion about actual translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, a generative mechanism, or universality across biological systems unless an additional contact specifically measuring that higher layer is supplied.
