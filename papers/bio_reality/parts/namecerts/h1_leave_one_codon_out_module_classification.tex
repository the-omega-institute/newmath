\subsection{Leave-one-codon module classification for the standard code}\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite codon record.} The record tests a window-six codon display on the standard-code label table. The carrier is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, with local faces, singleton label corners, three-point label classes, and the codon-to-label table used only as a curated contact for code-layer readback. In the verified row, the distinguished set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row identifies this set as a punctured-cube pattern with $f$-vector $(13,16,4)$. Thus the observed object is not a vague enrichment claim: it is a concrete thirteen-codon boundary set with sixteen one-dimensional incidences and four two-dimensional faces in the induced local geometry.

\paragraph{Spectral and sampling constraints.} The coordinate computation reports $\lambda_M=0.6752479308830213$, $\lambda_R=0.4758352843197002$, and $\mu=3.051487585298128$. The radial cubic residual is $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, also recorded as the cubic-polynomial residual, so the numerical check is at roundoff scale rather than a free symbolic assertion. The shape-significance run used $N=2000$ sampled controls with seed $42$. It found $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ controls with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\geq 0.9}=0.0125$. The sampling process made $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and encountered $55377$ duplicate retries, so the reported tail probabilities should be read as finite empirical estimates under a heavily constrained null sampler, not as closed-form biological probabilities.

\paragraph{Amino-acid-class preserving null.} A separate amino-acid-class preserving control run, also with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, records observed $M$-size $20$. It reports no exact class-preserving shape match, with exact-match count $0$ and $p=0.0$ at this finite resolution. The lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size is $7$, giving $p=0.0035$. This strengthens the code-layer observation: the local codon geometry is not merely recovering an arbitrary relabeling of amino-acid classes in the sampled finite controls. It still remains a statement about codon labels and local combinatorics, because the contact table supplies no measurement of translation kinetics, molecular structure, thermodynamic admissibility, or organismal function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code and leave-one-codon behavior.} In the leave-one-code-out row, $23$ code tables were evaluated against $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$. The family-level split has $5$ families: alternative yeast has hit rate $0.8333333333333334$, and bacterial plastid, candidate other, mitochondrial, and nuclear ciliate each have hit rate $1.0$. In the leave-one-codon-out row, the anchor count is $0$, the boundary count is $5$, the high-closure coverage count is $8$, and the predictable count is $8$, with recovery rate $1.0$. These numbers make the chapter's central point precise: the tested signal is concentrated in predictable or boundary behavior, not in a claimed universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Module classification.} The module classification is complete on the thirteen-codon set. The assigned count is $13$, equal to $|R|$, and the extra-module, missing-from-module, and unassigned lists are all empty. The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each listed module contains at least one codon classified as predictable or boundary-supported in the leave-one-codon-out analysis. This is the finite closure statement for the row: every codon in the thirteen-element boundary set is accounted for by the module partition, and every module touches the predictable-or-boundary side of the test.

\paragraph{Local reading of the standard table.} The curated standard genetic-code table is used here as a contact for codon-to-amino-acid or stop-label assignments. It permits checking whether the window-six local pattern separates codon labels in the recorded table. It does not, by itself, observe ribosomal translation, tRNA abundance, folding, structural order, physical viability, biological function, evolutionary necessity, or universality across organisms. The correct conclusion is therefore narrow but nontrivial: the standard-code labels align with a finite local codon-tile boundary in which the thirteen distinguished codons form a punctured-cube pattern, have the recorded spectral profile, pass the sampled shape tests at the listed rates, and admit a complete six-module classification.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.} No protein-level claim follows from this chapter. A readback from codons to amino-acid names is still only a code-layer readback. It is not a folded structure, not a translation experiment, not a physical admissibility assay, not a functional annotation, and not a proof that biology must obey this geometry. Any later statement crossing one of those boundaries would need its own independent contact: for example, a translation measurement for realization, a structural dataset for folding, a biochemical assay for admissibility, or a functional experiment for phenotype. Without such a contact, the result remains exactly a finite classification of local codon-label behavior in the curated standard-code table.
