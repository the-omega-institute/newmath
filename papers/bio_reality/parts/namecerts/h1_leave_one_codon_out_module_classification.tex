\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The carrier for this packet is the six-bit codon cube $Q_6$, read as a finite coordinate display for the $64$ codons of the standard code.  The biological contact used here is only the curated standard-code label table: it assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label, and it does not supply a translation event, a folded structure, a chemical admissibility certificate, a function assay, a mechanism, or a universal biological principle.  The claim tested in this section is therefore deliberately narrow.  It asks whether the window-six coordinates isolate a local boundary-tile pattern for the exceptional codons under leave-one-codon-out probing, while keeping the code labels as supplied observations rather than as consequences of the geometry.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The finite result is a code-layer statement.  It says that, for the curated standard-code labels and the six-bit codon coordinates, the residual codons form a $13$-point punctured-cube pattern whose six modules are completely classified by predictable-or-boundary behavior in the leave-one-codon-out test.  It also says that the associated shape and recovery statistics are strong in the recorded finite rows: exact-shape $p=0.0005$, high-Jaccard $p=0.0125$, amino-acid-class exact-match $p=0.0$, amino-acid-class lower-tail $p=0.0035$, and recovery rate $1.0$.

Nothing in this section upgrades that finite code-layer pattern into a claim about realized translation, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  Such a promotion would require a separate observation stream aimed at the higher layer.  The present contact is a codon-to-label table, and the valid conclusion remains local: window-six geometry organizes a specific boundary-tile split of the standard-code labels, while the biological labels themselves remain supplied by the curated table.
