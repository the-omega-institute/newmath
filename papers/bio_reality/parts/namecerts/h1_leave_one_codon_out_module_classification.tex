\subsection{Leave-One-Codon Module Classification in the Window-Six Codon Cube}
\label{sec:bioreality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This record concerns a finite code-read phenomenon inside the six-coordinate codon cube.  Codons are displayed as six-bit objects, and the relevant carrier is the finite cube $Q_6$ equipped with local faces, singleton label corners, three-point label classes, closure data, spectral data, and adjacency relations.  The biological object being read is the curated standard-code codon table.  The claim is not that the cube geometry is itself a biological mechanism.  The claim is that, after the standard-code labels are supplied as the reality contact, the leave-one-codon-out residue set decomposes into named local modules and every residue codon belongs to a module that is either predictable by the local rule or lies on the observed boundary.

\paragraph{Residue set.}
The finite residue set has cardinality $13$.  The observed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row verifies that this set is the punctured-cube residue with face-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the object under discussion is not an asymptotic statistic or a hidden population estimate; it is a fixed finite row in the standard-code readback.  The row has thirteen codon vertices, sixteen one-dimensional incidences, and four two-dimensional local faces in the punctured-cube presentation.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The module classification is complete: $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, missing codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{AGR}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{AR\_Lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{CUN}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{Ile\_Met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{SSN}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{Stop\_Trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Counting the displayed modules gives $2+1+4+1+2+3=13$, so the classification exhausts the residue set without overlap in the reported finite row.  In the leave-one-codon-out test, the observed counts are $0$ anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and $8$ predictable codons, with recovery rate $1.0$.  The point of the module row is therefore precise: each residue codon has a local module address, and each module has predictable or boundary support inside the code-read layer.

\paragraph{Spectral and null-row context.}
The same finite package reports spectral scalars for the local coordinate object.  The values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the radial cubic check evaluates to $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, within the stated numerical tolerance.  The shape row for $M$ used $N=2000$ samples with seed $42$; there was $1$ exact shape match and $25$ matches with Jaccard score at least $0.9$, giving $p\_\mathrm{exact}=0.0005$ and $p\_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null row, again with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed size was $7$, giving $p\_\mathrm{exact}=0.0$ and lower-tail value $0.0035$.  These rows do not prove a mechanism, but they do make the local geometry non-generic relative to the tested finite shuffles.

\paragraph{Code-table prediction rows.}
The leave-one-code-out evaluation used $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets.  Its baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$.  The hit rate in $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate in $M\setminus T$ plus boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast, $1.0$ for bacterial plastid, $1.0$ for candidate other, $1.0$ for mitochondrial, and $1.0$ for nuclear ciliate, across $5$ families.  These numbers place the module classification inside a broader code-read regularity: the local window-six rule is strong at recovering code-label targets when evaluated as a finite label-table task.

\paragraph{Reality contact and limit of interpretation.}
The reality contact is the curated standard genetic-code table: it supplies codon-to-amino-acid or codon-to-stop labels.  That contact can test only code-layer label readback and local codon-tile splits.  It does not observe ribosomal translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law.  Consequently, the module result must remain at the code-read layer.  The CUN, AGR, stop--tryptophan, isoleucine--methionine, lysine, and SSN modules are finite label-table modules in the six-bit codon display; they are not, by themselves, statements about molecular realization or organism-level function.  Any future statement crossing from codon labels to translation, folding, admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require an additional empirical contact designed for that layer.
