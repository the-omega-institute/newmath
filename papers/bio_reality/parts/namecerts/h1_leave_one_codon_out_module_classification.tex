\subsection{Leave-One-Codon-Out Module Classification}
\label{sec:h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}

\paragraph{Finite codon display.}
The object studied here is the standard-code codon table viewed through a six-bit codon cube $Q_6$.  The finite display has codon vertices as its carrier, local faces as its neighborhood tests, singleton label corners as its sharpest label contacts, and three-point label classes as the smallest nontrivial label clusters that can be inspected without leaving the code-read layer.  The relevant external contact is only the curated standard genetic-code table: it assigns each codon to an amino-acid label or to stop.  This contact is sufficient for codon-label readback and for finite split tests, but it is not a measurement of translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Distinguished residual set.}
The finite row records a residual codon set $R$ of cardinality $13$.  The codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The punctured-cube check gives the same set and records the face-vector signature
$$
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is not merely a count: it specifies the exact codon support and a local cubical shape summary.  In the sampled comparison used for the $M$-shape test, $N=2000$ distinct draws were attempted after $57377$ sample attempts and $55377$ duplicate retries.  There was $1$ exact shape match, giving $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.0005$, and $25$ samples had Jaccard overlap at least $0.9$, giving $p_{J\ge 0.9}=0.0125$.  Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$, the observed $M$-size was $20$, the exact-match count was $0$, and the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$-size was $7$, giving $p_{\mathrm{lower}}=0.0035$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out behavior.}
The leave-one-code-out experiment evaluated $23$ tables and $63$ total targets.  The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$.  The hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$, the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M\setminus T$ plus the boundary was $0.9841269841269841$.  By family, the hit-rate-above-baseline values were $1.0$ for bacterial/plastid, mitochondrial, nuclear ciliate, and candidate-other families, and $0.8333333333333334$ for alternative yeast.  These figures support a strong finite regularity at the code-read level, with the weaker alternative-yeast family retained as part of the displayed evidence rather than rounded away.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out behavior.}
The leave-one-codon-out row records no anchors, $5$ boundary codons, $8$ high-coverage closure codons, and $8$ predictable codons.  Its recovery rate is $1.0$, and the above-threshold recovery flag is also $1.0$.  The row therefore supports the following local conclusion: when a codon is withheld, the codon-coordinate and local-boundary data classify the withheld codon as either predictable or boundary-recoverable in the tested finite display.  The conclusion is deliberately local.  It concerns the curated codon-label table and the six-bit coordinate window; it does not state that the biological translation apparatus is generated by the window.

\paragraph{Module classification.}
The module row is complete: $R$ has size $13$, the assigned count is $13$, and the lists of extra module codons, missing module codons, and unassigned codons are all empty.  The six modules are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{agr}&=\{\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG}\},\\
\mathrm{ar\_lys}&=\{\mathrm{AAA}\},\\
\mathrm{cun}&=\{\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU}\},\\
\mathrm{ile\_met}&=\{\mathrm{AUA}\},\\
\mathrm{ssn}&=\{\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UUA}\},\\
\mathrm{stop\_trp}&=\{\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UGA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every module has a predictable-or-boundary witness in the finite row.  The singleton modules $\mathrm{ar\_lys}$ and $\mathrm{ile\_met}$ isolate individual label-corner events, the two-point modules $\mathrm{agr}$ and $\mathrm{ssn}$ retain paired boundary behavior, the four-codon $\mathrm{cun}$ module records a full local block, and $\mathrm{stop\_trp}$ records the stop/tryptophan neighborhood as a three-codon class.  Since the module assignment covers all $13$ residual codons and introduces no extras, the classification is exact for this finite data row.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The chapter-level claim is therefore a code-layer claim.  Window-six codon coordinates expose a finite local tile pattern in the standard-code table, and the leave-one-codon-out module classification gives a complete partition of the $13$ residual codons into predictable-or-boundary modules.  The evidence is strongest as a finite mathematical readback of a curated label table: exact residual support, punctured-cube face counts, spectral scalars, significant shape rarity under two null comparisons, strong leave-one-code-out hit rates, perfect leave-one-codon recovery in the reported row, and complete module assignment.  No statement about protein folding, structural order, molecular admissibility, biological function, translation realization, or universal mechanism follows from these rows.  Such a statement would require a separate contact that directly observes the higher layer being claimed.
