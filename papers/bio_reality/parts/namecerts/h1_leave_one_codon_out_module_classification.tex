\paragraph{Finite coordinate frame.}
The claim concerns a six-bit display of the standard-code codon table.  The relevant codon set is not introduced as a biochemical mechanism, but as a finite coordinate object whose labels are read from a curated standard-code table.  In the verified row, the selected boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same set is checked as a punctured-cube pattern with face data $(13,16,4)$.  Thus the finite object being classified is not an arbitrary list of exceptional codons: it is a thirteen-codon boundary trace inside the six-coordinate codon cube.  The contact used for labels is only the curated standard genetic-code table, which assigns each codon an amino-acid or stop label.  No ribosome, tRNA pool, folding environment, organismal context, or fitness landscape is part of this contact.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out row classifies all thirteen elements of $R$ into six named modules.  The complete assignment is

The module audit reports $R\_\mathrm{size}=13$ and $\mathrm{assigned\_count}=13$, with no extra module codons, no missing members, and no unassigned codons.  In particular, every listed module has at least one member marked by the predictable-or-boundary criterion.  This is the central finite-row content of the claim: after one codon is withheld, the remaining coordinate pattern still sorts the observed boundary codons into the six displayed modules without leaving a residue outside the partition.

\paragraph{Scientific scope.}
The contact is deliberately narrow.  The standard-code table can certify that a codon has a curated label and can test whether the six-bit local tile separates the displayed boundary modules.  It cannot certify translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, organism-level mechanism, or universality.  Amino-acid label readout is therefore not a protein-structure observation.  The CUN, UUR, AGR, stop, Trp, Ile, Met, Arg, Lys, Ser, and Leu label groupings may be used here as table entries, but they are not evidence of folding, dynamics, selection, or molecular implementation.  Any claim crossing that boundary would need a separate contact: for example, a translation assay for realized decoding, a structural measurement for folded conformation, or an organism-matched experiment for functional effect.

Within the available finite rows, the module classification is complete: the thirteen-codon boundary set is fully assigned, the punctured-cube count is fixed at $(13,16,4)$, the spectral values are recorded, the null tests show low exact-shape probabilities, and the leave-one rows show strong code-layer recovery.  The result is therefore ready as a code-read claim about a local codon-coordinate boundary pattern.  Its correct interpretation is bounded: it is a finite label-table result, not a statement that biological translation, folding, function, physical admissibility, or a universal mechanism follows from the window-six geometry.
