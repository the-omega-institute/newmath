\subsection{Leave-one-codon-out module classification in the standard code}\label{sec:biorality-h1-leave-one-codon-out-module-classification}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
The finite object considered here is the standard-code codon table read at the code-label layer. Codons are displayed as six-bit window coordinates, and the local question is whether the distinguished boundary set in this display decomposes into biologically recognizable reassignment modules when one codon is withheld. The contact used for interpretation is a curated standard genetic-code table: it supplies codon-to-label readback for amino-acid and stop symbols, but it does not supply translation dynamics, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, realized biological function, a mechanism, or a universal biological law. Those stronger layers require separate observations before they can be asserted.

\paragraph{Boundary carrier.}
The finite boundary set $R$ has cardinality $13$. In codon notation it is
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The same run records that this set is a punctured-cube boundary with face-vector data
$$\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(13,16,4).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus the chapter is not classifying all $64$ codons. It is classifying the thirteen codons selected by the local window-six boundary construction. The observed module accounting is complete: $R\_size=13$, $assigned\_count=13$, with no extra module codons, no codons missing from the module list, and no unassigned codons.

\paragraph{Module partition.}
The leave-one-codon-out module classification assigns every element of $R$ to one of six named codon modules. The recorded partition is
$$\begin{aligned}
AGR&=\{AGA,AGG\},\\
AR\_Lys&=\{AAA\},\\
CUN&=\{CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU\},\\
Ile\_Met&=\{AUA\},\\
SSN&=\{UCA,UUA\},\\
Stop\_Trp&=\{UAA,UAG,UGA\}.
\end{aligned}$$
Each listed module contains at least one codon recorded as predictable or as a boundary codon under the leave-one-codon-out test. This is the finite classification claim: the thirteen boundary codons do not remain as isolated names after withholding tests; they organize into the six displayed reassignment families, and the accounting is exact on the boundary set.

\paragraph{Leave-one-codon-out recovery.}
The codon-level withholding run reports $8$ predictable codons, $5$ boundary codons, and $0$ anchor codons in the relevant classification summary. The high-coverage count is $8$, and the recovery rate is
$$\begin{aligned}
recovery\_rate=1.0.
\end{aligned}$$
This number should be read narrowly. It says that within the tested local boundary carrier, the withheld-codon procedure recovers the recorded target classification at the code-label level. It does not say that the window geometry performs translation, determines amino-acid chemistry, produces a folded structure, or explains biological function.

\paragraph{Leave-one-code-out comparison.}
A related leave-one-code-out run evaluated $23$ code tables and $63$ total targets. The baseline hit rate was $0.19821739130434785$, while the hit rate above baseline was $0.9841269841269841$. The hit rate inside $M-T$ was $0.9365079365079365$, and the hit rate inside $M-T$ plus boundary codons was $0.9841269841269841$. By code-family grouping, $5$ families were recorded, with family-level hit rates above baseline
$$\begin{aligned}
alternative\_yeast&:0.8333333333333334,\\
bacterial\_plastid&:1.0,\\
candidate\_other&:1.0,\\
mitochondrial&:1.0,\\
nuclear\_ciliate&:1.0.

These row counts and rates support the local classification as a robust code-table readback phenomenon, while still leaving mechanism and organism-specific realization outside the tested claim.

\paragraph{Shape and null-model checks.}
The boundary classification is accompanied by finite null comparisons. In the shape-significance run with $N=2000$ samples and seed $42$, the observed exact-shape match count was $1$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p\_{exact}=0.0005.
\end{aligned}$$
The same run recorded $25$ matches at Jaccard threshold at least $0.9$, giving
$$\begin{aligned}
p\_{Jaccard\geq 0.9}=0.0125.
\end{aligned}$$
The sampler required $57377$ distinct-sample attempts and recorded $55377$ duplicate retries, so the finite comparison is also a statement about the observed discreteness of the sampled class. Under the amino-acid-class-preserving null with $N=2000$ and seed $42$, the observed $M$ size was $20$, the exact match count was $0$, the lower-tail count at or below the observed $M$ size was $7$, and the reported values were
$$\begin{aligned}
p\_{aa\ class\ exact\ shape}&=0.0,\\
p\_{aa\ class\ lower\ tail}&=0.0035.
\end{aligned}$$
These checks indicate that the displayed local shape and module behavior are not typical under the recorded finite null comparisons.

\paragraph{Spectral row.}
The spectral summary records three scalars for the finite carrier:
$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda\_M&=0.6752479308830213,\\
\lambda\_R&=0.4758352843197002,\\
\mu&=3.051487585298128.
\end{aligned}$$
The associated cubic and radial evaluations both give $-6.224354365258478\times 10^{-12}$, and the spectral-radius value in tolerance is $0.6752479308830213$. These numbers supply a separate finite consistency check on the coordinate-and-boundary computation; they are not biological force laws and do not turn codon-label readback into a physical model.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The ready claim is therefore the following: for the standard-code label table, the thirteen-codon boundary set selected by the six-bit local codon display is completely assigned to six biologically named reassignment modules, and every module has predictable or boundary support in the leave-one-codon-out summary. The numerical record includes exact boundary cardinality, punctured-cube face data, complete module accounting, perfect codon-level recovery in the tested carrier, high leave-one-code-out rates relative to baseline, significant finite null comparisons, and a stable spectral row. The scientifically admissible reading stops at the code-label layer. Without an additional contact, the chapter makes no claim about translation realization, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.
