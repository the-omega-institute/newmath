\subsection{Motif compression as an ORF-eligibility boundary packet}
\label{subsec:h0-r-motif-compression-orf-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite row under discussion.}
The claim identifier $\mathrm{h0.R.motif.compression}$ names a finite codon-level compression fact, not a biological realization claim.  The verified row records that the set $R$ is exactly the following thirteen-codon motif union:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row reports $\mathrm{no\_extra}=\varnothing$ and $\mathrm{no\_missing}=\varnothing$, so the compression equality is an exact finite enumeration rather than an approximate motif summary.  The experiment run for this equality is $338799579a87$, with verification time $2026$-$06$-$01\,07{:}28{:}18+00{:}00$.

\paragraph{Ambient codon set.}
The surrounding finite alphabet is not inferred from the motif row alone.  A linked verification row, $\mathrm{h0.M.equals.WNR.CUN}$, fixes the ambient set $M$ with cardinality $20$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $R$ occupies $13$ of the $20$ listed codons in $M$.  This numerical relation is useful for describing a boundary packet: it says that the motif row compresses a strict finite subcollection of the ambient codon set, while leaving seven listed codons outside $R$.

The same finite package records that $M=\mathrm{Med}_R\cup\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$, so the saturation row and the ambient row agree on which two codons remain outside that saturation summary.

These values describe finite combinatorial organization only.  They do not by themselves supply a nucleotide interval, a transcript observation, a ribosomal event, a folded product, or a cellular role.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility interpretation.}
The biological object named in the packet is open-reading-frame eligibility from start/stop codon enumeration.  At this level the word ``eligibility'' has a deliberately narrow meaning: a start/stop-window classifier may sort windows according to enumerated boundary conditions, but such sorting is not yet an observed ORF in a biological sequence.  The row $\mathrm{h2.orf.start\_stop.window\_eligibility}$ records one loaded external window and one classified window, with scope explicitly restricted to external window start/stop enumeration.  Its snapshot date is $2026$-$05$-$25$, and its classification statement is confined to $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  Therefore this row can support a boundary-level statement about what a classifier is allowed to test, but it does not turn the motif compression equality into an observation of translation.

\paragraph{Reality contact and stronger finite controls.}
A separate linked contact row, $\mathrm{h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact}$, records a curated interval dataset with $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for the Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$.  Within that contact row, $72$ positive curated ORF intervals are accepted at rate $1.0$, and $72$ matched non-ORF decoys are rejected at rate $1.0$.  The matched positive/decoy exact one-sided binomial tests report $p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$ for the positive interval statistic and the same value for matched decoy rejection.  The predeclared pairwise baseline comparison also reports $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, with win rate $1.0$ and $p=2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  Held-out split stability is recorded across three splits; each split has $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$.  Boundary cases are separately capped: $24$ boundary cases are listed, with $0$ promoted and promoted fraction $0.0$.

These contact statistics are important because they show what a genuine interval-facing test looks like when it is present.  They also sharpen the limitation on the present motif-compression row.  The compression equality for $R$, the $20$-codon ambient set $M$, the $18$-codon saturation row, and the quotient vectors are finite mathematical records.  They may organize a classifier or describe the finite search space used by an interval test, but they are not themselves a biological interval annotation.  When a contact row is used, the contact must remain separately visible as a curated biological input; it is not absorbed into the codon geometry.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The admissible claim is the following: the finite row $\mathrm{h0.R.motif.compression}$ exactly identifies a thirteen-codon motif set $R$ with no missing and no extra codons, and this row can participate in an ORF-eligibility boundary packet whose biological reading is limited to start/stop-window eligibility conditions.  The self-check row reinforces this restriction by recording a gate count of $3$ and the boundary statement that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.

Nothing stronger follows locally.  The chapter does not claim translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, organism-level effect, or universal biological law.  Such conclusions would require additional observations of the appropriate kind.  In particular, start/stop enumeration can be a rule for testing interval eligibility, but it cannot by itself establish that a sequence is translated, that a protein product exists, that the product folds, that the fold is physically admissible, or that the product performs a function.  The finite numbers above therefore serve as audited coordinates for a restricted ORF-eligibility packet, with the biological layer kept separate from the codon-combinatorial layer.
