\subsection{Motif compression as an ORF-eligibility boundary record}
\label{sec:h0-r-motif-compression-or-eligibility-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite packet.}
The record named \texttt{h0.R.motif.compression} is a finite codon-level compression statement. Its direct checked content is not an open-reading-frame claim. It says that the distinguished set $R$ is exactly the motif union returned by the finite enumeration, with no missing codons and no surplus codons. The verified row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the compressed motif carrier has $13$ codons. The same run reports \texttt{no\_extra} as the empty list and \texttt{no\_missing} as the empty list, so the equality is an exact finite-set equality rather than a sampled approximation.

\paragraph{Ambient codon frame.}
The packet is read inside the larger enumerated set $M$. The linked finite check \texttt{h0.M.equals.WNR.CUN} gives $|M|=20$ and lists
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AAG},\texttt{ACA},\texttt{ACG},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{AUG},\\
&\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UCG},\\
&\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UGG},\texttt{UUA},\texttt{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The compression therefore occupies $13$ of the $20$ codons in this finite frame. A separate saturation check reports that $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ contains $18$ codons, namely all listed members of $M$ except \texttt{ACA} and \texttt{ACG}, while the boundary contribution for $f_3$ is empty. In scalar terms, the compressed set has size $13$, the ambient set has size $20$, and the $f_3$-saturated image has size $18$.

\paragraph{Geometric bookkeeping.}
The quotient geometry attached to $M$ is also finite. The enumerated $f$-vector for $M$ is $(20,38,26,8,1)$, and the quotient $f$-vector is $(10,14,6,1)$. The projected quotient has size $10$. These numbers are useful only as internal bookkeeping for the codon enumeration. They do not by themselves identify an expressed transcript, a translated peptide, a folded molecule, or a biological role. The packet is therefore an eligibility-boundary record: it can say which codons and quotient cells were present in the finite symbolic enumeration, but it cannot turn that enumeration into a biological interval without a separate observation that names such an interval.

\paragraph{ORF boundary reading.}
The attached ORF seed is explicitly weaker than an ORF assertion. The start/stop-window enumeration check loaded one finite nucleotide-window manifest with \texttt{window\_count} $=1$, and it classified all $1$ of its $1$ input windows by the prescribed start/stop enumeration. The reported scope is only external window start/stop enumeration, and the row states that no geometry-to-translation promotion was made. This matters because the motif compression includes codons such as \texttt{AUG}, \texttt{UAA}, \texttt{UAG}, and \texttt{UGA} in the surrounding finite frame, but start and stop symbols alone are not an observed coding event.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
A later contact packet supplies a stronger, separately delimited ORF-eligibility test, but still only at the interval-eligibility layer. It loaded $168$ curated interval rows from a RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval dataset for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}. The input classes present were boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs. In the reported statistics, $72$ positive intervals were accepted with accept rate $1.0$, and the one-sided exact binomial upper-tail value was $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The matched decoy control rejected $72$ of $72$ decoys with reject rate $1.0$ and the same one-sided value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The paired baseline comparison recorded $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The boundary-case audit recorded $24$ boundary cases with $0$ promoted and promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Held-out stability.}
The interval contact also reports three held-out splits. Each split had $24$ positives and $24$ decoys. The left-locus fold, middle-locus fold, and right-locus fold each passed with positive accept rate $1.0$ and decoy reject rate $1.0$. These numbers support only the stated finite-contact behavior of the frozen interval classifier on the curated rows. They do not enlarge the claim to all genomes, all transcripts, all organisms, or all possible boundary windows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow. The motif compression verifies an exact finite equality for $R$ and gives a finite ambient frame $M$ with associated saturation and quotient counts. The ORF-related rows add a boundary seed and, where a curated interval contact is present, a finite interval-eligibility result on $168$ rows. None of this entails translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality. Such stronger claims require their own contact capable of testing that layer. The self-check row is consistent with this restriction: it records a boundary-only status and a checked gate count of $3$, with no promotion of codon geometry across biological layers.
