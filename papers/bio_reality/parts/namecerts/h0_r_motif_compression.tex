\subsection{R-motif compression and the ORF eligibility boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-r-motif-compression}

\paragraph{Finite codon packet.}
This record concerns the finite compression claim identified by $h0.R.motif.compression$, and uses it only as a codon-level packet. The verified motif union is the thirteen-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit reports no missing codons and no extra codons for this equality, so the compression is not a statistical tendency or a biological assertion; it is an exact set identity over the displayed RNA triplets. The surrounding finite carrier $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is verified as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local data distinguish a thirteen-codon compressed region $R$ from a twenty-codon ambient list $M$. The claim is deliberately finite: every codon appearing in the statement is listed, and the verification result is exhausted by membership in those displayed sets.

\paragraph{Compression relative to saturation.}
The saturation check supplies a second finite coordinate on the same ambient object. It verifies
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the saturated set contains $18$ codons, while the two excluded codons are exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$. The same row records an empty $f3$ boundary obstruction. In ordinary mathematical language, the compression point does not by itself cover all of $M$, but after the recorded $f3$ saturation it reaches every codon of $M$ except those two named triplets. The equality
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}
\end{aligned}
$$
was also verified in the same finite row, with the displayed right-hand side evaluating to the same twenty codons in $M$. These statements are exact bookkeeping facts about the codon list and its derived finite operators; they are not evidence that any nucleotide interval is translated or expressed.

The quotient row provides a compact geometric signature for $M$. The finite vector for $M$ is recorded as $(20,38,26,8,1)$, while the quotient vector is $(10,14,6,1)$ and the quotient has size $10$. The verified quotient representatives are the ten binary rows

This quotient information is useful because it fixes the size and shape of the finite coordinate system in which the compression is being read. It does not add any independent biological observation. In particular, the passage from $20$ codons to $10$ quotient representatives is a finite identification inside the packet, not a measurement of transcription, ribosome engagement, protein production, folding, phenotype, or fitness.

\paragraph{ORF eligibility seed.}
The biological object attached to this row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration. At the seed level, the record is intentionally weaker than an ORF claim. It says that a start/stop-window rule can be prepared as a testable boundary, but the finite codon geometry alone must not be counted as an observed biological interval. The window-enumeration row reports one input window and one classified window, with scope restricted to external-window start/stop enumeration only and snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$. The associated value explicitly marks the output as $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$, meaning that the finite rule stops at the eligibility layer. The self-check row records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. These facts are consistent with the chapter's local reading: the compression supplies a finite coordinate, while ORF eligibility requires contact with an independently curated sequence interval before any biological interval claim is made.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
The curated contact row can support interval-level ORF eligibility for its own curated finite dataset, but it does not retroactively turn the $R$-motif compression into a biological mechanism. The finite compression claim remains a codon-set equality, the saturation claim remains a finite closure calculation, and the quotient claim remains a finite geometry calculation. No translation realization follows from these rows. No folded molecular structure follows. No physical admissibility, biochemical mechanism, organismal function, or universal biological law follows. Such claims would require their own observations and their own finite rows. The present chapter therefore keeps two statements separate: first, $R$ is exactly the thirteen-codon motif union displayed above inside a twenty-codon ambient set; second, ORF eligibility can only be asserted when an independent interval contact supplies the relevant biological sequence evidence.
