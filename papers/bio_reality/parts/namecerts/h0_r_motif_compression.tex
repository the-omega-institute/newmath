Thus the finite assertion is not that an ORF has been observed, but that the row's compressed motif register contains precisely these thirteen codons.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The scientific content of the present row is the separation between finite codon geometry and biological realization. The motif compression fact says exactly which thirteen codons constitute $R$ and how that list sits with the twenty-element register $M$, its saturation, and its quotient geometry. The curated interval contact row, when used, may support an ORF-eligibility claim within its own dataset scope. Neither the compression row nor the curated interval row establishes translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, cellular mechanism, or a global law. Those stronger claims require separate contacts with the corresponding biological layer. The self-check row \texttt{0f47d2f0cf41} is consistent with this restriction: it records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
