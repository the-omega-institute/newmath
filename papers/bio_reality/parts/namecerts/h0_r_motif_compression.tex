\paragraph{ORF-eligibility seed boundary.}
The biological object named by this row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration.  That layer is deliberately weaker than translation.  A start--stop window can be checked as a syntactic interval candidate only after an external interval contact supplies the observed sequence coordinates or curated annotation.  Before such contact, the row remains a boundary test: it records what would have to be checked, not that the biological interval has already been observed.  The support condition is therefore local and negative: if the pipeline asserts ORF eligibility, translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality from the motif-compression coordinate alone, the row fails its own boundary discipline.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The self-check row reports $checked\_gate\_count=3$ and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  The resulting local rule is simple.  The compressed motif row $R$ may be used as a finite symbolic coordinate; the $20$-member $M$ row, the $18$-member saturation row, and the quotient vectors may be used as finite combinatorial audits; the curated interval rows may support start--stop ORF eligibility only where their external contacts actually apply.  None of these records, singly or jointly, proves translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality unless a separate contact specifically measures that higher layer.
