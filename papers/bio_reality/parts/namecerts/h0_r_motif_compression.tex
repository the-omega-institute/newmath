\subsection{Motif Compression and ORF-Eligibility Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-h0-r-motif-compression-orf-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite motif row.}
This record begins with a fully enumerated codon-level motif compression statement.  The verified row for claim $h0.R.motif.compression$ identifies $R$ with the thirteen-codon motif union
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit attached to experiment run $338799579a87$ reports no missing codons and no additional codons: $no\_missing=\emptyset$ and $no\_extra=\emptyset$.  Thus the compression assertion in this chapter is only the finite equality between the named motif class and the listed thirteen strings.  It is not a biological observation by itself, and it is not treated as evidence that any of these codons occurs in a translated transcript, a stable molecular product, or a functional locus.

\paragraph{Ambient finite codon set.}
The same packet records the surrounding twenty-codon universe $M$ through the verification row $h0.M.equals.WNR.CUN$.  In experiment run $a53ef0f36703$, the cardinality is reported as $|M|=20$, with

&CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.

This places the thirteen-codon $R$ row inside a larger finite enumeration rather than inside an open-ended biological population.  A second closure calculation, $h0.f3.saturation$, reports that $Sat_{f3}(R)$ contains eighteen codons, namely every codon in $M$ except $ACA$ and $ACG$:
$$
\begin{aligned}

&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same row records $M=Med(R)\cup\{ACA\}$ and an empty $f3$ boundary residual.  These are finite combinatorial facts about the codon strings in the packet; they do not create an experimental statement about expression, translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological mechanism, or universal genomic behavior.

Nevertheless, the quotient is still a symbolic classifier.  It can delimit which finite patterns are being discussed, but it cannot certify that an organism contains a corresponding open reading frame unless a separate biological contact supplies a sequence interval or curated annotation.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility seed.}
The attached conjecture is deliberately lower than a biological claim.  Its biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration, and its claimed layer is $orf\_eligibility$.  In the minimal form stored with this row, the carrier and readback are both explicitly awaiting contact with biological data, the only listed distinction is also awaiting such contact, and the internal structure field is empty.  This means that the codon geometry is allowed to propose a boundary test, but it is not allowed to assert an observed ORF.  The local rule is strict: start/stop-window eligibility alone does not imply translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  Any promotion above this boundary would require a separate sequence, transcript, or curated interval input capable of testing the proposed biological interval.

A stronger curated interval row, $h2.orf\_eligibility.curated\_interval\_contact$, loaded $168$ rows from an NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval dataset for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 accession $NC\_000913.3$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$. The held-out split stability check has three splits, each with $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
For the present row, the motif compression fact is ready as a finite mathematical statement: $R$ is exactly the thirteen listed codons, with neither omissions nor additions in the verified ledger.  The related $M$, saturation, and quotient rows provide concrete finite context: $|M|=20$, $|Sat_{f3}(R)|=18$, $|\pi(M)|=10$, and the recorded vectors are $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and $(10,14,6,1)$.  The ORF-eligibility material remains boundary-controlled unless the curated interval contact is explicitly invoked.  Even when the $168$-row curated contact is invoked, its conclusion is still confined to ORF-interval eligibility under the recorded protocol; it does not establish translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or a universal law across genomes.
