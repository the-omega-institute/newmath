\subsection{The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ two-axis closure boundary for $f_{3}$ stress}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-f3-stress-two-axis-closure}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate under review.}
This record concerns the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate after amino-acid projection, restricted to the third-position $f_{3}$ stress direction and to rows where a translation-layer readout could in principle be matched. The finite saturation check gives the concrete codon support on which the stress coordinate is allowed to act. The mediated set is
$M=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$,
while the $f_{3}$ saturation of the $R$ component removes exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$\operatorname{Sat}_{f_{3}}(R)=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The reported $f_{3}$ boundary is empty, so the finite row is not a boundary-codon artifact. The biological question is therefore narrower: among CDS-by-organism residual rows, does this codon-coordinate distinction survive after the amino-acid quotient as a bounded translation-readout contrast when tRNA or wobble weights break the third-position symmetry?

\paragraph{Observed finite-row outcome.}
The powered two-axis closure experiment requested 16 organisms and computed 11 organisms with at least 500 proteins per organism, using 41 synonymous design columns and 5 folds. Its recorded status is passed, with the conclusion \emph{third\_axis\_present}. The stated finite reason is that $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^{2}$ and that the $d_{\perp}$ directions are mutually same-signed across organisms. In symbols, the relevant decomposition can be read as a refusal of the two-axis span as a complete account:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{span}(d_{\mathrm{tRNA}},d_{f_{3}}) &\not\equiv \operatorname{span}(d_{\mathrm{observed}}),\\
d_{\perp} &\neq 0
\end{aligned}
$$
under the finite powered panel. The companion residual-axis run over the same requested/computed organism counts and the same 41 synonymous columns identifies the dominant non-tRNA residual direction as $f_{3}$ translation selection. The core tRNA-adaptation run also passes under the same 11-of-16 organism panel, so the finite picture is not a replacement of tRNA adaptation by $f_{3}$ stress; it is a layered residual result: a tRNA-associated core is present, an $f_{3}$-aligned residual direction is present, and a further perpendicular direction remains after the two-axis span is taken.

\paragraph{Spectrum and supporting finite checks.}
The axis-strength spectrum was computed on 11 organisms from 16 requested organisms, again with 5 folds, at least 500 proteins per organism, and 41 synonymous design columns. Its descriptive summary says that captured spectra are led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, with per-organism dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$. The domain counts are imbalanced, with $n=\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$, so no domain-level law is read from this table. The largest GC3 rank effect reported there is $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$. The third-axis identity run gives a provisional alignment of $d_{\perp}$ to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The corresponding mechanism-discrimination row reports that GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^{2}$, yielding the finite classification \emph{selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs\_external}. These numbers make the chapter a residual-coordinate and translation-boundary record, not a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Translation-row condition.}
The exact readback allowed here is a controlled survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}$ for a bounded translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. A positive statement requires a same-organism or same-CDS joined table containing codon counts, the $f_{3}$ residual coordinate, context tRNA or wobble weights with $w_c\neq w_{c^{f_3}}$, a translation readout, and matched mRNA abundance when TE is used. The controls must include amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, rank sufficiency, and simpler codon baselines. Without such a joined row, the powered two-axis closure result says only that the coordinate system needs a third direction; it does not say that the $f_{3}$ coordinate has been observed as ribosome traffic, translation efficiency, protein output, structure, localization, stability, function, phenotype, or a general biological principle.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
The finite contacts distinguish sequence composition, tRNA-copy or wobble-weight annotations, modeled adaptation weights, ribosome or TE measurements, and mRNA controls. Codon-usage rows can test sequence-level residual geometry; tRNA-copy and tAI or stAI rows can provide bounded translation-layer weights; ribosome profiling or TE rows can test a translation-readout statistic only after same-row joining and residualization. The current record therefore permits the following claim: the powered $B^{*}_{Q6}$ panel rejects closure of the observed residual geometry by the tRNA and $f_{3}$ axes alone, and the $f_{3}$ stress coordinate is a candidate translation-readout boundary only where wobble or tRNA asymmetry breaks third-position symmetry. It does not permit the stronger claim that $f_{3}$ stress changes translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Each of those layers requires its own layer-matched contact and statistic before it can be interpreted.
