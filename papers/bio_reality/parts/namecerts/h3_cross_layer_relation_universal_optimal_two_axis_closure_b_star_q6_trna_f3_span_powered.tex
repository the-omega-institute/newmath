\subsection{A wobble-broken translation row for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$ residual}
\label{subsec:bior reality-q6-f3-wobble-broken-translation-row}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
The record concerns a finite coding-sequence residual coordinate surface after amino-acid projection.  The named coordinate is the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress direction: it pairs synonymous codons by the third-position $f_3$ operation and asks whether the resulting imbalance remains visible in a translation-layer row after standard compositional and organism-level controls.  The finite saturation check gives a concrete boundary for this coordinate.  The marked set
$M = \operatorname{Med}(R) \cup \{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains the twenty codons
$\mathrm{AAA}, \mathrm{AAG}, \mathrm{ACA}, \mathrm{ACG}, \mathrm{AGA}, \mathrm{AGG}, \mathrm{AUA}, \mathrm{AUG}, \mathrm{CUA}, \mathrm{CUC}, \mathrm{CUG}, \mathrm{CUU}, \mathrm{UAA}, \mathrm{UAG}, \mathrm{UCA}, \mathrm{UCG}, \mathrm{UGA}, \mathrm{UGG}, \mathrm{UUA}, \mathrm{UUG}$.
The $f_3$-saturation of $R$ removes exactly the two boundary codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from that list and leaves the eighteen codons
$\mathrm{AAA}, \mathrm{AAG}, \mathrm{AGA}, \mathrm{AGG}, \mathrm{AUA}, \mathrm{AUG}, \mathrm{CUA}, \mathrm{CUC}, \mathrm{CUG}, \mathrm{CUU}, \mathrm{UAA}, \mathrm{UAG}, \mathrm{UCA}, \mathrm{UCG}, \mathrm{UGA}, \mathrm{UGG}, \mathrm{UUA}, \mathrm{UUG}$.
The recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  Thus the finite codon geometry itself is not the claimed biological output; it supplies the row on which a translation readout may be tested.

\paragraph{Two-axis test and finite outcome.}
The powered two-axis closure assay tested whether a universal tRNA direction together with an $f_3$ translation-selection direction was sufficient for the observed optimal residual surface.  The run used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous-design columns.  Its recorded outcome was \emph{third-axis present}: after the tRNA and $f_3$ span were fitted, the residual direction $d_{\perp}$ still had systematically positive held-out $R^2$, and the $d_{\perp}$ directions were mutually same-signed across organisms.  In local notation, the test rejects the closure assertion
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{span}(d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}, d_{f_3}) \text{ exhausts the powered residual surface}
\end{aligned}
$$
for this finite panel.  The result is not a negative result for $f_3$-stress; it says that $f_3$-stress and tRNA adaptation do not form a complete basis for the powered $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ optimality surface.

\paragraph{Neighboring finite facts.}
The surrounding assays locate the same coordinate in a larger residual picture.  The universal-core assay was also run on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, and passed as a tRNA-adaptation core result.  The non-tRNA residual-axis assay, on the same $11/16$ organism panel and the same $41$ synonymous-design columns, identified the dominant non-tRNA direction as $f_3$ translation selection.  The third-axis identity assay then classified $d_{\perp}$ as aligned to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p = 0.000488$.  Its mechanism assay kept significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$ after GC orthogonalization, giving the finite call ``selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs external measurement.''  The per-organism axis-strength spectrum found $11$ computed organisms with captured spectra led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; all $11$ per-organism dominance counts went to $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, and the largest GC3 rank effect was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho = 0.136$.  These numbers place the $f_3$ stress row inside a nontrivial residual hierarchy rather than inside a closed two-axis model.

\paragraph{Translation readout row.}
The biological readback is deliberately narrow.  The bounded quantity is a controlled survival entry $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ for a translation readout $T_j$, where $T_j$ may be a modeled tAI or stAI weight, a ribosome-occupancy row, or a translation-efficiency row.  The intended statistic is a same-organism or same-CDS comparison after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, matched mRNA abundance where applicable, rank sufficiency, and simpler-codon-baseline controls.  In the positive case the row would satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
  S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j} > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the improvement surviving the declared baselines.  In the null case every such row remains non-positive or rank-degenerate.  The three-organism all-$1.0$ controlled surface is therefore treated as saturation of rank, not as evidence of survival.

\paragraph{Wobble asymmetry condition.}
The specific mechanism gate is third-position symmetry breaking.  The coordinate becomes eligible for a translation-layer interpretation only in organisms or contexts where the relevant tRNA or wobble weight distinguishes a codon from its $f_3$ partner, so that $w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}$.  Codon topology alone, and even a modeled adaptation-weight surface alone, does not establish a dynamical readout.  A separate tRNA-supply assay supports the plausibility of such asymmetry: over $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, with $6$ skipped and $1000$ permutations at $\alpha = 0.05$, $R$ sense codons were systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tRNA gene copy number plus dos Reis wobble weights.  This supports an $R$-avoidance supply mechanism, but it does not by itself compute $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$.

\paragraph{Higher-layer refusal.}
This chapter does not infer protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism, or a universal biological law from the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate.  Those conclusions require their own layer-matched rows and controls.  The fourth-mechanism assays illustrate why this restriction matters: the three-axis sufficiency test found a fourth mechanism present on the $11/16$ organism panel; the mRNA-stability assay classified the fourth axis as not dominated by mRNA stability; the elongation-dwell assay in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} returned a partial call; the codon-pair assay returned ``not codon pair'' with $50$ shuffle nulls; and the translation-accuracy assay returned ``not accuracy'' using $200$ permutations.  These finite outcomes show that residual axes can persist while individual biological interpretations fail.  The local conclusion is therefore exact: tRNA adaptation and $f_3$ translation selection are both real finite coordinates in the powered panel, but their two-axis span is not closed; the $f_3$ coordinate becomes a bounded translation-readout claim only after a nondegenerate, controlled $S_{f_3\text{-stress},T_j}$ row is supplied.
