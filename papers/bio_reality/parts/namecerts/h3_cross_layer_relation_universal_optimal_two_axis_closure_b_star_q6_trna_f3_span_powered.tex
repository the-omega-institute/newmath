\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress translation-readout boundary}\label{subsec:bq6-f3-stress-translation-readout-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record isolates a finite coding-sequence coordinate surface rather than a general biological law. The carrier is the finite CDS-by-organism residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate and to the third-position involutive stress direction $f_3$. The finite saturation row fixes the relevant codon support. The ambient $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup ACA$ row is
$\{AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}$,
while the $f_3$-saturated $R$ row is
$\{AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}$.
The reported $f_3$ boundary residue is the empty list, so the finite row records no leftover boundary codon outside the declared saturated support. The biological question is therefore not whether an extra codon remains to be added. It is whether the already saturated $f_3$ residual direction survives after quotienting by amino acid and after ordinary composition controls have been applied.

\paragraph{Two-axis test and observed obstruction.}
The powered two-axis closure run evaluated whether the tRNA-adaptation direction and the $f_3$ residual direction exhaust the observable optimal residual surface. The experiment was computed on $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, using $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. Its conclusion was \emph{third\_axis\_present}. The reason recorded in the finite row is that $d_\perp$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and that the $d_\perp$ directions are mutually same-signed across organisms. Thus the bounded result is negative for two-axis sufficiency: the tRNA plus $f_3$ span is not closed under the available powered residual readout.

In the notation of this chapter, let $Q_{f_3}$ denote the amino-acid-quotiented $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate and let $T_j$ range over bounded translation readouts such as modeled tAI, modeled stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency. The intended survival statistic has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3,T_j}
  &= \operatorname{score}(T_j\mid Z,Q_{f_3})-\operatorname{score}(T_j\mid Z),\\
Z
  &= \{\text{amino-acid composition},\text{ organism},GC3,\text{ CDS length},M\text{-density},\text{ mRNA control}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This formula is a local definition of the readout gate used in this record. It is not a claim that a positive measured ribosome or translation-efficiency entry has already been observed for the $f_3$ coordinate.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring finite rows.}
Several adjacent powered rows constrain the interpretation. The universal core tRNA-adaptation row passed on the same scale: $11$ organisms were computed out of $16$, with $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The residual optimal-axis row also passed and identified the dominant non-tRNA residual direction as $f_3\_translation\_selection$ under the same $11/16$ organism count, $5$-fold design, and $41$ synonymous columns. These two rows justify treating the $f_3$ direction as a serious bounded residual coordinate rather than as a naming artifact.

The same evidence also refuses a stronger conclusion. The axis-strength spectrum row reports that the captured spectra are led on average by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, with per-organism dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$ and a largest GC3 rank effect for $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ of $\rho=0.136$. The third-axis identity row provisionally identifies $d_\perp$ with usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. The third-axis mechanism row states that the GC-orthogonalized $d_\perp$ keeps significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$, while classifying the mechanism as selection rather than GC, not wobble-clustered, and still needing an outside contact for the CSC or positional identity. Together these rows say that the two-axis surface leaves a reproducible residual axis; they do not say that $Q_{f_3}$ by itself has been measured as a ribosome, protein, structural, functional, or phenotype cause.

\paragraph{Wobble-breaking condition.}
The proposed biological contact for the $f_3$ row is asymmetric translation readout. If a codon $c$ and its third-position partner $c^{f_3}$ have equal contextual weight, then the $f_3$ contrast is invisible to that readout after amino-acid quotienting. A non-vacuous translation row therefore requires a local weight break
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_c &\neq w_{c^{f_3}},\\
S_{f_3,T_j} &>0
\quad\text{after the declared controls and after simpler codon baselines.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Modeled tAI or stAI weights may supply a bounded codon-adaptation readback, and ribosome profiling with matched mRNA abundance may supply a measured translation-efficiency readback. These are distinct contacts. A modeled wobble asymmetry is not an observed ribosome-occupancy row, and a ribosome-profiling contact name is not a computed survival statistic unless the same-CDS matrix has actually been joined and residualized.

\paragraph{Negative and positive evidence limits.}
The boundary-set tRNA-poverty row supplies a separate mechanistic hint: in $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested organisms, with $6$ skipped and $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, $R$ sense codons were systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. This supports a tRNA-supply interpretation for $R$ avoidance, but it still operates at the codon-weight and tRNA-supply layer. The fourth-mechanism rows further delimit the field: the three-axis sufficiency row reports \emph{fourth\_mechanism\_present}; the mRNA-stability row classifies the tested yeast half-life alignment as low and concludes that the fourth axis is not mRNA stability in that assay; the elongation-dwell row is only partial in Saccharomyces cerevisiae; the codon-pair row concludes \emph{not\_codon\_pair}; and the translation-accuracy row concludes \emph{not\_accuracy} over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ with $200$ permutations. These exclusions sharpen the finite readout boundary, but they do not promote the $f_3$ coordinate to a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Local falsification rule.}
The chapter-specific falsification condition is strict. The $f_3$-stress conjecture fails as a translation-readout claim if the controlled entries $S_{f_3,T_j}$ remain non-positive, rank-degenerate, or reducible to simpler codon baselines across a powered organism panel. In particular, an all-$1.0$ matrix from a three-organism rank-saturated design is a null boundary, not survival evidence. Conversely, a positive entry may only be reported at the bounded translation layer for the specific readout and control set used to compute it. It does not by itself establish protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, localization, stability, phenotype, mechanism, or universality.

\paragraph{Readout verdict.}
The current finite rows support the following bounded statement. The $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$ residual coordinate is a powered, nontrivial candidate translation-readout axis after amino-acid quotienting, and the tRNA plus $f_3$ two-axis span is empirically incomplete on the recorded $11$-organism powered panel. The positive evidence is therefore a coordinate-and-spectrum result with a concrete obstruction, not a completed measured translation-survival result. A later same-organism and same-CDS matrix joining codon counts, wobble-breaking tAI or stAI weights, measured ribosome or translation-efficiency rows, and matched mRNA abundance could turn the boundary into a positive $S_{f_3,T_j}$ entry. Until that matrix exists, the chapter remains deliberately confined to the finite residual surface and its translation-readout test condition.
