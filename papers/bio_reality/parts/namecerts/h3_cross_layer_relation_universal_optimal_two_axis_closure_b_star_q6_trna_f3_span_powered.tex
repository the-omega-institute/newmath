\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns the finite coding-sequence residual surface obtained after amino-acid quotienting, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate family and, inside that family, to the $f_3$-stress direction.  The coordinate is not treated as a protein-level variable.  Its carrier is the finite row table whose rows are coding sequences grouped by organism and whose columns include synonymous codon residuals, organism controls, amino-acid composition controls, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and translation-layer readouts only when such readouts are explicitly joined to the same row.  The intended readback is therefore a controlled survival entry $S_{f_3,T_j}$ for a translation readout $T_j$, such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency.  No downstream abundance, localization, folding, stability, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universal biological law is inferred from the coordinate itself.

\paragraph{Powered two-axis test.}
The powered two-axis closure run $99dc7e2d5abf$ tested whether the tRNA axis together with the $f_3$ residual axis closes the optimal residual surface.  The experiment used five folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, and used $41$ synonymous-design columns.  The result passed with conclusion \emph{third\_axis\_present}.  The recorded reason is that $d_\perp$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and that the $d_\perp$ directions are mutually same-signed across organisms.  In finite-row terms, the two-axis span is informative but not closed: after fitting the tRNA and $f_3$ components, there remains a reproducible residual direction that survives held-out testing.

\paragraph{Position of the $f_3$ component.}
The residual-axis run for mutation versus selection, also over five folds with $11$ computed organisms, $16$ requested organisms, a $500$-protein minimum, and $41$ synonymous-design columns, identifies the dominant non-tRNA residual optimality axis as \emph{$f_3$\_translation\_selection}. The conclusion is still bounded: it licenses a controlled translation-readout question, not a protein-output or mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Local falsification rule.}
For each organism or context, let $w_c$ be the context tRNA or wobble weight of codon $c$, and let $c^{f_3}$ be its third-position $f_3$ counterpart.  The $f_3$ coordinate is eligible for a translation-row test only where the finite contact distinguishes the pair, for example where $w_c\ne w_{c^{f_3}}$.  The local survival question is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f_3,T_j} > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, matched mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls.  The claim is broken at the translation interface if all such controlled entries are non-positive, or if the fitted surface is rank-degenerate, including the three-organism all-$1.0$ boundary described in the packet memory.  A modeled tAI or stAI surface may be used as a bounded codon-adaptation readout, but it is not the same object as observed ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
The powered rows establish a finite residual-geometry result: the tRNA plus $f_3$ span does not close the observed optimal residual surface, and a reproducible third direction remains under held-out testing.  They do not establish that $f_3$ stress changes translation in measured ribosome data.  They also do not establish folding, physical admissibility, protein abundance, localization, stability, biological function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Each of those stronger statements requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In this chapter, the admissible scientific claim is narrower: after amino-acid quotienting, $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance is a finite candidate translation-readout coordinate, and it becomes dynamically testable only in rows where tRNA or wobble weights break the third-position symmetry.
