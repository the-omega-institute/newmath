\subsection{The $B^*_{Q6}$ tRNA--$f_3$ plane and the residual third axis}\label{sec:bior-bstar-q6-trna-f3-plane}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Coordinate surface.}
This record isolates the finite coding-sequence residual surface obtained after amino-acid projection, organism matching, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and simpler codon-composition controls. The distinguished coordinate is the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress row: it compares synonymous codons paired by the third-position $f_3$ operation, but only after the amino-acid quotient has removed amino-acid composition as a direct explanatory path. The finite saturation row fixes the active codon set rather than treating the coordinate as an unconstrained alphabetic artifact. In the reported run, the combined $M$ row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_3$-saturated row removes the two ACA/ACG boundary points and leaves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite boundary list is empty, $\partial_{f_3}=\varnothing$, so the row being tested is not a boundary exception produced by an unmatched codon edge. It is a controlled synonymous residual coordinate with a specified finite support.

\paragraph{Two-axis hypothesis.}
The biological claim is deliberately narrow. A tRNA-adaptation direction and an $f_3$-stress translation-selection direction are allowed to span the first two explanatory axes of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface only when wobble or tRNA weights break the third-position $f_3$ symmetry. In the powered two-axis closure run, the design used $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, $41$ synonymous design columns, and computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested. The run status was passed, but its conclusion was not two-axis closure: the recorded conclusion was \emph{third\_axis\_present}. The reason was that $d_\perp$ had systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and that the $d_\perp$ directions were mutually same-signed across organisms. Thus the finite record says that the tRNA--$f_3$ span is informative but incomplete under the declared controls.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring finite rows.}
The same data family makes the two-axis interpretation more precise. The universal core tRNA-adaptation run also passed with $5$ folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The non-tRNA residual optimality run identified the dominant residual axis as $f_3$\_translation\_selection under the same row counts and fold structure. These two rows justify treating the proposed plane as biologically motivated at the translation-readout level. They do not justify treating the plane as complete. The axis-strength spectrum row reported that captured spectra are led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ on average, followed by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$, with per-organism dominant counts $\{d_{\mathrm{resid4}}:11\}$. Its domain counts were imbalanced, with $1$ archaeal, $2$ bacterial, and $8$ eukaryotic organisms, and the largest GC3 rank effect was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$. The finite row therefore separates an observed residual structure from a universal law.

\paragraph{Third-axis identity and mechanism pressure.}
The third-axis identity run classified $d_\perp$ as aligned to usage frequency within family. It reported mean cosine $0.128$ and a one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$, again on $5$ folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The mechanism row then reported selection-not-GC, not-wobble-clustered, and a CSC/positional identity requiring further contact; the stated reason was that GC-orthogonalized $d_\perp$ kept significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$. In local terms, the third direction is not explained away by GC3 and is not reduced to the tRNA--$f_3$ plane. It is a residual coordinate needing its own layer-matched measurement before any higher biological interpretation is assigned.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The only admissible readback in this chapter is a bounded translation-row entry such as $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ for tAI, stAI, ribosome-occupancy, or translation-efficiency data. A modeled tAI or stAI row can serve as a bounded codon-adaptation readout; it is not the same thing as observed ribosome occupancy. A ribosome-profiling or TE row would have to be organism-matched and CDS-matched, paired with mRNA abundance, and evaluated after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, and simpler-codon-baseline controls. The declared support condition is a strictly positive controlled survival entry, $S_{ij}>0$, with the agreed partial-$R^2$ threshold after ablation. The declared break condition is that every controlled entry remains non-positive or description-length non-improving. A three-organism all-$1.0$ matrix is not counted as survival; it is rank-degenerate and therefore a null boundary.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The finite rows do not by themselves establish translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, localization, stability, or universality. Translation itself requires a separate measured or explicitly bounded translation contact and a non-vacuous controlled statistic. Folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, localization, stability, and mechanism each require their own layer-appropriate contact after the translation row has survived. The powered result should therefore be read as a negative closure result for the proposed two-axis span: tRNA adaptation and $f_3$-stress explain important controlled structure, but the surviving $d_\perp$ row forces a third coordinate before downstream biological claims can be made.

\paragraph{Auxiliary exclusions.}
Several fourth-mechanism probes keep the interpretation bounded. The three-axis sufficiency run concluded \emph{fourth\_mechanism\_present} on $5$ folds, $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns. The mRNA-stability probe classified the fourth axis as not mRNA stability in its assay, because yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$. The codon-pair probe concluded \emph{not\_codon\_pair} using $50$ shuffle-null samples. The translation-accuracy probe concluded \emph{not\_accuracy} with $200$ permutations, again over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ and $41$ synonymous design columns. The elongation-dwell probe was only partial and was organism-specific to \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. These exclusions do not identify the remaining mechanism; they only prevent the $B^*_{Q6}$ tRNA--$f_3$ record from being overread as a completed cross-layer explanation.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
The chapter-level verdict is that the $B^*_{Q6}$ tRNA--$f_3$ plane is a controlled, rank-powered coordinate relation, not a closed universal optimum. Its finite support is specified by the $M$ and $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ codon lists, its powered estimation used $11$ computed organisms out of $16$, $5$ folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns, and its decisive row reports $d_\perp$ as held-out-positive and same-signed across organisms. The correct scientific reading is therefore a three-axis pressure: tRNA adaptation, $f_3$ translation-selection, and an additional usage-frequency-within-family residual direction must be kept distinct until a matched translation-readout contact supplies a nondegenerate survival statistic.
