\paragraph{Finite contact.}
This record attaches the alternative-polyadenylation side of the BioReality ledger to the Q6 cross-layer program without allowing the APA contact to promote a codon-coordinate association into a translation or protein mechanism.  The attached mouse PolyASite-style contact contains $167610$ polyadenylation clusters assigned across $20110$ genes, with experiment identifier $2ac7a388b975$ and verification timestamp 2026-06-24T09:42:38+00:00.  These two row counts are the finite data used here: they certify that the APA/PAS surface has a large mouse gene-by-cluster table, but they do not by themselves provide a fitted PAS-strength coefficient, a codon-residual coefficient, a mediation coefficient, a permutation $p$-value, or a translation-conditioned protein-omics score.

\paragraph{Objects kept distinct.}
Let $G$ denote the finite set of mouse genes appearing in the APA contact and let $C_g$ denote the set of polyadenylation clusters assigned to gene $g$.  The verified contact gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \sum_{g \in G} |C_g| &= 167610,\\
  |G| &= 20110.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mean cluster multiplicity represented by this finite contact is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
  167610 / 20110 &\approx 8.3347
\end{aligned}
$$
clusters per gene.  This is an APA-row statement, not a codon-usage, translation-efficiency, ribosome-occupancy, or protein-abundance statement.  A future PAS-strength statistic would have to add per-cluster PAS features, an isoform or site-usage readout, and a matched gene-level or transcript-level control design before any relation to B$^*_Q6$ coordinates could be scored.

\paragraph{Local admissible statement.}
The admissible BioReality statement is therefore narrow: a mouse APA contact exists at the scale of $167610$ polyadenylation clusters over $20110$ genes, and it is large enough to be a plausible future carrier for a PAS-strength or APA-usage statistic.  It is not yet a passed Q6-to-APA relation, and it is not a Q6-to-protein relation.  A valid finite statistic would require rows of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  (g,c, q_i, A_c, Z_g)
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g$ is a gene, $c$ is a polyadenylation cluster, $q_i$ is a matched B$^*_Q6$ synonymous residual coordinate for the coding sequence associated to $g$, $A_c$ is a measured APA or PAS-strength readout for cluster $c$, and $Z_g$ contains amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density, mRNA abundance, and any required batch or transcript-structure controls.  Only after this finite panel is attached may one estimate a controlled APA relation such as
$$
\begin{aligned}
  S^{QA}_{i} &= R^2(A \mid Z,q_i)-R^2(A \mid Z)
\end{aligned}
$$
or its multicoordinate analogue.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from the $167610$-cluster mouse APA contact.  No such conclusion follows from the existence of Q6 codon residual coordinates either.  A translation claim would require a separate ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency contact joined to the same coding sequences with matched mRNA controls.  A protein-omics claim would require matched protein-abundance rows and an explicit report of whether conditioning on a measured translation readout removes, reduces, or leaves the codon-to-protein signal.  A folding, localization, stability, function, phenotype, or mechanism claim would require its own layer-specific measurement.  The present chapter is ready only as a finite APA/PAS boundary chapter: it records the mouse row surface and states precisely what cannot be inferred until the missing joined statistics are supplied.
