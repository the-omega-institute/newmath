\subsection{Polyadenylation-Site Rows as a Boundary for Synonymous Residual Readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-apa-pas-strength-polyasite-mouse}

\paragraph{Finite contact.}
The mouse PolyASite contact supplies a large external row surface for alternative polyadenylation, but the present contact is only a row-count and gene-count certification. The attached run records $167610$ polyadenylation-site clusters over $20110$ genes, with runtime recorded as $0.0$ seconds for the row-count extraction. These numbers are sufficient to show that the proposed alternative-polyadenylation layer is not empty: there is a finite mouse table large enough to support later joins against coding-sequence annotations, gene identifiers, untranslated-region windows, and polyadenylation-site strength summaries. They are not sufficient to claim any relation between a synonymous residual coordinate and polyadenylation strength, because the run does not yet report a joined CDS-by-site matrix, a PAS score, a usage ratio, a tissue or condition axis, or a controlled regression surface.

\paragraph{Coordinate being tested.}
The upstream coding-sequence coordinate family is the nine-entry $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual basis after amino-acid quotienting. The same coordinate list is used in the protein-omics and mRNA-stability contacts: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The row design that has already been exercised in related contacts residualizes these coordinates against $24$ control columns, with $rank(Z)=23$ and $rank(\widetilde X)=9$ in the protein-abundance runs. For mouse protein abundance specifically, the matched PAXdb/CDS contact used $19395$ joined proteins, $19372$ residual degrees of freedom after $Z$, and produced $R^2_{QP}=0.049827984062539195$ with all $9$ coordinates surviving the positive threshold. That result is a protein-abundance association, not an alternative-polyadenylation association.

\paragraph{What the APA row count can and cannot support.}
The present mouse APA contact can support only the existence of a finite candidate surface:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{clusters}} &= 167610,\\
N_{\mathrm{genes}} &= 20110,\\
\mathrm{runtime}_{\mathrm{count}} &= 0.0\ \mathrm{sec}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A claim about $B^{*}_{Q6}$ and polyadenylation requires a stronger finite object. At minimum, each retained row must attach a coding-sequence residual vector $q_i$, a gene or transcript key, a polyadenylation-site usage or strength readout $A_k$, and controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding length, M-density, expression or mRNA abundance where available, and site-level confounders such as UTR position, local nucleotide composition, and batch or dataset source. The statistic then has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QA}_{ik} &= \Delta R^2(q_i \to A_k \mid Z_{APA}),\\
Z_{APA} &= \{\mathrm{AA},\ \mathrm{GC3},\ \mathrm{length},\ \mathrm{M\mbox{-}density},\ \mathrm{mRNA},\ \mathrm{site\ controls}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No such $S^{QA}_{ik}$ entries, rank report, effect signs, permutation values, or held-out scores are present in the current APA record.

\paragraph{Cross-layer context from neighboring contacts.}
The strongest related positive contact is not APA but direct protein abundance. Across the powered protein-abundance panel, the same $9$ residual coordinates were tested after amino-acid composition, GC3, length, and M-density controls; examples include $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.10767526677985896$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.04454565118019448$ in human, and $0.049827984062539195$ in mouse. The mRNA-stability contacts show separate readouts: yeast reports $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ and cosine $0.8040935468384264$ against the same-join protein-abundance direction; \emph{E. coli} reports $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$ with cosine $-0.7784923375569599$; human reports $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$ with cosine $0.6351183883924848$. These contacts show that the residual coordinate family has nontrivial finite behavior in several molecular readout layers. They do not transfer evidence to the mouse APA surface unless a joined APA statistic is computed on the same coordinate family under its own controls.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The modeled translation contact is explicitly weaker than a measured translation mechanism. It used $3$ organisms, computed modeled tAI weights for all $61$ sense codons, found $81$ bounded-coupling entries above threshold $0.01$, and recorded maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$. The same run states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that the result is modeled tAI bounded coupling rather than ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation. Therefore an APA association, even if later observed, would not by itself imply translation mediation. A translation-mediated statement would require matched measured translation rows $T_j$ and an attenuation or mediation fit showing that conditioning on $T_j$ removes or reduces a direct $q_i \to A_k$ or $q_i \to P_k$ signal.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The current verdict is data-limited. The PolyASite mouse row count establishes a large finite APA contact surface, but it does not establish an APA usage effect, PAS-strength effect, translation effect, protein effect, folded-structure effect, physical-admissibility effect, molecular-function effect, mechanism, organismal phenotype, or universal biological law. None of those follows from codon residual geometry, contact naming, row availability, modeled tAI weights, protein-abundance association, or mRNA-stability association. Each higher layer needs its own joined rows and its own controlled statistic. The present chapter therefore records the APA contact as a ready target for a controlled join, while refusing promotion beyond the finite counts $167610$ and $20110$ until PAS-strength or APA-usage values are attached and tested.
