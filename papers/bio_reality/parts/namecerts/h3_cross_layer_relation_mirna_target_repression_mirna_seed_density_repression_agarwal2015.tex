\subsection{A finite seed-density repression contact for miRNA targets}
\label{sec:bioreality-mirna-seed-density-repression-agarwal2015}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The miRNA target-repression record is a finite cross-layer surface whose rows are miRNA--target pairs and whose columns separate seed-density information from the downstream repression readout.  The registered run contains $11$ miRNAs and $56682$ miRNA--target pairs, with a recorded runtime of $1$ second.  Thus the chapter is not using an informal statement that miRNAs repress targets in general; it is using a bounded table whose first admissible mathematical object is a finite relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{miR}}
    &\subseteq \{1,\ldots,11\}\times \{1,\ldots,56682\},\\
  (m,g)&\mapsto \bigl(s_{mg},r_{mg}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $s_{mg}$ denotes the finite seed-density or target-site coordinate available for miRNA $m$ and target entry $g$, and $r_{mg}$ denotes the matched repression readout.  The verified finite counts are the part of the record that is presently closed: the panel size is $n_{\mathrm{miRNA}}=11$ and the pair count is $n_{\mathrm{pair}}=56682$.

\paragraph{Role in the BioReality dictionary.}
The seed-density contact is deliberately narrower than the neighboring codon-usage and translation records.  It records an RNA-regulatory surface: a miRNA seed-density coordinate can be compared against target repression, but that comparison is not a translation-efficiency measurement, a codon-usage measurement, a protein-folding measurement, or a functional assay.  The appropriate local readback is therefore a four-way finite classification:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  A &: \text{seed coordinate present and repression readout positive},\\
  B &: \text{seed coordinate present and repression readout null},\\
  C &: \text{seed coordinate absent and repression readout positive},\\
  D &: \text{seed coordinate absent and repression readout null}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This classification is a table operation on the $56682$ observed pairs.  It does not by itself identify the biochemical cause of repression, because repression can mix target-site architecture, transcript abundance, RNA decay, translation, and protein turnover.  The finite panel is therefore admitted as a measured RNA-regulatory contact, not as a mechanistic pathway certificate.

\paragraph{Separation from codon-residual translation records.}
The same BioReality ledger contains a rank-powered $B^*_{Q6}$ codon-residual family with $9$ residual coordinates: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Those coordinates live on coding-sequence codon composition after controls for amino-acid composition, CDS length, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline.  The miRNA seed-density panel is not one of those coordinates.  Its admissible comparison to the codon-residual layer is only a finite cross-layer relation: one may ask whether a transcript-level repression coordinate changes the interpretation of a codon-residual association, but one may not identify miRNA seed density with translation efficiency.

For calibration, the measured ribosome-TE record reports positive $B^*_{Q6}\to T$ survival in four organism-specific panels: $R^2_{QT}=0.04034217614654355$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.009394266754822262$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.018837911344127926$ for human, and $0.13681931343730436$ for yeast, each with $9$ surviving coordinates.  The joined TE row counts are $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$, respectively.  These numbers are cited here only to mark the distinction between a measured translation-efficiency surface and the miRNA seed-density repression surface.  A miRNA seed-density row is not a ribosome-profiling row unless a separate joined dataset supplies the same transcript or CDS units with ribosome-footprint and matched mRNA measurements.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance contrast.}
The direct protein-omics record gives a separate downstream surface.  It reports $B^*_{Q6}\to P$ residual association against measured PAXdb protein abundance in $15$ organism panels, each with $9$ surviving coordinates.  The recorded $R^2_{QP}$ values include $0.061906245886660105$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} 168, $0.10767526677985896$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.04454565118019448$ for human, $0.049827984062539195$ for mouse, $0.29837624994140827$ for yeast, and $0.06397514251295629$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The joined protein row counts include $4042$, $3739$, $19053$, $19395$, $5815$, and $1656$ for those same panels.  These protein-abundance facts prevent a common over-reading of the miRNA table: a seed-density association with repression is not the same claim as a codon-residual association with steady-state protein abundance.  A valid downstream protein statement would require matched protein rows and an explicit conditioning calculation.

\paragraph{Mediation boundary.}
When a translation-contact is present, the codon-residual protein record uses explicit attenuation rather than verbal mechanism language.  In the measured translation-mediated abundance run, the mediated fractions were $0.0$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The corresponding three-way joined row counts were $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.  These values show the local standard for a mediation statement: both $Q\to T$ and $Q\to P$ surfaces must be present, and conditioning on $T$ must be evaluated numerically.

The miRNA seed-density record has not yet supplied an analogous miRNA-conditioned mediation table of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow P_k,\\
  s_{mg} &\longrightarrow r_{mg}\quad\text{with matched }T_j\text{ and }P_k\text{ on the same rows}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently, the miRNA result can enter the paper as a measured repression contact with finite row support, but it cannot be promoted into a translation-mediated protein-abundance mechanism.  If a future joined table places miRNA seed density, matched transcript abundance, ribosome TE, protein abundance, and codon-residual coordinates on the same units, then the admissible test is attenuation: compare the direct seed-density or codon-residual association with the association after conditioning on the measured translation readout and transcript controls.

\paragraph{Joint readout comparison.}
The existing joint TE and mRNA-stability decomposition gives a second calibration for how multi-readout claims are handled.  In \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast, the recorded $R^2_{QP}$ values are $0.1181743523064932$, $0.04274178488511215$, and $0.2715520808117803$.  After conditioning on both measured TE and mRNA stability, the incremental codon-residual values are $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.08975732521542036$, $0.0355223930537993$, and $0.13047582645538838$.  The corresponding joint mediation fractions are $0.2404669586626661$, $0.16890712099923358$, and $0.5195182225621592$, with right-tail permutation values $0.000999000999000999$ in all three panels.  This is the correct mathematical style for cross-layer claims: the mediator columns are named, joined, residualized, and then compared by finite statistics.

By contrast, the miRNA seed-density run presently contributes the finite facts $11$ and $56682$, not a joint TE--stability--protein decomposition.  Its chapter-level conclusion is therefore intentionally modest: the Agarwal-style seed-density surface is a real finite table for miRNA target repression, and it can constrain the RNA-regulatory side of a larger cross-layer dictionary, but it does not close translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, evolutionary direction, or universality.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The record is ready as a finite BioReality chapter because the row counts and scope are concrete: $11$ miRNAs, $56682$ target pairs, and a recorded one-second run.  The admissible claim is that a bounded miRNA seed-density repression surface exists and can be kept distinct from codon-residual translation and protein-abundance surfaces.  The inadmissible claims are equally explicit.  No translation claim follows without a measured translation contact; no protein-output claim follows without matched protein abundance; no folding, physical-admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, or universal biological law follows without a separate layer-matched measurement and a finite statistic on the same units.
