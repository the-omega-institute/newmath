\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP occupancy bridge}
\label{sec:biorreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of transcript and CDS rows.  Each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^\ast_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a proposed decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy measurement at the editable RNA window.  The row type is intentionally narrow: the coordinate may stratify synonymous windows, the translation readout may condition the RNA half-life comparison, and the occupancy/rescue block may test whether the half-life contrast is tied to a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  It does not by itself contain protein output, folded structure, function, phenotype, or an organism-wide law.

\paragraph{Observed finite scalars.}
The local row ledger contains a protein-acetylation contact keyed to the Hansen-style lysine-acetylation/cysteine-proximity surface with $n=6288$ finite rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry values in run $4c330420a1bf$, completed in $8.456$ seconds.  This scalar is useful as a neighboring cross-layer reality check because it shows that the surrounding audit table is using concrete finite experimental surfaces rather than symbolic chapter names.  The RBP-binding contact supplies a separate finite binding surface with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run $b225890251c2$, completed in $223.648043$ seconds.  The translation-readout side has an mTOR translation table with $n=6494$ rows in run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, completed in $9.469$ seconds, and the nearby N-terminal acetylation table reports $n=657$, $426$ NatA-class rows, and AUROC $0.942081$ on $622$ high--low rows in run $f452e76f6363$.  These numbers do not prove the present RNA-decay bridge; they fix the scale of the finite-row environment in which the bridge is allowed to be asked.

\paragraph{Statistic to be admitted.}
For a positive bridge, the admissible statistic is not a raw half-life difference.  It is a translation-conditioned contrast in RNA half-life residuals between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels, after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout have been attached as controls.  In schematic form, the admitted finite comparison has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}} &= \widehat{\mathrm{HL}}(B^\ast_{Q6}=\mathrm{high}\mid Z,T)-\widehat{\mathrm{HL}}(B^\ast_{Q6}=\mathrm{low}\mid Z,T),\\
Z &= (\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{RNA\ structure},\mathrm{motifs},\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ context},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{batch},\mathrm{amino\ acid}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T$ is the same-row translation readout and $\widehat{\mathrm{HL}}$ denotes the fitted RNA half-life residual.  The bridge is present only if this finite contrast tracks measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, collapses after target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Any half-life association that lacks this occupancy--loss--rescue pattern remains an RNA-stability association, not an RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life once the listed controls and the translation readout are conditioned.  A finite nonzero half-life residual is not enough to break the null as an RBP bridge.  If measured local RBP occupancy is unchanged, if occupancy is not experimentally decoupled from the coordinate label, if target-RBP loss leaves the contrast intact, if wild-type rescue does not restore the effect, or if binding-defective or non-target rescue restores it, the bridge claim is rejected.  Likewise, RNA-structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair-context, and batch explanations remain live competitors unless their matched controls are explicitly represented in the finite panel.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback therefore has five local signs: a translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual, a measured local occupancy change for the declared decay-factor RBP, loss of the residual under target-RBP removal, return under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target controls.  The neighboring finite scalars above supply row-scale context but not the missing same-window perturbation panel.  In the present record, the RNA half-life, mRNA-control, and translation contacts name the required readout surfaces, while the same-organism same-window RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue rows are not yet present.  Thus the safe mathematical status is a bounded cross-layer question with a sharp null, not a positive RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone, even when the half-life rows are joined to a same-row translation readout.  No decay-factor RBP mechanism follows from a half-life association unless measured local occupancy and the perturbation/rescue pattern are present.  No protein abundance, protein acetylation mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry or from the row counts reported here.  Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.
