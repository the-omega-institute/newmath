\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP occupancy gate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched transcript/CDS panel whose rows carry a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance and transcription controls, and an externally prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation surface. The coordinate label is not itself a biological measurement. It is a finite row key used to ask whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows remain exchangeable after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented away and after the row has been conditioned on translation, abundance, transcript architecture, and sequence-composition controls.

\paragraph{Available numerical contact.}
The local finite fact attached to the Hansen acetylation contact is not an RNA-decay panel. It reports $n=6288$ assayed rows, $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, and runtime $8.456$ seconds for the verified protein-acetylation occupancy computation. This is useful as a nearby example of a bounded post-translational occupancy surface, but it does not by itself supply RNA half-life, decay-factor RBP binding, or RBP perturbation rows. A separate RBP-binding finite contact reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases with runtime $223.648043$ seconds. That second number shows that an RBP-contact table can be finite and auditable, but it is still not the required same-window decay-factor occupancy/loss/rescue panel for this claim. A third nearby acetylation-readout contact reports $n=657$, $426$ NatA rows, AUROC $0.942081$, and $622$ high-low AUROC rows; it is again a layer-matched occupancy example, not an RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Statistic licensed by the row design.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotient, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and $Z_i$ the finite control vector containing transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler-baseline terms. The admissible readout is not a raw $q_i \to H_i$ contrast. It is the bounded comparison in which the coordinate-associated half-life residual is evaluated only after the translation boundary and the RBP gate are present:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i, T_i, O_i, Z_i\bigr),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{loss}} &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i, T_i, O_i, Z_i, \mathrm{target\text{-}RBP\ loss}\bigr),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{rescue}} &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i, T_i, O_i, Z_i, \mathrm{wild\text{-}type\ rescue}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive bridge requires the finite pattern in which the translation-conditioned half-life residual follows measured local RBP occupancy, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue. If the half-life residual exists without that occupancy/loss/rescue pattern, the result remains an RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor, not an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null treats high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels as exchangeable for RNA half-life once amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been conditioned. In that null, any finite difference in $H_i$ must disappear or remain mechanistically unassigned unless $O_i$ changes in the prespecified local window and the perturbation controls move in the required directions. The null is broken only by a joined panel, not by naming separate contact routes.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The present finite evidence is therefore ready only as a scoped BioReality record: it defines the exact finite rows and the statistic that would certify a translation-conditioned RNA half-life/RBP bridge. The attached Hansen protein-acetylation fact supplies $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry values for a different protein-occupancy layer, while the RBP-binding fact supplies $900$ peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases for a separate binding surface. Neither fact contains the joined same-organism, same-window tuple $(q_i,T_i,H_i,O_i,Z_i)$ with target-RBP loss and rescue arms. Consequently no translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, decay mechanism, causal adaptation, phenotype, or universal biological law follows here. Those claims require their own layer-matched measurements and finite statistics.
