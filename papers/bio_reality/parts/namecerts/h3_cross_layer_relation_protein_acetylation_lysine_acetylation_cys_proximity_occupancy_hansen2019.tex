\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With a Decay-Factor RBP Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel of coding-transcript rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label is kept after the amino-acid quotient and is read together with same-row translation, RNA half-life, transcript-abundance control, and an explicitly prespecified decay-factor RBP boundary. The coordinate is not treated as a biological force. It is a row label whose possible biological use begins only after the row also carries the relevant measured readouts. For the present packet the protein-acetylation-linked Hansen row set supplies a finite neighboring measurement scale with $n=6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries under run $4c330420a1bf$. That finite scalar support establishes that the broader BioReality table can hold large matched biochemical readouts, but it does not by itself supply RNA half-life, local RBP occupancy, or perturbation-rescue evidence for this RNA-decay question.

\paragraph{Coordinate split.}
Let $R$ be the finite set of transcript or CDS rows that survive identifier matching. Each row $r\in R$ is allowed to carry a residual coordinate class $q(r)$, a translation readout $T(r)$, a transcript-abundance or transcription control vector $A(r)$, an RNA half-life readout $H(r)$, a local decay-factor RBP occupancy readout $O(r)$, and perturbation labels $P(r)$ recording target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls. The intended high-versus-low comparison is therefore not a raw codon comparison; it is a comparison after amino-acid identity has already been quotiented out and after the same-row translation readout is present as a conditioning boundary. In schematic form the admissible statistic has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{contrast}\bigl(H(r)\mid q(r)=q_{\mathrm{high}},T(r),A(r),Z(r)\bigr) \\
  &\quad - \operatorname{contrast}\bigl(H(r)\mid q(r)=q_{\mathrm{low}},T(r),A(r),Z(r)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z(r)$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline terms. A nonzero value of $\Delta_H$ is still only an RNA half-life association unless the RBP-specific conditions below are simultaneously present.

\paragraph{RBP bridge condition.}
The bridge from residual coordinate to an RNA-decay RBP statement is intentionally narrow. The same finite rows must show that local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP changes in the relevant editable RNA window, that the half-life effect collapses when the target RBP is lost, that wild-type RBP rescue restores the effect, and that binding-defective or non-target rescue does not restore it. Thus the admissible positive pattern is not merely
$ q(r) \leadsto H(r) $.
It is the finite conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
&q(r) \leadsto H(r) \quad \text{after } T(r) \text{ and } Z(r) \text{ are held fixed}, \\
&q(r) \leadsto O(r) \quad \text{at the same local RNA window}, \\
&\Delta_H \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss}, \\
&\Delta_H \text{ returns under wild-type rescue}, \\
&\Delta_H \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If any line is absent, the row set remains a bounded coordinate-associated RNA-stability record or a descriptor of a half-life association; it is not an RBP-mediated decay claim.

\paragraph{Available finite evidence.}
The current finite facts are useful but incomplete for this exact bridge. The protein-acetylation-related Hansen packet has $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry values, which shows a measured biochemical surface at substantial finite resolution. A separate RBP-binding packet records $900$ total peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases under run $b225890251c2$, showing that the BioReality ledger can represent measured RBP-binding evidence, but those rows are not the same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue panel required here. A translation readout packet from the Thoreen mTOR translation surface records $n=6494$ rows under run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, again demonstrating that finite translation-readout rows exist in the ledger, but not that they are joined to this Q6 residual coordinate, RNA half-life, and RBP perturbation panel. These three finite surfaces therefore define relevant neighboring capabilities, not a completed positive bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after all required same-row controls are attached. In particular, after conditioning on amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the mandatory translation readout, the labels $q_{\mathrm{high}}$ and $q_{\mathrm{low}}$ should not predict $H(r)$ beyond the finite error tolerated by the declared statistic. If a half-life difference survives this null but local RBP occupancy is unchanged, the result is not an RBP bridge. If occupancy changes but target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life effect, the result is not an RBP bridge. If loss collapses the effect but wild-type rescue fails, or if a binding-defective or non-target rescue also restores the effect, the result is not the claimed decay-factor mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a finite cross-layer test design for translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP-decay boundary. It does not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone, and it does not treat the residual coordinate, codon-window geometry, RBP-contact names, or workflow eligibility as observed mechanism. No protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from these rows. Such claims would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics. In the present state the correct scientific reading is therefore conditional: the packet is ready as a self-contained finite-row boundary statement, while a positive RBP-mediated RNA-decay conclusion still requires the missing same-window occupancy, target-loss, rescue, and control rows.
