\subsection{Finite matched record for lysine-acetylation occupancy and the Q6 RNA-decay boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-hansen2019-q6-rna-decay-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The finite record considered here is keyed by the claim identifier
\texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_acetylation.lysine\_acetylation\_cys\_proximity\_occupancy\_hansen2019}.  Its verified finite table contains $6288$ assayed rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry-bearing entries, recorded in run \texttt{4c330420a1bf} with runtime $8.456$ seconds.  These numbers establish that the claim is attached to a concrete finite measurement surface rather than to a merely verbal biological association.  The row surface is nevertheless not, by itself, a translation measurement, an RNA half-life panel, or a perturbation-and-rescue experiment.  It is a bounded finite contact point: a collection of assayed post-translational occupancy entries whose numerical scope is fixed by the observed row count and the distinct stoichiometry count.

\paragraph{Coordinate distinction.}
The conjectural coordinate layer uses $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual labels after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  In the present record, a high-coordinate label and a low-coordinate label may only be compared after the amino-acid quotient has been applied and after same-row controls have been attached.  The intended residual comparison is not a comparison of proteins, phenotypes, or global sequence laws.  It is a finite matched-row comparison in which coordinate labels are allowed to condition a downstream statistic only when the row also carries the required biological readout and its controls.  The protein-acetylation row count gives a finite measured surface for occupancy-style reasoning, but it does not provide the same-row RNA half-life and RBP-perturbation columns needed for the proposed RNA-decay bridge.

\paragraph{Declared bridge test.}
The bridge statement is the following finite-row implication.  Let $q$ be the amino-acid-quotiented $B^*_{Q6}$ residual label, let $T$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H$ be the RNA half-life residual after transcription, abundance, isoform, batch, local structure, motif-density, GC, dinucleotide, and codon-pair controls, and let $O$ record local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  A positive RBP-decay bridge is allowed only for a finite matched panel in which the half-life effect follows the measured occupancy contrast, disappears under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  In schematic form, the admissible positive pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
& q \not\equiv q' \text{ after amino-acid quotient},\\
& H(q,T,Z) \neq H(q',T,Z),\\
& O(q) \neq O(q'),\\
& \Delta H_{\mathrm{target\ loss}} \approx 0,\\
& \Delta H_{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} \approx \Delta H_{\mathrm{baseline}},\\
& \Delta H_{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} \approx 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta H_{\mathrm{non\ target}} \approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes the finite control block.  The verified Hansen-style occupancy table supplies the finite scale $n=6288$ and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry-bearing entries, but the supplied data do not contain the matched $H$, $O$, target-loss, rescue, and control columns for this RNA-decay test.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life after the declared controls.  Thus a half-life difference without same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy remains an RNA-stability association.  A half-life difference with occupancy but without target-RBP loss collapse remains an occupancy association.  A loss effect without wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure remains an incomplete perturbation trace.  None of these intermediate patterns licenses a decay-factor RBP mechanism.  The finite protein-acetylation surface is compatible with this null discipline: its row count and stoichiometry diversity make it a measured table, but they do not fill the RNA half-life or RBP rescue slots.

\paragraph{Readback permitted by the present table.}
The present finite facts support only a bounded statement about the existence and scale of a measured occupancy-style surface: $6288$ assayed rows, $5921$ distinct stoichiometry-bearing entries, and a reproducible run identifier.  These facts may be used to anchor a finite-row discussion of occupancy heterogeneity.  They may not be converted into a same-row translation effect, an RNA half-life effect, a decay-factor RBP mechanism, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact: ribosome or translation-efficiency rows for translation, RNA half-life rows for RNA stability, measured same-window RBP occupancy for the RBP arm, target-RBP loss and rescue rows for mechanism, and separate protein, structure, function, or phenotype assays for those higher layers.

\paragraph{Audit outcome.}
The finite-row evidence is therefore usable as a chapter-local measured surface but not as a completed RNA-decay RBP bridge.  The correct classification is a bounded cross-layer boundary record: the verified table establishes finite contact with measured stoichiometry-bearing occupancy data, while the proposed translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge remains blocked until same-organism, same-window rows provide translation readout, matched mRNA control, RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  The finite data are real and numerically specified; the mechanistic bridge is not yet realized by the supplied rows.
