\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and a prespecified RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-panel claim about coding transcripts, not a claim about a completed molecular mechanism.  Each admissible row has a coding-sequence coordinate label, the amino-acid quotient needed to compare synonymous residual labels, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a matched transcript-abundance control, and a declared place where a decay-factor RNA-binding protein could be measured.  The intended contrast is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual windows after the amino-acid sequence has been held fixed.  A row may support only a bounded cross-layer statement if the RNA half-life residual is read after conditioning on the same-row translation boundary and after the standard transcript-side controls are present.

The finite evidence currently attached to this packet is uneven across layers.  The protein-acetylation contact identified by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_acetylation.lysine\_acetylation\_cys\_proximity\_occupancy\_hansen2019} contains $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, with a recorded runtime of $8.456$ seconds.  That row count is useful as an example of a finite post-translational occupancy surface, but it is not an RNA half-life or RBP-perturbation panel.  The RBP-binding contact identified by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} has $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  The translation contact identified by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} has $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  A larger RNA-modification contact, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6am\_methylation.m6am\_flanking\_identity\_stoichiometry\_crownseq}, has $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, $20942$ variable-subset rows, a saturated fraction $0.343223$ above the $0.95$ threshold, and runtime $324.086$ seconds.  These finite surfaces show that the BioReality ledger can carry row-bounded readouts at several molecular layers, but none of these numbers alone supplies the same-window RNA half-life, translation, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, RBP loss, and rescue panel demanded by the present bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null hypothesis is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life once amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been conditioned.  In symbols, for a finite row set $I$ with coordinate class $Q_i$, translation readout $T_i$, half-life readout $H_i$, and controls $Z_i$, the null comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H_i \perp Q_i \mid (T_i,Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ includes the transcript-side and composition controls listed above.  A departure from this null is still only an RNA-stability association unless the decay-factor RBP bridge is separately observed on the same finite panel.

\paragraph{Bridge condition.}
A positive bridge requires a stricter finite pattern than a half-life difference.  Let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, let $L_i$ denote target-RBP loss, let $W_i$ denote wild-type rescue, and let $B_i$ denote a binding-defective or non-target control.  The admissible bridge has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  Q_i &\rightsquigarrow H_i \mid (T_i,Z_i),\\
  Q_i &\rightsquigarrow O_i,\\
  L_i &: \text{collapse of the } Q_i\text{-associated half-life effect},\\
  W_i &: \text{return of that effect},\\
  B_i &: \text{no return of that effect}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The arrow notation here is only a finite-row readout requirement, not a proof of biochemical causation.  The RBP must be chosen before the test by external occupancy and perturbation information; the residual-coordinate geometry is not allowed to select an RBP after seeing the half-life result.  If local occupancy is unchanged, if the effect survives target-RBP loss, if wild-type rescue fails, or if binding-defective or non-target rescue restores the effect, the bridge fails even when the RNA half-life statistic is nonzero.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the attached numbers.}
The $6288$ protein-acetylation rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries are finite post-translational measurements, not transcript half-life rows.  They therefore cannot be used to infer translation, RNA decay, RBP occupancy, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, or a general sequence law for this claim.  The $900$ RBP-binding peaks provide a bounded RBP contact surface in another packet, but the present record does not yet join those peaks to the same organisms, transcripts, coding windows, translation readouts, RNA half-lives, loss experiments, and rescue experiments required here.  The $6494$ translation rows give a useful scale for a translation-readout contact, but a translation row by itself does not measure RNA decay or RBP occupancy.  The $31886$ m6Am rows and the scalar values $19406$, $3230$, $20942$, and $0.343223$ similarly demonstrate finite RNA-layer measurement, not this particular decay-factor RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The present chapter licenses only a conditional finite-row criterion: a B$^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate may be discussed as a translation-conditioned RNA half-life association, and it may be promoted to an RBP-bridged association only if measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue all occur on the matched panel.  Without that joined panel, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA-stability association.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, biochemical mechanism, causation claim, or universal biological law follows unless another layer-specific contact supplies the corresponding measurements and controls.
