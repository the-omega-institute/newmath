\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life bridge with a prespecified decay-factor RBP}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel whose rows carry a synonymous residual coordinate window $B^{*}_{Q6}$, an amino-acid quotient label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, transcript-abundance controls, and a possible measured occupancy surface for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein.  The intended comparison is not a protein-output claim and not a sequence-law claim.  It is a narrow cross-layer test: after synonymous windows have been grouped by their amino-acid product, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels may be compared only with respect to a translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual, and only if the row also contains a measured local RBP-occupancy and perturbation/rescue arm.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The present reality ledger contains several bounded finite contacts, but they do not yet form the joined RBP-decay panel required by the claim.  The protein-acetylation contact indexed as \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_acetylation.lysine\_acetylation\_cys\_proximity\_occupancy\_hansen2019} has $n=6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, with runtime $8.456$ seconds in run \texttt{4c330420a1bf}.  This is useful as a separate finite stoichiometry surface, but it is a protein-acetylation measurement, not an RNA half-life, local RNA-window RBP occupancy, or RBP loss/rescue experiment.  The RBP-binding contact indexed as \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds in run \texttt{b225890251c2}; this gives a bounded RBP-binding readout class, but it is not the same-window decay-factor occupancy and perturbation matrix demanded here.  The translation contact indexed as \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} contains $6494$ finite rows with runtime $9.469$ seconds in run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}; it can witness that translation readouts are available as a row type, but it does not by itself supply RNA half-life or RBP rescue evidence.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
For a row $i$, let $q_i$ denote the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate class after amino-acid quotient, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, let $O_i$ be measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch.  A positive bridge would require a finite statistic of the following form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_O(q;Z) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z,\mathrm{target\ RBP\ loss}) \approx 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \approx \Delta_H(q;T,Z),\\
&\Delta_H(q;T,Z,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}) \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first line alone is insufficient.  A half-life association conditioned on translation remains an RNA-stability association unless the measured occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and failed binding-defective or non-target rescue rows are all present in the same finite design.  The row count information above confirms finite contacts, but it does not confirm the required joined tuple $(q_i,T_i,H_i,O_i,Z_i)$ with perturbation and rescue arms.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels with respect to RNA half-life once amino-acid identity, translation readout, transcription rate, RNA structure, motif density, nucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch are fixed.  In symbols, the admissible null is the finite-row statement
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, Z_i
\end{aligned}
$$
within the matched amino-acid quotient panel, strengthened by the requirement that any apparent difference must fail as an RBP bridge unless the local occupancy and perturbation/rescue pattern moves with it.  If $H_i$ differs across $q_i$ but $O_i$ is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss leaves the effect intact, or if a binding-defective rescue restores the effect, then the result is not a decay-factor RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded negative boundary rather than a positive biological mechanism.  The finite Hansen protein-acetylation stoichiometry surface has $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries; the finite RBP-binding surface has $900$ peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases; the finite mTOR translation surface has $6494$ rows.  These numbers show that the surrounding ledger contains concrete finite biological contacts, but the exact RNA half-life bridge requires a different joined panel.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No folded structure, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate geometry, from translation-readout routing, from protein-acetylation stoichiometry, or from an RBP-binding contact name.  A positive claim is licensed only by a row-matched experiment that simultaneously supplies translation readout, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control failure under the finite control set stated above.
