\paragraph{Finite carrier.}

Each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity, a same-row translation readout $T$, an RNA half-life readout $H$, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein occupancy readout $O$ at the editable RNA window. The coordinate label is allowed to separate high-coordinate from low-coordinate synonymous windows only after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC content, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been represented on the same finite table.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive bridge claim requires a finite statistic comparing high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows after the controls have been applied.  Let $Q_i$ denote the residual coordinate class, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP, and $Z_i$ the full control vector.  The relevant readout is a residual half-life term conditioned on translation and controls, together with an experimentally decoupled occupancy term:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \widehat{E}(H_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i) - \widehat{E}(H_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_O &= \widehat{E}(O_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{high},Z_i) - \widehat{E}(O_i \mid Q_i=\mathrm{low},Z_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
The bridge is present only if the half-life residual and the local occupancy residual are linked under a prespecified RBP, the half-life effect collapses when that target RBP is lost, wild-type rescue restores the effect, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it.  A mere association between $Q_i$ and $H_i$ is therefore insufficient; a mere association between $Q_i$ and $O_i$ is also insufficient.  The finite row must carry both the translation-conditioned half-life boundary and the perturbation-rescue pattern.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and the same-row translation readout are fixed.  Equivalently, for the panel actually used in the test,
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp Q_i \mid T_i, Z_i
\end{aligned}
$$
holds unless the measured occupancy and perturbation-rescue terms break the exchangeability in the declared direction.  If a half-life difference remains but measured local RBP occupancy is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss does not erase the effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it, the result remains a finite RNA-stability association rather than a decay-factor RBP bridge.  If a binding-defective rescue or a non-target control restores the signal, the proposed mechanism is rejected for this record.

\paragraph{Readback boundary.}
The chapter licenses only a bounded cross-layer statement about a finite RNA panel: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be read as an RBP-mediated bridge only when measured local occupancy, target loss, wild-type rescue, and failed defective or non-target rescue all appear on the same matched design.  No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No protein output, folding state, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the declared RBP perturbation pattern, or universal sequence law follows from this record without an additional layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  The protein-acetylation row count, the RBP-binding peak count, the translation-surface row count, and the RNA-modification null summaries are cited here as finite ledger constraints and calibration surfaces, not as substitutes for the missing same-window RBP-loss-rescue panel.
