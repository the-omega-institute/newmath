\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record is attached to the claim identifier \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_acetylation.lysine\_acetylation\_cys\_proximity\_occupancy\_hansen2019}.  Its presently verified finite table is a lysine-acetylation stoichiometry panel: run \texttt{4c330420a1bf} contains $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, with recorded runtime $8.456$ seconds.  Those numbers make the Hansen-indexed contact a real finite post-translational surface, not a merely named biological theme.  The same audit bundle also contains an RBP-binding contact, run \texttt{b225890251c2}, with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases over runtime $223.648043$ seconds; a translation contact, run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, with $6494$ rows over runtime $9.469$ seconds; and an N-terminal acetylation control contact, run \texttt{f452e76f6363}, with $657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, AUROC $0.942081$, and $622$ high--low AUROC rows.  These scalar facts are enough to define a finite biological neighborhood around the proposed bridge, but they are not enough to certify the bridge itself.

\paragraph{Coordinate question.}
The biological question is whether a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate window, after amino-acid quotienting, can be read as a bounded cross-layer relation for RNA half-life through a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The carrier is a finite matched panel whose rows would have to contain the residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for the target decay-factor RBP, and matched controls.  The intended contrast is not simply high-coordinate versus low-coordinate sequence geometry.  It is the conjunction of a coordinate contrast, a measured occupancy contrast at the editable RNA window, a target-RBP loss contrast, and a rescue contrast in which wild-type target-RBP returns the effect while binding-defective or non-target controls fail.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout.  In this null, an RNA half-life difference alone is only an RNA-stability association.  It becomes an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge only if the same finite rows also show the prespecified local RBP occupancy pattern and the perturbation--rescue pattern.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0 &: q_{\mathrm{high}} \sim q_{\mathrm{low}} \mid Z, T, A,\\
Z &:=(\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motif},\mathrm{GC},\mathrm{dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{batch}),\\
T &:={\rm same\mbox{-}row\ translation\ readout},\\
A &:={\rm matched\ mRNA\ abundance\ control}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The alternative requires a nonzero translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual together with measured local RBP occupancy, collapse under target-RBP loss, restoration by wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  The finite readback therefore has five necessary faces: half-life effect, occupancy change, loss collapse, wild-type return, and failed defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{What the current facts support.}
The Hansen acetylation-indexed finite table supports a post-translational stoichiometry surface with $6288$ measured rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry values.  It can be used as evidence that the BioReality ledger is able to host large finite protein-state panels.  It does not supply RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, or rescue rows.  The RBP-binding panel supplies a separate finite RBP-contact neighborhood with $900$ peaks and $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases, but peak concordance is not the same object as same-window occupancy for the target decay-factor RBP in the Hansen-indexed coordinate rows.  The translation panel supplies $6494$ rows of translation readout, but a translation readout is not an RNA-decay mechanism.  The N-terminal acetylation panel supplies an independent acetylation occupancy control with AUROC $0.942081$, but it is not a lysine-acetylation-to-RNA-half-life perturbation panel.

\paragraph{Admissible positive statement.}
A positive result for this chapter would have the following finite-row form.  Let $q_i$ be the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and $R_i$ the perturbation/rescue state.  The claimed bridge may be reported only if a declared statistic separates high from low $q_i$ in $H_i$ after conditioning on $T_i$ and the control set, and if this separation tracks $O_i$, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \widehat{E}(H_i\mid q_i={\rm high},T_i,Z_i)-\widehat{E}(H_i\mid q_i={\rm low},T_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_O &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H(R_i={\rm target\ loss}) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H(R_i={\rm wild\ type\ rescue}) &\approx \Delta_H(R_i={\rm wild\ type}),\\
\Delta_H(R_i={\rm binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target}) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is deliberately stricter than an association between codon geometry and RNA half-life.  The bridge is a finite matched-panel claim about a target RBP and an RNA-decay readout, not a sequence-law claim.

\paragraph{Rejected promotions.}
The present finite facts do not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  They do not license an RBP mechanism from an unchanged occupancy surface, from an occupancy association without perturbation, or from perturbation without wild-type rescue and defective-control failure.  They do not license protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  A separate layer-matched contact would be required for each of those claims.  In particular, the $6288$-row Hansen stoichiometry table is a protein-state readout, the $6494$-row translation table is a translation readout, and the $900$-peak RBP table is an RBP-binding readout; none of these alone is the same-window RNA half-life RBP perturbation panel required here.
