\subsection{Lysine-Acetylation Occupancy Rows as a Bounded Cross-Layer Check}
\label{sec:bioreality-lysine-acetylation-cys-proximity-occupancy-hansen2019}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row BioReality panel whose primary realized object is not a universal biochemical law but a row-indexed occupancy surface.  The protein-acetylation run identified $6288$ finite rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry-bearing entries for the Hansen-style lysine-acetylation and cysteine-proximity occupancy contact.  The run identifier was $4c330420a1bf$, and the measured computation time was $8.456$ seconds.  These numbers define the finite scale of the checked contact: the panel contains many site-level observations, but its certified content is only the existence of a bounded stoichiometry table with row identifiers and site-local readouts.

\paragraph{Row object.}
A row is treated as a tuple containing a protein or peptide identifier, a lysine-site coordinate, a local cysteine-proximity descriptor where present, and an acetylation-occupancy or stoichiometry readout.  The finite support may be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{acetyl}} &= 6288,\\
D_{\mathrm{stoich}} &= 5921,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{distinct}} &= \frac{5921}{6288}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This ratio is not interpreted as a mechanistic rate.  It only says that most rows carry distinct stoichiometry-bearing surface information after the local identifier and site fields are read in the repository-local data model.  No $R$ list, $M$ list, lambda parameter, $p$-value, module class, or hit-rate scalar is present for this acetylation contact in the supplied finite facts, so none is inferred.

\paragraph{Relation to the Q6 RNA-stability conjecture.}
The conjectural packet attached to this chapter is a translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP bridge over $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate windows.  That packet requires finite transcript or coding-sequence rows carrying a residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  The acetylation contact does not supply those rows.  It sits at a different biochemical layer: site-local protein post-translational occupancy rather than transcript-local decay-factor binding.

\paragraph{Auxiliary finite contacts.}
Two related finite checks help delimit the layer boundary.  The RBP-binding contact over RBNS/eCLIP-style rows reports $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with run identifier $b225890251c2$ and runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  This is a finite RBP-binding surface, but it is not the required same-window decay-factor occupancy, perturbation, and rescue panel for the Q6 RNA half-life bridge.  Separately, the mTOR translation contact reports $6494$ rows, a GEO readout source for GSE36892, run identifier $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  This supplies a translation-associated finite contact, but not a same-row RNA half-life statistic and not an RBP-loss or rescue experiment.

\paragraph{Admissible finite statement.}
The admissible statement is therefore a bounded negative one.  The acetylation contact confirms a finite lysine-site occupancy surface of size $6288$ with $5921$ distinct stoichiometry-bearing entries.  The RBP-binding and translation contacts confirm that separate finite surfaces exist at nearby biological layers.  The conjunction of these surfaces does not assemble the required Q6 RNA half-life bridge, because the matched row must carry the following fields at once:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{residual coordinate label},\\
&\text{same-row translation readout},\\
&\text{matched mRNA or transcription-abundance control},\\
&\text{RNA half-life readout},\\
&\text{measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy},\\
&\text{target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue},\\
&\text{binding-defective or non-target control failure}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The supplied finite facts verify only disjoint surfaces, not this joined panel.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is row exchangeability after the declared controls.  High-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels remain exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout have been controlled.  If a half-life difference is observed without measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue, the result remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated bridge.

\paragraph{Boundary of non-entailment.}
Nothing in the acetylation row count, the distinct stoichiometry count, the RBP peak count, or the mTOR translation row count licenses a claim about translation realization, RNA decay mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  A separate contact must supply each such layer.  In particular, a protein-acetylation occupancy table is not a ribosome-profiling matrix, not an RNA half-life panel, not a decay-factor RBP perturbation experiment, and not a rescue assay.  The present chapter may therefore cite the finite acetylation surface as a checked biochemical contact and may use the RBP and translation contacts to clarify nearby layer boundaries, but it must keep the Q6 RNA half-life RBP bridge in the blocked state until a single matched finite panel supplies the required occupancy, perturbation, rescue, and control rows.
