\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With a Decay-Factor RBP Gate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA panel in which the coordinate side is a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual label after quotienting by amino-acid identity, while the biological side is restricted to same-row translation readout, RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and perturbation or rescue controls.  The claim is not that the coordinate itself is a biological cause.  The claim is a bounded admissibility test for when a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be read as a cross-layer RBP-mediated decay bridge.

The finite registry row indexed by the Hansen lysine-acetylation contact supplies a concrete size calibration for the surrounding BioReality table: the run \texttt{4c330420a1bf} contains $n=6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry-bearing entries, with runtime $8.456$ seconds.  This number is cited here only as finite-row evidence that the registry stores row-resolved, externally computed biological readouts; it is not an RNA-decay measurement and it is not an RBP-occupancy measurement.  A separate RBP-binding calibration row, run \texttt{b225890251c2}, records $900$ total peak rows, cross-RBP concordance $3$, and runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  A translation-readout calibration row, run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}, records $6494$ translation rows from the Thoreen source with runtime $9.469$ seconds.  These three finite contacts therefore give the chapter its numerical scale: protein-state rows can be stored, RBP binding rows can be stored, and translation rows can be stored, but the required joined RNA half-life, translation, occupancy, loss, and rescue panel is still a stricter object than any one contact alone.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
Let each retained row carry a synonymous residual coordinate $q_i$, an amino-acid quotient class $a_i$, a same-row translation readout $T_i$, a matched mRNA abundance or transcription control $A_i$, an RNA half-life readout $H_i$, a measured local occupancy readout $O_i$ for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, and a control vector $Z_i$ containing local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and other declared nuisance fields.  The high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are admissible only after the amino-acid quotient has been applied.  The target test is a finite residual comparison in which $H_i$ is evaluated after conditioning on $T_i$, $A_i$, and $Z_i$, and in which the coordinate effect must track measured occupancy and perturbation behavior.

A compact description of the permitted readout is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \operatorname{resid}(H \mid T,A,Z,\text{amino-acid class})_{q=\mathrm{high}} - \operatorname{resid}(H \mid T,A,Z,\text{amino-acid class})_{q=\mathrm{low}},\\
\Delta_O &= O_{q=\mathrm{high}} - O_{q=\mathrm{low}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The chapter permits a positive bridge only when the half-life residual and occupancy residual are both present in the same finite panel, when target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, when wild-type target-RBP rescue restores it, and when binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore it.  The translation readout is mandatory because the chapter is translation-conditioned; RNA half-life alone does not promote the result to a translation-layer statement.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels for RNA half-life after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have been accounted for.  Under that null, a half-life contrast without local occupancy change remains an RNA-stability association.  A half-life contrast with occupancy change but without loss and rescue behavior remains an occupancy association.  A loss effect without wild-type rescue, or with rescue by a binding-defective or non-target construct, remains outside the RBP-bridge claim.

\paragraph{What the present rows establish.}
The available finite facts establish that the BioReality registry contains row-scale contacts in the relevant neighboring layers: a $6288$-row protein-state contact with $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, a $900$-peak RBP-binding contact with cross-RBP concordance $3$, and a $6494$-row translation contact.  They do not establish the required joined panel for this chapter.  In particular, the Hansen acetylation rows are protein post-translational rows, not RNA half-life rows; the RBP-binding rows are binding-surface rows, not target-RBP loss or rescue rows; and the translation rows are translation-readout rows, not RNA-decay mechanism rows.  The finite data therefore support the chapter as a well-scoped BioReality record rather than as a completed positive biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from the coordinate geometry, from a half-life difference, from a translation contact name, or from the protein-acetylation registry row.  A claim at any of those layers would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  For this chapter, the only admissible positive conclusion would be a bounded translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge in the strict sense above: same-row translation readout, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue, all evaluated on the same finite matched panel with the declared controls.
