\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP bridge}\label{subsec:bq6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite rows and coordinate scope.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel whose rows are coding-transcript windows carrying a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  The intended comparison is not a protein-state comparison.  It is a same-row RNA comparison: high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are evaluated only after attaching translation readout, mRNA abundance control, RNA half-life readout, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP route.  In the available finite audit table, the closest protein-state contact for the named acetylation claim contains $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry labels, with runtime $8.456$ seconds.  That table is useful as a reminder that protein-state measurements can be row-counted and bounded, but it does not supply RNA half-life, local RBP occupancy, RBP loss, or rescue rows for this conjecture.

\paragraph{Statistic that would license the bridge.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate class for row $i$, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $A_i$ be matched transcript abundance, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ be measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  A positive bridge requires more than a residual association between $q_i$ and $H_i$.  The admissible pattern is the finite conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
& H_i \text{ differs between high- and low-coordinate rows after conditioning on } T_i,A_i,\text{ and sequence controls},\\
& O_i \text{ changes locally in the same window under an experimentally decoupled occupancy comparison},\\
& \text{target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect},\\
& \text{wild-type target-RBP rescue restores the effect},\\
& \text{binding-defective or non-target rescue fails to restore the effect}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The controls are part of the mathematical object, not later annotations: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout must all remain in the finite conditioning set.

\paragraph{Observed finite counters.}
The current audit memory contains an RBP-binding contact with $900$ total peak rows and only $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries after the declared RBNS/eCLIP comparison, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  This scalar contact establishes that RBP occupancy-like rows can be counted under a finite protocol, but it is not the same-window decay-factor occupancy panel required here.  A translation contact is also present for an mTOR translation readout with $6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  That row count supplies a translation-layer precedent, not a same-row denominator for this RNA half-life claim.  The acetylation finite table with $6288$ rows and $5921$ stoichiometry labels remains a separate protein-state measurement surface.  None of these three counters contains the joined tuple $(q_i,T_i,A_i,H_i,O_i)$ together with target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life once the required controls and translation boundary are present.  In symbols, the tested residual should behave as
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, A_i, Z_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ includes transcription rate, RNA-structure, motif-density, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, isoform, abundance, batch, and amino-acid controls.  Rejection of this null is still insufficient for an RBP mechanism unless the occupancy, loss, and rescue clauses are observed in the same finite design.  A half-life difference without measured local decay-factor occupancy is an RNA-stability association.  An occupancy difference without perturbation collapse and wild-type rescue is an occupancy association.  A rescue signal restored by a binding-defective or non-target control is not evidence for the nominated RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Readback verdict.}
The present finite memory is therefore a bounded design record rather than a positive biological bridge.  It specifies exactly what a successful matched panel must contain: residual-coordinate labels, translation readout, mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and negative rescue controls.  It also states the local failure modes.  RNA half-life alone cannot be promoted to translation realization; translation readout alone cannot prove RNA decay; RBP occupancy alone cannot prove decay causation; and protein acetylation stoichiometry rows cannot substitute for RNA half-life or RBP perturbation rows.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism, protein output, folding statement, physical admissibility statement, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from this chapter.  Such conclusions would require their own finite contact at the relevant layer.  The only claim made here is the conditional shape of a cross-layer RNA half-life/RBP bridge and the present absence, in the supplied row memory, of the joined perturbation-and-rescue panel needed to instantiate it.
