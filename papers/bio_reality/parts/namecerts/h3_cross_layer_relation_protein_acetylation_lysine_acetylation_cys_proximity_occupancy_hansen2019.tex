\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP occupancy}\label{subsec:h3-cross-layer-protein-acetylation-lysine-acetylation-cys-proximity-occupancy-hansen2019}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is not a free biological generalization from codon geometry.  It is a finite matched-row problem.  A row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA and transcriptional controls, and an externally declared route for a decay-factor RNA-binding protein.  The coordinate label is allowed to sort the panel into high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows only after amino-acid identity has already been quotiented away.  The biological question is whether the remaining coordinate contrast is still visible in RNA half-life once translation and the usual transcript-level covariates have been attached to the same row.

The finite evidence table available for the named Hansen-linked acetylation contact contains $n=6288$ measured rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, with run id $4c330420a1bf$ and runtime $8.456$ seconds.  This table is useful as a concrete post-translational occupancy surface: it shows that the BioReality ledger can carry a finite stoichiometric contact rather than only a symbolic layer label.  It is not, however, an RNA half-life table and it is not a local decay-factor RBP occupancy table.  A second finite contact, the RBNS/eCLIP binding comparison, contains $900$ peak rows with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run $b225890251c2$; it supplies an example of a measured RBP-binding surface, but not the same-window decay-factor occupancy/loss/rescue panel required for the present RNA-decay bridge.  A third translation contact contains $n=6494$ rows in run $0c2bbfd3e2d9$, with source material from the Thoreen translation dataset and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  This supports the requirement that translation be represented as its own row-level readout rather than inferred from RNA stability alone.  For comparison, the N-terminal acetylation occupancy contact has $n=657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, and an AUROC of $0.942081$ on $622$ high-low cases in run $f452e76f6363$; this is again a layer-specific occupancy statistic, not a substitute for RNA decay or RBP perturbation evidence.

\paragraph{Statistic that would license the bridge.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate label, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, and $Z_i$ the control vector containing transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and identifier-mapping covariates.  The intended finite comparison is not the marginal contrast $q_i \to H_i$.  It is the controlled and intervention-checked pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\rightsquigarrow H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\\
q_i &\rightsquigarrow O_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\\
q_i &\not\rightsquigarrow H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\text{ target-RBP loss},\\
q_i &\rightsquigarrow H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\text{ wild-type rescue},\\
q_i &\not\rightsquigarrow H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\text{ binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The arrows here are finite-panel readbacks, not causal declarations by notation.  The bridge is licensed only when the same coordinate contrast that survives the translation-conditioned half-life model also follows measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, disappears under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  If any of these rows are absent, the claim remains a bounded association or a descriptor of the finite table rather than an RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels with respect to RNA half-life after the same-row translation readout and the transcript-level controls have been conditioned.  In symbols, the null keeps the empirical rows fixed but permutes the residual coordinate labels inside amino-acid, transcript, isoform, and covariate strata:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\qquad
O_i \perp q_i \mid T_i,Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
A half-life contrast that survives this null but lacks measured RBP occupancy is not a decay-factor RBP bridge.  An occupancy contrast that is not decoupled from coordinate construction, or that does not collapse under target-RBP loss, is not a perturbational bridge.  A target-RBP loss effect without wild-type rescue and without binding-defective or non-target controls is also insufficient.  The null is therefore stricter than ordinary RNA-stability association: the coordinate, translation readout, half-life readout, local RBP occupancy, loss condition, rescue condition, and negative rescue controls must all occur in one finite joined panel.

\paragraph{Readback from the supplied finite contacts.}
The supplied facts make the boundary sharp.  The Hansen-linked finite table contributes $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry values for a protein-acetylation occupancy surface.  The RBNS/eCLIP contact contributes $900$ binding peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases.  The translation contact contributes $6494$ rows for a translation readout route.  These are real finite contacts, but they are not yet the required joined RNA panel.  They do not provide same-organism, same-window RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue on the same rows.  Consequently the chapter records a precise readiness condition: the existing finite evidence supports the form of the proposed cross-layer test and the availability of neighboring layer-specific contacts, while the positive RNA half-life RBP bridge remains unasserted until the missing rows are present.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA-decay mechanism follows from a half-life contrast unless local decay-factor RBP occupancy changes in the required direction and the perturbation/rescue pattern is present.  No protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate label, from codon/window geometry, from a translation route, from an RBP-binding route, or from an acetylation stoichiometry table.  Each of those conclusions would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  In the present record the only admissible statement is the bounded one: a translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP bridge is well specified as a finite-row test, and the available facts identify adjacent measurable surfaces, but the positive bridge is not promoted without the same-row occupancy, loss, and rescue panel.
