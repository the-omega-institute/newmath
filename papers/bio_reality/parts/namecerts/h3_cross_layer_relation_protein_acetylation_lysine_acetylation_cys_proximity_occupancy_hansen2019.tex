\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life boundary with a prespecified RBP occupancy gate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-hansen2019}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel whose rows carry a synonymous residual coordinate label $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$, a coding-sequence or transcript identifier, the amino-acid quotient needed to compare synonymous windows, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and the proposed local occupancy state of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein.  The intended comparison is not a free association between codon geometry and RNA stability.  It is the narrower question whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels remain distinguishable in RNA half-life after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout have all been represented in the finite table.

The available finite-row evidence fixes the scale of the surrounding BioReality surface but does not yet provide the required RBP perturbation panel for this exact bridge.  The protein-acetylation contact named by the claim has $n=6288$ rows, $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, and a recorded runtime of $8.456$ seconds.  A separate RBP-binding contact has $900$ total peak rows, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and a runtime of $223.648043$ seconds.  A translation readout contact used as a boundary comparison has $n=6494$ rows and runtime $9.469$ seconds.  An RNA modification stoichiometry contact provides a larger scalar check, with $n=60000$, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance marked true, and coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$.  These numbers show that the registry contains finite empirical row sets at the post-translational, RBP, translation, and RNA-stoichiometry layers; they do not, by themselves, form the joined RNA half-life--RBP perturbation matrix required below.

\paragraph{Null model and statistic.}
Let $Q_i$ denote the high-versus-low residual coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ denote RNA half-life, and let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of the named decay-factor RBP in the editable RNA window.  The local null is exchangeability of the high- and low-coordinate labels with respect to $H_i$ after the declared controls and after conditioning on $T_i$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp Q_i \;\big|\; T_i, Z_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ contains transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch.  A positive RNA half-life association is therefore not enough.  The stronger RBP bridge requires a coordinated pattern: the half-life residual must track a measured local change in $O_i$, the effect must collapse under target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue must restore it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue must fail to restore it.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The finite panel crosses the claimed boundary only if all four tests are present on matched rows.  First, the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate contrast must survive the same-row translation conditioning and the controls in $Z_i$.  Second, the same rows must show experimentally separated local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, not merely a motif-density or RNA-structure proxy.  Third, target-RBP loss must remove or sharply attenuate the coordinate-associated half-life residual.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must return the residual while binding-defective or non-target rescue does not.  Written schematically, the admissible readout is a finite contrast of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(Q;T,Z,O) &= \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{high}}-\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{low}},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H(Q;T,Z,O),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The notation records the acceptance pattern only; the present data packet does not yet contain these four matched perturbation contrasts for the named subject.

\paragraph{Layer separation.}
The Hansen-style protein-acetylation row count is useful as a finite post-translational empirical scale, but it is not an RNA half-life assay and does not measure decay-factor RBP occupancy in an RNA window.  Likewise, the RBP-binding peak count records an RBP contact surface, but peak presence alone is not target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue.  Translation rows can supply the required same-row boundary $T_i$, but translation conditioning is not itself an RNA decay mechanism.  The RNA stoichiometry rows show that large finite RNA-layer statistics can be computed, yet they do not supply the specific half-life and perturbation columns demanded here.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
The local conclusion is therefore a bounded readiness statement, not a positive mechanism call.  Without a same-organism, same-window table containing $B^{*}_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared finite statistic, the coordinate remains a descriptor or at most an RNA-stability association.  No translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate geometry, the acetylation contact, the RBP peak contact, the translation contact, or review readiness alone.  Each of those stronger readings would require its own layer-matched empirical contact and a finite statistic computed after the relevant controls.
