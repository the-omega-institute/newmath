\subsection{Translation- and abundance-conditioned localization readback for mouse mitochondrial propensity}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-mts-localization-mouse-itzhak}

\paragraph{Finite matched surface.}
The record considered here is the mouse subcellular-localization packet identified by run \texttt{94942f5abfc4}.  Its usable table has $8944$ joined CDS/protein rows, of which $505$ rows are marked by the mitochondrial localization readout used in the experiment.  The recorded runtime for the finite join and statistic surface is $21.266$ seconds.  These numbers fix the scale of the present readback: the object is not an open-ended biological law, but a finite row-indexed panel in which a synonymous residual coordinate is allowed to meet a protein-localization label only after the relevant same-row conditioning surfaces have been attached.

\paragraph{Row variables and admissible direction.}
For each retained row $r$, let $q_r$ denote the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate after amino-acid quotienting, let $T_r$ denote the same-row translation readout or its explicitly bounded proxy, let $A_r$ denote matched mRNA abundance, let $P_r$ denote matched protein abundance, and let $L_r$ denote the protein-localization readout.  The intended ordering is not $q_r \to L_r$ directly.  The permitted readback has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_r &\longrightarrow T_r,\\
(q_r,T_r,A_r) &\longrightarrow P_r,\\
(q_r,T_r,A_r,P_r) &\longrightarrow L_r.
\end{aligned}
$$
The middle line is a conditioning and mediation surface, not a localization mechanism.  Protein abundance may reduce, explain, or bound an apparent localization association, but it does not by itself say how a protein reaches a compartment, how a targeting sequence is recognized, or whether any compartmental function is realized.

\paragraph{Conditioned localization criterion.}
The claim is therefore read as a survival test.  A localization association for a residual coordinate is admissible only when the localization term remains non-null after controlling for amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, CDS length, methionine-density baseline, matched mRNA abundance, same-row translation readout, matched protein abundance, and simpler sequence baselines.  In finite-row notation, the admissible statistic is a residual localization term of the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_r &= \alpha + \beta q_r + \gamma_T T_r + \gamma_A A_r + \gamma_P P_r + \Gamma C_r + \varepsilon_r,\\
\beta &\neq 0 \quad \text{only after the translation and abundance controls remain nondegenerate.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $C_r$ abbreviates the declared baseline controls.  The formula is not a commitment to a specific regression family; it records the row discipline: the $q_r$ coefficient is interpretable only after the translation and abundance surfaces have been represented in the same matched panel.  If the $q_r$ term disappears when $T_r$ or $P_r$ is added, the record returns to the translation or protein-abundance boundary rather than becoming a localization result.

\paragraph{Mouse localization contact.}
The localization contact is protein-state readback: a protein-level label, score, compartment distribution, fractionation enrichment, imaging annotation, or comparable surface keyed to protein identifiers and mapped back to CDS-derived rows.  In the present finite record the mitochondrial subset has $505$ rows inside the $8944$ joined-row panel.  That count is sufficient to name the mitochondrial readout surface and to state the intended conditioned test, but the count alone is not a mechanism for mitochondrial targeting.  It also does not imply that synonymous residual geometry determines protein localization.  The contact supplies the terminal $L_r$ column; the translation, mRNA, and protein-abundance columns decide whether a $q_r$-localization term is still visible after lower-layer explanations are attached.

\paragraph{Null and compression rule.}
The packet carries an explicit compression rule.  If a nondegenerate translation step is absent, the localization readout is not interpreted as a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ localization result.  If the apparent localization relation is absorbed by matched protein abundance, the readback is compressed to a protein-abundance boundary.  If both translation and abundance controls are present but the residual localization term is null, the finite table records a bounded negative result rather than a failed theory.  In symbols, the local rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_r \text{ absent or degenerate} &\Rightarrow q_r \not\leadsto L_r,\\
q_r \leadsto P_r \text{ only} &\Rightarrow \text{protein-abundance boundary},\\
q_r \leadsto L_r \mid T_r,A_r,P_r,C_r \text{ non-null} &\Rightarrow \text{conditioned localization readback}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This keeps the finite row result from being promoted beyond the measurement actually present in the table.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter does not claim translation realization from localization rows alone.  It does not claim folding, physical admissibility, protein trafficking mechanism, compartment identity mechanism, biological function, phenotype, fitness effect, causation, or universality from $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, codon-window geometry, modeled codon-adaptation weights, protein abundance, or the existence of a localization label.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact: ribosome or translation-efficiency data for translation, structural or folding data for folded state, perturbation or assay data for function, and an independently defined biological endpoint for phenotype or system-level claims.  The present result is narrower: in run \texttt{94942f5abfc4}, a finite mouse CDS/protein panel with $8944$ joined rows and $505$ mitochondrial-localization rows defines the row surface on which a translation- and abundance-conditioned residual-coordinate localization statistic may be evaluated.
