\subsection{Translation- and abundance-conditioned localization readback}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-abundance-localization}

\paragraph{Finite matched surface.}
This record concerns a finite coding-sequence table in which each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate $q_i$ from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate system after amino-acid composition has been quotiented out. The localization question is not posed on that coordinate by itself. A row is eligible only when the same entry also carries organism identity, coding-sequence length, $GC3$, methionine-density information, an mRNA control, a translation readout $T_j$, a protein-abundance control, and a protein-localization readout $L_k$. In the verified run $94942f5abfc4$, the joined surface contained $8944$ matched rows, of which $505$ were marked by the mitochondrial localization readout used in the run. Thus the observed mitochondrial-labelled fraction on the joined table was $505/8944 \simeq 0.0565$. The run completed in $21.266$ seconds. These numbers identify the finite surface on which the claim is allowed to speak; they do not by themselves license a universal statement about coding sequences, translation, protein trafficking, or cellular function.

\paragraph{Conditioning order.}
The allowed readback is a chained row-wise statement. First, the residual coordinate $q_i$ is evaluated against a translation-layer surface $T_j$ after amino-acid composition, organism, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, methionine density, and matched mRNA abundance have been represented. Second, protein abundance is treated as an intermediate conditioning surface. Third, the localization label or localization score $L_k$ is interpreted only as a protein-state readout attached to the same matched entry. In symbols, the admissible direction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\longrightarrow T_j \\
    &\longrightarrow \hbox{protein-abundance control} \\
    &\longrightarrow L_k .
\end{aligned}
$$
This ordering is part of the claim. A direct association from $q_i$ to $L_k$ is not the biological statement under audit, because such an association would confound synonymous-coordinate geometry with translation, protein amount, identifier matching, and localization measurement.

\paragraph{Positive content of the record.}
The finite result supports a bounded cross-layer localization-readout question: among $8944$ matched coding-sequence/protein rows in run $94942f5abfc4$, $505$ rows carried the mitochondrial localization mark, so the table is large enough to define a nonempty localization contrast after row joining. The admissible claim is that a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate may be carried forward into a localization analysis only if the coordinate remains informative after the translation readout, mRNA control, and protein-abundance control have all been attached to the same row. The role of protein abundance is deliberately intermediate. It may reduce, absorb, or condition a coordinate-localization association, but it is not itself a localization mechanism. If the translation step is null, degenerate, or missing, the row cannot be promoted to a localization claim; it returns to the translation/protein-abundance boundary.

\paragraph{Null readback.}
The null case is local and explicit. If $q_i$ fails to survive the same-row translation readout after the sequence and expression controls are applied, then any later localization contrast is interpreted as unsupported by this residual coordinate. If $q_i$ survives the translation readout but its apparent localization relation disappears after protein abundance is included, the record remains a translation-to-abundance result rather than a localization result. If the localization table is present but the row lacks a matched translation readout or matched mRNA control, the localization contact supplies only a measurement boundary, not a $q_i \to T_j \to L_k$ statistic. These null outcomes are not failures of the finite table; they are the intended gates that prevent a codon-coordinate signal from being read as a higher-layer biological explanation.

\paragraph{Scope restrictions.}
No translation event follows from the residual coordinate alone. No folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, trafficking mechanism, compartment-identity mechanism, phenotype, fitness statement, or universal biological law follows from this localization surface. The mitochondrial subset count $505$ and the joined-row count $8944$ show that the dataset supplies a concrete readout contrast, but they do not establish how a protein is transported, why a compartment label is realized, whether a protein is functional, or whether a coordinate pattern holds across all organisms. Any such claim would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own statistic. In this chapter, localization is therefore kept as a bounded readback surface: it is downstream of translation and abundance controls, and it remains observational rather than mechanistic.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The record is ready as a finite, row-bounded BioReality chapter because it has a concrete matched table, a specified organism/protein localization readout, and verified scalar facts from run $94942f5abfc4$: $8944$ joined rows, $505$ mitochondrial-labelled rows, and a runtime of $21.266$ seconds. The chapter does not claim a direct route from $B^{*}_{Q6}$ geometry to localization. It states the narrower and testable condition: a residual coordinate can be discussed at the localization layer only after the same row has passed through translation readout, mRNA control, and protein-abundance conditioning, with a declared null result whenever that chain is absent or collapses.
