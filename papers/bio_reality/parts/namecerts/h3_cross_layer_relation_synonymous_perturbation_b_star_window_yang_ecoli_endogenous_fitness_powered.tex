\subsection{Yang \emph{E. coli} endogenous synonymous-fitness window}
\label{sec:bioreality-yang-ecoli-endogenous-fitness-window}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The Yang \emph{E. coli} endogenous-fitness packet is a finite perturbation surface whose rows are synonymous coding edits inside endogenous bacterial genes.  The registered run contains $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edits, with $200$ effective resampling blocks and a recorded runtime of $491.605$ seconds.  The object of study is not a free association between codon usage and fitness.  It is the restricted question whether a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window coordinate remains informative when read only through synonymous edits that preserve amino-acid sequence while varying local codon-window geometry.

\paragraph{Admissible coordinates.}
The coordinate family used by the broader $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ program consists of nine residual axes,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.
For this chapter these axes are treated as finite predictors attached to synonymous-edit windows, not as biological causes.  The endogenous-fitness readout is a downstream perturbation surface, so the allowed mathematical form is a controlled edit-level comparison:
$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= \{\text{synonymous edit rows inside the }30\text{ genes}\},\\
|X| &= 46686,\\
B &= 200,\\
Y &= \text{endogenous fitness readout attached to the edited coding context}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count is large at the edit level but small at the gene level.  The gene count $30$ is therefore part of the interpretation, not a cosmetic detail: any statement that behaves like a population-wide law is outside the certified scope of this finite table.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
The most relevant neighboring matrix is the cross-organism modeled-translation mediation run, where the same nine-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ family was joined to modeled per-protein tAI and measured protein abundance.  In that run the \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 row used $3739$ joined proteins.  After residualizing amino-acid composition, coding length, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, the \emph{E. coli} mediation decomposition reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP} &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T} &= 0.04167869969498872,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= 0.06599656708487024,\\
M^{QTP} &= 0.612922252793667.
\end{aligned}
$$
Those numbers do not by themselves prove the Yang fitness effect.  They supply the nearby finite context in which a direct residual-to-protein interpretation is disallowed unless the residual has first passed through an explicitly declared translation readout.  The Yang chapter therefore uses the endogenous edit table as a perturbation contact and keeps the protein-abundance mediation matrix as supporting geometry for what would have to be checked before any protein-realization language were admitted.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The modeled-tAI survival audit gives a separate bounded contact rather than an observed ribosome-dynamics measurement.  It computed the same nine residual coordinates across $3$ organisms and found $81$ modeled coupling entries above the threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  The audit also records that $3$ organisms are not enough for a powered survival claim requiring at least $40$ matched species.  Consequently, the Yang endogenous-fitness run may be read as a finite synonymous-perturbation table, but not as a translation-realization theorem.  The translation layer would require same-organism, same-row translation data, such as ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency with matched mRNA abundance, before one could assert a chain $q_i \to T_j \to Y$ rather than a bounded synonymous-edit association.

\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\

$$
That contact is not interchangeable with the Yang endogenous-fitness table: it is a protein-output reporter system, whereas the Yang packet is an endogenous-fitness perturbation surface over $46686$ synonymous edits.  Its value here is comparative.  It shows that a bacterial synonymous-window signal can survive in a protein-output assay under its own controls, while the Yang run asks whether a much larger edit table inside endogenous genes supports a fitness-layer readout.  The cross-dataset transfer audit was also executed with $200$ effective resampling blocks and runtime $974.407$ seconds, but no single-dataset sign is promoted to a universal rule.

The certified content of this chapter is the existence of a finite Yang \emph{E. coli} endogenous synonymous-edit surface with $30$ genes, $46686$ synonymous edits, and $200$ resampling blocks, suitable for testing whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window residual family carries controlled fitness information.  The readback is therefore:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{\ast}_{Q6}\text{ window residuals} &\longrightarrow \text{synonymous edit table},\\
\text{synonymous edit table} &\longrightarrow \text{endogenous fitness readout},\\
\text{allowed conclusion} &\subseteq \text{bounded edit-level fitness association}.
\end{aligned}
$$
No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organism-wide selection law, or universal biological law follows from these rows alone.  A direct route from $q_i$ to a fitness value is not enough to claim translation-mediated protein realization.  Such a claim would require an additional matched contact that supplies a surviving translation step, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding length, $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance kept in the control set.  If that intermediate contact is absent, the Yang packet remains a finite endogenous synonymous-perturbation record rather than a mechanistic explanation of why the edits affect fitness.
