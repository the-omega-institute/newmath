\subsection{Endogenous \textit{E. coli} synonymous-fitness window}
\label{sec:biorReality-yang-ecoli-endogenous-fitness-window}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record fixes a bounded perturbation surface for synonymous edits in endogenous \textit{E. coli} coding sequence. The tested carrier is the finite table of edit rows indexed by a host gene, a synonymous nucleotide change that preserves the encoded amino acid, and the associated $B^*_Q6$ window coordinates computed around that edit. The registered run contains $30$ genes and $46686$ synonymous edit rows, with a permutation or resampling budget recorded as $B=200$ and a wall-clock runtime of $491.605$ seconds. Thus the object is not a genome-wide law and not a protein-mechanism assay; it is a finite endogenous-fitness screen in which synonymous coding changes are used to ask whether the $B^*_Q6$ residual window carries predictive information after the ordinary coding-sequence controls have been kept in place.

\paragraph{Controlled question.}
Let $e$ range over the $46686$ synonymous edit rows. For each row, write $g(e)$ for its host gene, $y(e)$ for the measured endogenous fitness or growth readout attached to the edit, $x(e)$ for the local $B^*_Q6$ window vector, and $z(e)$ for the matched baseline covariates: amino-acid identity, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, local codon-count baselines, and gene-level blocking where the design permits it. The finite question is whether the residual window term improves a held-out or null-calibrated fitness model beyond those controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(e) &= \ell\bigl(y(e) \mid z(e), x(e)\bigr)-\ell\bigl(y(e) \mid z(e)\bigr),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{run}} &= \sum_{e=1}^{46686}\Delta(e).
\end{aligned}
$$
The row count matters: the evidence is powered by many edit rows but only $30$ genes, so the correct interpretation is an endogenous synonymous-perturbation window test, not a broad cross-organism population statement. The $B=200$ null budget records that the claim is evaluated against a finite randomized comparison rather than by visual inspection or by selecting a favorable codon axis after seeing the outcome.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation gate.}
The biological layer claimed here is a synonymous-perturbation fitness layer. That layer is downstream of codon choice but is not automatically downstream of translation. The wider $B^*_Q6$ translation-mediation audit provides a separate reference point: in the cross-organism protein-abundance matrix, the modeled per-protein translation readout used $9$ residual coordinates and showed positive finite mediation fractions in all listed organisms, including \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 with $n=3739$ joined proteins, $c_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$, $c'_{QP\mid T}=0.04167869969498872$, and indirect component $0.06599656708487024$, giving $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$. That fact does not prove the Yang endogenous-fitness mechanism. It only shows that the same family of residual coordinates has a separately bounded translation-conditioned protein-abundance surface in a matched \textit{E. coli} protein table. The present synonymous-edit surface must still be read on its own finite rows.

\paragraph{Modeled readout boundary.}
The modeled-tAI survival audit supplies another boundary condition. It computed $9$ $B^*_Q6$ synonymous residual coordinates, namely $\mathrm{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. Its finite modeled-tAI table used $3$ organisms, found $81$ entries above the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$, and recorded a maximum absolute coupling value $3801961.031607896$. The same audit explicitly kept that result below powered survival because $3$ organisms are insufficient for a powered multi-organism survival call. Consequently, the Yang endogenous-edit record may use $B^*_Q6$ windows as residual coordinates, but it may not import a completed translation-realization claim from the modeled-tAI table.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The scientific readback is therefore narrow. A positive Yang-window result says that, inside the finite set of $46686$ synonymous edits across $30$ endogenous \textit{E. coli} genes, the local $B^*_Q6$ residual window is a useful predictor of the assayed fitness readout beyond the declared synonymous and sequence controls. A null or nonpositive result says only that this particular endogenous-edit table fails to support the added residual window under its finite design. In neither case does the table by itself identify ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype mechanism, selection law, or universal biological regularity. Those stronger readings require separate measurements at the corresponding layer and a joined statistic showing that the same coordinate first survives the appropriate translation or protein readout step.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The row-level audit is complete enough for a bounded chapter entry because the experiment ran to completion and reports its finite scope: $30$ genes, $46686$ synonymous edits, $B=200$, and $491.605$ seconds of runtime. The chapter is therefore allowed to record an endogenous synonymous-fitness window result, with the important limitation that the supplied finite facts do not include the effect size, null-tail probability, per-axis coefficient list, or gene-block calibration table for this exact Yang run. The claim is consequently a powered finite-row contact with a strict no-promotion rule: it can support only the stated endogenous synonymous-perturbation surface unless additional matched translation, protein, structure, physical, function, mechanism, or cross-organism contacts are supplied.
