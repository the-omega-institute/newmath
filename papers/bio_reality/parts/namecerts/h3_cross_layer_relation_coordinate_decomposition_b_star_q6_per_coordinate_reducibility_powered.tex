\subsection{Per-coordinate reducibility of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-b-star-q6-per-coordinate-reducibility}

\paragraph{Question.}
The powered per-coordinate experiment asks whether the nine synonymous residual coordinates of $B^*_{Q6}$ remain a single opaque block, or whether individual coordinates can be separately read against measured abundance after the same sibling controls are removed.  The source design is the nine-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ residual system, residualized against the control basis $Z$ consisting of coding-sequence length, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  The target is yeast $\log_{10}$ protein abundance, again residualized against the sibling $Z$ controls on the unified yeast context join.  The experiment is therefore not a statement about the genetic code in the abstract; it is a finite-row projection test on a particular joined yeast abundance table.

\paragraph{Finite design.}
The run records $1800$ base rows and a joined analysis table of $n=1258$ proteins for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The minimum protein-count gate was $500$ proteins per organism, the decomposition used $5$ held-out folds, and the deterministic null used $120$ target-label permutations with the same folds and design.  The residual control basis had rank $24$, while the measured readout dictionary used for reducibility had rank $3$.  For a single coordinate $x_i$, the statistic is the held-out coverage of the one-coordinate abundance projection $P_i=\operatorname{Proj}_{x_i}(\mathrm{target})$ by the fixed residualized dictionary $D_e$.  In the notation of the run, the coverage quantity is an orthogonal projection score, not a causal coefficient:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_i &= \operatorname{Proj}_{x_i}(y_{\perp Z}),\\
\operatorname{Cov}_i &= \frac{\|\operatorname{Proj}_{D_e}(P_i)\|^2}{\|P_i\|^2},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the held-out value compared against the deterministic permutation boundary.  The audited table has exactly $9$ coordinate rows: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.

\paragraph{Observed coordinate split.}
The decomposition completed all $9$ requested rows and classified eight coordinates as having a real measured-abundance source under the held-out/permutation gate: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The only listed coordinate not in the real-source set is $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$.  Thus the finite result is stronger than a one-entry existence statement but still weaker than a universal rule: the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component separates into coordinate-level sources on this yeast join, with one coordinate failing the reported gate.

\paragraph{Consistency with neighboring abundance rows.}
The same yeast abundance layer is not isolated from the broader finite record.  In the condition-specific yeast protein-abundance comparison, $2209$ shared proteins were available across YEPD and SD, with $R^2_{QP}=0.3280825603424665$ in YEPD and $R^2_{QP}=0.3050385538305022$ in SD; both conditions reported $f_3$-stress as the dominant coordinate, and the two $S^{QP}$ vectors had Pearson $0.9983880256003688$ and cosine $0.9993775935182967$.  On the same general yeast abundance interface, the mRNA-stability mediation row used $n=5085$ joined records and decomposed $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$ to $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, with an absorbed fraction $0.09994436477872035$ and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$.  The yeast mRNA half-life row itself reported $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, and cosine $0.8040935468384264$ between the $S^{QM}$ and same-join $S^{QP}$ vectors.  These neighboring rows do not prove the per-coordinate result, but they show that the coordinate decomposition sits in a numerically populated abundance-and-stability neighborhood rather than in a single unmatched statistic.

\paragraph{Coordinate substructure of $f_3$-stress.}
The family-level $f_3$-stress split provides a sharper local check on the largest stress-style coordinate.  That run used $n=1800$ joined yeast rows, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, and a rank-one $f_3$ source.  The full $f_3$ projection had norm squared $52.76911713166682$ and accounted for $0.12259712041843235$ of the target norm, with target norm squared $430.42705205115925$.  It decomposed $21$ synonymous-family subcoordinates and reported $19$ family sources passing the held-out/permutation gate, including $K_{AAA\to AAG}$, $R_{AGA\to AGG}$, $I_{AUU\to AUA}$, $L_{UUA\to UUG}$, $S_{UCU\to UCG}$, and $T_{ACU\to ACG}$.  This supports the interpretation that the per-coordinate abundance signal is not merely a naming artifact of the nine-coordinate basis; at least one aggregate coordinate also has a nontrivial family-level internal split.

\paragraph{Readout boundaries.}
The result is a measured protein-abundance reducibility statement, not a translation-realization theorem.  The H-candidate ranking row classifies the broader packet at the cross-layer relation level and reports, for example, a yeast $mRNA$ top candidate with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, score $-0.008853737772559931$, $n=5128$, and Bonferroni threshold $0.0013014749238591248$; the same ranking uses a description-length penalty $DL_H=1.0$ for one-column candidates and $DL_H=3.700439718141092$ for the localization dictionary.  Separately, the ribosome-occupancy row used a penalty parameter $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $DL=2$, and found the families $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, and $f_3$-stress passing its local occupancy gates.  Those auxiliary rows help locate possible readout directions, but they do not convert the present abundance decomposition into a mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For this finite yeast table, the verdict is that the per-coordinate decomposition is realized: all nine coordinates were evaluated, and eight have an audited real-source classification against residualized measured protein abundance under held-out and permutation controls.  The module assignment is cross-layer relation, because the source is codon-residual geometry and the target is measured protein abundance after covariate removal.  The claim remains deliberately bounded.  It does not establish ribosome-mediated translation, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, molecular mechanism, organismal phenotype, or a universal law.  Any such promotion would require a separate matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
