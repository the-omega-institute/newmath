\subsection{Per-coordinate reducibility of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bq6-per-coordinate-reducibility}

\paragraph{Object of measurement.}
This chapter records a finite-row decomposition test for the nine synonymous residual coordinates in the $B^*_{Q6}$ packet.  The carrier is the residualized coordinate family
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress, evaluated against a measured yeast protein-abundance target.  The target is $\log_{10}$ protein abundance after the same sibling control basis $Z$ has been removed.  Here $Z$ contains the amino-acid composition controls, coding-sequence length, GC3, organism context when applicable, and the $M$-density baseline used to prevent the residual coordinates from merely re-reading a simpler codon-density description.

\paragraph{Finite design.}
The powered decomposition run used \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} and a unified context join with $n=1258$ usable rows from a base pool of $1800$ rows.  The residual control basis had rank $24$, while the measured-readout dictionary after residualization had rank $3$.  For each coordinate $x_i$, the statistic first formed the single-coordinate abundance component $P_i=\operatorname{Proj}_{x_i}(target)$, then asked how much of this component is covered by the fixed measured-readout dictionary.  The coverage statistic was estimated with $5$ held-out folds and compared with a deterministic target-label permutation null using $120$ permutations.  Thus the experiment is not a population law; it is a finite, held-out, permutation-checked readback for one joined yeast abundance table.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= (x_{K},x_{Arg},x_{Ile},x_{Leu,CUN/UUR},x_{Leu,UUA/UUG},x_{Ser,UCR/AGY},x_{Ser,UCA/UCG},x_{Thr},x_{f_3}),\\
P_i &= \operatorname{Proj}_{x_i}(target_Z),\\
C_i &= \operatorname{coverage}_{D_Z}(P_i),\\
\text{real source}(i) &\Longleftrightarrow C_i\text{ exceeds the fixed-fold permutation }Null95_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Coordinate table outcome.}
The decomposition table was fully produced for all $9$ coordinates.  Eight coordinates passed the held-out/permutation gate as real abundance sources in this design: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The table still includes $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$ as a computed coordinate, but that coordinate is not listed among the passing sources for this finite run.  The local conclusion is therefore coordinate-resolved rather than merely joint: the abundance association is not carried by an unnamed nine-dimensional block alone; most individual residual coordinates have separately testable coverage by the measured readout dictionary after the declared controls.

\paragraph{Relation to the joint abundance component.}
The coordinate decomposition is consistent with the earlier yeast abundance projection in which the $B^*_{Q6}$ block explains a nonzero residual protein-abundance component.  In the cross-organism complement calculation, the yeast abundance projection reported $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624987287115$ with $n=5815$ proteins and a control rank of $23$, and the modeled-translation-complement fraction for yeast was $d=0.21173219572706703$ with complement mass $m=0.788267804272933$.  The present per-coordinate test is narrower: it does not reuse that joint value as proof for every coordinate, but decomposes the abundance component into single-coordinate projections and tests each projection against the same residualized measured-readout dictionary.

\paragraph{Relation to the $f_3$ family split.}
The $f_3$-stress coordinate also has an internal family decomposition on the same yeast abundance target.  That run used $n=1800$ joined rows from a base pool of $4759$, $5$ held-out folds, $120$ permutations, and a control-basis rank of $24$.  It decomposed $f_3$ into $21$ synonymous-family subcoordinates.  The full $f_3$ projection had squared norm $52.76911713166682$ and accounted for $0.12259712041843235$ of the target squared norm, with target norm squared $430.42705205115925$.  Nineteen family subcoordinates passed the real-source gate, including $K_{AAA\to AAG}$, $R_{AGA\to AGG}$, $I_{AUU\to AUA}$, $T_{ACU\to ACG}$, and several twofold $N$, $D$, $E$, $Y$, $C$, $H$, and $Q$ contrasts.  This supports the reading that the passing $f_3$ coordinate is not a single opaque aggregate in the finite yeast table; it has family-level structure under the same style of held-out/permutation test.

\paragraph{Mediation and translation contacts.}
The coordinate decomposition is an abundance-readout result, not a translation-mechanism result.  A separate yeast mRNA-stability mediation run on $n=5085$ joined rows reported $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, and an absorbed fraction $0.09994436477872035$ when residualized $\log_{10}$ mRNA half-life was inserted as mediator.  That mediation statistic had a right-tail permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$ against a null mean $0.00019674487584783138$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0014571003414947088$.  Those numbers show that part of the abundance association can be statistically partitioned through an mRNA-stability readout, but they do not turn the per-coordinate abundance table into a causal pathway.

A different local ribosome-occupancy contact tested codon-window separation under exact position and amino-acid bins.  In that contact, $9$ families were computed, all $9$ had at least $200$ within-bin permutations, and the families passing the $C_H>0$ gate with $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$ and $DL=2$ were $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, and $f_3$-stress.  This gives bounded ribosome-occupancy evidence for selected families, but it is not the same object as the yeast protein-abundance coordinate decomposition.  The two observations are compatible because they test different readout surfaces.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The supported statement is deliberately limited.  In the finite yeast join, after the declared controls and baseline ablation, the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate system has a complete nine-row per-coordinate decomposition, and eight of the nine coordinates pass the held-out/permutation criterion as measured abundance sources.  This establishes a coordinate-resolved statistical readback of residual codon usage into a protein-abundance table.  It does not establish ribosome-mediated translation realization for every coordinate, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, molecular mechanism, phenotype, or any universal biological law.  Each of those stronger interpretations requires its own layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic.  The result should therefore be read as a finite, coordinate-level abundance decomposition with explicit controls, not as a promotion from codon residual geometry to mechanism or function.
