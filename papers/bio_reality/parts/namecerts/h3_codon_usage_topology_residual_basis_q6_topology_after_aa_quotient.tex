\subsection{Codon-Usage Topology After the Amino-Acid Quotient}\label{sec:biorreality-q6-codon-usage-residual-basis}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim.}
The finite record in this section concerns codon-level synonymous residuals after amino-acid composition has already been quotiented out. The biological object is not a protein fold, a biochemical mechanism, or a universal law of translation. It is the per-organism codon-count table projected onto the residual coordinate family
$B^{*}_{Q6}$, whose named elements are
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and an antisymmetric third-position stress pair. The question is whether any of these residual coordinates can remain visible after controlling for amino-acid sequence $y$ and simpler codon-feature baselines. The finite data presently support the weaker coordinate claim: the residual basis is nontrivial on the available organism rows. They do not yet support a positive controlled survival score against an organism-matched translation readout.

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The verified computation loaded one codon-count table for each of three organisms: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$. The NCBI genetic-code table was loaded, and the contact scope is explicitly finite: three organism rows, not a population estimate. Across the nine residual coordinates in $B^{*}_{Q6}$, seven exceeded the stated variance threshold $0.01$. Thus the observed nonzero fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{7}{9} &= 0.7777777777777778.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a coordinate-level fact about codon-count variation after the amino-acid quotient. It says that the residual coordinates are not all annihilated by the quotient on these rows. It does not say that any coordinate improves prediction of tAI, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, function, physical admissibility, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Residual basis.}
The computed residual family has element count $9$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{*}_{Q6}=\{&K_{AAA}, Arg_{AGR}, Ile_{AUA}, Leu_{CUN/UUR}, Leu_{UUA/UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR/AGY}, Ser_{UCA/UCG}, Thr_{ACR/ACY}, f_{3}\text{-stress}_{anti}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The amino-acid quotient is essential here. A statistic that only measures amino-acid composition is not allowed to count as evidence for these residual coordinates, because the tested directions are precisely those left after fixing the amino-acid sequence. In particular, the $AAA/AAG$ lysine contrast and the $CUN/UUR$ leucine contrast are residual contrasts, not amino-acid-composition contrasts. A model that only uses the coarser $M$-density can locate a local codon-topological summary, but it cannot by itself test the $CUN/UUR$ or $AAA/AAG$ residual alternatives.

\paragraph{Motif compression check.}
The associated finite motif-compression row reports exact equality between the computed set $R$ and the motif union. The listed codons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU,\\
&UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
There were no missing codons and no extra codons in this equality check. The point of citing this row locally is to make clear that the residual-basis computation is not floating free of codon-level finite data: the motif side has a concrete thirteen-codon support, and the check reports exact agreement for that support.

These numbers record the finite topological quotient through which the residual claim is filtered. They support the statement that the residual test is being asked after a definite quotient operation, not before it.

\paragraph{Readout test.}
The intended survival statistic compares a baseline score with a score that includes one residual coordinate $q$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{Q}(r\mid Z)&=J_{0}(Z)-J_{Q}(Z,q),\\
Z&=(y,c,\text{simpler codon-feature baselines}).
\end{aligned}
$$
For the sharper test against an $M$-density baseline, the condition is $S_{Q}(r\mid Z,\Delta_{M})>0$ for at least one $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$ and at least one readout $r$ such as tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance. The present finite row does not include an organism-matched table combining codon usage, tRNA copy numbers, and tAI or stAI weights with amino-acid composition controlled. Therefore the positive-survival version of the claim remains blocked rather than confirmed or refuted. The current computation establishes nontrivial residual coordinates on three organism codon-count rows; it does not establish that those coordinates survive a controlled translation-layer readout.

\paragraph{Local limitation.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded coordinate result. Codon-count residual variance is a real finite contact with sequence composition, but it is not a measurement of ribosome motion, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, molecular mechanism, or a universal biological rule. Any claim in those directions requires a separate matched contact. In the present state, the legitimate conclusion is that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ is a nontrivial residual coordinate family on the available finite organism rows, with $7$ of $9$ residuals nonzero above threshold $0.01$, while the stronger claim that some residual improves a controlled translation readout over an $M$-only baseline is still awaiting the paired statistic.
