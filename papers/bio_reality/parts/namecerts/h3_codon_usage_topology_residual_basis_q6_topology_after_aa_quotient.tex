\subsection{Amino-Acid Residual Codon Topology After the Quotient}
\label{sec:bior-toplevel-residual-basis-q6-after-aa-quotient}

\paragraph{Object of the record.}
This record isolates a codon-level residual question after amino-acid identity has already been held fixed.  The carrier is the per-organism table of coding-sequence codon counts, projected onto the amino-acid residual basis $B^{*}_{Q6}$.  The basis used here has nine named coordinates:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and an antisymmetric third-position stress coordinate.  These coordinates are not amino-acid composition itself.  They are the remaining directions left after the amino-acid quotient has removed the part of codon usage that only repeats the protein sequence.

\paragraph{Finite contact.}
The finite computation used three organism-level codon-count tables: Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K-12.  The computation loaded the NCBI genetic code and treated each organism as one within-organism codon-count contact, so the scope is a finite three-row observational contact rather than a population estimate.  In this finite contact, the residual basis contains $9$ elements, of which $7$ have nonzero residual variance above threshold $0.01$.  Thus the observed nonzero fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \frac{7}{9} = 0.7777777777777778.
\end{aligned}
$$
This establishes only that the quotient did not erase all codon-topological variation in the finite organism table.  It does not yet say that the residual coordinates improve a translation readout.

\paragraph{Compressed motif support.}
The auxiliary motif computation reports that the relevant finite set $R$ is exactly the following $13$-codon union:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA, AGA, AGG, AUA, CUA, CUC, CUG, CUU, UAA, UAG, UCA, UGA, UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same computation reports no missing elements and no extra elements.  This matters because the residual-basis question is not being inferred from an open-ended motif search.  The finite codon list is closed at the level of this computation, and the subsequent residual analysis is performed against that closed list rather than against an unspecified codon family.

\paragraph{Quotient geometry.}
The quotient-side finite geometry supplies an independent check on the coordinate reduction.  The reported $M$-complex face vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  f(M) = (20, 38, 26, 8, 1),
\end{aligned}
$$
and the quotient face vector is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  f(M/{\sim}) = (10, 14, 6, 1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The quotient image has size $10$, with representative binary rows
$$
\begin{aligned}
&00001,\; 00011,\; 00101,\; 00111,\; 01000,\; 01001,\\
&10001,\; 10011,\; 10101,\; 10111.
\end{aligned}
$$
These numbers give the local combinatorial background for the residual claim: the amino-acid quotient is a real finite reduction, but it leaves enough residual directions for codon-level distinctions such as $AAA/AAG$, $AGR$ for arginine, $AUA$ for isoleucine, and the two leucine contrasts to remain testable.

\paragraph{Survival statistic to be tested.}
For a readout $r$ such as organism-scoped tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, the intended statistic compares a baseline model $J_0$ to a model $J_Q$ that includes one residual coordinate $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$.  With $Z$ containing the amino-acid sequence $y$, biological context $c$, and simpler codon-feature baselines, the local survival score is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  S_Q(r \mid Z) = J_0(Z) - J_Q(Z,q).
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive value for some $q$ would mean that the residual coordinate improves the chosen bounded readout after amino-acid composition and simpler codon features have been controlled.  A nonzero residual count by itself is weaker: it only says that the coordinate exists in the observed codon-count geometry.

\paragraph{Blocked readout boundary.}
The available contacts include per-organism codon-usage tables, organism-level tRNA gene-copy summaries, and possible tAI or stAI weights.  However, the current row does not yet contain a curated organism-matched statistic combining codon usage, tRNA copy numbers, and tAI or stAI under amino-acid-composition control.  Therefore the claim that an $M$-only baseline is insufficient remains blocked at the readout stage.  The appropriate null statement is not that $M$-only is adequate; it is that the paired controlled statistic has not yet been supplied.

\paragraph{Local limitation.}
The finite result may be reported as a codon-topology residual after amino-acid quotienting: three organism tables yield a $9$-coordinate residual basis with $7$ nonzero coordinates above the stated variance threshold, supported by the closed $13$-codon motif list and the quotient face-vector calculation.  Nothing stronger follows from this record alone.  In particular, the codon-count residuals do not establish translation realization, ribosome-mediated dynamics, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, protein-output causation, or a universal biological law.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate matched contact at the relevant layer, and the survival statistic must still control for amino-acid sequence before a positive residual score can be interpreted as readout-level evidence.
