\subsection{Amino-acid-residual codon topology after the quotient}\label{subsec:biorreality-q6-aa-residual-topology}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
This chapter records a bounded BioReality claim about synonymous codon residuals after amino-acid composition has been held fixed.  The object is not a protein-function claim and not a mechanism claim.  It is the finite coordinate question obtained by projecting per-organism coding-sequence codon counts onto the residual basis
$B^{*}_{Q6}$, where only distinctions invisible to the amino-acid sequence are allowed to remain.  The tested residual basis has nine named coordinates:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and an antisymmetric third-position stress pair.  The finite computation reports nine residual elements, with seven nonzero elements above variance threshold $0.01$, so the observed nonzero fraction is $7/9 = 0.7777777777777778$.  This is a finite-contact statement over three organism codon-count tables, namely human, budding yeast, and the K-12 strain of $E.\ coli$; it is not a population estimate over organisms.

These rows are used only as the finite quotient geometry controlling which codon distinctions are still visible after the amino-acid sequence has been accounted for.

\paragraph{Residual readout target.}
For a residual coordinate $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$ and a readout $r$, the intended survival comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes the amino-acid sequence $y$, local context, and simpler codon-feature baselines.  A positive value would mean that adding $q$ improves the readout model relative to the controlled baseline.  The admissible readouts for the next contact layer are bounded translation-associated quantities such as tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, but the present finite row has not yet supplied an organism-matched controlled statistic for those readouts.  The current result therefore certifies the existence and nontriviality of the residual coordinate family in finite codon-count contact; it does not certify positive translation survival.

\paragraph{Why the residual basis matters.}
An $M$-only summary can describe a local codon-density optimum, but it cannot by itself test distinctions such as $CUN$ versus $UUR$ in leucine or $AAA$ versus $AAG$ in lysine once the amino-acid sequence has already been fixed.  Those comparisons live in the residual direction perpendicular to amino-acid composition.  The finite row therefore separates two questions: first, whether the residual coordinates are present and nontrivial in observed organism codon tables; second, whether any such coordinate improves a translation-associated readout under the controlled statistic above.  The first question is answered in the finite data: nine residual elements are computed, seven are nonzero above the stated threshold, and the organism scope is exactly three tables.  The second question remains a controlled-data requirement rather than a conclusion.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The finite computation is a codon-count and coordinate-geometry contact.  It does not show ribosome-mediated translation realization from codon counts alone.  It also does not show protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, molecular mechanism, protein-output causality, or a universal biological rule.  A nonzero residual variance is a prompt for a sharper experiment, not a license to infer a higher biological layer.  Any claim about translation efficiency must come from a separate paired dataset containing organism-matched codon usage, relevant tRNA copy-number or adaptation weights, and the controlled statistic $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$.  Until that statistic exists, the absence of a positive survival value is a blocked null condition, not evidence against the residual topology and not evidence for $M$-only sufficiency.
