\subsection{Codon-usage residual topology after the amino-acid quotient}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-residual-topology-after-aa-quotient}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the codon-level topology of the $Q6$ coordinate system leaves any organism-specific residue after amino-acid identity has already been held fixed. The measured object is not protein structure, biochemical function, or a mechanism of translation. It is a finite codon-count contact: per-organism coding-sequence codon summaries are projected onto the amino-acid residual basis $B^{*}_{Q6}$, and each residual coordinate is read as a possible controlled predictor only after the amino-acid sequence variable $y$ and simpler codon-feature baselines have been included in the conditioning context $Z$.

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The carrier used here is the organism-indexed table of coding-sequence codon counts for three organisms: \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, and \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12}. The NCBI genetic code was loaded, and each organism contributes one codon-count table. Thus the present contact has organism count $3$ and is explicitly finite: it is not a population-level estimate across taxa, environments, tissues, growth phases, or expression regimes.

The residual basis has nine named coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{*}_{Q6}=\{&K_{\mathrm{AAA}},\ \mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}},\ \mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}/\mathrm{UUG}},\\
&\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}/\mathrm{AGY}},\ \mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}/\mathrm{UCG}},\ \mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}/\mathrm{ACY}},\ f_{3}\text{-stress}_{\mathrm{antisymmetric}}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite computation reports $9$ basis elements, with $7$ passing the nonzero variance threshold $0.01$. Equivalently, the nonzero fraction is $7/9=0.7777777777777778$. This number is a statement about the observed codon-count residual coordinates in the three-organism contact; it is not a claim that any residual has already survived against a translation readout.

\paragraph{Motif compression contact.}
A separate finite check identifies the relevant motif union $R$ without missing or extra codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported discrepancy lists are empty: no codon is missing from the finite motif union, and no extra codon is present. This matters because the residual-basis test depends on the specific codon distinctions that remain visible after amino-acid quotienting, not merely on a broad synonymous-codon density statistic.

The quotient geometry contact reports an $M$-side face vector

The quotient image has size $10$, with the finite image points

The product-decomposition witness for $M$ is empty in this finite row, while the expected quotient image and both face vectors match their checks. The scientific consequence is narrow: an $M$-only summary can record a local codon-density geometry, but it does not by itself expose the $\mathrm{CUN}/\mathrm{UUR}$ leucine split or the $\mathrm{AAA}/\mathrm{AAG}$ lysine split. Those distinctions sit in the amino-acid-residual basis and must be tested as residual coordinates.

\paragraph{Controlled survival statistic.}
For a readout $r$ such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, the intended residual score is the conditional improvement
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_Q(r\mid Z)=J_0(Z)-J_Q(Z,q),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q\in B^{*}_{Q6}$ and $Z$ contains the amino-acid sequence variable $y$, organism or gene context $c$, and simpler codon-feature baselines such as an $M$-density term $\Delta_M$. A positive value $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)>0$ would mean that a named residual coordinate improves the chosen readout after the amino-acid quotient and after the simpler baseline have both been accounted for. A nonpositive value for every $q$ would leave the $M$-only baseline unbroken for that readout and dataset.

\paragraph{Present finite result.}
The present row verifies that the residual basis was computed, that the finite cross-organism codon-count contact was loaded for three organisms, and that $7$ of $9$ residual coordinates are nonzero at variance threshold $0.01$. It also verifies the motif list $R$, the quotient image size $10$, and the face-vector data $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and $(10,14,6,1)$. These facts establish that the residual coordinate system is nontrivial on the finite codon-count contact. They do not establish that any residual coordinate improves a translation-layer readout, because the paired controlled statistic with organism-matched codon usage, tRNA copy numbers, and tAI or stAI weights has not yet been supplied.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from codon-count residual variance alone. No ribosome-mediated mechanism, protein abundance effect, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, or universal biological law is asserted here. A stronger claim would require a separate matched contact that computes $S_Q(r\mid Z,\Delta_M)$ against a concrete readout and reports the sign and magnitude for each organism and each tested residual coordinate. Until that contact exists, the record is a precise finite coordinate result with a blocked stronger interpretation, not a biological conclusion about translation, folding, function, or universality.
