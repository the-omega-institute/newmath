\subsection{Amino-acid residual coordinates for codon-topology readback}
\label{subsec:h3-codon-usage-topology-residual-basis-q6-topology-after-aa-quotient}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats per-organism coding-sequence codon counts as the observed carrier, after quotienting away the amino-acid sequence itself. The retained coordinates are the residual directions that remain visible after amino-acid composition control. The finite residual basis is
$$
\begin{aligned}
B^{*}_{Q6} = \{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN/UUR},\ Leu_{UUA/UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR/AGY},\ Ser_{UCA/UCG},\ Thr_{ACR/ACY},\ f_{3}\text{-stress antisymmetric}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The verified computation reports $9$ basis elements. Among these, $7$ have nonzero organism-level residual variance above the stated threshold $0.01$, giving a nonzero fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
7/9 = 0.7777777777777778.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite contact over three organisms, not a population estimate: the observed organisms are \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12. The computation loaded the NCBI genetic code and used one codon-count table per organism as its within-organism contact.

\paragraph{Topology retained before readout.}
The upstream finite motif check identifies the residual codon set $R$ exactly as the following $13$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA,\ AGA,\ AGG,\ AUA,\ CUA,\ CUC,\ CUG,\ CUU,\\
&UAA,\ UAG,\ UCA,\ UGA,\ UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same check reports no missing codons and no extra codons in this motif union. This matters because the residual basis is not being introduced as an unconstrained biological feature list; it is the finite codon-topology residue left after the amino-acid quotient and the specified motif compression agree.

\paragraph{Quotient geometry.}
The associated finite quotient computation has $M$-vector

\begin{aligned}
(20,38,26,8,1),
\end{aligned}
$$
and the quotient $f$-vector
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
The projected quotient has size $10$. Its listed cells are
$$
\begin{aligned}

&(1,0,1,0,1),\ (1,0,1,1,1).

$$
This finite geometry is enough to say that an $M$-density summary and the amino-acid-residual $Q6$ basis are distinct probes. In particular, an $M$-only local optimum cannot directly test contrasts such as $CUN$ versus $UUR$ in leucine, or $AAA$ versus $AAG$ in lysine, because those contrasts live in the residual coordinates rather than in the coarse $M$-density alone.

\paragraph{Readout statistic.}
For a readout $r$ and a context package $Z$, the intended survival comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{Q}(r\mid Z) = J_{0}(Z) - J_{Q}(Z,q),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$. The context $Z$ must include the amino-acid sequence $y$, organism context, and simpler codon-feature baselines; when the comparison is made against an $M$-density baseline, the tested quantity is $S_{Q}(r\mid Z,\Delta_{M})$. Positive survival would mean that a residual coordinate improves the bounded readout after the amino-acid quotient and the baseline controls have already been imposed. The readout family allowed by this record is restricted to translation-layer measurements such as tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance, and only when organism-matched data are supplied.

\paragraph{Current finite result.}
The finite computation establishes that the amino-acid-residual basis is nontrivial on the three-organism codon-count contact. It does not establish that any residual coordinate has positive survival on tAI, ribosome occupancy, or protein abundance. The available contacts are separated into codon-usage summaries, tRNA gene-copy spectra, and possible tAI or stAI weights. These are relevant contacts, but the present row does not contain a paired organism-matched statistic combining codon usage, tRNA copy number, and tAI or stAI with amino-acid composition controlled. Therefore the proper local conclusion is a bounded residual-geometry result: $B^{*}_{Q6}$ has measured finite variation after the amino-acid quotient, while the stronger translation-readout survival claim remains unmade.

\paragraph{Blocked-null boundary.}
The specific null boundary is the following. If $M$-density alone were the complete variable for the DNA-to-protein comparison at this resolution, then adding any $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$ to the controlled baseline would fail to improve every permitted readout in every organism:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{Q}(r\mid Z,\Delta_{M}) \leq 0
\end{aligned}
$$
for each tested residual coordinate $q$ and each readout $r$ in the permitted readout set. The current data do not yet evaluate that inequality, because the matched controlled statistic is absent. Hence this row blocks both directions of interpretation: it is not evidence for $M$-only sufficiency, and it is not evidence for positive $Q6$ residual survival.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome-mediated causal pathway, folding property, physical admissibility, protein function, biochemical mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the nonzero residual variance reported here. Codon-count summaries observe sequence composition; tRNA gene-copy numbers are genome-annotation summaries; tAI or stAI values are modelled translation-efficiency annotations unless independently tied to a controlled organism-matched readout. A positive biological interpretation would require a separate contact that supplies the relevant layer directly. Within this chapter, the only supported statement is that the specified residual coordinates are finite, computable, nontrivial after amino-acid quotienting in the three listed organisms, and suitable for a controlled survival test once matched translation-layer data are supplied.
