\subsection{Cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates leave a cross-ortholog imprint strong enough to be read as an evolutionary-selection signal after the simpler expression and abundance routes have been kept separate.  The finite table used for the registered audit has $80$ single-copy fungal orthogroups, $3$ species, and $240$ joined rows.  The data source is OrthoDB v12 at Fungi level 4751 for single-copy orthogroups, Ensembl Fungi release 63 for coding sequences, and PaxDb v6 integrated ppm for abundance.  The evaluated residual coordinates are the same nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ family used by the surrounding translation and abundance audits: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The biological question is not whether these coordinates can correlate with expression-linked codon bias; it is whether the cross-ortholog surface clears a stricter imprint gate after finite controls and null comparison.

\paragraph{Selection-imprint gate.}
The registered gate returned a completed bounded verdict, but it did not support the strong selection-imprint claim.  The fitted residual improvement was positive in the ordinary in-sample statistic, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -258.6010084301837.
\end{aligned}
$$
This improvement was not stable under the cross-clade check: the leave-clade-out statistic was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{leave\text{-}clade\text{-}out} &= -0.0021477729853302424.
\end{aligned}
$$
The null envelope was also larger than the observed positive increment, with $\max null_{95}(\Delta R^2)=0.05427157066522958$.  The gate value is therefore $positive\_selection\_imprint=false$, and the recorded verdict is $codon\_bias\_expression\_correlation$ rather than selection imprint.  In prose: the finite table contains an expression-associated codon-bias residual, but the current rows do not justify the stronger statement that $B^*_{Q6}$ has been detected as an evolutionary selection imprint across orthologs.

\paragraph{Readback discipline.}
The negative selection-imprint decision is compatible with bounded translation-layer and abundance-layer contacts elsewhere in the finite record.  A modeled-tAI contact was computed for $3$ organisms and found $81$ entries above the $0.01$ bounded-contact threshold, with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$; that entry is explicitly labeled as modeled tAI underpowered for powered survival because $3$ organisms are below the $40$ matched-species requirement.  A separate translation-mediated protein-abundance audit used $12$ organisms and joined, for example, $5815$ Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins, $19053$ Homo sapiens proteins, $19395$ Mus musculus proteins, and $3739$ Escherichia coli K12 MG1655 proteins.  In that audit the mediated fraction $M^{QTP}$ remained a statistical decomposition: it was $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, $0.27206289160119157$ in human, $0.15488799907323988$ in mouse, and $0.612922252793667$ in E. coli.  These numbers show that translation-conditioned abundance surfaces can be finite and nontrivial, but they do not repair the cross-ortholog selection gate in this chapter.

\paragraph{Coordinate-specific caution.}
The structural-order audit identifies $f_3$-stress as the only coordinate passing its fold-level sign-flip gate, with weighted held-out $\Delta R^2=0.0031744502294756994$ and right-tail sign-flip probability $0.03125$.  That fact is useful for naming a candidate residual axis, but it is not evidence that the evolutionary-selection imprint has passed.  The present chapter keeps these layers separate: a coordinate can be useful for a structural-order or abundance residual audit while still failing the cross-ortholog selection-imprint threshold.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is bounded and negative for the strong claim.  The table supports the statement that a codon-bias and expression-correlation surface was observed in a finite cross-ortholog audit, and that the audit ran to completion.  It does not support translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universality.  A direct $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not enough for protein realization unless a same-coordinate $Q_i \to T_j$ translation-readout step is observed or explicitly bounded and then shown to mediate a matched downstream $P_k$ readout under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA-abundance controls.  For this cross-ortholog packet, the finite verdict remains: expression-linked codon-bias correlation is present, while the strong evolutionary-selection imprint is not established.
