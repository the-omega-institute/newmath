\subsection{Cross-Ortholog Selection Imprint for the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residual Surface}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter treats the selection-imprint question as a finite row problem on ortholog groups rather than as a claim about a universal evolutionary law.  The coordinate side is the nine-dimensional B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The empirical table used here contains $80$ single-copy fungal orthogroups across $3$ species, hence $240$ ortholog-species rows.  The declared source is OrthoDB v12 at Fungi level $4751$, coding sequences from Ensembl Fungi release $63$, and integrated PaxDb v6 abundance in ppm units.  The question is whether the B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates leave a cross-ortholog imprint that survives the stated controls strongly enough to be read as positive evidence for evolutionary selection.

\paragraph{Controlled gate.}
Let $Q$ denote the residual-coordinate matrix after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, and the M-density baseline have been kept as controls rather than promoted to coordinates.  Let $Y$ denote the abundance-side readout attached to the ortholog rows.  The reported gate compares the controlled improvement against a null ceiling and a leave-clade-out stability check.  The finite numerical verdict is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{OG} &= 80,\\
 n_{rows} &= 240,\\
 n_{species} &= 3,\\
 \Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
 \Delta DL_{bits} &= -258.6010084301837,\\
 \Delta R^2_{leave\text{-}clade\text{-}out} &= -0.0021477729853302424,\\
 \max \Delta R^2_{null95} &= 0.05427157066522958.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive-selection-imprint gate is therefore false: the observed $\Delta R^2$ is below the reported null-$95$ ceiling, and the leave-clade-out value is slightly negative.  The run completed and returned the bounded verdict \emph{codon-bias expression correlation}, not a strong selection-imprint result.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein-abundance contacts.}
The negative selection-imprint call is not a denial that B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates can touch lower empirical readouts.  A separate modeled-tAI readout computed from GtRNAdb copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights had $81$ entries above the bounded-contact threshold $0.01$ across $3$ organisms, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}$ equal to $3801961.031607896$; however, that run explicitly remained underpowered for a measured survival claim because $3$ organisms are fewer than the declared $40$ matched-species requirement.  Another finite protein-abundance mediation audit joined $12$ organisms and reported positive statistical mediation fractions for modeled tAI conditioning, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, and $0.27206289160119157$ in \emph{Homo sapiens}.  Those quantities are useful bounded readbacks, but they do not by themselves turn the present $80$-orthogroup table into a selection mechanism.

\paragraph{Axis-level caution.}
The coordinate audit also found that structural-order signal, when tested in its own finite design, was carried by the $f3\_stress$ axis: its weighted held-out increment was $0.0031744502294756994$ with sign-flip right-tail probability $0.03125$, while the full nine-axis surface was significant only through that single passing coordinate.  This matters for the present chapter because a cross-ortholog expression correlation could be carried by a narrow coordinate axis, by lineage-specific abundance bias, by identifier reconciliation, or by a proteomics-integration artifact.  The selection-imprint gate therefore requires stability beyond the null ceiling and across clade removal.  The finite table did not supply that stability.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The admissible conclusion is narrow.  The rows support a completed negative audit for the strong evolutionary-selection imprint claim and a weaker positive description as codon-bias expression correlation.  In symbols, the present finite surface permits
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Observed}(Q,Y,Z) &:\quad \Delta R^2 = 0.03250515015613468,\\
\mathrm{Null95}(Q,Y,Z) &:\quad 0.05427157066522958,\\
\mathrm{SelectionImprint}(Q,Y,Z) &:\quad \mathrm{false},\\
\mathrm{AllowedReadback}(Q,Y,Z) &:\quad \mathrm{codon\text{-}bias\ expression\ correlation}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The phrase ``passed'' in the run status means that the registered offline computation reached a bounded verdict; it does not mean that the biological claim was supported.  The explicit claim-supported field is false.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
No translation realization, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, mechanism claim, organismal-phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from this chapter.  PaxDb ppm values are integrated across studies and species, not matched-condition absolute per-cell abundance measurements.  Failed or missing cross-reference reconciliation can remove proteins non-randomly.  A positive residual association, even when present, is at most an imprint criterion unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant mechanistic or perturbational rows.  In this finite audit the stronger gate fails, so B$^{\ast}_{Q6}$ remains here at the level of controlled codon-bias expression correlation rather than evolutionary selection imprint.
