\subsection{Cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-evolutionary-selection-imprint-b-star-q6-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The audit tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates carry an evolutionary selection imprint after the amino-acid quotient, rather than merely tracking a codon-bias correlation with abundance.  The finite table is built from OrthoDB v12 Fungi level 4751 single-copy orthogroups, Ensembl Fungi release-63 coding sequences, and PaxDb v6 integrated protein-abundance ppm.  The realized join contains $80$ orthogroups, $3$ species, and $240$ ortholog rows.  The row object is therefore not a population-level evolutionary law; it is a finite ortholog-abundance panel on which the proposed imprint criterion can be evaluated.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{orthogroups}} &= 80,\\
n_{\mathrm{species}} &= 3,\\
n_{\mathrm{rows}} &= 240.
\end{aligned}
$$

Each row carries a coding-sequence residual readout in the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate family, an orthogroup/species placement, and an abundance value.  The evaluated residual basis is the same nine-coordinate family used in the adjacent translation-readout and protein-abundance computations: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  In the separate modeled translation-readout computation, these coordinates had nonzero bounded tAI coupling over $3$ organisms with $81$ entries above the $0.01$ threshold and maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  That neighboring fact justifies treating the coordinate family as a translation-adjacent residual surface, but it does not by itself turn the present ortholog table into a measured selection mechanism.

\paragraph{Imprint criterion.}
The positive imprint gate compares the fitted residual improvement against a null threshold and against clade-leaving behavior.  The finite result is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
DL_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -258.6010084301837,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{leave\mbox{-}clade\mbox{-}out}} &= -0.0021477729853302424,\\
\max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null95}} &= 0.05427157066522958.
\end{aligned}
$$

The observed in-sample improvement is positive, but it remains below the null $95\%$ envelope and reverses under the leave-clade-out diagnostic.  The registered decision is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.03250515015613468 &< 0.05427157066522958,\\
-0.0021477729853302424 &< 0,\\
\mathrm{positive\_selection\_imprint} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The finite verdict is consequently not a strong evolutionary selection-imprint result.  The honest readback is a codon-bias--expression correlation on the joined ortholog panel.  This is still informative: the residual surface can contact abundance-correlated biology in a finite cross-species table, but the stronger interpretation requires a powered multi-organism design in which the residual survives the null envelope and remains positive when clade structure is withheld.

\paragraph{Relation to mediated protein-abundance evidence.}
The selection-imprint audit is narrower than the separate $Q \to T \to P$ mediation computation.  In that larger protein-abundance mediation panel, the same $9$ residual coordinates were joined to modeled per-protein tAI and PaxDb abundance across $12$ organisms, with organism-specific joined counts ranging from $1656$ proteins in $Sulfolobus\ solfataricus$ to $19395$ proteins in $Mus\ musculus$.  Several mediated fractions in that computation were positive and inside the unit interval, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, $0.612922252793667$ in $Escherichia\ coli\ K12\ MG1655$, and $0.27206289160119157$ in $Homo\ sapiens$.  Those values say that a modeled translation readout can statistically absorb part of a residual-to-abundance association in a cross-sectional abundance panel.  They do not rescue the present selection-imprint gate, because the present audit asks a different question: whether the cross-ortholog pattern is strong enough to be called an evolutionary selection imprint after the declared null and clade tests.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded negative for the strong imprint claim and a bounded positive only for the weaker codon-bias--expression association.  PaxDb ppm are integrated across studies and species rather than matched-condition absolute per-cell protein counts.  Identifier reconciliation can remove proteins non-randomly, and a positive statistical residual would in any case be an imprint criterion rather than direct evidence for the molecular selection mechanism.  With only $80$ orthogroups and $3$ species, the panel is underpowered for a strong evolutionary selection-imprint claim.

No translation mechanism follows from this finite result unless an organism- and condition-matched translation contact supplies the corresponding readout.  No protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from the residual association.  The admissible conclusion is exactly the bounded one: the registered offline experiment ran to completion, the strong selection-imprint gate failed, and the finite table supports only the weaker statement that the joined ortholog rows exhibit a codon-bias--expression correlation under the tested design.
