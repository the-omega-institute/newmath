\subsection{Cross-Ortholog Selection-Imprint Audit for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residuals}
\label{sec:biorereality-q6-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates leave a cross-ortholog selection imprint after the usual sequence and abundance baselines are held fixed.  The finite surface consists of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$, with $q_i$ ranging over the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress, with $t_j$ a bounded translation readout and $p_k$ a matched protein-abundance readout.  For this audit the measured row set is the offline cross-ortholog panel built from OrthoDB v12 Fungi level 4751 single-copy orthogroups, Ensembl Fungi release-63 coding sequences, and PaxDb v6 integrated protein abundance in ppm units.  The realized table has $n_{OG}=80$ orthogroups, $n_{species}=3$, and $n_{rows}=240$ rows.

\paragraph{Selection-imprint statistic.}
The registered test asks whether a residual block improves a protein-abundance or expression-linked ortholog model strongly enough to pass a positive selection-imprint gate.  In the completed run, the incremental fit was positive in-sample but did not survive the stronger cross-ortholog criteria.  The measured values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
\Delta DL_{bits} &= -258.6010084301837,\\
\Delta R^2_{leave\text{-}clade\text{-}out} &= -0.0021477729853302424,\\
\max \Delta R^2_{null95} &= 0.05427157066522958.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row contact supports the weaker verdict reported by the run, namely codon-bias/expression correlation, but it does not support the stronger evolutionary selection-imprint claim.  The Boolean gate was evaluated as $positive\_selection\_imprint=false$, and the claim support flag was $claim\_supported=false$.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and protein mediation.}
The negative selection-imprint verdict is not a rejection of all lower-level $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ contacts.  A separate modeled-tAI survival computation found a bounded nonzero coupling surface for three organisms, with $81$ entries above threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$, while explicitly marking $3$ organisms as insufficient for a powered survival claim.  A broader per-protein tAI/protein-abundance mediation computation used twelve organism panels and the same nine residual coordinates.  In that mediation surface, the yeast row had $n=5815$ joined proteins, total $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$, and mediated fraction $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$; the human row had $n=19053$, total $R^2_{QP}=0.04454565118019448$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.03242643251185274$, and mediated fraction $0.27206289160119157$.  These values show that translation-conditioned protein-abundance decompositions can be numerically nontrivial, but they do not by themselves prove cross-ortholog selection.

\paragraph{Structural and yeast mediation context.}
Other finite contacts further constrain the interpretation.  In the structural-order axis audit, exactly one coordinate passed the fold-level sign-flip gate: $f_3$-stress, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip right-tail probability $0.03125$.  In the yeast abundance-residual decomposition with five joined data streams, $4759$ genes were joined, $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction was $0.003284564452662019$, the structure-incremental fraction was $0.0005507778820193332$, the permutation probability for $S_Q$ structure association was $0.027972027972027972$, and the permutation probability for the incremental structure component was $0.1258741258741259$.  These contacts are useful finite comparisons: they show that abundance and structure-adjacent residual decompositions can be computed, while also showing that the cross-ortholog selection-imprint gate is stricter than merely observing a residual association.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter verdict is therefore bounded and negative for the named strong claim.  The finite rows establish a completed audit with a codon-bias/expression-correlation readback, not an evolutionary selection-imprint realization.  The main reason is that the positive in-sample increment, $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, is below the finite null reference maximum $0.05427157066522958$ and reverses under leave-clade-out evaluation.  The data source also carries explicit limitations: PaxDb ppm values are integrated across studies and species rather than matched-condition absolute per-cell measurements; cross-reference loss can be non-random; a positive residual would at most be an imprint criterion rather than direct evidence for the molecular selection process; and $80$ orthogroups is underpowered for a strong population-level conclusion.

\paragraph{No promotion above the tested layer.}
No translation realization, folded-structure statement, physical admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, mechanistic selection statement, phenotype statement, or universal biological law follows from this audit alone.  A direct $q_i\to p_k$ association is not treated as protein realization unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled $q_i\to t_j$ translation-readout step, and even a surviving translation step would require a separate layer-matched contact before any folding, function, perturbation, or organismal claim could be made.  The controls that remain controls are amino-acid sequence and composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, the $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance.  Under those rules, the present finite surface closes as an honest negative for strong cross-ortholog selection imprint and as a bounded positive only for codon-bias/expression correlation.
