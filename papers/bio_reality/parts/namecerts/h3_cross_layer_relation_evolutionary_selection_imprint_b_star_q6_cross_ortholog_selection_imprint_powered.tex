\subsection{Cross-Ortholog Selection-Imprint Boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}
\label{subsec:biorality-q6-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object considered here is a finite mediation-and-selection surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$, with $q_i$ drawn from the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, $t_j$ drawn from bounded translation readouts, and $p_k$ drawn from matched protein-abundance measurements.  The admissible coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \in \{&K\_AAA,\ Arg\_AGR,\ Ile\_AUA,\ Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\ Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
&Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\ Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\ Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\ f3\_stress\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A direct residual-to-protein association is not accepted as a protein realization unless the same residual first passes through an explicit translation readout.  The translation slot may be a modeled codon-adaptation weight, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency; the protein slot is a matched abundance readout.  Amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline remain controls rather than promoted coordinates, and matched mRNA abundance is used to separate translation-layer signal from transcription-only signal.

\paragraph{Selection-imprint test.}
The registered cross-ortholog experiment used OrthoDB v12 Fungi level 4751 single-copy orthogroups, Ensembl Fungi release-63 coding sequences, and PaxDb v6 integrated ppm abundance.  The finite matrix contained $80$ orthogroups across $3$ species, hence $240$ rows.  The evaluated gate asked whether residual information carried a positive evolutionary selection imprint after the relevant baselines, with the explicit understanding that a statistical residual is not itself a molecular mechanism.

The observed finite-row score was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
\Delta L_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -258.6010084301837,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{leave\ clade\ out}} &= -0.0021477729853302424,\\
\max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null},95} &= 0.05427157066522958.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive gate was therefore not met: the observed $\Delta R^2$ remained below the $95\%$ null benchmark, and the leave-clade-out increment was slightly negative.  The honest readback is a codon-bias/expression correlation on the sampled ortholog surface, not a powered selection-imprint claim.  In the experiment ledger this is recorded as a completed bounded run with `claim\_supported=false' and verdict `codon\_bias\_expression\_correlation'.

\paragraph{Relation to the translation layer.}
The negative selection-imprint verdict is compatible with the separate translation-readout contacts.  A modeled tAI survival calculation over $3$ organisms computed $81$ entries above the threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute score $3801961.031607896$, but the same record states that $3$ organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that at least $40$ matched species would be needed for the stronger powered claim.  Thus the modeled tAI surface can serve as a bounded translation-contact calculation, but it cannot be read as observed ribosome dynamics, protein output, or a population-level selection law.

A broader protein-abundance mediation calculation used the same nine residual coordinates and $12$ organism-specific joined protein panels.  It reported positive finite mediation fractions such as $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.4872268357227724$ in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, and $0.27206289160119157$ in human.  These values are useful as controlled protein-abundance decompositions, but they do not rescue the cross-ortholog selection-imprint gate, because the latter requires cross-clade robustness and null separation on the ortholog panel.  Likewise, a structural-order audit found that $f3\_stress$ was the only significant axis, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip $p=0.03125$, but that is a structural-order identity result rather than an evolutionary selection-imprint result.

\paragraph{Bounded conclusion.}
This chapter therefore records a negative boundary result for the strong claim.  The finite surface supports the weaker statement that codon-bias residuals correlate with expression or abundance-like summaries in the sampled fungal ortholog panel.  It does not support the stronger statement that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals carry a powered cross-ortholog evolutionary selection imprint.  The limiting facts are concrete: only $80$ orthogroups and $240$ rows were available; PaxDb ppm are integrated across studies and species rather than matched-condition absolute per-cell measurements; cross-reference reconciliation can remove proteins non-randomly; and the leave-clade-out increment did not survive.

No translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from this result.  A future positive claim at any such layer would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  In particular, a $Q_i \to P_k$ association is not evidence for protein realization unless a same-row $Q_i \to T_j$ translation step survives under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA controls; and a cross-ortholog selection-imprint claim is not licensed unless the residual improvement exceeds the relevant null benchmark and remains positive under clade-held-out evaluation.
