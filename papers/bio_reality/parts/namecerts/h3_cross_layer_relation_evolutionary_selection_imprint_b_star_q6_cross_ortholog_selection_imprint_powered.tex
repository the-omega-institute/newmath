\subsection{Cross-Ortholog Selection-Imprint Boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint-bstar-q6}

\paragraph{Finite question.}
This record asks whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, already defined at the coding-sequence coordinate level, leave a cross-ortholog imprint strong enough to be read as an evolutionary selection signal rather than as a bounded codon-bias and expression association. The finite surface consists of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ only after the first coordinate has passed through a translation-facing readout. In this audit the evolutionary panel is smaller and coarser than the translation-mediated protein-abundance panel: it contains $80$ single-copy fungal orthogroups, $3$ species, and $240$ orthogroup--species rows. The data source is OrthoDB v12 at Fungi level $4751$ for single-copy orthogroups, Ensembl Fungi release $63$ for CDS rows, and PaxDb v6 integrated ppm for protein abundance.

\paragraph{Admissible readback.}
The readback is not a direct claim that a synonymously defined coordinate is under selection. It is a controlled finite comparison in which the candidate imprint must improve a model beyond simpler codon-bias and expression structure and must survive a clade-withholding stress test. The evaluated positive-imprint gate reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{OG}} &= 80,\\
 n_{\mathrm{species}} &= 3,\\
 n_{\mathrm{rows}} &= 240,\\
 \Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
 \Delta L_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -258.6010084301837,\\
 \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{leave\mbox{-}clade\mbox{-}out}} &= -0.0021477729853302424,\\
 \max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null},95} &= 0.05427157066522958.
\end{aligned}
$$
The within-panel fit is therefore positive in ordinary residual variance terms, but it does not exceed the registered null envelope and it reverses under the leave-clade-out diagnostic. The finite verdict is consequently not a positive evolutionary selection-imprint result. The registered conclusion is the weaker statement that the data show a codon-bias/expression correlation on this panel.

\paragraph{Negative gate.}
The decisive inequality is the comparison between the observed improvement and the null threshold:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{observed}} &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
 \max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null},95} &= 0.05427157066522958,\\
 \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{observed}} - \max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null},95} &= -0.02176642050809490.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row statistic is below the required positive-imprint threshold. The leave-clade-out value, $-0.0021477729853302424$, also rules out treating the signal as a stable cross-clade improvement. The experiment status is reported as passed only in the procedural sense: the registered offline computation ran to completion and returned a bounded verdict. Its biological support flag is false for the strong selection-imprint claim.

\paragraph{Relation to translation contacts.}
The negative selection-imprint result should not be confused with the separate translation-layer contacts. A modeled tAI contact exists for $3$ organisms, with $81$ modeled coupling entries above threshold $0.01$ and maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$ under dos Reis wobble weights. That contact was explicitly marked as underpowered for a powered survival claim because $3$ organisms are insufficient where at least $40$ matched species would be needed. A broader protein-abundance mediation computation also exists on $12$ organism panels, with examples such as $5815$ joined yeast proteins, $19053$ joined human proteins, and $3739$ joined Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 proteins. In that separate matrix, the yeast statistical mediation fraction was $0.9491551041216973$, the human fraction was $0.27206289160119157$, and the Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 fraction was $0.612922252793667$. These numbers show that bounded translation-facing and protein-abundance decompositions can be computed on larger matched panels, but they do not repair the cross-ortholog evolutionary gate above, whose own finite panel remains $80$ orthogroups and $240$ rows.

\paragraph{Execution context.}
The surrounding BioReality register also contains an observable execution result in Saccharomyces cerevisiae with $3289$ genes, where at least one observed rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes. The earlier confirmatory genetic-code table contact used $40$ independent trials with $40$ successes in each of two exact one-sided binomial checks, giving $p=9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$ in both cases, while $20$ negative controls and $20$ critical ablation controls had $0$ unexpected successes. Those records justify that finite biological contacts can be attached and tested without promoting the residual geometry itself into a higher biological layer. They do not change the present cross-ortholog conclusion: the strong selection-imprint claim is not supported on this finite surface.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The allowed conclusion is narrow. The panel supports a bounded codon-bias/expression correlation, not a direct proof that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals are selected molecular targets. PaxDb integrated ppm values aggregate across studies and species, so they are not matched-condition absolute per-cell protein measurements. Failed or missing cross-reference reconciliation can remove proteins non-randomly. Most importantly, a positive residual association, even when present, is only an imprint criterion unless the registered selection gate is passed. Here the gate is not passed.

\paragraph{Layer boundary.}
No translation-level realization, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, mechanistic selection claim, organismal-phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from this result. Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. In particular, a direct $q_i\to p_k$ association cannot be used as evidence of protein realization unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled $q_i\to t_j$ translation-readout step, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, M-density baseline, and matched mRNA abundance kept in the control set. For this chapter the finite conclusion remains: the registered computation completed, but the powered cross-ortholog evolutionary selection-imprint claim is false on the available rows.
