\paragraph{Gate statistic.}
The positive selection-imprint gate was evaluated and failed.  The fitted increment was $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, while the empirical null boundary reached $0.05427157066522958$ at the reported $95\%$ threshold.  The leave-clade-out increment was negative, $\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{leave\ clade\ out}}=-0.0021477729853302424$, and the description-length comparison was $-258.6010084301837$ bits.  In the row ledger, the run status is \emph{passed}: this means the registered offline computation completed and returned a bounded verdict, not that the strong biological claim was supported.  The verdict is \emph{codon-bias expression correlation}; the field \emph{claim\_supported} is false, and the evaluated flag \emph{positive\_selection\_imprint} is false.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and abundance surfaces.}
The negative imprint call is consistent with nearby finite readouts.  A modeled-tAI surface over $3$ organisms computed $81$ entries above the coupling threshold $0.01$ and a maximum absolute score $3801961.031607896$, but that run explicitly remained underpowered for a powered survival claim because $3$ organisms are not enough for the declared $\geq 40$ species condition.  A separate translation-mediated protein-abundance decomposition used $12$ organisms and found positive statistical mediation fractions, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.6165167748739756$ in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, and $0.01850668564589326$ in \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  Those finite quantities are mediation decompositions on matched codon and abundance rows; they are not an evolutionary selection-imprint proof.  In the structural-order audit, $f_3$-stress was the only coordinate passing the fold-level sign-flip gate, with weighted held-out increment $0.0031744502294756994$ and sign-flip probability $0.03125$.  That axis identity gives a useful residual direction but does not rescue the cross-ortholog imprint gate.

\paragraph{Negative controls from neighboring layers.}
Other finite rows also keep the selection reading bounded.  In the dynamic mass-balance audit, the baseline $M$-only fit had $R^2=0.9960628201518571$, the $M+E_{\mathrm{stat}}$ fit had $R^2=0.996410628851051$, and the later layer comparison decreased to $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$ with $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-668.8046070381928$ bits across $120$ genes and $718$ intervals.  In the turnover audit, adding the residual layer decreased the fit from $R^2=0.055740851626933985$ to $R^2=0.050278872193227464$, giving $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$ and $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-13.68819707902732$ bits; the matched-bin and recoded null probabilities were $0.8855721393034826$ and $1.0$.  These numbers do not test the same claim, but they support the local discipline that a residual association is not upgraded unless the declared finite gate is actually passed.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The chapter conclusion is a bounded negative for the strong evolutionary selection-imprint claim.  The finite data support the weaker statement that the audited $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface carries a codon-bias expression correlation in this $80$-orthogroup, $3$-species fungal table.  They do not support a positive cross-ortholog selection imprint.  PaxDb integrated ppm are study-aggregated and species-aggregated abundance estimates rather than matched-condition absolute per-cell measurements; failed or missing cross-reference reconciliation can remove proteins non-randomly; and $n_{\mathrm{OG}}=80$ is underpowered for a strong evolutionary selection-imprint call.  Consequently, a direct residual-to-protein association is not admissible as evidence for protein realization unless the same residual first survives an appropriate translation-readout step and the downstream abundance relation remains after the declared controls.  No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal selection mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row.  Each such claim requires its own layer-matched contact and a separate finite statistic.
