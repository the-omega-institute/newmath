\subsection{Cross-ortholog selection-imprint audit for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-cross-ortholog-selection-imprint}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous-residual coordinates leave a cross-ortholog selection imprint after the translation and protein-abundance boundaries are kept distinct.  The finite table has $80$ fungal single-copy orthogroups, $3$ species, and $240$ joined rows.  The source surface is OrthoDB v12 at Fungi level $4751$ for single-copy orthogroups, Ensembl Fungi release $63$ for coding sequences, and PaxDb v6 integrated ppm for protein abundance.  The tested object is not a free residual-to-protein assertion: a residual coordinate may be read downstream only through a controlled finite matrix in which $q_i$ is one of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, $t_j$ is a translation readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout.  The audit therefore asks whether the observed cross-ortholog pattern exceeds simpler codon-bias and expression explanations strongly enough to count as a positive selection-imprint call.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout discipline.}
The admissible $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate list is the nine-coordinate residual basis used throughout the finite rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ f_3\text{-}\mathrm{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The coordinate family is kept separate from amino-acid sequence, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline.  Those quantities remain controls or baselines, not promoted coordinates.  The upstream modeled-translation contact supplies a bounded translation-layer check rather than an observed ribosome experiment: in the finite tAI surface, $3$ organisms were available, the codon-count space had $61$ sense codons, the coupling threshold was $0.01$, and $81$ modeled entries exceeded threshold, with maximum absolute $S_{ij}=3801961.031607896$.  The same contact explicitly records that $3$ organisms are insufficient for a powered survival claim requiring at least $40$ matched species.  Thus the present cross-ortholog run must be read as a finite statistical imprint audit, not as a completed molecular mechanism test.

\paragraph{Selection-imprint statistic.}
The registered finite run \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_cross\_ortholog\_selection\_imprint\_powered} completed with status \texttt{passed}, where passage means that the offline computation ran to a bounded verdict.  The positive selection-imprint gate reported
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.03250515015613468,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= -258.6010084301837,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{leave\ clade\ out}} &= -0.0021477729853302424,\\
\max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null95}} &= 0.05427157066522958.
\end{aligned}
$$
The observed in-sample gain is positive, but it does not clear the finite null boundary: $0.03250515015613468 < 0.05427157066522958$.  The leave-clade-out value is slightly negative, so the apparent fit does not persist as a robust cross-clade imprint.  The verdict field is \texttt{codon\_bias\_expression\_correlation}, and the gate value \texttt{positive\_selection\_imprint} is \texttt{false}.  The correct conclusion is therefore an honest negative for the strong evolutionary selection-imprint claim, with a weaker codon-bias/expression correlation left as the bounded observation.

\paragraph{Relation to protein-abundance mediation.}
A separate finite mediation run over $12$ organisms shows that $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals can participate in controlled statistical protein-abundance decompositions when modeled per-protein tAI is used as the translation readout.  In that matrix, the per-organism total $R^2_{QP}$ values included $0.29837624994140827$ in \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.10767526677985896$ in \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.10217906758013379$ in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $0.0770952887136557$ in \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, $0.06397514251295629$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, and $0.04454565118019448$ in human.  The mediated fractions ranged from $0.01850668564589326$ in \textit{Sulfolobus solfataricus} to $0.9491551041216973$ in yeast, with every recorded fraction inside the unit interval.  Those rows are useful as a finite mediation context, but they do not rescue the cross-ortholog selection-imprint call.  The present audit evaluates whether a stronger evolutionary imprint survives orthogroup, species, and null-boundary stress; on that question the finite verdict remains negative.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The data used here do not establish translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, or universality.  PaxDb ppm are integrated across studies and species rather than matched-condition absolute per-cell measurements; failed or missing cross-reference reconciliation can remove proteins non-randomly; and a positive residual association, even when present, is only an imprint criterion until a layer-matched experiment supplies the relevant contact.  The $n_{\mathrm{OG}}=80$ sample is also underpowered for a strong evolutionary selection-imprint assertion.  A direct $q_i\to p_k$ association is not admissible as protein realization unless a same-coordinate $q_i\to t_j$ translation step is tested and survives under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA-abundance controls.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The finite rows support a bounded codon-bias/expression correlation and reject the stronger selection-imprint promotion.  In compact form, the evaluated gate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&n_{\mathrm{species}}=3,\quad n_{\mathrm{orthogroups}}=80,\quad n_{\mathrm{rows}}=240,\\
&\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468,\quad \max \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{null95}}=0.05427157066522958,\\
&\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{leave\ clade\ out}}=-0.0021477729853302424,\quad \mathrm{positive\ selection\ imprint}=\mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Accordingly, $B^*_{Q6}$ remains at the controlled residual, modeled-translation, and finite protein-abundance mediation boundary for this claim.  It does not enter a strong evolutionary selection-imprint layer on the present cross-ortholog evidence.
