\subsection{N-end Degron Turnover Boundary in the Mathieson Human Half-Life Panel}\label{sec:bioreality-nend-degron-turnover-mathieson}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This record tests whether an N-end degron boundary, read against the Mathieson human primary-cell protein half-life panel, gives a stable turnover-side explanation for a protein-abundance residual.  The row is intentionally narrow: it is a finite joined-data test over $n=3898$ proteins, with seed $20260621$, $1000$ permutations per model, and $12000$ actual permutation evaluations across the model family.  The run time was $232.525$ seconds, and the cached data table is the Mathieson-derived file \texttt{tools/bio\_reality/data/nend\_turnover\_mathieson.json}.  The negative-control field is reported as $\mathrm{negctrl\_null}=\mathrm{false}$, so the row is not a clean null-control confirmation.  Its central finite scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{consistent\_sign\_frac} &= 0.5,\\
  n_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 3898,\\
  N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 12000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The value $0.5$ is exactly balanced at the sign-summary level.  It is therefore not evidence that the degron-side direction is consistently aligned with turnover across the fitted variants.

\paragraph{Relation to the human turnover panel.}
The broader human turnover contact used Mathieson et al. primary-cell half-lives, summarized by the median half-life across cell types, with a mapping from gene name through reviewed human UniProt primary gene annotation to STRING protein identifiers.  That contact contained $8164$ valid turnover proteins and joined $8057$ proteins to the abundance and coding-sequence ledger.  In the B$^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual ranking, human turnover had
$$
\begin{aligned}
  A_H &= 0.0005945344095654461,\\
  C_H &= 0.6818556702481351,\\
  A_H C_H &= 0.0004053866583198265,\\
  \mathrm{Score} &= -0.009594613341680174.
\end{aligned}
$$
It did not exceed its degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni boundary: the turnover threshold was $0.0013532753575986216$ and the null $95\%$ value was $0.0005731841659980483$ on the $8057$-protein join.  In the direct human-turnover absorption computation, the absorbed fraction was only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, with null mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and null $95\%$ value $0.0004342285066808464$.  These numbers put the N-end degron test in the same local neighborhood: turnover is present as an externally measured readout, but it is not a strong finite absorber of the residual under the stated projection tests.

\paragraph{Mathematical readback.}
Let $R$ denote the joined finite protein ledger, let $D$ denote the N-end degron-side boundary statistic, and let $T$ denote the Mathieson turnover readout after identifier matching.  The admissible assertion is only that the row computes a finite sign-stability summary from $R$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R &= \{\text{3898 joined proteins with degron and turnover fields}\},\\
  M &= \{\text{the fitted model variants replayed under deterministic permutations}\},\\
  \rho &= \frac{\#\{m\in M:\text{observed sign agrees with the tested orientation}\}}{\#M}=0.5.
\end{aligned}
$$
The associated permutation budget is $1000$ per model and $12000$ total evaluations.  Since $\rho=0.5$, the finite sign ledger is balanced rather than direction-selective.  The row can therefore be read as a boundary check: the N-end degron statistic is fetchable and computable in the Mathieson turnover setting, but it does not provide a stable directional compression of the turnover relation.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The computation is a descriptive projection-and-permutation readback.  It does not show that codon choice determines turnover, that N-end degron status realizes a translation process, that half-life explains protein folding, or that a degradation rule is universal.  It also does not promote the Mathieson half-life panel to a causal mediator of the B$^*_{Q6}$ residual.  No statement about folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, or cross-organism universality follows from this row unless a separate matched contact supplies that layer and a separate statistic is computed on it.  The local conclusion is more modest and more useful: in this finite human panel, the N-end degron boundary is testable, but the sign evidence is balanced and the surrounding human turnover absorption numbers remain below the declared turnover null boundary.
