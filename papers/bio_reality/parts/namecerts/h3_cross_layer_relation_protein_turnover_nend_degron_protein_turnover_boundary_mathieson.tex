\paragraph{Finite row.}
This record tests whether an N-end-degron-coded protein class boundary has a stable contact with measured human protein turnover in the Mathieson primary-cell half-life panel.  The row is deliberately narrow: it is a finite cross-layer table entry, not a biochemical pathway assertion.  The experiment joined $3898$ proteins to the N-end/degron turnover ledger and used seed $20260621$.  The reported run performed $12000$ total permutation evaluations, organized as $1000$ permutations per model, with runtime $232.525$ seconds.  The finite diagnostic reports
$$
\begin{aligned}
  n_{\mathrm{joined}} &= 3898,\\
  N_{\mathrm{perm,total}} &= 12000,\\
  N_{\mathrm{perm/model}} &= 1000,\\
  f_{\mathrm{consistent\ sign}} &= 0.5,\\
  \mathrm{negctrl\_null} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row is nonempty and replayed against a sizeable measured-turnover join, but its directional stability is only half of the checked model family.  The negative-control flag is not a license to infer mechanism; it only says that the specific negative-control null recorded by the run did not absorb the observed finite diagnostic.

This makes the N-end-degron row best read as a boundary probe around turnover, rather than as evidence that turnover explains the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation abundance residual.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite result supports only the following mathematical statement.  A human Mathieson-turnover table can be joined to an N-end-degron boundary ledger on $3898$ proteins, and the replayed permutation protocol records a nonempty, negative-control-checked finite diagnostic with $12000$ total permutation evaluations.  It does not establish that N-end degrons explain the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual, and it does not create a shared turnover channel across organisms.  In the surrounding human H-ranking table, turnover is directionally positive but weak: $C_H\approx 0.682$ while $A_H C_H\approx 4.05\times 10^{-4}$, below the stated score penalty and below the Bonferroni gate.  Consequently the row is a boundary contact for measured human turnover, not a mechanism discovery.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
No translation claim follows from this row: the readout is a protein half-life measurement, not ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.  No folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, causal degradation pathway, N-end-rule mechanism, or universality follows from the finite statistics.  The Mathieson contact supplies measured half-life rows, and the N-end-degron ledger supplies a finite class boundary; the only warranted readback is the joined-row diagnostic, the permutation scale, the sign-stability fraction, and the comparison with the weak human turnover absorption entry.  Any stronger claim would require a separate perturbational or layer-matched contact that directly measures the proposed biochemical step.
