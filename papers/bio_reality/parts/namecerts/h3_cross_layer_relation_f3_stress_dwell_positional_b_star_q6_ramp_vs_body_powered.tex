\subsection{Position-resolved dwell localization of the $f_3$-stress abundance component}\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-positional-ramp-body}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
This record tests whether the $f_3$-stress part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance residual can be localized to a position-resolved ribosome-occupancy window rather than merely detected as an undifferentiated dwell association. The finite carrier is the active \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} join with $n=1257$ genes, drawn from a base design size of $1800$ rows. The response is not total protein abundance itself: it is the one-dimensional component $P_{f3}$ obtained by projecting $Z$-residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance onto the $Z$-residualized $f_3$-stress coordinate on the active base-plus-positional-dwell join. In this fit the $f_3$ rank is $1$, the control basis rank is $24$, the squared norm of the target component is $44.20095750389323$, and the $f_3$ component accounts for $0.1999063916718794$ of the residual protein-abundance target under the recorded projection.

\paragraph{Controls and readout separation.}
The positional readout is a pair of per-gene relative ribosome-occupancy summaries computed separately from file-provided $5$-prime or head windows and $3$-prime or tail windows. The rows are tested with the base design and with segment-specific dwell increments under the same fold assignment. The reported run used $5$ folds and $120$ deterministic row-label permutations of $P_{f3}$, with the base and full designs held fixed separately for each segment and for the joint segment design. Thus the row labels, not the covariate geometry, supply the null comparison. This keeps the test at the level of a finite predictive localization statistic: it asks whether adding a positional dwell segment improves held-out prediction of the $f_3$-projected abundance component beyond the fixed base design.

\paragraph{Observed positional decision.}
The positional experiment passed. Its leading segment by held-out increment was the $5$-prime segment, and the recorded dominant segment is also $5$-prime. The five-prime ramp gate is positive, while the three-prime body gate is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{join}} &= 1257,\\
 n_{\mathrm{base}} &= 1800,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(f_3) &= 1,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 24,\\
 \|P_{f3}\|^2 &= 44.20095750389323,\\
 \operatorname{frac}(P_{f3}) &= 0.1999063916718794,\\
 N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 120.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-row readback is therefore not merely that a dwell statistic exists. It is that, on this yeast join and under this fixed null model, at least one positional window has positive held-out dwell increment above its permutation boundary, and the window carrying the decision is the $5$-prime ramp rather than the $3$-prime body segment.

\paragraph{Relation to the non-positional dwell result.}
The position-resolved call is consistent with the companion dwell-mediation run on the same organism and target family. In that run the joined row count was also $1257$, the base size was $1800$, the fold count was $5$, the $f_3$ rank was $1$, and the same $P_{f3}$ norm and target fraction were recorded. The undifferentiated dwell model had base held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, full held-out $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, and held-out increment $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$. Its dwell-alone $R^2$ was $0.034336580953121976$, the correlation between $f_3$ and dwell was $0.1615441450901653$, and the approximate $p$-value for that correlation was $7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. The row-label permutation null for the non-positional dwell increment had mean $-0.0032911642620130563$, maximum $0.005001350039772926$, and $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$. The positional chapter therefore refines a positive dwell association by asking where the useful dwell increment sits; it does not introduce a new abundance target.

\paragraph{Ramp versus body comparison.}
A separate structural-order ramp/body probe gives useful scale for the same ramp-body contrast without changing the meaning of the dwell-position decision. In that probe the primary ramp length was $50$ codons, the status was passed, and the module-level conclusion was \texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}. At the primary window size the weighted $f_3$ ramp increment was $0.0011165640103326878$, the weighted $f_3$ body increment was $-0.00026157313923957926$, and the weighted ramp-minus-body increment was $0.001378137149572267$. The sign-flip probability for positive ramp signal was $0.0478515625$, while the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability was $0.05859375$. The same run records positive ramp signal but not a stricter localization gate. Read together with the present dwell-position result, the finite evidence supports a $5$-prime-leading dwell readback for the $f_3$ abundance component, not a broad claim that ramp position alone determines structural order.

\paragraph{Dictionary and irreducibility context.}
The broader yeast reduction family also shows that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual is partly, but not completely, reducible to measured readouts. The partial-mediation certificate used $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and a join threshold $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$. Its selection-aware null $95$th percentile was $0.029149007242956115$, while the selected coverage was $0.24538211439730545$, leaving an unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$. The corresponding irreducibility certificate used the same base size, fold count, permutation count, $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$ on a unified yeast context join. These finite rows matter because the positional dwell call is not being used as a total explanation of the residual. It identifies one localized readout contribution to the $f_3$-projected abundance component inside a larger residual system that remains only partially compressed by the listed measured readout dictionary.

\paragraph{Measured translation and protein-abundance boundary.}
The chapter is downstream of measured translation-efficiency contacts but does not convert them into a causal mechanism. In the measured TE survival run, yeast had $5128$ genes with TE, a ribosome-TE join hit rate of $0.8818572656921754$, residual degrees of freedom after controls $5105$, $\operatorname{rank}(X_{\sim})=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and measured $R^2_{QT}=0.13681931343730436$ with all $9$ $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates surviving the positive threshold. In the measured TE to abundance decomposition, yeast had a three-way join of $5128$ rows, $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, measured $S_{QT}=0.13681931343730436$, and measured mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$. These facts show that a measured translation readout exists for the same yeast context, but the present position-resolved dwell result has a narrower statement: the $f_3$ abundance component has a detectable dwell increment localized to the $5$-prime segment under the recorded null.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The scientific content of the finite row is a positional discriminator. A positive $5$-prime ramp call and a negative $3$-prime body call say that the useful dwell increment for the $f_3$-projected abundance component is concentrated at the head-side window in this yeast matrix. This is compatible with a translation-layer readout because the dwell covariates are derived from ribosome-occupancy summaries, and it is compatible with the measured TE evidence because yeast has a nonzero measured $Q\to T$ survival statistic. It is not, by itself, evidence for a folded structural state, protein function, physical admissibility, organismal phenotype, causal translation mechanism, or universal biological law. Any such higher-layer conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic. The admissible conclusion here is only that, in the recorded yeast rows, the $f_3$-stress abundance component is better localized to the $5$-prime dwell window than to the $3$-prime body window under the stated deterministic permutation test.
