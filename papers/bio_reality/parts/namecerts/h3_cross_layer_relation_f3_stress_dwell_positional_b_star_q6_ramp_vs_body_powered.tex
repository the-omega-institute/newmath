\subsection{Position-resolved dwell absorption for the $f_3$-stress abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-positional-ramp-body}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record studies the $f_3$-stress coordinate inside the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual family
$\{K_{AAA},\operatorname{Arg}_{AGR},\operatorname{Ile}_{AUA},\operatorname{Leu}_{CUN/UUR},\operatorname{Leu}_{UUA/UUG},\operatorname{Ser}_{UCR/AGY},\operatorname{Ser}_{UCA/UCG},\operatorname{Thr}_{ACR/ACY},f_3\}$.
The measured target is not total protein abundance itself.  It is the rank-one component
$P_{f_3}$ obtained by projecting $Z$-residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance onto the $Z$-residualized $f_3$-stress column on the active positional-dwell join.  The control design has $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=24$, and the tested signal rank is $\operatorname{rank}(f_3)=1$.  The active organism is \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; the finite row count is $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1257$ inside a base frame of $1800$ rows.  The squared norm of the target component is $44.20095750389323$, and the fraction of the full target assigned to this component is $0.1999063916718794$.

\paragraph{Segmented dwell readout.}
The positional readout is a pair of per-gene relative ribosome-occupancy summaries computed from file-provided A-site occupancy windows.  One summary uses the $5'$ head or ramp window, and the other uses the $3'$ tail or body window.  The fitting question is whether adding one of these segment-specific dwell summaries to the base design improves held-out prediction of $P_{f_3}$ beyond the fixed base design, with folds held constant.  The registered calculation used five folds and $120$ deterministic row-label permutations of $P_{f_3}$, separately for each segment and for the joint segment design.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{f_3} &= \Pi_{\widetilde f_3}\,\widetilde P,\\
\widetilde P &= P-\Pi_ZP,\\
\widetilde f_3 &= f_3-\Pi_Zf_3,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{seg}} &= R^2_{\mathrm{held\ out}}(Z+\mathrm{base}+D_{\mathrm{seg}})-R^2_{\mathrm{held\ out}}(Z+\mathrm{base}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The registered outcome is asymmetric across position.  The $5'$ segment passed the positive-permutation gate, while the $3'$ segment did not.  The leading segment by held-out increment is therefore the $5'$ ramp, and the dominant segment recorded by the finite run is $5prime$.  This establishes a position-localized dwell absorption result for this finite matrix: the $f_3$-stress abundance component is detectably better read by the ramp-proximal dwell summary than by the body/tail dwell summary under the stated controls and permutation null.

\paragraph{Relation to the unsegmented dwell check.}
The unsegmented dwell mediation run on the same yeast target gives the scale of the positional finding.  In that companion calculation the base held-out value was $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, the full base-plus-dwell value was $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, and the held-out increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}}=0.01856473944057712.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fixed permutation reference had mean $-0.0032911642620130563$, minimum $-0.013799622026666476$, maximum $0.005001350039772926$, and $95\%$ cutoff $0.0013115362189692937$.  The observed $f_3$--dwell correlation was $0.1615441450901653$, with approximate right-tail probability $7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  The positional record refines this result by assigning the passed dwell increment to the $5'$ window rather than to the $3'$ body window.

\paragraph{Nearby finite checks.}
Several adjacent rows constrain the interpretation.  The direct protein-omics survival surface joined $5815$ yeast CDS rows to measured PAXdb abundance, with join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, and gave $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ with all nine residual coordinates surviving under the positive threshold.  The modeled translation-conditioned four-case calculation on yeast classified all nine coordinates as case $D$, with $R^2_{QT}=0.8213457220900503$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$.  A separate structural-order ramp/body calculation reports a positive but not fully localized structural-order ramp outcome: at primary ramp length $50$, the weighted ramp increment was $0.0011165640103326878$, the weighted body increment was $-0.00026157313923957926$, the paired ramp-minus-body increment was $0.001378137149572267$, the ramp sign-flip probability was $0.0478515625$, and the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability was $0.05859375$.  This is compatible with a ramp-positive signal, but it is not a structural-order localization theorem.  The partial readout-dictionary reduction row gives an additional scale check on yeast: with $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, $n_{\mathrm{join}}=1800$, five folds, and $120$ permutations, the selected coverage was $0.24538211439730545$ against a selection-aware null $95\%$ cutoff of $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The finite conclusion is therefore narrow.  For the yeast positional dwell matrix, the $f_3$-stress-associated abundance component has a passed $5'$ dwell increment and a failed $3'$ dwell increment.  The admissible statement is a position-resolved statistical readback: the ramp-proximal ribosome-occupancy summary carries the measured dwell absorption of $P_{f_3}$ in this joined panel.  The row does not by itself assert translation causation, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Those stronger readings would require a separate measurement surface at the appropriate layer, joined to the same CDS units and tested under its own controls.  In particular, a positive dwell absorption is not a synonymous-edit rescue experiment, not a causal mediation estimate, not a protein-folding assay, and not evidence that the same positional pattern must hold outside this yeast panel and its stated A-site occupancy windows.
