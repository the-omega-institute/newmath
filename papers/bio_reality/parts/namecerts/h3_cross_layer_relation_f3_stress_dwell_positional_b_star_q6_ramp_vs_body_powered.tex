\subsection{Positional dwell readback for the $f_3$ stress abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-positional-ramp-body}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The row object is a yeast matched panel in which the $f_3$ stress coordinate from the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual family is tested against positional ribosome-occupancy summaries and a protein-abundance target. The organism is Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The active positional-dwell join contains $1257$ genes, with a base design size recorded as $1800$, a five-fold held-out protocol, a rank-one $f_3$ source, and a $Z$ basis of rank $24$. The abundance target is not raw abundance: it is the projection $P_{f_3}$ of $Z$-residualized $\log_{10}$ protein abundance onto the $Z$-residualized $f_3$ coordinate on the active base plus positional-dwell join. The projection has squared norm $44.20095750389323$ and accounts for $0.1999063916718794$ of the target energy. Positional dwell is read as per-gene relative ribosome occupancy, computed separately from file-provided $5'$ head and $3'$ tail A-site occupancy windows. The finite test therefore has three named layers: the residual coordinate $f_3$, the positional dwell readout split into head and tail windows, and the protein-abundance component aligned with $f_3$ after the declared controls.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The registered null keeps the base and full designs fixed and permutes the row labels of $P_{f_3}$ deterministically. The same null is applied separately to each positional segment and to the joint segment design, with $120$ permutations and the deterministic seed $\texttt{sha256:b\_star\_q6\_f3\_stress\_dwell\_positional\_powered:deterministic}$. A segment passes only when its base-plus-segment held-out gain is positive and exceeds the corresponding fixed-readout permutation threshold. In the finite record, the leading segment by held-out gain is $5'$; the dominant segment is also $5'$. The head-window gate passes, while the tail-window gate does not:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{join}} &= 1257,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(f_3) &= 1,\\
 \operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 24,\\
 N_{\mathrm{fold}} &= 5,\\
 N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 120,\\
 \mathrm{passed}_{5'} &= \mathrm{true},\\
 \mathrm{passed}_{3'} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite conclusion is not merely that dwell helps somewhere; it localizes the supported dwell increment to the $5'$ ramp side of the coding sequence under the row-fixed permutation design.

\paragraph{Relation to the non-positional dwell certificate.}
The companion dwell mediation run on the same organism gives the scale of the effect before the head-versus-tail split. On its active join of $1257$ genes, the base held-out model has $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, the full base-plus-dwell model has $R^2=0.20412261520502883$, and the held-out increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}} &= 0.20412261520502883 - 0.1855578757644517\\
&= 0.01856473944057712.
\end{aligned}
$$
The dwell-only readout has $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, and the empirical $f_3$--dwell correlation is $0.1615441450901653$ with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$. The same deterministic null family has mean $-0.0032911642620130563$, minimum $-0.013799622026666476$, maximum $0.005001350039772926$, and $95$th percentile $0.0013115362189692937$. The observed held-out increment is therefore far beyond the registered null scale. The positional experiment refines this certificate by showing that the accepted increment is carried by the $5'$ window rather than by the $3'$ body window.

\paragraph{Ramp comparison.}
A separate structural-order ramp analysis also reports a positive $f_3$ ramp increment, but it does not convert the dwell result into a structure claim. At the primary ramp length $50$, the weighted $f_3$ ramp held-out increment is $0.0011165640103326878$, while the weighted $f_3$ body increment is $-0.00026157313923957926$, so the weighted ramp-minus-body contrast is $0.001378137149572267$. The ramp sign-flip probability for being greater than zero is $0.0478515625$, whereas the paired ramp-minus-body sign-flip probability is $0.05859375$. The recorded module classification is $\texttt{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$: the ramp signal is positive, but the stricter paired localization gate is not passed. For the present positional-dwell chapter, this means that the $5'$ dwell localization is a ribosome-occupancy readback on the $P_{f_3}$ abundance component, not an assertion of folded structural order.

\paragraph{Compression and irreducibility context.}
The same yeast context also has a partial-reduction certificate for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual. With $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, base size $4759$, active join $1800$, and five folds, the best selected readout dictionary covers $C^*=0.24538211439730545$ of the source signal. The raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.2745002077400602$, the selection-aware null $95$th percentile is $0.029149007242956115$, the naive fixed-subset null $95$th percentile is $0.00598374993486675$, and the remaining unexplained fraction is $0.7546178856026946$. This places the positional dwell result in a partially reducible regime: measured readouts explain a real part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance association, but most of the aligned abundance component is still outside the listed readout dictionary. The irreducibility audit uses the same $\lambda_{\mathrm{DL}}=0.01$, $\rho_{\mathrm{join}}=0.05$, base size $4759$, active join $1800$, five folds, and $120$ permutations; its registered interpretation is that the source signal is real and at least one listed reduction family refutes a global primitive-residual claim, without certifying irreducibility.

\paragraph{Translation and abundance boundaries.}
Measured translation-efficiency survival is available for several organisms, and the yeast entry is the largest among the listed measured-TE readbacks: $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$ with all nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates surviving on $5128$ genes joined to measured TE from $5815$ CDS rows. The measured-TE to protein-abundance decomposition in yeast has $S_{QP}=0.3146818503437042$, $S_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, and measured mediated fraction $0.4793069656345167$. The joint TE and mRNA-stability variance partition in yeast reports $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.1439989545940314$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.25453612246767165$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13047582645538838$, with TE-only fraction $0.4697188319693239$, stability-only fraction $0.0626618595344251$, joint fraction $0.5195182225621592$, and right-tail permutation probability $0.000999000999000999$. These numbers support a translation-conditioned abundance readout in yeast, but the positional dwell test is narrower: it says that the $f_3$-aligned abundance component is better predicted when the $5'$ ribosome-occupancy summary is included.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row verdict is positive for positional dwell localization. The supported statement is: in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, after the declared $Z$ residualization and fixed-row permutation audit, the $f_3$ component of protein abundance has a positional dwell increment, and the accepted segment is the $5'$ ramp window rather than the $3'$ body window. This is a rank-powered, row-level statistical readback of an abundance-aligned residual component through a positional ribosome-occupancy summary. It is not a causal mechanism and it does not by itself establish translation dynamics in general, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, evolutionary direction, or universality. Those stronger statements would require separate layer-matched measurements and their own finite tests.
