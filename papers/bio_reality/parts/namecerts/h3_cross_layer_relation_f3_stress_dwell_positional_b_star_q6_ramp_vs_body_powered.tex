\subsection{Positional dwell localization of the $f3\_stress$ abundance component}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-dwell-positional-ramp-body}

\paragraph{Claim.}
This chapter records a finite-row test of whether the $f3\_stress$ coordinate in the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis has a positionally localized dwell component in measured yeast ribosome-occupancy summaries.  The tested object is not total protein abundance, not a translation law, and not a structural claim.  It is the following narrower finite matrix question: after the usual sequence controls are applied, project the protein-abundance residual onto the one-dimensional $f3\_stress$ direction and ask whether positional dwell summaries from the ribosome readout improve held-out prediction of that projected component.  The result is a ramp-versus-body discrimination on $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$ rows, with the $5'$ segment leading the positional increment and the $3'$ body segment not passing the same gate.

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The active carrier is the joined yeast table on which codon-derived residual coordinates, protein abundance, and positional ribosome-occupancy summaries are all present.  The positional dwell experiment used $n=1257$ joined rows, a base design size of $1800$ rows before the active positional join, $5$ held-out folds, one $f3\_stress$ direction, and a control basis of rank $24$.  The abundance target was not the full abundance vector.  It was
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{f3} &= \operatorname{proj}_{\widetilde f3}\bigl(\widetilde{\log_{10} P}\bigr),\\
\|P_{f3}\|^2 &= 44.20095750389323,\\
\frac{\|P_{f3}\|^2}{\|\widetilde{\log_{10} P}\|^2} &= 0.1999063916718794.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here tildes denote residualization against the finite control block used by the experiment: sequence length, amino-acid composition, GC3, the $M$-density baseline, and the matched abundance-side covariates encoded in the table.  The dwell readout was computed per gene from file-provided positional A-site occupancy summaries, split into $5'$ head/ramp and $3'$ tail/body windows rather than collapsed into a single whole-CDS statistic.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
For each segment, the design held the base/full model structures and fold assignment fixed, then compared the observed segment increment with a deterministic row-label permutation null on $P_{f3}$.  The null was run separately for each segment and for the joint segment design, with $120$ permutations and deterministic seed
$\mathrm{sha256{:}b\_star\_q6\_f3\_stress\_dwell\_positional\_powered{:}deterministic}$.
The declared pass condition was segment-specific: a positional window must give positive held-out $\Delta R^2$ and exceed its fixed-readout permutation boundary.  Under that rule, the experiment returned
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dominant\_segment} &= 5'\text{ prime},\\
\operatorname{leading\_segment\_by\_}\Delta R^2 &= 5'\text{ prime},\\
\operatorname{five\_prime\_ramp\_passed} &= \mathrm{true},\\
\operatorname{three\_prime\_body\_passed} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite readback is not merely that dwell information helps somewhere in the yeast table.  The support is localized to the $5'$ positional dwell channel under this segment-wise null.

\paragraph{Relation to the non-positional dwell test.}
The companion dwell-abundance fit used the same biological target, namely $P_{f3}$, and supplied a whole-readout mediation check before the segment split.  On the same yeast organism class it reported $n=1257$ joined rows, $5$ folds, $120$ deterministic permutations, rank $1$ for the $f3\_stress$ direction, and control-rank $24$.  The base model had held-out $R^2=0.1855578757644517$, the dwell-only model had $R^2=0.034336580953121976$, and the base-plus-dwell model had $R^2=0.20412261520502883$.  The held-out dwell increment was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}} &= 0.01856473944057712,\\
\operatorname{Null}_{95} &= 0.0013115362189692937,\\
\operatorname{Null}_{\max} &= 0.005001350039772926,\\
\operatorname{Null}_{\mathrm{mean}} &= -0.0032911642620130563,\\
\operatorname{Null}_{\min} &= -0.013799622026666476.
\end{aligned}
$$
The measured correlation between the $f3\_stress$ direction and the dwell readout was $0.1615441450901653$, with approximate $p=7.873916057196935\times 10^{-9}$.  This whole-readout result is the reason the positional split is meaningful: the split asks where the already positive dwell increment sits, not whether a dwell route can be invented after seeing a positional label.

\paragraph{Ramp localization versus structural-order wording.}
A related structural-order ramp screen also found a positive ramp-side signal but did not certify localization under its paired ramp-body gate.  With primary ramp length $50$, the weighted held-out increment for the $f3$ ramp was $0.0011165640103326878$, while the body increment was $-0.00026157313923957926$; the paired ramp-minus-body increment was $0.001378137149572267$.  The sign-flip probabilities were
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(f3_{\mathrm{ramp}}>0) &= 0.0478515625,\\
p(f3_{\mathrm{body}}>0) &= 0.54296875,\\
p(f3_{\mathrm{ramp}}-f3_{\mathrm{body}}>0) &= 0.05859375.
\end{aligned}
$$
That companion screen classified the outcome as $\mathrm{ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized}$ because the paired localization gate did not pass.  The present chapter is therefore phrased more narrowly.  It records positional dwell localization for the abundance-projected $f3\_stress$ component under the dwell permutation gate, while it does not convert that result into a folded-structure, disorder, ramp-translation, or protein-state conclusion.

\paragraph{Background finite-row context.}
The same yeast organism supplies several bounded readouts that explain why this claim is cross-layer but still limited.  In the direct protein-omics survival table, yeast had $5815$ joined CDS/protein rows, join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, and direct residual-to-protein $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$ with all $9$ $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates surviving the strictly positive numerical threshold.  In the measured ribosome-TE survival table, yeast had $5128$ genes with TE, ribosome-TE join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, residual degrees of freedom $5105$, and measured translation-readout survival $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}=0.13681931343730436$, again with $9$ surviving coordinates.  A separate reduction-dictionary run on the unified yeast context used $n=1800$ rows from $4758$ available rows, $5$ folds, $120$ permutations, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, and $\rho_{join}=0.05$; it found selection-aware coverage $C^{\ast}=0.24538211439730545$ above a selection-aware null $95$ boundary of $0.029149007242956115$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.7546178856026946$.  These numbers support the local interpretation that the $f3\_stress$ abundance component is partly reducible to measured readouts, but still not exhausted by them.

\paragraph{Four-way readback.}
The local readback is a four-way finite distinction.  First, the coordinate is translation-readout-surviving in yeast in the measured TE matrix, because the yeast $R^2_{QT,\mathrm{meas}}$ is positive after controls.  Second, it is protein-omics-surviving in the direct abundance matrix, because yeast has positive $R^2_{QP}$ after controls.  Third, the dwell experiments show that a measured ribosome-occupancy summary contributes to the $P_{f3}$ abundance component, with whole-dwell $\Delta R^2=0.01856473944057712$ above the fixed permutation boundary.  Fourth, the positional split assigns the leading dwell increment to the $5'$ segment and rejects the $3'$ body segment under the same finite-row decision form.  In symbols, the chapter records the finite implication
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QT}_{\mathrm{yeast}}(f3\_stress) &> 0,\\
S^{QP}_{\mathrm{yeast}}(f3\_stress) &> 0,\\
\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{dwell}\rightarrow P_{f3}} &> \operatorname{Null}_{95},\\
\operatorname{lead}\bigl(\Delta R^2_{5'},\Delta R^2_{3'}\bigr) &= 5'.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last line is the chapter-specific claim.  The previous three lines are the finite context that prevents the positional result from being read as a free-floating window artifact.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No broader biological conclusion is licensed by this chapter alone.  The result does not prove translation rate, elongation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, or a universal biological law.  It also does not say that every $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate is ramp-localized.  It says that, in the powered yeast join used here, the $f3\_stress$ component of controlled protein abundance has a measured dwell increment, and the positional dwell test places the passing segment at the $5'$ ramp rather than the $3'$ body.  Claims about folding, localization, stability, function, phenotype, perturbation rescue, or causal mechanism require their own layer-matched measurements and their own controlled finite-row statistics.
