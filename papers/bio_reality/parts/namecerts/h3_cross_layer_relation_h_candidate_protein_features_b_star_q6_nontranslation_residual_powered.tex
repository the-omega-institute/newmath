\subsection{Protein-feature absorption as a bounded $B^*_{Q6}$ contact}
\label{subsec:bq6-protein-feature-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether coarse protein-feature annotations absorb any part of the modeled-translation-complement component left by the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates.  The tested coordinate list has nine entries,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The nuisance surface contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $q$ vectors.  The reported control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every protein-feature organism.  Each candidate feature contributes one scalar column, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count readouts.

\paragraph{Joined organisms and feature coverage.}
The feature test is not a full-CDS test: it is a feature-availability join.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, the CDS abundance join reports $14410$ CDS rows, but only $3180$ reviewed feature records are available and the all-readout feature join contains $2242$ rows.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding numbers are $19053$ CDS rows, $18842$ feature records, and $18335$ joined rows.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, they are $5815$, $6156$, and $5682$.  Thus the human and yeast feature joins almost preserve the underlying abundance-CDS surface, while the zebrafish join is a much narrower reviewed-annotation slice.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability check.}
The feature-contact target is the residual modeled-tAI complement, so the first diagnostic is whether the feature join preserves the modeled-complement fraction seen on the corresponding full abundance-CDS join.  The full-reference and feature-join values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad 0.874628721049266\ \text{versus}\ 0.7361678489976374,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad 0.9208949355679455\ \text{versus}\ 0.9188293375787641,\\
\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:&\quad 0.21173219572706703\ \text{versus}\ 0.21235197749007645.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute differences are $0.1384608720516286$, $0.002065597989181489$, and $0.0006197817630094193$, respectively.  With the stated $0.02$ tolerance, human and yeast pass this stability check, whereas zebrafish fails it.  The correct reading is therefore not a stable cross-organism protein-feature explanation; at least one organism changes the residual target when reviewed feature coverage is imposed.

\paragraph{Observed absorptions.}
Within the feature joins, the largest single-candidate absorptions are small but not all null-like.  In zebrafish, the absorptions are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  In human, they are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$.  In yeast, they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  These numbers describe finite projection absorption only: they do not identify a biochemical mediator and do not establish that a feature causes the residual coordinate pattern.

\paragraph{Null comparison and multiple-candidate boundary.}
The degree-of-freedom-matched null uses $200$ trials per organism-candidate pair, with Bonferroni thresholds computed separately.  Zebrafish PTM density exceeds its threshold, with absorption $0.014211240113472575$ against threshold $0.003775603188484381$, null mean $0.0004610486745564995$, and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  The other zebrafish candidates do not exceed their thresholds.  In human, complex membership exceeds $0.0006605662314432479$ with absorption $0.0075271999877804495$, PTM density exceeds $0.00042094892238120357$ with absorption $0.014884825920540636$, and transmembrane count exceeds $0.0002697448894057371$ with absorption $0.0015569064882873212$; domain count does not.  In yeast, no candidate exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: the largest yeast absorption, complex membership at $0.00069212177545487$, remains below $0.001015254045679051$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and tRNA contacts.}
The feature result sits downstream of, and separate from, measured translation-efficiency and tRNA-copy readbacks.  The measured-TE contact used the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates with matched translation-efficiency rows and reported $n=13140$ for zebrafish, $n=3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for human, and $n=5128$ for yeast, all with $24$ control columns and rank $23$.  Its measured-TE absorption values were $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in those organisms.  The measured tRNA-copy tAI contact, evaluated on full CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast, reported absorptions $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$.  These contacts show that translation-adjacent readouts have been bounded separately; they do not license promotion of the protein-feature projection into translation realization.

\paragraph{Turnover and organism-specific context.}
A separate yeast turnover contact joined $3640$ half-life rows, with a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and a turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  Its modeled-complement stability difference was $0.011538255214205373$, within the same $0.02$ tolerance, and its turnover absorption was only $0.0009086031889966327$.  At the broader organism-specific level, the dominant-coordinate contingency over $12$ complete organisms had Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail probability $0.5814185814185814$; the $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus $GC3$ correlation was $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation probability $0.35264735264735264$.  These finite summaries are heterogeneous rather than a resolved universal rule.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite protein-feature contact supports a narrow statement: on reviewed-feature joins, PTM density absorbs a detectable part of the modeled-complement residual in zebrafish and human, while human complex membership and transmembrane count also exceed their degree-of-freedom-matched Bonferroni thresholds.  The same evidence does not support a stable cross-organism $H$ closure, because zebrafish changes the modeled-complement target substantially under feature availability and yeast supplies no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate.  No translation, ribosome-occupancy, protein-abundance realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from these entries unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and its own controlled statistic.
