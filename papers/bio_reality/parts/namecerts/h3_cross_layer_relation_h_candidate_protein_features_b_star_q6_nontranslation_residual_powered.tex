\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a finite panel of fetchable protein annotations accounts for the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component left after the modeled translation surface has been removed. The row carrier is a protein-identifier join between real CDS codon counts, the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, the standard control block, and four one-dimensional protein-feature readouts. The controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $q$ vectors. In each organism the recorded control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Joined finite rows.}
The feature panel is deliberately not a genome-wide universal surface: it is the all-readout join available after reviewed UniProt/STRING feature parsing. For \emph{Danio rerio}, $3180$ feature records produce $2242$ joined CDS rows out of $14410$ reported CDS joins. For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records produce $18335$ joined rows out of $19053$ reported CDS joins. For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records produce $5682$ joined rows out of $5815$ reported CDS joins. The four candidate readouts are complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-count; each candidate contributes rank $K_c=1$ on every organismal join.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined rows show that the tested annotations have very different sparsity profiles. In zebrafish, the joined complex-member mean is $0.3104371097$ with $696$ nonzero rows, domain-count mean is $0.8563782337$ with $847$ nonzero rows and maximum $48$, PTM-density mean is $0.0012176950$ with $211$ nonzero rows, and transmembrane-count mean is $0.7292595897$ with $443$ nonzero rows and maximum $18$. In human, the corresponding means are $0.3626943005$, $1.0712844287$, $0.0064738678$, and $1.0721570766$, with nonzero counts $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ over $18335$ rows. In yeast, the means are $0.3505807814$, $0.4074269623$, $0.0027745358$, and $1.0007039775$, with nonzero counts $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ over $5682$ rows. These numbers make the contact usable as a bounded annotation test, but also explain why absence of absorption cannot be read as biological absence.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability.}
The residual target is stable only when the feature join preserves the modeled-complement reference surface. The diagnostic compares the feature-join value $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ with the full abundance-CDS complement reference:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Danio} &= |0.7361678489976374-0.874628721049266| = 0.1384608720516286,\\
\Delta_{Human} &= |0.9188293375787641-0.9208949355679455| = 0.002065597989181489,\\
\Delta_{Yeast} &= |0.21235197749007645-0.21173219572706703| = 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human and yeast joins match within the $0.02$ diagnostic tolerance, while the zebrafish join does not. Thus any cross-organism claim that treats the three feature surfaces as the same residual target is refused: the sparse zebrafish feature availability changes the object being projected.

\paragraph{Absorption estimates.}
The one-dimensional projection readbacks are small and heterogeneous. In zebrafish, complex membership absorbs $0.0003956638$, domain count absorbs $0.0008268196$, PTM density absorbs $0.0142112401$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.0013691427$ of the feature-join complement. In human, the corresponding absorptions are $0.0075272000$, $0.0000114055$, $0.0148848259$, and $0.0015569065$. In yeast they are $0.0006921218$, $0.0000702970$, $0.0005759337$, and $0.0003174149$. The largest entries are PTM density in zebrafish and human, but this is a descriptive projection of the available annotation surface, not a mechanistic statement about modification biology.

\paragraph{Null comparison and multiplicity.}
The finite null uses $200$ degree-of-freedom-matched trials per organism and candidate, with Bonferroni thresholds recorded separately for each readout. Zebrafish PTM density exceeds its threshold $0.003775603188484381$, while zebrafish complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$. Human complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, while human domain count does not exceed $0.0005934431422432757$. In yeast, none of the four candidates exceeds its threshold; the thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane count. The null means are all small: for example, human PTM density has null mean $0.000056988673240982024$ and null $95$th percentile $0.00022866792835099184$, while the observed absorption is $0.014884825920540636$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and other H-candidates.}
The feature test sits downstream of a measured-translation contact rather than replacing it. On the measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency join, the computed measured-TE absorptions are $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast. The measured-tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact absorbs $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast. The yeast turnover contact absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ on $3640$ joined rows. These neighboring finite contacts support the same methodological reading: the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual is being interrogated by bounded projections, and each projection must keep its own row join and readout semantics.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The protein-feature packet is ready as a computed descriptive boundary. It records real row counts, finite ranks, candidate absorptions, and null comparisons, and it identifies where the feature join changes the residual target. It does not license a stable cross-organism H-closure claim, because zebrafish fails the modeled-complement stability diagnostic by $0.1384608720516286$. It also does not license translation, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Those stronger conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts and statistics. In this chapter the permitted conclusion is narrower: reviewed-feature annotations can absorb small pieces of the modeled-translation-complement residual on their own sparse joins, with Bonferroni excess in selected human and zebrafish candidates, while the finite evidence remains descriptive and join-dependent.
