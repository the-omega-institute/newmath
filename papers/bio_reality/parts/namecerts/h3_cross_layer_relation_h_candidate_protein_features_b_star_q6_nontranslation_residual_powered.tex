\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This chapter records a bounded cross-layer test for whether coarse protein annotations absorb any part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after a modeled translation readout has already been removed. The finite surface is not a protein-function claim. It consists of coding-sequence rows joined to four reviewed feature coordinates, together with the same nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates used throughout this packet:
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f_3\_stress$.
The control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, giving $24$ control columns and rank $23$ in each organism retained for this feature test.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature candidates.}
The protein-feature panel is deliberately smaller than the coding-sequence abundance panel, because reviewed feature annotation is not complete. In \emph{Danio rerio}, $3180$ feature records yield $2242$ joined rows against $14410$ reported CDS-abundance joins. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records yield $18335$ joined rows against $19053$ reported CDS-abundance joins. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records yield $5682$ joined rows against $5815$ reported CDS-abundance joins. Each organism carries four rank-one candidate readouts: complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-count. Thus the candidate list is finite, $K_c=1$ for every candidate, and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for every candidate-specific projection.

\paragraph{Feature distributions on the joined rows.}
The joined feature rows have visibly different sparsity across organisms. In zebrafish, complex membership has mean $0.3104371097$ with $696$ nonzero rows out of $2242$, domain count has mean $0.8563782337$ with $847$ nonzero rows and maximum $48$, PTM density has mean $0.0012176950$ with $211$ nonzero rows and maximum $0.8014705882$, and transmembrane count has mean $0.7292595897$ with $443$ nonzero rows and maximum $18$. In human, complex membership has mean $0.3626943005$ with $6650$ nonzero rows out of $18335$, domain count has mean $1.0712844287$ with $8090$ nonzero rows and maximum $285$, PTM density has mean $0.0064738678$ with $9210$ nonzero rows and maximum $0.9126213592$, and transmembrane count has mean $1.0721570766$ with $4792$ nonzero rows and maximum $38$. In yeast, complex membership has mean $0.3505807814$ with $1992$ nonzero rows out of $5682$, domain count has mean $0.4074269623$ with $1574$ nonzero rows and maximum $11$, PTM density has mean $0.0027745358$ with $2282$ nonzero rows and maximum $0.2647058824$, and transmembrane count has mean $1.0007039775$ with $1173$ nonzero rows and maximum $23$.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
The feature test is evaluated on the all-feature join, so the residual target must be checked against the full abundance-CDS complement reference before any cross-organism conclusion is allowed. The modeled-complement fraction on the feature join is $0.7361678490$ in zebrafish, compared with a full-join complement reference of $0.8746287210$; the absolute difference is $0.1384608721$, which fails the $0.02$ stability tolerance. Human is stable by this diagnostic: the feature-join value is $0.9188293376$ against $0.9208949356$, with absolute difference $0.0020655980$. Yeast is also stable: the feature-join value is $0.2123519775$ against $0.2117321957$, with absolute difference $0.0006197818$. The finite verdict therefore cannot be a stable cross-organism closure, because one organism changes the target residual substantially when the feature annotation join is imposed.

\paragraph{Candidate absorption values.}
The absorbed fractions are small, with a few candidate-specific excesses over degree-of-freedom matched null thresholds. In zebrafish, the four absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{complex} &= 0.0003956638,\\
\mathrm{domain} &= 0.0008268196,\\
\mathrm{PTM} &= 0.0142112401,\\
\mathrm{TM} &= 0.0013691427.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only the PTM-density value exceeds its Bonferroni threshold $0.0037756032$; the corresponding null mean is $0.0004610487$ and the null $95$th percentile is $0.0017881512$ over $200$ trials. The other zebrafish candidates do not exceed their thresholds: complex membership has threshold $0.0027585461$, domain count has threshold $0.0051235776$, and transmembrane count has threshold $0.0034780137$.

\paragraph{Human feature absorption.}
Human shows three Bonferroni-exceeding candidates, but still only as descriptive projections. The absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{complex} &= 0.0075272000,\\
\mathrm{domain} &= 0.0000114055,\\
\mathrm{PTM} &= 0.0148848259,\\
\mathrm{TM} &= 0.0015569065.
\end{aligned}
$$
Complex membership exceeds its threshold $0.0006605662$ with null mean $0.0000478230$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0001937092$. PTM density exceeds its threshold $0.0004209489$ with null mean $0.0000569887$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0002286679$. Transmembrane count exceeds its threshold $0.0002697449$ with null mean $0.0000457169$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0001793447$. Domain count does not exceed its threshold $0.0005934431$, because its absorbed fraction is only $0.0000114055$.

\paragraph{Yeast feature absorption.}
The yeast feature panel supplies a negative control-like comparison: none of the four candidates exceeds its Bonferroni threshold. The absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{complex} &= 0.0006921218,\\
\mathrm{domain} &= 0.0000702970,\\
\mathrm{PTM} &= 0.0005759337,\\
\mathrm{TM} &= 0.0003174149.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding thresholds are $0.0010152540$ for complex membership, $0.0011639235$ for domain count, $0.0017136457$ for PTM density, and $0.0012579936$ for transmembrane count. The null $95$th percentiles are $0.0006332752$, $0.0006659744$, $0.0007361909$, and $0.0004977219$, respectively. This organism therefore does not support a feature-absorption call under the finite competition rule.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The protein-feature contact is downstream of a measured translation-efficiency contact, but it is not equivalent to it. The measured-TE panel uses four organisms and joins $13140$ zebrafish rows, $3415$ \emph{E. coli} rows, $11554$ human rows, and $5128$ yeast rows, again with $24$ control columns and rank $23$ per organism. Its measured-TE absorbed fractions are $0.0175942900$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ for human, and $0.0002762784$ for yeast. These values show that translation-efficiency projection was computed separately from the protein-feature projection. A feature association is not a translation association, and a translation-efficiency association is not a protein-feature mechanism.

\paragraph{Relation to turnover and localization contacts.}
A yeast turnover panel gives an additional bounded comparison. It joins $3640$ turnover proteins, reports a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419$ and a turnover join hit rate $0.8818572657$, and computes an absorbed fraction by turnover of $0.0009086032$ with combined residual fraction $0.2230650372$. This is in the same descriptive scale as the yeast feature values and does not convert the feature panel into a degradation mechanism. A separate localization boundary reports $8696$ joined rows, $657$ mitochondrial rows, and $2000$ permutations with runtime $11.034382$ seconds. Those rows are a localization-specific contact and cannot be substituted for the four reviewed protein-feature coordinates used here.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The wider organism-specific meta table remains exploratory. Its complete-feature subset contains $12$ organisms, with four organisms still marked as needing data for the $M^{QTP}$ summary. The dominant-coordinate contingency test has $n=12$, Pearson statistic $9.4$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The continuous proxy tests also remain weak: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500$ and two-sided permutation value $0.3526473526$; versus log protein count has $r=-0.0336281158$ and value $0.9130869131$; versus log tRNA gene count has $r=0.0602940521$ and value $0.8621378621$. These scalars keep the feature-absorption result in the proper class: organism-specific descriptive structure, not a resolved universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Finite verdict.}
The protein-feature experiment is ready as a bounded record because the rows, candidate list, controls, ranks, null trials, thresholds, and absorbed fractions are all finite and explicitly reported. The positive finite entries are zebrafish PTM density, human complex membership, human PTM density, and human transmembrane count. The negative finite entries are all four yeast feature candidates, zebrafish complex membership, zebrafish domain count, zebrafish transmembrane count, and human domain count. The cross-organism H-candidate is not closed, because sparse reviewed-feature coverage changes the residual target in zebrafish by $0.1384608721$, far beyond the $0.02$ tolerance. The chapter therefore supports only a descriptive protein-feature absorption statement on the reported joins.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation-realization, folding, physical-admissibility, biological-function, mechanistic, phenotypic, or universal-law claim follows from these feature projections. The feature coordinates are coarse reviewed annotations, not causal perturbations or physical measurements. A translation claim requires a same-row translation readout with matched mRNA control; a folding or physical-admissibility claim requires a structure or physical assay; a function or phenotype claim requires its own layer-matched assay; and a mechanism claim requires perturbation or mediation evidence beyond the projection quantities reported here.
