\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether fetchable protein-feature annotations account for the abundance residual component that remains after the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous-coordinate block is compared with the modeled translation-adaptation component.  The finite carrier is the all-readout join between coding-sequence rows, the nine residual coordinates
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$, and four one-dimensional protein-feature candidates: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-helix count.  The control matrix has $24$ columns but rank $23$ in every organism after including the intercept, $\log({\rm cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline.  The finite joins are materially unequal across organisms: Danio rerio contributes $2242$ joined rows from $3180$ feature records and $14410$ reported CDS joins, Homo sapiens contributes $18335$ joined rows from $18842$ feature records and $19053$ reported CDS joins, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae contributes $5682$ joined rows from $6156$ feature records and $5815$ reported CDS joins.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
The protein-feature test is not run on the same target as the full abundance-CDS complement in every organism.  On the feature join, the modeled residual fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\rm modeled}^{\rm feature}({\rm Danio}) &= 0.7361678489976374,\\
 d_{\rm modeled}^{\rm feature}({\rm human}) &= 0.9188293375787641,\\
 d_{\rm modeled}^{\rm feature}({\rm yeast}) &= 0.21235197749007645.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against the full abundance-CDS complement references, the absolute shifts are $0.1384608720516286$ for Danio rerio, $0.002065597989181489$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.0006197817630094193$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  Thus only the human and yeast feature joins remain within the declared $0.02$ stability window.  The zebrafish feature rows alter the residual target too strongly to support a stable cross-organism closure, even though the descriptive projections themselves are computable.

Each candidate contributes rank $1$ after residualization.  The absorbed fractions are finite and small, with the largest values concentrated in PTM density and human complex membership:

The degree-of-freedom matched nulls use $200$ trials for each organism-candidate pair.  Bonferroni excess is present for Danio PTM density only, with threshold $0.003775603188484381$ and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$; for human, complex membership, PTM density, and TM count exceed their thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, respectively.  No yeast candidate exceeds the Bonferroni boundary; its thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and TM count.

\paragraph{Feature sparsity and empirical meaning.}
The annotation surface is coarse and incomplete, especially for Danio rerio.  In the joined zebrafish rows, complex membership is nonzero in $696$ of $2242$ proteins, domain count is nonzero in $847$, PTM density is nonzero in $211$, and TM count is nonzero in $443$.  In human, the corresponding nonzero counts are $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ over $18335$ joined proteins.  In yeast, they are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ over $5682$ joined proteins.  The feature distributions therefore measure availability-weighted annotations rather than a complete molecular state.  The strongest descriptive entries say that some residual abundance component is aligned with sparse annotation axes on the available joins; they do not establish that the synonymous residual causes the feature, that the feature mediates the residual, or that the same relation is stable under a denser annotation panel.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and modeled adaptation contacts.}
The measured-TE companion computation gives a separate bound on translation-efficiency absorption: $0.01759429001506974$ in Danio rerio, $0.0010982956489726373$ in Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact gives similarly small absorbed fractions, namely $0.020817102845637092$ for Danio rerio, $0.00005426229300865148$ for Homo sapiens, and $0.001530747712165342$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  In yeast, the turnover contact absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ on $3640$ joined turnover rows.  These numbers are useful as neighboring finite controls: the protein-feature entries are not a replacement for measured translation, tRNA adaptation, or turnover contacts, and none of them licenses a path from codon-coordinate residuals to mechanism.

\paragraph{Cross-organism caution.}
A broader organism-specific table contains $16$ assembled rows, with $12$ complete rows for the $M^{QTP}$ fraction tests.  The domain test has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials; the dominant-coordinate by domain contingency has chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$.  Continuous tests also remain descriptive: $M^{QTP}$ versus GC3 has $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$, versus log proteome size has $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with value $0.913086913086913$, and versus log tRNA-gene count has $r=0.06029405206037823$ with value $0.8621378621378621$.  These finite meta-results support heterogeneity reporting, not a universal organism-level rule.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The ready result is a bounded descriptive record: protein-feature projections were computed on finite joined rows, their null comparisons were evaluated, and the instability of the Danio rerio feature join blocks a stable cross-organism protein-feature explanation of the residual complement.  A positive Bonferroni excess in a feature candidate is not a translation claim, not a folding claim, not a physical-admissibility claim, not a function claim, not a causal mechanism, and not a universal biological law.  Any such stronger interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact with its own joined rows, controls, and finite statistic.
