\subsection{B-star-Q6 residual contact with sparse protein feature annotations}
\label{subsec:b-star-q6-protein-feature-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual not already accounted for by the modeled translation proxy can be descriptively absorbed by a small panel of fetchable protein-feature annotations.  The coordinate basis is the nine-coordinate list
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each coordinate is residualized against the same finite control block used in the companion readouts: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  The resulting control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each reported organism.  The candidate protein-feature block contains four one-dimensional readouts, namely complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.

\paragraph{Joined rows and target stability.}
The feature panel is deliberately finite and uneven.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, $3180$ feature records join to $2242$ coding-sequence rows out of $14410$ reported CDS joins.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records join to $18335$ rows out of $19053$.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records join to $5682$ rows out of $5815$.  The modeled-complement target is therefore not identical across organisms.  The diagnostic comparison with the full abundance-CDS complement gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{Dr}} &= |0.7361678489976374-0.874628721049266|=0.1384608720516286,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{Hs}} &= |0.9188293375787641-0.9208949355679455|=0.002065597989181489,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{Sc}} &= |0.21235197749007645-0.21173219572706703|=0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only the human and yeast feature joins remain within the $0.02$ complement-stability tolerance.  The zebrafish feature join changes the residual target too strongly for a stable cross-organism positive conclusion.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature surfaces are sparse and highly organism-dependent.  In zebrafish, complex membership has mean $0.31043710972346117$ with $696$ nonzero rows among $2242$; domain count has mean $0.856378233719893$ with $847$ nonzero rows and maximum $48$; PTM density has mean $0.0012176949624096891$ with $211$ nonzero rows; and transmembrane-count has mean $0.7292595896520964$ with $443$ nonzero rows and maximum $18$.  In human, the corresponding joined means are $0.3626943005181347$, $1.071284428688301$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $1.0721570766293973$, with nonzero counts $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ among $18335$ rows.  In yeast, the joined means are $0.3505807814149947$, $0.4074269623372052$, $0.002774535811043876$, and $1.0007039774727209$, with nonzero counts $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ among $5682$ rows.  These summaries justify treating the four feature columns as coarse annotation readbacks rather than direct molecular-state measurements.

\paragraph{Absorption calculation.}
For each organism and candidate feature $H_c$, the reported number is the descriptive absorbed fraction after projecting the modeled-translation-complement residual onto the residualized feature column.  The finite values are

\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Dr}:\quad &C=0.00039566384726157463,\ D=0.0008268196005307971,\ P=0.014211240113472575,\ T=0.001369142724157357,\\
\mathrm{Hs}:\quad &C=0.0075271999877804495,\ D=0.000011405527940317433,\ P=0.014884825920540636,\ T=0.0015569064882873212,\\
\mathrm{Sc}:\quad &C=0.00069212177545487,\ D=0.00007029698692511431,\ P=0.0005759336988655581,\ T=0.00031741485098156394.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $C$ denotes complex membership, $D$ domain count, $P$ PTM density, and $T$ transmembrane-count.  The largest descriptive absorptions are the PTM-density entries in zebrafish and human, approximately $0.0142$ and $0.0149$ respectively.  Yeast shows no comparable feature absorption.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null and multiple-candidate boundary.}
The feature competition is evaluated against degree-of-freedom-matched nulls with $200$ trials per organism-candidate pair and Bonferroni thresholds.  Zebrafish PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: absorbed fraction $0.014211240113472575$ versus threshold $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  The other zebrafish candidates do not exceed threshold: complex membership threshold $0.00275854608923269$, domain-count threshold $0.005123577637652884$, and transmembrane-count threshold $0.0034780137133507$.  Human has three threshold exceedances: complex membership $0.0075271999877804495$ versus $0.0006605662314432479$, PTM density $0.014884825920540636$ versus $0.00042094892238120357$, and transmembrane-count $0.0015569064882873212$ versus $0.0002697448894057371$.  Human domain count does not exceed its $0.0005934431422432757$ threshold.  Yeast has no exceedance: its thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-count, respectively.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring translation contacts.}
The protein-feature row is not a replacement for measured translation.  The measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact reports much smaller absorptions of the modeled complement in the available same-readout joins: $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \textit{E. coli} K--12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast.  The tRNA-gene-copy tAI readback gives absorptions $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast.  The yeast turnover contact gives $0.0009086031889966327$ absorbed by half-life on $3640$ joined turnover rows, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$.  A separate localization check reports $8696$ joined human rows, $657$ mitochondrial rows, and $2000$ permutations.  These neighboring finite contacts show that the protein-feature result sits among bounded readouts, not above them.

\paragraph{Organism-level context.}
A cross-organism meta table was computed over $16$ organisms, with $12$ rows complete for the $M^{QTP}$ feature surface.  The domain contingency for dominant coordinates has $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  Continuous checks for $M^{QTP}$ fraction are also non-decisive: versus $GC3$, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; and versus log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The domain variance-ratio test has $n=12$, statistic $0.8889980452277374$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.4155844155844156$.  Thus the available organism-level evidence is heterogeneous rather than a resolved domain law.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
This chapter records a bounded descriptive contact between the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual and sparse protein-feature annotations.  The strongest finite entries are PTM-density absorption in zebrafish and human, with additional human complex-membership and transmembrane-count exceedances under the degree-of-freedom null.  The result is not a stable cross-organism closure because the zebrafish feature join changes the modeled-complement target by $0.1384608720516286$, far above the $0.02$ tolerance, and yeast supplies no Bonferroni-exceeding candidate.  Consequently the proper readback is a finite, annotation-limited H-candidate: it identifies where fetchable UniProt/STRING-style features absorb a small part of the residual, while preserving the unresolved residual boundary.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
No translation-efficiency survival, ribosome-occupancy realization, protein-abundance realization, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, biological-function statement, causal mechanism, phenotype statement, or universal biological law follows from these protein-feature absorptions.  Translation readout requires its own measured TE or ribosome-profiling contact with matched mRNA control.  Protein output, structure, localization, stability, function, and phenotype each require their own layer-matched finite rows and controls.  The present record licenses only the reported descriptive projections and their null comparisons on the stated joins.
