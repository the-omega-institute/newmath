\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}\origin{ai}
\paragraph{Finite readout surface.} This record tests whether coarse fetched protein annotations absorb part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component left after the modeled translation-adaptation direction has been removed. The finite carrier is the joined CDS-by-protein table on which nine residual coordinates are available: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$. The control block has $24$ columns, with rank $23$, and consists of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The candidate feature family has four one-dimensional readouts: complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-helix count. Thus the tested object is not a protein-function model; it is a finite projection question about whether these four annotated coordinates account for a small part of the remaining $Q6$-indexed abundance residual.
\paragraph{Joined rows and rank condition.} Three organisms have finite protein-feature joins. For \emph{Danio rerio}, $3180$ feature records produce $2242$ joined CDS-protein rows, compared with $14410$ CDS rows in the broader abundance join. For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records produce $18335$ joined rows, compared with $19053$ broader CDS rows. For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records produce $5682$ joined rows, compared with $5815$ broader CDS rows. In each organism the four candidate columns have candidate rank $K_c=1$ and fitted rank $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ when tested separately. The row counts are therefore powered for within-organism descriptive projections, but the zebrafish feature join is much narrower than its abundance join, so cross-organism stability must be evaluated through the change in the modeled-complement residual target, not through the feature effects alone.
\paragraph{Feature distributions.} The joined feature surfaces are sparse and uneven. In zebrafish, complex membership has mean $0.3104$ on $2242$ joined rows, domain count has mean $0.8564$ with maximum $48$, PTM density has mean $0.001218$ with $211$ nonzero rows, and transmembrane count has mean $0.7293$ with maximum $18$. In human, complex membership has mean $0.3627$ on $18335$ rows, domain count has mean $1.0713$ with maximum $285$, PTM density has mean $0.006474$ with $9210$ nonzero rows, and transmembrane count has mean $1.0722$ with maximum $38$. In yeast, complex membership has mean $0.3506$ on $5682$ rows, domain count has mean $0.4074$ with maximum $11$, PTM density has mean $0.002775$ with $2282$ nonzero rows, and transmembrane count has mean $1.0007$ with maximum $23$. These distributions justify treating the annotations as coarse finite readouts, not as complete measurements of molecular state.
\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability.} The first diagnostic asks whether the feature join preserves the modeled-complement residual fraction $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ from the broader abundance-CDS surface. The reported comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Danio} &= |0.7361678489976374 - 0.874628721049266| = 0.1384608720516286,\\
\Delta_{Homo} &= |0.9188293375787641 - 0.9208949355679455| = 0.002065597989181489,\\
\Delta_{Saccharomyces} &= |0.21235197749007645 - 0.21173219572706703| = 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human and yeast feature joins agree with the broader complement reference within the $0.02$ tolerance, while zebrafish does not. This is the central finite-row obstruction: at least one organism changes the residual target when reviewed UniProt or STRING feature availability is imposed. Consequently, the calculation may report organism-specific descriptive absorption values, but it cannot close a stable cross-organism $H$ candidate.
\paragraph{Per-candidate absorption.} The computed feature absorptions are small. In zebrafish, complex membership absorbs $0.00039566384726157463$, domain count absorbs $0.0008268196005307971$, PTM density absorbs $0.014211240113472575$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.001369142724157357$. In human, the corresponding values are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$. In yeast, they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$. The largest finite entries are the PTM-density absorptions in zebrafish and human, both near $1.4\%$ of the modeled-complement residual surface. Even these entries remain descriptive projections of annotation columns; they are not mechanism tests.
\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.} The feature candidates were compared with degree-of-freedom matched null columns using $200$ trials per candidate and Bonferroni-adjusted thresholds. In zebrafish, only PTM density exceeds its threshold: absorbed $0.014211240113472575$ against threshold $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95\%$ level $0.001788151234180097$. The other zebrafish candidates do not exceed their thresholds: complex membership threshold $0.00275854608923269$, domain-count threshold $0.005123577637652884$, and transmembrane-count threshold $0.0034780137133507$. In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their respective thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, while domain count does not exceed $0.0005934431422432757$. In yeast, none of the four candidates exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: $0.001015254045679051$ for complex membership, $0.0011639234792592378$ for domain count, $0.001713645678402473$ for PTM density, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for transmembrane count. The module-level classification is therefore mixed: human has three excess candidates, zebrafish has one PTM-density excess candidate but a shifted residual target, and yeast has no excess feature candidate.
\paragraph{Comparison with measured translation efficiency and turnover contacts.} The same residual family has separate bounded contacts to measured translation efficiency and yeast protein turnover. In the measured-TE contact, joined row counts are $13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, each with the same $24$-column control block and rank $23$. The absorbed fractions by measured TE are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively. The yeast turnover contact uses $3640$ joined turnover-abundance-CDS rows, preserves the complement reference within $0.011538255214205373$, and absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$ through half-life. These adjacent contacts show that the protein-feature calculation is one member of a bounded family of $H$-candidate projections; none of them by itself converts a residual coordinate into a causal translation, degradation, or protein-state mechanism.
\paragraph{Cross-organism context.} The broader organism-specific meta table contains $16$ assembled organism rows, of which $12$ have complete feature rows for the tested summaries. Its permutation checks do not establish a resolved domain or continuous predictor rule. The $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus domain test has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation probability $0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials. Against GC3, Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation probability $0.35264735264735264$; against log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with probability $0.913086913086913$; and against log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with probability $0.8621378621378621$. The dominant-coordinate contingency has Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, but its right-tail permutation probability is $0.5814185814185814$ on $12$ rows. These finite-row checks reinforce the same boundary: the available organism panel records heterogeneity, not a universal biological law.
\paragraph{Local conclusion.} The finite calculation supports a narrow statement: on the fetched protein-feature joins, several organism-specific annotation columns absorb small positive fractions of the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual, and some of those fractions exceed degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds. The stable cross-organism $H$ claim is not licensed, because the zebrafish feature join changes the modeled-complement target by $0.1384608720516286$ relative to the broader abundance-CDS reference, and the candidate pattern is not consistent across organisms. The appropriate readback is therefore a bounded descriptive table: PTM density is the clearest finite candidate in zebrafish and human, human also shows complex-membership and transmembrane excess, and yeast shows no Bonferroni-excess protein-feature absorption.
\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.} Nothing in this chapter proves translation realization, ribosome occupancy, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, phenotype, or universality. The feature columns are reviewed-UniProt or STRING-derived annotations with sparse and coarse coverage; the absorptions are projections under a fixed control block, not interventions, mediation tests, or mechanistic assays. A later claim about translation would require a matched ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact with matched mRNA controls. A later claim about folding, localization, function, physical admissibility, or phenotype would require a separate layer-matched measurement and its own controlled statistic. The present record stops at finite protein-feature absorption of a residual abundance component.
