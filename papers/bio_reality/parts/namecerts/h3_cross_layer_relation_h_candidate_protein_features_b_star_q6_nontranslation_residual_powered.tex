\subsection{Protein-feature contacts for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-contact}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coding-sequence residual that remains after modeled translation-adaptation projection is descriptively absorbed by fetchable protein-feature readouts.  The finite carrier is a CDS-by-organism table with nine residual coordinates, the standard sequence controls, the modeled-complement target, and four one-column protein-feature candidates.  The nine residual coordinates are $K_{\mathrm{AAA}}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{\mathrm{AGR}}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{\mathrm{AUA}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{CUN}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUR}}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{\mathrm{UUA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UUG}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{AGY}}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{\mathrm{UCA}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{UCG}}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{\mathrm{ACR}\,\mathrm{vs}\,\mathrm{ACY}}$, and $f3_{\mathrm{stress}}$.  The control design contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $\mathrm{GC3}$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism reported here the control matrix has 24 columns and rank 23.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature availability.}
The feature contacts are reviewed-UniProt and STRING-derived summaries for complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count readouts.  Each candidate contributes one feature column, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for every candidate.  The joined surfaces are not uniform across organisms.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, 3180 feature records reduce the 14410 CDS-joined rows to 2242 feature-joined rows.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, 18842 feature records reduce the 19053 CDS-joined rows to 18335 feature-joined rows.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, 6156 feature records reduce the 5815 CDS-joined rows to 5682 feature-joined rows.  This makes the protein-feature test a feature-availability contact, not a stable full-panel claim.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability check.}
The residual target used by the feature projection was checked against the full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The defining scalar is the modeled-complement fraction $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=\|P_{Q\perp T\mathrm{mod}}\|^2/\|P_Q\|^2$ on the relevant join.  The feature join changes this scalar materially in zebrafish but not in human or yeast:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{Danio}} &= |0.7361678489976374-0.874628721049266| = 0.1384608720516286,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{Homo}} &= |0.9188293375787641-0.9208949355679455| = 0.002065597989181489,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{Saccharomyces}} &= |0.21235197749007645-0.21173219572706703| = 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the stated tolerance $0.02$, the human and yeast joins match the complement reference, while the zebrafish join does not.  The failure is not a numerical exception; it is a substantive row-set warning.  Sparse feature coverage can alter the residual target before the feature candidate is even tested.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature summaries show why the candidate layer must be treated cautiously.  In zebrafish, the 2242-row join has complex membership mean $0.31043710972346117$ with 696 nonzero rows, domain-count mean $0.856378233719893$ with 847 nonzero rows, PTM-density mean $0.0012176949624096891$ with 211 nonzero rows, and transmembrane-count mean $0.7292595896520964$ with 443 nonzero rows.  In human, the 18335-row join has complex membership mean $0.3626943005181347$ with 6650 nonzero rows, domain-count mean $1.071284428688301$ with 8090 nonzero rows, PTM-density mean $0.006473867757948575$ with 9210 nonzero rows, and transmembrane-count mean $1.0721570766293973$ with 4792 nonzero rows.  In yeast, the 5682-row join has complex membership mean $0.3505807814149947$ with 1992 nonzero rows, domain-count mean $0.4074269623372052$ with 1574 nonzero rows, PTM-density mean $0.002774535811043876$ with 2282 nonzero rows, and transmembrane-count mean $1.0007039774727209$ with 1173 nonzero rows.  These are annotation readouts with organism-specific sparsity and scale; they are not direct measurements of mechanism.

\paragraph{Absorption values.}
For each feature candidate, the statistic reported is descriptive absorption of the modeled-complement residual.  In zebrafish the absorption values are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  In human the corresponding values are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$.  In yeast they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  Thus the largest reported absorptions are PTM density in human and zebrafish, both near $1.5\times 10^{-2}$, while yeast remains below $7\times 10^{-4}$ for all four candidates.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
The finite-row test includes a 200-trial degree-of-freedom-matched null and Bonferroni comparison for every organism-candidate pair.  In zebrafish, only PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: observed absorption $0.014211240113472575$ against threshold $0.003775603188484381$, null mean $0.0004610486745564995$, and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  Complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$.  In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed thresholds: $0.0075271999877804495>0.0006605662314432479$, $0.014884825920540636>0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0015569064882873212>0.0002697448894057371$, while domain count does not exceed $0.0005934431422432757$.  In yeast, no candidate exceeds its threshold; the four thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The positive finite entries are therefore organism- and feature-specific.  Human has three Bonferroni-excess candidates on a nearly full feature join, zebrafish has one such candidate on a strongly altered feature join, and yeast has no Bonferroni-excess feature candidate.  Because zebrafish also has $\Delta_{\mathrm{Danio}}=0.1384608720516286$ in the modeled-complement stability check, the cross-organism conclusion is a guarded one: the quantities were computed and bounded, but the available protein-feature panel does not close the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual as a stable cross-organism feature explanation.  The most defensible local statement is that reviewed protein annotations can capture small descriptive fractions of the residual in particular organisms, with PTM density recurring as the largest candidate in human and zebrafish, but the feature contact is too sparse and too join-sensitive to promote this into a stable biological account.

\paragraph{Relation to other bounded contacts.}
The nearby translation and modeled-adaptation contacts help fix the scope.  On measured translation efficiency, the same nine-coordinate design used organism-specific joins of 13140 zebrafish rows, 3415 \emph{E. coli} rows, 11554 human rows, and 5128 yeast rows; measured-TE absorption of the modeled complement was $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorbed $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast.  Yeast turnover absorbed $0.0009086031889966327$ on 3640 joined rows.  These contacts show that the protein-feature readout is one bounded H-candidate among several, not a replacement for translation-efficiency, tRNA, or turnover tests.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter reports statistical projection over finite joined rows.  It does not establish causality, translation realization, ribosome mechanism, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  A $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate can be said to have a bounded association with a protein-feature candidate only within the specified row join, covariate design, and null comparison.  Any claim about translation, protein output, folding, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, or a general biological rule requires a separate layer-appropriate contact and its own controlled statistic.
