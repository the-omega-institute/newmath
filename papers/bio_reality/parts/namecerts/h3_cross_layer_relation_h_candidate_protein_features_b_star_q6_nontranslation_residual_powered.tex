\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the residual component left after the modeled translation contact can be described by coarse protein-feature coordinates.  The finite carrier is a CDS-by-protein table whose rows join real CDS codon counts, the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, the standard control block $Z$, the modeled-complement abundance residual, and a small family of reviewed protein annotations.  The nine residual coordinates are
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block has $24$ columns before rank loss and rank $23$ in each feature organism; it consists of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions computed from the same CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature coverage.}
The feature surface is deliberately not a full-CDS surface.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, $14410$ CDS rows are reported at the abundance join, but only $3180$ reviewed feature records are available and $2242$ rows enter the joined feature table.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $19053$ CDS rows are reported, $18842$ feature records are available, and $18335$ rows join.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ CDS rows are reported, $6156$ feature records are available, and $5682$ rows join.  Each organism carries four one-dimensional feature candidates: complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane count.  Thus each candidate has $K_c=1$ and residualized candidate rank $1$.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined distributions show why the feature contact is useful but also why it is not a stable universal explanation.  In \textit{D. rerio}, complex membership has mean $0.3104371097$ over $2242$ rows with $696$ nonzero rows, domain count has mean $0.8563782337$ with $847$ nonzero rows and maximum $48$, PTM density has mean $0.0012176950$ with $211$ nonzero rows and maximum $0.8014705882$, and transmembrane count has mean $0.7292595897$ with $443$ nonzero rows and maximum $18$.  In human, over $18335$ joined rows, the corresponding means are $0.3626943005$, $1.0712844287$, $0.0064738678$, and $1.0721570766$; the nonzero counts are $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$.  In yeast, over $5682$ joined rows, the means are $0.3505807814$, $0.4074269623$, $0.0027745358$, and $1.0007039775$; the nonzero counts are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$.

\paragraph{Residual target stability check.}
The protein-feature experiment is not allowed to use a changed row set as if it were the same residual target.  Let $d_{\mathrm{full}}$ denote the full abundance-CDS complement reference and $d_{\mathrm{feat}}$ the same modeled-complement quantity recomputed on the feature join.  The observed comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{D. rerio}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{full}}=0.8746287210,\quad d_{\mathrm{feat}}=0.7361678490,\quad |\Delta|=0.1384608721,\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{full}}=0.9208949356,\quad d_{\mathrm{feat}}=0.9188293376,\quad |\Delta|=0.0020655980,\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{full}}=0.2117321957,\quad d_{\mathrm{feat}}=0.2123519775,\quad |\Delta|=0.0006197818.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only human and yeast match the full-join complement reference within $0.02$.  The zebrafish feature join shifts the target by about $0.13846$, so the cross-organism conclusion cannot be promoted to a stable $H$-closure claim.  The correct reading is narrower: the descriptive projections were computed on their actual finite joins, and coverage can move the target being projected.

\paragraph{Per-candidate absorption.}
On the joined feature rows, the largest single candidate absorptions are small in absolute terms.  The computed absorbed fractions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{D. rerio}:&\quad complex=0.0003956638,\ domain=0.0008268196,\ PTM=0.0142112401,\ TM=0.0013691427,\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:&\quad complex=0.0075272000,\ domain=0.0000114055,\ PTM=0.0148848259,\ TM=0.0015569065,\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:&\quad complex=0.0006921218,\ domain=0.0000702970,\ PTM=0.0005759337,\ TM=0.0003174149.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest numerical values are the PTM-density projections in zebrafish and human, near $1.4\%$ of the modeled-complement residual energy on the relevant feature joins.  Human complex membership is also visible at about $0.75\%$.  Yeast shows no comparable feature absorption in this four-candidate panel.

\paragraph{Null comparison and multiplicity.}
Each candidate was compared against a degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials and Bonferroni correction across the tested feature candidates.  In zebrafish, only PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: absorbed fraction $0.0142112401$ against threshold $0.0037756032$, with null mean $0.0004610487$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0017881512$.  The other zebrafish candidates remain below their thresholds: complex membership $0.0003956638$ below $0.0027585461$, domain count $0.0008268196$ below $0.0051235776$, and transmembrane count $0.0013691427$ below $0.0034780137$.

\paragraph{Human and yeast null results.}
In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their candidate-specific Bonferroni thresholds: complex membership $0.0075272000$ against $0.0006605662$, PTM density $0.0148848259$ against $0.0004209489$, and transmembrane count $0.0015569065$ against $0.0002697449$.  Domain count does not exceed threshold: $0.0000114055$ against $0.0005934431$.  In yeast, none of the four candidates exceeds its threshold: complex membership $0.0006921218$ below $0.0010152540$, domain count $0.0000702970$ below $0.0011639235$, PTM density $0.0005759337$ below $0.0017136457$, and transmembrane count $0.0003174149$ below $0.0012579936$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
The protein-feature contact is downstream of, and distinct from, a measured translation-efficiency contact.  In the measured-TE experiment, the all-readout joins contain $13140$ zebrafish rows, $3415$ \textit{E. coli} rows, $11554$ human rows, and $5128$ yeast rows, each again with control rank $23$.  Measured TE absorbs only small fractions of the modeled-complement residual: $0.0175942900$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ in human, and $0.0002762784$ in yeast.  These values keep the feature experiment in the intended residual regime: the feature panel asks about the component not already described by the modeled translation candidate, not about direct translation survival itself.

\paragraph{Relation to turnover and organism-level heterogeneity.}
The yeast turnover contact gives an additional negative comparison.  On $3640$ joined yeast protein half-life rows, with a reported abundance join hit rate $0.8818572657$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419$, turnover absorbs only $0.0009086032$ of the modeled-complement residual, while the modeled-complement target remains close to the full yeast reference: $d_{\mathrm{full}}=0.2117321957$, $d_{\mathrm{turnover}}=0.2232704509$, and $|\Delta|=0.0115382552$.  At the organism-summary level, the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814$ over $1000$ permutations and $n=12$ complete organisms; $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has Pearson $r=-0.3062616500$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.3526473526$, versus log protein count has $r=-0.0336281158$ with $p=0.9130869131$, and versus log tRNA-gene count has $r=0.0602940521$ with $p=0.8621378621$.  These finite summaries are consistent with heterogeneous organism-specific behavior rather than a resolved domain-wide rule.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite result is therefore a bounded protein-feature absorption statement.  Human has three Bonferroni-positive feature candidates, zebrafish has one, and yeast has none, but the zebrafish feature join changes the modeled-complement target enough to block a stable cross-organism $H$ claim.  The scientifically admissible statement is that reviewed protein annotations can absorb small portions of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual on particular finite joins, with PTM density the clearest repeated candidate in this panel.  This does not establish translation realization, protein abundance causation, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such higher-layer interpretation requires its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled finite statistic.
