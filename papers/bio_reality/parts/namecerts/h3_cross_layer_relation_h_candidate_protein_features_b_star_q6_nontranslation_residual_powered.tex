\subsection{Protein-feature readbacks for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioq6-protein-feature-readbacks}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation component is compressed by coarse protein-feature annotations. The tested coordinate family has nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. The control matrix has 24 columns and rank 23 in each organism, with controls consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors. The feature candidates are single-column readouts for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-count, so each candidate has $K_c=1$ and residualized rank $1$ on the joined surface.

\paragraph{Joined rows and coverage.}
The feature test is powered as a finite joined table, but the table is not equally stable across organisms. In \emph{Danio rerio}, 3180 reviewed feature records join to 2242 CDS rows out of 14410 reported CDS-abundance joins. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, 18842 feature records join to 18335 rows out of 19053 reported CDS-abundance joins. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, 6156 feature records join to 5682 rows out of 5815 reported CDS-abundance joins. Thus the human and yeast surfaces nearly preserve the abundance-CDS join, whereas the zebrafish feature surface is much sparser. The feature distributions also show the intended coarse scale: for zebrafish the joined PTM-density mean is $0.0012176949624096891$ with 211 nonzero rows, while human has mean $0.006473867757948575$ with 9210 nonzero rows, and yeast has mean $0.002774535811043876$ with 2282 nonzero rows. Domain counts, complex membership, and transmembrane counts are likewise sparse or low-rank summaries rather than direct measurements of folding, activity, localization mechanism, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
Let $d_{model}$ denote the remaining modeled-translation-complement fraction on the feature join. The feature join gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{model}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.7361678489976374,\\
 d_{model}(\mathrm{Homo}) &= 0.9188293375787641,\\
 d_{model}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces}) &= 0.21235197749007645.
\end{aligned}
$$
Against the full abundance-CDS complement references, the absolute differences are $0.1384608720516286$ for zebrafish, $0.002065597989181489$ for human, and $0.0006197817630094193$ for yeast. The declared $0.02$ stability tolerance is therefore met in human and yeast but not in zebrafish. This matters because the tested object is not merely a feature association; it is an association with the modeled-complement residual after the join has selected its rows. The zebrafish feature availability changes that residual target enough that the cross-organism protein-feature interpretation must stay descriptive.

\paragraph{Feature absorption values.}
The largest single-candidate absorptions are small on the absolute scale. In zebrafish, PTM density absorbs $0.014211240113472575$, transmembrane count absorbs $0.001369142724157357$, domain count absorbs $0.0008268196005307971$, and complex membership absorbs $0.00039566384726157463$. In human, PTM density absorbs $0.014884825920540636$, complex membership absorbs $0.0075271999877804495$, transmembrane count absorbs $0.0015569064882873212$, and domain count absorbs $0.000011405527940317433$. In yeast, complex membership absorbs $0.00069212177545487$, PTM density absorbs $0.0005759336988655581$, transmembrane count absorbs $0.00031741485098156394$, and domain count absorbs $0.00007029698692511431$. The repeated PTM-density signal in zebrafish and human is the strongest finite-row pattern, but it remains a projection of a residual vector onto a sparse annotation column.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses 200 trials per organism-candidate pair and a Bonferroni threshold for the candidate set. In zebrafish, only PTM density exceeds its threshold: absorbed $0.014211240113472575$ versus threshold $0.003775603188484381$ and null p95 $0.001788151234180097$. Complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds. In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds: complex membership has absorbed $0.0075271999877804495$ versus threshold $0.0006605662314432479$; PTM density has absorbed $0.014884825920540636$ versus threshold $0.00042094892238120357$; transmembrane count has absorbed $0.0015569064882873212$ versus threshold $0.0002697448894057371$. Human domain count does not exceed its threshold. In yeast, no candidate exceeds the Bonferroni threshold; the largest yeast absorption, complex membership at $0.00069212177545487$, remains below its threshold $0.001015254045679051$ and near the null p95 $0.0006332751600186105$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency rows.}
The measured translation-efficiency contact is a separate bounded readout. On the measured-TE joins, the remaining modeled-complement fractions are $0.8424312412078521$ for zebrafish, $0.6628211177352172$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.8566190269974241$ for human, and $0.22386566043976477$ for yeast. The measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively. These numbers show that translation-efficiency availability and feature availability are different row selections and different biological readout layers. A protein-feature absorption entry is therefore not a translation-efficiency survival entry, and a translation-efficiency absorption entry is not a protein-feature mechanism.

\paragraph{Organism-level context.}
The wider organism-specific summary remains exploratory. In the cross-organism meta table, the $M^{QTP}$ fraction tests use 12 complete-feature organisms. The domain test has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $0.4155844155844156$ across 1000 trials. Continuous tests give Pearson $r=-0.3062616500469447$ for $M^{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 with two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ versus log proteome size with value $0.913086913086913$, and $r=0.06029405206037823$ versus log tRNA gene count with value $0.8621378621378621$. The dominant-coordinate contingency has chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$. These organism-level rows do not resolve a stable domain or continuous covariate explanation for the protein-feature pattern.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite data support a bounded descriptive statement: coarse protein-feature columns can absorb a small part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement residual, most visibly through PTM density in zebrafish and human and through several human feature columns under the degree-of-freedom null. The same data do not support a stable cross-organism closure of the non-translation residual, because zebrafish feature coverage changes the residual target beyond the declared tolerance and yeast has no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate. The chapter therefore records a computed but non-promoted contact: the residual can be projected onto feature annotations, but the projection is not a causal mediator test and is not a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this record licenses a claim that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates cause translation output, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, mechanism, or universality. A measured translation claim requires its own same-row translation-efficiency or ribosome-profiling contact with matched mRNA controls. A protein-output claim requires a matched protein-abundance contact. Folding, stability, localization, interaction, function, phenotype, and mechanism each require layer-appropriate observations and their own controlled statistics. The present result is only a finite projection of residual coordinates onto sparse protein-feature annotations under the stated controls.
