\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{subsec:bior-reality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This chapter records a bounded protein-feature test for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates after the modeled-translation complement has already been formed.  The finite carrier is the joined surface of coding-sequence residual coordinates, organism-matched protein identifiers, and fetchable UniProt or STRING-derived feature columns.  The coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{Q6}=(&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ f3\_stress),
\end{aligned}
$$
so the coordinate count is $9$.  The tested feature list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H=(complex\_member,\ domain\_count,\ ptm\_density,\ tm\_count),
\end{aligned}
$$
with one fitted feature direction for each candidate in each organism, hence $K_c=1$ and $rank(H_c)=1$ for every candidate column actually tested.  The control matrix contains the intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined as the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The reported control-column count is $24$ and the control rank is $23$ in each organismal joined surface.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature coverage.}
The experiment used three organismal panels.  For \textit{Danio rerio}, $14410$ coding sequences were reported at the abundance/CDS join, $3180$ reviewed feature records were available, and $2242$ rows remained in the feature join.  For \textit{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding counts were $19053$, $18842$, and $18335$.  For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, they were $5815$, $6156$, and $5682$.  Thus the finite readout is not a uniform full-CDS surface: the zebrafish feature join keeps only $2242/14410$ of the reported CDS-abundance rows, whereas the human and yeast joins are much closer to their corresponding feature inventories.  This coverage asymmetry is part of the mathematical object being reported, not a side condition that can be ignored.

The joined feature distributions also differ sharply by organism.  In \textit{D. rerio}, the joined $domain\_count$ mean is $0.856378233719893$ with $847$ nonzero rows among $2242$, the joined $ptm\_density$ mean is $0.0012176949624096891$ with $211$ nonzero rows, and the joined $tm\_count$ mean is $0.7292595896520964$ with $443$ nonzero rows.  In human, the joined $domain\_count$ mean is $1.071284428688301$ with $8090$ nonzero rows among $18335$, $ptm\_density$ has mean $0.006473867757948575$ with $9210$ nonzero rows, and $tm\_count$ has mean $1.0721570766293973$ with $4792$ nonzero rows.  In yeast, the joined means are $0.4074269623372052$ for domains, $0.002774535811043876$ for PTM density, and $1.0007039774727209$ for transmembrane count, with nonzero counts $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ out of $5682$ rows.  These numbers show that the feature matrix is a sparse annotation surface, especially for PTM density in zebrafish and for domain counts in yeast.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability check.}
The protein-feature test is applied to the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual, but that residual must be checked on the same rows used by the feature matrix.  The full abundance/CDS complement references are $0.874628721049266$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, and $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast.  On the feature joins, the recomputed modeled-complement ratios are
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{feature}(D.\ rerio)&=0.7361678489976374,\\
d_{feature}(H.\ sapiens)&=0.9188293375787641,\\
d_{feature}(S.\ cerevisiae)&=0.21235197749007645.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute shifts from the full-join references are $0.1384608720516286$, $0.002065597989181489$, and $0.0006197817630094193$, respectively.  With the declared tolerance $0.02$, human and yeast preserve the complement target, but zebrafish does not.  Consequently the cross-organism feature result cannot be read as a stable common $H$-closure: in zebrafish the available protein-feature rows change the residual target itself.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
Each one-dimensional candidate is compared against a degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials and a Bonferroni-corrected threshold.  In zebrafish, the Bonferroni thresholds are $0.00275854608923269$ for complex membership, $0.005123577637652884$ for domain count, $0.003775603188484381$ for PTM density, and $0.0034780137133507$ for transmembrane count.  Only the PTM-density absorption exceeds its threshold.  In human, the thresholds are $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.0005934431422432757$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$ for the same four candidates; complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed threshold, while domain count does not.  In yeast, the thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$, and no candidate exceeds threshold.  The module-level classification is therefore not a single conserved feature module: zebrafish has a PTM-density excess on an unstable feature join, human has three excess columns on a stable feature join, and yeast has no Bonferroni excess on a stable feature join.

\paragraph{Cross-organism context.}
The broader organism-specificity diagnostics do not repair this instability.  In the cross-organism table with $12$ complete feature organisms, the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency test reports $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and a right-tail permutation value $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The continuous comparisons of $M_{QTP}$ fraction give $p=0.35264735264735264$ against $GC3$ with $r=-0.3062616500469447$, $p=0.913086913086913$ against $\log(n\_proteins)$ with $r=-0.033628115789504955$, and $p=0.8621378621378621$ against $\log(trna\_gene\_count)$ with $r=0.06029405206037823$.  The domain comparison of $M_{QTP}$ fraction has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These finite-row diagnostics are compatible with heterogeneous organism-specific behavior, but they do not supply a phylogenetically corrected or mechanism-level explanation for the protein-feature absorptions.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The ready result is a bounded negative-to-partial record.  The protein-feature columns were parsed, joined, projected, and compared against finite null surfaces.  Human shows Bonferroni excess for complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count; zebrafish shows PTM-density excess but fails the modeled-complement stability check; yeast shows no feature excess.  Therefore the chapter licenses the following statement and no stronger one: fetchable protein-feature annotations can absorb small fractions of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement residual on some organism-specific joins, but the finite evidence does not establish a stable cross-organism $H$ explanation of the non-translation residual.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation claim follows from this protein-feature projection.  The feature matrix is downstream annotation data, not ribosome occupancy, not translation efficiency, and not a matched mRNA-controlled translation readout.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, phenotype, causation, or universal biological law follows from these absorbed fractions.  A claim at any of those layers would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite rows and its own controlled statistic.  Here the only supported content is the finite descriptive projection of the modeled-complement residual onto sparse protein-feature columns under the stated controls.
