\subsection{Protein-feature projections for the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ complement}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-residual-complement}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a small set of fetchable protein-feature annotations accounts for the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ component that remains after the codon-level controls and the modeled translation-adaptation component have been separated.  The readout surface is finite: three organisms enter the protein-feature panel, namely \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Homo sapiens}, and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The residual coordinate family has nine entries,
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every organism in this feature panel.  Its named controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3 from the same CDS counts, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The four tested protein-feature coordinates are complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.

\paragraph{Join geometry.}
The feature contact is not the same finite population as the full abundance-CDS contact.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, the feature run has $3180$ feature records and joins $2242$ rows, while the reported CDS-abundance join has $14410$ rows.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records give $18335$ joined rows against a reported $19053$ CDS-abundance join.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records give $5682$ joined rows against a reported $5815$ CDS-abundance join.  The modeled-complement self-check therefore has to be read organism by organism.  Its distances are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.7361678489976374, & d_{\mathrm{full}}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.874628721049266, \\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9188293375787641, & d_{\mathrm{full}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9208949355679455, \\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21235197749007645, & d_{\mathrm{full}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute discrepancies are $0.1384608720516286$, $0.002065597989181489$, and $0.0006197817630094193$, respectively.  Thus the human and yeast feature joins preserve the full-join complement to within the $0.02$ check, while the zebrafish feature join does not.  The zebrafish result is consequently a feature-coverage readout on a changed row population rather than a stable full-contact statement.

\paragraph{Observed feature absorptions.}
The descriptive absorption values are small except for a few post-translational or coarse cellular-organization annotations.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, complex membership absorbs $0.00039566384726157463$, domain count absorbs $0.0008268196005307971$, PTM density absorbs $0.014211240113472575$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.001369142724157357$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding values are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  Each candidate has $K_c=1$ and residualized rank $1$ in the fitted feature coordinate, so the comparison is a one-degree-of-freedom feature projection rather than a high-dimensional feature manifold.

\paragraph{Null comparison and finite calls.}
The degree-of-freedom matched nulls use $200$ trials per organism-feature cell.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, only PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: its observed absorption is $0.014211240113472575$, while the Bonferroni threshold is $0.003775603188484381$, the null mean is $0.0004610486745564995$, and the null $95\%$ point is $0.001788151234180097$.  Complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their respective thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed threshold: complex membership has absorption $0.0075271999877804495$ against threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, PTM density has $0.014884825920540636$ against $0.00042094892238120357$, and transmembrane count has $0.0015569064882873212$ against $0.0002697448894057371$.  Human domain count does not exceed threshold, with absorption $0.000011405527940317433$ below $0.0005934431422432757$.  In yeast, no candidate exceeds threshold; the largest observed absorption among the four candidates is complex membership at $0.00069212177545487$, below its Bonferroni threshold $0.001015254045679051$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation readouts.}
The protein-feature projection is downstream of a separate measured-translation-efficiency contact and should not be read as a translation-efficiency result.  In the measured-TE run, the joined row counts are $13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, again with control-column count $24$ and control rank $23$ in each organism.  The measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  These values are descriptive projections of the modeled-complement abundance residual onto residualized measured TE; they do not license treating the protein-feature absorptions above as ribosome occupancy, translation mechanism, or translation-layer survival.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact is likewise bounded: it joins $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ rows for zebrafish, human, and yeast, with absorbed fractions $0.020817102845637092$, $0.00005426229300865148$, and $0.001530747712165342$.  Those numbers record a modeled codon-adaptation readout, not direct ribosome dynamics.

\paragraph{Turnover comparison.}
The yeast turnover contact gives an additional scale check for the non-translation residual.  It joins $3640$ yeast proteins, with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2232704509412724$ against the full-join reference $0.21173219572706703$, an absolute difference of $0.011538255214205373$, within the $0.02$ check.  The turnover absorption is $0.0009086031889966327$ and the combined distance is $0.22306503721327192$.  This is comparable in order to the yeast protein-feature absorptions and supports the local conclusion that, for yeast, the remaining residual is only weakly accounted for by these fetchable feature and turnover contacts.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
The admissible conclusion is deliberately narrow.  The finite data show that selected coarse protein-feature annotations absorb a small part of the residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement signal in particular organism-feature joins, with PTM density visible in zebrafish and human, complex membership and transmembrane count visible in human, and no Bonferroni-surviving feature in yeast.  Because the zebrafish feature join substantially changes the residual target, and because the surviving candidates are sparse reviewed-UniProt or STRING-derived annotations, this is not a stable cross-organism closure of the residual.  No claim about translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from these entries.  Any such claim would require its own layer-matched finite contact and its own controlled statistic.  The present chapter records a bounded protein-feature projection over a finite joined surface, not a causal explanation of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual.
