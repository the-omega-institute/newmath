\subsection{Protein-feature tests for the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-protein-features-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Question.}
The $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis supplies nine synonymous residual coordinates, namely $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The present contact asks whether the part of the abundance-associated $Q6$ signal left after a modeled-translation readout is better described by coarse protein features.  The feature side is deliberately narrow: reviewed UniProt or STRING-derived annotations give four one-column candidates, complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.  These are not treated as mechanistic explanations.  They are tested as finite readout coordinates against the modeled-complement residual under the same sequence control family used elsewhere: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline supported by the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The feature panel is rank-admissible at the control layer in each included organism: the design reports $24$ control columns and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ for Danio rerio, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  The joined feature surfaces are uneven.  Danio rerio has $14410$ CDS rows in the abundance-aligned codon table, $3180$ fetched feature records, and only $2242$ joined rows.  Homo sapiens has $19053$ CDS rows, $18842$ feature records, and $18335$ joined rows.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5815$ CDS rows, $6156$ feature records, and $5682$ joined rows.  Thus the same statistical question is evaluated on three different availability regimes, with Danio rerio carrying the strongest coverage restriction.

\paragraph{Reference-residual stability.}
Before interpreting any feature coordinate, the modeled-complement residual itself must remain stable after restricting to the feature join.  The full abundance-CDS reference values and the feature-join values disagree substantially in Danio rerio but not in the other two organisms:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.874628721049266, & d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{Danio}) &= 0.7361678489976374, & |\Delta| &= 0.1384608720516286,\\
 d_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathrm{Homo}) &= 0.9208949355679455, & d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{Homo}) &= 0.9188293375787641, & |\Delta| &= 0.002065597989181489,\\
 d_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces}) &= 0.21173219572706703, & d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{Saccharomyces}) &= 0.21235197749007645, & |\Delta| &= 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using a $0.02$ stability tolerance, the Homo sapiens and Saccharomyces cerevisiae feature joins preserve the modeled-complement target, while Danio rerio does not.  This prevents a stable cross-organism protein-feature explanation of the residual, even though all three organism-specific projections can still be reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined annotations are sparse and coarse.  In Danio rerio, complex membership is nonzero for $696$ of $2242$ joined rows, domain count for $847$ rows, PTM density for $211$ rows, and transmembrane count for $443$ rows.  In Homo sapiens, the corresponding nonzero counts are $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ out of $18335$.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, they are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ out of $5682$.  The feature scales also differ: Homo sapiens has a maximum domain count of $285$, whereas Saccharomyces cerevisiae has a maximum of $11$ and Danio rerio a maximum of $48$ on the joined rows.  PTM density has joined means $0.0012176949624096891$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $0.002774535811043876$ in Danio rerio, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae, respectively.  These summaries make the contact a sparse annotation test rather than a uniform molecular-state assay.

\paragraph{Absorption results.}
For each feature candidate $c$, the reported scalar is the fraction of the modeled-complement residual absorbed after projecting onto the residualized feature column.  All candidate ranks are one.  Danio rerio shows its largest descriptive absorption for PTM density, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{\mathrm{Danio}}(\mathrm{complex}) &= 0.00039566384726157463,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Danio}}(\mathrm{domain}) &= 0.0008268196005307971,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Danio}}(\mathrm{PTM}) &= 0.014211240113472575,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Danio}}(\mathrm{TM}) &= 0.001369142724157357.
\end{aligned}
$$
Homo sapiens has two larger descriptive entries and one nearly zero domain-count entry:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{\mathrm{Homo}}(\mathrm{complex}) &= 0.0075271999877804495,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Homo}}(\mathrm{domain}) &= 0.000011405527940317433,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Homo}}(\mathrm{PTM}) &= 0.014884825920540636,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Homo}}(\mathrm{TM}) &= 0.0015569064882873212.
\end{aligned}
$$
Saccharomyces cerevisiae has uniformly small entries:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{\mathrm{Saccharomyces}}(\mathrm{complex}) &= 0.00069212177545487,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Saccharomyces}}(\mathrm{domain}) &= 0.00007029698692511431,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Saccharomyces}}(\mathrm{PTM}) &= 0.0005759336988655581,\\
 A_{\mathrm{Saccharomyces}}(\mathrm{TM}) &= 0.00031741485098156394.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest raw feature entries therefore occur for PTM density in Danio rerio and Homo sapiens, but the Danio rerio residual target is not stable under the feature join, and the yeast panel does not reproduce the same feature pattern.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
A degree-of-freedom-matched null with $200$ trials per organism and feature supplies the local scale for interpreting the one-column projections.  Danio rerio PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: the observed absorption $0.014211240113472575$ is above $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  The other Danio rerio candidates do not exceed their thresholds.  Homo sapiens has three threshold exceedances: complex membership exceeds $0.0006605662314432479$ with observed $0.0075271999877804495$, PTM density exceeds $0.00042094892238120357$ with observed $0.014884825920540636$, and transmembrane count exceeds $0.0002697448894057371$ with observed $0.0015569064882873212$.  Homo sapiens domain count does not exceed its threshold.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae has no Bonferroni exceedance among the four candidates; its thresholds range from $0.001015254045679051$ to $0.001713645678402473$, while all observed absorptions remain below $0.0007$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation contacts.}
The feature test sits downstream of, and is weaker than, a measured translation-efficiency contact.  On measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency rows, the same $Q6$ coordinate family was evaluated with matched abundance controls and $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$ in four organisms.  The measured-TE join sizes were $13140$ for Danio rerio, $3415$ for Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, $11554$ for Homo sapiens, and $5128$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  Measured TE absorbed only $0.01759429001506974$ in Danio rerio, $0.0010982956489726373$ in Escherichia coli, $0.0028947149257963897$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  A separate measured-tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact also leaves most of the modeled-complement residual in place: the absorbed fractions are $0.020817102845637092$ in Danio rerio, $0.00005426229300865148$ in Homo sapiens, and $0.001530747712165342$ in Saccharomyces cerevisiae.  These comparison contacts support the interpretation that the protein-feature run is an exploratory residual readout, not a replacement for a translation-efficiency survival matrix.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite computation supports a bounded descriptive statement: reviewed feature annotations can absorb small fractions of the modeled-complement $B^*_{Q6}$ residual, and some one-column feature projections exceed degree-of-freedom-matched Bonferroni thresholds in particular organisms.  The result does not support a stable cross-organism protein-feature closure.  The decisive obstruction is the Danio rerio target shift, where the feature join changes $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ by $0.1384608720516286$ relative to the full abundance-CDS reference.  The secondary obstruction is biological heterogeneity: Homo sapiens has complex, PTM, and transmembrane exceedances; Danio rerio has only the PTM exceedance and an unstable target; Saccharomyces cerevisiae has no feature exceedance.  Therefore the contact is ready as a bounded negative-to-partial result: feature projections were computed, sparse annotation coverage was quantified, and no causal or universal conclusion is licensed.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No statement in this subsection promotes the feature entries to translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  A positive or Bonferroni-exceeding feature projection is only a finite statistical association on a particular joined surface.  Translation-efficiency survival requires a same-row ribosome or translation-efficiency contact with matched mRNA controls.  Protein abundance requires its own abundance readout.  Folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, and organism-level law each require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.
