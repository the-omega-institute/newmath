\subsection{Reviewed protein-feature candidates for the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{subsec:biorality-q6-protein-feature-candidates}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The readout surface is a finite CDS-by-protein panel on which nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are first formed after the sequence controls and then tested against four reviewed protein-feature candidates.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.
The control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organism, using an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The four candidate feature coordinates are complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count, each with candidate rank $K_c=1$ on the tested joins.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature availability.}
The feature joins are uneven across organisms.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, the CDS-abundance join contains $14410$ reported CDS rows, but the reviewed feature table contributes $3180$ feature records and only $2242$ joined rows.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19053$ reported CDS rows meet $18842$ reviewed feature records and yield $18335$ joined rows.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ reported CDS rows meet $6156$ reviewed feature records and yield $5682$ joined rows.  Thus the human and yeast surfaces are close to the abundance-CDS reference surface, whereas the zebrafish feature surface is a much smaller reviewed-annotation slice.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
Let $d_{model}$ denote the modeled-tAI-complement residual fraction on the feature join, and compare it with the full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The finite audit gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{model}^{Drerio} &= 0.7361678489976374, & d_{ref}^{Drerio} &= 0.874628721049266, & |\Delta| &= 0.1384608720516286,\\
 d_{model}^{Hsapiens} &= 0.9188293375787641, & d_{ref}^{Hsapiens} &= 0.9208949355679455, & |\Delta| &= 0.002065597989181489,\\
 d_{model}^{Scerevisiae} &= 0.21235197749007645, & d_{ref}^{Scerevisiae} &= 0.21173219572706703, & |\Delta| &= 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
The prespecified $0.02$ stability check is passed in human and yeast but fails in zebrafish.  The failed zebrafish comparison is not a numerical detail: it says that reviewed UniProt/STRING feature availability changes the protein-id surface on which the non-translation residual is being projected.  Consequently the experiment can report bounded descriptive absorptions, but it does not close a stable cross-organism protein-feature explanation of the residual.

\paragraph{Feature distributions on the joined panels.}
The joined feature panels are sparse and coarse.  In zebrafish, complex membership has mean $0.31043710972346117$ with $696$ nonzero rows among $2242$, domain count has mean $0.856378233719893$ with $847$ nonzero rows and maximum $48$, PTM density has mean $0.0012176949624096891$ with $211$ nonzero rows and maximum $0.8014705882352942$, and transmembrane count has mean $0.7292595896520964$ with $443$ nonzero rows and maximum $18$.  In human, the same four joined means are $0.3626943005181347$, $1.071284428688301$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $1.0721570766293973$, with nonzero counts $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ out of $18335$.  In yeast, the joined means are $0.3505807814149947$, $0.4074269623372052$, $0.002774535811043876$, and $1.0007039774727209$, with nonzero counts $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ out of $5682$.

\paragraph{Candidate absorptions.}
Each candidate is tested as a one-dimensional projection of the modeled-complement residual on the organism-specific feature join.  The observed absorption fractions are small.  For zebrafish they are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  For human they are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$ in the same order.  For yeast they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  The largest descriptive entries are therefore PTM density in zebrafish and human, but these entries remain projection statistics, not mechanistic explanations.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
The audit uses degree-of-freedom-matched null trials with Bonferroni correction.  In zebrafish, PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: the observed absorption $0.014211240113472575$ is above the threshold $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95\%$ point $0.001788151234180097$ over $200$ trials.  Zebrafish complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$.  In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, while domain count does not exceed $0.0005934431422432757$.  In yeast, no candidate exceeds its Bonferroni threshold; the four thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The finite result is therefore a bounded candidate screen rather than a closure of the residual.  Human has three Bonferroni-excess candidates on a stable feature join, zebrafish has one Bonferroni-excess PTM-density candidate on an unstable, coverage-shifted join, and yeast has no Bonferroni-excess candidate on a stable join.  This pattern is not a single shared protein-feature module.  It says that some reviewed protein annotations can absorb a small part of the modeled-complement residual on particular joined panels, while the cross-organism explanation remains open.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation-efficiency survival, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, causal mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from these feature projections.  The measured-translation-efficiency companion surface gives organism-specific TE absorptions of $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast, but the protein-feature screen does not become a translation contact by citing those numbers.  A positive higher-layer claim would require a separate layer-matched contact with the relevant rows and controls.  Here the only licensed conclusion is the finite descriptive one: reviewed UniProt/STRING features produce small, organism-dependent absorptions of the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual, and sparse feature coverage prevents a stable cross-organism $H$-candidate closure.
