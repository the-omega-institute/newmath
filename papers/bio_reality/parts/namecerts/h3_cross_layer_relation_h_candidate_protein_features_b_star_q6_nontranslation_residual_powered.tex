\subsection{Protein-feature readout for the residual complement}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-residual-complement}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether the non-translation component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual surface is absorbed by a small panel of fetched protein-feature annotations.  The carrier is a finite coding-sequence by organism table with nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, residualized against a control block containing an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  Each tested feature candidate has one degree of freedom after residualization: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.

The joined feature surface is uneven across organisms.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, $3180$ reviewed feature records yield $2242$ joined rows against $14410$ reported CDS joins.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records yield $18335$ joined rows against $19053$ reported CDS joins.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records yield $5682$ joined rows against $5815$ reported CDS joins.  In all three organisms the control design has $24$ columns and rank $23$, so the numerical test is not a rank-empty calculation; the main limitation is the biological and annotation coverage of the feature rows.

\paragraph{Target being tested.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the residualized nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ surface on the joined rows, and let $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the component remaining after the modeled translation-adaptation complement has been removed.  For each organism and each one-column feature candidate $H_c$, the computed number is the descriptive absorption fraction of $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ by the residualized feature column.  The local decision rule is not whether a candidate is biologically interesting in isolation, but whether it exceeds the degree-of-freedom-matched null after Bonferroni correction and remains stable under the row join used for the complement.

The complement target itself changes with feature availability.  The full abundance/CDS reference values for $d_{modeled}$ are $0.874628721049266$ in \emph{D. rerio}, $0.9208949355679455$ in human, and $0.21173219572706703$ in yeast.  On the feature joins these become $0.7361678489976374$, $0.9188293375787641$, and $0.21235197749007645$, respectively.  Thus the absolute discrepancies are
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{D.rerio} &= 0.1384608720516286,\\
\Delta_{human} &= 0.002065597989181489,\\
\Delta_{yeast} &= 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}$$
Only human and yeast stay within the $0.02$ complement-matching tolerance.  The zebrafish feature join changes the residual target too strongly to support a stable cross-organism protein-feature explanation.

\paragraph{Observed absorptions.}
The largest single protein-feature absorptions are small in absolute size.  In zebrafish, the four candidate absorptions are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  PTM density is the only zebrafish candidate exceeding its Bonferroni threshold, with threshold $0.003775603188484381$ and null $95\%$ point $0.001788151234180097$ from $200$ trials.  The other zebrafish candidates do not pass: complex membership has threshold $0.00275854608923269$, domain count has threshold $0.005123577637652884$, and transmembrane count has threshold $0.0034780137133507$.

In human, three of the four candidates exceed the local corrected null: complex membership absorbs $0.0075271999877804495$ against threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, PTM density absorbs $0.014884825920540636$ against threshold $0.00042094892238120357$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.0015569064882873212$ against threshold $0.0002697448894057371$.  Domain count is essentially absent as an absorber, with $0.000011405527940317433$ against threshold $0.0005934431422432757$.  These human feature signals are therefore real as finite descriptive projections, but they are not a common four-candidate module and they are not replicated as the same feature pattern across the other organisms.

In yeast, no candidate exceeds the corrected null.  The absorptions are $0.00069212177545487$ for complex membership, $0.00007029698692511431$ for domain count, $0.0005759336988655581$ for PTM density, and $0.00031741485098156394$ for transmembrane count.  The corresponding Bonferroni thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$.  Yeast is therefore a negative feature-absorption case on this candidate set, even though its row join preserves the modeled-complement target.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The finite rows also show why the result should be read as an annotation-bounded projection.  In zebrafish joined rows, PTM density has mean $0.0012176949624096891$, median $0$, nonzero count $211$ out of $2242$, and $95$th percentile $0.003048780487804878$; transmembrane count has mean $0.7292595896520964$, nonzero count $443$, and $95$th percentile $6$.  In human joined rows, PTM density is much denser, with mean $0.006473867757948575$, nonzero count $9210$ out of $18335$, and $95$th percentile $0.024291497975708502$; complex membership is nonzero for $6650$ rows, and transmembrane count is nonzero for $4792$ rows.  In yeast joined rows, complex membership is nonzero for $1992$ of $5682$ rows, PTM density is nonzero for $2282$ rows, and transmembrane count is nonzero for $1173$ rows.  The three organisms are therefore not sampling the same annotation geometry, even before biological interpretation is considered.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and turnover contacts.}
The measured translation-efficiency contact gives a useful scale check.  On the measured-TE all-readout joins, the modeled complement values are $0.8424312412078521$ in zebrafish, $0.6628211177352172$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.8566190269974241$ in human, and $0.22386566043976477$ in yeast, with measured-TE absorptions $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$.  These are descriptive projection quantities computed on matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency rows; they do not make the protein-feature signals causal or mechanistic.

The yeast turnover contact is likewise small on the joined half-life surface.  It joins $3640$ turnover proteins, reports a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and a turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, and computes $d_{modeled}=0.2232704509412724$ with turnover absorption $0.0009086031889966327$.  This scale agrees with the feature result in one important respect: yeast does not supply a strong protein-feature or turnover absorber for the remaining $B^*_{Q6}$ complement in this finite panel.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite calculation licenses a narrow statement.  Human has three corrected one-column protein-feature absorbers on a nearly full feature join, zebrafish has a PTM-density absorber on a much smaller and complement-shifting join, and yeast has no corrected absorber among the four tested candidates.  Because the zebrafish feature join changes the residual complement by $0.1384608720516286$, and because the passing candidates are not stable across organisms, this chapter does not close a cross-organism protein-feature explanation of the non-translation residual.  The appropriate classification is a bounded descriptive feature-projection result with unstable cross-organism support.

No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from these rows.  A separate layer-matched contact would be needed for each such claim.  The present chapter only records how much of the modeled-translation-complement residual is absorbed by four sparse protein-feature annotations under the stated finite controls.
