\subsection{Protein-feature tests for the non-translation residual}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-nontranslation-residual}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether the remaining $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance-side residual, after the modeled translation-adaptation component has been removed, is absorbed by four fetchable protein-feature readouts: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-helix count. The empirical object is finite: for each joined protein row, the surface contains nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, a control matrix $Z$, one modeled-complement residual target, and one scalar feature column at a time. The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$. The controls are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors. In all three organisms, the control column count is $24$ and the observed rank is $23$.

\paragraph{Finite joins.}
The feature test uses reviewed UniProt or STRING-linked protein annotations and therefore changes the row support relative to full CDS-abundance joins. In \textit{Danio rerio}, $14410$ CDS rows were reported at the abundance join, but only $3180$ feature records were available and the all-readout feature join contained $2242$ rows. In \textit{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding counts were $19053$ CDS rows, $18842$ feature records, and $18335$ joined feature rows. In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the counts were $5815$ CDS rows, $6156$ feature records, and $5682$ joined feature rows. Each feature candidate contributes one column, so $K_c=1$ and $\mathrm{rank}(H_c)=1$ for each of complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane count.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The row support is not merely smaller; it is feature-biased in an organism-dependent way. For \textit{D. rerio}, the joined feature rows have mean complex membership $0.3104371097$, mean domain count $0.8563782337$, mean PTM density $0.0012176950$, and mean transmembrane count $0.7292595897$, with nonzero counts $696$, $847$, $211$, and $443$ respectively out of $2242$. For human, the corresponding joined means are $0.3626943005$, $1.0712844287$, $0.0064738678$, and $1.0721570766$, with nonzero counts $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ out of $18335$. For yeast, the joined means are $0.3505807814$, $0.4074269623$, $0.0027745358$, and $1.0007039775$, with nonzero counts $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ out of $5682$. The support difference matters because a protein-feature explanation must be read on the rows where the feature exists, not on the larger abundance-CDS surface.

\paragraph{Modeled-complement stability check.}
Let $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ denote the norm ratio of the residual abundance component after removal of the modeled translation-adaptation direction. The feature experiment is stable only when the value recomputed on the feature join agrees with the full abundance-CDS complement reference. The three observed comparisons are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{D. rerio}:&\quad 0.8746287210\ \hbox{on }14410\hbox{ rows versus }0.7361678490\ \hbox{on }2242\hbox{ rows},\\
&\quad |\Delta|=0.1384608721,\quad \hbox{outside the }0.02\hbox{ tolerance};\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:&\quad 0.9208949356\ \hbox{on }19053\hbox{ rows versus }0.9188293376\ \hbox{on }18335\hbox{ rows},\\
&\quad |\Delta|=0.0020655980,\quad \hbox{inside the }0.02\hbox{ tolerance};\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:&\quad 0.2117321957\ \hbox{on }5815\hbox{ rows versus }0.2123519775\ \hbox{on }5682\hbox{ rows},\\
&\quad |\Delta|=0.0006197818,\quad \hbox{inside the }0.02\hbox{ tolerance}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fish feature join therefore fails the stability check. The computation can still be reported as a descriptive projection, but it cannot support a stable cross-organism residual explanation.

\paragraph{Absorption values.}
For each feature column $H_c$, the reported quantity is the fraction of the modeled-complement residual absorbed after projecting onto the residualized feature column. The finite table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{D. rerio}:&\quad complex=0.0003956638,\ domain=0.0008268196,\\
&\quad PTM=0.0142112401,\ TM=0.0013691427;\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:&\quad complex=0.0075272000,\ domain=0.0000114055,\\
&\quad PTM=0.0148848259,\ TM=0.0015569065;\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:&\quad complex=0.0006921218,\ domain=0.0000702970,\\
&\quad PTM=0.0005759337,\ TM=0.0003174149.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest observed absorptions are PTM density in human, $0.0148848259$, and PTM density in fish, $0.0142112401$. These are small descriptive fractions of the residual target, not mechanistic effects.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
Each feature column was compared against a degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials and a Bonferroni threshold. In fish, only PTM density exceeds its threshold: the PTM null mean is $0.0004610487$, the null $95\%$ value is $0.0017881512$, and the threshold is $0.0037756032$, while the observed absorption is $0.0142112401$. Fish complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds. In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds: complex membership has threshold $0.0006605662$ and observed absorption $0.0075272000$; PTM density has threshold $0.0004209489$ and observed absorption $0.0148848259$; transmembrane count has threshold $0.0002697449$ and observed absorption $0.0015569065$. Human domain count does not exceed its threshold. In yeast, none of the four feature candidates exceeds its threshold; the largest yeast observed value is complex membership at $0.0006921218$, below the Bonferroni threshold $0.0010152540$.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and turnover contacts.}
The same residual family was also tested against bounded translation-side and turnover-side readouts. Measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency absorbed $0.0175942900$ in fish, $0.0010982956$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ in human, and $0.0002762784$ in yeast. Measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI absorbed $0.0208171028$ in fish, $0.0000542623$ in human, and $0.0015307477$ in yeast. Yeast protein half-life absorbed $0.0009086032$ on $3640$ joined turnover rows. These neighboring contacts show that the feature-column result is not an isolated numerical accident, but they do not repair the instability caused by sparse or shifted feature availability. A separate localization boundary was also computed on $8696$ joined rows, with $657$ mitochondrial rows and $2000$ permutations; that contact is a different layer and does not convert the feature test into a localization or mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Cross-organism setting.}
A broader organism-specific summary was assembled over $16$ organisms, with $12$ complete-feature organisms for the $M^{QTP}$ comparisons. The dominant-coordinate contingency test used $n=12$, had Pearson $\chi^2=9.4$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785$, and a right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The $M^{QTP}$ fraction had weak and non-resolving associations with continuous covariates: versus $GC3$, $r=-0.3062616500$ with two-sided permutation value $0.3526473526$; versus log protein count, $r=-0.0336281158$ with value $0.9130869131$; versus log tRNA-gene count, $r=0.0602940521$ with value $0.8621378621$. These meta-level numbers support a cautious reading: the residual geometry is organism-specific and heterogeneous, but the present finite surfaces do not supply a phylogenetically corrected or universal rule.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The protein-feature experiment is a computed finite-row projection, not a closed biological explanation. It reports bounded evidence that some sparse protein annotations, especially PTM density in fish and human and complex or transmembrane status in human, absorb small fractions of the modeled-complement $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual beyond a degree-of-freedom matched null. The chapter-level claim remains negative with respect to stable cross-organism closure, because at least one organism, \textit{D. rerio}, changes the residual target substantially when restricted to feature rows: $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ shifts by $0.1384608721$, well beyond the $0.02$ tolerance. Thus the correct readback is: feature projections were computed and bounded, but sparse reviewed-protein annotations and row-support changes prevent a stable cross-organism $H$-candidate claim.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from this protein-feature table. No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, causal mechanism, adaptation claim, or universal biological law follows from any single absorption entry or from the Bonferroni comparisons. Matched ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, matched mRNA abundance, protein abundance, turnover, localization, structure, function, and perturbation contacts each require their own finite joined surfaces and their own controlled statistic before they can be used at their respective biological layers. The present result only says that, on the rows where coarse reviewed protein features are available, a few scalar feature columns explain small descriptive fractions of the modeled-complement residual, while the row-support instability blocks promotion to a stable cross-organism explanation.
