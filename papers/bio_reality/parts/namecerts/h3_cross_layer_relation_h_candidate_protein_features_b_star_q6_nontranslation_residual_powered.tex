\subsection{Protein-feature readbacks for the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioq6-protein-feature-readbacks}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The object tested here is not a protein mechanism.  It is a finite CDS-by-organism readout surface in which nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates are first formed after amino-acid quotient controls and then compared with four fetchable protein-feature summaries.  The coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3\text{-stress}$.  The control block has $24$ columns with rank $23$ in every organism and contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The feature candidates are $complex\_member$, $domain\_count$, $ptm\_density$, and $tm\_count$, each contributing one candidate direction.  Thus the experiment asks whether these four coarse protein annotations absorb any part of the already-isolated modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual; it does not ask whether a codon coordinate has caused a folded structure, molecular function, phenotype, or universal biological law.

\paragraph{Joined rows and rank.}
The finite joins are uneven across organisms.  For $Danio\ rerio$, the feature join contains $2242$ rows, compared with $14410$ CDS rows reported before the feature restriction and $3180$ reviewed feature records.  For $Homo\ sapiens$, the feature join contains $18335$ rows, compared with $19053$ CDS rows and $18842$ reviewed feature records.  For $Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$, the feature join contains $5682$ rows, compared with $5815$ CDS rows and $6156$ feature records.  All three feature joins keep the same control rank, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and each candidate has $K_c=1$ and residualized feature rank $1$.  The row restriction is therefore not a cosmetic detail: it changes the target on which the residual projection is measured.

\paragraph{Reference-stability check.}
The modeled-complement reference check is the main reason the result is bounded rather than promoted to a stable cross-organism claim.  On the full abundance/CDS complement reference, the modeled residual fractions are $0.874628721049266$ for $D.\ rerio$, $0.9208949355679455$ for $H.\ sapiens$, and $0.21173219572706703$ for $S.\ cerevisiae$.  On the feature-restricted joins these become $0.7361678489976374$, $0.9188293375787641$, and $0.21235197749007645$, respectively.  Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
|d_{\mathrm{feature}}-d_{\mathrm{reference}}|&=0.1384608720516286 &&\text{for }D.\ rerio,\\
|d_{\mathrm{feature}}-d_{\mathrm{reference}}|&=0.002065597989181489 &&\text{for }H.\ sapiens,\\
|d_{\mathrm{feature}}-d_{\mathrm{reference}}|&=0.0006197817630094193 &&\text{for }S.\ cerevisiae.
\end{aligned}
$$
With the declared $0.02$ tolerance, the human and yeast feature joins preserve the complement target, but the zebrafish feature join does not.  This failure is interpreted as a data-surface change caused by sparse reviewed-UniProt/STRING coverage, not as a biological negative theorem about zebrafish proteins.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature tables are sparse and coarse.  In $D.\ rerio$, $complex\_member$ is nonzero in $696$ of $2242$ joined rows, $domain\_count$ in $847$ of $2242$, $ptm\_density$ in $211$ of $2242$, and $tm\_count$ in $443$ of $2242$.  Their joined means are $0.31043710972346117$, $0.856378233719893$, $0.0012176949624096891$, and $0.7292595896520964$.  In $H.\ sapiens$, the corresponding nonzero counts are $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ out of $18335$, with means $0.3626943005181347$, $1.071284428688301$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $1.0721570766293973$.  In $S.\ cerevisiae$, the nonzero counts are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ out of $5682$, with means $0.3505807814149947$, $0.4074269623372052$, $0.002774535811043876$, and $1.0007039774727209$.  These numbers define the observational texture of the feature surface: the test is a projection onto available annotations, not a measurement of all possible protein states.

\paragraph{Absorption scores.}
The per-candidate absorption fractions are small.  In $D.\ rerio$, the four values are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  In $H.\ sapiens$, they are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$.  In $S.\ cerevisiae$, they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  The largest values occur for PTM density in zebrafish and human, but their interpretation is constrained by the sparse annotation surface and the reference-stability check.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
Each candidate was compared with a degree-of-freedom-matched null using $200$ trials and a Bonferroni threshold.  The excess calls are organism-specific.  In $D.\ rerio$, only $ptm\_density$ exceeds its threshold: its absorption $0.014211240113472575$ is above $0.003775603188484381$, while $complex\_member$, $domain\_count$, and $tm\_count$ do not exceed thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$.  In $H.\ sapiens$, $complex\_member$, $ptm\_density$, and $tm\_count$ exceed their thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, while $domain\_count$ does not exceed $0.0005934431422432757$.  In $S.\ cerevisiae$, no candidate exceeds its threshold; the thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$.  The pattern is therefore not a single conserved feature explanation.  It is a finite descriptive matrix whose positive-looking entries depend on organism and feature availability.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation efficiency.}
A separate measured-translation-efficiency contact gives the scale of the upstream translation readout.  On matched TE joins, the sample sizes are $13140$ for $D.\ rerio$, $3415$ for $E.\ coli$ K-12 MG1655, $11554$ for $H.\ sapiens$, and $5128$ for $S.\ cerevisiae$, again with control rank $23$.  The measured-TE absorption fractions are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$.  These values show that the feature experiment is downstream of a bounded translation-efficiency readout rather than a substitute for it.  A feature association is not a translation-survival entry unless the same coordinate also survives the translation-efficiency contact under matched mRNA control.

\paragraph{Organism-level context.}
The cross-organism meta table also cautions against a universal reading.  In the $12$-organism complete-feature subset, the dominant-coordinate-versus-domain contingency has Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials.  The $M_{QTP}$ fraction has weak or unsupported continuous associations in this finite table: versus GC3, $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$; versus log proteome size, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with $p=0.913086913086913$; versus log tRNA gene count, $r=0.06029405206037823$ with $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The domain variance-ratio test for $M_{QTP}$ gives $0.8889980452277374$ with right-tail $p=0.4155844155844156$.  These finite-row checks leave room for organism-specific behavior, but they do not license a phylogenetically corrected or mechanistic law.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The protein-feature packet is ready as a bounded descriptive record.  It computes a finite projection surface over three organisms, nine residual coordinates, four feature candidates, and rank-$23$ controls.  It supports the statement that reviewed feature annotations can absorb small pieces of the modeled-tAI-complement abundance residual in some organism-feature pairs, with strongest observed feature absorption for PTM density in zebrafish and human.  It does not support a stable cross-organism H closure, because the zebrafish feature restriction changes the complement target by $0.1384608720516286$, and the Bonferroni-excess pattern is not conserved across organisms.  No translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from these rows.  Each of those claims would require its own layer-matched contact and a separately computed statistic.
