\subsection{Protein-feature contacts for the non-translation Q6 residual}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-protein-features-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record tests whether coarse protein-feature annotations can account for the part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation component has been removed. The finite surface has nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, together with four one-column candidate feature readouts: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count. The control block $Z$ has $24$ columns before rank reduction and rank $23$ in every organism tested. Its columns are the intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions measured from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline given by the fraction of sense codons on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined finite rows.}
The feature readout is deliberately not a whole-proteome contact. It is the part of the CDS-by-protein surface for which reviewed protein-feature annotations can be matched to the codon-count rows. In Danio rerio, $3180$ feature records yield $2242$ joined rows out of $14410$ CDS-joined rows. In Homo sapiens, $18842$ feature records yield $18335$ joined rows out of $19053$ CDS-joined rows. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $6156$ feature records yield $5682$ joined rows out of $5815$ CDS-joined rows. Thus the same candidate family is numerically dense for human and yeast but much sparser for zebrafish, and the zebrafish fit must be read as a feature-availability surface rather than a stable full-CDS organismal surface.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
For each organism the modeled-translation-complement residual was recomputed on the feature join and compared with the full abundance-CDS join reference. The resulting finite check is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.7361678489976374,\quad d_{\mathrm{reference}}=0.874628721049266,\quad |\Delta|=0.1384608720516286,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.9188293375787641,\quad d_{\mathrm{reference}}=0.9208949355679455,\quad |\Delta|=0.002065597989181489,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\quad d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.21235197749007645,\quad d_{\mathrm{reference}}=0.21173219572706703,\quad |\Delta|=0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
The declared tolerance is $0.02$. Human and yeast pass this stability check; zebrafish does not. Consequently the feature experiment is a bounded descriptive projection, not a cross-organism closure of the non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Absorption entries.}
The candidate projections are rank-one in every organism and candidate, with $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$. The absorbed fractions are small in absolute terms, but some exceed their degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds. In zebrafish, complex membership absorbs $0.00039566384726157463$, domain count absorbs $0.0008268196005307971$, PTM density absorbs $0.014211240113472575$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.001369142724157357$. The corresponding Bonferroni thresholds are $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, $0.003775603188484381$, and $0.0034780137133507$; only PTM density exceeds its threshold. The zebrafish PTM null has mean $0.0004610486745564995$, p95 $0.001788151234180097$, and $200$ trials.

\paragraph{Human entries.}
In human, complex membership absorbs $0.0075271999877804495$, domain count absorbs $0.000011405527940317433$, PTM density absorbs $0.014884825920540636$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.0015569064882873212$. The Bonferroni thresholds are respectively $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.0005934431422432757$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$. Complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds, whereas domain count does not. The corresponding null p95 values are $0.00019370923778419034$, $0.0002238191777893124$, $0.00022866792835099184$, and $0.0001793447174515382$, each from $200$ trials. Human therefore gives the clearest finite feature contact, but it remains an annotation-level absorption of a residual component, not a mechanism.

\paragraph{Yeast entries.}
In yeast, complex membership absorbs $0.00069212177545487$, domain count absorbs $0.00007029698692511431$, PTM density absorbs $0.0005759336988655581$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.00031741485098156394$. The Bonferroni thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$. No yeast candidate exceeds threshold. The degree-of-freedom null p95 values are $0.0006332751600186105$, $0.0006659744465236375$, $0.0007361908685600422$, and $0.0004977219301829905$, again with $200$ trials per candidate. Yeast therefore keeps the non-translation complement mostly outside these four fetchable feature summaries.

\paragraph{Feature distribution check.}
The joined feature surfaces are sparse and coarse. In zebrafish, among $2242$ joined rows, complex membership has mean $0.31043710972346117$ and $696$ nonzero rows, domain count has mean $0.856378233719893$ and $847$ nonzero rows, PTM density has mean $0.0012176949624096891$ and $211$ nonzero rows, and transmembrane count has mean $0.7292595896520964$ and $443$ nonzero rows. In human, among $18335$ joined rows, the corresponding means are $0.3626943005181347$, $1.071284428688301$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $1.0721570766293973$, with nonzero counts $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$. In yeast, among $5682$ joined rows, the means are $0.3505807814149947$, $0.4074269623372052$, $0.002774535811043876$, and $1.0007039774727209$, with nonzero counts $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$. These distributions explain why the output is interpreted as a finite annotation contact rather than as a complete protein-state measurement.

\paragraph{Data provenance.}
The reviewed-feature payloads were loaded from cached UniProt searches. The zebrafish payload has $833307$ bytes and SHA256 $75d9a575851a72b30a876ce36c370153c482c7dfd3e212365212d949286ee560$; the human payload has $15404449$ bytes and SHA256 $c67efa3514fc81b99ce7b21f3c2006e8a970ad5c2c83d252b7188d0cb47467bb$; the yeast payload has $2408945$ bytes and SHA256 $ee094c02302399b283f5c92d5b0ac3fe131102a713ae8669849c179f52b10dab$. Each payload queries reviewed UniProt records with accessions, STRING cross-references, modified-residue features, domain features, transmembrane features, subunit comments, and protein length. This provenance supports a reproducible finite readout, but it also fixes the limitation: the contact is only as complete and as granular as the reviewed annotation surface.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is that protein-feature candidates were computed and bounded, with excess entries in zebrafish PTM density and in human complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count, but without a stable cross-organism H-candidate closure. The deciding obstruction is not numerical failure of every entry; it is the combination of sparse or shifted feature joins and organism-specific candidate behavior. In particular, the zebrafish feature join changes the modeled-complement target by $0.1384608720516286$, far beyond the $0.02$ stability tolerance, while yeast supplies no Bonferroni-exceeding candidate. Therefore the record licenses only this statement: coarse reviewed protein features can absorb small, bounded portions of the modeled-translation-complement Q6 residual on some finite joins.

\paragraph{No promotion beyond the measured contact.}
No translation-efficiency survival, ribosome-occupancy realization, mature protein abundance, folding statement, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, mechanism, phenotype claim, or universal biological rule follows from these entries. Those conclusions require separate layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics. The present contact is a statistical projection from residual CDS coordinates onto sparse protein-feature annotations, corrected against matched rank-one candidate nulls; it is not a causal mediator test and it is not evidence that the Q6 residual has been explained as a biological mechanism.
