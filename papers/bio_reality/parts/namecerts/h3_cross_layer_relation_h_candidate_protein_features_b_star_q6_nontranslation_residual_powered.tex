\subsection{Protein-feature absorption for the non-translation residual}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-protein-features-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection can be described by fetchable protein-feature annotations.  The tested surface is finite: three organisms, nine residual coordinates, four scalar feature candidates, and a shared control design.  The residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The feature candidates are complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.  The covariate block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; it has $24$ columns and numerical rank $23$ in every tested organism.

\paragraph{Feature joins.}
The joined matrices are uneven across organisms, which is already part of the result rather than a nuisance to hide.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, $3180$ reviewed feature records give $2242$ joined coding-sequence rows against $14410$ reported CDS joins.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records give $18335$ joined rows against $19053$ reported CDS joins.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records give $5682$ joined rows against $5815$ reported CDS joins.  Each candidate contributes one feature column, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for every organism-candidate pair.

\paragraph{Residual-target stability.}
The protein-feature projection is only interpretable as a stable cross-organism $H$ candidate if the feature join leaves the modeled-complement residual target close to the full abundance-CDS reference.  The checked distance values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\textit{Danio}) &= 0.7361678489976374, & d_{\mathrm{reference}}(\textit{Danio}) &= 0.874628721049266,\\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\textit{human}) &= 0.9188293375787641, & d_{\mathrm{reference}}(\textit{human}) &= 0.9208949355679455,\\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\textit{yeast}) &= 0.21235197749007645, & d_{\mathrm{reference}}(\textit{yeast}) &= 0.21173219572706703.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute differences are $0.1384608720516286$, $0.002065597989181489$, and $0.0006197817630094193$, respectively.  With the declared tolerance $0.02$, human and yeast preserve the residual target, while zebrafish does not.  Thus the finite table supports descriptive per-organism readback, but not a stable cross-organism closure for the protein-feature explanation.

\paragraph{Absorption by candidate.}
The largest raw feature absorption in zebrafish is PTM density, with $0.014211240113472575$ absorbed.  The other zebrafish values are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  In human, PTM density is again largest at $0.014884825920540636$, followed by complex membership at $0.0075271999877804495$, transmembrane count at $0.0015569064882873212$, and domain count at $0.000011405527940317433$.  In yeast the absorptions are smaller: $0.00069212177545487$ for complex membership, $0.00007029698692511431$ for domain count, $0.0005759336988655581$ for PTM density, and $0.00031741485098156394$ for transmembrane count.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
The finite null screen used $200$ degree-of-freedom matched trials per organism-candidate pair and a Bonferroni competition across the four candidates.  Zebrafish PTM density exceeds its threshold: absorbed $0.014211240113472575$ against Bonferroni threshold $0.003775603188484381$, null mean $0.0004610486745564995$, and null $95\%$ point $0.001788151234180097$.  The other zebrafish candidates do not exceed their thresholds.  Human has three exceeding candidates: complex membership exceeds $0.0006605662314432479$ with absorbed $0.0075271999877804495$, PTM density exceeds $0.00042094892238120357$ with absorbed $0.014884825920540636$, and transmembrane count exceeds $0.0002697448894057371$ with absorbed $0.0015569064882873212$; human domain count does not exceed.  Yeast has no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate, despite the stable residual-target join.

\paragraph{Translation and adjacent contacts.}
The same coordinate family has bounded translation-adjacent checks, but these do not license a protein-feature mechanism.  Measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency absorption is small on the same broad program: $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast.  Measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI absorption is also small: $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast.  Yeast turnover gives $3640$ joined turnover proteins, a reported join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, and turnover absorption $0.0009086031889966327$.  These contacts bound nearby readout surfaces, but none converts the protein-feature table into a causal translation, degradation, or structural account.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite readout supports the following narrow conclusion.  Reviewed UniProt and STRING-derived features can be projected against the modeled-translation-complement residual, and a few organism-candidate pairs exceed degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds.  The strongest descriptive signals are PTM density in zebrafish and human, with additional human signal in complex membership and transmembrane count.  However, sparse feature coverage changes the residual target in zebrafish, yeast has no Bonferroni-exceeding feature, and the candidate pattern is not stable across organisms.  The claim is therefore a bounded descriptive contact: protein-feature annotations can be reported as finite absorption entries, but they do not close the non-translation residual as a cross-organism $H$ explanation.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion boundary.}
No translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from these projections.  The inputs are annotations and statistical residual projections, not perturbation experiments or causal mediation assays.  A stronger claim would require a separate layer-matched contact with same-row translation readout, protein abundance or state measurement, and the corresponding controls; the present table only states what the finite feature projections absorb under the declared covariates.
