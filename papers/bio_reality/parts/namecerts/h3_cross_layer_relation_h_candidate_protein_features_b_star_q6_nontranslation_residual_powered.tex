\subsection{Sparse protein-feature contacts for the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bior-h3-q6-protein-feature-contact}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question and finite surface.}
This record asks whether the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual abundance component not accounted for by the modeled translation readout is detectably aligned with coarse protein-feature annotations.  The tested coordinate family has nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The control design has 24 columns and rank 23 in each organism: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the 20 amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The feature candidates are transmembrane-segment count, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and complex-membership indicator; each candidate enters as a one-dimensional feature block.  Thus the tested finite surface is not a protein-mechanism model.  It is a controlled projection check on four fetchable annotations against the residual component left after the modeled translation coordinate has already been separated.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature sparsity.}
The available feature rows are highly uneven across organisms.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, 3,180 reviewed UniProt-derived feature records give 2,242 joined CDS-feature rows, compared with 14,410 CDS rows in the abundance join.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, 18,842 feature records give 18,335 joined rows, close to the 19,053 CDS abundance join.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, 6,156 feature records give 5,682 joined rows, close to the 5,815 CDS abundance join.  The joined feature distributions also differ by organism: in zebrafish, the mean transmembrane count is $0.7292595897$, mean domain count is $0.8563782337$, mean PTM density is $0.0012176950$, and complex membership is nonzero for 696 of 2,242 rows.  In human, the corresponding means are $1.0721570766$, $1.0712844287$, $0.0064738678$, and 6,650 complex-member rows among 18,335.  In yeast, they are $1.0007039775$, $0.4074269623$, $0.0027745358$, and 1,992 complex-member rows among 5,682.  These row counts make the contact strongest as a finite annotation audit in human and yeast, while zebrafish is coverage-limited.

\paragraph{Residual-target stability check.}
Before interpreting any feature absorption, the modeled-complement residual on the feature join was compared with the full abundance-CDS reference residual.  The stability criterion was a difference at most $0.02$.  The human feature join passes this check, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{ref}} &= 0.9208949356,\\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}} &= 0.9188293376,\\
 |d_{\mathrm{feature}}-d_{\mathrm{ref}}| &= 0.0020655980.
\end{aligned}
$$
Yeast also passes, with $d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.2117321957$, $d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.2123519775$, and absolute difference $0.0006197818$.  Zebrafish fails the same stability check: $d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.8746287210$, $d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.7361678490$, and absolute difference $0.1384608721$.  Therefore the zebrafish feature projection is not measuring the same residual target as the full abundance-CDS reference surface; its feature readback is retained only as a sparse-coverage diagnostic.

\paragraph{Observed feature absorption.}
The largest descriptive feature absorption in zebrafish is PTM density, with absorbed fraction $0.0142112401$; the other zebrafish candidates are much smaller: transmembrane count $0.0013691427$, domain count $0.0008268196$, and complex membership $0.0003956638$.  Against the degree-of-freedom matched null, zebrafish PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold $0.0037756032$, while complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their respective thresholds $0.0027585461$, $0.0051235776$, and $0.0034780137$.

In human, PTM density is again the largest feature projection, with absorbed fraction $0.0148848259$.  Complex membership absorbs $0.0075272000$, transmembrane count absorbs $0.0015569065$, and domain count is essentially absent at $0.0000114055$.  Human complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their Bonferroni thresholds $0.0006605662$, $0.0004209489$, and $0.0002697449$, respectively; domain count does not exceed its threshold $0.0005934431$.  In yeast, all four absorbed fractions are small: complex membership $0.0006921218$, PTM density $0.0005759337$, transmembrane count $0.0003174149$, and domain count $0.0000702970$.  None exceeds its Bonferroni threshold, whose values range from $0.0010152540$ for complex membership to $0.0017136457$ for PTM density.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation-efficiency contact.}
The same coordinate family was separately evaluated against measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency with matched abundance controls.  That contact has larger organism joins for translation efficiency: 13,140 rows in zebrafish, 3,415 in \textit{E. coli}, 11,554 in human, and 5,128 in yeast, with the same rank-23 control design.  The measured-TE projection absorbed only $0.0175942900$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ in human, and $0.0002762784$ in yeast.  These numbers show that the protein-feature contact is not a substitute for translation-efficiency survival.  It is downstream and annotation-based, whereas the measured-TE contact is the bounded ribosome-profiling readout with mRNA normalization.  A feature absorption entry cannot be used to infer translation realization unless a separate translation readout survives under its own controls.

\paragraph{Cross-organism interpretation.}
The finite pattern is heterogeneous rather than a stable cross-organism feature law.  Human and zebrafish show PTM-density feature absorption above their finite null thresholds, and human also shows complex-member and transmembrane entries above threshold.  Yeast shows no above-threshold feature candidate.  The zebrafish residual target is coverage-shifted by the feature join, so its positive PTM entry is not stable evidence for the same residual surface used by the full abundance-CDS reference.  A separate organism-specificity audit over 12 complete-feature organisms likewise does not resolve a domain-level rule: the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has permutation right-tail value $0.5814185814$, and $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 has two-sided permutation value $0.3526473526$.  The feature contact therefore supports only a bounded statement: sparse reviewed annotations can absorb small portions of the modeled-translation-complement residual in some organisms, with human giving the clearest finite-row signal, but the pattern is not closed as a stable cross-organism $H$ explanation.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
The chapter licenses no causal or mechanistic conclusion.  UniProt feature annotations and STRING-style complex membership are sparse, coarse, and not perturbation assays.  The computed quantities are projection absorptions and Bonferroni-filtered finite null comparisons, not causal mediation, not folding evidence, not physical-admissibility evidence, not molecular-function evidence, and not phenotype evidence.  No protein abundance, translation realization, folded structure, physical feasibility, function, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from these feature projections alone.  Any such stronger statement would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own joined rows and controlled statistic.  If all feature candidates fail, or if a feature join changes the residual target, the correct reading is not biological irrelevance of the $B^*_{Q6}$ basis; it is that this particular annotation surface has not supplied a stable explanation of the non-translation residual.
