\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:protein-feature-absorption-bstar-q6-nontranslation-residual}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This chapter records a bounded cross-layer test for whether a residual component of the $B^*_{Q6}$ codon-coordinate surface is descriptively absorbed by fetchable protein-feature annotations.  The carrier is a finite protein-indexed table joining coding-sequence codon counts, the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, organism-matched controls, a modeled-translation-complement abundance residual, and four scalar feature candidates.  The residual-coordinate list is
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from the real CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $q$-vectors.  In all three organisms the control-column count is $24$ and the realized rank is $23$.

\paragraph{Joined organisms and feature candidates.}
The finite rows are uneven across organisms, and this matters for interpretation.  In zebrafish, $14{,}410$ CDS rows were available at the CDS-abundance boundary, but only $3{,}180$ reviewed feature records were fetched and $2{,}242$ rows joined to the feature table.  In human, $19{,}053$ CDS rows were available, $18{,}842$ feature records were fetched, and $18{,}335$ rows joined.  In budding yeast, $5{,}815$ CDS rows were available, $6{,}156$ feature records were fetched, and $5{,}682$ rows joined.  The four one-dimensional feature candidates are complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-segment count; for every organism and every candidate the candidate rank is $1$.

\paragraph{Target stability check.}
The first check asks whether the modeled-complement residual being tested on the feature join is the same finite target as the full abundance-CDS join.  The answer is mixed.  For zebrafish, the full-join complement reference has $d_{modeled}=0.874628721049266$, while the feature-join target has $d_{modeled}=0.7361678489976374$, an absolute difference of $0.1384608720516286$, so the $0.02$ stability window is not met.  For human, the corresponding values are $0.9208949355679455$ and $0.9188293375787641$, with difference $0.002065597989181489$, so the target is stable under the feature join.  For budding yeast, the corresponding values are $0.21173219572706703$ and $0.21235197749007645$, with difference $0.0006197817630094193$, also stable.  Thus the protein-feature contact is computable, but the zebrafish feature join changes the residual target enough to prevent a stable cross-organism closure.

\paragraph{Absorption estimates.}
For each organism-feature pair, the statistic is the fraction of the modeled-translation-complement residual absorbed by the feature candidate after the declared controls.  The observed absorption table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{zebrafish}:&\quad complex=0.00039566384726157463,\ domain=0.0008268196005307971,\\
&\quad ptm=0.014211240113472575,\ tm=0.001369142724157357,\\
\mathrm{human}:&\quad complex=0.0075271999877804495,\ domain=0.000011405527940317433,\\
&\quad ptm=0.014884825920540636,\ tm=0.0015569064882873212,\\
\mathrm{yeast}:&\quad complex=0.00069212177545487,\ domain=0.00007029698692511431,\\
&\quad ptm=0.0005759336988655581,\ tm=0.00031741485098156394.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest raw entries are the PTM-density absorptions in human and zebrafish, both near $1.5\%$ of the residual target.  The yeast entries are all below $0.001$, consistent with the smaller upstream non-translation residual in yeast rather than with a resolved protein-feature explanation.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
The feature candidates were compared against degree-of-freedom matched nulls with $200$ trials and a Bonferroni threshold for each organism-candidate pair.  Zebrafish PTM density exceeds its threshold: observed absorption $0.014211240113472575$ versus threshold $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  Zebrafish complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds.  In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds: complex membership has threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, PTM density has threshold $0.00042094892238120357$, and transmembrane count has threshold $0.0002697448894057371$.  Human domain count does not exceed its threshold.  In budding yeast none of the four candidates exceeds its Bonferroni threshold; for example, PTM density is bounded by threshold $0.001713645678402473$ while the observed absorption is $0.0005759336988655581$.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and other bounded contacts.}
The protein-feature result is not a translation-efficiency survival result.  In the matched measured-TE contact, the available TE joins are $13{,}140$ zebrafish CDS rows, $3{,}415$ \emph{E. coli} rows, $11{,}554$ human rows, and $5{,}128$ yeast rows, all with control rank $23$.  The measured-TE absorption values are $0.01759429001506974$ for zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ for human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for yeast.  A separate measured-tRNA-copy tAI contact gives absorption values $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast.  A yeast turnover contact on $3{,}640$ joined proteins gives turnover absorption $0.0009086031889966327$.  These comparisons locate the protein-feature contact among other bounded readouts: the feature projections are descriptive surfaces adjacent to translation, tRNA-copy, and turnover readouts, not substitutes for them.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite record supports a narrow statement: protein-feature projections were computed on real joined rows, several human and zebrafish entries exceed degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni thresholds, and the human and yeast feature joins preserve the modeled-complement target within $0.02$.  It does not support a stable cross-organism protein-feature explanation of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual, because zebrafish feature coverage changes the residual target by $0.1384608720516286$ and the significant candidates are not stable across organisms.  The correct conclusion is therefore a bounded absorption report, not a mechanism claim.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation-efficiency survival, ribosome-occupancy statement, mature protein-abundance statement, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, biological-function statement, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from these feature absorptions.  Reviewed UniProt and STRING-derived fields are sparse and coarse annotations; they can define finite covariates for projection tests, but they do not by themselves measure translation realization, folding, function, localization mechanism, or organismal phenotype.  Any such higher-layer interpretation requires a separate matched contact and its own controlled statistic.
