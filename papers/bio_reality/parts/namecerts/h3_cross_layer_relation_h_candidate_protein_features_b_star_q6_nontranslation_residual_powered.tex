\subsection{Protein-feature readbacks for the non-translation residual}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-protein-features-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}

\paragraph{Object of the test.}
The finite row tests whether the part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance-associated residual that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation complement can be described by a small panel of fetchable protein annotations. The surface is deliberately narrow. It starts with nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$, and tests four one-dimensional protein-feature candidates: complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-segment count. The control block has rank $23$ in every organism and contains $24$ columns before rank reduction: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions obtained from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined by the fraction of sense codons supported on the union of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. Each candidate has $K_c=1$ and residualized feature rank $1$, so the comparison is between single-feature absorption and a degree-of-freedom matched null rather than a high-dimensional feature fit.

\paragraph{Joined finite surfaces.}
The feature contact is not equally dense across organisms. For \textit{Danio rerio}, the codon-side join reports $14410$ CDS rows, but reviewed feature coverage gives $3180$ feature records and only $2242$ joined rows. For \textit{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding counts are $19053$ reported CDS rows, $18842$ feature records, and $18335$ joined rows. For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the counts are $5815$, $6156$, and $5682$. Thus the human and yeast surfaces nearly retain the full feature-eligible coding set, whereas the zebrafish surface is much smaller and must be read as a coverage-limited contact. This is visible in the modeled-complement stability check: the full-abundance complement reference $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ is $0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish, but the feature-join value is $0.7361678489976374$, an absolute difference of $0.1384608720516286$, outside the $0.02$ stability tolerance. Human remains stable, with $0.9208949355679455$ versus $0.9188293375787641$ and difference $0.002065597989181489$; yeast also remains stable, with $0.21173219572706703$ versus $0.21235197749007645$ and difference $0.0006197817630094193$.

\paragraph{Observed feature distributions.}
The joined feature distributions show that the tested annotations are sparse or coarse rather than continuous molecular readouts. In zebrafish, complex membership is nonzero for $696$ of $2242$ joined proteins with mean $0.31043710972346117$, domain count is nonzero for $847$ proteins with mean $0.856378233719893$ and maximum $48$, PTM density is nonzero for only $211$ proteins with mean $0.0012176949624096891$ and maximum $0.8014705882352942$, and transmembrane count is nonzero for $443$ proteins with mean $0.7292595896520964$ and maximum $18$. In human, complex membership is nonzero for $6650$ of $18335$ joined proteins with mean $0.3626943005181347$, domain count is nonzero for $8090$ proteins with mean $1.071284428688301$ and maximum $285$, PTM density is nonzero for $9210$ proteins with mean $0.006473867757948575$ and maximum $0.912621359223301$, and transmembrane count is nonzero for $4792$ proteins with mean $1.0721570766293973$ and maximum $38$. In yeast, complex membership is nonzero for $1992$ of $5682$ joined proteins with mean $0.3505807814149947$, domain count is nonzero for $1574$ proteins with mean $0.4074269623372052$ and maximum $11$, PTM density is nonzero for $2282$ proteins with mean $0.002774535811043876$, and transmembrane count is nonzero for $1173$ proteins with mean $1.0007039774727209$ and maximum $23$.

\paragraph{Absorption readback.}
The finite-row absorption values are small in absolute scale and heterogeneous by organism. In zebrafish, the four candidate absorptions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{complex}} &= 0.00039566384726157463,\\
A_{\mathrm{domain}} &= 0.0008268196005307971,\\
A_{\mathrm{PTM}} &= 0.014211240113472575,\\
A_{\mathrm{TM}} &= 0.001369142724157357.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only PTM density exceeds its degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni threshold: $0.014211240113472575 > 0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95\%$ quantile $0.001788151234180097$ over $200$ trials. The other zebrafish candidates remain below their thresholds: complex membership has threshold $0.00275854608923269$, domain count $0.005123577637652884$, and transmembrane count $0.0034780137133507$.

\paragraph{Human and yeast candidate behavior.}
In human, three of four candidates exceed their Bonferroni thresholds. Complex membership absorbs $0.0075271999877804495$ against threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, PTM density absorbs $0.014884825920540636$ against threshold $0.00042094892238120357$, and transmembrane count absorbs $0.0015569064882873212$ against threshold $0.0002697448894057371$. Domain count is essentially absent as an absorber in this row, with $0.000011405527940317433$ against threshold $0.0005934431422432757$. In yeast, no candidate clears the Bonferroni boundary. The absorptions are $0.00069212177545487$ for complex membership, $0.00007029698692511431$ for domain count, $0.0005759336988655581$ for PTM density, and $0.00031741485098156394$ for transmembrane count, all below the respective thresholds $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-efficiency and organism-level summaries.}
The feature row should be read beside, not in place of, the measured translation-efficiency row. On measured TE joins, the modeled complement was $0.8424312412078521$ in zebrafish, $0.6628211177352172$ in \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.8566190269974241$ in human, and $0.22386566043976477$ in yeast; measured TE absorbed only $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively. The feature row therefore does not certify a translation mechanism; it asks whether residual structure left after modeled translation-adaptation complement has descriptive overlap with coarse protein annotations. A separate organism-level summary also found no resolved domain preference in dominant-coordinate enrichment: the domain-by-dominant-coordinate contingency had $n=12$, Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$ over $1000$ trials. The same summary reported exploratory correlations of $M_{QTP}$ fraction with GC3 at $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$, with log tRNA gene count at $r=0.06029405206037823$ and value $0.8621378621378621$, and with log proteome size at $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and value $0.913086913086913$. These numbers support a conservative reading: the feature absorptions are finite descriptive contacts, not a cross-organism law.

\paragraph{Verdict for this finite row.}
The row is ready as a bounded protein-feature readback. It records that human has Bonferroni-exceeding descriptive absorption for complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count; zebrafish has a PTM-density excess but fails the feature-join complement-stability check; yeast has stable coverage but no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate. Consequently the row does not close a stable cross-organism explanation of the non-translation residual. It supplies a ranked finite clue: PTM density is the only candidate with an excess in both zebrafish and human, but zebrafish coverage changes the residual target, and yeast supplies a negative control for the same candidate panel.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
No translation-efficiency survival, ribosome-occupancy claim, protein-abundance mediation, folded-structure claim, physical-admissibility statement, molecular-function statement, mechanistic assertion, phenotype statement, or universal biological rule follows from these numbers. The feature values are reviewed UniProt or STRING-derived annotations joined to coding-sequence rows and used in statistical projections. They do not measure translation itself, do not establish causality, and do not show that a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate realizes a protein state. Any such higher-layer conclusion would require a separate layer-matched contact and a separately reported controlled statistic.
