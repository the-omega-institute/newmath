\subsection{Protein-feature projections for the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-protein-features-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record tests whether a residual abundance component left after the modeled-translation contact can be described by fetchable protein-feature coordinates.  The tested residual basis has nine coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  The control block $Z$ contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 from the observed CDS codon counts, and the M-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In each organism the reported control-column count is $24$ and the rank of $Z$ is $23$.  Four candidate feature readouts are used: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.  Each candidate contributes one feature coordinate, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for every candidate in every organism.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature coverage.}
The finite joins are uneven across organisms.  For \textit{Danio rerio}, the CDS-abundance join contains $14410$ rows, but the reviewed-feature join contains $2242$ rows from $3180$ feature records.  For \textit{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding numbers are $19053$ CDS-joined rows and $18335$ feature-joined rows from $18842$ feature records.  For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the corresponding numbers are $5815$ CDS-joined rows and $5682$ feature-joined rows from $6156$ feature records.  Thus the human and yeast contacts retain most of the abundance-CDS surface, while the zebrafish feature contact is sparse enough to alter the target residual.

\paragraph{Residual-target stability.}
Let $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ denote the fraction of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ projected abundance residual remaining after removing the modeled-tAI component on the active join.  The complement reference values from the full abundance-CDS joins are $0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, and $0.21173219572706703$ for yeast.  On the protein-feature joins the corresponding values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{zebrafish}) &= 0.7361678489976374,\\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{human}) &= 0.9188293375787641,\\
 d_{\mathrm{feature}}(\mathrm{yeast}) &= 0.21235197749007645.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absolute deviations from the full-join reference are $0.1384608720516286$, $0.002065597989181489$, and $0.0006197817630094193$, respectively.  With the declared $0.02$ tolerance, human and yeast preserve the target residual, while zebrafish does not.  This single failed organism is enough to block a stable cross-organism H-candidate claim, even though all descriptive projections below are well-defined on their own joins.

\paragraph{Feature absorption values.}
The per-candidate absorbed fractions are small in absolute scale.  In zebrafish they are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  In human they are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$ for the same four candidates.  In yeast they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  The largest observed entries are therefore PTM density in human and zebrafish, but their interpretation differs because the zebrafish join changes the residual target.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses $200$ trials per organism-candidate pair.  Zebrafish PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni boundary: the observed absorption $0.014211240113472575$ is above the threshold $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  The other zebrafish candidates do not exceed their boundaries: complex membership has threshold $0.00275854608923269$, domain count has threshold $0.005123577637652884$, and transmembrane count has threshold $0.0034780137133507$.

For human, three candidates exceed the Bonferroni boundaries.  Complex membership has absorption $0.0075271999877804495$ against threshold $0.0006605662314432479$, null mean $0.000047823004194137224$, and null $95$th percentile $0.00019370923778419034$.  PTM density has absorption $0.014884825920540636$ against threshold $0.00042094892238120357$, null mean $0.000056988673240982024$, and null $95$th percentile $0.00022866792835099184$.  Transmembrane count has absorption $0.0015569064882873212$ against threshold $0.0002697448894057371$, null mean $0.00004571692851089026$, and null $95$th percentile $0.0001793447174515382$.  Human domain count does not exceed its threshold: $0.000011405527940317433$ is below $0.0005934431422432757$.

For yeast, no candidate exceeds its Bonferroni boundary.  The thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$ for complex membership, $0.0011639234792592378$ for domain count, $0.001713645678402473$ for PTM density, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for transmembrane count, while all observed absorptions remain below $0.0007$.

\paragraph{Translation and turnover context.}
The feature projection is downstream of a measured-translation-efficiency check, not a substitute for it.  In the measured TE contact, the active readout joins contain $13140$ zebrafish rows, $3415$ \textit{E. coli} rows, $11554$ human rows, and $5128$ yeast rows, each again with control-column count $24$ and rank $23$.  Measured TE absorbs only $0.01759429001506974$ of the modeled-complement residual in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \textit{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast.  A yeast protein-turnover contact joins $3640$ rows and absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ of the same style of residual, with $d_{\mathrm{model}}=0.2232704509412724$ on the turnover join and an absolute complement-reference difference of $0.011538255214205373$.  These companion contacts show that the protein-feature result is a descriptive H-candidate projection, not a resolved translation or degradation mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite computation supports the following bounded statement.  Reviewed UniProt or STRING-derived feature coordinates can be projected against the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual under the declared covariates, and some organism-candidate pairs exceed degree-of-freedom matched Bonferroni boundaries.  The strongest recurring signals are PTM density in human and zebrafish, with additional human signals for complex membership and transmembrane count.  However, the zebrafish feature join shifts $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ by $0.1384608720516286$ relative to the full abundance-CDS reference, and yeast has no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate.  The record is therefore a computed descriptive contact with unstable cross-organism closure, not a stable protein-feature explanation of the non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation-efficiency survival, ribosome mechanism, protein abundance claim, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, phenotype claim, causal claim, or universal biological rule follows from these feature projections alone.  A stronger biological interpretation would require a separate layer-matched contact with same-row translation readouts, mRNA controls, protein abundance or state measurements as appropriate, and a statistic computed under the same control discipline.
