\subsection{Protein-feature projections from the non-translation residual}\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-h-candidate-protein-features-b-star-q6-nontranslation-residual-powered}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether fetchable protein-feature annotations absorb any part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection has been removed.  The carrier is the finite CDS-by-protein surface obtained by joining real CDS codon counts, the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, organism-specific controls, the modeled-complement abundance residual, and four scalar protein-feature candidates: complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-count.  The nine coordinates are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K_{AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg}_{AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile}_{AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f3_{stress}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The common control block has $24$ columns with rank $23$ in every feature organism and consists of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  Each feature candidate contributes one residualized column, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for each candidate in each joined organism.

\paragraph{Joined rows and coverage distortion.}
The feature join is deliberately not treated as the same finite surface as the full abundance-CDS join.  In zebrafish, $3180$ reviewed feature records join to $2242$ CDS/protein rows out of $14410$ reported CDS-abundance joins.  In human, $18842$ feature records join to $18335$ rows out of $19053$.  In yeast, $6156$ feature records join to $5682$ rows out of $5815$.  This difference matters because the modeled-complement residual itself changes when the row set changes.  The feature-join modeled-complement fractions are $0.7361678489976374$ for zebrafish, $0.9188293375787641$ for human, and $0.21235197749007645$ for yeast.  Against the full abundance-CDS complement references $0.874628721049266$, $0.9208949355679455$, and $0.21173219572706703$, the absolute discrepancies are $0.1384608720516286$, $0.002065597989181489$, and $0.0006197817630094193$.  Thus human and yeast remain within the $0.02$ stability tolerance, while zebrafish does not.  The chapter therefore reports a bounded feature-projection computation, not a stable cross-organism closure of the residual.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature surfaces are sparse and uneven.  In zebrafish, complex membership is nonzero for $696$ of $2242$ joined rows, domain count for $847$, PTM density for $211$, and transmembrane count for $443$; the joined means are $0.31043710972346117$, $0.856378233719893$, $0.0012176949624096891$, and $0.7292595896520964$.  In human, the corresponding nonzero counts are $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ among $18335$ rows, with means $0.3626943005181347$, $1.071284428688301$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $1.0721570766293973$.  In yeast, the nonzero counts are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ among $5682$ rows, with means $0.3505807814149947$, $0.4074269623372052$, $0.002774535811043876$, and $1.0007039774727209$.  The largest observed joined domain count is $48$ in zebrafish, $285$ in human, and $11$ in yeast; the largest joined PTM densities are $0.8014705882352942$, $0.912621359223301$, and $0.2647058823529412$.  These finite distributions justify using the feature columns as descriptive readouts, but they also expose annotation-density differences that can move the residual target.

\paragraph{Candidate absorption.}
Let $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ denote the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual on the feature join, and let $H_c$ be the residualized one-column feature candidate after the common control block has been removed.  The reported scalar is the descriptive absorbed fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_c = 1 - \frac{\lVert P_{Q\perp Tmod\perp H_c}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_{Q\perp Tmod}\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For zebrafish, the absorbed fractions are $0.00039566384726157463$ for complex membership, $0.0008268196005307971$ for domain count, $0.014211240113472575$ for PTM density, and $0.001369142724157357$ for transmembrane count.  For human, they are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$.  For yeast, they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  The largest single feature absorptions are therefore PTM density in zebrafish and human, and complex membership in yeast; however, the yeast maximum remains below $0.001$.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
Each one-column candidate is compared with $200$ degree-of-freedom-matched null trials and a Bonferroni threshold.  In zebrafish, only PTM density exceeds the corrected threshold: its threshold is $0.003775603188484381$, with null mean $0.0004610486745564995$ and null $95$th percentile $0.001788151234180097$.  Zebrafish complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$.  In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, while domain count does not exceed its threshold $0.0005934431422432757$.  In yeast, none of the four candidates exceeds its corrected threshold; the thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$.

\paragraph{Relation to the neighboring translation rows.}
The feature result is interpreted together with the adjacent finite computations, because the tested residual is the part left after modeled translation adaptation rather than a direct protein-state mechanism.  Measured ribosome-profiling translation efficiency absorbs $0.01759429001506974$ of the same kind of complement in zebrafish, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149257963897$ in human, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast on the measured-TE joins.  Measured tRNA gene-copy tAI absorbs $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast on the full tRNA joins.  Yeast turnover absorbs $0.0009086031889966327$ on $3640$ joined turnover-abundance-CDS rows.  These neighboring rows show that the feature candidates are not being used to replace translation-efficiency, tRNA, or turnover contacts; they are a separate descriptive probe on a protein-annotation join.

\paragraph{Verdict of this finite row.}
The computation is complete as a descriptive projection: all four candidate columns were residualized against the declared controls, absorption fractions were computed, and degree-of-freedom null comparisons were recorded.  The strongest finite signals are PTM density in zebrafish and human, plus human complex membership and human transmembrane count.  The cross-organism claim remains limited because zebrafish feature coverage changes the modeled-complement residual by $0.1384608720516286$ relative to the full abundance-CDS reference, and yeast shows no Bonferroni-exceeding candidate.  The resulting statement is therefore: sparse fetchable protein annotations can absorb small, bounded portions of the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual on the rows where those annotations exist, but the row-set instability and organism-specific candidate pattern block a stable universal H-candidate conclusion.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
No translation realization, protein abundance mechanism, folding conclusion, structural-order conclusion, physical-admissibility conclusion, function claim, phenotype claim, causal mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from these feature projections alone.  The quantities above are statistical projections on finite joined tables.  Any stronger interpretation requires a separate layer-appropriate contact and a separately recorded statistic on its own matched rows.  In particular, a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-to-feature absorption entry is not evidence of ribosome occupancy, not evidence of mature protein abundance, not evidence of folding, and not evidence of biological function.
