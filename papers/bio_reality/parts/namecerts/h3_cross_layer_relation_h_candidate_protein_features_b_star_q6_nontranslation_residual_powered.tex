\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bior reality-q6-protein-feature-residual}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether the part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation proxy is absorbed by coarse protein-feature annotations.  The carrier is a finite CDS-by-protein join with nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, residualized against the control block $Z$.  The control block contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline, where $M$-density is the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In every organism reported here the control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$.

\paragraph{Observed joins.}
The tested feature panel contains four one-dimensional candidates: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-helix count.  The finite joins are organism-specific.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, $3180$ reviewed feature records join to $2242$ CDS rows out of $14410$ reported CDS-abundance rows.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records join to $18335$ rows out of $19053$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records join to $5682$ rows out of $5815$.  Thus the human and yeast surfaces nearly preserve the abundance-CDS target, whereas the zebrafish surface is a substantially thinned feature join.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
Let $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ denote the fraction of $\widehat P_Q$ left in the modeled-translation-complement direction on the row set actually used by the candidate feature surface.  The reference full abundance-CDS values are $0.8746287210$ for zebrafish, $0.9208949356$ for human, and $0.2117321957$ for yeast.  On the feature joins the corresponding values are $0.7361678490$, $0.9188293376$, and $0.2123519775$.  The absolute deviations are therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\Delta d|_{\mathrm{Dr}} &= 0.1384608721,\\
|\Delta d|_{\mathrm{Hs}} &= 0.0020655980,\\
|\Delta d|_{\mathrm{Sc}} &= 0.0006197818.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only the human and yeast joins meet the $0.02$ reference-stability tolerance.  The zebrafish feature surface changes the residual target itself, so cross-organism closure cannot be asserted from the three-organism feature table.

\paragraph{Feature absorption.}
The descriptive absorption fractions are small.  In zebrafish, complex membership absorbs $0.0003956638$, domain count $0.0008268196$, PTM density $0.0142112401$, and transmembrane count $0.0013691427$.  In human, complex membership absorbs $0.0075272000$, domain count $0.0000114055$, PTM density $0.0148848259$, and transmembrane count $0.0015569065$.  In yeast, the corresponding values are $0.0006921218$, $0.0000702970$, $0.0005759337$, and $0.0003174149$.  Each candidate has fitted rank $1$, so the comparison is not a high-rank feature expansion but a four-way competition among scalar readouts.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom-matched nulls use $200$ trials for each candidate.  Zebrafish PTM density is the only zebrafish candidate exceeding its Bonferroni threshold: its observed absorption $0.0142112401$ exceeds the threshold $0.0037756032$, with null mean $0.0004610487$ and null $95\%$ quantile $0.0017881512$.  Zebrafish complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed their thresholds.  In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their Bonferroni thresholds: the thresholds are $0.0006605662$, $0.0004209489$, and $0.0002697449$, respectively, against observed absorptions $0.0075272000$, $0.0148848259$, and $0.0015569065$.  Human domain count does not exceed its threshold $0.0005934431$.  In yeast, no candidate exceeds threshold: the largest yeast observed absorption among the four candidates is complex membership at $0.0006921218$, below its threshold $0.0010152540$.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature surfaces are sparse and coarse.  In zebrafish, joined complex membership is nonzero in $696$ of $2242$ rows, domain count in $847$ rows, PTM density in $211$ rows, and transmembrane count in $443$ rows.  In human, the corresponding nonzero counts are $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ out of $18335$.  In yeast they are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ out of $5682$.  The joined PTM-density means are $0.0012176950$ for zebrafish, $0.0064738678$ for human, and $0.0027745358$ for yeast; the joined transmembrane-count means are $0.7292595897$, $1.0721570766$, and $1.0007039775$.

\paragraph{Relation to translation contacts.}
The protein-feature test is downstream of, and weaker than, the measured translation-efficiency and modeled-translation readouts.  In the measured-TE contact, row counts are $13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast, with rank $23$ control matrices.  Measured TE absorbs only $0.0175942900$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ in human, and $0.0002762784$ in yeast.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact preserves the full abundance-CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast, with $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ joined rows, and absorbs $0.0208171028$, $0.0000542623$, and $0.0015307477$, respectively.  Yeast turnover, tested on $3640$ joined rows, absorbs $0.0009086032$.  These comparison contacts show that the protein-feature surface is not a replacement for a measured translation-efficiency step; it is only a bounded downstream descriptive projection on the rows where reviewed annotations exist.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite computation supports a narrow statement: on the supplied feature joins, some scalar protein-feature annotations absorb a small portion of the modeled-translation-complement $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance residual, and several human or zebrafish candidate entries exceed their degree-of-freedom-matched Bonferroni thresholds.  It does not support a stable cross-organism H-closure because the zebrafish feature join changes $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ by $0.1384608721$, far beyond the $0.02$ stability tolerance.  The result is therefore a bounded feature-readout record, not a mechanism claim.  No translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein abundance mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, universality, or causal explanation follows from these absorption entries unless an independent layer-matched contact supplies that measurement and the corresponding controlled statistic.
