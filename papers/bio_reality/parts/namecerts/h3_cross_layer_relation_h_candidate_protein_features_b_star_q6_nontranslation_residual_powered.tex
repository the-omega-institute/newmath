\subsection{Protein-feature readout of the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-h-candidate}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The row tests whether the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation projection can be descriptively absorbed by fetchable protein-feature coordinates.  The finite carrier is a CDS-by-organism join with nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$, residualized against a control block consisting of an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in each organismal feature join.  The candidate feature block has four one-dimensional readouts: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-helix count.  Thus each organism contributes four rank-one descriptive absorption tests, not a mechanistic model of protein state.

\paragraph{Joined rows and feature coverage.}
The feature join is deliberately smaller than the abundance-CDS join in two of the three organisms.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, $14{,}410$ CDS-abundance rows are available in the reference join, but only $3{,}180$ reviewed feature records are loaded and $2{,}242$ rows enter the all-readout feature join.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19{,}053$ reference rows, $18{,}842$ feature records, and $18{,}335$ joined rows are available.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5{,}815$ reference rows, $6{,}156$ feature records, and $5{,}682$ joined rows are available.  The resulting feature distributions show the sparsity of the contact.  On the joined rows, complex membership has means $0.3104$, $0.3627$, and $0.3506$ in zebrafish, human, and yeast.  Domain count has means $0.8564$, $1.0713$, and $0.4074$, with maxima $48$, $285$, and $11$.  PTM density has means $0.001218$, $0.006474$, and $0.002775$, with nonzero counts $211$, $9{,}210$, and $2{,}282$.  Transmembrane count has means $0.7293$, $1.0722$, and $1.0007$, with nonzero counts $443$, $4{,}792$, and $1{,}173$.  These numbers make the contact informative enough for a bounded projection test, but not uniform enough to support a stable cross-organism biological closure.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
The first audit is whether the modeled-complement residual target on the feature join matches the full abundance-CDS complement reference.  The reference-to-feature comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Dr} &= |0.7361678489976374 - 0.874628721049266| = 0.1384608720516286,\\
\Delta_{Hs} &= |0.9188293375787641 - 0.9208949355679455| = 0.002065597989181489,\\
\Delta_{Sc} &= |0.21235197749007645 - 0.21173219572706703| = 0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only human and yeast satisfy the $0.02$ agreement tolerance.  Zebrafish fails it because reviewed-UniProt and STRING feature availability changes the joined protein set strongly enough to move the residual target.  The row is therefore read as a computed descriptive contact with unstable cross-organism feature support, not as a closed explanation of the non-translation residual.

\paragraph{Absorption entries.}
Per-candidate absorption is small on the feature surface.  In zebrafish, complex membership absorbs $0.00039566384726157463$, domain count $0.0008268196005307971$, PTM density $0.014211240113472575$, and transmembrane count $0.001369142724157357$.  In human, the corresponding entries are $0.0075271999877804495$, $0.000011405527940317433$, $0.014884825920540636$, and $0.0015569064882873212$.  In yeast, they are $0.00069212177545487$, $0.00007029698692511431$, $0.0005759336988655581$, and $0.00031741485098156394$.  A degree-of-freedom matched null with $200$ trials supplies Bonferroni thresholds for each candidate.  Zebrafish has only PTM density above threshold, with $0.014211240113472575 > 0.003775603188484381$.  Human has complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count above threshold, with thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$.  Yeast has no feature candidate above its corresponding Bonferroni threshold.  The pattern is heterogeneous: the same candidate is not selected in all organisms, and the organism whose PTM entry is largest in relative terms is also the organism whose feature join destabilizes the residual target.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and tRNA contacts.}
The measured translation-efficiency contact is a separate bounded readout.  With matched mRNA abundance included in the construction of TE, the all-readout joins have $n=13{,}140$ for zebrafish, $3{,}415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11{,}554$ for human, and $5{,}128$ for yeast, again with rank-$23$ controls.  The measured-TE absorption entries are $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, respectively.  The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact gives absorption entries $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast, using $8{,}846$, $431$, and $273$ tRNA genes.  These contacts show that translation-adjacent readouts can be computed on the same residual family, but they do not license a protein-feature interpretation by themselves.  They only locate the feature row in the broader cross-layer test: feature absorption is evaluated after the modeled-complement residual is formed, and any measured translation-efficiency claim must keep the matched mRNA boundary explicit.

\paragraph{Turnover and organism specificity.}
The yeast turnover contact supplies a further comparison point.  On $3{,}640$ joined turnover-abundance-CDS rows, the modeled complement is $0.2232704509412724$, within $0.011538255214205373$ of the full-join reference $0.21173219572706703$, but turnover absorbs only $0.0009086031889966327$.  Thus, in yeast, the fetchable turnover and feature coordinates both leave most of the residual component undescribed.  At the cross-organism descriptive level, the organism-specific meta table contains $16$ assembled organisms and $12$ complete-feature organisms.  The domain test for $M_{QTP}$ fraction has variance ratio $0.8889980452277374$ and right-tail permutation value $0.4155844155844156$ over $1000$ trials.  Continuous tests give $r=-0.3062616500469447$ for GC3 with two-sided permutation value $0.35264735264735264$, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ for log proteome size with value $0.913086913086913$, and $r=0.06029405206037823$ for log tRNA-gene count with value $0.8621378621378621$.  The dominant-coordinate-by-domain contingency has Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$ but a right-tail permutation value $0.5814185814185814$, so it remains heterogeneous rather than a resolved domain rule.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
This row is ready as a finite descriptive BioReality record: the projection quantities were computed, the row counts and ranks are explicit, the feature candidates were compared to degree-of-freedom matched nulls, and the unstable join is visible in the zebrafish residual-target check.  The scientific reading is narrow.  There is evidence for small, organism-dependent absorption of the modeled-complement $B^*_{Q6}$ residual by reviewed feature annotations, strongest for PTM density in zebrafish and human and additionally for complex membership and transmembrane count in human.  There is not a stable cross-organism H closure, because feature availability changes the residual target in zebrafish and yeast shows no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate.  No translation realization, folding statement, physical admissibility statement, functional interpretation, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row.  Such claims require their own layer-matched contacts and statistics; the present row only reports bounded finite-row absorption on sparse protein-feature annotations.
