\subsection{Protein-feature absorption of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:biorreality-q6-protein-feature-absorption}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This chapter tests whether coarse protein-feature annotations can account for the part of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance residual that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation component has been removed. The finite surface is not a protein-function claim. Its rows are coding-sequence records joined to reviewed UniProt or STRING-derived protein-feature records, and its columns are the nine residual coordinates
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress, residualized against the declared control panel. The candidate feature list is deliberately small: complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-helix count. Each candidate contributes one fitted direction, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for every candidate in every organism where the feature join exists.

\paragraph{Finite joins and control rank.}
The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the M-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors. The reported control-column count is $24$ and the numerical rank is $23$ in all three feature-tested organisms. In \textit{Danio rerio}, the feature surface contains $3180$ feature records and joins $2242$ CDS/protein rows out of $14410$ CDS rows previously joined for abundance. In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $18842$ feature records produce $18335$ joined rows out of $19053$. In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $6156$ feature records produce $5682$ joined rows out of $5815$. These row counts are part of the statement: the surface is a reviewed-feature join, not a complete organism-wide proteome table.

\paragraph{Projection statistic.}
For each candidate feature $H_c$, the experiment projects the modeled-translation-complement residual $\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ onto the residualized candidate direction after the control matrix has been removed. The reported quantity is the descriptive absorbed fraction
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_c &= \frac{\|\Pi_{H_c\perp Z}\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2}{\|\widehat P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2},
\end{aligned}
$$
with a degree-of-freedom matched null and a Bonferroni threshold computed from $200$ null trials for each candidate-organism pair. The statistic is an absorption score on a finite projection surface. It is not a causal mediation test, and it does not identify a molecular mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed absorption entries.}
In \textit{Danio rerio}, PTM density is the only candidate exceeding its finite null threshold: $A_{ptm}=0.014211240113472575$ with Bonferroni threshold $0.003775603188484381$ and null $p95=0.001788151234180097$. The other candidates remain below threshold: complex membership $0.00039566384726157463$ against $0.00275854608923269$, domain count $0.0008268196005307971$ against $0.005123577637652884$, and transmembrane count $0.001369142724157357$ against $0.0034780137133507$. In \textit{Homo sapiens}, three candidates exceed their thresholds: complex membership $0.0075271999877804495$ against $0.0006605662314432479$, PTM density $0.014884825920540636$ against $0.00042094892238120357$, and transmembrane count $0.0015569064882873212$ against $0.0002697448894057371$. Human domain count is essentially absent on this projection, $0.000011405527940317433$ against threshold $0.0005934431422432757$. In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, no candidate exceeds threshold: complex membership is $0.00069212177545487$ against $0.001015254045679051$, domain count is $0.00007029698692511431$ against $0.0011639234792592378$, PTM density is $0.0005759336988655581$ against $0.001713645678402473$, and transmembrane count is $0.00031741485098156394$ against $0.0012579935618985945$.

\paragraph{Join stability check.}
The feature result is not promoted to a stable cross-organism $H$ closure because the feature availability changes the residual target in at least one organism. The full abundance-CDS complement reference gives $d_{modeled}=0.874628721049266$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, whereas the feature join gives $0.7361678489976374$, an absolute difference of $0.1384608720516286$, which fails the $0.02$ agreement check. The human comparison is stable at this tolerance: $0.9208949355679455$ versus $0.9188293375787641$, absolute difference $0.002065597989181489$. Yeast is also stable: $0.21173219572706703$ versus $0.21235197749007645$, absolute difference $0.0006197817630094193$. Thus the finite result is a bounded feature-absorption readout, with a strong warning that reviewed-feature sparsity can move the projection surface itself.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature tables are sparse and coarse. In \textit{Danio rerio}, PTM density is nonzero for only $211$ of $2242$ joined rows, with mean $0.0012176949624096891$ and $p95=0.003048780487804878$; complex membership is nonzero for $696$ rows; domain count is nonzero for $847$ rows; transmembrane count is nonzero for $443$ rows. In humans, PTM density is nonzero for $9210$ of $18335$ joined rows, complex membership for $6650$, domain count for $8090$, and transmembrane count for $4792$. In yeast, PTM density is nonzero for $2282$ of $5682$ joined rows, complex membership for $1992$, domain count for $1574$, and transmembrane count for $1173$. These summaries explain why the candidate signals must be read as finite annotation projections rather than direct measurements of protein state.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring translation contacts.}
The measured-TE contact shows that ribosome-profiling translation efficiency absorbs only small fractions of the modeled-complement residual on its own all-readout join: $0.01759429001506974$ in \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.0010982956489726373$ in \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $0.0028947149257963897$ in humans, and $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast. The measured tRNA-gene-copy tAI contact is also small on the full CDS joins: $0.020817102845637092$ in \textit{Danio rerio}, $0.00005426229300865148$ in humans, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast. The yeast turnover contact gives $0.0009086031889966327$ absorption by log half-life on $3640$ joined turnover rows. Against this background, the protein-feature table is useful because it shows where annotation features can absorb a small but nonzero portion of the residual, while also showing that the effect is not organism-stable under the available joins.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite computation supports a restricted descriptive statement: on the available reviewed-feature joins, PTM density absorbs a Bonferroni-excess fraction of the modeled-translation-complement residual in \textit{Danio rerio} and humans, while human complex membership and transmembrane count also exceed their null thresholds. Yeast supplies no excess candidate among the four tested features. Because the \textit{Danio rerio} feature join changes $d_{modeled}$ by $0.1384608720516286$ relative to the full abundance-CDS complement reference, the result is not a stable cross-organism closure for the residual. It is a bounded finite-row observation about sparse protein-feature annotations.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. A $Q_i$ residual coordinate associated with one of these feature projections remains a statistical association on a joined table. Translation requires a same-row translation readout with matched mRNA control; protein abundance requires a separate abundance contact; folding, localization, stability, function, phenotype, and mechanism each require their own layer-appropriate observations. The present chapter only records that a few coarse protein-feature annotations absorb finite fractions of the non-translation residual on the stated joins, and that sparse reviewed-feature coverage prevents a stable organism-wide claim.
