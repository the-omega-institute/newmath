\subsection{Protein-feature readbacks for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-readbacks}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether the remaining $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ abundance component, after the modeled translation-adaptation component has been removed, is visibly compressed by fetchable protein-feature annotations.  The finite surface is a CDS-by-protein join carrying nine residual coordinates,
$K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each candidate feature is represented as a one-column readout: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.  The control block has rank $23$ in each organism and contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Finite joined panels.}
The computation is organism-specific rather than a single pooled biological law.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, the full CDS-abundance join reports $14410$ CDS rows, but the reviewed feature join contains only $2242$ joined rows from $3180$ feature records.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, the corresponding numbers are $19053$ reported CDS rows, $18335$ joined feature rows, and $18842$ feature records.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, they are $5815$ reported CDS rows, $5682$ joined feature rows, and $6156$ feature records.  Thus the test is powered for human and yeast feature coverage, but sparse for zebrafish.  The feature distributions are also visibly different across organisms: zebrafish joined rows have mean domain count $0.8563782337$, mean PTM density $0.0012176950$, and mean transmembrane count $0.7292595897$; human joined rows have means $1.0712844287$, $0.0064738678$, and $1.0721570766$; yeast joined rows have means $0.4074269623$, $0.0027745358$, and $1.0007039775$.

\paragraph{Residual target stability.}
The feature join changes the target residual in one organism enough that the cross-organism interpretation must remain bounded.  Let $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ denote the modeled-complement residual fraction on the full abundance-CDS reference join and let $d_{\mathrm{feat}}$ denote the same quantity recomputed on the feature join.  The observed comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:\quad &d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.8746287210,\quad d_{\mathrm{feat}}=0.7361678490,\quad |\Delta|=0.1384608721,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:\quad &d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.9208949356,\quad d_{\mathrm{feat}}=0.9188293376,\quad |\Delta|=0.0020655980,\\
\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:\quad &d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.2117321957,\quad d_{\mathrm{feat}}=0.2123519775,\quad |\Delta|=0.0006197818.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human and yeast joins match the reference within $0.02$, while zebrafish does not.  This is the main reason the result is not promoted to a stable cross-organism explanation of the residual.  The finite computation is still informative: it says which sparse feature readouts absorb measurable pieces of the residual on their own joined surfaces.

\paragraph{Feature absorption.}
For each organism and each candidate feature, the absorbed fraction is computed after the same rank-$23$ control residualization.  The observed one-dimensional absorptions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}:\quad
&\mathrm{complex}=0.0003956638,\quad \mathrm{domain}=0.0008268196,\\
&\mathrm{PTM}=0.0142112401,\quad \mathrm{TM}=0.0013691427,\\
\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}:\quad
&\mathrm{complex}=0.0075272000,\quad \mathrm{domain}=0.0000114055,\\
&\mathrm{PTM}=0.0148848259,\quad \mathrm{TM}=0.0015569065,\\
\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}:\quad
&\mathrm{complex}=0.0006921218,\quad \mathrm{domain}=0.0000702970,\\
&\mathrm{PTM}=0.0005759337,\quad \mathrm{TM}=0.0003174149.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest entries are PTM density in zebrafish and human, near $1.4\%$ to $1.5\%$ of the modeled-complement residual.  Human complex membership is smaller but non-negligible at $0.0075272000$.  Yeast entries are all below $0.001$ on this feature panel.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null uses $200$ trials for each organism-feature pair and applies the recorded Bonferroni thresholds.  In zebrafish, PTM density exceeds its threshold: absorbed fraction $0.0142112401$ against threshold $0.0037756032$, with null mean $0.0004610487$ and null $95$th percentile $0.0017881512$.  The other zebrafish features do not exceed their thresholds: complex membership threshold $0.0027585461$, domain-count threshold $0.0051235776$, and transmembrane-count threshold $0.0034780137$.  In human, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane-count exceed their thresholds: $0.0075272000>0.0006605662$, $0.0148848259>0.0004209489$, and $0.0015569065>0.0002697449$.  Human domain count does not: $0.0000114055<0.0005934431$.  In yeast, no candidate exceeds its Bonferroni threshold; the thresholds are $0.0010152540$, $0.0011639235$, $0.0017136457$, and $0.0012579936$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane-count respectively.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and tRNA readbacks.}
The feature result should be read against two nearby finite surfaces.  On the measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency surface, the joined row counts are $13140$ for zebrafish, $3415$ for \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast.  Measured TE absorbs only $0.0175942900$ in zebrafish, $0.0010982956$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.0028947149$ in human, and $0.0002762784$ in yeast.  On the measured tRNA gene-copy tAI surface, the full CDS joins are $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ for zebrafish, human, and yeast, with tRNA absorptions $0.0208171028$, $0.0000542623$, and $0.0015307477$.  These neighboring computations show that the protein-feature panel is not a disguised translation-efficiency or tRNA-copy result.  The feature readouts occupy a downstream annotation layer, and their strongest finite entries are feature-specific rather than a direct observation of translation.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
This chapter supports a bounded descriptive statement: reviewed UniProt and STRING-derived protein features can absorb small but measurable pieces of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ modeled-complement residual on the available feature joins, with Bonferroni excess for zebrafish PTM density and for human complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane-count.  It does not support a stable cross-organism closure of the residual, because zebrafish feature availability changes the modeled-complement target by $0.1384608721$ relative to the full reference join, and yeast shows no Bonferroni-exceeding feature.  No translation realization, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, biological-function statement, causal mechanism, phenotype conclusion, or universal rule follows from these finite projections.  Any such claim would require a separate matched contact at that layer, with its own joined rows and its own controlled statistic.
