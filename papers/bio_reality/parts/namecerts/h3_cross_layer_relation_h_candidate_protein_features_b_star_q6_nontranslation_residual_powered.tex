\subsection{Protein-feature probes for the residual abundance component}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-protein-feature-h-candidate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite contact.}
This record tests whether fetchable protein-feature annotations absorb any part of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component that remains after the modeled translation-adaptation complement has already been separated.  The finite carrier is a per-protein joined surface whose rows carry coding-sequence residual coordinates, ordinary sequence controls, the modeled complement residual, and one scalar feature candidate at a time.  The nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  The shared control block has $24$ columns with rank $23$: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the $20$ amino-acid composition proportions from the actual CDS codon counts, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline, defined as the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors.

\paragraph{Joined feature surface.}
The feature contact is deliberately narrower than the available CDS contact.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, $14410$ CDS rows are reported at the abundance join, but only $3180$ reviewed feature records yield $2242$ joined rows for this feature test.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $19053$ CDS rows and $18842$ feature records yield $18335$ joined rows.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ CDS rows and $6156$ feature records yield $5682$ joined rows.  Four scalar candidates are evaluated in each organism: complex membership, domain count, post-translational-modification density, and transmembrane-count.  Each candidate contributes one feature column, so $K_c=1$ and $\operatorname{rank}(H_c)=1$ for every organism-candidate pair.

\paragraph{Reference-target stability.}
The first diagnostic is whether the feature join preserves the modeled complement target used by the abundance-complement calculation.  It does not do so uniformly.  The reference and feature-join complement values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{D. rerio}:\quad & d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.874628721049266,\quad d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.7361678489976374,\quad |\Delta|=0.1384608720516286,\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:\quad & d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.9208949355679455,\quad d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.9188293375787641,\quad |\Delta|=0.002065597989181489,\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:\quad & d_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.21173219572706703,\quad d_{\mathrm{feature}}=0.21235197749007645,\quad |\Delta|=0.0006197817630094193.
\end{aligned}
$$
The human and yeast feature joins stay within the $0.02$ tolerance, while the zebrafish feature join does not.  This single failure is decisive for the scope of the record: the calculation may be reported as a bounded descriptive projection, but it is not a stable cross-organism explanation of the $H$ component.

\paragraph{Feature distributions.}
The joined feature rows are sparse and heterogeneous, which is part of the contact rather than a nuisance to hide.  In \textit{D. rerio}, complex membership is nonzero in $696$ of $2242$ joined rows with mean $0.31043710972346117$, domain count is nonzero in $847$ rows with mean $0.856378233719893$ and maximum $48$, PTM density is nonzero in $211$ rows with mean $0.0012176949624096891$ and maximum $0.8014705882352942$, and transmembrane count is nonzero in $443$ rows with mean $0.7292595896520964$ and maximum $18$.  In \textit{H. sapiens}, the same candidates have nonzero counts $6650$, $8090$, $9210$, and $4792$ over $18335$ joined rows, with means $0.3626943005181347$, $1.071284428688301$, $0.006473867757948575$, and $1.0721570766293973$.  In \textit{S. cerevisiae}, the nonzero counts are $1992$, $1574$, $2282$, and $1173$ over $5682$ joined rows, with means $0.3505807814149947$, $0.4074269623372052$, $0.002774535811043876$, and $1.0007039774727209$.  These summaries make the measurement boundary explicit: the rows are reviewed annotation readbacks, not direct assays of causal protein state.

\paragraph{Absorption scores.}
For each organism and candidate, the statistic is the fraction of the modeled-complement residual absorbed by the residualized feature column after the sequence controls have been applied.  The observed absorption values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{D. rerio}:\quad & a_{complex}=0.00039566384726157463,\quad a_{domain}=0.0008268196005307971,\\
& a_{ptm}=0.014211240113472575,\quad a_{tm}=0.001369142724157357,\\
\textit{H. sapiens}:\quad & a_{complex}=0.0075271999877804495,\quad a_{domain}=0.000011405527940317433,\\
& a_{ptm}=0.014884825920540636,\quad a_{tm}=0.0015569064882873212,\\
\textit{S. cerevisiae}:\quad & a_{complex}=0.00069212177545487,\quad a_{domain}=0.00007029698692511431,\\
& a_{ptm}=0.0005759336988655581,\quad a_{tm}=0.00031741485098156394.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest entries are PTM density in human and zebrafish, followed by human complex membership.  The yeast entries are all small on this scale.

\paragraph{Degree-of-freedom null comparison.}
The null comparison uses $200$ degree-of-freedom-matched trials for every candidate.  In \textit{D. rerio}, only PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold: its observed value $0.014211240113472575$ is above $0.003775603188484381$, while complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not exceed thresholds $0.00275854608923269$, $0.005123577637652884$, and $0.0034780137133507$.  In \textit{H. sapiens}, complex membership, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their thresholds $0.0006605662314432479$, $0.00042094892238120357$, and $0.0002697448894057371$, whereas domain count does not exceed $0.0005934431422432757$.  In \textit{S. cerevisiae}, no candidate exceeds its threshold; the four thresholds are $0.001015254045679051$, $0.0011639234792592378$, $0.001713645678402473$, and $0.0012579935618985945$ for complex membership, domain count, PTM density, and transmembrane count.  Thus the strongest repeated finite-row signal is PTM-density absorption in zebrafish and human, but the organism panel does not support a stable protein-feature explanation because the zebrafish feature join changes the residual target and yeast supplies no Bonferroni-exceeding feature candidate.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation contacts.}
The feature calculation is downstream of, and weaker than, the measured translation-efficiency boundary.  The matched ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency computation used the same nine coordinates and the same $24$-column control family, but with joined measured-TE rows: $13140$ in \textit{D. rerio}, $3415$ in \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $11554$ in \textit{H. sapiens}, and $5128$ in \textit{S. cerevisiae}.  Its measured-TE absorption values were $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$ for those organisms.  A separate measured tRNA-copy tAI calculation also stayed descriptive, with absorbed fractions $0.020817102845637092$ in zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast.  These contacts show why the present protein-feature row must remain a bounded readout: feature absorption is being tested after translation-related residual structure has already been isolated, not as a replacement for a translation readout.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite result is a computed but non-promoted feature-contact record.  The calculation supports the following narrow statement: on the available reviewed-feature joins, several rank-one protein-feature columns absorb small but nonzero fractions of the modeled-complement residual, and some of those fractions exceed degree-of-freedom-matched Bonferroni thresholds.  It does not support a stable cross-organism $H$ closure, because the zebrafish feature join changes the modeled-complement target by $0.1384608720516286$, and the yeast panel supplies no Bonferroni-exceeding candidate.  It also does not license translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Those stronger interpretations require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics; a sparse UniProt or STRING-derived feature column is only a descriptive annotation readback for this chapter.
