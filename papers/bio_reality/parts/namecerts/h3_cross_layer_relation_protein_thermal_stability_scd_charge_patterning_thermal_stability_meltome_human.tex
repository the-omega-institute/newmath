\subsection{Translation-Bounded RNA Decay and RBP Occupancy for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Windows}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and, only when externally supplied, prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation-control columns. The admissible row is therefore not a bare codon-window object. It is a joined measurement row in which coordinate, transcript stability, abundance, translation boundary, and RBP-control information remain separate fields. The intended finite statistic asks whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows differ in RNA half-life after amino-acid composition, local sequence composition, local RNA-structure controls, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation-readout boundary have been represented.

\paragraph{Available finite facts.}
The repository-local verified scalar attached to the named cross-layer protein-thermal-stability claim is the human Meltome-side row count $n=6961$, with experiment run id $8873440304f9$ and deterministic runtime bucket $70.0$ seconds. Two linked cross-layer controls are also finite: the terminal-degron stability run $1a932ef5684e$ reports $n_{\mathrm{cterm}}=5303$ and $n_{\mathrm{proteins}}=5303$ with runtime $40.3029$ seconds, while the acidic-microenvironment ubiquitylation run $0b96a8e4da3c$ reports $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=10410$, $n_{\mathrm{prots}}=2883$, and $2000$ actual permutations. These scalars document finite panels already present for neighboring protein-layer claims. They do not, by themselves, provide the RNA-decay RBP columns needed for the present $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ bridge.

\paragraph{Local readout rule.}
For this chapter the positive bridge condition is deliberately narrow. Let $q_i$ denote the residual-coordinate state of a synonymous RNA/CDS window, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $A_i$ the matched mRNA abundance control, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, and $O_i$ a measured occupancy value for a prespecified decay-factor RBP. A bounded RBP-decay bridge is admissible only when the finite joined panel supports the comparison
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i &\sim q_i + T_i + A_i + O_i + Z_i,\\
O_i &\not\equiv q_i \quad \text{under declared coordinate/occupancy decoupling},\\
\Delta_H(q_i) &\text{ collapses under target-RBP loss and returns under wild-type rescue},\\
\Delta_H(q_i) &\text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_i$ abbreviates the declared local and transcript-level controls. The equations are not a mechanistic model; they specify the row fields that must exist before the RNA half-life association can be read as a bounded cross-layer RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The present finite data support a boundary statement rather than a positive RBP-decay conclusion. RNA half-life contacts can identify transcript or gene rows with half-life estimates. Matched mRNA abundance contacts can separate transcription-layer abundance from a translation-efficiency denominator. Ribosome-profiling contacts can supply a translation-readout boundary when joined to the same organism and row system. None of those contacts supplies same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective and non-target controls. Consequently a coordinate-associated half-life difference, even if observed on a finite panel, remains an RNA-stability association unless the RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue columns are present and pass the declared finite statistic.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and same-row translation boundary have been applied. In finite-row terms, the null does not say that transcript half-life is biologically unstructured. It says that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels are not permitted to carry an RBP-mediated cross-layer interpretation unless their apparent half-life effect follows measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and the effect obeys the target-loss and rescue pattern. If the coordinate label remains predictive while measured RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not decoupled from coordinate state, the result is not an RBP bridge. If target-RBP loss fails to remove the effect, or wild-type rescue fails to restore it while binding-defective and non-target controls remain negative, the result is not an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Local exclusion of stronger claims.}
No translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal law follows from RNA half-life alone. The same-row translation readout is a boundary condition, not proof of protein realization. Matched mRNA abundance is a control, not an RNA-decay mechanism. A ribosome-profiling route is not RBP occupancy, RNA half-life, or perturbation-rescue evidence. The $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate is therefore treated as a finite descriptor until the joined panel contains the required RBP and rescue fields. Only that stricter panel can upgrade a bounded RNA half-life association into a bounded RBP-mediated cross-layer readback.
