\subsection{Translation-Bounded RNA Half-Life With a Decay-Factor Occupancy Gate}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels, synonymous-window labels, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and externally attached decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation columns when such columns exist.  The intended object is not a free-standing sequence law.  It is a row-bounded test of whether an RNA half-life contrast associated with a synonymous residual coordinate can be promoted beyond an RNA-stability association only after a translation boundary and a measured RBP-decay boundary are both present on the same finite panel.

The primary finite audit row for the linked thermal-stability surface contains $6961$ human meltome rows under experiment run \texttt{8873440304f9}, with a deterministic recorded runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds.  Two additional finite cross-layer audits are available as calibration anchors for the same reporting discipline: the ubiquitylation occupancy surface contains $10410$ sites on $2883$ proteins with $2000$ actual permutations under run \texttt{0b96a8e4da3c}, and the C-terminal degron surface contains $5303$ C-terminal rows, equivalently $5303$ proteins, under run \texttt{1a932ef5684e}.  These numbers are not evidence for the RNA-decay bridge itself.  They certify that the BioReality packet is being handled as a finite-row object rather than as an unrestricted biological generalization.

\paragraph{Local statistic.}
Let $q_i$ denote the B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label of a synonymous RNA/CDS window, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout when it is present, let $H_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, let $A_i$ denote matched mRNA abundance, and let $O_i$ denote measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  A positive bridge is permitted only for rows in which the translation readout and abundance control are attached before the half-life contrast is interpreted.  The minimal finite contrast has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high}, T_i, A_i, Z_i\bigr)
     - \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low}, T_i, A_i, Z_i\bigr),\\
\Delta_{H\mid O}
  &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid O_i=1, q_i, T_i, A_i, Z_i\bigr)
     - \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H_i \mid O_i=0, q_i, T_i, A_i, Z_i\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ includes transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure summaries, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, transcript abundance, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline terms.  The coordinate-associated half-life contrast is not enough.  The RBP occupancy term must be measured, prespecified, and experimentally separable from the coordinate state.

\paragraph{Acceptance rule.}
The bridge is accepted only if four finite-row conditions hold together.  First, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows remain distinguished after amino-acid composition and local sequence controls while the same-row translation boundary is present.  Second, the half-life difference follows measured occupancy of the specified decay-factor RBP under a design that decouples coordinate state from occupancy.  Third, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, while wild-type rescue restores it.  Fourth, binding-defective rescue or non-target controls fail to restore the same effect.  In compact form, the required pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_{H\mid O} &\text{ accounts for the coordinate-associated half-life contrast},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{target\ loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{native}},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with all comparisons evaluated inside the declared finite panel.  If any of these rows or controls is absent, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA half-life association.

\paragraph{Observed boundary in the present packet.}
The currently attached contacts provide RNA half-life readout surfaces, matched mRNA abundance controls, and translation-efficiency routes.  They do not provide a same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy matrix, a coordinate/occupancy decoupling design, target-RBP loss rows, wild-type rescue rows, binding-defective rescue rows, or non-target controls for this subject.  Therefore the present chapter records a guarded cross-layer request, not a positive RNA-decay mechanism.  The finite thermal-stability audit row with $n=6961$ is cited only as the linked claim surface for this BioReality object; the ubiquitylation and degron audits are cited only to make explicit that other cross-layer surfaces in the same finite-row regime have their own row counts and do not substitute for the missing RBP-decay panel.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
RNA half-life by itself does not establish translation, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality.  A same-row translation readout can bound the interpretation of an RNA half-life statistic, but it does not turn the half-life statistic into protein realization.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not an RNA-decay mechanism.  A ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact is a translation-layer readout, not RBP occupancy, RNA half-life, or rescue evidence.  B$^{*}_{Q6}$ residual geometry and codon-window coordinates are mathematical labels on finite rows; they are not curated biological observations unless an external row supplies the relevant measurement.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The local null states that, after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, the same-row translation readout, and matched mRNA controls are included, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  Under the current data attachment this null is not defeated, because the RBP occupancy and perturbation/rescue requirements are absent.  The record is therefore scientifically useful as a boundary certificate: it says exactly which finite rows would be needed before an RNA half-life association could be reported as a translation-bounded, RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.
