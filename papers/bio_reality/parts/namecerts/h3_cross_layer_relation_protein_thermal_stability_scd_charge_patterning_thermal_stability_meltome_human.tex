\subsection{Translation-Bounded RNA-Decay RBP Bridge for Residual Q6 Windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of synonymous RNA/CDS windows carrying residual $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, same-row translation readouts when available, RNA half-life readouts, matched mRNA abundance controls, and optional measured occupancy columns for prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding proteins.  The carrier is rowwise rather than asymptotic: each admissible row is a local transcript window with a residual coordinate label, a same-organism sequence context, and the control columns needed to decide whether the coordinate is being tested as an RNA-stability descriptor or as a translation-bounded cross-layer relation.  The current finite audit attaches the thermal-stability-linked comparison table with $n=6961$ rows under run id $8873440304f9$, with deterministic runtime recorded as $70.0$ seconds for the JSON bucket and shell verification below the $300$ second ceiling.  A neighboring post-translational occupancy audit is retained only as a scale check: run id $0b96a8e4da3c$ reports $2883$ proteins, $10410$ sites, and $2000$ actual permutations.  These numbers do not transfer biological support to the present RNA-decay question; they only document that the BioReality ledger is using finite tabular contacts with explicit row counts and run identities.

\paragraph{Admissible readout.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate class of window $i$, let $H_i$ denote its matched RNA half-life readout, let $A_i$ denote matched mRNA abundance, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout when present, and let $O_i$ denote measured occupancy by the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The positive claim is not that $q_i$ directly causes decay.  The only admissible positive form is a bounded relation in which the half-life contrast associated with $q_i$ follows measured RBP occupancy after translation and abundance boundaries are represented on the same rows.  In schematic finite-row notation the permitted comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_H(q; Z, A, T, O)\neq 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q; Z, A, T, O)\text{ is attenuated after target-RBP loss},\\
&\Delta_H(q; Z, A, T, O)\text{ returns under wild-type rescue},\\
&\Delta_H(q; Z, A, T, O)\text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ abbreviates the declared local controls: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, batch, amino-acid composition, rank sufficiency, and simpler sequence baselines.  The translation readout is a boundary condition, not a conclusion.  If $T_i$ is absent, the row can still contribute to an RNA half-life association, but it cannot support a translation-bounded cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null statement is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows with respect to RNA half-life after the control set is installed.  Written at the level of the finite table, the null is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i\;\mid\; Z_i,A_i,T_i,O_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the further requirement that any apparent coordinate contrast disappear when measured RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not experimentally separated from coordinate state.  This is stronger than asking whether high-coordinate windows and low-coordinate windows have different half-lives in a marginal table.  A marginal contrast can be a useful screen, but it remains below the cross-layer threshold unless the same window also carries translation, abundance, RBP occupancy, loss, rescue, and control information.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
The RBP component is treated as an independent measured column, not as something selected by the residual coordinate geometry.  A candidate decay-factor RBP must be named before the statistic is read, its occupancy must be observed on the relevant local windows, and coordinate state must be separated from occupancy state closely enough to test whether the half-life effect tracks the RBP rather than the coordinate label alone.  The perturbation criterion has four finite-row clauses: the coordinate-associated half-life contrast is present in the wild-type table, target-RBP loss collapses that contrast, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it.  If any of these clauses is missing, the record remains a bounded RNA-stability association or a coordinate descriptor.  It is not promoted to an RNA-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Local negative scope.}
The chapter therefore blocks several stronger readings.  RNA half-life alone does not establish translation, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general biological law.  A ribosome-profiling contact can supply translation-efficiency rows only when it is actually joined to the same organism and same CDS/window rows; its name alone is not an RNA half-life measurement and is not RBP perturbation evidence.  Matched mRNA abundance is a denominator or covariate for separating transcription-layer signal from translation-layer signal, not an RNA-decay readout.  Likewise, residual $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates and codon/window geometry are descriptors inside the finite table; they are not themselves curated biological measurements of RBP binding, decay, protein abundance, structure, function, or universality.

\paragraph{Present verdict.}
For the available finite-row data, the chapter is ready as a scoped BioReality record but not as a positive RBP-decay mechanism claim.  The verified table contributes a concrete row count, $n=6961$, and reproducible run metadata, but the required same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are not present in the supplied rows.  The scientific content is therefore a bounded admissibility rule: a $B^*_{Q6}$ RNA half-life contrast may be considered a translation-bounded RBP bridge only after the missing RBP and perturbation columns are joined to the same finite panel and the declared statistic passes under the local control set.  Until then, the record states a disciplined null-facing boundary for future data rather than a realized biological mechanism.
