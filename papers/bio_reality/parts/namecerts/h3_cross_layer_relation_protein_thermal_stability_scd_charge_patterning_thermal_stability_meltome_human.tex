\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA decay contact for B*\_Q6 residual windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry B*\_Q6 residual coordinate labels, synonymous-window annotations, a same-row translation readout when available, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or control columns only when those columns are attached by measurement.  The intended biological object is not an unrestricted sequence law.  It is a bounded panel of transcript windows in which a coordinate label may be compared with RNA stability only after amino-acid composition, local sequence content, RNA-structure proxies, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the translation-readout boundary have been declared as row-level controls.

\paragraph{Coordinate split.}
The finite contrast separates high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA windows after local amino-acid and sequence controls.  A second split separates windows with measured decay-factor RBP occupancy from windows without such occupancy, but this split is admissible only if coordinate state and occupancy are experimentally or statistically decoupled.  A third split compares target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue against binding-defective or non-target controls.  A fourth split records whether the same row contains a translation readout.  The local readback is therefore the finite predicate asking whether coordinate-associated RNA half-life differences track the measured RBP occupancy pattern after the same-row translation and matched mRNA abundance controls are already present.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The supplied finite run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.protein\_thermal\_stability.scd\_charge\_patterning\_thermal\_stability\_meltome\_human} contributes a concrete audited row count, $n=6961$, with deterministic runtime bucket $70.0$ seconds under experiment run \texttt{8873440304f9}.  That run is a protein thermal-stability contact, not an RNA half-life or RBP-occupancy contact, so it can anchor finite-row discipline and row accounting but cannot by itself certify an RNA-decay mechanism.  The second supplied finite run, \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.ubiquitylation\_occupancy.ub\_occupancy\_acidic\_microenvironment\_steger2024}, records $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=10410$, $n_{\mathrm{prots}}=2883$, and $2000$ actual permutations under experiment run \texttt{0b96a8e4da3c}.  That run is a ubiquitin-occupancy finite panel, not a transcript-window RBP perturbation panel.  It is useful here only as a separate example of finite biological contact accounting: site count, protein count, and permutation count are row-level scalars, while the present RNA-decay claim still requires its own layer-matched rows.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
Let $Q$ denote the B*\_Q6 residual coordinate label, $T$ the same-row translation readout, $A$ the matched mRNA abundance control, $H$ the RNA half-life readout, and $B$ the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation state.  The admissible positive pattern is not $Q \to H$ alone.  It is the finite statement that the residual coordinate contrast in $H$ follows $B$ after conditioning on $T$ and $A$, collapses under target-RBP loss, and returns under wild-type rescue but not under binding-defective or non-target controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&Q \not\Rightarrow \text{protein output},\qquad Q \not\Rightarrow \text{RNA-decay mechanism},\\
&Q,T,A,B \text{ jointly attached } \Rightarrow \text{test the controlled } Q\text{-associated half-life contrast},\\
&\text{target-RBP loss collapses the contrast and wild-type rescue restores it} \\
&\hspace{2em}\Rightarrow \text{bounded RBP-mediated RNA-stability bridge for the finite panel.}
\end{aligned}
$$
If the half-life contrast remains when measured RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not decoupled from coordinate state, the interpretation remains a coordinate-associated RNA-stability association.  If target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it under binding-defective and non-target controls, the RBP-mediated reading is rejected for this panel.

\paragraph{Contact boundary.}
The matched RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with curated half-life estimates or consensus half-life readouts.  The matched mRNA abundance contact supplies the transcription-layer denominator needed to keep transcript abundance separate from translation efficiency.  The ribosome-profiling contact supplies a possible same-row translation readout when it is joined to the same CDS or transcript identifiers.  None of these contacts, by name or routing alone, supplies measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective control behavior, or non-target control behavior.  Consequently the present finite rows do not license a positive RBP mechanism claim unless those additional columns are attached and the declared statistic is computed.

\paragraph{Local null.}
The null model says that, after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout are controlled, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  Rejection of this null is still insufficient for a mechanism claim unless the measured decay-factor RBP bridge also passes the occupancy, decoupling, perturbation, and rescue tests.  In particular, an RNA half-life association alone does not imply translation, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Those layers require their own row-matched measurements and finite statistics.
