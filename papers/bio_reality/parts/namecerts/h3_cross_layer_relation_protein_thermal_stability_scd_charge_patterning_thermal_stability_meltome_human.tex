\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA decay and the RBP occupancy boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel in which each row would contain a synonymous $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, a matched mRNA abundance control, an RNA half-life readout, and, when the mechanism bridge is claimed, prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation columns. The row is not a free biological assertion. It is a bounded measurement tuple whose positive interpretation depends on the simultaneous presence of the relevant columns. A row with coordinate and RNA half-life but no same-row translation readout remains an RNA-stability association. A row with coordinate, translation, and half-life but no measured decay-factor RBP occupancy remains a translation-bounded RNA half-life association. A row with occupancy but no coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls remains below the RBP-mechanism threshold.

\paragraph{Row predicate.}
For a finite panel $W$, write each admissible row as $w \in W$ with residual coordinate $q(w)$, translation readout $T(w)$, matched abundance control $A(w)$, RNA half-life $H(w)$, and an optional RBP block $B(w)$. The intended bridge is present only on the subpanel where $T(w)$ and $A(w)$ are observed and where $B(w)$ contains measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a coordinate/occupancy decoupling design, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. The local claim can be expressed as the finite condition
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_H(q \mid Z,T,A) \neq 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{RBP}) \text{ follows the measured RBP occupancy split},\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{target\ loss}) \text{ collapses},\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}) \text{ returns},\\
&\Delta_H(q \mid Z,T,A,\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target}) \text{ does not return in the same way}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z$ denotes the declared controls: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baselines. If any required column is absent, the row predicate is not false as biology; it is simply not the claimed cross-layer bridge.

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence currently attached.}
The available verified scalar for the named thermal-stability contact is a human meltome finite run with $n=6961$ rows and a deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds, verified at 2026-06-27T08:56:11+00:00 under run id 8873440304f9. Two linked cross-layer assays are also present as finite anchors outside the RNA-decay RBP bridge: a degron terminal-motif stability run with $n_{\mathrm{proteins}}=5303$, $n_{\mathrm{Cterm}}=5303$, and runtime $40.3029$ seconds under run id 1a932ef5684e; and an acidic-microenvironment ubiquitylation occupancy run with $n_{\mathrm{prots}}=2883$, $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=10410$, and $2000$ actual permutations under run id 0b96a8e4da3c. These numbers establish that the surrounding BioReality ledger contains finite row-bearing assays, but they do not supply the same-window RNA half-life, translation, mRNA abundance, RBP occupancy, and perturbation/rescue matrix required for the present RBP-decay bridge. In particular, the $6961$-row thermal-stability result cannot be re-used as an RNA half-life or RBP occupancy statistic.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA windows with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and the same-row translation-readout boundary are attached. In finite-row terms, after conditioning on $Z$, $T$, and $A$, the coordinate label is not allowed to explain half-life unless the effect is also aligned with the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy split and passes the perturbation/rescue tests. The null therefore has two separate failure points for overinterpretation. First, a coordinate-associated half-life difference without the translation boundary is only a bounded RNA half-life association. Second, a coordinate-associated half-life difference with translation but without measured occupancy and intervention rows is not an RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The chapter accepts only the following scoped readback: a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate-associated RNA half-life effect may be reported as a bounded cross-layer RNA-decay/RBP bridge if and only if the same finite panel contains the coordinate label, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared statistic satisfies the occupancy-following, loss-collapse, and rescue-return clauses. Otherwise the coordinate remains a descriptor of synonymous RNA windows, or at most a finite RNA-stability association with an explicit missing-column boundary.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation output, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological law follows from RNA half-life alone. Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not an RNA half-life mechanism. Ribosome profiling or translation efficiency is a translation boundary, not an RBP perturbation experiment. A thermal-stability, degron, or ubiquitylation finite assay may be cited as a separate row-bearing contact, but it cannot substitute for same-window RBP occupancy or rescue data. The positive bridge is therefore deliberately narrow: it is a finite, row-local claim about whether an RNA half-life difference follows a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy pattern after the translation and abundance boundaries are present.
