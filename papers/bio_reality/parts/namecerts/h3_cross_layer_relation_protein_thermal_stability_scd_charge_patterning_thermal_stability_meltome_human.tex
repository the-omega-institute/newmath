\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This BioReality record treats a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate window as a finite row label, not as a biological mechanism by itself.  The intended carrier is a matched RNA/CDS window table whose rows contain a residual coordinate, the amino-acid and local-sequence controls needed to compare synonymous windows, a same-row translation readout when available, a matched mRNA-abundance control, an RNA half-life readout, and prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation columns only when those columns are independently attached.  In this row family, a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate contrast is admissible only after amino-acid composition, local sequence, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and simpler-baseline controls have been declared on the same finite table.

\paragraph{Permitted statistic.}
Let each admissible row $i$ carry a residual coordinate $q_i$, a translation readout $T_i$ when present, matched transcript abundance $A_i$, RNA half-life $H_i$, control vector $Z_i$, and measured decay-factor RBP occupancy $O_i$ only when the experiment supplies it.  The bridge statistic is allowed to ask whether the residual-coordinate contrast in $H_i$ is explained through measured occupancy and perturbation behavior after the translation boundary and abundance control are present:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\in B^{*}_{Q6},\\
T_i &\neq \varnothing,\\
A_i,H_i,O_i,Z_i &\text{ are observed on the same finite row},\\
\Delta_H(q_i) &\text{ follows } O_i \text{ under coordinate/occupancy decoupling.}
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive bridge therefore needs more than a half-life association.  It needs the effect to track measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, collapse when the target RBP is lost, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.  If any of those columns are absent, the coordinate may still be reported as a bounded RNA-stability association or as a descriptive residual label, but not as an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.

\paragraph{Null exchangeability.}
The local null is that, after the declared controls and the same-row translation boundary are imposed, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  Written as a finite-row criterion, the null says that no admissible residual-coordinate partition produces a half-life contrast beyond the controls:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid Z_i,T_i,A_i
\end{aligned}
$$
for the rows on which $T_i$ and $A_i$ are present.  If measured occupancy is absent, unchanged across the contrast, or not experimentally decoupled from coordinate state, then an apparent $q_i$--$H_i$ association remains below the mechanism threshold.  If RBP loss does not remove the half-life difference, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it while binding-defective and non-target controls remain inactive, the proposed bridge is rejected for this panel.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
This chapter does not infer translation, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence law from RNA half-life.  The ribosome-profiling contact can supply a translation-efficiency boundary only after a same-row joined matrix exists; matched mRNA abundance is a control denominator, not a translation or decay mechanism; curated half-life rows are RNA-stability readouts, not RBP occupancy or perturbation data.  The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate remains an internal row coordinate until a layer-matched experiment supplies the corresponding biological measurement.  Consequently, the present record is ready as a sharply bounded BioReality chapter, but its positive RBP-decay bridge is conditional on the missing same-window occupancy, decoupling, loss, rescue, and control arms.
