\subsection{A Translation-Bounded RNA Half-Life RBP Bridge for Residual Q6 Windows}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The record considered here is a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate labels, synonymous window annotations, amino-acid-composition controls, local sequence controls, same-row translation readouts when available, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, and externally attached decay-factor RNA-binding-protein columns when those columns are measured on the same window system. The biological object is not a free-standing claim that codon geometry determines RNA stability. It is a bounded query about whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA windows remain separated in RNA half-life only when a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy pattern is also present, experimentally separable from the coordinate label, and perturbable in the expected direction.

The row count already verified for the linked human meltome thermal-stability surface is $6961$, with experiment run id $8873440304f9$ and deterministic runtime bucket $70.0$ seconds. That number is used here only as a checked finite-row scale for a related cross-layer protein-stability surface, not as evidence for RNA decay or RBP occupancy. Two additional checked cross-layer panels give context for the finite-row discipline: the degron terminal-motif stability panel has $5303$ C-terminal rows over $5303$ proteins under run id $1a932ef5684e$, and the ubiquitylation acidic-microenvironment panel has $10410$ sites across $2883$ proteins with $2000$ actual permutations under run id $0b96a8e4da3c$. These scalar checks show that the surrounding BioReality workflow uses explicit finite rows and declared computations; they do not by themselves supply the RBP-decay rows needed for the present RNA-half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Bridge statistic.}
For a row $i$, let $q_i$ denote the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate class of the synonymous RNA window, let $T_i$ denote the same-row translation readout when present, let $A_i$ denote matched mRNA abundance, let $H_i$ denote RNA half-life, and let $O_i$ denote measured occupancy by the prespecified decay-factor RBP. The intended finite readout is a controlled comparison of $H_i$ between high-coordinate and low-coordinate windows after conditioning on the usual transcript and sequence controls and after checking that the apparent coordinate effect follows $O_i$ rather than surviving as an unexplained coordinate-only association. In schematic form, the positive bridge would require the same finite panel to support
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\leadsto H_i \quad \text{only within a row system carrying } T_i, A_i, O_i, \\
q_i &\not\equiv O_i \quad \text{under coordinate/occupancy decoupling}, \\
\Delta H(q_i \mid O_i) &\text{ collapses after target-RBP loss}, \\
\Delta H(q_i \mid O_i) &\text{ returns after wild-type rescue but not after binding-defective or non-target controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite criterion, not a mechanistic license. The coordinate label is allowed to participate only when the translation boundary, matched mRNA control, measured occupancy column, loss condition, rescue condition, and negative-control conditions are all present in the same declared row universe.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The operative null states that, after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation-readout boundary, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. If the labels remain predictive only when RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or confounded with coordinate state, the record stays at the level of a bounded RNA-stability association. If target-RBP loss does not remove the half-life separation, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it while binding-defective or non-target controls fail to do so, the RBP bridge is not established. If the translation readout is missing, the result cannot be promoted beyond an RNA-layer readout, even if an RNA half-life difference is numerically present.

\paragraph{What the available contacts can and cannot supply.}
The matched RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with curated half-life estimates or normalized consensus half-life readouts. It is a valid boundary for asking whether RNA stability can be joined to the coordinate panel, but the listed payloads are not same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation/rescue panels. The matched mRNA abundance contact supplies a transcription-layer denominator or control surface, not translation realization and not RNA half-life evidence by itself. The ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contact can supply a same-CDS translation readout when joined to matched mRNA abundance, but it is not an RNA-decay assay and does not observe RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, or binding-defective controls.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim rule.}
No translation, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal sequence law follows from RNA half-life alone. No RBP mechanism follows from coordinate-associated half-life unless measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is attached, decoupled from coordinate state, and tested by target loss plus wild-type rescue against binding-defective or non-target controls. No protein-layer interpretation follows unless the same-row translation readout is present and separately survives its own controls. In the current finite-row state, the admissible conclusion is therefore conditional: the record specifies exactly what a positive RNA-decay RBP bridge would have to contain, while the presently named contacts stop short of measuring the decisive occupancy and perturbation columns.
