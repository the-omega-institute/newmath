\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA half-life and the decay-factor RBP bridge}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel in which each row carries a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate label, the corresponding amino-acid window, local composition controls, a same-row translation readout when available, matched mRNA abundance, and an RNA half-life readout. The intended bridge to RNA decay is narrower than an ordinary RNA-stability association: the row must also be joined to a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or control column, and the occupancy state must be experimentally separable from the coordinate state. The claim is therefore not that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ geometry is itself a biological mechanism. The claim is only that a finite coordinate-labeled panel may be used to test whether a coordinate-associated half-life difference tracks a measured RBP-decay surface after translation and abundance boundaries have already been represented on the same rows.

\paragraph{Admissible readout.}
For a row $r$, write $q(r)$ for the high- or low-coordinate synonymous window label, $T(r)$ for the same-row translation readout, $A(r)$ for matched mRNA abundance, $H(r)$ for RNA half-life, and $O(r)$ for measured occupancy by the nominated decay-factor RBP. The positive bridge requires a statistic whose support is contained in rows with all five quantities present, together with loss and rescue controls. In schematic finite form the permitted comparison is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H \mid q=\mathrm{high},T,A,O,Z\bigr)
          - \operatorname{stat}\bigl(H \mid q=\mathrm{low},T,A,O,Z\bigr),\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wt\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline controls. The finite test is broken if the half-life difference remains only a coordinate label effect while measured RBP occupancy is absent, unchanged, or not decoupled from coordinate state.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The surrounding BioReality table already contains several finite cross-layer runs, but these runs do not themselves supply the RBP-decay bridge. The protein thermal-stability surface for the human meltome-linked SCD charge-patterning record reports $n=6961$ rows, with experiment run id $8873440304f9$ and a deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds. A separate C-terminal degron terminal-motif stability run reports $n_{\mathrm{cterm}}=5303$ and $n_{\mathrm{proteins}}=5303$ under run id $1a932ef5684e$, with runtime $40.3029$ seconds. The ubiquitylation acidic-microenvironment occupancy surface reports $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=10410$, $n_{\mathrm{prots}}=2883$, and $2000$ actual permutations under run id $0b96a8e4da3c$. These scalar facts show that the table is using finite, auditable row sets for cross-layer contacts, but none of the three supplies the missing same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective control required here.

\paragraph{Boundary condition.}
The RNA half-life contact contributes only organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with curated half-life estimates or normalized consensus half-life readouts. The matched mRNA contact contributes an abundance denominator or covariate for separating transcript abundance from translation efficiency. The ribosome-profiling contact contributes a translation-efficiency or ribosome-occupancy surface when the same organism and CDS rows can be joined. Taken together, these contacts can define a translation-bounded RNA half-life association, but they do not create an RNA-decay mechanism. In particular, half-life alone cannot be read as protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general sequence law.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
The local null says that after the stated controls are imposed, high- and low-coordinate synonymous windows are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. A non-null RNA-stability association would still remain below the RBP bridge unless the nominated RBP occupancy follows the coordinate-associated half-life difference, target-RBP loss collapses the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it. Without that perturbation and rescue pattern, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor of a finite RNA-stability association rather than a cross-layer RBP-decay explanation.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim scope.}
This chapter does not infer translation from RNA half-life, does not infer a protein abundance result from transcript stability, and does not infer folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate. Those statements would require separate layer-matched measurements and their own finite statistics. The present record is ready as a constrained null-boundary chapter: it specifies the exact finite rows, controls, perturbation requirements, and break conditions under which an RNA half-life association could be promoted to a measured decay-factor RBP bridge, while recording that the decisive RBP occupancy and perturbation rows are not yet present.
