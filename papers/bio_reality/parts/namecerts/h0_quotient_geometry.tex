\paragraph{Boundary interpretation for ORF eligibility.}
The biological object attached to this row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration.  The row does not turn the quotient geometry into an ORF call.  Before a sequence interval, transcript, or curated annotation is attached, the admissible reading is only that the geometry supplies a boundary test for what would have to be checked.  The probe conditions state that the packet breaks if it asserts ORF eligibility, translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, or biological function from start/stop enumeration alone.  Thus the quotient data may organize a finite set of codon windows, but it may not certify that any such window occurs as an observed biological interval.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The claim $h0.quotient.geometry$ is ready as a finite quotient-geometry statement.  It verifies the codon lists $R$ and $M$, the saturation boundary, the face vector of $M$, the ten-point quotient image, and the quotient face vector.  It does not by itself establish ORF eligibility in a biological sample.  Even when the curated-interval contact is considered, the resulting claim remains confined to interval-level ORF eligibility for that dataset and classifier.  No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the quotient coordinate computation unless an additional contact, with its own finite audit trail, supplies that stronger layer.  The self-check row is consistent with this restriction: it records a boundary-only status and explicitly prevents codon geometry from being promoted across biological layers.
