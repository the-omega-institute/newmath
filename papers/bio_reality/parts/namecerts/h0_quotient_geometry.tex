\paragraph{Biological layer guarded by contact.}
The biological object attached to this row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration.  That wording is intentionally weaker than translation.  The seed layer is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$, and the packet itself has no attached contact rows in its local contact field.  Consequently, the finite geometry can only identify a structured coordinate condition that may be tested against a separate biological interval source.  It cannot by itself say that a nucleotide segment is an open reading frame in a genome, that a transcript is present, that a product is translated, that a folded structure exists, that a molecule is physically admissible, that a biological function is realized, that a mechanism has been established, or that a universal biological law has been found.

\paragraph{Separate interval evidence.}
The row set nevertheless records what happens when an external curated interval contact is supplied elsewhere in the same finite audit bundle.  The start--stop window enumeration run $\mathrm{c26a0d6656f7}$ loads one external nucleotide-window record, classifies one input window, and marks its scope as external window start--stop enumeration only, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$.  A stronger curated interval contact, experiment $\mathrm{7c9b30b02d46}$, loads $168$ rows from an NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval source for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  In that contact row, the required input classes are positive curated ORF intervals, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary cases.  The positive interval statistic accepts $72$ of $72$ positives, giving accept rate $1.0$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched decoy statistic rejects $72$ of $72$ decoys, again with rate $1.0$ and the same reported upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison records $72$ wins from $72$ pairs, win rate $1.0$, and the same one-sided binomial value.  Three held-out splits are listed: left, middle, and right locus folds, each with $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$.  Boundary cases have $n=24$ and promoted count $0$.

\paragraph{Protocol separation.}
The interval-contact protocol was frozen before the cited external observation: the protocol timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$29\mathrm{T}19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, while the external evidence observation timestamp is $2026$-$05$-$30\mathrm{T}10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  The row states that classifier rules, baselines, input classes, and held-out splits were fixed before observation, and that the analysis did not use translation or structure input.  This matters because the geometry row and the interval-contact row answer different questions.  The quotient geometry supplies a finite symbolic partition over $M$ and its quotient image $\pi M$; the curated contact tests interval eligibility against an externally supplied biological annotation set.  The former is not allowed to manufacture the latter.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is therefore bounded and positive only at the stated layer.  The data support an internally checked quotient geometry for $M$, a motif compression row for $R$, a saturation row with an empty $f3$ boundary mismatch list, and a separately contacted ORF-eligibility audit with $168$ curated interval rows and exact $72/72$ positive, $72/72$ decoy, and $72/72$ paired outcomes.  The self-check row reports three checked gates and explicitly records that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Hence the chapter may record a ready finite BioReality claim for start--stop interval eligibility under separate curated contact, while still denying every stronger reading: no translation, no folding, no physical admissibility, no function, no mechanism, and no universality follows from the quotient geometry or from start--stop enumeration alone.
