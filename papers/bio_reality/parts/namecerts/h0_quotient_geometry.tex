\paragraph{Finite codon universe.}
This record concerns the finite combinatorial coordinate that precedes any biological assertion about open-reading-frame eligibility. The biological object under discussion is start/stop codon enumeration as a possible boundary test for an ORF interval, not translation, expression, folding, activity, mechanism, or evolutionary conservation. The verified finite universe $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The separate motif-compression row identifies the reduced set $R$ as

&\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},

with no missing and no extra codons in the finite check. These are syntax-level rows over codon labels. They do not by themselves say that a genome, transcript, protein product, or cellular event has been observed.

In particular, the quotient geometry is a combinatorial compression of the codon table fragment, not a biological interval annotation.

\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The $f_3$ saturation row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is $M$ with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ absent. The same finite row records
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where the displayed value is the full $20$-element list $M$ above, and the boundary list for $f_3$ is empty. This is a useful internal consistency check on the codon-coordinate construction: the quotient, motif compression, and saturation rows agree on a finite table. It is not an observation that any nucleotide interval is an ORF in a biological sample.

\paragraph{Reality-contact separation.}
The ORF-eligibility seed associated with this coordinate is therefore held at boundary status until a separate biological interval contact is attached. A start codon and a stop codon can define a syntactic window, but the existence of that window in a curated sequence, transcript, or annotation record is a further datum. Without that further datum, the only admissible statement is conditional: this quotient geometry describes what the codon-coordinate test would inspect if an interval contact were supplied. It does not assert an ORF, translation initiation, termination, peptide production, folded structure, biochemical admissibility, organismal function, regulatory mechanism, or universal biological law.

\paragraph{Attached finite interval contact.}
A later curated-interval contact supplies such a separate row for a restricted ORF-eligibility test. That contact loads $168$ rows from an NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS interval dataset for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$. It records three required input classes: boundary cases, matched non-ORF decoys, and positive curated ORFs. The positive interval test accepts $72$ of $72$ positives, with accept rate $1.0$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The matched decoy test rejects $72$ of $72$ decoys, also with one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The predeclared paired comparison records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The held-out split table has three folds, each with $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$. Boundary cases have $n=24$ and $0$ promotions.

\paragraph{Local claim.}
The chapter-specific conclusion is consequently narrow. The finite quotient row verifies the codon-coordinate geometry: $|M|=20$, $|\pi M|=10$, $f(M)=(20,38,26,8,1)$, and $f(\pi M)=(10,14,6,1)$. The motif and saturation rows verify compatible finite codon lists. The curated-interval row, when separately attached, supports ORF-eligibility classification only for its own $168$-row contact protocol. None of these rows permits promotion to translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality. Those stronger layers require their own contacts and cannot be inferred from quotient codon geometry or start/stop-window enumeration alone.
