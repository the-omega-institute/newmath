\subsection{Quotient Geometry as a Boundary Object for ORF Eligibility}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate record.}
The finite packet considered here is the quotient-geometry record with claim identifier $h0.quotient.geometry$, read in the biological layer called $orf\_eligibility$.  Its combinatorial side is fully finite.  The motif-level set $R$ was verified as the thirteen-codon set

\begin{aligned}

&UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The larger codon set $M$ was verified as the twenty-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
&CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality certificate records $M=WNR\cup CUN$ and gives $|M|=20$.  The motif compression certificate records that the displayed $R$ has no missing member and no extra member relative to the finite motif union used by the packet.

\paragraph{Boundary saturation.}
The third-neighborhood saturation check gives a second finite diagnostic.  It verifies
$$
\begin{aligned}

&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
which is $M$ with $ACA$ and $ACG$ not included.  The same run records
$$
\begin{aligned}

Thus the finite geometry has a precise boundary: the combinatorial closure captures eighteen of the twenty $M$ codons, and the remaining two codons are exactly $ACA$ and $ACG$ in the recorded certificate.  This is useful as a test scaffold because it identifies what the coordinate system can and cannot settle before biological contact is attached.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility interpretation.}
The biological reading is deliberately weaker than translation.  The seed says only that start and stop codon enumeration can define an $orf\_eligibility$ question once an external interval is supplied.  In the start-stop-window run, one external window dataset was loaded, one input window was present, and one window was classified by start-stop enumeration.  The scope string of that run is $external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$.  The run explicitly records the output as $orf\_eligibility\_only$.  Therefore the finite coordinate packet may organize a proposed boundary test, but the geometry by itself is not an observed open reading frame.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The chapter-level conclusion is therefore conditional and narrow.  The quotient geometry of $M$, the quotient classes of $\pi_M$, and the saturation boundary around $R$ provide a finite coordinate organization for an ORF-eligibility test.  The curated interval contact then shows that the frozen start-stop-window classifier separates the curated positives from matched non-ORF decoys in the recorded $168$-row dataset.  Nothing stronger follows from these rows.  The packet does not establish translation realization, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical mechanism, organism-level function, or a universal biological law.  Any promotion beyond $orf\_eligibility$ would require a separate contact whose data directly test that higher layer.
