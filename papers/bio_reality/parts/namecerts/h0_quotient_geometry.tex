\subsection{Quotient geometry as an ORF-eligibility boundary record}\label{sec:h0-quotient-geometry-orfe-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The row named \texttt{h0.quotient.geometry} records a finite codon-level quotient computation and not, by itself, an observed biological interval.  The biological reading attached to the row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration, but the row-level carrier is still marked as awaiting contact with biological observation.  The distinction is essential: a start/stop pattern can describe a proposed eligibility test, while an observed ORF interval requires a separate sequence, transcript, or curated-annotation contact.  Thus the row is usable as a boundary datum for the layer \texttt{orf\_eligibility}, but it is not a claim of translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, or a universal genomic law.

\paragraph{Codon sets used by the row.}
The finite alphabet facts used by this row are explicit.  The motif-compression row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing codons and no extra codons in the audited motif union.  The larger finite set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly the listed union
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AAG},\texttt{ACA},\texttt{ACG},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{AUG},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
&\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UCG},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UGG},\texttt{UUA},\texttt{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The saturation row further reports that the $f_{3}$ boundary is empty and that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_{3}}(R)=M\setminus\{\texttt{ACA},\texttt{ACG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so the only two codons in $M$ not reached by this saturation list are \texttt{ACA} and \texttt{ACG}.  These finite equalities are the complete mathematical input to the quotient row; they do not introduce any biochemical observation.

Its scope remains mathematical and classificatory: the quotient witnesses a compressed organization of finite codon patterns, not a biological interval seen in a genome.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility boundary.}
The ORF layer is therefore handled as an eligibility boundary.  The row allows the following limited assertion: if a later biological contact supplies a concrete nucleotide interval, then start and stop codon enumeration can be checked against that interval under a frozen eligibility classifier.  Without such a contact attached to the row, the quotient data remain a proposed test site rather than an empirical ORF record.  This is why the row has no row-level contacts and why the two boundary probes require a curated ORF interval annotation before the layer can be asserted for an observed case.

\paragraph{Contact evidence kept separate.}
The linked start/stop-window experiment reports one loaded external window and one classified input window, with scope restricted to external-window start/stop enumeration only.  A later curated interval contact is stronger but still bounded: it loads $168$ rows from a curated ORF interval dataset dated 2026-05-30, with $72$ positive curated ORFs, $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, and $24$ boundary cases.  The positive interval statistic has $72$ acceptances out of $72$ and one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$.  The matched decoy statistic has $72$ rejections out of $72$ with the same reported value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$, and the predeclared paired baseline comparison records $72$ wins out of $72$ pairs with the same value.  Three held-out splits are reported, each with $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$.  The $24$ boundary cases have zero promotions.

\paragraph{Layer discipline.}
Those contact numbers support the finite ORF-eligibility contact experiment only where the curated interval data are actually present.  They do not feed back into the quotient row as a claim that codon geometry itself discovers translation.  They also do not establish folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality.  The classifier is recorded as \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified after freezing, and the protocol records frozen baselines, input classes, held-out splits, and classifier rules before external evidence was observed.  Consequently the row has a precise two-part status: the quotient computation is a finite mathematical fact over $M$ and its quotient, while ORF eligibility becomes an empirical claim only when an independent biological interval contact supplies the observed sequence context.

\paragraph{Verdict for the row.}
The row is ready as a self-contained boundary chapter because all finite mathematical quantities needed for the quotient computation are present: $|M|=20$, the $13$-codon list $R$, the full $20$-codon list $M$, the empty $f_{3}$ boundary, the two omitted saturation codons \texttt{ACA} and \texttt{ACG}, the $10$ quotient representatives, and the two face vectors.  The biological conclusion is deliberately narrower than the surrounding contact experiments: the row supplies an eligibility gate for what can later be tested, not an intrinsic biological realization.
