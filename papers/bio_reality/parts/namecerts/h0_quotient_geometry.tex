\paragraph{Contact status for ORF eligibility.}
The seed conjecture attached to this row states only a waiting condition for $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$. In the seed packet itself, the carrier, distinctions, and readback are all marked as awaiting contact with biological data, and the internal structure list is empty. Thus the quotient rows above can organize a proposed start--stop boundary test, but they cannot assert that a biological interval exists. The two probes make this limitation explicit: a start--stop window remains a boundary record unless a separate sequence, transcript, or curated interval annotation supplies the observed interval; and the packet breaks if it records ORF eligibility in the absence of such a contact.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The quotient geometry row is therefore admissible as a finite coordinate certificate that helps define a start/stop-window eligibility test, and the later curated interval contact supplies a separate biological dataset on which that classifier is evaluated. The two roles must remain distinct. From the quotient rows, the $M$ and $R$ codon lists, the saturation equality, or the start/stop enumeration alone, one may not infer translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biochemical mechanism, or a universal biological law. Those stronger claims would require their own contacts and their own finite tests. The self-check record agrees with this reading: it reports three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. Consequently, the present chapter closes only the following narrow claim: \texttt{h0.quotient.geometry} supplies a finite, internally checked coordinate boundary for an ORF-eligibility test, while any actual ORF assertion depends on an attached external interval contact.
