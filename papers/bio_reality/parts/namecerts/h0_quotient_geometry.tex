\subsection{Quotient geometry as an ORF-eligibility boundary record}\label{sec:bioreality-h0-quotient-geometry}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate object.}
The finite record begins with a codon-side coordinate calculation and stops before any biological promotion. The verified $M$ row contains exactly $20$ codons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the verified compressed motif-side row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The run records no missing and no extra terms in the motif union representation of $R$. It also records that $M$ is the union of the WNR and CUN windows, with $|M|=20$. These are finite enumerations, not biochemical observations: they say which codon strings occur in the syntactic packet, not that a cell has translated, tolerated, conserved, or used any corresponding interval.

\paragraph{Quotient calculation.}
The quotient row records the face-count vector of $M$ as $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and the quotient face-count vector as $(10,14,6,1)$. The projected quotient has size $10$ and is represented by the ten binary rows
$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi(M)=\{&00001,00011,00101,00111,01000,01001,\\
&10001,10011,10101,10111\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records $M$ as a product decomposition. The quotient therefore supplies a finite geometry of identifications inside the codon packet: it compresses the row structure and exposes a ten-class image. The calculation is useful because an ORF-start/stop boundary predicate can be posed against a finite coordinate source. It is not useful as direct evidence of expression. Nothing in the quotient vector, the ten projected rows, or the product-decomposition flag observes a nucleotide interval in a genome, an RNA molecule, a ribosome event, a folded chain, or a selected phenotype.

\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The saturation row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so this saturation has $18$ listed codons. The same row records $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{ACA,ACG\}$ as the full $20$-codon list and records an empty $f3$ boundary. This is a sharp finite distinction: $ACA$ and $ACG$ are present in $M$ but are not in the displayed $f3$ saturation of $R$. The empty boundary entry is therefore a statement about the finite closure table supplied to the packet. It is not a license to say that every genomic candidate satisfying a start/stop pattern is an open reading frame, nor that a putative ORF has a translated product.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility gate.}
The ORF seed is intentionally weaker than an ORF claim. A separate window-enumeration run records one external nucleotide-window input, one classified window, scope limited to external start/stop enumeration, and the label \texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only}. Its source note says the finite nucleotide-window manifest is an external annotation input to the experiment rather than a fact generated by the codon geometry. Thus the start/stop calculation can classify a supplied window only after a window is actually supplied. Without such a contact, the packet remains a boundary test: it says what would be checked, not that the biological interval exists.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
A later curated-interval contact supplies a stronger finite observation while preserving the same layer boundary. The contact row contains $168$ records from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession NC\_000913.3, dated 2026-05-30. It records three required input classes: \texttt{boundary\_case}, \texttt{matched\_non\_orf\_decoy}, and \texttt{positive\_curated\_orf}. The frozen classifier is \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}, with classifier rules unmodified. Across the positive interval test, $72$ of $72$ positives are accepted, giving acceptance rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. Across matched decoys, $72$ of $72$ decoys are rejected, giving rejection rate $1.0$ and the same upper-tail value. The paired baseline sign-test records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, again with $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$. The three held-out splits each contain $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, each with positive acceptance rate $1.0$, decoy rejection rate $1.0$, and pass status true. Boundary cases record $24$ cases with $0$ promoted, so the promoted fraction is $0.0$.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore has two different assertions. First, the quotient geometry and saturation rows establish a finite codon-coordinate packet: $|M|=20$, $|R|=13$, $|\pi(M)|=10$, face-count vectors $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and $(10,14,6,1)$, and the displayed saturation behavior. Second, the curated contact establishes a finite ORF-eligibility observation over a stated external interval dataset. Neither assertion entails translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, evolutionary role, or a universal rule for unseen windows. Those stronger claims require separate contacts of the appropriate biological kind. The self-check row is consistent with this restriction: it records a boundary statement saying that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers, with $3$ checked gates. The admissible reading is consequently narrow and testable: start/stop-window eligibility may be recorded only for externally supplied windows or curated intervals, and the codon quotient alone remains a coordinate calculation rather than a biological realization.
