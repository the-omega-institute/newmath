The second part is a boundary rule saying that this geometry may prepare a testable ORF-eligibility question, but it does not itself count as an observed ORF.

The finite quotient geometry of $M$ is verified, the codon-name rows supporting $R$, $M$, saturation, and the quotient are populated by explicit finite lists, and separate curated interval rows show that an externally supplied ORF-interval contact can be tested by a frozen start--stop eligibility classifier.  Without such a separate biological contact, the geometry row remains only a preparation for a question. No translation realization, folded molecular structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universal law follows from the quotient geometry, from the start--stop enumeration, or from the curated interval row unless another independently specified contact supplies evidence for that higher layer.
