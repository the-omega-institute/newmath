\paragraph{ORF-eligibility reading.}
The biological object named in the row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start/stop codon enumeration.  The finite geometry can suggest where an eligibility test should be asked, but it does not itself observe a sequence interval.  Two boundary probes record this limitation.  One says that a start/stop-window seed must remain only a proposed test unless an external sequence, transcript, or curated annotation supplies the interval being tested.  The other says that an ORF-eligibility record is inadmissible as a biological claim when no sequence database, transcript evidence, or curated annotation can test the proposed interval.  Thus the local rule is: codon-window geometry may form a finite question, but the answer requires a separate contact with biological annotation or sequence evidence.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The quotient computation does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.  It also does not establish that any codon window is realized as an expressed ORF.  Those stronger readings require a separate contact whose data type can test them.  Even when a curated interval contact is present, the accepted conclusion is confined to ORF interval eligibility under the stated finite annotation protocol; it does not become protein production, structural order, physical viability, cellular function, or a global biological law.  The self-check row agrees with this boundary: it records $3$ checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Consequently, the present chapter treats \texttt{h0.quotient.geometry} as a finite quotient and enumeration result whose proper biological role is to delimit a testable ORF-eligibility question, not to answer higher biological questions by geometry alone.
