\subsection{Quotient geometry at the ORF-eligibility boundary}
\label{sec:h0-quotient-geometry-orf-eligibility-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite codon substrate.}
This record isolates a finite codon geometry before any biological interval is allowed to count as an observed open reading frame.  The verified codon family $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same audit identifies $M$ with the union of the recorded $\mathrm{WNR}$ and $\mathrm{CUN}$ classes, with no additional codons beyond the listed twenty.  A smaller motif-compression set $R$ is separately verified as
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing entries and no surplus entries in the compression check.  These finite lists are the only codon-level objects used here; they do not by themselves assert transcript presence, ribosomal use, protein production, molecular stability, or cellular role.

\paragraph{Boundary interpretation.}
The quotient rows are treated as a finite eligibility coordinate, not as a biological observation.  A separate one-window enumeration contact reports that an external window dataset was loaded with $1$ input window and that all $1$ window was classified by start--stop enumeration.  That result is explicitly scoped to $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility}$ only.  It therefore says that the finite start--stop test can classify the supplied window, but it does not say that the window is translated, folded, physically admissible, functional, mechanistic, conserved, or universal.  The self-check records $3$ checked gates and states the same boundary: no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
A stronger contact exists for curated interval data, but it is still layer-limited.  The curated interval dataset contains $168$ rows from an NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS-interval source for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$.  The required classes are present: positive curated ORF intervals, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary cases.  The positive interval test accepts $72$ of $72$ positives, and the matched decoy test rejects $72$ of $72$ decoys.  Both exact one-sided binomial upper-tail values are recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
p = 2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The predeclared paired baseline also records $72$ wins in $72$ pairs, with the same upper-tail value.  The held-out checks use three splits; each split has $24$ positives accepted at rate $1.0$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1.0$.  The boundary-case control has $24$ cases and promotes $0$ of them, giving promoted fraction $0.0$.  The protocol was fixed before the external observation: classifier rules, input classes, held-out splits, and baselines were fixed before the evidence timestamp, and the classifier is recorded as geometry-only.

\paragraph{Local claim.}
The resulting claim is deliberately narrow.  The finite quotient computation supplies a ten-row quotient of a twenty-codon family, the motif and saturation audits describe how that family is organized, and the curated interval contact supports an ORF-eligibility classifier on the stated finite dataset.  Nothing in this chapter upgrades codon geometry or interval eligibility into translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or a general law over genomes.  Any such assertion would require an additional contact whose measured object belongs to that higher biological layer.
