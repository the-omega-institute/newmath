\paragraph{Finite coordinate content.}
The finite packet records a codon-level geometry before any biological interval is allowed to count as an observed open reading frame.  The verified carrier $M$ has $20$ codon entries, namely
$$\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The associated motif-reduced set $R$ has $13$ entries,
$$\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\},
\end{aligned}$$
with no missing and no extra codons relative to the motif-union computation.  A separate finite check identifies $M$ with $WNR\cup CUN$ and again returns size $20$.  Thus the chapter is not using an inferred biological object; it is using an explicitly enumerated codon table fragment and its quotient coordinates as the object under audit.

The $f_3$ saturation row also remains bounded: $\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)$ is $M$ without $ACA$ and $ACG$, while the $f_3$ boundary check returns the empty list.  Equivalently, the finite closure test supplies a controlled codon-coordinate boundary rather than a biological realization.

\paragraph{ORF eligibility scope.}
The biological layer named here is open-reading-frame eligibility from start/stop codon enumeration.  At the seed stage, no sequence interval, transcript measurement, or curated annotation is attached to this finite-row record.  Therefore the quotient geometry by itself does not assert that any interval is an ORF.  It only says that the codon enumeration has a fixed finite form that could be used in a later test once an independent biological interval is supplied.  A separate start/stop-window experiment classified $1$ input window, loaded $1$ external window dataset, and explicitly kept its scope at external window start/stop enumeration only.  Its own output records the non-promotion tag $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$, so the enumeration does not become translation, protein structure, physical admissibility, or biological function.

\paragraph{Reality-contact comparison.}
The later curated-interval contact is informative because it shows what a genuine ORF-eligibility contact looks like.  That contact loaded $168$ rows from a curated interval dataset based on NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for the Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession NC\_000913.3.  The protocol was frozen at $2026$-$05$-$29\,19{:}11{:}13+00{:}00$, before the external evidence observation at $2026$-$05$-$30\,10{:}29{:}31+00{:}00$.  It used $72$ positive curated ORF intervals, $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, and $24$ boundary cases.  The positive accept rate was $72/72=1$, the matched decoy rejection rate was $72/72=1$, and both corresponding one-sided binomial tail values were $2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}$.  The predeclared paired baseline comparison also returned $72$ wins out of $72$ pairs with the same one-sided value $2.117582368135751\cdot 10^{-22}$.  Three held-out splits each reported $24$ positives accepted at rate $1$ and $24$ decoys rejected at rate $1$, while the $24$ boundary cases had promoted count $0$ and promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
Those curated-contact numbers do not retroactively upgrade the quotient geometry.  They only demonstrate that when a separate biological annotation contact is present, ORF-eligibility can be tested in a finite and externally anchored way.  The present quotient packet remains an $h0$ geometry record: it fixes $M$, $R$, $\pi M$, and their face-vector data, and it permits a boundary question about start/stop-window eligibility.  It does not establish translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, evolutionary universality, or a global biological law.  Any such assertion would require its own independent contact and its own finite audit trail.  The self-check agrees with this interpretation: it counted $3$ checked gates and recorded that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
