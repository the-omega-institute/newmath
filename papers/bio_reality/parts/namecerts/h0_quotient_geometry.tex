\paragraph{Saturation boundary.}
The third-face saturation check records
$\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}$.
The displayed saturation omits exactly $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ from $M$, and the recorded $f_3$ boundary obstruction is empty. In finite terms,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 |M|&=20,\\
 |\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)|&=18,\\
 M\setminus \operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)&=\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a boundary datum for a quotient-and-saturation pattern. It is not an empirical assertion that any of these windows is an open reading frame in an organism.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility interpretation.}
The biological reading attached to this record is deliberately weak. The intended layer is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration. Before contact with an observed sequence, transcript, or curated interval annotation, the finite quotient geometry is only a proposed test surface: it says what kind of finite start/stop-window question would be asked, not that the question has already been answered by biology. The boundary probes therefore require a curated ORF interval annotation before an eligibility assertion is made.

One later finite contact does instantiate that kind of test in a restricted setting. The start/stop-window enumeration record loaded one external window, classified that one input window, and explicitly marked its scope as external window start/stop enumeration only. A stronger curated-interval contact loaded $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$. Its frozen protocol records $72$ positive curated intervals and $72$ matched non-ORF decoys across three held-out splits, with $24$ positives and $24$ decoys per split. Each split accepted all positives and rejected all decoys. The positive exact binomial statistic records $72/72$ accepted with one-sided upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$; the matched-decoy statistic records $72/72$ rejected with the same value; the paired baseline sign test records $72/72$ wins with the same value. Boundary cases have $n=24$ and promoted count $0$.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The finite quotient geometry and the curated interval contact must be kept in different evidential roles. The quotient facts $f(M)=(20,38,26,8,1)$, $f(\pi M)=(10,14,6,1)$, $|\pi M|=10$, and the listed representatives are internal finite geometry over codon labels. The curated ORF contact is an external finite annotation test with $168$ rows, $72$ positive cases, $72$ matched decoys, and $24$ boundary cases. Neither source, alone or together, licenses a claim of translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universal biological law. Those claims would require separate contacts whose measurements address those layers directly.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The record is therefore admissible as a finite quotient-and-boundary chapter for ORF-eligibility testing. It establishes that the codon table, motif compression, quotient representatives, saturation calculation, and curated interval controls are numerically specified and mutually compatible within the declared finite rows. Its strongest local conclusion is conditional and layer-limited: start/stop-window eligibility may be discussed only where the external interval contact supplies the observed biological interval, and even there the result remains an eligibility classification rather than evidence for translation, folding, physical viability, function, mechanism, or universality.
