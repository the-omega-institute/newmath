The recorded third-coordinate boundary is empty, so this part is a closed finite enumeration statement rather than a biological observation.

\paragraph{Boundary for ORF eligibility.}
The biological object named by the row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration. The seed statement remains at the $orf\_eligibility$ layer unless a distinct sequence, transcript, or curated interval contact is present. The checked geometry can say that certain finite codon patterns and quotient coordinates have been enumerated; it cannot by itself say that a genomic interval is an observed ORF. In particular, the local cannot-claim boundary is: no translation, no folded protein structure, no physical admissibility, no biological function, no mechanism, and no universal biological law follow from this quotient geometry alone.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite quotient record and the curated biological contact must be kept logically separate. The quotient computation supplies a small, explicitly enumerated coordinate geometry over codon symbols. The curated interval contact supplies an independent biological test over external annotation rows. The former can organize a seed boundary for ORF eligibility; the latter can test a restricted interval-level claim. Neither component, and not their conjunction in this row, licenses translation realization, protein folding, physical viability, biological function, mechanism, or universality. The result is therefore ready as a chapter-level finite record of quotient geometry plus a locally stated promotion boundary for ORF eligibility.
