\paragraph{Finite codon geometry.}
This BioReality record isolates the finite geometry that is actually present before any biological interval is observed.  The verified quotient row for claim $h0.quotient.geometry$ reports a finite codon complex $M$ with face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$
and a quotient image $\pi_M$ with $10$ listed quotient rows and quotient face vector
$$
\begin{aligned}
  f(\pi_M)=(10,14,6,1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The ten quotient rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}

The product-decomposition check is empty-valued, so the row records a negative or vacuous product obstruction rather than an additional geometric factorization.  The content of the row is therefore combinatorial: a finite list of codon-level coordinates and quotient coordinates, not an assertion that a nucleotide interval has been realized in an organism.

\paragraph{Boundary status for ORF eligibility.}
The biological layer named in this record is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration.  The seed statement is deliberately weaker than an ORF observation: it says that the finite codon geometry describes a pattern that could be tested once an external sequence, transcript, or curated interval annotation is supplied.  In the row under discussion, the top-level contact list is empty, and the probes explicitly require a curated ORF interval annotation before the boundary packet can be used as an eligibility claim.  Thus the quotient geometry can organize a start/stop-window test, but it is not itself a contact with an observed biological interval.

\paragraph{Separate contact evidence and its scope.}
Two linked ORF rows show what such a separate contact can look like without changing the meaning of the quotient-geometry row.  The start/stop-window row $h2.orf.start_stop.window_eligibility$ loads one external nucleotide-window record from a curated manifest dated $2026$-$05$-$25$ and classifies all $1$ input window by start/stop enumeration.  Its scope is explicitly restricted to external-window start/stop enumeration, and it records the layer as $orf\_eligibility\_only$.  The curated interval contact row $h2.orf_eligibility.curated_interval_contact$ then loads $168$ rows from a curated ORF interval dataset derived from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for the Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 complete genome accession $NC\_000913.3$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  Within that separate contact record, $72$ positive intervals are accepted with accept rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$; $72$ matched non-ORF decoys are rejected with reject rate $1.0$ and the same reported upper-tail value; $72$ predeclared positive/decoy pairs give $72$ wins, win rate $1.0$, and the same reported value.  The held-out split check uses three folds, each with $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, and each fold reports positive accept rate $1.0$, decoy reject rate $1.0$, and passed status.  A boundary-case check records $24$ cases with $0$ promotions and promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Protocol separation.}
The curated interval contact also records that the protocol was fixed before the external observation: the protocol time is $2026$-$05$-$29T19:11:13+00:00$, while external evidence was observed at $2026$-$05$-$30T10:29:31+00:00$.  The classifier identifier is $orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility$; classifier rules were not modified; input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were frozen; and the analysis used no translation or structure input.  This is why the contact row may support interval-level ORF eligibility on its own dataset while the quotient-geometry row remains a finite codon-coordinate record.  The self-check row reinforces this separation by reporting $3$ checked gates and by stating that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter makes no claim that the quotient geometry establishes translation, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical mechanism, organismal function, or a universal biological law.  Even the separate curated interval contact is limited to external ORF interval eligibility in the stated dataset and does not establish translation, protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, global lawfulness, or classification outside the recorded controls.  The quotient coordinates, the $20$-element codon carrier, the $13$-element motif union, the $18$-element saturation, the ten quotient rows, and the reported ORF contact statistics are therefore read as finite evidential constraints with sharply bounded scope: geometry can define the enumerative test, and curated external intervals can test eligibility, but neither component licenses promotion to higher biological layers without another contact designed for that layer.
