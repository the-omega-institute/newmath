\subsection{Rank-admissible translation gate for the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual basis}
\label{sec:biorreality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite residualized design problem, not a biological promotion claim.  Its rows are organism-indexed codon and readout records; its columns contain $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, candidate translation-readout surfaces, and controls $Z$ for amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline.  A proposed entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is admissible only when both sides have first been residualized against $Z$ and the remaining row space is not exhausted by the fitted design.  Thus the gate asks whether a translation-readout survival statistic is testable at all, before asking whether it is positive.

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
For a residual coordinate $q_i$ and translation readout $T_j$, the operative statistic is allowed to enter the survival ledger only if the residualized matrix retains independent degrees of freedom after the control columns have been removed.  In symbols, the local test is organized as
$$\begin{aligned}
\widetilde q_i &= (I-P_Z)q_i,\\
\widetilde T_j &= (I-P_Z)T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij} &> 0 \quad\text{is reportable only if}\quad
\operatorname{rank}([Z,q_i,T_j]) < n
\quad\text{and held-out rows remain.}
\end{aligned}$$
If the post-control design is saturated, or if the only positive score is an in-sample artifact, the entry remains a coupling or data-request record.  In particular, an all-$1.0$ fitted surface on a three-organism panel is treated as rank exhaustion, not as translation survival.

\paragraph{Observed residual-basis rows.}
The finite computation identified four organisms with sufficient local readout data and two organisms requiring more data.  The computed organisms were \textit{Danio rerio}, \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, \textit{Homo sapiens}, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; \textit{Gallus gallus} and \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155 were marked \emph{needs data}.  The recursive residual extraction used the stated regularization $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length term $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  The joined row counts were $14410$ for \textit{D. rerio}, $3415$ for \textit{E. coli}, $16008$ for human, and $3046$ for yeast.  The candidate counts were respectively $1$, $2$, $6$, and $9$.

\paragraph{Null comparisons.}
The degree-of-freedom matched deterministic Gaussian nulls show that several selected readout blocks absorb residual energy, but this is still descriptive compression unless the rank and held-out translation boundary are passed.  The \textit{D. rerio} step selected measured tRNA/tAI with increment $0.02081710285096942$ against null p95 $0.0002306374780841125$.  In \textit{E. coli}, mRNA exceeded the null at step $1$ with increment $0.0017706954554909888$ against $0.0013371669456573104$, while measured TE at step $2$ did not exceed the null: $0.0002819723320884449$ against $0.0010272364744326006$.  Human selected PTM density, complex membership, transmembrane count, measured tRNA/tAI, domain count, and localization; the first four and localization exceeded their nulls, while domain count did not, with increment $3.840392718095609\times 10^{-5}$ against null p95 $0.00021932006910265595$.  Yeast selected measured TE, PTM density, transmembrane count, turnover, complex membership, measured tRNA/tAI, domain count, mRNA, and localization; measured TE, PTM density, transmembrane count, turnover, and localization exceeded their matched nulls, while complex membership, measured tRNA/tAI, domain count, and mRNA did not.

\paragraph{Coupling is not survival.}
A separate finite coupling computation loaded three organisms: human, yeast, and \textit{E. coli} K-12.  It computed nine synonymous residual coordinates: $\mathrm{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{AUA}$, $K_{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,\mathrm{vs}\,UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,\mathrm{vs}\,UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,\mathrm{vs}\,UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,\mathrm{vs}\,AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,\mathrm{vs}\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Against a proxy tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum, the maximum absolute coupling was $1882464.1106377328$, all nine entries were nonzero above the $0.01$ threshold, and the break condition requiring all nine $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ was not met.  This establishes a finite coupling contact only.  It does not establish any $S^{QT}_{ij}>0$ entry, because the proxy readout is not a held-out, rank-admissible, $Z$-controlled translation survival surface.

\paragraph{Boundary against overreading.}
The paper therefore records a refusal boundary with finite numerical content.  The residual basis is persistent across the computed organisms, the greedy readout blocks sometimes exceed matched nulls, and the proxy coupling matrix is nonzero.  None of these facts by itself reports ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Such claims require their own layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.  The branchpoint contact, for example, has $n=10584$ expression-controlled rows with runtime $138.47155$ seconds; the uORF/Kozak contact has $41862$ fixed and $16739$ varying rows with two-sample concordance; the mRNA half-life contact has $16500$ human and $18924$ mouse rows with raw $\rho=0.126224$.  These are valid finite contacts for their own questions, but they do not convert the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual gate into translation survival.  The current conclusion is narrower: a proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry must pass residualized rank admissibility and held-out support before it may be called translation survival; otherwise it remains coupling-only or needs more data.
