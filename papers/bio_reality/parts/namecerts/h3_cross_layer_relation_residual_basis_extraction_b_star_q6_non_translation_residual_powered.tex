\subsection{Rank-admissible translation-survival gate for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{subsec:q6-rank-admissible-translation-survival-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object of this record is not a translation claim.  It is a finite admissibility test for deciding when a proposed entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ may even be read as a falsifiable survival statistic.  The carrier consists of residualized $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinates $q_i$, organism-indexed translation readout surfaces $T_j$, and a control block $Z$ containing amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding length, and the $M$-density baseline.  The intended readback is per entry: after removing $Z$ from both $q_i$ and $T_j$, the remaining rows must still have non-saturated rank, positive held-out degrees of freedom, and enough independent organism or coding-sequence support for a failure of survival to be observable.  If this rank gate fails, an in-sample score, a proxy coupling, or an all-$1.0$ fitted surface is classified as a projection artifact rather than translation survival.

\paragraph{Residual basis evidence below the gate.}
The finite residual-basis computation was run on six organism slots.  Four slots were computed and two returned missing-data status.  The computed organisms were \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, \emph{Homo sapiens}, and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; \emph{Gallus gallus} and \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155 lacked the joined rows needed for the statistic.  The row counts and selected residual curves are concrete: \emph{Danio rerio} used $14410$ joined rows with one candidate and $\rho=(1.0,0.9791828971490305)$; \emph{E. coli} used $3415$ joined rows with two candidates and $\rho=(1.0,0.9982293045445091,0.9979478314995476)$; \emph{Homo sapiens} used $16008$ joined rows with six candidates and final $\rho=0.9723341658092314$; \emph{S. cerevisiae} used $3046$ joined rows with nine candidates and final $\rho=0.9798667977250413$.  Across the four computed organisms the minimum final residual ratio was $0.9723341658092314$ against a persistence floor of $0.97$, so the finite outcome is persistent residual signal rather than compression into the available readout blocks.

\paragraph{Null comparison.}
The greedy extraction used the stated regularization $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length penalty $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  Its selected readout increments were compared against a deterministic Gaussian null with matched degrees of freedom.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, the measured-trna-tAI block at step $1$ had increment $0.02081710285096942$ against null $95\%$ level $0.0002306374780841125$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the mRNA block at step $1$ exceeded its null with increment $0.0017706954554909888$ against $0.0013371669456573104$, while measured TE at step $2$ did not, with increment $0.0002819723320884449$ against $0.0010272364744326006$.  In \emph{Homo sapiens}, PTM density, complex membership, transmembrane count, measured-trna-tAI, and localization exceeded their respective null thresholds, whereas domain count at step $5$ did not: $0.00003840392718095609$ against $0.00021932006910265595$.  In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, measured TE, PTM density, transmembrane count, turnover, and localization exceeded their matched nulls, while complex membership, measured-trna-tAI, domain count, and mRNA did not.

\paragraph{Coupling is not survival.}
A separate finite proxy contact computed nine synonymous residual coordinates: Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, K\_AAA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and $f_3$-stress.  It used a $61$-codon count space and three loaded organism rows: \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \emph{E. coli} K-12.  Against a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy proxy, the break condition was not met: not all nine entries had $|A_{ij}|<0.01$, and the maximum absolute proxy coupling was $1882464.1106377328$ with nine nonzero entries above the $0.01$ threshold.  This is evidence of a finite proxy contact, not evidence that any $S^{QT}_{ij}$ has survived the rank-admissibility gate.

\paragraph{Rank-admissibility rule.}
For each proposed coordinate-readout pair, the admissible statistic is defined only after residualization:
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^{\perp Z} &= q_i - \Pi_Z q_i,\\
T_j^{\perp Z} &= T_j - \Pi_Z T_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
The entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is reportable as translation survival only when the residual design has enough independent rows for held-out evaluation and the post-control rank is strictly below the row limit needed to interpolate the readout.  Thus a saturated design returns no survival entry even if its in-sample fit is positive.  In the present finite memory, the three-organism proxy surface and the modeled tAI or stAI routes are useful contact surfaces for asking the question, but they do not provide the organism- or CDS-matched measured translation rows needed to certify a non-saturated $S^{QT}$ entry.

\paragraph{Local scientific boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a refusal boundary with a constructive use: it prevents residual codon topology from being promoted beyond the evidence actually carried by the rows.  The residual-basis computation shows persistent unexplained residual structure after available readout blocks, and the proxy coupling computation shows nonzero finite contact with a tRNA-copy-derived readout.  Neither result is a claim of ribosome occupancy, measured translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.  Any such claim requires a separate layer-matched biological contact and a new controlled statistic on joined rows.  Until that contact exists, the correct output for $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is coupling-only or needs-data, not translation survival.
