\subsection{Rank-admissible translation survival gate for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-survival-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record is a finite-row gate for deciding whether a proposed translation-readout entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is statistically admissible before it is interpreted as survival.  The carrier consists of residualized $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinates $q_i$, organism-indexed translation readout surfaces $T_j$, and a control block $Z$ containing amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline.  The gate is deliberately placed at the translation-readout boundary: it asks whether the post-control row space has enough independent support to make a held-out positive entry falsifiable.  If the residual design is rank-saturated, underpowered, or all fitted values are explained by the controls, the entry is not promoted to survival even when an in-sample score is positive or equal to $1.0$.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate inventory.}
The finite $B^*_{Q6}$ inventory used by the associated coupling computation contains nine synonymous residual coordinates: Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, K\_AAA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and f3\_stress.  The codon-count ambient space has $61$ sense-codon coordinates, and the finite organism panel in the coupling contact contains homo\_sapiens, saccharomyces\_cerevisiae, and escherichia\_coli\_k12.  Against the proxy tRNA-Leu CUN-vs-UUR log-copy spectrum, the coupling screen used threshold $0.01$ and found $9$ nonzero entries with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}| &= 1882464.1106377328,\\
\#\{(i,j): |A_{ij}| \geq 0.01\} &= 9.
\end{aligned}
$$
This only establishes finite coupling contact with the proxy readout.  The break condition for that proxy screen, namely that all nine $q_i$ satisfy $|A_{ij}|<0.01$, was not met.  It nevertheless remains a coupling result, not a survival result, because it does not include the rank-admissible held-out $S^{QT}$ test under the full control set.

\paragraph{Rank-admissibility rule.}
For each candidate pair $(q_i,T_j)$, the admissibility test residualizes both columns against $Z$ and then asks whether the remaining design has non-saturated rank and enough held-out support to make $S^{QT}_{ij}>0$ refutable.  The local decision can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde q_i &= (I-P_Z)q_i,\\
\widetilde T_j &= (I-P_Z)T_j,\\
\mathrm{admissible}(i,j) &= [\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X_{ij}) < n_{\mathrm{eff}}]\ \wedge\ [\mathrm{df}_{ij}>0]\ \wedge\ [\mathrm{heldout}_{ij}\ \mathrm{exists}].
\end{aligned}
$$
Only after this condition is satisfied may a positive controlled readout statistic be discussed as translation-readout survival.  If the condition fails, the finite entry is classified as coupling-only or needs\_data.  This rule is especially important for small panels: an all-$1.0$ controlled surface on a three-organism panel is a rank-saturation warning, not biological survival.

\paragraph{Local exclusion rule.}
This chapter does not assert ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Codon-usage tables, tRNA gene-copy counts, and tAI or stAI weights can define bounded readout surfaces, but modeled adaptation weights are not direct ribosome measurements.  A separate matched contact is required before any claim above the rank-admissible translation boundary can be made.  Thus the present finite record supports a precise refusal discipline: proxy coupling and residual persistence can be reported, but $S^{QT}_{ij}$ remains unclaimed until organism- or CDS-matched measured translation rows, the full control block $Z$, non-saturated residual rank, and a held-out survival statistic are all present.
