\subsection{Rank-admissible translation boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-boundary}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record fixes a finite-row rule for deciding when a proposed $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate may even be tested as a translation-readout survival entry.  The object under inspection is not a protein, a structure, a function, or a phenotype.  It is a residualized design surface: synonymous $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates $q_i$, organism-indexed translation readout candidates $T_j$, and control columns $Z$ for amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and $M$-density baseline.  The admissibility question is whether a proposed entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ has enough remaining row support after residualization to make held-out survival falsifiable.  If the residualized row space is saturated, or if the remaining degrees of freedom cannot support a held-out test, the entry is not promoted to translation survival.

\paragraph{Finite coordinate panel.}
The computed $B^*_{Q6}$ residual panel contains four organisms with usable local readout joins and two organisms without enough joined rows.  The computed organisms are \texttt{danio\_rerio}, \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  The unavailable organisms are \texttt{gallus\_gallus} and \texttt{mycobacterium\_smegmatis\_str\_mc2\_155}, both marked \texttt{needs\_data}.  The joined-row counts are concrete: $14410$ for \texttt{danio\_rerio}, $3415$ for \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, $16008$ for \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, and $3046$ for \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}.  Candidate readout counts are respectively $1$, $2$, $6$, and $9$.  Thus the finite panel is large at the row level for the four computed organisms, but it is not uniformly populated across organisms or readout types.

\paragraph{Residual extraction statistic.}
The recursive residual-basis computation starts from $R_0=P_{Q\perp T_{\mathrm{mod}}}$ and uses a stated description-length penalty $\lambda=0.01$ with $DL=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  At each step a readout block $H_k$ is selected to absorb residual energy, and the remaining fraction $\rho_k$ is recorded.  The finite result is not a collapse of residual geometry.  All four computed organisms are classified as persistent, with no organism classified as compressible or partially compressible.  The recorded floor is $\rho_{\mathrm{floor}}=0.97$, and the smallest final residual fraction among computed organisms is $0.9723341658092314$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{final}}(\texttt{danio\_rerio}) &= 0.9791828971490305,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{final}}(\texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}) &= 0.9979478314995476,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{final}}(\texttt{homo\_sapiens}) &= 0.9723341658092314,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{final}}(\texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}) &= 0.9798667977250413.
\end{aligned}
$$

These numbers say that the residual basis is not exhausted by the selected readout blocks.  The computation is therefore useful as a boundary test: it identifies where residual coordinates remain unresolved, not where translation realization has already been established.

\paragraph{Per-organism readout increments.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null calculation gives a sharper finite-row picture.  In \texttt{danio\_rerio}, the selected \texttt{measured\_trna\_tai} block has increment $0.02081710285096942$ against null $95\%$ level $0.0002306374780841125$, so that step exceeds its matched null.  In \texttt{escherichia\_coli\_k12\_mg1655}, \texttt{mrna} has increment $0.0017706954554909888$ against $0.0013371669456573104$, while \texttt{measured\_te} has increment $0.0002819723320884449$ against $0.0010272364744326006$ and does not exceed its null.  In \texttt{homo\_sapiens}, \texttt{ptm\_density}, \texttt{complex\_member}, \texttt{tm\_count}, \texttt{measured\_trna\_tai}, and \texttt{localization} exceed their matched nulls, with increments $0.014780177462400674$, $0.004618389163869967$, $0.0014438137599134737$, $0.0005032876020244136$, and $0.006528084968494762$ respectively; \texttt{domain\_count} does not, with increment $0.00003840392718095609$ against $0.00021932006910265595$.  In \texttt{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}, \texttt{measured\_te}, \texttt{ptm\_density}, \texttt{tm\_count}, \texttt{turnover}, and \texttt{localization} exceed their matched nulls, while \texttt{complex\_member}, \texttt{measured\_trna\_tai}, \texttt{domain\_count}, and \texttt{mrna} do not.

\paragraph{Why this is still not translation survival.}
The positive readout-block increments are not the same object as a rank-admissible $S^{QT}_{ij}$ survival entry.  They arise inside a greedy descriptive compression of residual energy.  A proposed survival entry must still pass the separate rank gate after residualizing both $q_i$ and $T_j$ against $Z$.  The gate asks for non-saturated residual rank, enough independent organism or coding-sequence support, and a held-out boundary where failure would be visible.  In symbols, the admissibility condition is the local prerequisite

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}\bigl((I-P_Z)[q_i,T_j]\bigr)&< n_{\mathrm{rows}},\\
\operatorname{df}_{ij\mid Z}&>0,\\
S^{QT}_{ij}&\text{ is estimated on a non-saturated held-out design.}
\end{aligned}
$$

If any of these clauses fails, the entry remains coupling-only or data-insufficient even when an in-sample score is positive.  This rule is especially important for very small organism panels: an all-$1.0$ fitted surface on a saturated residualized design is a rank artifact, not a biological survival result.

\paragraph{Proxy coupling and refusal of promotion.}
A separate finite contact computed nine synonymous residual coordinates against a proxy tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum.  The residual coordinates were \texttt{Arg\_AGR}, \texttt{Ile\_AUA}, \texttt{K\_AAA}, \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}, \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}, \texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}, \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}, and \texttt{f3\_stress}.  The codon-count space has $61$ codons.  The proxy coupling matrix has $9$ entries above the $0.01$ threshold, and the maximum absolute entry is $1882464.1106377328$.  The break condition for that coupling test would have been that all nine $q_i$ satisfy $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ against the proxy readout; that break condition is false.  This establishes a coupling contact with a modeled readout surface, but it does not establish $S^{QT}_{ij}>0$ under the rank-admissible survival gate.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row conclusion is a guarded one.  The residual extraction has been computed for four organisms with joined rows $14410$, $3415$, $16008$, and $3046$, and the residuals remain persistent with final $\rho$ values no lower than $0.9723341658092314$.  Several selected readout blocks exceed their degree-of-freedom matched nulls, and the proxy coupling matrix is nonzero.  Those facts justify keeping the $B^*_{Q6}$ translation boundary open for further measurement.  They do not justify reporting translation survival.  A survival entry may be stated only after the same coordinate and readout are tested on a non-saturated residualized design with controls $Z$ and a held-out condition.  Without that additional row-matched statistic, no claim follows about ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality.
