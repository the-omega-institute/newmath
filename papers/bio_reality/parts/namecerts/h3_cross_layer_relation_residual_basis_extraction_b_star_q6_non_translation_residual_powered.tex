\subsection{Rank-admissible translation-survival gate for $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals}\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-survival-gate}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The finite carrier for this record is a residualized design table whose rows are organism-indexed coding-sequence observations and whose columns separate three roles: $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates $q_i$, translation-side readout surfaces $T_j$, and control columns $Z$. The intended readout is not protein function, folding, phenotype, or mechanism. It is only the admissibility of a proposed translation-survival entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ after $q_i$ and $T_j$ have both been residualized against controls such as amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline. The gate asks whether the remaining row space can still falsify a survival claim, rather than whether an in-sample regression score is large.

\paragraph{Gate definition.}
For a candidate coordinate--readout pair $(q_i,T_j)$, let $\widetilde q_i$ and $\widetilde T_j$ denote the residuals after projection away from $Z$. The entry is rank-admissible only when the residual design has enough independent rows beyond the fitted column space to support a held-out or otherwise non-vacuous test. In compact form the admissibility rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde q_i &= (I-P_Z)q_i,\\
\widetilde T_j &= (I-P_Z)T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij}\ \hbox{may be read as survival} &\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{rank}[Z,\widetilde q_i,\widetilde T_j] < n
\ \hbox{and residual degrees of freedom remain positive.}
\end{aligned}
$$
If the controlled table is rank-saturated, or if the remaining degrees of freedom are too small to make failure possible, the entry is assigned to the refusal side of the ledger: it may remain a coupling contact, a modeled readout contact, or a request for more rows, but it is not a translation-survival result.

\paragraph{Observed finite rows.}
The residual-basis extraction run computed four organism panels and left two panels without sufficient joined rows. The computed panels were $\mathit{Danio\ rerio}$ with $n=14410$ joined rows and one selected candidate, $\mathit{Escherichia\ coli}$ K12 MG1655 with $n=3415$ and two candidates, $\mathit{Homo\ sapiens}$ with $n=16008$ and six candidates, and $\mathit{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$ with $n=3046$ and nine candidates. $\mathit{Gallus\ gallus}$ and $\mathit{Mycobacterium\ smegmatis}$ str. MC2 155 remained in the needs-data class, with no joined row count or residual curve available.

The regularized extraction used the stated penalty $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length cost $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$. Its residual-ratio curves did not collapse to a compressible translation explanation. The four computed panels were all classified as persistent, with final residual ratios $0.9791828971490305$ for $\mathit{D.\ rerio}$, $0.9979478314995476$ for $\mathit{E.\ coli}$ K12 MG1655, $0.9723341658092314$ for human, and $0.9798667977250413$ for yeast. The minimum final ratio over computed panels was therefore $0.9723341658092314$ against a persistence floor of $0.97$, and the run reported no compressible or partially compressible organism panel.

\paragraph{Step-level readout evidence.}
The degree-of-freedom matched null comparisons show why the present record is a rank and boundary result rather than an unrestricted translation claim. In $\mathit{D.\ rerio}$, the measured-trna-tai step had increment $0.02081710285096942$ above a null $95\%$ level of $0.0002306374780841125$. In $\mathit{E.\ coli}$ K12 MG1655, the mRNA step exceeded its null level with increment $0.0017706954554909888$ versus $0.0013371669456573104$, while the measured-TE step did not, with increment $0.0002819723320884449$ below $0.0010272364744326006$. Human selected ptm-density, complex-member, tm-count, measured-trna-tai, domain-count, and localization in that order; all but domain-count exceeded the matched null, with the largest recorded increment $0.014780177462400674$ at the ptm-density step and a late localization increment $0.006528084968494762$ against $0.0011598998794309233$. Yeast selected measured-TE, ptm-density, tm-count, turnover, complex-member, measured-trna-tai, domain-count, mRNA, and localization; the measured-TE, ptm-density, tm-count, turnover, and localization steps exceeded their matched null levels, while complex-member, measured-trna-tai, domain-count, and mRNA did not.

These numbers support a narrower conclusion: several finite panels contain nontrivial residual contacts with translation-adjacent or downstream readout blocks, but the persistent residual curves prevent the extraction from being read as a complete explanation. The survival gate therefore remains row-rank-sensitive. A selected readout block that beats its matched null in the greedy extraction is not automatically a positive $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry unless the same coordinate--readout entry is evaluated in a non-saturated controlled survival design.

\paragraph{Coupling is not survival.}
A separate coupling calculation loaded three organism-level rows, human, yeast, and $\mathit{E.\ coli}$ K12, over a 61-codon count space and nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates: Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, K\_AAA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and $f_3$-stress. That contact used a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy proxy rather than a measured translation panel. Its coupling threshold was $0.01$, the break condition requiring all nine $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ was not met, and nine nonzero entries were reported, with maximum absolute coupling $1882464.1106377328$. This is a finite coupling contact, not a promoted survival contact. A large or nonzero $A_{ij}$ can justify looking for an organism-matched translation readout, but it does not by itself establish $S^{QT}_{ij}>0$.

\paragraph{Rank-saturation refusal.}
The gate is designed to reject the misleading case in which every fitted controlled score appears perfect because the table has exhausted its degrees of freedom. In such a case, an all-$1.0$ fitted surface is evidence of rank saturation, not evidence of translation survival. The local decision rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{df}_{ij} \le 0
&\Longrightarrow S^{QT}_{ij}\ \hbox{is not reported as survival},\\
\operatorname{rank}\hbox{-saturated in-sample fit}
&\Longrightarrow \hbox{coupling-only or needs-data status},\\
S^{QT}_{ij}>0
&\Longrightarrow \hbox{non-saturated residual design plus held-out or falsifiable support.}
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the central contribution of the packet: it distinguishes rank-admissible translation-survival entries from projection artifacts produced by too few independent organism or readout rows.

\paragraph{Local exclusion rule.}
Nothing in this record licenses promotion from codon-topology residuals to ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law. Modeled tAI or stAI weights are bounded translation-layer annotations, not direct ribosome measurements. Codon-usage tables and tRNA gene-copy counts are sequence or genome-annotation contacts, not protein-output assays. The present finite data can motivate a rank-audited translation-readout test, and it can record persistent residual structure after several readout blocks are tried, but any stronger layer claim requires a separate matched contact and its own controlled statistic.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The readback for this chapter is therefore a per-entry admissibility certificate. For each proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$, the certificate must state the residualized rank after $Z$, the remaining degrees of freedom, the organism or coding-sequence support behind the translation readout, the held-out or otherwise falsifiable test boundary, and the failure condition that returns the entry to coupling-only or needs-data status. Under the currently recorded finite rows, the residual-basis extraction is computed for four organisms and persistent in all four, while two organisms remain needs-data. The available three-organism coupling matrix is positive as a proxy contact, but the rank-admissible measured translation-survival matrix is not yet present.
