\subsection{Rank-admissible refusal for $B^*_{Q6}$ translation survival}
\label{subsec:bior-reality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-refusal}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record studied here is a finite residualized design over $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous coordinates, organism-indexed translation readout surfaces, and control columns $Z$.  The intended entry is not a raw correlation and not a fitted in-sample score.  For a coordinate $q_i$ and a translation readout $T_j$, the proposed surface $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is admissible only when both residuals remain testable after the declared controls are removed.  The control set includes amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and an $M$-density baseline; mRNA abundance is a control whenever the readout is translation efficiency.  The gate therefore asks a rank question before it asks a biological question: after residualizing $q_i$ and $T_j$ against $Z$, is there enough independent row support to falsify held-out survival?

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
Let $X_Q$ be the finite matrix of residual $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates and let $X_T$ be the finite matrix of bounded translation readouts.  With $P_Z$ denoting projection onto the control span, the tested columns are
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde q_i &= (I-P_Z)q_i, \\
  \widetilde T_j &= (I-P_Z)T_j .
\end{aligned}
$$
The entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ is reportable only if the post-control design has unsaturated rank and enough remaining degrees of freedom for a held-out check.  A fitted value of $1.0$ in a three-organism or otherwise saturated panel is therefore a refusal case, not survival.  The same rule blocks promotion from a coupling surface $A^{QT}$, from modeled tAI or stAI weights, or from any all-perfect regression surface whose residual degrees of freedom have collapsed.

\paragraph{Finite rows used by the gate.}
The residual-basis run has computed rows for four organisms and missing local readout support for two.  The computed organisms are \textit{Danio rerio} with $14410$ joined rows and one candidate block, \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 with $3415$ joined rows and two candidate blocks, \textit{Homo sapiens} with $16008$ joined rows and six candidate blocks, and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} with $3046$ joined rows and nine candidate blocks.  \textit{Gallus gallus} and \textit{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. mc2 155 are labeled needs-data because their joined row counts and candidate counts are absent.

The regularized extraction used $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length penalty $DL=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  The final residual curves remain high: \textit{Danio rerio} has $\rho=(1.0,0.9791828971490305)$; \textit{E. coli} has $\rho=(1.0,0.9982293045445091,0.9979478314995476)$; human has $\rho=(1.0,0.9852198225375993,0.9806696939851618,0.9792537895870558,0.9787609432955213,0.9787233550315274,0.9723341658092314)$; yeast has $\rho=(1.0,0.9962498989663637,0.9929843170647626,0.9915730985485388,0.9903314906684438,0.9894054695002641,0.9885902293718664,0.9882386501212531,0.9881524582851271,0.9798667977250413)$.  The minimum final value among computed organisms is $0.9723341658092314$, above the stated residual floor $0.97$, so the finite result is persistent residual structure, not compression to a translation explanation.

\paragraph{Per-step null comparisons.}
The degrees-of-freedom-matched null gives local evidence about descriptive absorption, but it does not by itself certify translation survival.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, measured tRNA/tAI is selected at step $1$ with increment $0.02081710285096942$ against null $p95=0.0002306374780841125$.  In \textit{E. coli}, mRNA is selected at step $1$ with increment $0.0017706954554909888$ against null $p95=0.0013371669456573104$, while measured TE at step $2$ has increment $0.0002819723320884449$ below null $p95=0.0010272364744326006$.  In human, selected blocks exceeding their nulls include PTM density at step $1$ with $0.014780177462400674$ versus $0.0002845846156909306$, complex membership at step $2$ with $0.004618389163869967$ versus $0.000206488525646134$, transmembrane count at step $3$ with $0.0014438137599134737$ versus $0.0002435268444123588$, measured tRNA/tAI at step $4$ with $0.0005032876020244136$ versus $0.00020173994173736226$, and localization at step $6$ with $0.006528084968494762$ versus $0.0011598998794309233$; domain count at step $5$ has only $3.840392718095609\times 10^{-5}$ against null $p95=0.00021932006910265595$.  In yeast, measured TE, PTM density, transmembrane count, turnover, and localization exceed their local nulls, while complex membership, measured tRNA/tAI, domain count, and mRNA do not.  These rows demonstrate bounded finite contacts with several readout blocks, but the high terminal $\rho$ values keep the chapter in a non-promotion regime.

\paragraph{Coupling is not survival.}
A separate three-organism codon-usage and tRNA-copy proxy computation loaded human, yeast, and \textit{E. coli} rows in a $61$-codon count space and computed nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates: Arg\_AGR, Ile\_AUA, K\_AAA, Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR, Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG, Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG, Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY, Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY, and f3\_stress.  Its proxy readout is the tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum.  The coupling threshold is $0.01$, all nine entries are nonzero above that threshold, and the largest absolute entry is $1882464.1106377328$; the declared break condition, that all nine $|A_{ij}|<0.01$, is not met.  This is a contact with a proxy coupling matrix, not a controlled survival matrix.  The proxy lacks true tAI or stAI observations and lacks organism-matched measured translation rows for the present $S^{QT}$ rank gate.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row evidence supports a rank-admissibility rule and a refusal boundary.  A proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ entry may be evaluated only when the residualized design is not rank-saturated and has enough held-out support after $Z$ is removed.  If the design is saturated, underpowered, or based only on modeled adaptation weights or proxy tRNA-copy coupling, the correct output is coupling-only or needs-data.  No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or universal biological law follows from this chapter.  Those claims require separate matched contacts and their own controlled statistics at the corresponding layer.
