\subsection{Rank-admissible refusal for $B^*_{Q6}$ translation survival}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rank-admissible-translation-survival-gate}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matrix problem, not a protein-level interpretation.  Its rows are organism-indexed coding-sequence or organism summaries; its $Q$-columns are the computed $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates; its candidate readout columns are bounded translation surfaces such as tRNA-copy spectra, modeled tAI or stAI weights, measured translation efficiency when present, and matched transcript or protein covariates when they are explicitly joined.  The question is whether an entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$ may be read as a translation-survival score after residualizing both the coordinate $q_i$ and the translation readout $T_j$ against the declared controls $Z$.  The admissibility test is deliberately prior to biology-rich language: it asks whether the remaining row space has enough independent support to make a held-out survival assertion falsifiable.

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
For a proposed coordinate-readout pair, write $\widetilde q_i=(I-P_Z)q_i$ and $\widetilde T_j=(I-P_Z)T_j$.  The entry is eligible only when the residualized design has nonsaturated rank and nonzero held-out degrees of freedom:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}([Z,q_i,T_j]) &> \operatorname{rank}(Z),\\
\operatorname{rank}([Z,q_i,T_j]) &< n,\\
\nu_{ij} &= n-\operatorname{rank}([Z,q_i,T_j])>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
If these inequalities fail, the matrix entry remains a contact or modeling relation rather than a survival result.  In particular, a fitted score equal to $1.0$ in a rank-saturated panel is treated as a projection artifact, not as evidence for translation realization.

\paragraph{Finite residual-basis computation.}
The associated residual-basis extraction used the regularizer $\lambda=0.01$ with description-length penalty $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  Four organisms had computed local readout panels and two remained uncomputed.  The computed organism row counts were $16008$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $3046$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $3415$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, and $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}.  \emph{Gallus gallus} and \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155 were labeled \emph{needs data}.  The selected-block counts were respectively $6$, $9$, $2$, and $1$ for the four computed organisms.  Their final residual ratios stayed high: $0.9723341658092314$ for human, $0.9798667977250413$ for yeast, $0.9979478314995476$ for \emph{E. coli}, and $0.9791828971490305$ for zebrafish.  Thus no computed organism was classified as compressible; all four computed cases were persistent residual cases, with global reported floor $0.97$ and minimum final value $0.9723341658092314$.

\paragraph{Per-step null readback.}
The deterministic degree-of-freedom matched nulls were computed for the same panels, but their role is limited to describing selected residual-block increments.  In human, the first four selected increments exceeded their null thresholds: $0.014780177462400674$ versus $0.0002845846156909306$ for ptm-density, $0.004618389163869967$ versus $0.000206488525646134$ for complex membership, $0.0014438137599134737$ versus $0.0002435268444123588$ for transmembrane count, and $0.0005032876020244136$ versus $0.00020173994173736226$ for measured tRNA/tAI.  The fifth human step, domain count, did not exceed its threshold: $0.00003840392718095609$ versus $0.00021932006910265595$; the sixth localization step did exceed it, $0.006528084968494762$ versus $0.0011598998794309233$.  In yeast, measured translation efficiency, ptm-density, transmembrane count, turnover, and localization exceeded their null thresholds, while complex membership, measured tRNA/tAI, domain count, and mRNA did not.  In \emph{E. coli}, the mRNA step exceeded its null threshold, $0.0017706954554909888$ versus $0.0013371669456573104$, while the measured-TE step did not, $0.0002819723320884449$ versus $0.0010272364744326006$.  In zebrafish, measured tRNA/tAI exceeded the null threshold, $0.02081710285096942$ versus $0.0002306374780841125$.

\paragraph{Coupling is not survival.}
A separate three-organism coupling calculation loaded \emph{Homo sapiens}, \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 over a $61$-codon count space.  It computed nine synonymous residual coordinates: Arg AGR, Ile AUA, K AAA, Leu CUN versus UUR, Leu UUA versus UUG, Ser UCA versus UCG, Ser UCR versus AGY, Thr ACR versus ACY, and $f_3$ stress.  Against the proxy tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum, all nine entries exceeded the coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute coupling $1882464.1106377328$ and $9$ nonzero entries.  This is useful contact information, but it is not an $S^{QT}$ survival matrix.  The readout is a proxy copy-number spectrum rather than an organism-matched held-out translation measurement under the full control set.

\paragraph{Boundary statement.}
The admissible conclusion is therefore a rank-and-readout refusal rule.  A positive coupling coefficient, a modeled tAI or stAI contact, or an in-sample fitted value at the ceiling does not license a translation-survival entry unless the residualized design has nonsaturated rank and enough held-out support after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, coding-sequence length, M-density baseline, and simpler codon baselines are accounted for.  Likewise, the persistent residual curves do not imply ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, or any universal biological law.  Those claims require their own matched measurements and their own controlled finite-row statistics.  This chapter records the pre-translation boundary: $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates may be carried forward to a survival test only when the finite design makes that test nonvacuous.
