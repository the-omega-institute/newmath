\subsection{miRNA Seed Repression as a Bounded Cross-Dataset RNA Readout}\label{sec:bioreality-mirna-seed-crossdataset-agarwal2015}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row panel.}
The record considered here is the finite cross-dataset miRNA seed-repression panel indexed by the claim id \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mirna\_target\_repression.mirna\_seed\_crossdataset\_agarwal2015}.  Its biological object is not an unrestricted statement about miRNA biology.  It is a row-level construction in which coding or transcript windows are compared through a prespecified seed-target coordinate, an RNA abundance or repression readout, and controls that keep transcript abundance, translation readout, local sequence context, and dataset batch from being silently identified with one another.  The verified run used $n=55170$ finite rows and returned an aggregate per-miRNA partial Spearman median
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{partial}}=-0.224966,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $7$ replicated gradient checks and runtime $400.408$ seconds.  The sign is the expected repression direction: stronger seed-associated target burden is associated with lower target expression or stronger repression after the declared controls.  The magnitude is deliberately read as a bounded RNA-layer statistic, not as a claim about protein output or organismal phenotype.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout separation.}
The admissible coordinate is the finite seed-target label attached to each joined row.  The admissible readout is the same-row RNA repression or abundance quantity used by the Agarwal-style cross-dataset statistic.  The row is only interpretable when the coordinate is kept distinct from transcript abundance, translation efficiency, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch.  In this chapter the coordinate label is therefore a selector for a controlled RNA-repression comparison, not an observed mechanism.  A compact way to state the accepted statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{accepted} \quad &\Longleftrightarrow\quad n=55170,\; \widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{partial}}<0,\; g=7,\\
&\text{and the row interpretation remains inside the RNA-repression readout.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $g=7$ denotes the replicated gradient count.  The negative median partial correlation licenses a finite cross-layer RNA relation only in the narrow sense that the seed coordinate continues to carry information after the controls used by the statistic.  It does not by itself identify the decay factor, the biochemical binding event, the ribosome state, or the final protein abundance.

\paragraph{Relation to neighboring RNA-boundary checks.}
The miRNA result is supported as an RNA-layer boundary by comparison with other finite RNA-stability records, but those records are not imported as evidence for the miRNA mechanism.  One neighboring mRNA half-life audit on an m6A site-load panel retained $5837$ genes, reported a static Spearman value $-0.388929$, a causal Spearman value $0.245804$, a length-partial Spearman value $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$.  A separate ARE deletion stability audit used $2242$ Jurkat rows and $226$ BEAS2B rows, ran $1000$ actual permutations with $2000$ grading permutations, and recorded sign concordance as true.  These scalar checks show that the BioReality table can hold finite RNA readouts with explicit controls and permutation semantics.  They do not convert the miRNA seed statistic into an RBP occupancy claim, a decay mechanism, or a universal law.

\paragraph{Accepted content.}
The accepted content is the following bounded statement.  In the finite cross-dataset seed panel, the miRNA coordinate survives as a controlled RNA-repression descriptor because the verified statistic has $n=55170$, median partial correlation $-0.224966$, and $7$ replicated gradients.  The direction is compatible with target repression, and the row count is large enough for the statistic to be treated as a stable finite readout rather than as a single-row anecdote.  The chapter therefore records a cross-layer relation from a finite regulatory coordinate to an RNA abundance or repression readout.  The relation is local to the declared panel, the declared controls, and the declared statistic.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The record refuses stronger interpretations.  No translation realization follows from this RNA statistic unless a separate same-row ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency contact is attached and evaluated.  No protein abundance conclusion follows unless a matched proteomics contact is added after the translation boundary.  No folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biochemical function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality follows from the negative partial correlation.  If seed-coordinate labels become exchangeable after the controls, the coordinate remains only a descriptor.  If an apparent RNA effect can be reproduced by batch, isoform state, abundance normalization, local structure, motif density, or composition, the positive reading is withdrawn.  If a decay-factor RBP mechanism is claimed, then measured local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective control, and non-target control rows must be present in the same finite panel; none of those mechanism rows is supplied by the verified miRNA cross-dataset statistic alone.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
This chapter is ready as a bounded BioReality record.  It preserves the observed negative finite-row miRNA seed association, records the concrete values $n=55170$, $\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{partial}}=-0.224966$, $g=7$, and runtime $400.408$, and keeps every higher biological layer outside the claim until a separate layer-matched contact supplies that layer's data.
