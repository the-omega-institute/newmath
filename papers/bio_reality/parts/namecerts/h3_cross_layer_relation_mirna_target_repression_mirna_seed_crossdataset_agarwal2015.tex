\subsection{MicroRNA Seed Repression as a Translation-Bounded RNA-Stability Boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-mirna-seed-crossdataset-agarwal2015}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record concerns a finite panel of coding-RNA windows whose synonymous labels are interpreted only as row coordinates.  Each admissible row must carry, at minimum, a coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life or abundance-stability readout, local sequence and structure covariates, transcript abundance, batch, splice or isoform state, and the stated perturbation status.  For the stronger decay-factor RBP interpretation, the same finite rows would also need measured local occupancy of the specified decay factor, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, a binding-defective rescue arm or non-target control, and an explicit break condition separating coordinate association from mechanism.  The available verified contact for the present claim is narrower: it is a cross-dataset microRNA seed repression statistic, not a same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy and rescue panel.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}
The verified microRNA target-repression run is indexed by experiment run \texttt{50f22b191476}.  It retained $n=55170$ finite rows and reported an aggregate per-microRNA median partial Spearman coefficient of $-0.224966$, with gradient replication in $7$ cases and runtime $400.408$ seconds.  Thus the empirical sign is compatible with seed-associated repression at the RNA-output surface: higher seed-context targeting is associated with lower expression or stability after the declared partial controls used by the run.  In compact notation, the row-level finding can be summarized as
$$\begin{aligned}
 n &= 55170,\\
 \operatorname{median}_{\mu\mathrm{RNA}}\rho_{\mathrm{partial}} &= -0.224966,\\
 N_{\mathrm{replicated\ gradients}} &= 7.
\end{aligned}$$
This is a finite association over joined transcript rows.  It does not, by itself, identify a decay-factor RBP bridge, a translation mechanism, protein output, folded structure, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal sequence law.

\paragraph{Neighboring RNA half-life calibration.}
A separate RNA-modification half-life boundary run provides a useful scale comparison but not a substitute mechanism.  In experiment \texttt{7cd189f15c4b}, the joined m6A and half-life panel kept $5837$ genes, reported static Spearman association $-0.388929$ between m6A load and DMSO log half-life, causal Spearman association $0.245804$ between m6A-load change and half-life change, length-partial Spearman association $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial permutation value $0.000999001$.  That neighboring result shows that finite RNA half-life panels can cross a preregistered RNA-stability boundary when the dataset contains the required joined rows and controls.  It does not repair the missing microRNA-specific decay-factor occupancy, perturbation, and rescue information in the present record.

\paragraph{Licensed interpretation.}
The present chapter therefore licenses only a bounded microRNA seed repression readout: within the retained finite rows, the microRNA seed-context statistic has a negative aggregate partial association with the measured RNA-output surface, and the sign is replicated across seven microRNA-gradient instances.  The row coordinate may be used as a descriptor of seed-associated repression strength only under the finite controls actually present in the verified run.  The claim remains local to the measured RNA layer.  A same-row translation readout may be used as a boundary marker preventing accidental promotion of RNA stability to translation, but the mere presence or naming of such a boundary is not evidence of translation realization.

\paragraph{Refused interpretation.}
The stronger conjecture asks for a coordinate-associated RNA half-life relation mediated by a prespecified decay-factor RBP.  That stronger statement is not established here.  A positive RBP bridge would require the same finite rows to show measured local decay-factor occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, loss of the effect under target-RBP depletion, restoration by wild-type rescue, failure of binding-defective or non-target controls to restore the bridge, and persistence after transcription-rate, local RNA-structure, motif-density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, translation readout, and batch controls.  The supplied contact explicitly notes that available half-life payloads are not same-window RBP occupancy or perturbation-rescue panels.  Consequently, an RNA half-life or repression association without those rows is only an RNA-layer association.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation output, protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality follows from the verified microRNA statistic.  Such claims require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite rows and declared statistics.  In particular, ribosome-profiling rows would be needed before discussing translation efficiency, proteomics rows before discussing protein abundance, structural or physical assays before discussing folding or admissibility, and perturbation-rescue RBP occupancy rows before discussing an RNA-decay mechanism.  The current evidence is therefore useful as a bounded RNA-output contact and insufficient for the proposed decay-factor RBP bridge.
