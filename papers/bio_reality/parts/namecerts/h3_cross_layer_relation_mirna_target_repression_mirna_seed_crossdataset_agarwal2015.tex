\subsection{miRNA seed repression as a bounded RNA-layer relation}
\label{sec:bioreality-mirna-seed-crossdataset-agarwal2015}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record concerns a finite panel of coding or transcript-associated RNA rows in which a synonymous or near-synonymous coordinate label is not allowed to become a claim about protein production by itself.  Each usable row must be read as a tuple containing the coordinate label, a same-row translation readout when that boundary is invoked, an RNA half-life or repression readout, transcript abundance control information, local sequence and structure covariates, batch information, perturbation state where available, and an explicit condition under which the proposed relation is refused.  In this chapter the anchored biological surface is the cross-dataset miRNA seed repression statistic from the Agarwal-style target-repression table.  The verified finite table has
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{rows}} &= 55170,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{partial,median}} &= -0.224966,\\
G_{\mathrm{replicated}} &= 7,\\
t_{\mathrm{run}} &= 400.408\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The negative median partial correlation is used only as a bounded repression-direction readout: after the declared controls, rows carrying the relevant miRNA seed information tend to move in the repressive direction.  The seven replicated gradients are the finite reproducibility handle for that direction, not a license to infer a universal sequence law.

\paragraph{Licensed relation.}
The admissible statement is a cross-layer boundary statement with a narrow scope.  A miRNA seed coordinate may be said to participate in a bounded RNA-repression relation when the same finite table preserves a negative target-repression gradient after the specified controls and when the interpretation remains at the level of transcript regulation.  The table may support a relation of the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{seed\ coordinate}+Z &\longmapsto \mathrm{RNA\ repression\ readout},\\
Z &= \{\mathrm{abundance,\ isoform,\ local\ composition,\ structure,\ batch,\ and\ matched\ controls}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ is part of the row definition and not an after-the-fact explanation.  The scalar value $\rho_{\mathrm{partial,median}}=-0.224966$ is therefore interpreted as a finite controlled association between seed-status and repression readout.  It is not interpreted as translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, cellular function, adaptation, or mechanism.

\paragraph{Same-row boundary.}
The biological object named by this record is stricter than a seed-match table alone.  The intended high-standard panel contains synonymously editable coding RNA windows, matched translation readout, RNA half-life readout, measured decay-factor RNA-binding-protein occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target control, transcription-rate control, mRNA abundance control, isoform state, motif density, and local-structure covariates.  The Agarwal-style finite statistic supplies a strong seed-repression contact, but it does not by itself supply every row required for the stronger decay-factor RBP bridge.  Thus the finite result is retained as an RNA-layer repression boundary while the RBP-mediated half-life mechanism remains conditional on additional same-row measurements.

\paragraph{Comparison with other RNA stability contacts.}
The boundary is calibrated against two other finite RNA-layer contacts in the same evidence family.  In the m6A site-load half-life table, the joined gene count is $5837$, the static Spearman statistic between m6A load and DMSO log half-life is $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman statistic between m6A load and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life is $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman statistic is $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$.  In the ARE deletion stability table, the finite row counts are $n_{\mathrm{BEAS2B}}=226$ and $n_{\mathrm{Jurkat}}=2242$, with $1000$ actual permutations, $2000$ grading permutations, and a sign-concordant deletion result.  These comparison contacts show that RNA stability and repression surfaces can be finite, directional, and statistically controlled without becoming claims about translation, folding, function, or organism-level law.  The miRNA seed record is treated in the same disciplined way: it is an RNA-repression relation with a measured finite direction, not a promoted biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim fails if the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  It also fails as an RBP-bridge claim if RNA half-life changes but measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy does not change, if coordinate and occupancy cannot be decoupled, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA-structure covariates, motif-density covariates, transcription controls, isoform stability, or batch reproduce the pattern.  Under any of those outcomes the result remains at most a finite RNA association.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation claim follows from RNA half-life, miRNA seed repression, transcript abundance, or a coordinate label alone.  No protein-output, folding, physical-admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality claim follows from the finite statistics above unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, a ribosome-profiling contact would be needed for translation efficiency, a protein-abundance contact would be needed for protein output, a structural or physical assay would be needed for folding or admissibility, and perturbation-rescue rows with measured RBP occupancy would be needed for the stronger RNA-decay RBP mechanism.  The present chapter therefore records a bounded cross-dataset miRNA seed repression relation, together with the exact point at which stronger biological readings are stopped.
