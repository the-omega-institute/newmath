\subsection{Finite miRNA seed repression as a bounded RNA-layer readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-mirna-seed-crossdataset-agarwal2015}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel of coding or transcript-associated RNA rows in which a synonymous or near-synonymous coordinate label is not allowed to stand by itself.  Each admissible row must carry a declared RNA-side readout and the covariates needed to keep the interpretation local: transcript abundance, batch, local composition, motif density, isoform state when available, and any matched translation readout that is needed before statements are made across layers.  In this chapter the realized finite statistic is the miRNA seed repression contact indexed by the Agarwal-style cross-dataset rows, not a direct assay of protein output, folding, phenotype, adaptation, or a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Observed finite statistic.}
The verified miRNA entry used here is
$h3.cross_layer_relation.mirna_target_repression.mirna_seed_crossdataset_agarwal2015$.  Its run identifier is $50f22b191476$.  The finite table contains $n=55170$ joined rows and reports an aggregate per-miRNA partial Spearman median
$\rho=-0.224966$ after the declared controls.  The sign is the biologically expected repression direction: higher seed-target signal is associated with lower target readout in the bounded RNA repression surface.  The same run records $7$ replicated gradient instances and a runtime of $400.408$ seconds.  The chapter therefore treats the miRNA seed relation as a finite negative-gradient readout, not as an unbounded law over all transcripts or conditions.

\paragraph{Local mathematical form.}
Let $W$ be the finite set of joined rows.  For each $w\in W$, let $q(w)$ be the coordinate or seed-label summary, let $Y(w)$ be the measured repression-side RNA readout, and let $Z(w)$ collect abundance, batch, sequence-composition, local-context, and isoform controls that are present in the finite table.  The licensed statement is only that the residual association computed on this table has the observed negative median direction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
|W|&=55170,\\
\operatorname{median}_{\mu}\,\rho_{\mu}(q,Y\mid Z)&=-0.224966,\\
\#\{\text{replicated gradient instances}\}&=7.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a row-bounded relation between a seed-coordinate summary and an RNA repression readout.  It does not identify the molecular route by which every row is repressed, and it does not promote the coordinate into a protein-layer or organism-layer claim.

\paragraph{Comparison contact.}
A second RNA-layer boundary statistic is useful for calibrating the scope of this kind of claim.  The verified m6A half-life entry
$h3.cross_layer_relation.rna_modification.m6a_site_load_mrna_halflife_boundary_gse163500$ has run identifier $7cd189f15c4b$ and $n=5837$ genes under the stated coverage rule.  It reports static Spearman association $-0.388929$ for m6A load versus DMSO log half-life, a causal Spearman value $0.245804$ for m6A-load change versus log half-life change, a length-partial Spearman value $0.227683$, and a two-sided partial-permutation value $p=0.000999001$.  That comparison is not evidence for miRNA repression; it is cited here only to show the shared discipline of finite RNA-layer contacts: a scalar RNA statistic may cross a declared boundary while still refusing protein, function, phenotype, or mechanism unless those layers are directly measured.

\paragraph{Mechanism boundary.}
The present miRNA seed result is compatible with a bounded target-repression reading, but it is not a decay-factor RBP bridge.  No row in the cited finite statistic supplies same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue behavior.  Consequently a half-life or repression association may not be re-described as an RBP-mediated RNA-decay mechanism.  A positive RBP bridge would require the same finite rows to carry local RBP occupancy, perturbation loss, rescue return, and control arms that separate RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform state, transcript abundance, codon-pair context, and batch.

\paragraph{Translation and higher-layer refusal.}
The chapter also refuses promotion from RNA repression to translation realization.  A same-row translation readout may be used as a boundary or control when present, but RNA half-life, miRNA seed association, or transcript abundance alone does not measure ribosome occupancy, translation efficiency, protein abundance, folding, localization, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Any such statement would need a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite rows and its own declared statistic.  The accepted content here is therefore narrow: the Agarwal-style cross-dataset finite panel supports a negative miRNA seed repression association over $55170$ rows with $7$ replicated gradient instances, while all protein-layer, mechanism-layer, and universal claims remain outside the licensed conclusion.
