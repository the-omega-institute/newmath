\subsection{Finite seed repression as a bounded RNA-layer contact}
\label{subsec:bioreality-mirna-seed-crossdataset-agarwal2015}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel of transcript-level rows in which a microRNA seed-derived coordinate is read together with a repression statistic. The panel is not treated as a genome-wide law and is not promoted into protein output, folding, cellular function, adaptation, or mechanism. Its positive content is narrower: the coordinate labels can be compared with a measured RNA-layer repression readout after the declared controls have removed the most immediate transcript-level confounders. In the audited run for \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mirna\_target\_repression.mirna\_seed\_crossdataset\_agarwal2015}, the joined finite table contains $n=55170$ rows. The aggregate per-microRNA partial correlation median is $-0.224966$, and $7$ replicated gradient checks preserve the expected direction. The run identifier is \texttt{50f22b191476}, with a recorded runtime of $400.408$ seconds. These numbers make the row set large enough to support a bounded repression contact, but they do not by themselves identify the molecular route by which repression is implemented.

\paragraph{Local statistic.}
The statistic used here is a finite-row comparison between seed-coordinate status and the measured repression readout. The sign of the audited median is negative, so the observed direction is compatible with seed-associated down-regulation in the finite joined rows. Written schematically, the admissible statement has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{median}_{\mu}\,\rho_{\mathrm{partial}}(\mathrm{seed}_{\mu},\mathrm{repression}\mid Z)=-0.224966,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes the declared row controls rather than an unobserved biological explanation. The number is therefore a controlled association surface, not a mechanistic certificate. The replicated-gradient count $7$ is used only as finite support for directional stability across the audited partitions; it is not read as proof of causation, universality, or direct protein-level consequence.

\paragraph{RNA-stability comparison rows.}
The same audit memory contains RNA-half-life boundary rows that are useful for calibrating the scope of this chapter. In the m6A half-life boundary run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500}, $5837$ genes pass the coverage rule, the static Spearman statistic between m6A load and DMSO log half-life is $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman statistic for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life is $0.245804$, the length-partial statistic is $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial permutation value is $0.000999001$. In the ARE deletion boundary run \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.utr3\_are.are\_deletion\_mrna\_stability\_boundary\_siegel2022}, the finite rows include $226$ BEAS2B observations and $2242$ Jurkat observations, with $1000$ actual permutations and sign concordance recorded as true. These neighboring RNA-layer audits show that the BioReality table can record finite RNA-stability contacts with concrete sample sizes and permutation counts. They do not license importing the miRNA seed result into an RBP-decay mechanism unless the same rows also carry measured RBP occupancy and perturbation or rescue controls.

\paragraph{Required separation.}
The present chapter therefore keeps three readouts distinct. First, the microRNA seed row supplies a bounded repression coordinate with $n=55170$ and median partial correlation $-0.224966$. Second, RNA half-life rows, when present, supply RNA-stability measurements such as the $5837$-gene m6A half-life panel and the ARE deletion panels with $226$ and $2242$ rows. Third, translation readouts are only a boundary condition: ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows may prevent an RNA-only association from being misread as translation, but their presence alone does not create an RNA-decay mechanism. A valid RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge would require the same finite rows to include local decay-factor RBP occupancy, a target-RBP loss arm, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, transcription-rate or mRNA-abundance controls, local structure and motif-density controls, isoform-state controls, batch controls, and a declared break condition.

\paragraph{Admissible claim.}
The admissible claim is consequently limited to the following statement. In a finite cross-dataset microRNA seed panel, seed-coordinate rows show a bounded repression association with median partial correlation $-0.224966$ over $55170$ joined observations, and $7$ replicated gradient checks preserve the observed direction. This supports a finite RNA-layer repression boundary for the audited rows. It does not assert that a synonymous coding-window coordinate changes RNA half-life through a decay-factor RBP, because the necessary same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, rescue, and binding-defective controls are absent from the verified miRNA seed row. It also does not assert that RNA half-life differences are translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a general biological law.

\paragraph{Break condition.}
The claim must be refused if the seed-coordinate statistic is used as a proxy for translation output, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality. It must also be refused if a half-life difference is interpreted as an RBP-decay bridge without measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls on the same finite rows. If transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, or non-target controls reproduce the pattern, the observation remains a finite RNA-layer association and the mechanism reading is blocked.
