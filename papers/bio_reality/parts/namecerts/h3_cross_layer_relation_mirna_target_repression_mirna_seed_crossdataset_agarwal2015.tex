\subsection{Finite miRNA seed repression as a bounded cross-layer RNA readout}\label{subsec:bioreality-mirna-seed-crossdataset-agarwal2015}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record concerns the claim id \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mirna\_target\_repression.mirna\_seed\_crossdataset\_agarwal2015}.  The finite rows are interpreted as matched transcript-level or window-level observations in which a miRNA seed relation is read together with an RNA-layer repression statistic and the local controls needed to prevent promotion into a stronger biological conclusion.  In the verified run \texttt{50f22b191476}, the usable panel has $n=55170$ rows.  Across the per-miRNA summaries, the aggregate partial Spearman direction is negative, with median
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{partial,median}}&=-0.224966,
\end{aligned}
$$
and the replicated gradient count is $7$.  The computation reported runtime $400.408$ seconds.  This is enough for a bounded cross-layer RNA-repression statement: miRNA seed structure is not merely a string label, because it survives as a finite relation against the matched RNA readout after the declared controls used by the run.  The sign is also biologically coherent for repression: higher seed-target burden is associated with lower target-side expression or stability readback in the controlled aggregate.

\paragraph{Readout geometry.}
The scientific object is not a universal miRNA law.  It is a finite relation among seed membership, target transcript rows, and a same-row RNA-layer response.  The observed statistic can be summarized as a controlled map
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mathrm{seed\ relation},\mathrm{target\ row},Z)&\longmapsto R_{\mathrm{RNA}},\\
\operatorname{median}_{\mathrm{miRNA}}\,\rho(R_{\mathrm{seed}},R_{\mathrm{RNA}}\mid Z)&=-0.224966,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ denotes the finite adjustment surface present in the cross-dataset run rather than an unbounded biological background.  The row count $55170$ fixes the empirical support: the chapter licenses a finite repression readout over those joined rows and does not license extrapolation to every transcript, organism, cell state, seed context, or perturbation regime.

\paragraph{Cross-layer status.}
The result is a cross-layer relation only in the modest sense that a sequence-level miRNA target coordinate is read against an RNA-layer outcome.  It is not a translation result.  It is not a protein-output result.  It is not a folding, physical-admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universality result.  Those stronger statements would require separate rows carrying the corresponding layer-specific measurements and controls.  In particular, an RNA half-life or expression gradient cannot by itself be re-described as ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, protein function, system phenotype, or a general law of coding sequence organization.

\paragraph{Local comparison to other RNA-layer boundaries.}
The miRNA seed run sits in the same finite-readout family as other RNA-layer contacts, but the families remain distinct.  In the m6A half-life boundary record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_modification.m6a\_site\_load\_mrna\_halflife\_boundary\_gse163500}, the joined panel has $5837$ genes, static Spearman $-0.388929$ for m6A load against DMSO log half-life, causal Spearman $0.245804$ for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life, length-partial Spearman $0.227683$, and two-sided permutation value $0.000999001$.  In the endogenous ARE half-life transfer record \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.utr3\_are.are\_endogenous\_3utr\_halflife\_transfer\_rnadecaycafe}, the panel has $14933$ genes, $\beta_{\mathrm{ARE}}=-0.0607953$, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, and permutation value $0.00049975$.  These comparisons show that the present miRNA seed record should be read as one member of a finite RNA-regulatory boundary class: each case has its own rows, statistic, and controls, and no case transfers its mechanism to the others merely by sharing an RNA-layer outcome.

\paragraph{Mechanism boundary.}
The finite result does not by itself identify the decay machinery.  A decay-factor RBP bridge would need the same finite rows to carry measured local RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy separation, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or non-target controls, transcription-rate controls, mRNA abundance controls, isoform-state controls, motif-density controls, local-structure controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair context, batch, and a declared break condition.  If the RNA readout changes but the presumed RBP occupancy does not, or if loss and rescue of the target RBP do not track the effect, the record remains an RNA association.  If binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, transcript structure, motif density, transcription, isoform composition, or batch reproduce the pattern, the mechanistic bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Accepted claim.}
The accepted statement is therefore narrow.  On the verified cross-dataset miRNA seed panel, $55170$ finite rows support a negative controlled miRNA seed repression readout, summarized by median partial $\rho=-0.224966$ and replicated gradient count $7$.  The chapter records this as a bounded sequence-to-RNA relation.  It deliberately stops before translation, protein abundance, protein state, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, causation, adaptation, or universal sequence law.  Any such promotion would need a new finite panel in which the relevant layer is measured directly and in which the same-row controls prevent transcript abundance, RNA decay, structure, motif density, isoform state, batch, or identifier mapping from explaining the pattern.
