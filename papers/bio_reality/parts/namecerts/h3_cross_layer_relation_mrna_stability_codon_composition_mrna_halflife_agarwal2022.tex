\subsection{Rank-admissible codon-composition contact with mRNA half-life}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rank-admissible-codon-composition-mrna-halflife-agarwal2022}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
This record concerns the finite-row question whether synonymous residual coordinates in $B^{*}_{Q6}$ may be promoted from codon-composition geometry to a translation-layer survival entry.  The directly checked mRNA-stability contact is intentionally modest: the Agarwal-style codon-composition half-life packet contains $16500$ human rows and $18924$ mouse rows, with a raw cross-row Spearman-type association recorded as $\rho = 0.126224$.  The run used the circularity guard ``tAI non-CSC'' and completed in $46.349$ seconds.  These numbers are evidence that a finite table exists and that codon-composition information can be joined to mRNA half-life readouts; they are not, by themselves, evidence for ribosome-mediated survival.

\paragraph{Rank gate.}
For a proposed entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$, the row object is not the raw codon vector.  The coordinate $q_i$ and the candidate translation readout $T_j$ must first be residualized against controls $Z$ containing amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding length, and the methionine-density baseline.  A survival entry is admissible only when the residualized design has remaining degrees of freedom and a non-saturated row space.  The gate can be summarized as
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i^{\perp Z} &= q_i - \Pi_Z q_i,\\
T_j^{\perp Z} &= T_j - \Pi_Z T_j,\\
S^{QT}_{ij} &> 0 \quad \text{is reportable only if } \operatorname{rank}(q_i^{\perp Z},T_j^{\perp Z}) \text{ is not saturated on the held-out rows.}
\end{aligned}
$$
If the fitted surface reaches an all-perfect in-sample score because the post-control rank consumes the available rows, the entry is rejected as a projection artifact.  In that case the finite table may still define a coupling or a required-data target, but it does not define translation survival.

\paragraph{Observed codon and proxy-translation contacts.}
The associated $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coupling computation loaded three organisms: human, budding yeast, and \emph{E. coli} K-12.  It worked in the $61$-codon count space and computed nine synonymous residual coordinates: $\mathrm{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{AUA}$, $\mathrm{K}_{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  Against the proxy spectrum given by the tRNA-Leu $CUN$-versus-$UUR$ log-copy readout, the maximum absolute coupling was $1882464.1106377328$, with $9$ nonzero entries above the $0.01$ threshold.  The declared null condition, requiring all nine $|A_{ij}| < 0.01$, was not met.  This establishes only that a codon-usage/tRNA-copy proxy contact is nonzero on the finite panel.  It does not certify $S^{QT}_{ij}$, because the proxy is not an organism-matched measured translation readout under the full control set.

\paragraph{Residual-basis audit.}
The broader residual-basis extraction gives the same caution in a different finite form.  For human, the extraction joined $16008$ rows, selected $6$ candidate blocks, and ended with $\rho$ decreasing from $1.0$ to $0.9723341658092314$, with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}} = 0.9020707321191321$ and $d_{\mathrm{difference}} = 0.018824203448813415$.  For budding yeast, it joined $3046$ rows, selected $9$ candidates, and ended at $\rho = 0.9798667977250413$, with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}} = 0.2199040339633322$ and $d_{\mathrm{difference}} = 0.008171838236265161$.  For \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, it joined $3415$ rows, selected $2$ candidates, and ended at $\rho = 0.9979478314995476$.  For zebrafish, it joined $14410$ rows, selected $1$ candidate, and ended at $\rho = 0.9791828971490305$.  Chicken and \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} were marked as needing data.  The regularizer was $\lambda = 0.01$ with description-length penalty $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  The four computed organisms were classified as persistent rather than compressible, with a reported floor of $0.97$ and minimum final $\rho = 0.9723341658092314$.  Thus the finite residual geometry remains largely unabsorbed by the tested readout blocks; this is a negative constraint, not a failure of the table.

\paragraph{Control examples from nearby contacts.}
Two adjacent finite-row checks show why the gate must remain local to its own row design.  The branchpoint motif contact used $10584$ rows with expression controlled by UCSC hg19 gtexGeneV8 log-median tissue expression, and the uORF/Kozak ribosome-load contact recorded $41862$ fixed rows, $16739$ varying rows, and two-sample concordance.  These contacts demonstrate that row-counted biological surfaces can be evaluated with explicit controls, but neither supplies a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation-survival matrix.  They are therefore useful as methodological comparisons, not as substitutes for the missing $Q_i \to T_j$ statistic.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite data support a bounded statement: codon-composition and proxy translation-readout contacts exist, the mRNA half-life packet has large human and mouse row counts, and the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual panel contains nonzero coupling and persistent residual structure.  The same data do not yet support a positive translation-survival entry.  A reportable $S^{QT}_{ij}$ requires organism- or CDS-matched measured translation rows, residualization against $Z$, held-out testing, and a non-saturated residualized design.  Until that computation exists, the correct status is coupling-only or needs-data at the translation boundary.  No claim about translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and statistic.
