\paragraph{Finite codon layer.}
This record isolates the codon-level calculation behind the $f_3$ saturation claim and states exactly what it does, and does not, authorize for open-reading-frame eligibility. The finite motif set $R$ is the thirteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The twenty-codon ambient set $M$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite audit for $M$ records $|M|=20$ and identifies it with the listed $\mathrm{WNR}\cup \mathrm{CUN}$ codons. The motif audit has no missing elements and no extra elements relative to the stated union representation of $R$.

\paragraph{Saturation calculation.}
The $f_3$ saturation output is the eighteen-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\\
&\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded equality is that this is precisely $M$ with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ removed. The complementary reconstruction also records
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the displayed twenty codons as the resulting list. The boundary term for the $f_3$ calculation is empty, recorded as $f_3\text{-boundary}=\varnothing$. Thus the finite statement is not an asymptotic or statistical assertion: it is a closed enumeration over the listed codon rows.

\paragraph{ORF eligibility boundary.}
For the ORF-eligibility layer, the row is deliberately a seed record awaiting contact with curated biological interval data. The start/stop-window enumeration audit loaded one external nucleotide-window manifest with $1$ input window and classified all $1$ of $1$ windows by start/stop enumeration. Its scope is explicitly restricted to external-window start/stop enumeration, and its module classification is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$. That classification is a boundary statement: a start and stop pattern may define an eligibility test, but it is not itself evidence of translation.

\paragraph{Local non-entailment limits.}
The finite saturation claim and the curated interval audit must remain separate. The saturation calculation over $R$, $M$, and $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ supplies a codon-level geometry. The curated interval contact supplies external annotation rows for one ORF-eligibility experiment. Neither source by itself, nor their juxtaposition, entails translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, or universality. Any one of those stronger layers would require an additional contact whose observations are appropriate to that layer. In particular, start/stop-window enumeration alone is not an observed open reading frame, the $f_3$ saturation set is not a protein-structure result, and the RefSeq interval audit remains curated biological evidence rather than content generated by the codon geometry.

\paragraph{Self-check.}
The row-level self-check reports three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. With no contact attached at the seed row itself, the correct reading of this chapter is therefore narrow: $R$ saturates under $f_3$ to the eighteen listed codons, the missing pair inside $M$ is $\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$, the $f_3$ boundary is empty, and ORF eligibility remains a contact-dependent biological classification rather than a consequence of finite codon geometry alone.
