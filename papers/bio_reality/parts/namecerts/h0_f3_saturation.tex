\subsection{Saturation Boundary for Start--Stop ORF Eligibility}
\label{sec:h0-f3-saturation}

\paragraph{Finite codon census.}
This record isolates a codon-level saturation calculation and keeps it separate from biological realization.  The finite ambient set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The motif-compressed subset $R$ contains $13$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
& UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The compression audit reports no missing and no extra entries in this representation.  Thus the chapter begins with a closed finite enumeration rather than a biological assertion.  Its purpose is to say what the codon geometry permits as a formal boundary and, just as importantly, what the geometry does not license.

\paragraph{Saturation fact.}
For the $f_3$ saturation test, the observed row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) = \{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
& UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the set $M$ with precisely $ACA$ and $ACG$ absent.  The companion equality records
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \operatorname{Med}(R) \cup \{ACA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with the displayed row listing the same $20$ codons as the ambient census.  The $f_3$ boundary contribution is empty, recorded as $f3\_boundary\_is\_zero=[]$.  The local mathematical reading is therefore narrow: the saturation operator reaches $18$ of the $20$ codons in $M$, misses $2$ named codons, and contributes no separate boundary residue in the finite row.  Nothing in this calculation observes a transcript, a translated product, a cellular state, or an organismal function.

\paragraph{ORF eligibility scope.}
The linked start--stop enumeration row is deliberately weaker than translation evidence.  It reports one loaded external nucleotide-window manifest, one input window, and one classified window, with scope restricted to external-window start--stop enumeration.  The snapshot date is $2026$-$05$-$25$.  The declared output layer is only ORF eligibility.  This means that the start and stop positions can be checked against a supplied window, but the finite codon geometry alone remains silent about whether any cellular system transcribes, translates, stabilizes, folds, localizes, or uses the corresponding sequence.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The chapter-level claim is therefore conditional and stratified.  The saturation row proves a finite codon relation: $R$ compresses to the listed $13$ codons, $M$ contains $20$ codons, $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ has the $18$ displayed members, and the finite boundary residue for $f_3$ is empty.  The curated interval dataset, when invoked, supplies biological contact for ORF-interval eligibility over its own $168$ rows and its stated controls.  These are two different kinds of evidence.  The codon saturation computation can help define what a start--stop eligibility test is allowed to ask, while the curated annotation contact supplies the observed interval data needed to answer it for that dataset.

\paragraph{Negative scope.}
No translation realization follows from this chapter.  No folded structure follows.  No physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, or universal law follows.  The finite saturation row does not claim that $AUG$ initiates translation in a given organismal context, that $UAA$, $UAG$, or $UGA$ terminates a realized product in a sampled cell, or that any listed interval produces a stable protein.  Such conclusions require a distinct contact capable of testing the corresponding biological layer.  The only ready claim here is the finite saturation boundary and its disciplined use in ORF-eligibility bookkeeping.
