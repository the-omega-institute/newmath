\subsection{Third-face saturation and ORF-eligibility boundary status}
\label{sec:biorality-h0-f3-saturation-or/eligibility-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite codon packet.}
The record indexed by \texttt{h0.f3.saturation} isolates a finite codon calculation before any biological interval is asserted. The relevant motif row contains exactly the thirteen codons
$\{\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UUA}\}$,
with no missing and no extra entries in the motif-union check. The ambient finite row $M$ contains twenty codons:
$\{\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AAG},\texttt{ACA},\texttt{ACG},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{AUG},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UCG},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UGG},\texttt{UUA},\texttt{UUG}\}$.
The independent enumeration check reports $|M|=20$ and identifies $M$ with the listed $\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ finite row. Thus the saturation statement is not a biological observation; it is a closed finite enumeration over the listed codon alphabet slice.

\paragraph{Saturation calculation.}
The verified saturation row states that the third-face saturation of the motif row fills every member of $M$ except \texttt{ACA} and \texttt{ACG}. In the notation of this chapter, the calculated row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)
  ={}& \{\texttt{AAA},\texttt{AAG},\texttt{AGA},\texttt{AGG},\texttt{AUA},\texttt{AUG},\texttt{CUA},\texttt{CUC},\texttt{CUG},\texttt{CUU},\\
  &\texttt{UAA},\texttt{UAG},\texttt{UCA},\texttt{UCG},\texttt{UGA},\texttt{UGG},\texttt{UUA},\texttt{UUG}\},\\
M ={}& \operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\texttt{ACA},\texttt{ACG}\},\\
|M| ={}& 20,\qquad |\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)|=18.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded boundary component for this finite operation is empty, $f3\_boundary=\varnothing$. This means that the packet has no additional codon-row exception beyond the two named absent members \texttt{ACA} and \texttt{ACG}. It does not mean that a genomic interval, transcript, translated product, or molecular role has been observed.

\paragraph{Quotient geometry kept separate from biology.}

These numbers certify a finite combinatorial shape only. They do not identify an open reading frame, because ORF eligibility requires a separate observed interval or curated annotation. The geometric calculation may propose where a boundary test should be aimed, but it is not itself the test result.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility gate.}
The seed claim attached to this row is deliberately weaker than an ORF assertion. Its biological object is open-reading-frame eligibility from start/stop codon enumeration, and its layer is \texttt{orf\_eligibility}. The corresponding one-window start/stop enumeration audit loaded one external nucleotide-window dataset, classified one input window, and recorded the scope as \texttt{external\_window\_start\_stop\_enumeration\_only}. The same audit explicitly stored the module outcome \texttt{orf\_eligibility\_only}. Hence the finite codon row is allowed to state only that a start/stop-window classifier can be applied when an external window is supplied. It is not allowed to infer translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological rule.

\paragraph{Curated interval contact.}
A later contact row supplies a separate curated-interval test rather than promoting the codon geometry by itself. That contact loaded 168 rows from a curated interval dataset derived from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, snapshot date 2026-05-30. The frozen classifier was \texttt{orf\_start\_stop\_window\_eligibility}; its rules were not modified after the protocol freeze. The row contains $72$ positive curated ORF intervals, $72$ matched non-ORF decoys, and $24$ boundary cases. All $72$ positives were accepted and all $72$ matched decoys were rejected. The exact one-sided binomial statistics reported for the positive, decoy, and paired baseline comparisons are all $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{positive accept rate} &= 72/72 = 1.0,\\
\text{matched decoy reject rate} &= 72/72 = 1.0,\\
\text{paired baseline win rate} &= 72/72 = 1.0,\\
 p_{\mathrm{exact}} &= 2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The three held-out splits each used $24$ positives and $24$ decoys; each split reported positive accept rate $1.0$, decoy reject rate $1.0$, and pass status true. The boundary cases were not promoted: $0$ of $24$ boundary rows were promoted, for promoted fraction $0.0$.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The correct reading of this chapter is therefore two-stage. First, the row \texttt{h0.f3.saturation} proves a finite third-face saturation statement over a twenty-codon set, with an eighteen-codon saturated subrow and empty recorded boundary residual. Second, a distinct curated contact can test ORF-eligibility on external biological intervals. The first stage alone has no authority to assert an observed ORF. Even after the curated-interval contact, the certified layer remains ORF eligibility for that contact scope. No translation event, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biochemical function, causal mechanism, organism-level role, or universal genomic principle follows from these rows unless an additional contact is supplied for that higher layer.

\paragraph{Self-check outcome.}
The self-check row records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers. This is the intended discipline of the packet. The finite saturation calculation can be used as a precise boundary probe: it says which codon-window patterns are available for a classifier and which two codons, \texttt{ACA} and \texttt{ACG}, remain outside the saturated row. It does not transform a combinatorial coordinate into biological reality without an independently attached observation.
