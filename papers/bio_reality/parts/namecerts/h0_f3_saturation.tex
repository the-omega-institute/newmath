\paragraph{Finite codon packet.}
The claim identified by \texttt{h0.f3.saturation} is a finite codon-level record. Its biological reading is deliberately narrow: it concerns open-reading-frame eligibility as a start/stop-window enumeration problem, not as an assertion that translation, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular mechanism, biological function, or universal biological law has been established. & CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The correct local reading is therefore two-level. First, \texttt{h0.f3.saturation} gives a finite codon-geometry certificate: $|M|=20$, $|R|=13$, $|\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)|=18$, the two ambient elements outside that saturation are $ACA$ and $ACG$, and the finite $f_3$ boundary is empty. Second, curated biological interval rows can test start/stop-window eligibility against external annotation data, and in the supplied dataset the positive, decoy, boundary, paired, and held-out checks are all numerically sharp. Even so, the conclusion remains restricted to ORF-eligibility classification for the contacted interval data. No translation event, folded structure, physical admissibility, cellular function, mechanism of action, or universal biological rule follows from the finite geometry or from the start/stop enumeration unless a distinct contact is supplied for that stronger layer.
