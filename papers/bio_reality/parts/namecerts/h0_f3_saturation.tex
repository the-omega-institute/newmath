Equivalently, the two codons absent from this saturated image inside $M$ are $\texttt{ACA}$ and $\texttt{ACG}$. The row also verifies the finite recovery identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{\texttt{ACA}\},\\
\partial_{f_3} &= \varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the local boundary reported by the saturation routine is empty, even though the saturation set itself is not all of $M$. This distinction is the point of the packet: a closed finite codon calculation can certify a combinatorial closure condition while still withholding a biological assertion. In particular, start and stop codon enumeration can define a candidate window language, but the language is not the same thing as an observed open reading frame.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The only claim made here is the following finite mathematical one: the verified codon row has the stated $M$, $R$, $f_3$-saturation, empty $f_3$ boundary, and quotient profile. The biological reading is deliberately narrower. Start--stop enumeration may specify an ORF-eligibility test once a sequence, transcript, or curated annotation contact supplies an interval to test. Without such a contact, the packet remains a specification of a testable boundary. Even with the linked curated-interval evidence, the conclusion is limited to the declared ORF-eligibility experiment and its finite dataset. No translation event, folded protein structure, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, organism-wide law, or universal genomic principle follows from the codon geometry or from the finite interval classifier unless another contact is supplied for that higher layer. The self-check row agrees with this interpretation: it records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
