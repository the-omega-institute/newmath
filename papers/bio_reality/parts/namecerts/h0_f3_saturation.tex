\paragraph{Finite row under examination.}
This record concerns the finite codon packet identified by the claim key $h0.f3.saturation$.  The verified saturation computation gives a concrete list of twenty codons in $M$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\nonumber\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The motif-compressed row gives the smaller thirteen-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UGA,UUA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing and no extra entries in the recorded motif union.  The independently checked union row records $|M|=20$ and presents the same twenty members as $WNR\cup CUN$.  These are finite symbolic facts about the codon table slice; by themselves they are not observations of a transcript, a translated protein, a folded molecule, a physical viability condition, or a biological role.

\paragraph{The $f_3$ saturation fact.}
The row named $h0.f3.saturation$ verifies that the $f_3$-saturation of $R$ reaches exactly eighteen members:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\nonumber\\
&UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two elements of $M$ not included in this saturated list are $ACA$ and $ACG$.  The same run records the completing identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{ACA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where the displayed right-hand side is certified by the finite row as the same twenty-member list for $M$.  The boundary output for the $f_3$ computation is empty, recorded as $f3\_boundary\_is\_zero=[]$.  This makes the row a closed finite enumeration statement about codon-symbol saturation, not a contact with organismal reality.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility boundary.}
The biological object named by the surrounding packet is open-reading-frame eligibility from start/stop codon enumeration.  The local rule is strict: a start--stop window can be used only as a proposed eligibility test until a separate sequence, transcript, or curated-annotation contact supplies an actual biological interval.  The finite saturation row can say that $R$, $M$, $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$, and the quotient packet have the displayed members and counts.  It cannot say that any open reading frame exists in a genome, that translation occurs, that a protein folds, that the molecule is physically admissible, that a function is realized, that a mechanism has been found, or that a general biological law follows.

\paragraph{What the separate contact rows show.}
A linked window-enumeration row records one external nucleotide-window input and classifies that one input by start/stop enumeration, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$ and scope restricted to external-window start/stop enumeration.  A later curated-interval row records a larger external contact: $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-$12$ MG1655 accession $NC\_000913.3$, snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  In that contact row, the required input classes are positive curated ORFs, matched non-ORF decoys, and boundary cases.  It reports $72$ accepted positives out of $72$, $72$ rejected matched decoys out of $72$, and $24$ boundary cases with $0$ promoted.  The positive exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value is $2.117582368135751\times 10^{-22}$, the matched decoy rejection value is the same, and the predeclared paired baseline sign-test records $72$ wins out of $72$ pairs with the same upper-tail value.  The three held-out splits each contain $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, each with positive acceptance rate $1.0$ and decoy rejection rate $1.0$.

\paragraph{Separation of roles.}
Those external rows demonstrate the kind of biological contact that an ORF-eligibility claim needs, but they do not retroactively turn the $h0.f3.saturation$ row into a translation, folding, physical, functional, mechanistic, or universal claim.  The self-check row records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.  Therefore the present packet is admissible as a finite saturation-and-boundary record.  It supports the statement that the displayed codon sets and quotient data are verified finite enumerations, and it supports the local warning that ORF eligibility requires an additional biological interval contact before the claim can leave boundary status.
