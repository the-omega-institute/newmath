\paragraph{Finite codon support.}
The packet indexed by $h0.f3.saturation$ records a finite codon-level saturation check whose biological reading is deliberately narrow: it is a test of open-reading-frame eligibility from start--stop enumeration, not an observation of translation or protein production.  The verified motif support $R$ consists of the thirteen codons
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The larger finite set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly the recorded union of the WNR and CUN components:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The saturation fact verified at experiment run $f4eee68dd2c3$ states that the $f_3$ saturation of $R$ reaches every element of $M$ except $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run records an empty $f_3$ boundary residual, $f3\_boundary\_is\_zero=\varnothing$, after this finite saturation calculation is evaluated in its own combinatorial coordinate system.

The associated quotient-geometry audit reports the finite vector data $M_f=(20,38,26,8,1)$ for $M$, and the quotient vector $(10,14,6,1)$ after the projection $\pi_M$.  The projection contains ten rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}

This is a finite algebraic compression statement.  It shows how the enumerated codon support behaves under the chosen quotient coordinates, but it does not by itself identify an observed transcript interval, an expressed coding sequence, or a translated protein.  The quotient size $10$ and the vector match are therefore used here only as internal finite-row evidence for the boundary behavior of the codon enumeration.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility reading.}
The biological object attached to this row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration.  The available start--stop-window fact contains one input window and records that all one of the input windows was classified by the start--stop enumeration.  Its scope is explicitly limited to external-window start--stop enumeration, and its classification label is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$.  Thus the row may say that a window has passed the declared start--stop eligibility rule inside the finite enumeration protocol.  It may not say that a living system translated that window, that a protein folded, that a molecule was physically admissible, that a function was realized, that a mechanism was discovered, or that a general biological law has been established.

\paragraph{Reality-contact boundary.}
The contact list for this packet is empty.  Consequently the present row remains a boundary record whose scientific content is conditional: a curated sequence, transcript, or interval annotation must be supplied before an ORF-eligibility assertion about an observed biological interval can be made.  The probes named $\mathrm{orf.eligibility.boundary.requires.contact}$ and $\mathrm{orf.eligibility.contact.gap.check}$ both enforce this point.  They break if the pipeline treats start--stop geometry alone as an observed ORF, or if it promotes the enumeration to translation, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality without a separate contact.

\paragraph{Separate curated-contact comparison.}
A distinct finite contact experiment in the ledger supplies an example of what a contact-bearing ORF-eligibility audit looks like: it loads $168$ curated interval rows from the NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$30$.  That audit reports $72$ accepted positive intervals with accept rate $1.0$ and exact one-sided binomial upper-tail value $2.117582368135751\times10^{-22}$; $72$ matched decoys were rejected with reject rate $1.0$ and the same upper-tail value; and $72$ predeclared positive--decoy pairs gave win rate $1.0$ with $72$ wins.  It also records $24$ boundary cases with zero promotion, three held-out splits, and in each split $24$ positives accepted and $24$ decoys rejected.  These numbers show the form of a contact-backed interval test, but they are not imported into the present $h0.f3.saturation$ packet as its own contact.  In this subsection the $f_3$ computation therefore remains a finite codon-enumeration boundary calculation, not a biological realization claim.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The row is ready as a self-contained finite statement: $R$ has $13$ codons, $M$ has $20$ codons, $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ has the $18$ codons of $M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$, the recorded $f_3$ boundary residual is empty, and the quotient projection has $10$ rows with vector $(10,14,6,1)$.  Its biological interpretation is intentionally conservative.  It marks the start--stop ORF-eligibility boundary that a later external contact may test.  Until such a contact is attached to this packet, no statement about translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality follows from the finite codon geometry.
