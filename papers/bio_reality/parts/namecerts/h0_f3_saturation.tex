\paragraph{Finite saturation record.}
The finite packet records a third-face saturation calculation over the codon alphabet fragment used by the ORF-eligibility boundary test.  The verified set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The motif-compression row records the smaller region
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&AAA,AGA,AGG,AUA,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,\
& UCA,UGA,UUA\},
\end{aligned}
$$
with no missing terms and no extra terms in that comparison.  The third-face saturation row then gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f3}(R)=\{&AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\
& UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\},
\end{aligned}
$$
so this saturation contains $18$ codons.  The two codons present in $M$ but absent from this saturated third-face region are $ACA$ and $ACG$.  The same run records $M=\operatorname{Med}(R)\cup\{ACA\}$ at the finite-row level, while the boundary value $f3\_boundary\_is\_zero$ is the empty list.  Thus the calculation is a closed combinatorial enumeration of codon-window eligibility data; it is not itself an observation of a translated product.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility interpretation.}
The biological layer named here is \texttt{orf\_eligibility}.  Its admissible interpretation is start/stop-window eligibility from enumeration of codon windows.  The direct window-enumeration check loaded an external finite window dataset with $1$ input window, classified $1$ window, and recorded the scope as external window start/stop enumeration only, with snapshot date $2026$-$05$-$25$.  This is the narrow claim: the packet can organize the start/stop boundary question and can report that the available finite window was classified by the enumerator.  It does not license a claim that translation occurred, that a peptide folded, that any physical realization is admissible, that a biological function exists, or that the same rule governs unobserved organisms or contexts.
