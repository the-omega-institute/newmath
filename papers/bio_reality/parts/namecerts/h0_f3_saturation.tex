\subsection{Saturation Boundary for Start--Stop ORF Eligibility}
\label{sec:h0-f3-saturation}

\paragraph{Finite codon packet.}
The record isolates a finite codon-level saturation calculation and asks what, if anything, it licenses about open-reading-frame eligibility. The answer is deliberately narrow. The calculation is a closed enumeration over RNA triplets, while ORF eligibility is a biological layer that requires a separate contact with an observed sequence interval, transcript record, or curated annotation. Thus the codon packet may define a boundary test for a later biological contact, but it does not itself produce an observed ORF.

The motif-compressed row gives the set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\\
&\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded comparison has no missing elements and no extra elements, so the motif row contains exactly these $13$ triplets. The larger finite set is
$$
\begin{aligned}

&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\},
\end{aligned}
$$

Nothing in this equality mentions ribosome binding, transcript abundance, reading-frame usage in a genome, peptide production, tertiary structure, thermodynamic feasibility, or biological function.

\paragraph{The $f_3$ saturation computation.}
For the present row the relevant closure operation is the $f_3$ saturation of $R$. Its output is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the saturation fills all of $M$ except the two codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row also records
$$
\begin{aligned}

$$

This is the saturation fact used here: the finite procedure reaches an $18$-element subset of the $20$-element ambient codon set, with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ remaining outside the $f_3$-saturated image. The empty boundary entry is a statement about this finite enumeration, not a biological certificate.

\paragraph{Reality-contact boundary for ORF eligibility.}
The start--stop-window enumeration row contains one external window and reports that all input windows were classified by start/stop enumeration: $1$ classified window out of $1$ input window. Its scope is explicitly restricted to external-window start/stop enumeration, and its recorded layer is $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$. This means the calculation may say that a supplied window satisfies the start/stop eligibility rule. It does not say that translation occurs, that a peptide is produced, that the peptide folds, that the product is physically admissible, that it has function, that it explains a mechanism, or that it supports a universal law.

A stronger later contact row uses a curated interval dataset with $168$ rows from NCBI RefSeq GenBank XML CDS intervals for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 accession $\mathrm{NC\_000913.3}$. Its protocol records that classifier rules, input classes, baselines, and held-out splits were fixed before the external evidence time. The held-out split audit is also finite: each of the three splits has $24$ positives and $24$ decoys, with positive accept rate $1.0$, decoy reject rate $1.0$, and a passed flag. They remain outside the codon kernel: the curated annotation is an observation-facing input, not a consequence of the $f_3$ saturation table.

No translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality follows unless a separate biological contact supplies that information.
