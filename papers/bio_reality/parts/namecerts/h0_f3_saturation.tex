\paragraph{ORF-eligibility contact discipline.}
The biological object named by the row is open-reading-frame eligibility from start and stop codon enumeration.  The seed statement is intentionally weaker than an ORF assertion: before a separate curated sequence, transcript, or interval annotation is attached, start--stop geometry is only a proposal for what could be tested.  The row includes one external-window enumeration record with $1$ input window and $1$ classified window, from a snapshot dated $2026$-$05$-$25$, and the recorded scope is external window start--stop enumeration only.  That finite contact supports only the classification of the window under the frozen start--stop rule; it does not convert the codon geometry into translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{Local boundary statement.}
The row therefore has two distinct readings.  First, $\mathrm{h0.f3.saturation}$ is a finite codon-saturation fact: $\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ is exactly $M$ with $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ removed, and its recorded boundary is empty.  Second, the ORF-eligibility layer is allowed to use external curated interval contact when such a contact is actually present.  Without that contact, no ORF eligibility claim is made.  With it, the claim remains confined to start--stop interval eligibility in the cited finite dataset.  Nothing in this chapter upgrades codon geometry into translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, or universality.
