This is a finite combinatorial fact about the row, not a measurement of translation or cellular expression.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility interpretation.}
For this chapter the biological phrase ``open-reading-frame eligibility'' is used only in the narrow start/stop-window sense.  A start codon and a terminating codon can delimit a candidate interval in a nucleotide string, but the codon geometry above does not supply such a nucleotide string.  The seed row therefore remains a boundary record for what would need to be tested: an external interval annotation, transcript observation, or comparable sequence-level contact must be supplied before an ORF-eligibility assertion can be made about a biological interval.  In particular, the absence of a third-coordinate boundary in the finite saturation computation does not say that a ribosome translates the interval, that a peptide folds, that the product is physically admissible, that the product has a function, that a mechanism has been established, or that a universal biological rule has been found.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
Those contact rows show how an ORF-eligibility packet can be tested when a separate biological interval source is present.  They do not retroactively turn the $\mathrm{h0.f3.saturation}$ codon geometry into an observed ORF.  The present row contributes the finite codon enumeration, the $13$-element motif subset, the $18$-element third-coordinate saturation, the two excluded codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$, the empty third-coordinate boundary, and the quotient statistics $(20,38,26,8,1)$ and $(10,14,6,1)$.  It does not establish translation, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, taxonomic generality, or universality.  Any such assertion requires an additional contact whose data type can actually observe the relevant biological layer.  The self-check row $\mathrm{0f47d2f0cf41}$ is consistent with this reading: it records three checked gates and states that no codon geometry is promoted across biological layers.
