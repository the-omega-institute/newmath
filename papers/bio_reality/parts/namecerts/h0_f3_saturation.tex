\subsection{The $f_3$ Saturation Boundary for ORF-Eligibility Seeds}
\label{sec:h0-f3-saturation-orf-eligibility-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite codon packet.}
The record studied here is the finite saturation claim $\mathrm{h0.f3.saturation}$, attached to an ORF-eligibility seed whose biological reading is restricted to start--stop codon enumeration. The packet does not contain an observed transcript, a curated genomic interval, a ribosome-contact measurement, a peptide product, a folded structure, or a functional assay. Its carrier is therefore a finite codon-level boundary object: it can say which codon strings are present in the enumerated combinatorial packet, but it cannot by itself assert that any biological open reading frame has been realized in an organism.

The motif-compression component records the thirteen-element set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UUA}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit for $\mathrm{h0.R.motif.compression}$ reports no missing elements and no extra elements, so this list is the finite motif row used by the present packet. The larger codon set $M$ has cardinality $20$ and is recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
M = \{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\\
&\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\\
&\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The equality $M=\mathrm{WNR}\cup\mathrm{CUN}$ is recorded with size $20$, so the saturation claim is not being evaluated against an unspecified biological universe; it is evaluated inside this explicitly listed codon row.

\paragraph{Saturation statement.}
The verified $f_3$ saturation row says that the mediated closure of $R$ together with the boundary codon contribution reaches exactly the same twenty codons listed for $M$. In the recorded finite data this appears as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Med}(R)\cup\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}=M.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding saturated subset omits precisely the two codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\\
&\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\\
&\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}=M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary list for $f_3$ is empty, recorded as $0$ boundary cases. Thus the finite-row conclusion is a sharp codon-enumeration fact: the saturation operation has no remaining listed boundary residue, while two named codons remain outside the saturated image and enter only through the separate mediated-row equality.

\paragraph{ORF-eligibility boundary.}
The biological object associated with the packet is open-reading-frame eligibility from start--stop codon enumeration. The finite row has no attached reality contact in its own contact list, and its two probes both require a curated ORF interval annotation before any ORF-eligibility assertion can be made. The first probe rejects any pipeline behavior that treats start--stop-window geometry as an observed ORF without a separate sequence, transcript, or curated-annotation input. The second probe rejects any ORF-eligibility claim made while no sequence database, transcript evidence, or curated annotation can test the proposed interval.

A separate start--stop window enumeration row reports one external window and one classified window, with scope explicitly limited to external-window start--stop enumeration only. Its snapshot date is $2026$-$05$-$25$, and its classification field states $\mathrm{orf\_eligibility\_only}$. This is deliberately weaker than translation. It records that a window can be classified by the declared start--stop enumeration rule, not that a ribosome used the interval, not that a protein exists, and not that any structural or functional property follows.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The conclusion of this chapter is therefore deliberately narrow. The $f_3$ row verifies a finite codon saturation fact: $M$ has $20$ listed codons, $R$ has $13$ listed motif codons with no missing or extra motif entries, $\mathrm{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$ has the $18$ listed codons $M\setminus\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$, and the recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty. The ORF-eligibility seed remains a boundary-level object until an external sequence, transcript, or curated interval contact is attached. From this saturation packet alone one may not infer translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, biochemical mechanism, or universal law. Any such stronger claim requires a separate contact whose finite rows explicitly test that stronger layer.
