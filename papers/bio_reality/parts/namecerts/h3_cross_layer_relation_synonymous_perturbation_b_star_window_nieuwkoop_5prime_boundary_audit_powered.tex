\subsection{Nieuwkoop 5-prime Boundary Audit for the Synonymous B-Star Window}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-5prime-boundary-audit}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record audits whether the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-variant signal assigned to the $B^{*}$ coding-window coordinates is merely a disguised 5-prime boundary effect.  The finite table has $1459$ synonymous variants and carries three observable levels: a start-window baseline, a stronger explicitly fitted boundary/control layer, and a downstream $B^{*}$ window layer tested after that boundary layer is already present.  The relevant readout is protein output in the Nieuwkoop mRFP perturbation panel, while the $B^{*}$ coordinates remain sequence-derived residual coordinates.  The audit is therefore not asking whether synonymous sequence geometry alone is protein realization; it asks a narrower question: after a 5-prime boundary explanation has absorbed the large predictable component, does the downstream $B^{*}$ window still add stable protein-output information?

\paragraph{Registered numerical result.}
The run identifier for the boundary audit is \texttt{3cb8ca415be5}.  The first fitted layer gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step1}} &= 0.06748192482378434 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the explicit boundary/control layer gives a large improvement,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step2}} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{step2-step1}} &= 628.6217154500001 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The downstream $B^{*}$ window then fails to improve the already boundary-aware model:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step3}} &= 0.6049438075310105,\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{step3-step2}} &= -2.082965600280344,\\
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The block-level audit gives $p_{\mathrm{block}}=0.1691542288557214$ against a null95 value of $0.5254770343527547$, with $\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream}}=-2.082967365015851$ and $\Delta \ell_{B,16+}=-2.1989730953428164$.  The start-only $B$ contribution is numerically negligible,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6},\\
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9} .
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign-stability flag is false.  With $B=200$ resampling blocks, the finite verdict is that the 5-prime boundary/control layer explains the dominant recoverable protein-output variation in this panel, while the downstream $B^{*}$ window has no supported residual increment after that layer is installed.

\paragraph{Relation to the same Nieuwkoop protein-output panel.}
The companion Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output run, \texttt{a18abe395a72}, uses the same $1459$ variants and reports a positive controlled $B^{*}$ signal before this specific boundary audit is imposed: $R^2$ moves from $0.12648386762689323$ for the level-two controls to $0.1798829276988051$ after the perpendicular $B$ component, with $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched and within-panel $p$ values both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  That earlier finite contact shows that a $B^{*}$-window statistic can be detected in the mRFP variant table.  The present audit localizes the interpretation: the detected association is not allowed to be read as downstream coding-window mediation once the strong 5-prime boundary/control component has already been fitted.  The two records are compatible only under this scoped reading: positive panel-level association, but no stable downstream increment beyond the 5-prime boundary surface.

\paragraph{Cross-layer admissibility.}
The broader translation-mediated protein-abundance matrix supplies a separate statistical context for $Q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ mediation.  In that matrix, the per-protein readout is modeled tAI followed by matched protein abundance, and \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} has $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$, $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, and an indirect component of $0.28320534058057895$; \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 has $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$, $c=0.10767526677985896$, $c'=0.04167869969498872$, and an indirect component of $0.06599656708487024$.  Those numbers justify keeping a finite mediation vocabulary in the BioReality paper, but they do not rescue the Nieuwkoop 5-prime downstream window.  The Nieuwkoop boundary audit is a stricter perturbation-panel question, and its own downstream increment is negative.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
This chapter permits only the following conclusion: in the Nieuwkoop mRFP synonymous-variant table, the 5-prime boundary/control layer dominates the fitted protein-output signal, and the downstream $B^{*}$ window does not survive as a stable additional predictor under the recorded audit.  No claim about translation dynamics, ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, causal mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality follows from these rows.  A protein-output association is also not a folded-protein or function assay.  Any such stronger reading would require a separate layer-matched measurement and a new finite statistic that first establishes the intervening translation readout rather than jumping directly from a residual coordinate to protein abundance.
