\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for the B-star window readout}\label{subsec:biorreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record audits whether the protein-output signal in the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} synonymous perturbation panel is carried by a start-proximal boundary artefact rather than by the declared B-star window coordinate. The finite surface contains $1459$ synonymous variants. The fitted audit was run with $200$ block-null draws and completed in $326.292$ seconds. The relevant coordinates are not promoted to amino-acid change, folded state, molecular function, organismal fitness, or selection; they are used only as synonymous sequence-window covariates measured against the same reporter-output table.

\paragraph{Nested readout decomposition.}
The audit uses three nested prediction levels. The boundary-only starting surface has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{1} &= 0.06748192482378434.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the dominant non-boundary explanatory block gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{2} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
\Delta \ell_{2\mid 1} &= 628.6217154500001.
\end{aligned}
$$
The subsequent B-star downstream boundary audit step gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{3} &= 0.6049438075310105,\\
\Delta \ell_{3\mid 2} &= -2.082965600280344.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite-row calculation does not show a downstream boundary gain after the stronger preceding block is already present. In the same audit vocabulary, the start-window B-star increment is essentially zero:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9},\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the downstream B-star window the recorded increment is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -2.082967365015851,\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream},16+} &= -2.1989730953428164.
\end{aligned}
$$
The block-null reference has null $95\%$ level $0.5254770343527547$, while the block permutation tail probability is $p=0.1691542288557214$. The sign-stability flag is false. These numbers force the local verdict: the five-prime boundary audit does not support interpreting the Nieuwkoop reporter signal as a start-proximal B-star boundary effect.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein-output panel.}
The companion Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output fit over the same $1459$ variants reported a positive B-star residual contribution after controls, with $R^2_{\mathrm{controls}}=0.12648386762689323$, $R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B_\perp}=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched-null and within-null probabilities both equal to $0.004975124378109453$. The present boundary audit is therefore not a rejection of every synonymous protein-output association in the dataset. It is a localization check: the detected protein-output association is not explained by the audited five-prime boundary slot, because the start increment is numerically negligible, the downstream increment is negative, and the block-null comparison does not stabilize the boundary direction.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row readback is a controlled negative for the five-prime boundary explanation. It supports the narrower statement that, in this Nieuwkoop synonymous perturbation panel, the B-star window signal cannot be collapsed to the audited start-proximal boundary coordinate. The result is still a reporter-output association, not a direct translation-mechanism measurement. No claim of translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, biological mechanism, organismal phenotype, evolutionary selection, or universal biological law follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the corresponding controlled statistic.
