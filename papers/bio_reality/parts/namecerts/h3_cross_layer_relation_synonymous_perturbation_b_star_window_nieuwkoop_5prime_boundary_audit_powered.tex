\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for a synonymous B-star window}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}
The audit is a finite perturbation readback on the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-variant panel.  The row set contains $1459$ variants and is read as a boundary test for whether the B-star window signal is confined to the five-prime start-proximal region or remains after the start segment is separated from the downstream coding sequence.  The registered statistic uses $B=200$ finite null or block resampling replicates.  Its rows carry measured protein output and synonymous coding-window coordinates, but the audit does not by itself create a translation mechanism, folding readout, physical-admissibility assay, molecular-function assay, or organism-level law.

\paragraph{Nested finite models.}
Let $Y$ denote the measured protein-output readout on the $1459$ variants.  The audit compares three finite regressions.  The first model uses the lower-level control surface and has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{1} &= 0.06748192482378434 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the start-proximal explanatory block gives a large finite improvement,
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{2} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
\Delta \ell_{2:1} &= 628.6217154500001 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the downstream B-star block after the start-proximal block does not improve the held surface:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{3} &= 0.6049438075310105,\\
\Delta \ell_{3:2} &= -2.082965600280344 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite comparison assigns the explanatory jump to the start-proximal slot and rejects promotion of the downstream block as an additional protein-output coordinate in this panel.

\paragraph{Start and downstream B-star contrasts.}
The direct start-block B-star increment is numerically present only at numerical-noise scale,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 1.7624020537495221 \times 10^{-9},\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 3.26370933437133 \times 10^{-6} .
\end{aligned}
$$
The downstream B-star contrast is negative:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -2.082967365015851,\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream},16+} &= -2.1989730953428164 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The block-null calibration gives $\mathrm{null}_{95}=0.5254770343527547$ and $p_{\mathrm{block}}=0.1691542288557214$.  The sign-stability flag is false.  Consequently the correct finite conclusion is not that the B-star downstream window mediates protein output in this audit, but that the measured protein-output association in the Nieuwkoop panel is start-boundary dominated after the declared controls and null comparisons.

\paragraph{Relation to the positive Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output fit.}
A separate Nieuwkoop mRFP fit on the same $1459$-variant scale reports a positive B-window increment when the full B-perpendicular block is compared against controls: $R^2_{L2}=0.12648386762689323$, $R^2_{L3}=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, with matched and within-panel permutation probabilities both $0.004975124378109453$.  That result says that a B-window coordinate can carry protein-output information in this perturbation panel.  The present five-prime audit refines the location of that information: after the start-proximal block is admitted, the downstream B-star addition has negative likelihood gain and negative $\Delta R^2$.  The two finite records are therefore compatible only under a boundary reading: the panel supports a protein-output association for the B-window surface, while the downstream-after-start residual is not separately supported.

\paragraph{Cross-layer mediation boundary.}
Across a broader finite protein-abundance mediation matrix, the B-star Q6 coordinate family has nine coordinates, and the modeled translation-mediated protein-abundance study reports positive statistical mediation fractions in several organisms, for example $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $0.6165167748739756$ in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, and $0.27206289160119157$ in \emph{Homo sapiens}.  Those cross-sectional mediation fractions use modeled per-protein tAI and PAXdb abundance and are not ribosome-footprint measurements.  They justify treating $Q \to T \to P$ as a bounded finite mediation surface when its rows are present, but they do not license a shortcut from a synonymous residual coordinate directly to protein realization.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit is therefore a ready negative-boundary chapter for downstream promotion.  The admissible statement is that, on $1459$ finite variants with $200$ null replicates, the start-proximal model accounts for the large protein-output gain, while the downstream B-star block after that start boundary has $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, $\Delta \ell=-2.082967365015851$, $p_{\mathrm{block}}=0.1691542288557214$, and no stable sign.  No translation-realization claim follows from these rows alone; no folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, causal mechanism, phenotype, selection claim, or universal biological law follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding measurement and the same-coordinate translation step survives its own controlled readout.
