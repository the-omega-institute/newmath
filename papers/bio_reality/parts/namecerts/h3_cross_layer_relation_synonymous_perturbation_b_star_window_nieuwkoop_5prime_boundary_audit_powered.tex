\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for the $B^*$ synonymous window}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}

\paragraph{Finite row.}
The finite surface for this record is the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-variant panel restricted to the same windowed $B^*$ coordinate family used in the protein-output run.  The row count is $n=1459$ variants, and the permutation budget is $B=200$.  The audit separates three questions.  First, it asks whether the leading coding boundary alone carries the apparent signal.  Second, it asks whether the main fitted non-synonymous-control and expression-scale surface explains most of the measurable protein-output variation.  Third, it asks whether the downstream $B^*$ window contributes positive held-out information after the earlier terms have already been admitted.

\paragraph{Controlled likelihood ledger.}
The registered row reports a first-stage coefficient-of-determination value
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_1 &= 0.06748192482378434 .
\end{aligned}
$$
After the second-stage surface is added, the fit rises to
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_2 &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
\Delta \ell_{2:1} &= 628.6217154500001 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the assay contains a strong finite protein-output signal, but the signal is not automatically a $B^*$ downstream-window signal.  The third stage, which tests the downstream $B^*$ contribution after the earlier surface, moves in the wrong direction:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_3 &= 0.6049438075310105,\\
\Delta R^2_{3:2} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
\Delta \ell_{3:2} &= -2.082965600280344 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The same downstream term is recorded directly as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -2.082967365015851,\\
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
p_{\mathrm{block}} &= 0.1691542288557214,\\
\mathrm{null}_{0.95} &= 0.5254770343527547 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The downstream-after-position-sixteen audit is also negative, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream}\,16+} &= -2.1989730953428164 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The five-prime start term is numerically negligible:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 0.00000326370933437133,\\
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{start}} &= 0.0000000017624020537495221 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The sign-stability flag is false.  This is the decisive finite-row fact for the chapter: the protein-output assay is real enough to be fitted, but the audited $B^*$ downstream window does not survive as a positive, sign-stable improvement once the boundary and earlier explanatory surface are present.

\paragraph{Relation to the paired mRFP output row.}
The companion mRFP protein-output run on the same $n=1459$ variants reported a positive controlled increment before this boundary audit: $R^2_{\mathrm{controls}}=0.12648386762689323$, $R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B_\perp}=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta \mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched-null and within-null values both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  The boundary audit therefore does not erase the existence of a perturbational protein-output association in the broader row.  It narrows the scientific readback: the association may not be promoted to a stable downstream-window law for $B^*$ in this assay, because the explicit downstream term is nonpositive after the audit split.

\paragraph{Translation-mediated interpretation.}
The larger $Q\to T\to P$ program requires a controlled translation-readout step before a residual coordinate is treated as protein-realization evidence.  In the multi-organism modeled-tAI mediation surface, the protein-abundance row uses nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates and per-protein modeled tAI as $T$, with organism-specific mediated fractions such as $0.612922252793667$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.9491551041216973$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, and $0.27206289160119157$ for human.  Those values belong to a cross-sectional modeled-readout mediation surface, not to the Nieuwkoop perturbation boundary audit.  The modeled-tAI survival record itself is also bounded: it used only three organisms in the initial modeled-contact calculation, with $81$ entries above the $0.01$ coupling threshold and a maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$, while explicitly recording that three organisms are insufficient for powered survival.  Consequently, the present chapter reads the Nieuwkoop row only as a finite synonymous-perturbation boundary test on protein output, not as a direct observation of ribosome motion or translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
For this finite row, the admissible statement is negative and bounded.  The experiment supports a completed audit of the five-prime and downstream-window alternatives, and the downstream $B^*$ window fails the positive-improvement requirement under the registered likelihood and block-null checks.  The correct readback is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &< 0,\\
\Delta \ell_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &< 0,\\
p_{\mathrm{block}} &= 0.1691542288557214,\\
\mathrm{sign\ stable} &= \mathrm{false} .
\end{aligned}
$$
This keeps the row inside a controlled perturbational protein-output audit rather than a positive downstream-window realization claim.  No translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organism-level phenotype, selection law, or universality follows from this record.  Any such higher-layer statement would need its own layer-matched contact and a fresh finite statistic showing the appropriate readout after amino-acid sequence, organism, GC3, length, codon-density baseline, and matched abundance controls have been applied.
