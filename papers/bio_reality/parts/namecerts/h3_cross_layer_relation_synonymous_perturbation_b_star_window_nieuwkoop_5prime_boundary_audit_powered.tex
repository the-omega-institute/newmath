\subsection{Nieuwkoop Five-Prime Boundary Audit for the $B^{\ast}$ Window}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record fixes a finite perturbation audit for the Nieuwkoop synonymous mRFP variant panel. The working table has $1459$ variants and asks whether the $B^{\ast}$ window signal remains a downstream coding-sequence signal after the extreme five-prime boundary is separated from the rest of the coding sequence. The audited carrier is the finite row family
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Omega_{\mathrm{Nwk}}=
  \{(v, y_v, z_v, b^{\mathrm{start}}_v, b^{\mathrm{down}}_v): v=1,\ldots,1459\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $y_v$ is the measured protein-output readout used by the synonymous variant assay, $z_v$ denotes the local sequence and simpler-baseline controls used by the run, $b^{\mathrm{start}}_v$ is the five-prime boundary component, and $b^{\mathrm{down}}_v$ is the downstream $B^{\ast}$ component. The assay is therefore not a general codon-usage claim. It is a finite comparison between a boundary-local feature and a downstream-window feature on the same matched variant panel.

\paragraph{Three-step fit.}
The run first fits the available baseline surface and obtains
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_1 &= 0.06748192482378434.
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the strong five-prime/boundary component gives a large finite improvement,
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta \ell_{2-1} &= 628.6217154500001,\\
  R^2_2 &= 0.6060702157093043.
\end{aligned}
$$
The audit question is then whether the downstream $B^{\ast}$ component still carries protein-output information after that boundary component is present. The third step gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta \ell_{3-2} &= -2.082965600280344,\\
  R^2_3 &= 0.6049438075310105.
\end{aligned}
$$
Equivalently, the registered downstream statistic is negative on the held model comparison,
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{down}} &= -0.001126409133973234,\\
  \Delta \ell_{\mathrm{down}} &= -2.082967365015851.
\end{aligned}
$$
The block-null calibration used $B=200$ resamples and gives a downstream null $95\%$ cutoff of $0.5254770343527547$ with $p_{\mathrm{block}}=0.1691542288557214$. The sign-stability flag is false. The stricter downstream component beginning at codon $16$ is also negative, with $\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{down},16+}=-2.1989730953428164$.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The finite result is not that the Nieuwkoop panel lacks a synonymous signal. The companion Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output run on the same $1459$ variants reports a broader $B^{\ast}$ improvement with
$$
\begin{aligned}
  R^2_{\mathrm{controls}} &= 0.12648386762689323,\\
  R^2_{\mathrm{controls}+B_\perp} &= 0.1798829276988051,\\
  \Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
  \Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296,
\end{aligned}
$$
and matched and within-null probabilities both equal to $0.004975124378109453$. The boundary audit refines that positive row-level contact: once the first five-prime boundary component has been admitted, the remaining downstream $B^{\ast}$ window does not earn a positive finite-row increment in this panel. Thus the correct readout is a localized protein-output boundary effect, not a claim that a distributed downstream codon-window coordinate independently survives after the five-prime component is controlled.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-mediated protein abundance.}
The broader multi-organism mediation surface gives a useful scale for what would be required to promote a codon residual into a translation-mediated protein-abundance statement. In that separate finite panel, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual family has $9$ coordinates and the per-protein modeled-tAI/protein-abundance mediation table includes $12$ organisms. For example, the Escherichia coli row has $3739$ joined proteins, total $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04167869969498872$, and an indirect difference $0.06599656708487024$, with mediated fraction $0.612922252793667$. The yeast row has $5815$ joined proteins, total $R^2_{QP}=0.29837624994140827$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.0151709093608293$, and indirect difference $0.28320534058057895$, with mediated fraction $0.9491551041216973$. Those numbers belong to a modeled cross-organism mediation audit, not to the Nieuwkoop five-prime perturbation boundary. They show the kind of separate translation-readout surface needed before a protein-output association can be described as translation mediated.

\paragraph{Allowed statement.}
For the Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit, the supported statement is finite and local: among $1459$ synonymous mRFP variants, the large improvement occurs at the five-prime boundary step, while the downstream $B^{\ast}$ component is nonpositive after that boundary component is included. The start component itself is effectively null as a separate $B^{\ast}$ increment in the registered scalar, with $\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{start}}=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$ and $\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{start}}=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$, so the finite audit should be read as a boundary-decomposition result rather than as a new independent downstream survival claim. The observed mRFP protein-output contact remains real at the panel level, but its boundary-resolved interpretation is constrained by the negative downstream increment.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this audit by itself. A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not enough to claim translation-mediated realization unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled $Q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout step, with matched mRNA abundance and simpler baseline controls. Protein output in this section means the finite Nieuwkoop reporter readout only. Folding, stability, localization, physical feasibility, function, and generality would each require their own layer-matched measurement surface and a separate statistic on rows joined to the same perturbation or sequence coordinates.
