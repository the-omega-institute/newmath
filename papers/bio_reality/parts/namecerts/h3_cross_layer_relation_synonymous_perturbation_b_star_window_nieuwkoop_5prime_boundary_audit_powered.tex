\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for the $B^{\ast}$ window}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record isolates the Nieuwkoop synonymous perturbation panel as a finite boundary test for whether the $B^{\ast}$ window signal should be read as a five-prime start effect, as a downstream coding-window effect, or as no stable window-specific improvement after the ordinary experimental controls are already present.  The row carrier is the matched variant table used by the registered run: $1459$ synonymous mRFP variants with a fixed audit budget of $200$ block-null resamples.  Each row is read through three nested predictive surfaces.  The first surface contains the baseline controls.  The second adds the dominant experimental explanation carried by the Nieuwkoop design.  The third asks whether the downstream $B^{\ast}$ window coordinate adds anything after that second surface has already been fitted.

\paragraph{Observed numerical trace.}
The finite trace is not a positive downstream-window result.  The baseline surface has $R^2=0.06748192482378434$.  The second surface raises the fit to $R^2=0.6060702157093043$ with a log-likelihood gain of $628.6217154500001$ against the first surface.  Adding the downstream $B^{\ast}$ window after that point lowers the fit to $R^2=0.6049438075310105$ and gives $\Delta\ell=-2.082965600280344$ relative to the second surface.  In the direct downstream audit the same sign appears as $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$ and $\Delta\ell=-2.082967365015851$, while the block-null reference has null $95\%$ value $0.5254770343527547$ and block $p=0.1691542288557214$.  The start-window entry is also negligible: $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$ and $\Delta\ell=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$.  The downstream window restricted beyond the first sixteen positions remains negative, with $\Delta\ell=-2.1989730953428164$.  The sign-stability flag is false.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step\ 1}} &= 0.06748192482378434,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step\ 2}} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step\ 3}} &= 0.6049438075310105,\\
\Delta \ell_{2-1} &= 628.6217154500001,\\
\Delta \ell_{3-2} &= -2.082965600280344.
\end{aligned}
$$

\paragraph{Boundary interpretation.}
The numerical pattern says that the Nieuwkoop design contains a strong non-$B^{\ast}$ explanatory step, but the audited $B^{\ast}$ downstream coordinate does not survive as an additional five-prime boundary term on this panel.  The result is therefore a refusal of promotion, not a failure of the full perturbation dataset.  In the related Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output run on the same variant count, the broader $B^{\ast}$ residual surface had $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched as well as within-null $p=0.004975124378109453$, with the real-CAI control not absorbing the signal.  The present boundary audit asks a narrower question: whether the five-prime start or downstream window decomposition itself remains a stable explanatory coordinate after the stronger nested surface is present.  On that narrower question the downstream increment is nonpositive and the sign is not stable.

\paragraph{Mediation status.}
The cross-layer mediation carrier for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ is only admissible when a residual coordinate first survives a translation-readout step and is then carried to a matched protein-abundance readout.  The broader multi-organism mediation run supplies that kind of bounded statistical decomposition for organism-matched codon and protein rows: for example, the per-protein mediation fractions include $0.612922252793667$ in \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655 and $0.9491551041216973$ in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, computed from modeled per-protein tAI and PAXdb abundance after amino-acid composition, GC3, CDS length, and the $M$-density baseline controls.  Those values justify keeping the mediation formalism available as a finite statistical route.  They do not rescue the present five-prime boundary audit, because this chapter's row surface reports a negative downstream increment and no sign-stable boundary contribution.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded negative boundary call.  The allowed statement is that, in this Nieuwkoop five-prime audit with $1459$ variants and $200$ block-null resamples, the downstream $B^{\ast}$ window does not add a stable positive improvement after the nested experimental surface is present.  It is not allowed to infer translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbation mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or universality from this audit.  Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is also insufficient here unless the same coordinate has first passed a controlled $Q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout step under amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density baseline, and matched mRNA-abundance controls.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is ready as a boundary result.  The Nieuwkoop five-prime audit should be reported as a nonpositive downstream-window and negligible start-window audit, while the broader Nieuwkoop mRFP protein-output result remains a separate positive perturbation surface.  The distinction matters: the dataset can contain a protein-output association while this particular five-prime boundary decomposition still refuses promotion to a stable downstream $B^{\ast}$ window claim.
