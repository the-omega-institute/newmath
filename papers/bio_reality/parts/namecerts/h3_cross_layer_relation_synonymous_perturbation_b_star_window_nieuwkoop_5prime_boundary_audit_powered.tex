\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous window}
\label{sec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record evaluates the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-variant table on the finite surface whose rows are synonymous coding variants and whose tested coordinates separate a five-prime boundary component from the downstream $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window.  The concrete audit contains $1459$ variants and uses a bounded comparison of nested readout models.  The first model explains only a small part of the protein-output readout, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step1}} &= 0.06748192482378434 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding the declared five-prime boundary layer gives the large finite-row improvement
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{step2}\,|\,\mathrm{step1}} &= 628.6217154500001,\\
R^2_{\mathrm{step2}} &= 0.6060702157093043 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The decisive question is then whether the downstream $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window still contributes after that boundary term is already present.  In the registered run it does not: the third model has
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{step3}} &= 0.6049438075310105,\\
\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{step3}\,|\,\mathrm{step2}} &= -2.082965600280344,\\
\Delta R^2_{B,\mathrm{downstream}} &= -0.001126409133973234 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The block-level statistic for the downstream coordinate gives $p_{\mathrm{block}}=0.1691542288557214$, with a null $95\%$ reference value $0.5254770343527547$, and the sign-stability flag is false.  The downstream sixteen-plus-codon version is likewise negative, with $\Delta \ell=-2.1989730953428164$.  The start-local $B$ term is numerically negligible, with $\Delta \ell=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$ and $\Delta R^2=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence therefore supports a boundary assignment rather than a downstream $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ promotion.  The same Nieuwkoop mRFP perturbation family has a protein-output signal in the broader registered readout: the companion mRFP run reports $1459$ variants, a control-only value
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L2} &= 0.12648386762689323,
\end{aligned}
$$
and a controls-plus-$B_{\perp}$ value
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{L3} &= 0.1798829276988051,
\end{aligned}
$$
so that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R^2 &= 0.05339906007191191,\\
\Delta \mathrm{DL}_{\mathrm{bits}} &= 19.089327473282296,\\
p_{\mathrm{matched}} &= p_{\mathrm{within}} = 0.004975124378109453 .
\end{aligned}
$$
That positive companion result is not erased by the boundary audit.  Rather, the audit says that the portion of the signal tested here is not a stable downstream-window effect once the five-prime boundary layer is admitted.  The correct readback is therefore a finite boundary decomposition: protein-output association is visible in the Nieuwkoop mRFP surface, while this particular downstream $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window fails the post-boundary survival test.

\paragraph{Cross-layer mediation context.}
The broader cross-layer mediation registry gives a separate comparison class.  In the twelve-organism $Q\to T\to P$ table, the joint $9$-coordinate $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ surface is evaluated after amino-acid composition, organismal context, $\mathrm{GC3}$, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline are kept as controls.  The mediation fractions are finite and organism-scoped: for example, yeast has
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.9491551041216973,\\
R^2_{QP,\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,\mathrm{yeast}} &= 0.0151709093608293,
\end{aligned}
$$
whereas \emph{E. coli} K12 has
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.612922252793667,\\
R^2_{QP,\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.10767526677985896,\\
R^2_{QP\mid T,\mathrm{E.coli}} &= 0.04167869969498872 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Those values show that a controlled $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition can be computed on matched codon, modeled-translation, and protein-abundance rows.  They do not license the Nieuwkoop downstream-window term by themselves, because the five-prime audit is a separate perturbation-boundary test with its own finite row set and its own negative downstream increment.

\paragraph{Admissible statement.}
The admissible chapter claim is thus narrow.  On $1459$ Nieuwkoop synonymous mRFP variants, the five-prime boundary term carries the large improvement from step $1$ to step $2$, while adding the downstream $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window after that boundary reduces held explanatory value and has no stable sign.  The result is a refusal of downstream-window promotion for this audit, not a refusal of every codon-mediated protein-output association and not a universal statement about synonymous perturbations.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
Nothing in this chapter establishes translation dynamics, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  A direct $Q_i\to P_k$ association is not enough for protein-realization language unless a same-row $Q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout step survives the declared controls and the downstream protein readout is evaluated through that mediator.  A protein-output association from the companion Nieuwkoop table remains a bounded perturbation readout; it is not a folding assay, not a functional assay, not a proof of ribosome motion, and not an organism-general law.  The five-prime boundary audit keeps the finite evidence at the level actually tested: an mRFP synonymous protein-output surface whose downstream $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ window contribution is nonpositive after the boundary layer is included.
