\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous window}\label{subsec:nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row surface.}  The audit is carried by the finite Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP synonymous-variant panel with $1459$ variants and $200$ block-null replicates.  The biological object is not the full codon table and not a free residual-to-protein assertion.  It is the ordered comparison between a start-proximal explanatory surface, a downstream $B^*_{Q6}$ window surface, and the protein-output readout on the same variant table.  The central question is whether the synonymous residual window keeps a measurable protein-output contribution after the dominant five-prime boundary term has already been admitted.  In symbols, the local audit separates a start term $B_{\mathrm{start}}$ from a downstream term $B_{\mathrm{down}}$ and evaluates the sequence of fitted readouts
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{1} &= 0.06748192482378434,\\
R^2_{2} &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
R^2_{3} &= 0.6049438075310105 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The large jump from the first to the second surface is finite-row evidence that the start-proximal boundary absorbs the protein-output structure in this panel: the log-likelihood gain from step 1 to step 2 is $628.6217154500001$.  The next term, the downstream $B^*_{Q6}$ window after that start boundary is present, does not continue the improvement.  Its fitted increment is negative: $\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{down}}=-2.082967365015851$ and $\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{down}}=-0.001126409133973234$.

\paragraph{Boundary statistic.}  The audit is therefore a localization test rather than a new protein-realization claim.  The start coordinate itself has only a numerically tiny isolated increment in the registered contrast, $\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{start}}=3.26370933437133\times 10^{-6}$ and $\Delta R^2_{\mathrm{start}}=1.7624020537495221\times 10^{-9}$, but the staged fit shows that adding the five-prime boundary block accounts for the major likelihood gain before the downstream synonymous-window term is tested.  Once this boundary is in place, the downstream block is below the block-null reference: its null $95\%$ level is $0.5254770343527547$, whereas the observed downstream log-likelihood increment is negative.  The block-null tail probability is $p=0.1691542288557214$, and the sign-stability flag is false.  A stricter downstream window beginning at codon $16$ gives the same direction, $\Delta \ell_{\mathrm{down},16+}=-2.1989730953428164$.  Thus the registered finite-row verdict is not that $B^*_{Q6}$ creates a transferable downstream protein-output law in this assay.  The local verdict is narrower: the Nieuwkoop synonymous protein-output signal is concentrated at the five-prime boundary, and the residual downstream $B^*_{Q6}$ window is not supported after that boundary is controlled.

\paragraph{Relation to the paired protein-output result.}  The same Nieuwkoop mRFP variant universe also has a positive protein-output registration for the broader $B^*_{Q6}$ window design: $n=1459$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matched}}=0.004975124378109453$, and $p_{\mathrm{within}}=0.004975124378109453$.  The five-prime audit does not erase that finite protein-output association; it locates its effective source.  The broader registration says that a synonymous residual window improves the mRFP protein-output model under its declared controls.  The boundary audit says that the improvement should be read as start-proximal in this construct, not as a downstream, position-free $B^*_{Q6}$ propagation rule.  This distinction matters because the finite row set is a perturbational reporter design, not a multi-organism translation-to-abundance mediation matrix.

\paragraph{Cross-layer mediation context.}  A separate finite mediation matrix over organism-matched coding sequences and protein abundance gives a bounded comparison point for what a genuine $Q\to T\to P$ surface looks like.  In that matrix, $B^*_{Q6}$ has nine residual coordinates, and the readout $T$ is per-protein modeled tAI while $P$ is log$_{10}$ PAXdb abundance.  For \emph{E. coli} K12 MG1655, $3739$ joined proteins give total $R^2_{QP}=0.10767526677985896$, direct $R^2_{QP\mid T}=0.04167869969498872$, indirect difference $0.06599656708487024$, and mediated fraction $0.612922252793667$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $5815$ joined proteins give mediated fraction $0.9491551041216973$.  These values show how the paper treats a mediated readout when the translation coordinate is explicitly present.  The Nieuwkoop five-prime audit is different: it is a synonymous perturbation boundary test inside one reporter construct.  Its negative downstream increment means that the construct should not be used to bypass the translation-readout step or to infer a general residual-to-protein mechanism.

\paragraph{Local claim and exclusions.}  The finite conclusion is therefore the following.  On the registered Nieuwkoop mRFP panel, the five-prime boundary is the active explanatory location for the protein-output association, while the downstream $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous window has $\Delta R^2<0$, $\Delta \ell<0$, non-significant block-null behavior, and unstable sign after the boundary is admitted.  The result supports a boundary audit of where the reporter signal sits; it does not support a downstream protein-realization claim.  No translation realization, ribosome mechanism, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological principle follows from this chapter.  Any such assertion would require a separate layer-matched contact: for example measured ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency for translation, a matched protein-state assay for downstream protein realization, a structure or physical assay for folding or admissibility, and an independent functional or phenotype assay for higher biological readout.
