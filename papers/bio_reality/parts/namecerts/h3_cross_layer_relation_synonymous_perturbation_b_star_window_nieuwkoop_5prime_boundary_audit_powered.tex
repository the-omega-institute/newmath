\subsection{Nieuwkoop five-prime boundary audit for the B-star window}
\label{subsec:bioreality-nieuwkoop-five-prime-boundary-audit}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns the synonymous perturbation panel for the Nieuwkoop \emph{E. coli} mRFP reporter, restricted to the question of whether a $B^\ast$ window effect is genuinely carried by the coding residual window or is absorbed by a five-prime boundary description.  The finite carrier is the set of $1459$ synonymous variants equipped with the measured protein-output response, boundary/start descriptors, and a downstream $B^\ast$ window coordinate.  The comparison is deliberately local: it does not ask whether the whole reporter panel contains a protein-output signal, but whether the downstream $B^\ast$ coordinate retains a sign-stable contribution after the five-prime boundary terms have already been admitted.

\paragraph{Nested finite regressions.}
Let $Y$ denote the measured reporter output, let $C$ denote the base control block, let $G$ denote the five-prime/start boundary block, and let $B_{\mathrm{down}}$ denote the downstream $B^\ast$ window coordinate used in this audit.  The registered rows give
$$
\begin{aligned}
 n &= 1459,\\
 R^2(C) &= 0.06748192482378434,\\
 R^2(C,G) &= 0.6060702157093043,\\
 R^2(C,G,B_{\mathrm{down}}) &= 0.6049438075310105.
\end{aligned}
$$
The boundary block is therefore the dominant finite improvement in this local decomposition: the transition from the first to the second block has
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta \ell(C \to C,G) &= 628.6217154500001.
\end{aligned}
$$
By contrast, the downstream $B^\ast$ coordinate does not improve the fitted surface after the boundary block is present.  The registered downstream increment is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta \ell(C,G \to C,G,B_{\mathrm{down}}) &= -2.082965600280344,\\
 \Delta R^2(C,G \to C,G,B_{\mathrm{down}}) &= -0.001126409133973234.
\end{aligned}
$$
The separately reported downstream audit agrees with this sign: $b_{\mathrm{downstream}}$ has $\Delta \ell=-2.082967365015851$, $\Delta R^2=-0.001126409133973234$, block $p=0.1691542288557214$, and a block-null $95\%$ reference value $0.5254770343527547$.  The downstream-sixteen-plus check is also negative, with $\Delta \ell=-2.1989730953428164$.  The start-specific $B^\ast$ increment is effectively zero at this scale, with $\Delta \ell=0.00000326370933437133$ and $\Delta R^2=0.0000000017624020537495221$.  The sign-stability flag is false.

\paragraph{Relation to the protein-output panel.}
The same Nieuwkoop mRFP perturbation family has a broader protein-output result in which a $B^\ast$-orthogonal component improved the held surface: the companion finite run used $1459$ variants, had base $R^2=0.12648386762689323$, full $R^2=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched and within-null $p$-values both equal to $0.004975124378109453$.  The present chapter does not erase that finite protein-output contact.  It refines its location: the five-prime boundary audit says that this particular downstream-window reading is not allowed to be reported as an independent boundary-stable residual once the start/boundary block is already in the model.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite verdict is a boundary refusal, not a biological null for all synonymous effects.  The admissible statement is that the five-prime/start block explains the local reporter surface much more strongly than the downstream $B^\ast$ window coordinate, and the downstream coordinate has no positive, sign-stable increment after that block is included.  In symbols, this row supports
$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta \ell(B_{\mathrm{down}}\mid C,G) &< 0,\\
 \Delta R^2(B_{\mathrm{down}}\mid C,G) &< 0,\\
 \mathrm{sign\_stable}(B_{\mathrm{down}}\mid C,G) &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the claim is retained only as an audited five-prime boundary result: a positive downstream-window protein-output interpretation is blocked for this slice, while the larger reporter-panel contact remains a separate perturbation fact.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome occupancy, folding effect, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, selection law, or universal biological rule follows from this boundary audit.  The rows contain a reporter-output regression and its finite controls; they do not contain a separate ribosome-profiling contact, folding assay, physical assay, functional assay, or population-level law.  Any future promotion beyond this local protein-output boundary would require a distinct layer-matched measurement and a new controlled statistic on the same relevant rows.
