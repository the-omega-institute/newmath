\subsection{A translation-gated boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual compressibility}\label{subsec:bior reality-q6-minimal-compilation-law}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates are first residualized against amino-acid composition before any cross-layer readout is interpreted.  The tested coordinate list has nine entries, namely $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The admissible readback has the ordered form
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow P_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is a residual codon-coordinate entry, $T_j$ is a measured or explicitly bounded translation readout, and $P_k$ is a downstream protein-state, stability, degradation, or abundance surface only after the translation step has survived the declared controls.  The control set includes CDS length, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, organism identity when pooled, an $M$-density baseline on the support of the nine projected coordinates, and matched mRNA controls when the readout requires a transcription denominator.

\paragraph{Minimal-compilation test.}
The powered finite-row experiment defines the residual source $P_Q$ and then removes the contribution of a low-dimensional dictionary $D_o$ by a fixed readout order.  The descriptive compression score is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Comp}(P_Q,D_o) = 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $D_o$ is not a mechanism; it is only the measured dictionary allowed to absorb residual energy.  The finite result is negative for the proposed minimal-compilation law.  Across the four computed organisms, the median compressibility is $0.026$, so the measured low-dimensional dictionary does not highly compress $P_Q$.  The organism-level values are: for \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, $D_o=(\mathrm{measured\_te})$, $\operatorname{DL}_{D_o}=1$, raw baseline dimension $17$, residual fraction $0.9756319042010854$, compressibility $0.024368095798914635$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$; for \textit{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $D_o=(\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability})$, $\operatorname{DL}_{D_o}=2$, raw baseline dimension $2$, residual fraction $0.9942823785118229$, compressibility $0.005717621488177116$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$; for \textit{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $D_o=(\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density})$, with the selected higher dictionary entry $\mathrm{ptm\_density}$, $\operatorname{DL}_{D_o}=3$, raw baseline dimension $19$, residual fraction $0.9736442326306781$, compressibility $0.02635576736932188$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$; for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $D_o=(\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability})$, $\operatorname{DL}_{D_o}=2$, raw baseline dimension $19$, residual fraction $0.8676586345294415$, compressibility $0.13234136547055853$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Rank and readout diagnostics.}
The structural-order companion panel gives a useful check that the coordinate system is not rank-empty.  In the $B^*_{Q6}$ structural-order run, both organisms had powered rank sufficiency for all nine coordinates.  For \textit{E. coli}, the design used $24$ control columns, rank $Z=23$, rank $\widetilde X=9$, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, explained energy $4178.935925251874$, and response residual energy $136607.52873622958$.  The codon-to-structure-confidence join used $3736$ matched proteins from $3739$ codon-joined rows, with hit rate $0.999198$, and the controlled predicted-structure-confidence association had $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ with all nine coordinates surviving the positive threshold.  For \textit{S. cerevisiae}, the corresponding diagnostics were $24$ control columns, rank $Z=23$, rank $\widetilde X=9$, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, explained energy $44421.14342043593$, response residual energy $620506.1490869389$, $5804$ matched proteins from $5815$ codon-joined rows, hit rate $0.998108$, and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$.  This check supports finite estimability of the coordinate matrix, but it remains a predicted structure-confidence readout and not an experimental folding or function assay.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned downstream boundary.}
A separate stability-boundary experiment tested whether $B^*_{Q6}$ residuals can be read at a downstream stability or turnover surface after conditioning on translation and controls.  The unconditioned $B^*_{Q6}$-to-stability predictive summary over three joined organisms was positive but small: minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, maximum $0.03602995605625481$, with $3$ positive and $0$ negative entries.  The translation-conditioned full-control increment was not positive in aggregate: minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, with $2$ negative and $1$ positive entries, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$.  The organism calls were two partial cases and one mediated-blocked null.  The joined stability surfaces were $2517$ \textit{E. coli} rows with mRNA half-life as the primary readout, $6697$ human protein-turnover rows plus $11322$ human mRNA-half-life rows, and $3537$ yeast protein-turnover rows plus $4759$ yeast mRNA-half-life rows.  Thus the downstream result is only partial and does not convert a direct codon-coordinate association into a stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
The translation-conditioned structural-order boundary is stronger as a finite association but remains layer-limited.  The two-organism controlled increment was positive in both cases, with full-control $\Delta R^2$ minimum $0.002688284523492457$, median and mean $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.  The permuted comparison was negative in both entries, with minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, median and mean $-0.00212186632044159$, and maximum $-0.000779938262202795$.  The matched row counts were $3412$ for \textit{E. coli} K12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{S. cerevisiae}.  This places $B^*_{Q6}$ on a translation-conditioned structural-order boundary for the supplied finite panel, but the readout is still a bounded structure-confidence or order surface, not a proof of folded molecular state, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Local verdict and claim boundary.}
The minimal-compilation law is not supported by the supplied finite rows.  The residual source $P_Q$ retains most of its energy after projection through the measured $D_o$ dictionary, with residual fractions from $0.8676586345294415$ to $0.9942823785118229$.  The correct interpretation is therefore irreducibility relative to these measured readouts and this ordered descriptive test, not irreducibility in nature and not a causal law.  The admissible claim is narrower: $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be carried into downstream protein-state questions only through a same-row translation gate and then through translation-conditioned residual association.  No translation mechanism, folding pathway, protein-stability mechanism, degradation law, physical admissibility statement, biological function, phenotype, or universal biological rule follows from these coordinates, from modeled adaptation weights, from mRNA controls, from protein abundance contacts, or from predicted structural-order scores unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that readout and the corresponding controlled finite statistic is computed.
