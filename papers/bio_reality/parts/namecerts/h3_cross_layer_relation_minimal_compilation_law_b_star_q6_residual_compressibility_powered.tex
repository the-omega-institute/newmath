\subsection{Minimal Compilation Test for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ Residual Coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-minimal-compilation-law}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record tests whether the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual source can be compressed into a small measured downstream dictionary after amino-acid quotient residualization. The carrier is a finite organism-by-CDS panel containing $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, matched translation readouts where available, matched mRNA controls, and downstream protein-state surfaces only when the corresponding rows exist. The coordinate family used by the linked translation-gated structural-order computation has nine entries,
$K\_{AAA}$, $Arg\_{AGR}$, $Ile\_{AUA}$, $Leu\_{CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $Leu\_{UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $Ser\_{UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $Ser\_{UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $Thr\_{ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f\_3\_stress$. The admissible interpretation is rowwise and conditional: a coordinate $q_i$ is first read against a translation surface $T_j$ under length, amino-acid composition, organism, $GC3$, $M$-density, and mRNA controls; only then may a downstream protein-stability or protein-state association be described, and even then only as a translation-conditioned residual association.

\paragraph{Test statistic.}
Let $P_Q$ denote the projected residual source and let $R_{\mathrm{final}}$ be the residual after projecting through the selected measured dictionary $D_o$. The reported compression score is
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_o &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The quantity $DL(D_o)$ is the summed dimension of the retained measured readouts, while the raw baseline description length is the summed dimension of all available candidate readouts before scored selection. This is a descriptive variance-partition statistic. It is not a causal compilation map, and the readout order $T \to S \to H$ fixes an attribution convention rather than a biological law.

\paragraph{Per-organism finite rows.}
Four computed organisms entered the compilation audit. For \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$ rows were available, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, the selected dictionary was $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te}\}$, $DL(D_o)=1$, the raw baseline dimension was $17$, the final residual fraction was $0.9756319042010854$, the compression score was $0.024368095798914635$, and the permutation value was $p=0.000999000999000999$. For \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, $DL(D_o)=2$, the raw baseline dimension was $2$, the residual fraction was $0.9942823785118229$, the compression score was $0.005717621488177116$, and $p=0.000999000999000999$. For \textit{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$ with $\mathrm{ptm\_density}$ also selected in the scored $H$ dictionary, $DL(D_o)=3$, the raw baseline dimension was $19$, the residual fraction was $0.9736442326306781$, the compression score was $0.02635576736932188$, and $p=0.000999000999000999$. For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, $DL(D_o)=2$, the raw baseline dimension was $19$, the residual fraction was $0.8676586345294415$, the compression score was $0.13234136547055853$, and $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism reading.}
The finite rows do not support a minimal compilation law. The median compression is $0.026$, so the observed low-dimensional measured dictionary absorbs only a small part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual source in this descriptive test. Equivalently, the residual source remains largely uncompressed by the specific measured readouts used here: the final residual fractions are $0.9756319042010854$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9736442326306781$, and $0.8676586345294415$ across the four organisms. The strongest compression in this panel occurs in \textit{S. cerevisiae}, where $C_o=0.13234136547055853$; even there the remaining residual fraction is $0.8676586345294415$, which is too large to justify saying that the measured dictionary minimally compiles the source. The weakest case is \textit{E. coli}, where $C_o=0.005717621488177116$ despite a two-dimensional selected dictionary matching the raw candidate dimension.

\paragraph{Relation to downstream boundary checks.}
The linked protein-stability boundary remains only partial. Across three joined stability surfaces, the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ stability association had positive entries with mean $0.02753157569086806$, median $0.027727034742559664$, minimum $0.018837736273789707$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$, but the translation-conditioned full-control delta $R^2$ had mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip value $0.946441650390625$. The joined stability rows were $2517$ \textit{E. coli} mRNA-half-life rows, $6697$ human protein-turnover rows with $11322$ human mRNA-half-life rows, and $3537$ yeast protein-turnover rows with $4759$ yeast mRNA-half-life rows. Thus the stability surface does not rescue the minimal-compilation claim.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
A separate structural-order boundary has stronger positive finite-row behavior, but it does not convert the minimal-compilation result into a law. The structural-order boundary reported two positive full-control entries with aggregate delta $R^2$ mean $0.005284160876443678$, median $0.005284160876443678$, minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip value $0.0029296875$. Its joined row counts were $3412$ for \textit{E. coli} and $5119$ for yeast. In the translation-gated structural-order computation, the AlphaFold mean-pLDDT readout was explicitly interpreted only as predicted structure confidence. It used $3736$ matched \textit{E. coli} proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined rows, with hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched yeast proteins out of $5815$ codon-joined rows, with hit rate $0.998108$. The reported structural-order $R^2$ values were $0.03059081709413562$ for \textit{E. coli} and $0.0715885627979685$ for yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving on that predicted-confidence surface. Those values show that a downstream boundary can contain finite associations while the low-dimensional compilation law still fails for the broader $P_Q$ source.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The result is therefore a negative compilation statement, not a negative biology statement. Low compression means that these measured readouts do not explain most of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual energy on these rows; it does not prove that no other readout, perturbation, assay, or mechanistic model could explain the source. Conversely, neither a nonzero translation readout nor a downstream association licenses promotion to translation mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causation, or universality. Each such layer requires its own matched contact and its own controlled finite-row statistic. In this chapter the accepted claim is narrower: the measured dictionary $D_o$ does not minimally compile $P_Q$ across the computed organisms, and any downstream protein-stability or structural-order language remains conditional on separately joined rows and controls.
