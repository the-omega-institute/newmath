\subsection{Bstar-Q6 Residual Compressibility after Translation-Conditioned Readout}
\label{sec:bioreality-bstar-q6-residual-compressibility}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Claim scope.}
This record tests whether the residual $B^*_{Q6}$ source can be compressed by a small panel of measured downstream readouts after amino-acid quotienting and ordinary sequence controls.  The finite carrier is an organism-by-coding-sequence panel with $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, measured translation-efficiency readouts where available, matched mRNA controls, and downstream protein-state surfaces where available.  The tested object is not a direct codon-to-protein-stability mechanism.  It is the narrower question of whether the projected residual source $P_Q$ loses most of its squared energy after projection through an ordered readout dictionary.

\paragraph{Residual and compression statistic.}
For each organism, the source vector is the projected residual panel $P_Q = \Pi_{Q_e} P_e$.  A finite readout dictionary $D_o$ is then built from measured readouts in the order used by the run: translation readout first, stability-side readouts where present, and a scored higher dictionary where it is admitted by the finite data.  The statistic reported here is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{compressibility} &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2},\\
R_{\mathrm{final}}\text{-fraction} &= \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus a value near $1$ would mean that the measured dictionary removes most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual energy, while a value near $0$ means that the measured dictionary leaves the source largely intact.  The description-length counter $DL_{D_o}$ is the summed dimensionality of the retained $D_o$ readouts; the raw baseline counter is the summed dimensionality of every available candidate readout before scored selection.

\paragraph{Finite organism panel.}
The run contains four computed organisms.  In \textit{Danio rerio}, the joined panel has $n=13140$ rows and $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$.  The retained dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te}\}$, with $DL_{D_o}=1$ against raw baseline $17$.  The final residual fraction is $0.9756319042010854$, so the compressibility is only $0.024368095798914635$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  In \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, the panel has $n=2517$ rows and $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$.  The retained dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, with $DL_{D_o}=2$ and raw baseline $2$.  The final residual fraction is $0.9942823785118229$, giving compressibility $0.005717621488177116$, again with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  In \textit{Homo sapiens}, the panel has $n=11250$ rows and $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$.  The retained dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$, with $\mathrm{ptm\_density}$ also selected in the scored higher dictionary.  Here $DL_{D_o}=3$, the raw baseline is $19$, the final residual fraction is $0.9736442326306781$, and the compressibility is $0.02635576736932188$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the panel has $n=4759$ rows and $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$.  The retained dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, with $DL_{D_o}=2$ against raw baseline $19$.  The final residual fraction is $0.8676586345294415$, so the compressibility is $0.13234136547055853$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism verdict.}
The median compressibility across the four computed organisms is reported as $0.026$.  This is a negative result for the minimal-compilation reading: $P_Q$ is not highly compressed by the measured low-dimensional $D_o$ set.  The finite result says that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source remains mostly unreduced after the measured readout projections in this descriptive panel.  The strongest organism in the set, \textit{S. cerevisiae}, still leaves $86.76586345294415\%$ of the source energy in the final residual.  The weakest, \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, removes less than $1\%$ of the source energy.  The run therefore supports irreducibility relative to these measured readouts, not a universal impossibility theorem about future readouts.

\paragraph{Rank and control boundary.}
The linked structural-order run supplies a useful rank check for the $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates.  The residual basis has $9$ coordinates: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  In the structural-order panel, both organisms have full residual coordinate rank after controls: $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$ for $9$ coordinates.  The control system contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an M-density baseline.  For \textit{E. coli}, the control matrix has $24$ columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, explained energy $4178.935925251874$, and readout residual energy $136607.52873622958$.  For \textit{S. cerevisiae}, the same $24$-column control design has $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, explained energy $44421.14342043593$, and readout residual energy $620506.1490869389$.  The rank diagnostic is therefore adequate for finite association testing, but rank adequacy is not biological interpretation.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned downstream checks.}
The same claim family contains downstream tests that must remain logically downstream of translation readout survival.  For protein stability or degradation-side readouts, the joined data include \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 with $2517$ mRNA half-life rows, \textit{H. sapiens} with $11322$ mRNA half-life rows and $6697$ protein-turnover rows, and \textit{S. cerevisiae} with $4759$ mRNA half-life rows and $3537$ protein-turnover rows.  The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ prediction summary for stability has $n=3$, minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$.  After full translation conditioning and controls, the aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ has minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, and maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, with two negative organism calls and one positive organism call.  The sign-flip probability for being greater than zero is $0.946441650390625$, and the weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ is $-0.001572140012981015$.  The finite call is therefore partial, with one mediated-blocked null and two partial organism calls, not a stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
The structural-order boundary behaves differently and helps localize the negative minimal-compilation result.  The structural-order joined panels have $3412$ rows for \textit{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ rows for \textit{S. cerevisiae}.  The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ prediction summary for order has $n=2$, minimum $0.03664544696481631$, median and mean $0.09855549686555976$, and maximum $0.1604655467663032$.  The translation-conditioned order test has full-control $\Delta R^2$ values between $0.002688284523492457$ and $0.007880037229394898$, with mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, two positive organism calls, sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$, and weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$.  The separate structural-order survival run also reports AlphaFold mean pLDDT only as predicted structure-confidence readout: $3736$ matched proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined rows for \textit{E. coli}, hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched proteins out of $5815$ codon-joined rows for \textit{S. cerevisiae}, hit rate $0.998108$.  The corresponding $R^2_{SO}$ values are $0.03059081709413562$ and $0.0715885627979685$, each with $9$ surviving coordinates.  These numbers show a finite association with a prediction-confidence surface, not experimental folded structure or function.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The minimal-compilation law is not supported in this finite panel.  The measured dictionary $D_o$ removes only a small fraction of $P_Q$ in the cross-organism summary, despite low permutation values under the computed selection procedure.  The correct reading is descriptive variance partitioning: measured TE, mRNA-stability, and selected PTM-density readouts do not absorb most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source.  This does not imply that $B^*_{Q6}$ has no biological contact, and it does not imply that all possible readout dictionaries would fail.  It only says that the available finite dictionary is insufficient to compile away the residual source.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
No claim of translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality follows from these numbers alone.  A translation-efficiency row can be used only as a measured translation-side readout under its own controls.  A protein-turnover, stability, localization, or structural-order row can be interpreted only after a surviving same-coordinate translation step has been established and the downstream association is recomputed under translation conditioning.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights, contact names, workflow readiness, rank sufficiency, codon-window geometry, bulk abundance, and prediction-confidence surfaces do not by themselves supply ribosome dynamics, degradation mechanism, folded structure, biological function, phenotype, physical admissibility, or a general law.
