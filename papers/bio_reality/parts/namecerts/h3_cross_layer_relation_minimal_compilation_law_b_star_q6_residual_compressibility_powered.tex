\subsection{Residual non-compilation of $B^{*}_{Q6}$ through measured downstream readouts}
\label{sec:q6-minimal-compilation-residual-compressibility}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The test starts from a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates have already been residualized against first amino-acid quotient structure. The finite source object is the projected residual energy $P_Q = \Pi_{Q_e}P_e$, and the tested downstream dictionary $D_o$ consists only of measured readouts available for the organism: measured translation efficiency where present, mRNA stability where present, and a scored higher-layer entry only when the finite row surface supports it. The control boundary is part of the object being tested: amino-acid composition, organism identity, $GC3$, coding length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA controls are not optional interpretation aids but the finite coordinates with respect to which the residual is read.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
For each organism the recorded statistic is the fraction of the $P_Q$ energy not absorbed after the selected measured readouts are projected out. The reported compressibility is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{comp}(Q;D_o) &= 1-\frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2},\\
R_{\mathrm{final}}\text{ fraction} &= \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus a large value would mean that the chosen measured dictionary captures much of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual source, while a value near zero means that the residual source remains mostly outside this measured dictionary. This is a variance-partition statement on finite joined rows, not a claim that the dictionary gives a causal explanation.

\paragraph{Per-organism rows.}
The computed organisms all have explicit $P_Q$ energies and row counts. For \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$ rows give $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te}\}$ with $DL(D_o)=1$ against a raw baseline description length of $17$. The final residual fraction is $0.9756319042010854$, so the compressibility is $0.024368095798914635$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. For \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$ rows give $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$ with $DL(D_o)=2$ and raw baseline description length $2$. The final residual fraction is $0.9942823785118229$, giving compressibility $0.005717621488177116$ with the same permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. For \textit{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$ rows give $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$, with $\mathrm{ptm\_density}$ also the selected higher-layer entry. Here $DL(D_o)=3$, the raw baseline description length is $19$, the final residual fraction is $0.9736442326306781$, and the compressibility is $0.02635576736932188$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$ rows give $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$ with $DL(D_o)=2$ against raw baseline description length $19$. Its final residual fraction is $0.8676586345294415$, giving the largest organism-level compressibility, $0.13234136547055853$, again with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism verdict.}
The finite computation does not support the proposed minimal compilation law. The median compressibility across the computed organisms is $0.026$, so the measured low-dimensional dictionary does not highly compress $P_Q$. Equivalently, the residual fractions remain large: approximately $0.976$, $0.994$, $0.974$, and $0.868$ for zebrafish, \textit{E. coli}, human, and budding yeast respectively. The negative result is informative in a narrow sense: $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual variation is not reducible to these measured readouts in this descriptive panel. It is not evidence that no biological explanation exists, since the tested dictionary is finite, platform-specific, and ordered by a modeling choice.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and downstream surfaces.}
The same coordinate family has finite contacts at nearby boundaries, but those contacts do not reverse the minimal-compilation verdict. In the rank-powered structural-order run, the nine residual coordinates were $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The structural-order readout used AlphaFold mean pLDDT only as predicted structure confidence. It joined $3736$ of $3739$ \textit{E. coli} proteins, hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ of $5815$ yeast proteins, hit rate $0.998108$. The controlled rank diagnostics were nondegenerate: for \textit{E. coli}, $rank(X_{\sim})=9$, $rank(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, explained energy $4178.935925251874$, and residual readout energy $136607.52873622958$; for yeast, $rank(X_{\sim})=9$, $rank(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, explained energy $44421.14342043593$, and residual readout energy $620506.1490869389$. The corresponding $R^2_{SO}$ values were $0.03059081709413562$ for \textit{E. coli} and $0.0715885627979685$ for yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving on that predicted-confidence surface. A later translation-conditioned structural-order boundary was positive in both organisms, with full-control $\Delta R^2$ values between $0.002688284523492457$ and $0.007880037229394898$, weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$.

\paragraph{Stability boundary.}
The protein-stability boundary remains much weaker than the structural-order boundary. Three joined stability surfaces were recorded: \textit{E. coli} used mRNA half-life with $2517$ rows as the primary readout, human used protein turnover with $6697$ turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA half-life rows available, and yeast used protein turnover with $3537$ turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA half-life rows available. The unconditioned $B^{*}_{Q6}$ stability-prediction summary was positive but small, with values from $0.018837736273789707$ to $0.03602995605625481$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and median $0.027727034742559664$. After full controls, however, the aggregate translation-conditioned stability $\Delta R^2$ ranged from $-0.004953854025603666$ to $0.0006378518321674687$, with mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$. The organism calls were two partial calls and one mediated-blocked null. This supports only a guarded downstream boundary, not a direct stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
The conclusion is deliberately local. The minimal-compilation test says that the observed $D_o$ readouts do not compress $P_Q$ strongly in the finite panels. It does not by itself establish translation realization, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Translation claims require a same-row translation readout that survives the declared controls. Stability or degradation claims require a separate stability, turnover, pulse-chase, or degradation-rate contact and must be reported only after the translation step is conditioned on. Structural-order statements here are restricted to the stated predicted-confidence readout and its finite controls. Protein abundance, folding quality, function, and organism-level phenotype remain outside the claim unless their own layer-matched measurements are attached and evaluated on joined rows.
