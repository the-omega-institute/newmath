\subsection{B exorpdfstring{$^{\ast}_{Q6}$}{*Q6} residual compressibility is not a minimal compilation law}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-residual-compressibility-minimal-compilation-law}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The tested object is a finite organism-by-CDS panel in which the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are first quotiented against amino-acid composition and then compared with measured readout coordinates.  The residual source is denoted $P_Q$, and the tested compression statistic is the fractional energy removed after projecting through the available readout dictionary.  In the computed run, the readout dictionary is not a single biological layer: it is an ordered descriptive absorber using measured translation efficiency where available, adding mRNA stability where available, and allowing a scored downstream dictionary entry only when the finite rows support it.  The reported statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{compressibility} &= 1 - \frac{\|R_{\mathrm{final}}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\|R_{\mathrm{final}}\|^2/\|P_Q\|^2$ is the residual energy fraction left after the selected readouts have been fitted.  The dimensional bookkeeping is also finite: $DL_{D_o}$ is the summed dimension of kept readouts, while the raw baseline dimension is the summed dimension of all available candidate readouts before scored selection.

\paragraph{Organism-level readout table.}
The four computed organisms give a direct check against the proposed minimal compilation law.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, the joined panel has $n=13140$ rows and $P_Q$ energy $605.0092168669606$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te}\}$ with $DL_{D_o}=1$ against raw baseline dimension $17$.  Its residual fraction is $0.9756319042010854$, giving compressibility $0.024368095798914635$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K--12 MG1655, the panel has $n=2517$ rows and $P_Q$ energy $225.84556319953032$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$ with $DL_{D_o}=2$ against raw baseline dimension $2$.  Its residual fraction is $0.9942823785118229$, compressibility $0.005717621488177116$, and the same permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, the panel has $n=11250$ rows and $P_Q$ energy $286.3673197177688$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$, with $\mathrm{ptm\_density}$ also selected in the downstream scored dictionary, $DL_{D_o}=3$, and raw baseline dimension $19$.  Its residual fraction is $0.9736442326306781$, compressibility $0.02635576736932188$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, the panel has $n=4759$ rows and $P_Q$ energy $229.86450794006544$; the selected dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$ with $DL_{D_o}=2$ against raw baseline dimension $19$.  Its residual fraction is $0.8676586345294415$, compressibility $0.13234136547055853$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Compression verdict.}
The numbers do not support a minimal compilation law.  The decisive observation is not that every organism has exactly the same residual behavior, but that the selected low-dimensional readouts leave most of $P_Q$ in the residual.  The cross-organism median compressibility is reported as $0.026$, while the residual fractions remain close to one in three of the four organisms: $0.9756319042010854$ in zebrafish, $0.9942823785118229$ in \emph{E. coli}, and $0.9736442326306781$ in human.  Yeast is the strongest case in the table, but even there the selected readouts remove only $0.13234136547055853$ of the $P_Q$ energy.  Thus the finite panel says that the measured readout dictionary absorbs a statistically detectable but small part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual source.  It does not say that the residual source has been compiled into the measured translation, mRNA-stability, or scored downstream readouts.

\paragraph{Translation and downstream gates.}
The broader record also keeps the downstream interpretation narrow.  A separate translation-conditioned stability run is marked as passed only in the sense that the finite statistic was computed, and its final call is partial rather than a positive stability mechanism.  Across three organisms, the full-control stability delta-$R^2$ summary has mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, two negative entries, one positive entry, and weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$; its sign-flip probability for being greater than zero is $0.946441650390625$.  The same record reports that raw $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ prediction of the stability readout has median $0.027727034742559664$ over three organisms, but that value is not promoted past the translation-conditioned control gate.  The organism calls are two partial cases and one mediated-blocked null case.

\paragraph{Structural-order contrast.}
The structural-order side gives a useful contrast without changing the minimal-compilation verdict.  In the translation-gated structural-order panel, the nine residual coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  The \emph{E. coli} structural-order join uses $3736$ matched proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined entries, with hit rate $0.999198$, rank $9$ for the residualized coordinate matrix, rank $23$ for the controls, and residual degrees of freedom $3713$.  Its structural-order $R^2$ is $0.03059081709413562$ with all nine coordinates surviving as finite associations.  The yeast join uses $5804$ matched proteins out of $5815$ codon-joined entries, with hit rate $0.998108$, rank $9$ for the residualized coordinate matrix, rank $23$ for the controls, and residual degrees of freedom $5781$.  Its structural-order $R^2$ is $0.0715885627979685$, again with nine surviving coordinates.  A separate translation-conditioned structural-order boundary reports positive full-control delta-$R^2$ in both organisms, with mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$.  These values support a bounded structural-order readout association in that specific finite panel, but they do not rescue the stronger claim that $P_Q$ is generally compressed by the measured low-dimensional dictionary.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The appropriate reading is therefore negative for the minimal compilation law and positive only for the existence of finite, layer-scoped associations in some adjacent tests.  The tested readouts can be used as residual absorbers in a variance partition, but the absorber role is not a biological mechanism.  The order in which translation, stability-related, and scored downstream readouts are fitted is a modeling order, so a different order could redistribute attribution.  The organism panels also come from different finite datasets and platforms, so the result is descriptive at the organism level rather than universal.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation mechanism, protein folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, molecular-function claim, phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from these numbers alone.  A $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate may be carried into a downstream protein-stability or degradation-rate question only after a same-row translation readout survives the declared controls, and the downstream statistic must then be reported as a translation-conditioned residual association.  Modeled tAI or stAI coupling is not survival evidence for protein stability.  Bulk proteomics abundance is not a stability assay, a degradation-rate assay, or a folding readout.  The present finite-row result says that the proposed minimal compilation law is not supported by the measured dictionary, while leaving open the narrower possibility that particular $q_i \to T_j$ and translation-conditioned downstream entries may survive in separately matched panels.
