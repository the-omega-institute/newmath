\subsection{Residual compressibility of $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ after translation-layer contact}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-minimal-compilation-residual-compressibility}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record tests whether amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ become substantially absorbed by a small measured downstream readout dictionary.  The panel consists of coding-sequence rows equipped with residual $Q6$ coordinates, organism labels, amino-acid composition, $GC3$, coding length, an $M$-density baseline, measured translation efficiency where available, matched mRNA stability or abundance controls where available, and a small set of downstream readouts.  The tested source vector is denoted $P_Q$, and the residual after the selected readout dictionary is denoted $R_{\mathrm{final}}$.  The reported compression statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{compressibility} &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A value near $1$ would mean that the measured readout dictionary absorbs most of the residual source energy.  The observed values are near $0$ for three of the four computed organisms, so the tested minimal-compilation claim is not supported by this finite panel.

\paragraph{Per-organism computation.}
For \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$ coding-sequence rows were joined, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, the selected dictionary had $DL_{D_o}=1$ against a raw candidate baseline dimension of $17$, and the final residual fraction was $0.9756319042010854$.  The resulting compressibility was $0.024368095798914635$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $DL_{D_o}=2$, raw baseline dimension $2$, final residual fraction $0.9942823785118229$, and compressibility $0.005717621488177116$, again with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $DL_{D_o}=3$, raw baseline dimension $19$, final residual fraction $0.9736442326306781$, and compressibility $0.02635576736932188$, with the same permutation value.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $DL_{D_o}=2$, raw baseline dimension $19$, final residual fraction $0.8676586345294415$, and compressibility $0.13234136547055853$, also with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Selected readout dictionary.}
The selected readout dictionary was deliberately small.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, only measured translation efficiency was retained.  In \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 and \emph{S. cerevisiae}, measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability were retained.  In \emph{H. sapiens}, measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density were retained, with PTM density the only selected $H$-dictionary component.  Thus the descriptive law being tested is not whether every possible biological readout can eventually explain the residual source, but whether the measured low-dimensional $D_o$ surface in hand substantially compresses $P_Q$.  It does not: the cross-organism median compressibility is $0.026$, and three organisms leave more than $97\%$ of the $P_Q$ energy in $R_{\mathrm{final}}$.

\paragraph{Translation-gated downstream checks.}
The companion translation-conditioned protein-stability boundary gives a more local downstream reading.  Across three stability joins, the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ stability-prediction summary had $n=3$, minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$.  However, after full controls and translation conditioning, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ had minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, two negative calls, one positive call, sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$, and weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=-0.001572140012981015$.  This is a partial and mostly attenuated boundary, not a stability mechanism.  The finite joins used mRNA half-life for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 with $2517$ readout rows, protein turnover for \emph{H. sapiens} with $6697$ turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows, and protein turnover for \emph{S. cerevisiae} with $3537$ turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
A separate structural-order contact behaves differently and therefore should not be collapsed into the compressibility result.  For the translation-conditioned structural-order boundary, $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ predicted order with $n=2$, minimum $0.03664544696481631$, mean and median $0.09855549686555976$, and maximum $0.1604655467663032$.  The full-control structural-order $\Delta R^2$ was positive in both organisms, with minimum $0.002688284523492457$, mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$, and weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$.  The joined structural-order row counts were $3412$ for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  In the translation-gated structural-order run, the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis contained nine coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Both organisms had rank-sufficient residual design: rank $9$ after controls, $24$ control columns, $23$ control rank, and $20$ amino-acid composition controls.  The structural-order joins were nearly complete, with $3736$ matched proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined rows for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched proteins out of $5815$ codon-joined rows for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, hit rate $0.998108$.  Their structural-order $R^2$ values were $0.03059081709413562$ and $0.0715885627979685$, respectively.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The finite result is a negative result for the minimal-compilation law as stated.  The measured low-dimensional dictionary $D_o$ does not compress $P_Q$ strongly: the residual source remains largely present after measured translation, mRNA-stability, and selected downstream readouts.  This should be read as a descriptive finite-row variance-partition result, not as a causal theorem.  The readout order $T \to S \to H$ fixes an attribution convention; a different ordering could redistribute explained variance.  The organism panels come from different datasets and platforms, so the computation does not establish a universal biological law.  Conversely, low compression does not prove that no biological explanation exists; it only says that these measured readouts, in this finite construction, do not absorb the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual source.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, molecular function claim, phenotype claim, degradation mechanism, or universal rule follows from the compressibility table alone.  A downstream statement may be made only at the layer where a separate matched contact exists and only after the relevant controlled statistic has been computed.  Modeled adaptation weights are not ribosome occupancy.  Bulk protein abundance is not protein stability.  Predicted structure confidence is not folding kinetics or function.  A direct $q_i \to$ protein-state association is not a mediated statement unless the same coordinate first survives a controlled translation-readout step and conditioning on that translation readout changes the downstream association.
