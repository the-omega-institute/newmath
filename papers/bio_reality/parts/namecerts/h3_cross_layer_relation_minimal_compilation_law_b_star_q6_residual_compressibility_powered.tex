\subsection{B*\_Q6 Residual Compressibility Through Measured Cross-Layer Readouts}
\label{sec:bior-reality-bstar-q6-residual-compressibility}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests whether the residual coding-coordinate source $P_Q$ becomes small after projection through the measured downstream readouts available in the panel. The carrier is a finite organism-by-CDS table with $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, measured translation-efficiency entries where available, matched mRNA controls, and later readout surfaces used only after the translation layer has been represented. The coordinates are treated as amino-acid-quotient residuals before any cross-layer reading is attempted. The test is therefore not a direct biological mechanism claim: it asks whether a low-dimensional measured readout set $D_o$ absorbs most of the $P_Q$ energy.

\paragraph{Compression statistic.}
For each organism, the reported statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{compressibility} &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2},\\
R_{\mathrm{final}\,\mathrm{fraction}} &= \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The description length $DL_{D_o}$ is the summed dimensionality of kept $D_o$ readouts; the raw baseline description length is the summed dimensionality of every available candidate readout before scored selection. The finite rows give the following organism-level outcomes. In \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, $DL_{D_o}=1$, raw baseline $DL=17$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\,\mathrm{fraction}}=0.9756319042010854$, compressibility $0.024368095798914635$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $DL_{D_o}=2$, raw baseline $DL=2$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\,\mathrm{fraction}}=0.9942823785118229$, compressibility $0.005717621488177116$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \textit{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $DL_{D_o}=3$, raw baseline $DL=19$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\,\mathrm{fraction}}=0.9736442326306781$, compressibility $0.02635576736932188$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $DL_{D_o}=2$, raw baseline $DL=19$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\,\mathrm{fraction}}=0.8676586345294415$, compressibility $0.13234136547055853$, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Readout dictionary actually selected.}
The retained readout lists are small. \textit{Danio rerio} keeps only measured translation efficiency. \textit{E. coli} keeps measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability. \textit{H. sapiens} keeps measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density, with PTM density also selected in the scored higher dictionary. \textit{S. cerevisiae} keeps measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability. Thus the strongest form of the proposed minimal-compilation statement would require these low-dimensional lists to remove most of the $P_Q$ energy. They do not. The median compressibility is reported as $0.026$, so the measured readout dictionary leaves most of the residual coordinate energy unabsorbed. The finite result is a negative result for a strong minimal-compilation law over these measured surfaces: $P_Q$ is not highly compressed by the available low-dimensional $D_o$ panel.

\paragraph{Relation to downstream translation-conditioned surfaces.}
The negative compression result is compatible with bounded downstream associations, because the tests ask different questions. The translation-conditioned protein-stability boundary has three joined organism entries and reports $B^*_{Q6}$ direct stability predictive values with min $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and max $0.03602995605625481$. After full controls, however, the aggregate translation-conditioned stability delta has min $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, max $0.0006378518321674687$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$, yielding a partial rather than full crossing call. By contrast, the translation-conditioned structural-order boundary has two organisms, both positive after full controls: aggregate delta values run from $0.002688284523492457$ to $0.007880037229394898$, with mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$. The structural-order support is also tied to concrete row joins in a separate translation-gated predicted-structure-confidence surface: \textit{E. coli} uses $3736$ matched proteins with join hit rate $0.999198$ and $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$, while \textit{S. cerevisiae} uses $5804$ matched proteins with join hit rate $0.998108$ and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The finite-row verdict is that the minimal compilation law is not supported across the computed organisms. The evidence does not show that the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source is reducible to the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary. The correct reading is narrower: these data provide a controlled variance-partition test in which the residual coordinate source remains largely present after the available readouts are used. The result is descriptive, organism-scoped, and platform-scoped. It does not imply that no future readout could explain more of $P_Q$, and it does not identify the selected readouts as mechanisms.

\paragraph{No-promotion boundary.}
No translation realization, folding result, physical admissibility claim, biological function claim, mechanism claim, phenotype claim, or universal biological law follows from this compression panel alone. Modeled codon-adaptation weights, ribosome-profiling routes, mRNA controls, protein-abundance routes, and predicted-structure-confidence surfaces each have their own measurement scope. A downstream protein-stability, degradation, localization, folding, function, or phenotype interpretation requires a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic computed after the translation-readout step survives the declared controls. Direct $q_i \to$ protein-state wording is therefore inadmissible unless the same finite panel first supports a controlled $q_i \to T_j$ entry and then shows the stated translation-conditioned residual relation.
