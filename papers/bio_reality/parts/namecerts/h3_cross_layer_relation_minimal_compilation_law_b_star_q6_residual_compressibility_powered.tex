\subsection{Rank-Powered Residual Compressibility Boundary for $B^*_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-minimal-compilation-law}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are read only after amino-acid quotienting and ordinary sequence controls. The residual source $P_Q$ is not treated as a biological mechanism. It is a controlled coordinate surface tested against a small collection of measured readouts: translation efficiency where available, RNA stability where available, and a scored downstream dictionary entry only when the finite panel supports it. The organisms with computed residual energy are Danio rerio, Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Their joined row counts and residual energies are, respectively, $n=13140$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606$, $n=2517$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032$, $n=11250$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=286.3673197177688$, and $n=4759$ with $\|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544$. These numbers define the scale of the test: the question is whether the measured low-dimensional readout set compresses the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source, not whether codon geometry directly explains protein fate.

\paragraph{Description-length statistic.}
For each organism $o$, the reported compressibility is the residual-energy fraction removed by the selected readout dictionary $D_o$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_o &= 1 - \frac{\|R_{o,\mathrm{final}}\|^2}{\|P_{Q,o}\|^2}, \\
\frac{\|R_{o,\mathrm{final}}\|^2}{\|P_{Q,o}\|^2} &= 1-C_o .
\end{aligned}
$$
The retained description length $DL(D_o)$ is the summed dimensionality of the selected readouts, while the raw baseline description length is the summed dimensionality of the available candidate readouts before scored selection. Danio rerio keeps only measured translation efficiency, with $DL(D_o)=1$, raw baseline $17$, residual fraction $0.9756319042010854$, compressibility $0.024368095798914635$, and permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$. Escherichia coli keeps measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, with $DL(D_o)=2$, raw baseline $2$, residual fraction $0.9942823785118229$, compressibility $0.005717621488177116$, and $p=0.000999000999000999$. Homo sapiens keeps measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density, with $DL(D_o)=3$, raw baseline $19$, residual fraction $0.9736442326306781$, compressibility $0.02635576736932188$, and $p=0.000999000999000999$; in this case the scored downstream dictionary selects PTM density. Saccharomyces cerevisiae keeps measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, with $DL(D_o)=2$, raw baseline $19$, residual fraction $0.8676586345294415$, compressibility $0.13234136547055853$, and $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism readback.}
The median compressibility across the computed organisms is $0.026$. This is the decisive finite-row outcome for the minimal-compilation law: the measured low-dimensional $D_o$ set does not highly compress $P_Q$ across the computed organisms. The strongest organism in the present panel is Saccharomyces cerevisiae at $C_o=0.13234136547055853$, but the other three organisms remain near zero compression: $0.024368095798914635$ for Danio rerio, $0.005717621488177116$ for Escherichia coli, and $0.02635576736932188$ for Homo sapiens. Thus the residual source is not compiled into the measured readouts in this descriptive test. The correct local reading is narrower: these readouts remove a statistically nonzero but small fraction of residual energy, and the remaining residual energy is still dominant in every computed organism.

\paragraph{Translation and downstream gates.}
The same coordinate family has separate boundary tests that help delimit what this chapter may say. In the translation-conditioned protein-stability boundary, three joined organism surfaces were available: Escherichia coli with $2517$ mRNA half-life rows, Homo sapiens with $11322$ mRNA half-life rows and $6697$ protein-turnover rows, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae with $4759$ mRNA half-life rows and $3537$ protein-turnover rows. The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ stability association summary was positive but small, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$ over $n=3$ organisms. After full translation and control conditioning, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ summary was not positive overall: minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip value $p=0.946441650390625$. The recorded call is partial, with two partial organism calls and one mediated-blocked null call. This supports a boundary reading rather than a protein-stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
A separate predicted-structure-confidence readout gives a useful contrast. In the translation-gated structural-order panel, the coordinate list has nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The control set contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an M-density baseline. Escherichia coli has $3739$ codon-joined entries, $3736$ matched proteins, a structural-order join hit rate of $0.999198$, residual rank $9$ after controls, $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$, and $9$ surviving coordinates. Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $5815$ codon-joined entries, $5804$ matched proteins, a hit rate of $0.998108$, residual rank $9$ after controls, $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$, and $9$ surviving coordinates. In the translation-conditioned structural-order boundary, the full-control $\Delta R^2$ values are positive for both organisms, with minimum $0.002688284523492457$, mean and median $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip value $p=0.0029296875$. This comparison does not rescue the minimal-compilation law; it only shows that a distinct layer-specific surface can have a stronger finite association than the broad residual-compression test.

\paragraph{Local claim.}
The chapter therefore records a negative result for the minimal-compilation law in its measured form. The finite panel does not show that $P_Q$ is efficiently compiled into the available low-dimensional readouts $D_o$. The permissible statement is that $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can be carried into controlled organism-level readback matrices, and that some downstream surfaces show bounded associations, but the broad compression law is not supported by the present row sets. The result is descriptive, organism-scoped, and platform-dependent. The readout order $T\to S\to H$ is a modeling convention for variance partitioning; changing the order can change attribution.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation mechanism, ribosome-dynamics statement, protein folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, biological-function statement, phenotype statement, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this chapter. Modeled tAI or stAI quantities are not observed ribosome occupancy. Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not a translation readout. Bulk proteomics is not a stability, folding, localization, function, or mechanism assay. A protein-stability or degradation-rate claim would require a separate organism- and CDS-matched stability, turnover, pulse-chase, degradation, or perturbation contact, together with a surviving same-coordinate $q_i\to T_j$ translation-readout step and a translation-conditioned residual association. In the present minimal-compilation test, the finite numbers instead say that the measured readouts leave most of $P_Q$ uncompressed.
