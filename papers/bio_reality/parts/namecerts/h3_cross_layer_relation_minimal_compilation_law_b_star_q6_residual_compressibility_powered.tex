\subsection{Rank-powered residual compressibility of $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates}
\label{sec:bioq6-minimal-compilation-law-residual-compressibility}

\paragraph{Finite panel and question.}
This record tests whether the amino-acid-quotient residual coordinate block $B^*_{Q6}$ is compressed by a small measured readout dictionary after the usual coding-sequence controls have been applied. The finite carrier is an organism-by-CDS panel: each row carries $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, available translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, and downstream readout surfaces where the data table supplies them. The biological question is deliberately narrow. It is not whether codon topology causes translation, protein stability, folded structure, function, phenotype, or a universal rule. It is whether the measured low-dimensional dictionary $D_o$ absorbs a large fraction of the projected residual energy $P_Q$.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
For each organism, the test projects the residual source $P_Q$ through measured readout coordinates $D_o$ selected from translation, RNA-stability, and scored higher-layer entries when available. The reported compression is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{compressibility}
  &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}, \\
R_{\mathrm{final}}\ \mathrm{fraction}
  &= \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $D_o$ denotes the kept measured readout dictionary and the raw baseline dimension is the number of available candidate readout coordinates before the scored selection step. This is a variance-partition statistic on finite joined rows, not a causal compilation mechanism.

\paragraph{Per-organism readout dictionaries.}
The four computed organisms all have explicit row counts and residual energies. For \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, the kept dictionary is $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te}\}$, $\mathrm{DL}_{D_o}=1$, and the raw baseline dimension is $17$. For \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, $\mathrm{DL}_{D_o}=2$, and the raw baseline dimension is $2$. For \textit{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$, $\mathrm{DL}_{D_o}=3$, and the selected higher-layer coordinate is $\mathrm{ptm\_density}$. Its raw baseline dimension is $19$. For \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, $\mathrm{DL}_{D_o}=2$, and the raw baseline dimension is $19$.

\paragraph{Compression values.}
The measured dictionary does not strongly compress the residual source in any broad cross-organism sense. The organism-level values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\textit{Danio rerio}:&\quad R_{\mathrm{final}}=0.9756319042010854,\quad c=0.024368095798914635,\quad p=0.000999000999000999,\\
\textit{Escherichia coli}:&\quad R_{\mathrm{final}}=0.9942823785118229,\quad c=0.005717621488177116,\quad p=0.000999000999000999,\\
\textit{Homo sapiens}:&\quad R_{\mathrm{final}}=0.9736442326306781,\quad c=0.02635576736932188,\quad p=0.000999000999000999,\\
\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}:&\quad R_{\mathrm{final}}=0.8676586345294415,\quad c=0.13234136547055853,\quad p=0.000999000999000999.
\end{aligned}
$$
The median compressibility is $0.026$. Thus the finite panel supports the opposite of a strong minimal-compilation reading: the residual source remains mostly outside the measured low-dimensional dictionary. The yeast panel is the largest absorber among the four, with $c=0.13234136547055853$, but even there most residual energy remains in $R_{\mathrm{final}}$.

\paragraph{Relation to translation-conditioned downstream tests.}
The same record family contains downstream boundary checks, and they are useful for fixing the interpretation of the compressibility result. In the translation-conditioned stability boundary, three joined stability panels were available after a minimum joined-row threshold of $300$ and five folds. The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ stability association was positive but small, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$. After full controls and translation conditioning, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ had mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, and weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$; the sign-flip probability for being greater than zero was $0.946441650390625$. The verdict was partial, with one mediated-blocked-null organism call and two partial calls. These numbers do not promote a protein-stability mechanism; they show that the available stability surface is weak and translation-conditioned.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
A separate structural-order boundary is more positive, but it also remains a bounded readout rather than a mechanism. In the two-organism structural-order boundary, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ order association had minimum $0.03664544696481631$, median and mean $0.09855549686555976$, and maximum $0.1604655467663032$. The translation-conditioned full-control $\Delta R^2$ had minimum $0.002688284523492457$, median and mean $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$. Both organisms were called full-control-positive. In the translation-gated structural-order computation, the joined structural-order hit rates were $0.999198$ for \textit{E. coli} with $3736$ matched proteins used from $3739$ codon-joined rows, and $0.998108$ for yeast with $5804$ matched proteins used from $5815$ codon-joined rows. The structural-order readout was AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale and is interpreted only as predicted structure confidence.

\paragraph{Coordinate and control discipline.}
The structural-order computation used nine residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $\mathrm{Arg}_{AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile}_{AUA}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu}_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $\mathrm{Ser}_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $\mathrm{Thr}_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The controlled design included an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and an M-density baseline. The rank diagnostics were powered in both structural-order organisms: for \textit{E. coli}, $\mathrm{rank}(X_{\sim})=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $3713$, explained energy was $4178.935925251874$, and residual readout energy was $136607.52873622958$; for yeast, $\mathrm{rank}(X_{\sim})=9$, $\mathrm{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ were $5781$, explained energy was $44421.14342043593$, and residual readout energy was $620506.1490869389$.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The minimal-compilation law is not supported on the computed finite rows. The small median compressibility, $0.026$, says that the measured low-dimensional readouts do not absorb most of $P_Q$. This is an irreducibility statement relative to the present panels and readout order, not a claim that no biological explanation exists. The downstream boundary records should be read in the same finite-row manner: a translation-conditioned stability association is partial and weak after controls, while predicted structural-order confidence crosses its bounded readout threshold in the available two-organism panel. None of these finite statistics by itself establishes translation realization, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, molecular mechanism, phenotype, or universality. Those claims require separate layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.
