\subsection{Residual compressibility of the $B^*_{Q6}$ panel}
\label{sec:bior-reality-q6-minimal-compilation-residual-compressibility}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The object tested here is a finite organism-by-CDS panel carrying $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, and downstream protein or structure-adjacent readout surfaces where those surfaces are available. Each $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate is first residualized against amino-acid composition. The residual panel is then interpreted only through controlled readout rows: organism identity, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density, amino-acid composition, and mRNA-related covariates remain in the control frame before any downstream association is read. The computed $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate list contains nine residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress. The minimal-compilation question is not whether these coordinates have no biological contact. It is the narrower question of whether the projected residual source $P_Q$ is highly absorbed by the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary $D_o$.

\paragraph{Score definition.}
The experiment defines the residual-compressibility score by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{compressibility} &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $D_o$ is the selected readout dictionary and $DL(D_o)$ is its summed dimensionality. The raw baseline description length is the summed dimensionality of every available candidate readout before scored selection. Thus a small value of the score means that the selected readout dictionary has left most of the $P_Q$ energy in the final residual. This is a finite variance-partition statistic; it is not a causal compilation mechanism and it does not identify a molecular process.

\paragraph{Per-organism readout rows.}
Four organism panels were computed. In \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$ CDS rows were used, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, $DL(D_o)=1$, and the raw baseline description length was $17$. The selected dictionary was only measured translation efficiency. The final residual fraction was $0.9756319042010854$, giving compressibility $0.024368095798914635$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $DL(D_o)=2$, and raw baseline $DL=2$; the selected dictionary was measured translation efficiency together with mRNA stability. The final residual fraction was $0.9942823785118229$, giving compressibility $0.005717621488177116$ with the same permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $DL(D_o)=3$, and raw baseline $DL=19$; the selected dictionary contained measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density, with PTM density also selected in the scored $H$ component. The residual fraction was $0.9736442326306781$, giving compressibility $0.02635576736932188$ and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $DL(D_o)=2$, and raw baseline $DL=19$; the selected dictionary was measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability. The residual fraction was $0.8676586345294415$, giving compressibility $0.13234136547055853$ and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite-row result does not support the proposed minimal compilation law. Across the computed organisms, the median compressibility is $0.026$, so the measured low-dimensional $D_o$ dictionary does not highly compress $P_Q$. Most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual energy remains after projection through the available readout dictionary. The strongest of the four finite scores is the yeast value $0.13234136547055853$, but even that value describes only partial absorption by the measured dictionary, not a closed reduction of synonymous residual structure to the chosen readouts. The weakest case is the \emph{E. coli} panel, where the final residual fraction is $0.9942823785118229$, leaving almost all of $P_Q$ outside the selected dictionary despite $DL(D_o)=2$ matching the raw baseline dimensionality.

\paragraph{Readout conditioning.}
The surrounding finite records sharpen the boundary rather than weakening it. A separate translation-conditioned stability test passed its computational status gate but returned only a partial verdict: the weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.001572140012981015$, the aggregate median was $-0.0033500708066446228$, two of three organism calls were partial, one was a mediated-blocked null, and the full-control sign-flip probability for being greater than zero was $0.946441650390625$. The joined stability surfaces were mRNA half-life in \emph{E. coli} with $2517$ rows, protein turnover in human with $6697$ protein-turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows, and protein turnover in yeast with $3537$ protein-turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows. This does not license a direct $q_i$ to stability or degradation claim. It says that when stability-like surfaces are tested, they must remain conditional on a surviving translation readout and on the declared controls.

\paragraph{Structural-order contrast.}
A different finite contact, using AlphaFold mean pLDDT only as predicted structure-confidence and not as experimental folded structure, showed a bounded positive structural-order association in two organisms. The matched structural-order join used $3736$ proteins in \emph{E. coli} with join hit rate $0.999198$ and $5804$ proteins in yeast with join hit rate $0.998108$. Both organisms had rank-sufficient residual design matrices with $9$ $q$ coordinates; the control column count was $24$, $rank(Z)=23$, and $rank(\widetilde X)=9$. The structural-order survival values were $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ in \emph{E. coli} and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ in yeast. In the later translation-conditioned order boundary, the weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $0.004764742175882462$, the aggregate mean was $0.005284160876443678$, and the sign-flip probability for a positive full-control effect was $0.0029296875$. These numbers show that some controlled readout surfaces can retain finite $B^*_{Q6}$ signal, but they do not rescue the minimal-compilation law: a positive bounded readout association is not the same as compressing the whole $P_Q$ residual source into a small universal dictionary.

\paragraph{Local claim boundary.}
The allowed conclusion is therefore negative and descriptive. The observed panels say that $P_Q$ is not highly compressed by the measured $D_o$ readouts in this finite test, with median compressibility $0.026$. They do not show that the residual coordinates are biologically irrelevant, because the claim being tested is only this dictionary-compression law. They also do not show translation realization, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality. A translation claim requires a same-row translation readout that survives the controls. A stability, degradation, localization, interaction, functional, or phenotypic claim requires its own layer-matched measurement after the translation step. Modeled adaptation weights, codon-table geometry, workflow eligibility, and direct residual-to-protein associations are insufficient for those conclusions without the corresponding finite contact and statistic.
