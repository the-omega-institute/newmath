\subsection{B-star Q6 Residual Compressibility as a Translation-Conditioned Boundary}\label{sec:b-star-q6-residual-compressibility-translation-conditioned-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record tests whether the amino-acid-quotient residual coordinates in $B^{*}_{Q6}$ can be treated as a low-dimensional cross-layer compilation once they are compared with measured downstream readouts. The carrier is a finite organism-by-CDS panel with $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates, matched translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, and downstream protein-state surfaces where available. The admissible reading is deliberately ordered: a coordinate $q_i$ is first residualized against amino-acid composition and sequence covariates, then tested against a translation readout $T_j$, and only then allowed to enter a translation-conditioned downstream readout. A direct $q_i$ to protein-stability, degradation, abundance, structural-order, function, phenotype, or mechanism statement is not licensed by this panel.

\paragraph{Residual-compression statistic.}
For each computed organism the residual source $P_Q$ is projected through the available dictionary $D_o$ of measured readouts. The statistic is the remaining energy fraction after the selected readout dictionary is used, with compressibility defined by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{comp}(P_Q,D_o) &= 1-\frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite rows do not support a strong minimal-compilation law. The median compressibility is $0.026$, so the measured low-dimensional readout set does not absorb most of $P_Q$. In \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te}\}$, $\operatorname{DL}(D_o)=1$, the raw candidate baseline dimension is $17$, the final residual fraction is $0.9756319042010854$, and compressibility is $0.024368095798914635$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, $\operatorname{DL}(D_o)=2$, the raw baseline dimension is also $2$, the final residual fraction is $0.9942823785118229$, and compressibility is $0.005717621488177116$ with the same permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability},\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$, the selected higher dictionary is $\{\mathrm{ptm\_density}\}$, $\operatorname{DL}(D_o)=3$, the raw baseline dimension is $19$, the final residual fraction is $0.9736442326306781$, and compressibility is $0.02635576736932188$ with $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $D_o=\{\mathrm{measured\_te},\mathrm{mrna\_stability}\}$, $\operatorname{DL}(D_o)=2$, raw baseline dimension is $19$, the final residual fraction is $0.8676586345294415$, and compressibility is $0.13234136547055853$ with $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned interpretation.}
The organism rows are better read as a negative result for compression by the measured dictionary than as evidence for a universal compilation principle. The strongest compression occurs in yeast, but even there $86.76586345294415\%$ of the $P_Q$ energy remains after the selected readouts. Human and zebrafish retain more than $97\%$ of the source energy, and the bacterial row retains more than $99\%$. These numbers mean that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual variation is not highly reducible to the measured translation, mRNA-stability, and selected PTM-density readouts in this finite descriptive test. They do not show that no biological explanation exists; they show only that this particular measured dictionary is insufficient as a compact absorber of the residual source.

\paragraph{Translation survival and downstream surfaces.}
The same coordinate family has bounded downstream contacts, but their interpretation remains conditional. In the rank-powered structural-order panel, the computed $B^{*}_{Q6}$ basis contains $9$ coordinates: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. The control design uses an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, $20$ amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the M-density baseline. Both reported organisms are rank-sufficient: \emph{E. coli} has control-column count $24$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, explained energy $4178.935925251874$, and residual readout energy $136607.52873622958$; yeast has control-column count $24$, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, explained energy $44421.14342043593$, and residual readout energy $620506.1490869389$. The matched structural-order rows use AlphaFold mean pLDDT only as predicted structure-confidence readout: \emph{E. coli} joins $3739$ codon rows to $3736$ matched proteins with hit rate $0.999198$ and has $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ across $9$ surviving coordinates; yeast joins $5815$ codon rows to $5804$ matched proteins with hit rate $0.998108$ and has $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ across $9$ surviving coordinates. A separate translation-conditioned structural-order boundary reports a positive controlled aggregate with weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, range $0.002688284523492457$ to $0.007880037229394898$, and sign-flip $p=0.0029296875$ over $2$ organisms. This supports only a controlled association to the named predicted-confidence surface, not folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Protein-stability boundary.}
The protein-stability surface is weaker and remains only partial. Three organisms have joined stability rows: \emph{E. coli} contributes $2517$ mRNA-half-life rows as the primary readout; human contributes $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows and $6697$ protein-turnover rows, with protein turnover primary; yeast contributes $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows and $3537$ protein-turnover rows, also with protein turnover primary. The unconditioned $B^{*}_{Q6}$ stability association summary is positive in all $3$ rows, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$. After full controls and translation conditioning, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ has mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$, and sign-flip $p=0.946441650390625$. The organism calls are $2$ partial and $1$ mediated-blocked null. Thus the downstream stability contact does not establish a stability mechanism; it records, at most, a partial and mostly nonpositive translation-conditioned boundary.

\paragraph{Local no-promotion rule.}
The finite data separate measured readout association from biological realization. Codon-usage rows, modeled tAI or stAI weights, ribosome-profiling routes, mRNA controls, and bulk proteomics routes are different measurement surfaces, not interchangeable evidence. A $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate may be discussed beyond sequence composition only after the corresponding row-level translation readout survives the stated controls. Even then, downstream reports are residual associations conditioned on that translation step. No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, protein function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the residual geometry, from the selected dictionary, from a protein-abundance contact name, or from AlphaFold pLDDT alone. Each such interpretation requires its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
