\subsection{Minimal compilation boundary for $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-minimal-compilation-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record concerns a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which the $B^\ast_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates are read only after amino-acid quotienting and ordinary sequence controls. The coordinate list used in the powered translation-to-structure contact has nine entries: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f\_3\_stress$. The common control surface contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length\ in\ nt})$, twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of those nine projected coordinates. In the structural-order contact, the controlled design is full-rank for the nine $q$ coordinates in both organisms with available rows: for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 the control rank is $23$, the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $3713$, and $\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$; for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} the control rank is $23$, the residual degrees of freedom after controls are $5781$, and $\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$.

\paragraph{Minimal compilation statistic.}
The tested object is not a direct biological mechanism. It is a finite variance-partition question: after projecting the residual source $P_Q$ through the available readout dictionary $D_o$, how much squared energy remains? The descriptive statistic is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{compressibility} &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2},\\
R_{\mathrm{final}\ fraction} &= \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout dictionary is intentionally small. Its selected dimensions are $DL(D_o)=1$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $DL(D_o)=2$ for \emph{E. coli}, $DL(D_o)=3$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $DL(D_o)=2$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}. The corresponding raw candidate dimensionalities are $17$, $2$, $19$, and $19$. Thus the finite test asks whether a low-dimensional set of measured readouts almost exhausts the residual $Q6$ source, not whether codon geometry causes translation, stability, folding, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Per-organism outcome.}
The powered computation gives a negative answer to the minimal-compilation law. The residual source is not highly compressed by the measured readout set. In \emph{D. rerio}, $n=13140$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\ fraction}=0.9756319042010854$, and the compressibility is $0.024368095798914635$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{E. coli}, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\ fraction}=0.9942823785118229$, and the compressibility is $0.005717621488177116$ with the same permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{H. sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\ fraction}=0.9736442326306781$, and the compressibility is $0.02635576736932188$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$. In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $R_{\mathrm{final}\ fraction}=0.8676586345294415$, and the compressibility is $0.13234136547055853$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Readout dictionary.}
The selected readouts are organism-specific. For \emph{D. rerio}, the retained dictionary is only measured translation efficiency. For \emph{E. coli}, the retained dictionary is measured translation efficiency together with mRNA stability. For \emph{H. sapiens}, the retained dictionary is measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density, with PTM density selected from the higher-layer candidate set. For \emph{S. cerevisiae}, the retained dictionary is measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability. These choices leave most of $P_Q$ as residual energy in every organism. The cross-organism median compressibility is $0.026$, so the finite result is better read as an irreducibility observation relative to the measured dictionary than as a compilation law.

\paragraph{Translation and downstream gates.}
The same family of records contains translation-conditioned downstream checks, but those checks do not rescue the minimal-compilation law. In the protein-stability boundary computation, the joined stability surfaces are finite but uneven: \emph{E. coli} uses $2517$ mRNA half-life rows as its primary readout, \emph{H. sapiens} has $11322$ mRNA half-life rows and $6697$ protein-turnover rows with protein turnover primary, and \emph{S. cerevisiae} has $4759$ mRNA half-life rows and $3537$ protein-turnover rows with protein turnover primary. The raw $B^\ast_{Q6}$-to-stability predictive values are all positive but small, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$ across $n=3$ organisms. After full controls, the aggregate translation-conditioned stability $\Delta R^2$ has minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, and weighted value $-0.001572140012981015$; the sign-flip probability for being greater than zero is $0.946441650390625$. The verdict for that downstream contact is therefore partial, with one mediated-blocked-null organism call and two partial calls, not a stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
The structural-order branch is stronger than the stability branch but remains separately bounded. The translation-gated structural-order contact uses AlphaFold mean pLDDT as predicted structure confidence, not an experimental folding assay. The codon-to-structural-order join is nearly complete in the two organisms tested: \emph{E. coli} has $3739$ codon-joined entries, $3736$ matched proteins used, $3736$ structural-order proteins, and hit rate $0.999198$; \emph{S. cerevisiae} has $5815$ codon-joined entries, $5804$ matched proteins used, $5804$ structural-order proteins, and hit rate $0.998108$. The controlled structural-order associations are finite, with $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ for \emph{E. coli} and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ for \emph{S. cerevisiae}, each retaining all nine coordinates. In the separate translation-conditioned structural-order boundary, the full-control $\Delta R^2$ values are positive in both organisms: minimum $0.002688284523492457$, median and mean $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$. This supports a bounded crossing to a predicted-structural-order surface, while still leaving folding dynamics, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, and mechanism outside the claim.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The finite-row verdict is that $B^\ast_{Q6}$ residual information is not minimally compiled by the available low-dimensional measured readout dictionary. The strongest descriptive statement is negative: measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability where present, and PTM density in the human panel do not absorb most of $P_Q$. The observed permutation values show that the computed projections are not random bookkeeping, but the small compressibilities show that the retained readouts are far from an exhaustive account. Consequently, the claim is a boundary result: $B^\ast_{Q6}$ may be carried into downstream readout tests only through a surviving translation-readout step and then only as a conditioned residual association. No translation realization, protein stability, degradation process, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from the residual coordinates themselves, from modeled adaptation weights, from contact names, or from a direct $q_i$ association unless a separate layer-matched measurement supplies that specific contact and the corresponding controlled statistic.
