\subsection{Residual Compressibility After the Translation Gate}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-residual-compressibility-powered}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record considered here is a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel for the $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates.  The coordinate side is not read as amino-acid composition: each coordinate is residualized after the first amino-acid quotient, and the controlled design includes amino-acid composition, organism identity, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density on the projected coordinate support, and matched transcript controls where the readout requires them.  The measured residual source is denoted $P_Q$.  For each organism, the test first computes the energy of $P_Q$, then asks how much of that energy can be absorbed by a low-dimensional dictionary $D_o$ of available downstream readouts.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
The descriptive compression score is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  C &= 1 - \frac{\|R_{\mathrm{final}}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $R_{\mathrm{final}}$ is the residual left after projecting $P_Q$ through the selected readout dictionary.  This is a variance-partitioning statistic, not a causal mechanism.  The description length $DL_{D_o}$ is the summed dimensionality of the selected readouts, while the raw baseline description length is the dimensionality of all available candidate readouts before scored selection.

\paragraph{Organism rows.}
The four computed rows do not support a strong minimal-compilation law.  In \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$ coding sequences give $\|P_Q\|^2=605.0092168669606$; the selected dictionary is only measured translation efficiency, so $DL_{D_o}=1$ against a raw baseline $DL=17$.  The remaining residual fraction is $0.9756319042010854$, hence $C=0.024368095798914635$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  In \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$ gives $\|P_Q\|^2=225.84556319953032$; the selected dictionary contains measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, with $DL_{D_o}=2$ and raw baseline $DL=2$.  The remaining residual fraction is $0.9942823785118229$, so $C=0.005717621488177116$, again with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Eukaryotic rows.}
In \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$ gives $\|P_Q\|^2=286.3673197177688$.  The selected readouts are measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density; $DL_{D_o}=3$ against raw baseline $DL=19$, and PTM density is the selected higher dictionary entry.  The final residual fraction is $0.9736442326306781$, so $C=0.02635576736932188$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  In \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$ gives $\|P_Q\|^2=229.86450794006544$; the selected dictionary contains measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, with $DL_{D_o}=2$ against raw baseline $DL=19$.  The residual fraction is lower than in the other rows, $0.8676586345294415$, but the corresponding compression is still only $C=0.13234136547055853$, with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism verdict.}
The median compression is reported as $0.026$.  Thus the tested residual source is not highly compressed by the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary.  The result is not that $B^*_{Q6}$ lacks biological relevance; it is narrower.  It says that, on these finite panels and readouts, the residual coordinate energy is mostly not absorbed by the measured $D_o$ surfaces.  The selected readouts are therefore residual absorbers for this descriptive model, not an explanation of the coordinate source.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned stability boundary.}
A separate downstream stability test is present and must be interpreted after the translation gate.  Three joined stability rows were available.  The direct $B^*_{Q6}$-to-stability predictive summaries are positive but small, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$ over $n=3$ organism-level entries.  The joined readout table uses mRNA half-life as the primary readout in \emph{E. coli} with $2517$ rows, protein turnover in human with $6697$ protein-turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows, and protein turnover in yeast with $3537$ protein-turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows.  After full controls, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ has minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, and maximum $0.0006378518321674687$.  Its weighted value is $-0.001572140012981015$, and the greater-than-zero sign-flip probability is $0.946441650390625$.  The organism calls are two partial rows and one mediated blocked-null row.  This supports only a partial boundary statement: direct residual association is not enough; the downstream stability interpretation remains translation-conditioned and cannot be promoted to folding, degradation mechanism, function, or phenotype.

\paragraph{Structural-order comparison.}
The related structural-order computation shows why the stability record should remain separate.  For predicted structural-order confidence, the joined rows are $3412$ in \emph{E. coli} and $5119$ in yeast.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ predictor summary over the two rows has minimum $0.03664544696481631$, mean and median $0.09855549686555976$, and maximum $0.1604655467663032$.  The translation-conditioned order statistic has aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ values $0.002688284523492457$ and $0.007880037229394898$, with mean $0.005284160876443678$, weighted value $0.004764742175882462$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$.  That packet crosses to a predicted structural-order confidence layer, whereas the stability packet remains partial.  The two facts should not be merged into one mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Rank and readout sufficiency.}
The structural-order gate also records the controlled coordinate rank.  The nine residual coordinates are $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  For \emph{E. coli}, $3739$ codon rows are joined to $3736$ matched proteins, giving a structural-order hit rate $0.999198$; the controlled design has $24$ control columns, $23$ control rank, residual degrees of freedom $3713$, residualized coordinate rank $9$, explained energy $4178.935925251874$, and readout residual energy $136607.52873622958$.  For yeast, $5815$ codon rows are joined to $5804$ matched proteins, with hit rate $0.998108$; the design again has $24$ control columns, $23$ control rank, residual degrees of freedom $5781$, residualized coordinate rank $9$, explained energy $44421.14342043593$, and readout residual energy $620506.1490869389$.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter therefore reports a negative or weak compression result, not a global biological law.  No translation realization follows from the residual coordinates alone; translation readouts must be measured or explicitly bounded on matched rows.  No protein stability, degradation, folding quality, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or universality follows from $B^*_{Q6}$ geometry, modeled adaptation weights, or bulk abundance contacts unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding readout and the controlled statistic is computed on the same finite panel.  The finite result is a disciplined boundary: $P_Q$ is largely irreducible to the measured $D_o$ dictionary in this test, while any downstream stability or structural interpretation must pass through the translation-conditioned row structure rather than bypass it.
