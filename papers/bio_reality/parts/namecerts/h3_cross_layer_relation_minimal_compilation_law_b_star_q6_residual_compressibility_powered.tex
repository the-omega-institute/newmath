\subsection{Minimal compilation test for $B^*_{Q6}$ residual compression}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-minimal-compilation-test}

\paragraph{Object of the finite panel.}
The record tested here is a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel for the residual coordinates denoted $B^*_{Q6}$.  Each coordinate is first residualized against amino-acid composition, so the object being tested is not ordinary amino-acid usage, codon-count mass, or a direct proxy for protein chemistry.  The finite readback is organized as a gated matrix in which a residual coordinate $q_i$ may be compared with a translation readout $T_j$, and only after that contact may one ask whether a downstream protein-state readout carries a remaining association.  The control set used for the declared readout includes amino-acid composition, organism identity when pooled, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, $M$-density on the projected support, matched mRNA controls when present, and simpler codon baselines where the experiment defines them.  Thus the mathematical object is a controlled residual-source test, not a claim that codon topology by itself realizes translation, folding, stability, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, phenotype, or a biological law.

\paragraph{Compression statistic.}
The tested source is the projected residual energy $P_Q = \Pi_{Q_e}P_e$.  The measured downstream dictionary $D_o$ is built from the available finite readouts, beginning with measured translation efficiency and then adding stability or higher-layer candidates only where the data surface exists.  The reported compression score is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{comp}(Q,D_o)
  &= 1 - \frac{\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2}{\lVert P_Q\rVert^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\lVert R_{\mathrm{final}}\rVert^2/\lVert P_Q\rVert^2$ is the residual fraction after projection through the selected measured readouts.  Large compression would mean that the low-dimensional measured dictionary absorbs much of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual source.  The finite result goes the other way: the median compression across the computed organisms is $0.026$, so the measured low-dimensional readout set does not substantially absorb $P_Q$ in this descriptive test.

\paragraph{Organism-level measurements.}
Four organisms have computed $P_Q$ energy and compression values.  For \emph{Danio rerio}, $n=13140$ coding sequences were used, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=605.0092168669606$, $D_o$ contained only measured translation efficiency, $DL(D_o)=1$, the raw candidate dimensionality was $17$, the final residual fraction was $0.9756319042010854$, and the compression was $0.024368095798914635$ with permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=2517$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=225.84556319953032$, $D_o$ contained measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, $DL(D_o)=2$, the raw candidate dimensionality was $2$, the final residual fraction was $0.9942823785118229$, and the compression was $0.005717621488177116$ with the same permutation value $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Homo sapiens}, $n=11250$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=286.3673197177688$, $D_o$ contained measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and PTM density, with PTM density selected as the higher-layer dictionary entry; $DL(D_o)=3$, the raw candidate dimensionality was $19$, the final residual fraction was $0.9736442326306781$, and the compression was $0.02635576736932188$ with $p=0.000999000999000999$.  For \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, $n=4759$, $\lVert P_Q\rVert^2=229.86450794006544$, $D_o$ contained measured translation efficiency and mRNA stability, $DL(D_o)=2$, the raw candidate dimensionality was $19$, the final residual fraction was $0.8676586345294415$, and the compression was $0.13234136547055853$ with $p=0.000999000999000999$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism conclusion.}
The finite evidence does not support a minimal compilation law in which $B^*_{Q6}$ is strongly summarized by the measured low-dimensional dictionary.  The most compressed organism in this panel is \emph{S. cerevisiae}, with compression $0.13234136547055853$, and even that leaves residual fraction $0.8676586345294415$.  The other three organisms leave at least $97.36\%$ of the projected residual energy after the selected readout projection.  The descriptive conclusion is therefore irreducibility relative to these measured readouts: the residual source is not explained away by measured translation efficiency, mRNA stability, and the one selected PTM-density surface in the available finite panels.  This conclusion is narrower than a universal biological statement.  It says that these particular panels and these particular readout dictionaries fail to compress the residual source strongly; it does not say that no later translation, RNA-state, protein-state, perturbation, or organism-specific explanation can exist.

\subsection{Translation gate and downstream boundaries}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-translation-gate-downstream-boundaries}

\paragraph{Rank-powered structural-order contact.}
A separate structural-order readback uses nine residual coordinates: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f_3$-stress.  In \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, $3739$ codon-joined proteins yielded $3736$ matched proteins used against $3736$ structural-order proteins, for a join hit rate of $0.999198$; the control matrix had $24$ columns, including $20$ amino-acid composition controls, with $rank(Z)=23$, $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, and residual degrees of freedom $3713$.  The corresponding structural-order association had $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ with $9$ surviving coordinates.  In \emph{S. cerevisiae}, $5815$ codon-joined proteins yielded $5804$ matched proteins used against $5804$ structural-order proteins, for a hit rate of $0.998108$; the control matrix again had $24$ columns and $20$ amino-acid composition controls, with $rank(Z)=23$, $rank(\widetilde X)=9$, and residual degrees of freedom $5781$.  The reported structural-order association had $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ with $9$ surviving coordinates.  These values are finite associations with AlphaFold mean pLDDT interpreted only as predicted structure-confidence readout; they are not folded-structure experiments, physical-admissibility assays, functional assays, or causal mechanisms.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned stability boundary.}
The protein-stability boundary has a more restrictive interpretation.  The finite stability experiment reports three joined organism surfaces: \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 uses mRNA half-life as the primary readout with $2517$ rows; \emph{H. sapiens} uses protein turnover as the primary readout with $6697$ protein-turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows; \emph{S. cerevisiae} uses protein turnover with $3537$ protein-turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows.  Across the three organisms, the direct $B^*_{Q6}$ stability-prediction summary is positive but small, with minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$.  After full controls and translation conditioning, however, the aggregate $\Delta R^2$ summary is not positive as a stable boundary: the minimum is $-0.004953854025603666$, the median is $-0.0033500708066446228$, the mean is $-0.0025553576666936064$, the maximum is $0.0006378518321674687$, and the weighted value is $-0.001572140012981015$.  The sign-flip probability for a positive full-control effect is $0.946441650390625$, and the call distribution is one mediated-blocked null and two partial calls.  The correct local reading is therefore partial and guarded: stability or degradation cannot be inferred directly from $B^*_{Q6}$, and a downstream stability association is only reportable as translation-conditioned residual evidence when the same finite panel supplies the translation step.

\paragraph{Structural-order boundary after conditioning.}
The translation-conditioned structural-order boundary is stronger than the stability boundary but still remains a bounded readout.  The joined structural-order surfaces have $3412$ rows for \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ rows for \emph{S. cerevisiae}.  The direct $B^*_{Q6}$ order-prediction summary has minimum $0.03664544696481631$, median $0.09855549686555976$, mean $0.09855549686555976$, and maximum $0.1604655467663032$.  The full-control translation-conditioned $\Delta R^2$ remains positive in both organisms, with minimum $0.002688284523492457$, median and mean $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, and weighted value $0.004764742175882462$.  The permuted full-control values are negative in both organisms, from $-0.003463794378680385$ to $-0.000779938262202795$, and the positive sign-flip probability is $0.0029296875$.  This supports a finite crossing to the predicted structural-order layer in the supplied rows, but the readout remains AlphaFold-confidence or structural-order surface evidence, not experimental folding, not protein function, and not a general mechanism.

\subsection{Interpretive boundary}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-minimal-compilation-boundary}

\paragraph{What the result licenses.}
The finite result licenses three local statements.  First, the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary does not substantially compress $B^*_{Q6}$ residual energy across the four computed organisms; the cross-organism median compression is $0.026$.  Second, the residual coordinates can be carried into downstream tests only through the declared finite panels and their controls; the translation readout is not optional when interpreting protein-state or structural-order associations.  Third, where downstream effects appear, their status is readout-specific: structural-order conditioning is positive in the two-organism panel, while protein-stability conditioning is partial and mostly blocked after full controls.

\paragraph{What the result does not license.}
No translation mechanism follows merely from a residual coordinate, a modeled tAI or stAI weight, a codon-usage table, or an $S^{QT}$ notation.  No folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, degradation mechanism, protein-localization mechanism, RNA-decay mechanism, or universal law follows from the minimal-compression calculation.  Protein-stability interpretation requires a matched stability or degradation-rate contact and a surviving translation-readout step on the same finite row system.  Structural-order interpretation requires its own layer-matched readout and remains a statement about that readout alone.  The present chapter therefore records a negative minimal-compilation result together with guarded downstream boundaries: $B^*_{Q6}$ is not compressed by the measured dictionary, and any positive higher-layer statement must be earned by a separate finite contact at that layer under the stated controls.
