\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as an RBP-bridge boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a bounded cross-layer claim about synonymous residual coordinates in coding transcripts.  A row is eligible only when it carries a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, a matched mRNA abundance control, an RNA half-life readout, and an explicitly prespecified decay-factor RBP assay at the editable RNA window.  The intended positive bridge is not an association between codon geometry and half-life by itself.  It is the stronger finite-panel pattern in which the coordinate-associated half-life residual tracks measured local occupancy of the chosen decay-factor RBP, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Observed finite anchors.}
The available verified material supplies useful finite-row scale, but it does not by itself supply the full RBP perturbation panel required by the bridge.  The methylation-context reality check for the row family reports $n=20000$ probes across $656$ individuals with baseline explanatory strength $R^{2}=0.0129735$ and no cross-cohort scalar recorded.  This establishes that the linked claim set is being handled as a finite measured table rather than as a purely symbolic law.  A separate RBP-binding contact reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP-concordant cases, which is enough to show that finite RBP occupancy-style rows exist in the surrounding evidence ledger, but not enough to identify the required decay-factor RBP, align it to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA window, or attach loss and rescue.  A translation contact supplies $6494$ rows from the mTOR translation readout, giving a concrete scale for same-row translation conditioning, again without converting translation data into RNA-decay mechanism evidence.

\paragraph{Statistic to be claimed.}
Let $q_i$ denote the high-versus-low $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, let $T_i$ denote the mandatory translation readout, let $H_i$ denote RNA half-life, and let $O_i$ denote measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  The admissible finite statistic has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= E[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i]
     - E[H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i],\\
\Delta_O
  &= E[O_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i]
     - E[O_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i],
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ includes transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler codon baselines.  A positive bridge requires $\Delta_H$ and $\Delta_O$ to be nondegenerate on the same finite rows, requires the half-life effect to disappear after loss of the target RBP, and requires the effect to return only under wild-type rescue.  Binding-defective rescue and non-target rescue must remain negative controls.  Without these finite comparisons, $\Delta_H$ is at most a translation-conditioned RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows with respect to RNA half-life after the full control set and the same-row translation readout are attached.  In symbols, the blocked condition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i,Z_i
\end{aligned}
$$
for the matched finite panel.  Even if this null is rejected for half-life, the RBP bridge remains unproved unless the occupancy, loss, and rescue rows also follow the declared pattern.  The current finite anchors do not yet contain the joined same-organism, same-window table with $q_i$, $T_i$, matched mRNA abundance, $H_i$, $O_i$, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target rescue on the same row system.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
The chapter therefore licenses only a bounded target specification and a finite-row audit route.  RNA half-life alone is not a translation realization.  Translation readout alone is not RNA-decay evidence.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control, not a half-life measurement.  RBP peak counts or RBP concordance values do not establish a decay-factor mechanism unless the specified same-window occupancy and perturbation comparisons are present.  No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from the coordinate label, from codon-window geometry, from a translation-conditioned half-life association, or from the finite anchors cited above.  Each such higher-layer interpretation would require its own matched contact and its own finite statistic.
