\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP bridge boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched-panel claim whose intended rows are coding transcript or CDS records carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$ after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched control fields. The admissible comparison is not a bare high-versus-low coordinate contrast. It is the finite contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the mandatory same-row translation readout. The bridge is allowed only when the same rows also contain measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, a target-RBP loss condition, wild-type target-RBP rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.

\paragraph{Available finite measurements.}
The row data presently attached to this record do not yet form that joined RNA-decay/RBP panel. The strongest directly cited finite table under the submitted claim identifier is the GSE40279 DNA-methylation flanking-context run, with $n_{\mathrm{individuals}}=656$, $n_{\mathrm{probes}}=20000$, baseline $R^2=0.0129735$, no cross-cohort value reported, run id $\mathrm{bd781331dc42}$, and verification time 2026-06-27T05:12:32+00:00. That table is useful as a finite-row reminder that weak context variance can be recorded without licensing a mechanism claim, but it is not an RNA half-life or RBP perturbation panel. The RBP-contact audit supplies a separate finite binding surface with $n_{\mathrm{peaks,total}}=900$, cross-RBP concordant count $3$, runtime $223.648043$ seconds, and run id $\mathrm{b225890251c2}$. These finite facts show that row-counted contacts exist for methylation context, RBP binding, translation, and RNA modification, but they remain separate surfaces rather than a single same-organism, same-window RNA half-life/RBP perturbation matrix.

\paragraph{Local statistic.}
For this chapter the positive bridge statistic would have to be computed on one finite panel. Let $q_i$ denote the residual coordinate class, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window, $L_i$ the target-RBP loss condition, $W_i$ the wild-type rescue condition, and $D_i$ the binding-defective or non-target rescue condition. The relevant comparison is therefore not $q_i \to H_i$ alone, but the controlled pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\to H_i \mid T_i, Z_i,\\
q_i &\to O_i \mid T_i, Z_i,\\
(q_i \to H_i)_{L_i} &\approx 0,\\
(q_i \to H_i)_{W_i} &\text{ returns toward the wild-type effect},\\
(q_i \to H_i)_{D_i} &\text{ does not return toward the wild-type effect},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler-baseline controls. A half-life contrast without the occupancy, loss, rescue, and defective-control pattern is at most a bounded RNA-stability association. It is not a decay-factor RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Null boundary.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls and after conditioning on the same-row translation readout. In symbols, the null is the finite-row assertion that the conditional distribution of $H_i$ is unchanged by replacing the coordinate label within the matched amino-acid and control stratum:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,Z_i
&\sim
H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
A detected half-life difference breaks this exchangeability only for the RNA half-life readout. It still does not identify an RBP mechanism unless measured local occupancy changes in the prespecified RBP channel, the effect collapses after target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it. The current finite facts do not contain those same-window perturbation and rescue rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the attached contacts.}
The methylation run, the RBP-binding run, the mTOR translation run, and the RNA-modification run each contribute finite-row discipline: they record row counts, scalar summaries, and provenance-bearing run ids. They do not by themselves supply the joined panel required here. In particular, $n_{\mathrm{peaks,total}}=900$ and cross-RBP concordance $3$ do not become measured decay-factor occupancy for the $B^*_{Q6}$ RNA half-life windows unless the peaks are joined to the same transcript windows and perturbation conditions. Likewise, the translation table with $n=6494$ does not become an RNA half-life statistic, and the methylation baseline $R^2=0.0129735$ does not become an RNA decay mechanism. The numbers are therefore cited as finite contacts and as negative boundaries, not as a positive bridge result.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses only a bounded conditional test specification: a $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinate-associated RNA half-life effect may be called an RBP bridge only if the same finite rows show translation-conditioned half-life association, measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP-loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure under the stated controls. No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No folding, physical admissibility, protein output, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the specified RBP-decay pattern, or universal biological law follows from the residual coordinate geometry, from codon/window context, from contact names, or from any single finite table cited here. Each such higher-layer assertion would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.
