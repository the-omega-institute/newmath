\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
The record considered here is the finite matched-row claim identified by \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.dna\_methylation.methylation\_flanking\_context\_variance\_gse40279}, but its operative biological question is not a methylation mechanism.  The attached conjectural object is a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate window in a coding transcript, joined to a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcript-abundance controls, and a proposed decay-factor RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue bridge.  The finite empirical payload currently available for the named claim contains $656$ individuals and $20000$ methylation probes, with baseline coefficient $R^{2}=0.0129735$ and no recorded cross-cohort value.  This scalar is usable only as a finite precedent for a weak context-associated readout; it is not itself an RNA half-life, translation, or RBP perturbation measurement.

\paragraph{Coordinate and null model.}
The coordinate split is the quotient after amino-acid identity: high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are compared only after amino-acid sequence has been held fixed.  A positive RNA-stability statement would require a finite panel in which each row carries the residual coordinate $q_i$, translation readout $T_i$, RNA half-life $H_i$, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy $O_i$, matched mRNA abundance $A_i$, and control block $Z_i$.  The intended null is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to $H_i$ after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid T_i, A_i, Z_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
A half-life difference without the specified occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern remains an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Required bridge statistic.}
The bounded bridge can be stated only when four finite-row signs occur together.  First, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP must differ at the editable RNA window in the direction compatible with the half-life effect.  Second, the coordinate-associated half-life residual must collapse under target-RBP loss.  Third, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore the residual.  Fourth, binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to restore it.  In compact form, the positive bridge requires a nonzero translation-conditioned half-life contrast together with an RBP-specific perturbation signature:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= E(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{high},T_i,A_i,Z_i)-E(H_i \mid q_i=\mathrm{low},T_i,A_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_O &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wildtype\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The current finite rows do not yet provide these quantities for the RNA-half-life conjecture.  The available RBP-binding contact has $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  Those rows establish that RBP binding can be represented as a finite measured layer, but they are not the same-window decay-factor occupancy, loss, and rescue rows required here.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The translation side must be present as a same-row condition, not as a label attached after the fact.  A separate translation-related verified surface reports $6494$ rows for the mTOR translation readout, with runtime $9.469$ seconds and source route \texttt{GSE36892\_RAW.tar}.  That finite count shows that translation readouts can be made row-indexed, but it does not join this $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA-half-life panel to ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency.  Therefore the chapter permits only the conditional form: if the same transcript or CDS row contains $q_i$, $T_i$, $H_i$, $A_i$, $O_i$, and the perturbation-rescue columns, then the RBP bridge statistic may be evaluated.  Without that joined matrix, translation conditioning is a requirement, not an achieved result.

\paragraph{What the present rows support.}
The present finite evidence supports a scoped research object: a residual-coordinate RNA-stability question with a sharply specified RBP-bridge test.  It also supports a negative audit statement.  The methylation payload has $n=20000$ probe rows and a small baseline $R^{2}$, the RBP-binding payload has $900$ peaks with $3$ concordant cross-RBP cases, and the mTOR translation payload has $6494$ translation rows.  These are three separate finite surfaces.  They do not yet form a single same-organism, same-window, same-transcript panel.  Consequently, the current status is not a positive RNA-decay mechanism; it is a well-bounded finite-row demand for a future joined statistic.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No RNA half-life association becomes an RBP mechanism unless measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy changes, target-RBP loss removes the effect, wild-type rescue returns it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue fails.  No transcript-abundance control, RNA-structure-only model, motif-density-only model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair model, splice or isoform model, batch model, or coordinate geometry by itself supports the bridge.  The same finite-row record also gives no protein output, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the tested RBP-decay bridge, or universal biological law.  Each such layer would require its own matched contact and statistic.
