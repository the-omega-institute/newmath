\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the decay-factor RBP boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel in which synonymous residual coordinate labels of type $B^{*}_{Q6}$ are attached to coding-transcript or CDS rows, then compared against same-row translation readouts, matched mRNA controls, RNA half-life measurements, and a separately measured decay-factor RBP route. The intended object is not a general codon law and not a protein-output claim. It is a bounded cross-layer question: after amino-acid sequence has been quotiented away, can a high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual label be associated with RNA half-life only in the presence of a measured local RBP occupancy pattern, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue?

\paragraph{Available finite measurements.}
The currently attached methylation-row finite fact is the GSE40279-style methylation context screen with $656$ individuals and $20000$ probes. Its recorded baseline scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{2}_{\mathrm{baseline}} &= 0.0129735,\\
N_{\mathrm{individuals}} &= 656,\\
N_{\mathrm{probes}} &= 20000,
\end{aligned}
$$
with no cross-cohort value recorded for this row. This fact is useful as a finite-row context-control datum, but it is not itself an RNA half-life experiment, an RBP occupancy experiment, or a perturbation/rescue panel. It therefore cannot certify the proposed RNA-decay bridge.

A separate RBP-binding finite fact is present for the RBNS/eCLIP/ENCODE-style route. That record has $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant entries. In symbols, the attached scalar summary is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{peaks}} &= 900,\\
C_{\mathrm{cross\text{-}RBP}} &= 3.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the only linked measurement family in the supplied data that touches RBP binding. Even so, a peak-count and concordance summary is not the same as same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy on the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ transcript rows, and it does not include target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue controls.

The linked translation-readout fact from the mTOR translation screen contributes $6494$ finite rows. Its recorded source is a GEO raw readout archive, and its role here is limited to showing that translation-layer row data can be represented as a separate finite surface. The relevant scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{translation}} &= 6494.
\end{aligned}
$$
This measurement family still does not turn RNA half-life into translation realization; it only names the kind of same-row translation boundary that would have to be joined before any translation-conditioned half-life statement is evaluated.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
A positive claim would require a single finite panel whose rows carry, at minimum, the tuple
$$
\begin{aligned}
(&q_i, T_i, A_i, H_i, O_i, L_i, W_i, D_i, C_i, Z_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, $T_i$ is a same-row translation readout, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance or transcription control, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $W_i$ records wild-type rescue, $D_i$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue, $C_i$ records coordinate/occupancy decoupling, and $Z_i$ packages the declared nuisance controls. The nuisance set must include transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, same-row translation readout, batch, rank sufficiency, and simpler baseline models.

The statistic is admissible only if the half-life contrast is read after conditioning on the translation boundary and the usual abundance controls, and only if the RBP tests have the expected direction. A schematic finite-row criterion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H(q_i \mid T_i,A_i,Z_i) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_O(q_i \mid T_i,A_i,Z_i) &\neq 0,\\
\Delta_H(q_i \mid L_i=\mathrm{target\ loss},T_i,A_i,Z_i) &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H(q_i \mid W_i=\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue},T_i,A_i,Z_i) &\approx \Delta_H(q_i \mid T_i,A_i,Z_i),\\
\Delta_H(q_i \mid D_i=\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target},T_i,A_i,Z_i) &\approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
These equations are not asserted as verified by the supplied data. They specify the local shape of the finite-row test that would be needed before the record could cross from a bounded RNA-stability association into an RBP-mediated half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Current verdict.}
The present data support only a scoped boundary record. The methylation finite fact gives $656$ individuals, $20000$ probes, and $R^{2}_{\mathrm{baseline}}=0.0129735$ for a context-variance screen, not for RNA half-life or RBP perturbation. The RBP-binding route gives $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but does not provide the required same-window occupancy/loss/rescue matrix. The translation route gives $6494$ rows, but it is not joined here to the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate rows, matched mRNA controls, RNA half-life, and RBP intervention rows.

Thus the finite readback is ready as a chapter record of a blocked cross-layer boundary: the proposed object is precisely formulated, the row requirements are finite, and the current measurements identify what is missing. The claim does not yet assert that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ geometry produces an RNA-decay mechanism. It also does not license translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. Any one of those conclusions would require its own layer-matched measurement surface and its own finite statistic, separate from the methylation context scalar, the RBP peak summary, and the translation-row count reported here.

\paragraph{Null interpretation.}
Under the matched null, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life after the declared controls are applied. If half-life differs but local decay-factor RBP occupancy is unchanged, the result remains an RNA-stability association at most. If occupancy differs but target-RBP loss does not collapse the half-life effect, the RBP mechanism is not established. If target-RBP loss collapses the effect but wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or binding-defective or non-target rescue also restores it, the proposed bridge is rejected as non-specific. The current finite data do not contain the observations required to defeat this null.
