\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and the decay-factor RBP boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows equipped with a $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched abundance and transcription controls, and an externally specified decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation route. The coordinate is not itself a biological measurement. It is a finite label placed after the amino-acid quotient, and the proposed cross-layer statement is only allowed to speak about a bounded RNA half-life relation when the relevant translation and RBP rows are present in the same finite panel. The audited claim identifier is \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.dna\_methylation.methylation\_flanking\_context\_variance\_gse40279}; its finite scalar payload records $656$ individuals, $20000$ methylation probes, and a baseline coefficient of determination $R^2=0.0129735$. That payload is therefore a finite cross-layer calibration surface, not an RNA half-life or RBP perturbation experiment. It is cited here as an example of the ledger discipline: a scalar relation may be recorded only at the layer actually measured.

\paragraph{Coordinate and null model.}
For the present RNA-half-life conjecture, each admissible row has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i = (q_i,T_i,H_i,A_i,O_i,L_i,C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $A_i$ is matched mRNA abundance or transcriptional control information, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss or depletion, and $C_i$ records rescue and control arms. The finite null says that, after amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout are fixed, high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. In symbols, the positive relation must be tested against
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid Z_i,T_i,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z_i$ abbreviates the non-translation controls. A half-life difference that survives this conditioning is still not an RBP bridge unless it is accompanied by the required occupancy and perturbation pattern.

\paragraph{RBP bridge criterion.}
The bridge criterion is conjunctive. A positive readback requires: first, a coordinate-associated half-life residual after the declared controls; second, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window; third, experimental decoupling showing that the coordinate and occupancy signals are not merely the same compositional or structural proxy; fourth, collapse of the half-life effect after target-RBP loss; fifth, restoration by wild-type RBP rescue; and sixth, failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue controls. The finite decision rule can be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Bridge}(q,H,O,L,C) = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
&\Delta_H(q\mid Z,T)\neq 0,\\
&O(q)\neq O(\mathrm{null}),\\
&\Delta_H(q\mid Z,T,L_{\mathrm{loss}})=0,\\
&\Delta_H(q\mid Z,T,C_{\mathrm{WT}})\neq 0,\\
&\Delta_H(q\mid Z,T,C_{\mathrm{bind\mbox{-}def}})=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every implication in this display is row-level and finite-panel dependent. If any term is absent, the record falls back to a bounded RNA-stability association or to a coordinate descriptor.

\paragraph{Observed finite contacts.}
The available ledger supplies several finite contacts adjacent to, but not sufficient for, this bridge. The RBP-binding contact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rbp\_binding.rbns\_affinity\_eclip\_binding\_encode} records $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant instances. That is relevant because it demonstrates that finite RBP occupancy or binding surfaces can be represented in the same audit vocabulary, but it does not by itself provide the same-organism, same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, and rescue arms needed here. The mTOR translation contact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.mtor\_translation.topscore\_mtor\_translation\_thoreen2012} records $6494$ rows from a translation readout source, so it supplies an example of a finite translation-layer readout surface rather than an RNA-decay mechanism. The m6Am stoichiometry contact \texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.rna\_m6am\_methylation.m6am\_flanking\_identity\_stoichiometry\_crownseq} records $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct entries, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, a saturated fraction of $0.343223$ at or above $0.95$, and cross-cell concordance marked true. Those values show that row-level modification stoichiometry and flanking identity can be audited as finite readouts, but they do not replace RNA half-life, translation conditioning, or RBP perturbation rows.

\paragraph{Local conclusion.}
The present chapter therefore records a bounded cross-layer target with a sharp negative gate. A coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual is not enough. A translation-conditioned half-life residual is not enough. A measured RBP occupancy association is not enough. The bridge is admitted only when the same finite panel contains the coordinate label, translation readout, RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared control set. Without those rows, the strongest licensed statement is that the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate remains a finite descriptor that may be compared against bounded RNA-stability or translation-conditioned half-life surfaces. No translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, causal decay mechanism, adaptation, phenotype, or universal sequence law follows from the coordinate, from half-life alone, from contact names, or from the adjacent finite scalar facts.
