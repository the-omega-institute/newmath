The computed baseline flanking-context coefficient of determination is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{\mathrm{baseline}} &= 0.0129735,\\
N_{\mathrm{individuals}} &= 656,\\
N_{\mathrm{probes}} &= 20000.
\end{aligned}
$$
The cross-cohort field is null, so the available contact is a single finite-row readout of methylation-context variance and not a replicated cross-cohort stability claim.  No $R$-list, $M$-list, $\lambda$ value, nominal $p$-value, adjusted $p$-value, module class, or hit-rate scalar is present in the supplied finite-row payload for this methylation run; the only numerical methylation effect size available here is the baseline $R^2$ above.

\paragraph{Local scientific reading.}
The table can support only the following narrow statement: in this finite GSE40279-derived methylation surface, the prespecified flanking-context baseline explains about $1.29735\%$ of probe-level variance under the encoded model summary.  This is a weak but concrete finite-row contact: it supplies an observed methylation readout, a row count, a subject count, and a bounded variance statistic.  It does not by itself identify a causal methylation mechanism, a conserved biological grammar, or a transport rule between sequence coordinates and downstream expression states.  Since the cross-cohort field is absent, the same number should be read as an in-panel summary rather than as evidence of transfer across cohorts.

\paragraph{Separation from the Q6 translation--RNA-decay conjecture.}
The conjectural object attached to this record asks for a much more specific joined panel: coding-transcript or CDS rows carrying $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual-coordinate labels, same-row translation readouts, RNA half-life measurements, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue controls.  The methylation table does not contain those rows.  In particular, $R^2_{\mathrm{baseline}}=0.0129735$ over $20000$ methylation probes is not a same-window RNA half-life statistic, not a ribosome-profiling statistic, not a local RBP-occupancy statistic, and not a perturbation/rescue contrast.  It may be used only as a bounded cross-layer control surface showing that a finite biological assay can carry weak context-associated variance; it cannot be promoted into the RNA-decay RBP bridge described by the conjecture.

\paragraph{Null statement.}
The operational null is local and finite.  Conditional on the available methylation rows alone, high- and low-context labels are exchangeable with respect to any RNA half-life, translation, RBP-occupancy, rescue, protein-output, folding, physical-admissibility, function, phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or universal-law claim, because none of those readouts is present in the methylation carrier.  A positive RNA-decay RBP bridge would require a new joined table in which the same transcript or CDS rows carry the coordinate label, translation readout, matched mRNA control, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate--occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
This record licenses a finite methylation-context variance statement and nothing stronger.  No translation realization follows from the methylation $R^2$.  No RNA half-life association follows without matched half-life rows.  No RBP mechanism follows without measured local occupancy, loss collapse, wild-type rescue, and failed binding-defective or non-target rescue.  No protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal biological rule follows unless a distinct layer-matched contact supplies the corresponding rows and a declared finite statistic.  The chapter is therefore a bounded negative control for the proposed cross-layer bridge: it records real finite methylation data while refusing to treat that data as evidence for the absent translation-conditioned RNA-decay mechanism.
