\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as an RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched-row target for coding transcripts.  Each admissible row is required to carry a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, matched transcription and abundance controls, and a predeclared decay-factor RNA-binding-protein window.  The intended positive statement is intentionally narrow: a high-versus-low $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label may be reported as a bounded cross-layer RNA-half-life/RBP bridge only when the same finite rows also show local occupancy of the named decay-factor RBP, collapse of the half-life contrast after loss of that target RBP, restoration under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

The available finite evidence does not yet contain such a joined panel.  The directly indexed finite DNA-methylation contact is the GSE40279 methylation-context run, with $n_{\mathrm{individuals}}=656$, $n_{\mathrm{probes}}=20000$, baseline $R^{2}=0.0129735$, no cross-cohort value recorded, and run id \texttt{bd781331dc42}.  This is a genuine finite reality row set, but it is not an RNA half-life, translation, or RBP perturbation panel.  It can therefore serve only as a warning that a finite context statistic is not automatically a mechanism bridge.  The separate RBP-binding finite contact, run id \texttt{b225890251c2}, records $n_{\mathrm{peaks,total}}=900$ and cross-RBP concordance $3$ for an RBNS/eCLIP-style surface.  That gives a bounded RBP-binding reality surface, but not a same-window decay-factor occupancy/loss/rescue experiment for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ RNA-half-life rows.  A translation-layer anchor is also present elsewhere in the finite register: the mTOR translation run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} has $n=6494$ rows.  It is useful as an example of a translation readout surface, but it is not joined here to RNA half-life, decay-factor RBP occupancy, and rescue controls.

\paragraph{Local mathematical form of the gate.}
Let $I$ be the finite set of rows that survive identifier matching.  A row $i\in I$ would have to carry
$$(\begin{aligned}
q_i &\in \{\mathrm{high},\mathrm{low}\},\\
T_i &\in \mathbb{R},\\
H_i &\in \mathbb{R},\\
O_i &\in \{0,1\},\\
L_i,W_i,B_i,N_i &\in \{0,1\},
\end{aligned})$$
where $q_i$ is the synonymous residual coordinate label, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is the RNA half-life residual after controls, $O_i$ records measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ records target-RBP loss, $W_i$ records wild-type rescue, $B_i$ records binding-defective rescue, and $N_i$ records a non-target rescue or control.  With $Z_i$ denoting transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and simpler codon baselines, the admissible bridge claim requires the contrast in $H_i$ across $q_i$ to remain visible only in the presence of the measured occupancy pattern and the translation-conditioned model:
$$(\begin{aligned}
H_i &= \alpha + \beta q_i + \gamma T_i + \delta O_i + \eta Z_i + \varepsilon_i,\\
\beta &\neq 0 \quad \text{only with the prespecified occupancy and perturbation pattern.}
\end{aligned})$$
The bridge fails locally if the contrast persists without measured occupancy, if target-RBP loss does not reduce the contrast, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, or if binding-defective or non-target rescue restores it.  In that case the row set may still describe a bounded RNA-stability association, but it does not identify an RBP-mediated decay bridge.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The finite facts now in hand do not satisfy the gate above.  The methylation-context rows give a concrete baseline statistic, $R^{2}=0.0129735$ over $20000$ probes and $656$ individuals, but their biological layer is DNA methylation context rather than RNA half-life.  The RBP-binding rows give a finite binding surface with $900$ peaks and only $3$ cross-RBP concordant outcomes, but they do not provide the same-row target-RBP loss and rescue columns.  The mTOR translation rows give a separate translation-scale count of $6494$, but no row-wise join to the RNA half-life and RBP perturbation columns is present.  Thus the present record can be written as a bounded negative boundary:
$$(\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{available} &= \{\mathrm{methylation\ context},\ \mathrm{RBP\ binding\ surface},\ \mathrm{translation\ surface}\},\\
\mathrm{missing} &= \{\mathrm{same\ row\ RNA\ half\ life},\ \mathrm{same\ window\ RBP\ occupancy},\ \mathrm{loss},\ \mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue},\ \\n&\qquad \mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ controls}\}.
\end{aligned})$$
Consequently, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable with respect to the demanded RBP-mediated RNA-half-life bridge under the declared null.  Any half-life difference observed without the occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern would be classified as an RNA-stability association at most, not as a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no claim about translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It also licenses no claim about protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  Codon-window geometry, residual-coordinate notation, methylation-context variance, a finite RBP-binding surface, and an unjoined translation readout do not by themselves observe an RNA-decay mechanism.  A positive bridge would require a new finite panel in which the same organism, same transcript or CDS row, and same editable RNA window carry $B^{*}_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.  Until that panel exists, the scientifically correct reading is a bounded boundary record, not a positive cross-layer mechanism result.
