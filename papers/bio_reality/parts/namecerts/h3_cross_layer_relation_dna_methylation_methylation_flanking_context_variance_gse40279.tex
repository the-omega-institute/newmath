\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and RBP occupancy boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite record.}
This chapter records a bounded BioReality claim about synonymous residual coordinate windows in coding transcripts. The carrier is a finite matched panel whose rows would have to contain a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcription, abundance, composition, structure, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch. The intended contrast is not a broad sequence law. It is the finite distinction between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after the amino-acid quotient, evaluated only after the translation readout is attached to the same row and after the RBP perturbation logic has been separately observed.

The available verified rows do not yet contain that full joined panel. The claim identifier supplied with this record points to a DNA methylation contact, where the finite verification reports $n_{\mathrm{individuals}}=656$, $n_{\mathrm{probes}}=20000$, and a baseline flanking-context value $R^{2}=0.0129735$, with no cross-cohort value recorded. Those numbers are real finite-row evidence for the methylation contact, but they do not measure RNA half-life, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or rescue. The same packet also carries nearby finite evidence surfaces: the RBP binding contact records $n_{\mathrm{peaks,total}}=900$ with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and the translation contact records $n=6494$ rows for the mTOR translation readout surface. These are useful boundary rows, not a joined RNA-decay mechanism panel.

\paragraph{Claim tested by the row design.}
The positive statement would require a finite statistic comparing high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows while conditioning on the same-row translation readout and the declared controls. In a joined panel, the admissible readback would have the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}}
  &= E[H \mid Q=\mathrm{high},T,Z]-E[H \mid Q=\mathrm{low},T,Z],\\
\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{occ}}
  &= E[O \mid Q=\mathrm{high},T,Z]-E[O \mid Q=\mathrm{low},T,Z],
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ is the RNA half-life residual, $O$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, and $Z$ collects transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and rank-sufficiency controls. The coordinate is allowed to become a cross-layer RBP bridge only if the half-life residual follows experimentally decoupled measured occupancy, collapses when the target RBP is lost, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life once the control set is fixed. In finite-row language,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(Q=\mathrm{high},H,O,L,R_{\mathrm{WT}},R_{\mathrm{def}}) \mid T,Z
\quad \text{and} \quad
(Q=\mathrm{low},H,O,L,R_{\mathrm{WT}},R_{\mathrm{def}}) \mid T,Z
\end{aligned}
$$
are not separated by the coordinate label unless the occupancy, loss, and rescue rows supply the separation. Here $L$ denotes target-RBP loss, $R_{\mathrm{WT}}$ denotes wild-type rescue, and $R_{\mathrm{def}}$ denotes binding-defective or non-target rescue. A half-life difference without the measured occupancy trajectory is therefore only an RNA-stability association. An occupancy association without coordinate/occupancy decoupling is only an RBP-binding association. A loss effect without wild-type rescue and failed defective rescue is only a perturbation-sensitive association. None of these weaker outcomes licenses the bridge.

\paragraph{What the current finite facts establish.}
The methylation verification establishes a separate finite surface: $20000$ probe rows over $656$ individuals with baseline $R^{2}=0.0129735$. This confirms that the named record has a measured finite-row contact, but that contact is on methylation flanking context rather than RNA half-life. The RBP binding verification contributes a distinct finite RBP surface with $900$ total peak rows and $3$ cross-RBP concordant outcomes. The mTOR translation verification contributes a translation readout surface with $6494$ rows. These three surfaces show that the surrounding BioReality ledger can hold finite contact rows for methylation, RBP binding, and translation, but they remain unjoined for the present RNA half-life RBP-bridge claim.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
The current chapter therefore treats the packet as a bounded design record with a precise failure mode. The coordinate may be retained as an internal residual label for finite matching, and the translation, RBP-binding, and methylation facts may be cited as row-counted contacts in their own domains. They do not jointly demonstrate RNA decay. They do not show that synonymous residual geometry selects a decay-factor RBP. They do not imply translation realization, protein output, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality. Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact and statistic.

\paragraph{Required positive panel.}
A positive version of the chapter would require same-organism, same-window rows containing $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate labels, matched translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, a coordinate/occupancy decoupling test, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target rescue, and the declared finite half-life statistic. Until those rows are present, the correct BioReality conclusion is narrower: the packet defines an experimentally sharp RNA half-life RBP-bridge test, but the supplied finite evidence supports only adjacent contact boundaries and a null-preserving design.
