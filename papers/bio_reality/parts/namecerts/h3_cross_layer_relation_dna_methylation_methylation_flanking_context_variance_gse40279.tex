\paragraph{Finite row carrier.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel whose intended rows are transcript or coding-sequence rows equipped with a synonymous residual-coordinate label $B^*_{Q6}$, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, perturbation rows for loss of that target factor, rescue rows for the wild-type factor, and negative rescue controls supplied by binding-defective or non-target constructs. The scientific question is not whether a coordinate label can be named, but whether the coordinate remains associated with RNA half-life after the translation boundary and the decay-factor bridge are both represented in the same finite row system. The relevant contrast is the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual label after quotienting by amino-acid identity, with the comparison conditioned on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout.

\paragraph{NameCert content.}
The certified name for this chapter is a translation-conditioned RNA half-life RBP-bridge record. A positive instance would require a finite row table in which each eligible transcript window has a residual coordinate label, the amino-acid sequence control needed to compare synonymous alternatives, a same-row translation measurement, a same-row half-life measurement, and a prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy measurement at the editable RNA window. The bridge is counted only when the half-life contrast tracks experimentally separated local RBP occupancy, disappears after loss of the target RBP, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target controls. If a half-life difference survives the ordinary covariates but does not show this occupancy-loss-rescue pattern, the result remains a finite RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null treats high- and low-coordinate labels as exchangeable for RNA half-life after conditioning on amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. In symbols, the intended tested contrast has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}} &= E[H \mid q=\mathrm{high}, Z] - E[H \mid q=\mathrm{low}, Z],\\
Z &= (\mathrm{AA}, \mathrm{translation}, \mathrm{transcription}, \mathrm{structure}, \mathrm{motif}, \mathrm{GC}, \mathrm{dinucleotide}, \mathrm{codon\ pair}, \mathrm{isoform}, \mathrm{abundance}, \mathrm{batch}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The RBP bridge would require more than $\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}} \neq 0$. It would require a coupled finite pattern in which local occupancy differs in the decoupled occupancy rows, target-RBP loss collapses the half-life effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target rescue does not restore it. The current verified finite scalar surface does not contain those rows.

\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The matched RNA half-life contact can test whether transcript or gene rows carry organism-scoped half-life estimates. The matched mRNA abundance contact can serve as a transcription-layer control when translation efficiency is computed from ribosome-profiling and RNA abundance rows. The ribosome-profiling contact can serve as a translation-readout surface when the same coding sequences are joined to mRNA controls. None of these contact names alone supplies measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue. Consequently, the available record licenses a bounded target specification and a null boundary, not a positive mechanism claim.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA-decay mechanism follows from a same-row translation measurement unless the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue controls are present. No protein output, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from the residual-coordinate geometry, from the baseline scalar surface, from contact routing, or from review eligibility. Any such claim would require a separate layer-matched finite contact with its own statistic and controls. In the present record the safe conclusion is narrower: the chapter fixes the finite conditions under which a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate could be promoted from an RNA-stability association to a translation-conditioned RBP bridge, and it records that the verified scalar surface with $656$ individuals, $20000$ probes, and $R^2_{\mathrm{baseline}}=0.0129735$ does not yet contain the required bridge rows.
