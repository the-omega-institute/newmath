\subsection{Random-promoter expression contact as a boundary test for $f_3$-stress translation survival}
\label{sec:bioreality-f3-stress-position-resolved-translation-survival-deboer-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite table under discussion.}
This record concerns a finite coding-window table whose intended rows would join a residual $f_3$-stress coordinate, local synonymous-codon counts, a context-dependent wobble-weight surface, matched position-resolved ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, matched mRNA abundance, and amino-acid or position-bin controls. The proposed biological object is not a promoter grammar effect and not a protein-output effect. It is the narrower claim that an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate can be tested as a bounded cross-layer coordinate only when wobble weights break the $f_3$ symmetry and the same coding windows carry a controlled translation readout. The operative statistic is a per-window survival entry $S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$, where $Z$ includes amino-acid composition or position bins, GC3, coding-sequence length, methionine-density baseline, codon-count controls, matched mRNA abundance, coverage and mappability restrictions, rank checks, and simpler-baseline ablations.

\paragraph{Why the De Boer promoter run is admissible only as a comparator.}
The verified finite run attached to the chapter id is a random-promoter expression run, not a coding-window translation run. It reports $75{,}111$ designed promoter rows, $8{,}027$ high-quality rows, and an analysis subsample of $2{,}500$ high-quality rows, with subsample seed $20260239$, $200$ actual permutations, and runtime $530.4038379192352$ seconds. These numbers certify that the cited expression-contact surface is finite and auditable, but they do not supply codon-indexed ribosome-footprint rows, translation-efficiency denominators, wobble-weight asymmetry for coding windows, or an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate. The promoter table therefore serves as a same-paper example of a finite expression boundary, while the $f_3$ translation-survival packet remains unpromoted until its own row join exists.

\paragraph{Required survival statistic.}
The intended row type can be written schematically as
$$
\begin{aligned}
  r &= (g, c, p, W_{c,p}, q_{f3}(g,p), T_{g,p}, m_g, Z_{g,p}),\\
  Z_{g,p} &= (\mathrm{AA}_{g,p}, \mathrm{pos}_{g,p}, \mathrm{GC3}_{g,p}, \ell_g, M_g, \mathrm{codon}_{g,p}, m_g, \mathrm{coverage}_{g,p}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $g$ is a coding sequence or transcript identifier, $p$ is a codon position or local window, $W_{c,p}$ is the bounded wobble-weight context, $q_{f3}(g,p)$ is the residual $f_3$ coordinate after the amino-acid quotient, $T_{g,p}$ is the matched position-resolved ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, and $m_g$ is the matched mRNA control. The proposed survival condition is not the presence of this notation. It is the empirical inequality
$$
\begin{aligned}
  S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
after the stated controls and after a held-out comparison against simpler baselines. If the joined matrix is absent, or if the held-out improvement is nonpositive, the packet remains a boundary request rather than a positive cross-layer result.

\paragraph{Rank and wobble asymmetry.}
The $f_3$ coordinate is allowed into the test only after two local checks. First, the residual coordinate must survive the amino-acid quotient rather than merely restating amino-acid composition. Second, the wobble-weight context must show an observed asymmetry $w_c \ne w_c^{f_3}$ on the relevant windows. This asymmetry may be supplied by bounded tAI or stAI weights, but modeled adaptation weights alone are not ribosome occupancy. They can help define a codon-adaptation surface; they cannot replace a measured or explicitly bounded translation readout. Rank sufficiency is also a row-level condition: a saturated or degenerate panel is not evidence for survival, even if a fitted table looks numerically perfect.

\paragraph{Relation to the powered translation-conditioned structural-order run.}
A separate verified linked run gives a useful positive-control scale for cross-layer testing without resolving the present packet. In that run the joined structural-order counts were $3{,}412$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5{,}119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; the fold count was $5$, the minimum joined threshold was $300$, and the reported status was passed. Its aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ values were positive in both organisms, with minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, and weighted value $0.004764742175882462$. The reported full-control sign-flip probability for greater-than-zero behavior was $0.0029296875$, while the permuted full-control $\Delta R^2$ values were negative in both organisms, with mean $-0.00212186632044159$. These finite numbers show what a recorded, controlled cross-layer survival result looks like in a different setting. They do not transfer to the $f_3$ position-resolved translation-survival packet, because the present packet still lacks the same-organism, same-CDS, same-window codon, wobble-weight, mRNA, and footprint rows.

\paragraph{Local refusal boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a bounded admissible question, not a biological promotion. The current finite facts support a self-contained audit trail for a promoter-expression run and for a separate translation-conditioned structural-order run, but they do not compute $S_{f3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z)$. No translation realization, protein abundance, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, adaptation claim, causation claim, or universal biological law follows from the $f_3$ geometry, from wobble-weight notation, from codon-window coordinates, from review readiness, or from contact names. Such conclusions require their own layer-matched contacts and their own controlled statistics.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
This record is ready as a BioReality boundary chapter because its finite evidence and non-promotion rule are explicit. The positive content is the specification of the required finite row join and survival statistic. The negative content is equally important: the De Boer promoter run gives $75{,}111$ designed rows and $8{,}027$ high-quality expression rows, while the linked structural-order run reports a passed controlled result with weighted full-control $\Delta R^2=0.004764742175882462$ and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$; neither supplies the missing position-resolved translation matrix for $f_3$-stress. The next empirical step is to attach same-window coding-sequence rows with codon counts, wobble weights, mRNA controls, and codon-indexed ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency readouts, then estimate the declared held-out survival statistic under the full control set.
