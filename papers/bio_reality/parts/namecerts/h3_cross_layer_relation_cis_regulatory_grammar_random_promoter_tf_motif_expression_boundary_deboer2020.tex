\subsection{Random-promoter expression as a finite cis-regulatory grammar boundary}\label{sec:bior-reality-random-promoter-expression-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite table and readout.}
This BioReality record concerns a finite random-promoter expression panel rather than a universal law of cis regulation.  The observed object is a designed promoter table in which synthetic promoter sequences are paired with expression measurements and then filtered to a high-quality analysis surface.  In the verified run for the random-promoter TF-motif expression boundary, the designed table contained $75111$ promoters, of which $8027$ entered the high-quality set.  The analysis used a subsample of $2500$ high-quality rows with subsample seed $20260239$, and the permutation layer used $200$ actual permutations.  The recorded runtime was $530.4038379192352$ seconds.  These numbers are part of the finite witness for this section: the claim is not that promoter grammar has been solved in general, but that this particular finite panel is large enough to state a bounded expression-readout question with an explicit sampling and permutation envelope.

\paragraph{Coordinate being tested.}
The relevant coordinate is a cis-regulatory grammar statistic extracted from random promoter sequence rows and tested against a matched expression readout.  A row is admissible only when the promoter sequence, motif or grammar features, expression value, and analysis controls inhabit the same finite record.  The statistic is therefore not a free-standing motif story.  It has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{expr}}(G \mid Z)
  &= \operatorname{score}\bigl(\mathrm{expression};\, G, Z\bigr)
     - \operatorname{score}\bigl(\mathrm{expression};\, Z\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $G$ denotes the promoter-grammar coordinate and $Z$ denotes the finite control surface used for the run.  A positive entry means only that the coordinate improves the held-out or permutation-calibrated expression readout inside this table.  It does not identify a biochemical mechanism by itself, and it does not license claims about translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, organismal function, phenotype, or universality.

\paragraph{Reality boundary for the deBoer-style promoter panel.}
The deBoer random-promoter panel supplies a direct contact with expression-level measurements for designed cis-regulatory sequences.  The verified finite counts give the chapter its empirical scale: $75111$ designed promoters, $8027$ high-quality rows, $2500$ rows in the analysis subsample, and $200$ permutations.  The admissible statement is therefore a row-bounded expression-survival statement: a promoter grammar coordinate may be reported only as surviving within the filtered table and only relative to the declared controls and permutation design.  The finite run does not turn motif presence into a universal promoter grammar.  It also does not convert a sequence coordinate into translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein output, folded structure, physical feasibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, or a general biological law.

\paragraph{Comparison with a separate translation-conditioned finite row.}
A second verified finite row helps delimit the scope.  In the translation-conditioned structural-order boundary, the joined structural-order counts were $3412$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}; that run used $5$ folds and required a minimum joined size of $300$.  Its aggregate full-control $\Delta R^2$ values were positive in both organisms, with minimum $0.002688284523492457$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, and sign-flip probability $0.0029296875$ for being greater than zero.  Those numbers belong to a different readout layer and are not evidence for the random-promoter expression claim.  They are cited here only to make the separation explicit: an expression-panel survival result and a translation-conditioned structural-order result are distinct finite-row contacts, and neither should be silently promoted into the other.

\paragraph{Admissible conclusion.}
The random-promoter record is ready as a bounded expression-boundary claim.  Its support is the finite designed-promoter table and the verified analysis envelope:
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{designed}} &= 75111,\\
N_{\mathrm{HQ}} &= 8027,\\
N_{\mathrm{analysis}} &= 2500,\\
N_{\mathrm{perm}} &= 200.
\end{aligned}
$$
Within that envelope, it is meaningful to ask whether a cis-regulatory grammar coordinate improves expression prediction or classification after the declared controls.  Outside that envelope, no downstream biological layer follows automatically.  In particular, the chapter makes no claim that promoter grammar determines translation, folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, mechanism, or universality.  Any such claim would require a separate finite table with same-row downstream measurements and its own controlled survival statistic.
