\subsection{A position-resolved f\_3-stress boundary for translation readout}
\label{sec:h3-cross-layer-relation-cis-regulatory-grammar-random-promoter-tf-motif-expression-boundary-deboer2020}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record isolates a finite table whose rows are coding-sequence windows, not whole genes in abstraction and not proteins after translation.  A row is admissible for this chapter only when it carries an $f_3$-stress residual coordinate after the amino-acid quotient, local synonymous-codon counts, a bounded wobble-weight context, a same-window ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout, a matched mRNA control, and amino-acid or position-bin controls.  The proposed survival entry is therefore not a free association between codon geometry and biological outcome.  It is the controlled finite-row statistic
$$
\begin{aligned}
  S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{position}}\mid Z) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_{\mathrm{position}}$ is the matched position-resolved translation readout and $Z$ includes amino-acid composition, position-bin structure, GC3, CDS length, M-density, matched mRNA abundance, codon-count controls, rank sufficiency, coverage limits, mappability limits, and simpler-baseline ablations.  The coordinate is allowed to survive only if it improves held-out prediction after these controls.  Without that same-row statistic the object remains a boundary request, not a translation claim.

\paragraph{Observed finite evidence attached to the packet.}
The present finite audit supplies one experimentally verified promoter-expression run and two linked cross-layer status records.  The promoter-expression run has experiment id $8d1d1ac78ab7$ and reports $75111$ designed sequences, $8027$ high-quality rows, an analysis subsample of $2500$ high-quality rows, subsample seed $20260239$, $200$ actual permutations, and runtime $530.4038379192352$ seconds.  These values certify that the associated De Boer-style random-promoter grammar surface has a concrete finite-row analysis footprint, but they do not by themselves supply the CDS-window ribosome-footprint matrix required for this chapter's $f_3$-stress translation boundary.

The linked translation-conditioned structural-order record gives a separate finite signal.  It has two joined organisms, with $3412$ joined structural-order rows for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 and $5119$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The five-fold analysis reports a weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ of $0.004764742175882462$, organism-level full-control deltas with minimum $0.002688284523492457$, median $0.005284160876443678$, mean $0.005284160876443678$, maximum $0.007880037229394898$, and $2$ positive out of $2$ organisms.  The corresponding permuted full-control deltas are negative in both organisms, with minimum $-0.003463794378680385$, median $-0.00212186632044159$, mean $-0.00212186632044159$, and maximum $-0.000779938262202795$.  The reported sign-flip probability for being greater than zero is $0.0029296875$.  This linked result supports the general possibility that controlled translation-conditioned coordinates can cross into a downstream layer, but it does not replace the position-resolved ribosome-occupancy statistic demanded here.

\paragraph{Rank and asymmetry condition.}
The $f_3$ coordinate is admitted only under a broken wobble-weight symmetry.  The chapter therefore distinguishes the residual coordinate $f_3$ from a readout-weighted counterpart $f_3$ under codon-specific weights.  In finite-row form the asymmetry requirement is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  w_c \neq w_c^{f_3}
\end{aligned}
$$
for the relevant codon context, together with enough rank after amino-acid and position controls to prevent the statistic from being a saturated fit.  A modeled tAI or stAI surface may define a bounded codon-adaptation covariate, but it is not observed ribosome occupancy.  A contact name for position-resolved ribosome occupancy identifies the needed measurement class, but it is not itself the joined matrix.  The same distinction applies to the mRNA control: transcript abundance is a denominator or covariate for translation-efficiency residualization, not a translation-layer observation by itself.

\paragraph{Local decision rule.}
The finite decision rule is intentionally narrow.  The packet passes only as a cross-layer boundary if the same organism, same CDS, and same local window contain ordered codon coordinates, the $f_3$ residual, bounded wobble weights, matched mRNA abundance, and codon-indexed ribosome-footprint occupancy or an explicitly bounded translation-efficiency readout.  The supported claim then has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{held\ out}}(f_3;T_{\mathrm{position}},Z) > 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the improvement is computed against amino-acid, position, codon-usage, mRNA, GC3, length, M-density, rank, coverage, mappability, and simpler-baseline controls.  Failure of this inequality blocks the translation-readout survival statement for this coordinate without saying that $f_3$ has no biological relevance in any other assay.

\paragraph{What the record does not license.}
The verified promoter-expression counts, the finite-state dwell-transducer pass status, and the linked structural-order deltas do not supply the missing position-resolved CDS-window ribosome rows for this packet.  Consequently this chapter does not infer protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, mechanism, adaptation, causation, or a universal biological law from the $f_3$-stress coordinate.  It also does not treat review eligibility, contact routing, modeled wobble weights, tAI or stAI weights, or symbolic survival notation as measured translation realization.  Any later claim at the protein, structure, physical, functional, phenotypic, mechanistic, or universal level must attach its own layer-matched finite rows and must pass its own controlled statistic after the translation-readout step has first survived.
