\subsection{Codon-Pair Non-Identity for the Fourth Residual Mechanism}\label{sec:bior- q6-codon-pair-fourth-mechanism}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite claim.} This record tests whether the fourth residual mechanism attached to the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual surface is explained by codon-pair context.  The finite carrier is a coding-sequence-by-organism residual coordinate table with $41$ synonymous design columns, restricted to organisms with at least $500$ proteins and evaluated by $5$ folds.  The powered codon-pair assay has run identifier $\mathrm{b519b5ebd34d}$ and deterministic seed $\mathrm{sha256:f611d16ff9b000a2701e43b49cb06c63bd5d14cb7892cc21260545a756923f33}$.  Its codon-pair null used $50$ shuffled controls.  The recorded conclusion is $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$, so this chapter does not identify codon-pair preference as the fourth mechanism.

\paragraph{Residual-coordinate setting.} The ambient $f_3$ saturation row fixes the codon support on which the later residual tests are interpreted.  The finite row records

Thus $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$ are in $M$ but not in the saturated $R$ row, while the recorded $f_3$ boundary is empty.  The codon-pair assay is therefore not a free-standing codon-topology assertion; it is read inside a finite residual surface where amino-acid projection, synonymous imbalance, and the third-position $f_3$ involution have already fixed the coordinate grammar.

\paragraph{Observed powered verdict.} The codon-pair test passed as a computation but returned a negative identity call.  In compact form, the row says
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{folds} &= 5,\\
\mathrm{min\ proteins\ per\ organism} &= 500,\\
\mathrm{synonymous\ columns} &= 41,\\
\mathrm{shuffle\ nulls} &= 50,\\
\mathrm{codon\ pair\ identity} &= \mathrm{false}.
\end{aligned}$$
This is a substantive negative result: codon-pair context is not the named fourth mechanism under the declared finite assay.  It does not say that codon pairs are irrelevant to translation in general, nor that all codon-pair effects vanish in every organism.  It says only that the residual fourth-axis signal captured in this $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ powered row is not explained by the codon-pair mechanism tested here.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding finite spectrum.} The negative codon-pair verdict sits inside a larger rank-powered residual spectrum.  A three-axis sufficiency run over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, again using $5$ folds, $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous columns, concluded $\mathrm{fourth\_mechanism\_present}$.  The axis-strength spectrum over the same $11$ computed organisms records $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ as the leading average direction and as the dominant per-organism direction in all $11$ computed cases; the same summary reports domain counts $n_{\mathrm{archaea}}=1$, $n_{\mathrm{bacteria}}=2$, $n_{\mathrm{eukaryote}}=8$, and the largest GC3 rank effect as $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$.  The third-axis identity row independently reports alignment to usage frequency within family, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$, while the mechanism row says the GC-orthogonalized $d_{\perp}$ retains significant cross-species alignment and held-out $R^2$.  These facts make the codon-pair row interpretable as a discriminant inside a nontrivial residual spectrum: a fourth mechanism is present in the finite assay family, but the codon-pair candidate is not its identity.

\paragraph{Comparison with neighboring mechanism tests.} The codon-pair result is not isolated.  A yeast half-life assay classified the mRNA-stability candidate as $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ had low alignment with the abundance-associated $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates.  A translation-accuracy assay over $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms, with $200$ permutations, concluded $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$.  An elongation-dwell assay in $\mathrm{saccharomyces\_cerevisiae}$ returned $\mathrm{partial}$.  The finite picture is therefore asymmetric: the fourth axis is not dissolved by three-axis closure, is not classified as codon-pair preference, is not classified as mRNA stability in the recorded half-life assay, is not classified as translation accuracy in the recorded powered assay, and has only partial contact with the elongation-dwell candidate.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.} The associated $f_3$-stress conjecture remains stricter than the codon-pair rejection.  After amino-acid quotienting, an $f_3$-paired synonymous imbalance may be called a translation-layer distinction only when a bounded translation readout is present and nondegenerate: tAI or stAI as a modelled weight surface, or ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency as a measured readout, with amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA controls in the design.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface is a null boundary, not survival evidence.  Likewise, a wobble-weight asymmetry $w_c \ne w_{c^{f_3}}$ can motivate a test, but it is not by itself a measured translation outcome.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.} The only ready claim in this chapter is the negative mechanism identity: the powered finite row supports $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$ for the tested fourth residual mechanism.  No translation realization, ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, localization, stability, molecular function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from this row alone.  Any such statement would require its own layer-matched contact and its own controlled statistic.  In particular, the codon-pair rejection does not authorize a direct residual-to-protein interpretation, and the $f_3$-stress translation conjecture remains blocked until same-organism and same-CDS codon, wobble-weight, translation-readout, and matched mRNA rows yield a positive nondegenerate survival entry under the declared controls.
