\subsection{Codon-pair refusal for the fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ mechanism}
\label{sec:b-star-q6-codon-pair-fourth-mechanism-refusal}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is the finite coding-sequence surface obtained after amino-acid projection, with rows indexed by organisms and by matched synonymous-design coordinates.  The tested coordinate is the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual direction associated with $f_3$ stress, and the proposed fourth-mechanism candidate is a codon-pair explanation.  The finite assay for this candidate used $41$ synonymous design columns, $5$ folds, a minimum of $500$ proteins per organism, and $50$ shuffle-null replicates.  Its run identifier is $\mathrm{b519b5ebd34d}$, and the recorded status is passed with conclusion $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$.

\paragraph{Saturation coordinates.}
The $f_3$ boundary is not an arbitrary biological label; it is tied to an explicit finite codon surface.  The closed set $M=\operatorname{Med}_R\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ contains
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $f_3$ saturation of $R$ removes the two $AC$ codons and leaves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded $f_3$ boundary set is empty.  Thus the finite surface is already a closed residual surface before any codon-pair mechanism is assigned to the remaining fourth direction.

\paragraph{Negative codon-pair call.}
The codon-pair mechanism test asks whether the fourth residual component is better understood as a pair-context signal after the amino-acid, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, and simpler synonymous baselines have been accounted for.  The powered finite row gives a negative answer: the fourth-mechanism candidate is classified as $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$.  In symbols, if $d_4$ denotes the residual fourth direction and $C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ denotes the codon-pair candidate statistic, the accepted finite reading is not
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_4 \equiv C_{\mathrm{pair}},
\end{aligned}
$$
but rather a refusal of that identification under the declared design:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Id}(d_4,C_{\mathrm{pair}})=\mathrm{false}
\end{aligned}
$$
inside the assayed finite panel.  This is a mechanism-identification refusal, not a denial that codon-pair effects can exist elsewhere.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding residual spectrum.}
The codon-pair refusal is consistent with the broader finite spectrum.  A separate powered spectrum computed $11$ organisms out of $16$ requested organisms and found $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ leading on average, with per-organism dominant counts $\{\mathrm{d\_resid4}:11\}$; the domain means were explicitly descriptive under the imbalanced domain counts $\{\mathrm{archaea}:1,\mathrm{bacteria}:2,\mathrm{eukaryote}:8\}$.  The largest GC3 rank effect in that spectrum was small, with $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ rank correlation $\rho=0.136$.  The three-axis sufficiency assay, also on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms with $41$ synonymous design columns and $5$ folds, concluded that a fourth mechanism is present.  The codon-pair test therefore does not erase the fourth direction; it removes one proposed identity for it.

\paragraph{Comparison with other mechanism filters.}
Other finite filters narrow the same fourth-direction question.  The mRNA-stability assay concluded $\mathrm{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because the yeast $d_{\mathrm{opt\_halflife}}$ direction had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under its fixed gates.  The translation-accuracy assay, run on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested organisms with $200$ permutations and $41$ synonymous design columns, concluded $\mathrm{not\_accuracy}$.  The elongation-dwell assay in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} concluded $\mathrm{partial}$.  These facts leave a finite pattern: the fourth component is detected as a residual direction, but codon-pair context, mRNA stability, and translation accuracy are not accepted as its dominant identity in the recorded tests, while elongation dwell remains only partially aligned.

\paragraph{Translation-readout boundary.}
The cross-layer reading is deliberately bounded.  The codon-pair refusal concerns the identity of a residual mechanism candidate on the finite synonymous surface.  It does not by itself establish a measured translation-efficiency survival row, a ribosome-occupancy effect, a protein-abundance effect, folded structure, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or a universal biological law.  Such claims require a separate organism-matched contact and a non-vacuous controlled statistic.  In particular, a positive statement about $f_3$-stress translation survival would require a controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-}\mathrm{stress},T_j}>0$ for a bounded translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency, after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, $M$-density, mRNA, rank, and simpler-baseline controls.  The present record supplies a negative codon-pair mechanism call, not that downstream survival claim.

\paragraph{Scientific reading.}
The finite conclusion is therefore sharp but modest.  The $B^*_{Q6}$ fourth residual direction survives the surrounding residual-spectrum logic, yet the powered codon-pair assay classifies the candidate mechanism as $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$.  The result should be used as an exclusion constraint in the mechanism search: any later interpretation of the fourth direction must explain why the $41$-column, $5$-fold, $50$-shuffle codon-pair assay fails to identify it, while remaining compatible with the observed fourth-mechanism presence and with the partial elongation-dwell signal.  No stronger biological promotion follows from this finite row alone.
