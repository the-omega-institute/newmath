\paragraph{Tested codon-pair identity.}
The powered codon-pair assay was run as experiment \texttt{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_codon\_pair\_powered} with run id \texttt{b519b5ebd34d}.  It used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, used $41$ synonymous design columns, and compared the observed codon-pair direction against $50$ shuffle nulls under deterministic seed \texttt{sha256:f611d16ff9b000a2701e43b49cb06c63bd5d14cb7892cc21260545a756923f33}.  The finite verdict of that row is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{classification}(B^{\ast}_{Q6},\mathrm{codon\ pair}) &= \mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, within this assay, codon-pair context is excluded as the identity of the powered fourth mechanism.  This is a negative mechanism-identification result, not a deletion of the residual coordinate and not a denial that codon-pair effects may exist elsewhere.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding finite spectrum.}
The refusal is sharpened by the other finite rows. The same finite panel supplies a useful contrast class. \paragraph{Boundary of what follows.}

It does not assert a positive translation survival score, does not identify the remaining fourth mechanism, and does not establish translation realization unless a separate same-organism or same-CDS translation contact supplies a nondegenerate controlled readout. Each of those layers requires its own matched contact and its own finite statistic; the codon-pair row is a scoped negative identity result inside the residual-coordinate program.
