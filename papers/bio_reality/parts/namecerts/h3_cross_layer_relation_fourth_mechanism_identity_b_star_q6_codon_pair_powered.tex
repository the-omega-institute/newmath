\subsection{Codon-pair refusal for the fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ mechanism}
\label{sec:biorality-q6-codon-pair-fourth-mechanism-refusal}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object tested here is the finite coding-sequence residual surface restricted to the $f_3$-stress coordinate of $B^*_{Q6}$ and to translation-adjacent rows for which codon usage, synonymous design columns, and organism-level controls can be placed in the same assay.  The assay is not a free interpretation of codon syntax.  It asks whether the residual direction previously isolated as a fourth non-tRNA component is better explained by codon-pair structure.  The powered codon-pair experiment used $5$ folds, required at least $500$ proteins per organism, used $41$ synonymous design columns, and compared the learned signal against $50$ shuffle-null realizations.  Its recorded status is passed, and its finite conclusion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{FourthMechanism}_{Q6,f_3} &\neq \operatorname{CodonPair},\\
\operatorname{conclusion} &= \text{not\_codon\_pair}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the codon-pair route is excluded as the dominant identity of this particular fourth mechanism under the declared finite design.  The statement is deliberately narrower than saying that codon-pair effects do not exist in biology.  It says only that the tested $B^*_{Q6}$ fourth residual mechanism is not identified with the codon-pair axis in this controlled panel.

\paragraph{The $f_3$ boundary being tested.}
The coordinate is anchored by the finite $f_3$ saturation record.  The mediated $R$ union with the two $ACA/ACG$ boundary codons gives the $20$-codon set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&\qquad UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
After $f_3$ saturation, the recorded set removes $ACA$ and $ACG$ and leaves the $18$-codon surface
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{AAA,AAG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU,\\
&\qquad UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same finite record reports an empty $f_3$ boundary, $\partial_{f_3}=\varnothing$.  Consequently the codon-pair test is not being used to repair a missing boundary row.  It is a mechanism-identity test on a saturated residual coordinate: if codon-pair adjacency were the fourth mechanism, the codon-pair design should absorb the fourth residual direction after the amino-acid projection and the declared sequence controls.

\paragraph{Observed finite decision.}
The codon-pair mechanism assay returned status passed with experiment id $\text{b\_star\_q6\_fourth\_mechanism\_codon\_pair\_powered}$ and run id $\text{b519b5ebd34d}$.  The finite decision is a refusal, not a promotion.  In the notation of the assay, let $d_4$ denote the fourth residual direction isolated after the lower-dimensional $B^*_{Q6}$ axes have been accounted for, and let $C_{pair}$ denote the codon-pair design family generated by the admissible synonymous rows.  The recorded result is read as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Align}(d_4,C_{pair}\mid Z) &\text{ does not pass the identity gate},\\
Z &= \{\text{amino-acid projection},\text{ organism},\text{ GC3},\text{ length},\text{ M-density},\text{ simpler codon baselines}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $50$ shuffle-null count matters because the tested alternative is not merely that codon pairs have nonzero descriptive content.  The tested alternative is that codon-pair structure is the stable identity of the residual fourth mechanism.  The assay rejects that identity in the available finite row.

\paragraph{Relation to the surrounding $Q6$ spectrum.}
The refusal is coherent with the broader powered record.  The optimality-axis sufficiency assay, run on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested with $5$ folds, concluded that a fourth mechanism is present after the three-axis account.  The axis-strength spectrum on the same $11$ computed organisms found that captured spectra are led on average by $d_{resid4}$, with per-organism dominant counts reported as $\{d_{resid4}:11\}$; its largest GC3 rank effect was only $\rho=0.136$ for $d_{usage}$.  The third-axis identity assay identified $d_{\perp}$ with usage frequency within family at mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$, while the third-axis mechanism assay recorded selection-not-GC and not-wobble-clustered.  Separately, the universal residual optimality assay named $f_3$ translation selection as the dominant non-tRNA residual axis across $11$ computed organisms.  These finite entries make the codon-pair conclusion more specific: the fourth mechanism is not absent, and the residual spectrum is not being collapsed into GC or a trivial codon-usage artifact; rather, codon-pair identity fails as the tested explanation for this fourth residual component.

\paragraph{Mechanism exclusions.}
The codon-pair refusal belongs to a small family of fourth-mechanism identity tests.  The mRNA-stability assay returned $\text{fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability}$ because yeast $d_{opt\_halflife}$ had low alignment with abundance $d_{resid4}$ under fixed gates.  The translation-accuracy assay returned $\text{not\_accuracy}$ on $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, using $5$ folds, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $200$ permutations.  The elongation-dwell assay in $\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}$ returned $\text{partial}$ rather than a complete identity.  The codon-pair assay therefore supplies one negative identity decision inside a larger mechanism search: codon-pair structure is not the fourth mechanism; mRNA stability and translation accuracy are also not dominant explanations in their respective tests; elongation dwell remains only partial in the recorded yeast row.

\paragraph{Translation readout gate.}
This chapter does not infer a protein, phenotype, structural, or universal claim from the codon-pair refusal.  A bounded translation statement would require a non-vacuous survival entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ for a translation readout such as tAI, stAI, ribosome occupancy, or translation efficiency after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, M-density, matched mRNA, and simpler-baseline controls.  A rank-degenerate all-$1.0$ surface on a three-organism panel is not survival; it is a null boundary caused by insufficient rank.  Likewise, modeled adaptation weights can serve as bounded translation-layer annotations, but they are not observed ribosome occupancy and they are not protein output.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim rule.}
The finite conclusion is only that the powered codon-pair route is not the identity of the tested fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ mechanism.  No translation realization follows unless a separate translation contact supplies a controlled positive row.  No folded structure, physical admissibility, localization, stability, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism in the biochemical sense, or universal biological law follows from this assay.  Those stronger readings would each require their own layer-matched measurements and their own controlled statistics.  The present record keeps the finite mathematical decision and the biological readout boundary separate: $\text{not\_codon\_pair}$ is an identity refusal for the residual mechanism, not a downstream biological promotion.
