\paragraph{Readout boundary.}
The conclusion of this chapter is only an exclusion: the powered finite assay says that the fourth $B^*_{Q6}$ residual mechanism is not identified as codon-pair structure in the tested surface.  It does not say that codon pairs have no biological role in general, and it does not say that the residual coordinate has been realized as translation, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, mechanism, or a universal biological law.  Any such promotion requires a separate layer-matched contact: for translation, organism- and CDS-matched ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency rows with mRNA controls; for protein output, matched proteomics; for structure, localization, stability, function, phenotype, or perturbation, a contact at that corresponding layer.

The locally admissible readback is therefore bounded.  The finite codon-pair test used $5$ folds, a $500$-protein minimum, $41$ synonymous design columns, and $50$ shuffle-null realizations, and it returned $\mathrm{not\_codon\_pair}$.  The $f_3$ bookkeeping row has $20$ codons in $M$, $18$ in $\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)$, and empty $f_3$ boundary.  These numbers support a chapter-level statement that codon-pair identity is rejected for this fourth residual-mechanism assay, while downstream biological interpretations remain unavailable until their own finite contacts and controlled statistics are present.
