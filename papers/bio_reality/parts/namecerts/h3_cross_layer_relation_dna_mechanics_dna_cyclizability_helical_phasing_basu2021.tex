\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP-occupancy gate}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of transcript or CDS rows carrying four kinds of row-local information: a synonymous residual coordinate label $q \in B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity, a same-row translation readout $T$, an RNA half-life readout $H$, and a measured occupancy readout $O$ for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window. The intended row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
r=(g, c, q, T, A, H, O, L, W, B, Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $g$ is the gene or transcript key, $c$ is the coding-window key, $A$ records the amino-acid sequence class, $L$ records target-RBP loss or knockdown state, $W$ records wild-type rescue state, $B$ records binding-defective or non-target rescue controls, and $Z$ denotes the declared control block: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the same-row translation readout. The coordinate label is not itself a biological measurement; it is only the finite partition used to compare high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows after the amino-acid quotient.

\paragraph{Observed finite material.}
The available finite facts do not yet contain the joined RNA half-life--RBP perturbation panel required for a positive bridge. The data snapshot for the DNA-mechanics claim key reports a completed run, $\mathrm{runtime}=196.505904\,\mathrm{s}$, but no row count, $p$-value, phase list, residual matrix, or half-life statistic is attached to that key. A separate RBP-binding finite run supplies an RBP-binding-scale calibration surface with $900$ total peaks, $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, and $\mathrm{runtime}=223.648043\,\mathrm{s}$. A separate translation finite run supplies a translation-scale readout with $n=6494$ rows and $\mathrm{runtime}=9.469\,\mathrm{s}$. These two auxiliary surfaces show that finite RBP and translation contacts exist elsewhere in the BioReality table, but they are not the same organism, same transcript, same editable-window rows as the present RNA half-life bridge. Thus they may bound the vocabulary of admissible contacts, not certify the bridge.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
A positive bridge would require a finite statistic comparing high-coordinate and low-coordinate windows after conditioning on $Z$ and $T$, followed by an RBP-specific intervention check. One admissible form is a signed residual half-life contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H
  &= \mathbb{E}[H \mid q \in Q_{\mathrm{high}}, Z, T]
   - \mathbb{E}[H \mid q \in Q_{\mathrm{low}}, Z, T],\\
\Delta_O
  &= \mathbb{E}[O \mid q \in Q_{\mathrm{high}}, Z, T]
   - \mathbb{E}[O \mid q \in Q_{\mathrm{low}}, Z, T].
\end{aligned}
$$
The bridge is not merely $\Delta_H \neq 0$. It also requires the effect to track experimentally decoupled local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, to collapse under target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and to fail under binding-defective or non-target rescue. In row language, the same finite panel must distinguish
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{target\ loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{baseline}},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{non\ target}} &\approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the approximation thresholds declared before inspecting the outcome. Without these intervention rows, a half-life association remains an RNA-stability association and not an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H \perp q \mid A, T, Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
A stricter RBP-bridge null keeps any observed half-life residual outside the mechanism class unless the same panel also carries an occupancy displacement, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and binding-defective or non-target rescue failure. This prevents a motif-density-only model, RNA-structure-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, transcript-abundance model, GC/dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model from being counted as support for the RBP bridge.

\paragraph{Readback boundary.}
The current finite evidence supports only a bounded design statement: the proposed row type is testable once the same-window half-life, translation, abundance, RBP occupancy, and perturbation-rescue fields are joined. The DNA-mechanics run contributes only the fact that the named claim key has a completed computation with $196.505904\,\mathrm{s}$ runtime; it does not contribute R lists, M lists, $\lambda$ values, $p$-values, module classification, hit rate, or row counts for this bridge. The RBP-binding run contributes $900$ peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, but not measured occupancy for the same editable windows. The translation run contributes $6494$ rows, but not half-life or rescue behavior. Therefore the present chapter records a finite, falsifiable gate rather than a positive biological result.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No RNA half-life difference may be interpreted as a decay-factor RBP mechanism unless measured local RBP occupancy changes in the prespecified window, target-RBP loss collapses the effect, wild-type rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it. No protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causal mechanism, or universal biological law follows from the coordinate label, codon-window geometry, half-life association, translation contact name, or RBP contact name. Each such higher layer requires its own finite contact and its own declared statistic.
