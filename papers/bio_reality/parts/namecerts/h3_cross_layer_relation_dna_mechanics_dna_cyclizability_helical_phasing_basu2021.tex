\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as an RBP-bridge boundary}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
The record studied here is the finite cross-layer packet with identifier \texttt{q6.translation-conditioned.rna-half-life-rbp-bridge.cross-layer}. Its rows are matched transcript or coding-sequence entries carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for abundance and transcript state. The intended positive readback is deliberately narrow: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be called an RBP bridge only when the same finite rows show local decay-factor RBP occupancy, collapse of the coordinate effect after target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and failure under binding-defective or non-target rescue. Without that four-part occupancy and perturbation pattern, the coordinate remains a bounded descriptor of a finite RNA-stability association, not a mechanism.

The available memory contains bounded readout surfaces rather than the full RBP perturbation panel. The RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped gene or transcript rows with curated half-life estimates, keyed before any same-window RBP statistic is computed. The matched mRNA contact supplies the transcription-layer denominator for translation-efficiency residualization, and the ribosome-profiling contact supplies the translation-readout route through per-CDS ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency. These contacts are necessary for the requested panel, but they do not by themselves contain measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, or binding-defective control arms. \paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after controlling the declared covariates. In row notation, let $q_i$ denote the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ measured target-RBP occupancy, and $Z_i$ the finite control block containing transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, batch, and simpler-baseline terms. The positive bridge is not the bare contrast $q_i \mapsto H_i$. It requires a finite witness of the following form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
& q_i \not\perp H_i \mid T_i,Z_i, \\
& q_i \not\perp O_i \mid T_i,Z_i, \\
& \Delta(q_i,H_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss}, \\
& \Delta(q_i,H_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \text{ returns under wild-type rescue}, \\
& \Delta(q_i,H_i \mid T_i,Z_i) \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If the first line holds but the occupancy and perturbation lines fail, the result is only a finite RNA-stability association. If occupancy changes without coordinate/occupancy decoupling, the packet still does not identify an RBP bridge. If target-RBP loss has an effect but wild-type rescue and binding-defective or non-target controls are absent, the packet remains below the mechanism boundary.

\paragraph{Readback discipline.}
The readback therefore has a strict ordering. First, the same finite rows must establish the translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual, using matched mRNA abundance and the same-row translation readout rather than transcript abundance alone. Second, the same window must carry measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RBP; the coordinate is not allowed to choose an RBP after seeing the outcome. Third, perturbation and rescue must separate binding-competent target-RBP action from non-target and binding-defective controls. Only after those steps can the finite statistic be called a bounded RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge. The attached contacts now support only the earlier boundaries: RNA half-life readout, translation-readout routing, and abundance control.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from an RNA half-life difference alone. No RNA decay mechanism follows from the presence of a same-row translation readout. No measured RBP bridge follows from an RNA half-life contact unless local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are present in the same finite panel. Codon-window geometry and the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label do not constitute curated biological evidence by themselves. This chapter also makes no claim about protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, system phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. Each of those layers would require its own layer-matched contact and its own finite statistic.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter is ready as a bounded BioReality boundary record, not as a positive RBP-mechanism report. The finite memory is sufficient to state the carrier, controls, null, and failure modes, and it cites audited neighboring scalar facts showing the required evidential standard. The positive biological bridge remains unlicensed until a same-organism, same-window panel supplies $B^{*}_{Q6}$ labels, translation readout, mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, coordinate/occupancy decoupling, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and the declared finite RBP-decay half-life statistic.
