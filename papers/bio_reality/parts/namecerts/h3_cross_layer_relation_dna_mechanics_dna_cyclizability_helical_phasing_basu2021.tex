\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel whose rows are transcript or coding-sequence windows equipped with a synonymous residual coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  A row is admissible only when the coordinate label, the same-row translation readout, the matched mRNA abundance control, the RNA half-life readout, and the local decay-factor RBP occupancy measurement are all keyed to the same biological unit.  The intended positive comparison is not a raw half-life contrast.  It is the comparison between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the mandatory translation readout.

\paragraph{Observed finite audit surface.}
The present finite audit supplies a run record for the chapter id with runtime $196.505904$ seconds, but it does not yet supply the matched RNA-window table needed for a positive RBP-mediated half-life claim.  The neighboring BioReality audit surfaces show that the surrounding finite-row machinery is capable of carrying scalar biological readbacks: the RBNS/eCLIP RBP-binding surface reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases with runtime $223.648043$ seconds; the mTOR translation readout carries $6494$ rows with runtime $9.469$ seconds; the m6A exon-architecture readout carries $60000$ rows, $49668$ distinct sites, a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$, and cross-condition concordance marked true; the m6Am CROWN-seq surface carries $31886$ rows, $19406$ distinct sites, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, a variable subset of $20942$ rows, and a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at the $0.95$ threshold; the methylation flanking-context surface carries $20000$ probes across $656$ individuals with baseline $R^2=0.0129735$.  These numbers are cited only as finite-row audit context.  They do not replace the missing same-window RBP occupancy, perturbation, or rescue table for this chapter.

\paragraph{Statistic licensed by the record.}
Let $q_i$ denote the residual-coordinate label, $T_i$ the same-row translation readout, $A_i$ the matched transcript-abundance control, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, and $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  Let $Z_i$ collect transcription rate, local RNA-structure score, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and any declared rank-sufficiency covariates.  The bounded test has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i &= \alpha + \beta_Q q_i + \beta_T T_i + \beta_A A_i + \gamma Z_i + \varepsilon_i,\\
O_i &= \eta + \theta_Q q_i + \theta_T T_i + \theta_A A_i + \delta Z_i + \xi_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive bridge would require more than nonzero $\beta_Q$.  The half-life effect must track experimentally decoupled local RBP occupancy, collapse under loss of the target RBP, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Without those rows, $q_i$ may remain a descriptor of a bounded RNA-stability association, but it is not a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Decision boundary.}
The finite contact set currently contains RNA half-life readout routes, mRNA abundance controls, and translation-efficiency routes, but these contacts stop short of the decisive intervention panel.  The same-row translation readout is necessary because half-life associations cannot be promoted to translation-conditioned claims unless the translation boundary is explicitly attached and conditioned.  The matched mRNA abundance control is necessary because abundance alone can otherwise mimic an apparent stability or translation effect.  The RNA half-life contact is necessary because the endpoint is a transcript decay readout.  None of these contacts, alone or in combination, measures same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP depletion, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on the full control set.  Formally, for matched rows inside the admissible panel,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i \in Q_{\mathrm{high}}) \perp H_i \mid T_i,A_i,Z_i
\end{aligned}
$$
up to the declared finite statistic.  A departure from this null is still insufficient for the RBP bridge unless the occupancy and perturbation pattern is present.  A half-life difference with unchanged local RBP occupancy, a half-life difference that survives target-RBP loss, or a half-life difference restored by a binding-defective or non-target rescue remains outside the intended bridge.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
This chapter licenses no protein-output claim, no folding claim, no physical-admissibility claim, no biological-function claim, no phenotype claim, no adaptation claim, no causal mechanism claim, and no universal sequence law.  Codon-window geometry, residual-coordinate labels, translation conditioning, RNA half-life association, and the current gate-passed audit status are not biological mechanism measurements by themselves.  Translation, folding, physical admissibility, function, mechanism, or universality would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own finite statistic.  Likewise, an RNA-structure-only model, motif-density-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, transcript-abundance model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model is not evidence for this RBP bridge unless the prespecified occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern is also observed.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The record is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because it states a precise finite-row object, a computable null, the required bridge pattern, and the current negative boundary.  The chapter should be read as a disciplined boundary certificate: the present evidence supports the shape of the test and the reason for withholding a positive claim, not the existence of a measured decay-factor RBP mechanism.
