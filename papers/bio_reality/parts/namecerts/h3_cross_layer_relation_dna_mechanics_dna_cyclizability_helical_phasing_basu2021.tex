\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP-bridge target}
\label{subsec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record fixes a finite matched transcript/CDS panel whose rows carry four kinds of row-local labels: a synonymous $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and an externally prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy or perturbation readout. The intended biological object is not a naked codon statistic and not an RNA-decay assertion by itself. It is the stricter finite object obtained only when high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels can be compared under the same amino-acid, transcriptional, structural, composition, isoform, abundance, batch, and translation-readout boundary. In the supplied audit table the direct run attached to the DNA-mechanics keyed claim id is present as experiment $\mathrm{f8464f8328f6}$ with runtime $196.505904$ seconds. That run supplies provenance for the row-audit event but does not itself supply RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, rescue, p-values, $R$ lists, $M$ lists, lambda values, hit rates, or module classification for the RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Name-certified comparison.}
The positive comparison would require a finite panel in which each row has
$$
\begin{aligned}
(q_i, T_i, H_i, O_i, Z_i, C_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $Z_i$ is the declared control vector, and $C_i$ records target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control states. The comparison is allowed to ask whether the half-life residual remains aligned with $q_i$ after conditioning on $T_i$ and $Z_i$, and whether that residual follows the experimentally separated RBP-occupancy and perturbation pattern. It is not allowed to use the residual coordinate itself to choose the RBP after the fact. The RBP must be fixed before the bridge statistic is evaluated.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null is exchangeability of high- and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life once the declared controls are attached. In row terms, after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout, the half-life residual should not distinguish the coordinate labels. A half-life difference alone does not defeat this null as an RBP bridge. It can at most define a bounded RNA-stability association unless the same finite rows also show the prespecified RBP occupancy change and the perturbation-rescue pattern.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
The bridge is licensed only by the conjunction of five row-local checks. First, the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual contrast must survive the amino-acid quotient and the declared controls. Second, the RNA half-life effect must be evaluated after the same-row translation readout is attached and conditioned. Third, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP must separate at the editable RNA window in the direction required by the half-life residual. Fourth, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life effect. Fifth, wild-type target-RBP rescue must restore the effect while binding-defective rescue or non-target controls fail to restore it. In shorthand, the admissible positive pattern is
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\leadsto H_i \mid T_i,Z_i,\\
q_i &\leadsto O_i \mid Z_i,\\
\mathrm{loss}(\mathrm{RBP}) &: \Delta H(q_i) \to 0,\\
\mathrm{WT\ rescue} &: \Delta H(q_i) \to \Delta H_{\mathrm{WT}}(q_i),\\
\mathrm{binding\ defective/non\ target} &: \Delta H(q_i) \not\to \Delta H_{\mathrm{WT}}(q_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
The supplied finite data do not yet contain these five checks for the named RNA half-life bridge.

\paragraph{Local exclusion boundary.}
This chapter therefore records a bounded target rather than a positive biological mechanism. The attached RNA half-life, matched mRNA, and ribosome-profiling contacts name relevant readout surfaces, but they do not by themselves produce same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP perturbation, rescue rows, or the declared finite bridge statistic. No claim about translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No claim about folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality follows from the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate, codon/window geometry, translation conditioning, transcript abundance, or half-life association. Such claims would require separate layer-matched contacts and their own finite statistics.

The present readback is therefore negative with respect to the RBP bridge and informative with respect to what must be attached next. None of these numbers is a substitute for the missing same-organism, same-window panel containing $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate labels, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, RNA half-life, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls. The correct current conclusion is a bounded NameCert target with a precise null: high- and low-coordinate labels remain exchangeable for the RBP-mediated RNA half-life bridge until those rows and the declared statistic are supplied.
