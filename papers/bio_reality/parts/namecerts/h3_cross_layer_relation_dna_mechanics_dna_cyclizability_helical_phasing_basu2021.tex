\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With an RBP Occupancy Boundary}\label{sec:bioreality-q6-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of transcript or CDS rows. Each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label of type $B^*_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a matched transcript-abundance control, and fields reserved for a prespecified decay-factor RBP contact. The coordinate label is not treated as a molecular mechanism. It is a row classifier whose only admissible use is to ask whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows remain distinguishable after the declared controls are attached.

The row schema may be written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i = (&q_i, a_i, T_i, H_i, A_i, Z_i, O_i, L_i, W_i, B_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $q_i$ is the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual label, $a_i$ is the amino-acid quotient class, $T_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $H_i$ is RNA half-life, $A_i$ is transcript abundance, $Z_i$ collects transcription-rate, local RNA-structure, motif-density, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, isoform, batch, and length controls, $O_i$ is measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, $L_i$ is the target-RBP loss condition, $W_i$ is wild-type rescue, and $B_i$ records binding-defective or non-target controls. The finite statistic is only meaningful on rows for which these symbols are attached to the same biological window or to an explicitly declared matched unit.

\paragraph{Positive criterion.}
A coordinate-associated half-life contrast is allowed to cross the RNA-stability boundary only when it survives a strict conjunction of finite checks. First, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate classes must differ in translation-readout-conditioned RNA half-life after the amino-acid quotient and all declared baseline controls. Second, the same finite window must show a measured occupancy contrast for the prespecified decay-factor RBP. Third, the half-life contrast must collapse under loss of that target RBP. Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the contrast. Fifth, binding-defective rescue or a non-target rescue must fail to restore it. These clauses are conjunctive, not alternative.

A compact decision rule is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{BridgeReady} ={}& \mathrm{HalfLifeResidual}(q_i,H_i\mid T_i,A_i,Z_i)\\
&{}\wedge \mathrm{OccupancyShift}(q_i,O_i\mid Z_i)\\
&{}\wedge \mathrm{CollapseUnderLoss}(L_i)\\
&{}\wedge \mathrm{ReturnUnderWildTypeRescue}(W_i)\\
&{}\wedge \neg\mathrm{ReturnUnderBindingDefectiveOrNonTarget}(B_i).
\end{aligned}
$$
If any term in this conjunction is absent or false, the row set remains a bounded RNA half-life association or a bounded coordinate descriptor, not an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null says that, after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the mandatory same-row translation readout, high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. In symbols, for the finite joined panel $P$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i \perp q_i \mid (a_i,T_i,A_i,Z_i)
\end{aligned}
$$
unless the observed residual is accompanied by the measured occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and failed binding-defective or non-target rescue pattern described above. A half-life difference without that pattern is not discarded; it is classified more narrowly as a finite RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Boundary against over-reading.}
The coordinate label, codon-window geometry, translation conditioning, and review eligibility do not by themselves observe translation, RBP occupancy, RNA decay mechanism, protein output, folding, localization, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. A ribosome-profiling contact can condition on translation, but it is not an RNA half-life assay. A matched mRNA contact can separate transcription-layer abundance from translation efficiency, but it is not decay-factor occupancy. An RNA half-life contact can measure stability, but it is not a target-RBP perturbation and rescue panel. The bridge is therefore licensed only by a same-organism, same-window finite panel containing the residual coordinate, translation readout, RNA half-life, local measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls.

The admissible conclusion is narrower: a $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate can be tested against translation-conditioned RNA half-life, but promotion to an RBP-mediated cross-layer bridge requires the full measured occupancy, loss, rescue, and negative-control pattern on the same finite matched panel.
