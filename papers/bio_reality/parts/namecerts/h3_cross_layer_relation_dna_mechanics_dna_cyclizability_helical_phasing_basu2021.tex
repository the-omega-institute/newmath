\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP bridge}
\label{sec:bioreality-q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record is a finite matched-panel claim about coding transcripts.  Each row must carry a synonymous residual-coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a measured local occupancy value for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls for transcription rate, abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, amino-acid composition, and batch.  The coordinate comparison is only between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels after the amino-acid sequence has been held fixed.  The intended biological question is therefore not whether codon geometry alone predicts RNA stability, but whether a bounded residual-coordinate half-life difference remains attached to a separately measured decay-factor RBP contact after translation and transcriptional controls have been placed on the same finite row.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
Let $q_i$ denote the high-versus-low residual-coordinate label for transcript row $i$, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $H_i$ be the RNA half-life residual after transcription and abundance controls, and let $O_i$ be measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP at the editable RNA window.  A positive bridge requires a finite statistic of the following form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
& q_i \longmapsto H_i \mid T_i,\ Z_i,\\
& q_i \longmapsto O_i \mid T_i,\ Z_i,\\
& \Delta_H(q_i) \text{ collapses under target-RBP loss},\\
& \Delta_H(q_i) \text{ returns under wild-type target-RBP rescue},\\
& \Delta_H(q_i) \text{ does not return under binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $Z_i$ is the declared control bundle: transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, amino-acid composition, and batch.  The same-row translation term is a boundary condition, not a mechanism by itself.  It prevents an RNA half-life association from being promoted to a translation-realization claim unless the row actually contains the translation readout and the half-life residual has been conditioned on it.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null states that high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels are exchangeable with respect to translation-conditioned RNA half-life once $Z_i$ and $T_i$ are fixed.  A half-life difference alone is therefore insufficient.  Even a coordinate-associated occupancy difference is insufficient if the RBP was selected after seeing the coordinate effect, if occupancy is not measured at the local editable RNA window, if coordinate and occupancy are not experimentally decoupled, or if target-RBP loss and rescue rows are absent.  The bridge is only licensed when occupancy, loss, wild-type rescue, and failed binding-defective or non-target rescue form one finite matched panel.

\paragraph{Readback.}
The intended positive readback has five parts: a translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual, a local occupancy shift for the prespecified decay-factor RBP, loss of the half-life effect after target-RBP depletion or disruption, return of the effect under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and failure of return under binding-defective or non-target controls.  The present finite ledger does not yet contain those five same-window rows.  Consequently, the current chapter records a bounded test design and a strict null boundary rather than a completed RBP-mediated RNA decay result.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation output, ribosome mechanism, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence law follows from this record.  Local RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform state, abundance, GC composition, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and amino-acid composition remain live alternative explanations unless explicitly controlled in the finite statistic.  An RNA half-life association without measured decay-factor RBP occupancy remains only an RNA-stability association.  A measured occupancy association without target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue remains only an occupancy association.  A rescue by a binding-defective construct or a non-target control does not support the bridge.
