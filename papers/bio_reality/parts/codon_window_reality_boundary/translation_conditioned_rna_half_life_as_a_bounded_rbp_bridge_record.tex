\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life as a bounded RBP-bridge record}\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel of coding-transcript rows in which synonymous residual coordinate windows are compared only after quotienting by amino-acid sequence.  A row is admissible for the positive reading only when it carries the same transcript or coding-sequence identifier across four kinds of measurements: a $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, and an experimentally measured local occupancy readout for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window.  The coordinate contrast is therefore not a statement about codon geometry alone.  It is a restricted finite-row comparison between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous residual labels under matched controls for transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout.

\paragraph{Local statistic.}
For an admissible row $i$, let $q_i$ denote the high-versus-low residual coordinate label after the amino-acid quotient, let $t_i$ denote the attached translation readout, let $h_i$ denote the RNA half-life readout, and let $o_i$ denote measured occupancy of the target decay-factor RBP at the declared local RNA window.  The intended statistic is a finite residual comparison of $h_i$ across $q_i$ after conditioning on $t_i$ and the listed transcript-level controls.  The RBP bridge is not licensed by a half-life contrast alone.  It requires the half-life contrast to track experimentally separated local occupancy, to disappear under loss of the target RBP, to return under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and to fail to return under a binding-defective rescue or a non-target control.  In schematic finite-row form, the positive pattern has the shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta h(q\mid t, C) &\neq 0,\\
  \Delta h(q\mid t, C, \text{target RBP loss}) &\approx 0,\\
  \Delta h(q\mid t, C, \text{wild-type rescue}) &\approx \Delta h(q\mid t, C),\\
  \Delta h(q\mid t, C, \text{binding-defective or non-target rescue}) &\approx 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $C$ abbreviates the finite matched controls rather than an unobserved population law.

\paragraph{Reality contact.}
The available RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows carrying curated mRNA half-life estimates or normalized consensus half-life readouts with dataset provenance and join keys.  Its resolution is per-gene or per-transcript RNA half-life before any same-window RBP-decay or $B^*_{Q6}$ statistic is computed.  The known limitations are material: platform, growth condition, cell type, transcript-isoform ambiguity, gene or CDS identifier mapping loss, normalization differences, and the half-life estimation method can all shift the readout.  The present payloads do not themselves provide same-window RBP occupancy or perturbation-and-rescue panels.  A matched mRNA abundance control can separate translation-layer signal from transcription-layer signal through a translation-efficiency residual, and ribosome-profiling data can supply a same-condition translation readout, but neither of those contacts measures local RBP occupancy or rescue behavior.

\paragraph{Concrete checked scalar anchor.}
The only supplied checked finite scalar block belongs to the linked cross-layer methylation-flanking-context run `bd781331dc42`, with $656$ individuals, $20000$ probes, baseline $R^2 = 0.0129735$, no cross-cohort value reported, and recorded runtime $0.0$ seconds.  These numbers do not measure the RNA half-life RBP bridge.  They are cited here only as an available finite-row cross-layer scalar anchor in the same audit payload.  They cannot substitute for the missing row counts, effect sizes, local RBP occupancy contrasts, target-RBP loss measurements, rescue measurements, p-values, hit rates, module classifications, $R$ lists, $M$ lists, or $\lambda$ values for this RNA-decay claim.

\paragraph{Null reading.}
The local null is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and same-row translation readout.  If a half-life difference remains but measured local target-RBP occupancy is unchanged, or if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it, the finite rows may still support a bounded RNA-stability association.  They do not support the stated RBP bridge.  Likewise, restoration by a binding-defective construct or by a non-target control points away from the declared local target-RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary of interpretation.}
This chapter licenses only a finite cross-layer RNA-decay readback under the stated contacts and controls.  RNA half-life by itself is not a translation realization, and a translation-conditioned half-life residual by itself is not a protein-output, folding, physical-admissibility, biological-function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universal sequence-law result.  Modeled tAI or stAI weights, codon-window geometry, residual-coordinate survival, transcript abundance, and protein-abundance associations remain separate surfaces unless directly joined to the relevant measurement layer.  A positive RBP bridge requires measured local occupancy plus target-RBP loss and rescue behavior in the same finite matched design; without that pattern the coordinate remains a descriptor or a bounded RNA-stability association.
