\subsection{Decay-factor RBP occupancy as a bounded RNA half-life boundary}\label{subsec:decay-factor-rbp-rna-half-life-with-translation-boundary-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row carries a synonymous coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, transcription-rate and mRNA-abundance controls, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, batch, target-RBP perturbation, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control, and a declared break condition.  The intended comparison is not a naked contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels.  It is a controlled finite statistic in which the coordinate label may be discussed only after amino-acid composition, transcription, abundance, local structure, motif density, sequence composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch have been carried with the same rows.

\paragraph{Claim shape.}
The positive reading would be narrow.  A coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference may be treated as a cross-layer RNA-stability relation only if it tracks measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, separates coordinate and occupancy by an explicit decoupling contrast, collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and is not restored by binding-defective rescue or non-target controls.  In finite-row notation the admissible statistic has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}}
  = E[H \mid C=\mathrm{high}, O=\mathrm{high}, Z]
  - E[H \mid C=\mathrm{low}, O=\mathrm{low}, Z],\\
&Z=(T,R,S,M,G,D,P,I,A,B),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $H$ is the RNA half-life readout, $C$ is the synonymous coordinate label, $O$ is measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, $T$ is the same-row translation readout, $R$ is transcription rate, $S$ is local structure, $M$ is motif density, $G$ and $D$ are GC and dinucleotide composition, $P$ is codon-pair context, $I$ is splice or isoform state, $A$ is transcript abundance, and $B$ is batch.  The perturbation requirement is that the same contrast is absent after target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective rescue or non-target control.  Without these perturbation and rescue rows, the object remains an RNA half-life association rather than an RBP-mediated relation.

\paragraph{Empirical calibration from neighboring finite-row contacts.}
The repository already contains several finite-row RNA or cross-layer contacts that calibrate the size and style of acceptable bounded evidence, but none of them supplies the required decay-factor RBP occupancy and rescue panel for this claim.  The m6A site-load half-life contact used $n=5837$ joined genes and reported static Spearman association $\rho=-0.388929$ between m6A load and DMSO log half-life, causal Spearman association $\rho=0.245804$ for m6A load versus $\Delta\log_2$ half-life, length-partial Spearman $\rho=0.227683$, and two-sided partial-permutation value $p=0.000999001$.  A cross-organism m6A load contact carried $n_{\mathrm{genes}}=15845$, $n_{\mathrm{sites}}=135300$, $1000$ permutations, and sign concordance in $3$ organisms.  The 3-prime UTR ARE deletion contact carried $n=226$ BEAS2B deletion rows and $n=2242$ Jurkat deletion rows, used $1000$ actual permutations with a $2000$-permutation grading check, and found sign concordance under a pooled label-permutation statistic.  The endogenous 3-prime UTR half-life transfer contact used $n=14933$ genes, baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, ARE coefficient $\beta=-0.0607953$, and permutation value $p=0.00049975$ over $2000$ permutations.  These numbers show that finite RNA-stability boundaries can be measured, but they do not by themselves identify a decay-factor RBP as the causal occupancy channel for synonymous coding windows.

\paragraph{Translation boundary.}
The translation readout is mandatory because the coding-window coordinate is otherwise too easily promoted into a biological interpretation it has not earned.  The same-row translation measurement must be present before the RNA half-life contrast is interpreted, but its presence does not turn the RNA half-life statistic into translation realization, mature protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, or universality.  A separate contact at each of those layers would be needed.  The miRNA target-repression contact illustrates the appropriate restraint: it contains $n=55170$ rows, aggregate per-miRNA partial median $\rho=-0.224966$, and $7$ replicated gradient counts, yet it remains a target-repression readout rather than a global law about function.  The transcript-surveillance NMD position-rule contact kept $n=38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations; it supplies a position/readout boundary, not a license to infer downstream protein state.  The meiotic-axis redistribution contact used $n=11408$ one-kilobase windows, had held-out $R^2=0.0692$ for GC and wild-type-level controls, held-out $R^2=0.0916$ after adding sequence, incremental held-out $R^2=0.0224$, and permutation value $p=0.001$ with $1000$ permutations; it is likewise a finite redistribution statistic, not a general biological principle.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim fails locally under any of the following finite-row outcomes.  If high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after conditioning on transcription rate, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, abundance, and batch, the coordinate is only a bounded descriptor.  If RNA half-life changes but measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy does not, the result is only a finite RNA-stability association.  If target-RBP loss does not collapse the contrast, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, or if binding-defective rescue or non-target control reproduces the pattern, the RBP reading is refused.  If RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, abundance, or batch explains the same contrast, the rows do not support the proposed occupancy-mediated relation.

\paragraph{Present status.}
The finite contacts presently available include RNA half-life payloads, matched mRNA-abundance controls, and ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contacts.  They do not yet include the same coding-window rows with measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control, and coordinate-occupancy decoupling.  Therefore the chapter can state the admissible finite-row test and its refusal logic, and it can anchor the scale of neighboring RNA-stability evidence using the verified row counts, correlations, $R^2$ increments, and permutation values above.  It cannot assert the positive decay-factor RBP relation until the missing matched occupancy and perturbation/rescue rows are supplied.
