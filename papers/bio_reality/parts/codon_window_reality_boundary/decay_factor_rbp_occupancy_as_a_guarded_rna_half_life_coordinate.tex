\subsection{Decay-factor RBP occupancy as a guarded RNA half-life coordinate}
\label{sec:decay-factor-rbp-rna-half-life-with-translation-boundary-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite-row object.}
The object is a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row carries a synonymous coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy by a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP perturbation state, wild-type rescue state, binding-defective control state, non-target control state, transcription-rate control, mRNA abundance control, isoform annotation, motif-density covariates, local-structure covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, batch label, and a declared stopping condition.  The row is not allowed to promote synonymous coordinate geometry directly into RNA decay interpretation.  The coordinate is only a finite descriptor until the RNA half-life contrast is shown on rows that also preserve the translation boundary and the matched transcript controls.

\paragraph{Coordinate contrast.}
The intended contrast separates high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after amino-acid composition has been held fixed.  The finite statistic may be written schematically as a controlled half-life contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}}
  &= T_{\mathrm{HL}}(\mathrm{high\ coordinate},\mathrm{high\ occupancy})
   - T_{\mathrm{HL}}(\mathrm{low\ coordinate},\mathrm{low\ occupancy}),\\
\mathcal{C}
  &= \{\mathrm{transcription\ rate},\mathrm{mRNA\ abundance},\mathrm{local\ structure},
      \mathrm{motif\ density},\mathrm{GC/dinucleotide},\\
  &\qquad \mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform\ state},\mathrm{batch},\mathrm{same\ row\ translation\ readout}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive record would require the coordinate-associated half-life shift to track measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy after conditioning on $\mathcal{C}$, to collapse under target-RBP loss, to return under wild-type rescue, and not to return under a binding-defective rescue or a non-target control.  If the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after these controls, the coordinate remains an annotated finite label rather than a cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Why the translation boundary is part of the row.}
RNA half-life by itself is not a translation readout.  The required same-row translation measurement prevents a transcript-stability signal from being re-described as translation realization, protein output, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, or a universal sequence rule.  The translation readout functions as a boundary condition: it is observed beside the half-life and RBP variables, but it does not by itself authorize protein-level or functional language.  Any later claim about translation, folding, admissible structure, function, causal mechanism, phenotype, or universality would require an additional layer-matched contact that directly measures that layer.

\paragraph{Relation to available checked finite data.}
The present repository-level verified rows do not yet contain the same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy and perturbation-rescue panel required for this claim.  They do, however, show that the surrounding RNA-stability boundary can be expressed as finite external readouts.  In the m6A and mRNA half-life boundary run with identifier $7\mathrm{cd}189\mathrm{f}15\mathrm{c}4\mathrm{b}$, the joined dataset retained $5837$ genes under the coverage rule $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ DMSO},\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ in\ STM2457})$ at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  The static Spearman association between m6A load and $\log_2$ half-life in DMSO was $-0.388929$; the length-partial Spearman association between m6A load and $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.227683$; the causal Spearman statistic for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$; and the two-sided partial-permutation value was $0.000999001$.  These numbers demonstrate a bounded RNA-stability contact for a different molecular mark, not a decay-factor RBP result and not a protein-layer result.

A second checked boundary, the transcript-surveillance NMD position-rule run with identifier $\mathrm{c}362\mathrm{df}6\mathrm{daa}98$, retained $38576$ rows, used $1000$ permutations, and ran for $55.602$ seconds.  This is useful only as another example that finite transcript-level relations can be audited with declared row counts and permutation budgets.  It does not supply local decay-factor RBP occupancy, synonymous editing rows, or perturbation-rescue behavior for the present panel.

\paragraph{Acceptance rule.}
The guarded RBP-decay claim becomes reportable only when the finite rows contain all of the following information in the same matched design: synonymously edited coding windows, measured local occupancy by the named decay-factor RBP, RNA half-life, same-row translation readout, transcription-rate and mRNA abundance controls, local-structure and motif-density covariates, isoform state, codon-pair and composition controls, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control, and batch annotation.  The expected direction is not enough.  The decisive pattern is the joint control pattern: the half-life effect follows occupancy under coordinate-occupancy decoupling, disappears when the target RBP is lost, returns with wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The claim is refused if RNA half-life differs while measured local RBP occupancy does not, if transcription rate or mRNA abundance reproduces the pattern, if local RNA structure or motif density explains the contrast, if isoform state or batch absorbs the effect, if a binding-defective rescue restores the pattern, or if a non-target control behaves like the target perturbation.  It is also refused if the rows lack the translation readout boundary, because then a stability signal could be mistaken for a translation-layer statement.  With the currently supplied finite data, the chapter can state the protocol and cite neighboring transcript-stability boundary statistics, but it cannot certify the proposed decay-factor RBP cross-layer relation.
