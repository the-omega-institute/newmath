\subsection{Measured Survival Scores}\label{sec:q6-ribosome-te-survival-scores}

\paragraph{Partial measured-TE survival.}
The positive threshold for a measured survival entry was $10^{-12}$ in partial-$R^2$ units.  All four organism panels reported all nine residual coordinates as surviving under that threshold.  The organism-level controlled measured-TE values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{E. coli}) &= 0.009394266754822262,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{S. cerevisiae}) &= 0.13681931343730436,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{H. sapiens}) &= 0.018837911344127926,\\
R^2_{QT,meas}(\emph{D. rerio}) &= 0.04034217614654355.
\end{aligned}
$$
The largest finite value in this row set is the yeast value, $0.13681931343730436$, and the smallest is the \emph{E. coli} value, $0.009394266754822262$.  The result therefore records positive controlled survival across these four measured contacts, but with materially different strengths by organism and data source.

\paragraph{Interpretation of positive entries.}
A positive $S^{QT,meas}$ entry is a codon-residual to measured-TE association after the stated controls.  It is not a claim about universal translation efficiency, elongation kinetics, protein abundance, protein function, or any higher biological layer.  The measured response is condition-specific, cross-sectional, and drawn from one or a small number of ribosome-profiling studies per organism.  Agreement or disagreement with a modeled tAI-style contrast may be used only as coordinate-pattern evidence; it is neither causal validation nor causal falsification.  The matched mRNA normalization is essential because raw ribosome occupancy alone would mix transcription abundance with the translation-efficiency readout.
