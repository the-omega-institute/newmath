\subsection{Rank-powered translation-conditioned protein mediation for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}\label{subsec:q6-rank-powered-translation-conditioned-protein-mediation-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite matrix under test.}
This record tests whether synonymous residual coordinates in the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ panel can be read as a measured cross-layer signal from coding-sequence composition to protein abundance through a translation readout.  The finite carrier consists of nine residual coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Each coordinate is residualized against a common control block containing an intercept, $\log(cds\_len\_nt)$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline equal to the fraction of sense codons lying on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control matrix has $24$ columns and rank $23$ in every reported powered fit.

\paragraph{Measured translation-conditioning readout.}
The measured mediation run uses $T=\log_{10}(TE)$, where $TE$ is the ribosome-footprint to mRNA ratio from organism-local ribosome-profiling files, and $Y=\log_{10}(abundance\_ppm)$ from matched protein-abundance rows joined by protein identifier.  The four powered organisms are danio, Escherichia coli K12 MG1655, human, and yeast.  Their three-way CDS joins are $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$, respectively.  The corresponding reported CDS join counts against abundance are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$, while the valid measured-TE rows are $13140$, $3415$, $11554$, and $5128$.  Thus the test is not a rank-degenerate all-one survival boundary: after the $Z$ block is removed, the residual degrees of freedom are $13117$, $3392$, $11531$, and $5105$.

\paragraph{Survival and mediation scalars.}
For each organism the residual coordinate block has rank $9$, the measured translation readout has rank $1$, and the joint block has rank $10$.  The protein-abundance survival $S^{QP}$ and measured translation survival $S^{QT}_{meas}$ are both positive in all four organisms, but the amount of direct $Q \to P$ association absorbed by measured $T$ is strongly organism-dependent:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{QP}_{danio} &= 0.04926578454614652, & S^{QT}_{danio} &= 0.04034217614654355, & M^{QTP}_{danio} &= 0,\\
S^{QP}_{ecoli} &= 0.10941535951828074, & S^{QT}_{ecoli} &= 0.009394266754822262, & M^{QTP}_{ecoli} &= 0.04091158340950673,\\
S^{QP}_{human} &= 0.04288464996165745, & S^{QT}_{human} &= 0.018837911344127926, & M^{QTP}_{human} &= 0.0027966485036786744,\\
S^{QP}_{yeast} &= 0.3146818503437042, & S^{QT}_{yeast} &= 0.13681931343730436, & M^{QTP}_{yeast} &= 0.4793069656345167.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw danio fraction is negative, $-0.04801486048721136$, so the reported bounded fraction is clipped to $0$.  Yeast is the clearest measured-translation case: its measured translation readout absorbs nearly half of the joint $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ protein-abundance association, while retaining positive $S^{QT}_{meas}$ and positive $S^{QP}$.  E. coli has a smaller but positive measured fraction.  Human shows positive translation and protein survival but almost no measured-TE absorption at this cross-sectional scale.

\paragraph{Energy decomposition.}
The finite-row decomposition also records the raw explained-energy components.  In danio, $X_Q$ explains $1092.0114819316218$ units of residual protein-abundance energy and measured $T$ explains $16.418999670692678$, while the incremental improvement of $T$ given $X_Q$ is $68.85177862607247$ and the direct improvement of $X_Q$ given $T$ is $1144.4442608870015$.  In E. coli these values are $393.78692134854015$, $35.56347808682994$, $19.45303160850625$, and $377.67647487021645$.  In human they are $565.3850203147235$, $2.0678517436514716$, $0.486668572585927$, and $563.803837143658$.  In yeast they are $675.3275481062249$, $593.5847863333478$, $269.89558844115504$, and $351.63835021403213$.  This is the numerical reason the yeast row is classified as a strong measured-readout absorption row, while human is a positive-survival but weak-mediation row.

\paragraph{Residual signals beyond measured translation.}
Two additional finite probes ask whether residual protein-abundance signal left after a modeled translation complement can be absorbed by nontranslation readouts.  For reviewed subcellular localization, the joined rows are $1984$ for danio, $16008$ for human, and $4656$ for yeast, with $12$ localization columns in each case.  The absorption fractions are $0.009508864544725801$, $0.009254913329269913$, and $0.011225105608516642$.  The corresponding degree-of-freedom matched null $95\%$ cutoffs are $0.011321328712802819$, $0.0013758521256775384$, and $0.004958116973149795$.  Therefore localization is above the matched null in human and yeast but below it in danio on the available localization join.  The modeled-complement agreement check is within $0.02$ for human and yeast, with absolute differences $0.018824203448813193$ and $0.011946809265360736$, and outside that band in danio, where the difference is $0.11375632672819314$ because the localization join is much smaller than the abundance-CDS reference join.

\paragraph{Human turnover check.}
The human protein-turnover probe joins $8057$ rows after mapping Mathieson gene names through reviewed UniProt primary gene names to STRING identifiers.  The reported human abundance-CDS reference join is $19053$, the valid turnover count is $8164$, and the abundance count is $19483$.  The modeled translation complement has $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490492302532$ and residual norm squared $1262.2237678771598$, with modeled complement scalar $d_{modeled}=0.9208949355679455$.  Human half-life explains almost none of that residual: the turnover absorption is $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, the basis check gives $3.5740764900125626\times 10^{-6}$, $R^2_{Q,Tturn}=0.004615408769189838$, and the combined scalar is $0.8901589776585174$.  The degree-of-freedom matched null has mean $0.0001262572958089998$ and $95\%$ cutoff $0.0004342285066808464$, so the turnover row is not promoted as a residual explanation.

\paragraph{Readback classification.}
The finite readback has four organism-specific outcomes.  Yeast is a positive measured-translation mediation row: $S^{QP}>0$, $S^{QT}_{meas}>0$, and the measured mediation fraction is $0.4793069656345167$.  E. coli is a positive but small measured-mediation row.  Human is a positive-survival row with weak measured-TE absorption, and the separate turnover probe does not absorb the remaining residual.  Danio is a positive-survival row with a suppressive measured fraction under this join, and its localization check is not above the matched-null cutoff.  These outcomes support the bounded statement that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates can survive into measured translation and measured protein-abundance readouts on rank-powered finite panels, with yeast giving the strongest measured $Q \to T \to P$ row.  They do not support a universal mediation law.

\paragraph{Local boundary of interpretation.}
The statistical quantities above are projections and conditional decompositions on finite joined tables.  They do not establish causality, temporal ordering, perturbation response, synonymous-edit rescue, elongation mechanism, initiation mechanism, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, or evolutionary direction.  A $Q \to P$ association is not by itself a protein mechanism claim.  A mechanism-level interpretation is allowed only when an additional measured contact supplies that layer and when conditioning on the measured translation readout removes or materially reduces the direct $Q \to P$ component.  Modeled tAI or stAI is kept separate from observed ribosome-profiling TE, and direct protein-omics survival remains a prediction or association unless the measured translation step supplies the mediation row.
