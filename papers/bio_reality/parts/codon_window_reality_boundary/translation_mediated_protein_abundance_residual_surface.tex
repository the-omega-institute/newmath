\subsection{Translation-mediated protein-abundance residual surface}
\label{sec:q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite mediation surface whose rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$.  The coordinate $q_i$ ranges over the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\ \mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\ \mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\ \mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \mathrm{f3\_stress}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout $t_j$ is the per-protein modeled translation-adaptation score computed as the codon-count-weighted mean of tAI weights from GtRNAdb tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The readout $p_k$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from PAXdb protein abundance after joining to real CDS codon counts.  The row is admitted only after residualizing against the local control block $Z$: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline defined from the union support of the nine projected residual vectors.  In every organism below the control matrix has $23$ effective rank and $24$ columns before rank loss, while the residualized $Q$ block has rank $9$ and the residualized translation readout has rank $1$.

\paragraph{Mediated readback.}
The powered finite experiment joined twelve organism-scoped CDS, tRNA, and protein-abundance surfaces.  The joined protein counts were $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The tRNA-copy totals used for the modeled translation score were respectively $86$, $581$, $8874$, $290$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $407$, $49$, $417$, $275$, and $47$; geometric-mean filling of zero raw weights occurred only in \emph{Bacillus subtilis} with $3$ codons, \emph{Escherichia coli} with $1$, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} with $1$, and \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} with $1$.

The measured quantity is the attenuation of the residual association $Q \to P$ after conditioning on the translation readout $T$.  For each organism the scalar
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} = \frac{R^2(Q \to P)-R^2(Q \to P\mid T)}{R^2(Q \to P)}
\end{aligned}
$$
was finite and lay in the unit interval.  The organism-level values were $0.6084594553$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $0.4872268357$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $0.3157053954$ for zebrafish, $0.6165167749$ for fly, $0.6129222528$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.1976903965$ for chicken, $0.2720628916$ for human, $0.1548879991$ for mouse, $0.0839760292$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.1748968180$ for rat, $0.9491551041$ for yeast, and $0.0185066856$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}.  Thus every finite row block gives a nonnegative translation-conditioned attenuation, with yeast showing the largest fraction and \emph{S. solfataricus} the smallest.

\paragraph{Residual energy accounting.}
The same table gives the unconditioned and conditioned residual fits.  In yeast, for example, $R^2(Q\to P)=0.2983762499$ and $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.0151709094$, so the indirect residual difference is $0.2832053406$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the corresponding values are $0.1076752668$, $0.0416786997$, and $0.0659965671$.  In human they are $0.0445456512$, $0.0324264325$, and $0.0121192187$.  In mouse they are $0.0498279841$, $0.0421102273$, and $0.0077177567$.  The smallest mediated difference is the archaeal row, where $R^2(Q\to P)=0.0639751425$, $R^2(Q\to P\mid T)=0.0627911747$, and the difference is $0.0011839679$.  The largest direct protein-abundance explanation by the residual block occurs in mouse, where the $X$-explained energy on $Y$ is $2431.9284360408$ and the direct improvement energy given $T$ is $2055.2519066931$; the largest translation-conditioned attenuation fraction occurs in yeast, where $X$ explains $5.6717034045$ units of residualized $T$ energy and the modeled $T$ block explains $941.8858618614$ units of residualized $Y$ energy.

\paragraph{Translation-contact boundary.}
A separate modeled-tAI survival computation confirms that the nine residual coordinates can be placed against an organism-scoped codon-adaptation readout without collapsing the standard codon table.  The computation used $61$ sense codons and found $81$ entries above the bounded-contact threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  The tRNA payload covered all $20$ standard amino-acid families in \emph{E. coli} K12, human, and yeast; their FASTA header counts were $89$, $432$, and $275$, with matched header counts $87$, $432$, and $275$.  This is still a modeled adaptation readout rather than ribosome profiling or perturbation evidence: the organism count for that survival surface is $3$, below the $40$-species threshold declared for powered survival.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
The yeast structure-mediation table supplies an orthogonal comparison surface rather than a replacement for the translation-mediated claim.  Its five-way join contains $4759$ rows, a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419$, and an overall join hit rate $0.8818572657$.  On that surface $R^2(Q\to P)=0.2715520808$, $R^2(Q\to P\mid S)=0.2706601505$, $R^2(Q\to P\mid T,M)=0.1377396770$, and $R^2(Q\to P\mid T,M,S)=0.1375901121$, with $S_Q$ structure $R^2=0.0037742485$ and right-tail permutation value $0.0279720280$.  These numbers show that the present chapter is not using structure as the mediating contact: the translation and abundance rows stand on their own finite matrix, while structure remains a distinct sequence-derived proxy.

Other linked runs constrain overinterpretation.  The cross-ortholog selection-imprint run used $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species; its positive selection-imprint gate was false, with $\Delta R^2=0.0325051502$, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477730$, null $95\%$ maximum $0.0542715707$, and description-length change $-258.6010084$ bits.  The mass-balance run on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals reported $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628202$, $R^2_{L1}=0.9964106289$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443378$, $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568$, and $\Delta R^2=-0.0758443809$, with $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168$ and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693$.  The turnover audit on $3290$ genes also remained negative beyond its controls, with $R^2_{L2}=0.0557408516$, $R^2_{L3}=0.0502788722$, $\Delta R^2=-0.0054619794$, matched-bin $p=0.8855721393$, and recoded $p=1.0$.  These checks keep protein abundance, dynamic mass balance, selection imprint, and turnover as separate surfaces rather than one promoted biological conclusion.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The finite row data support a bounded cross-layer statement: a nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block has a reproducible statistical association with matched protein abundance, and in all twelve organism blocks a positive fraction of that association is attenuated after conditioning on the modeled translation-adaptation readout.  The strongest finite mediation fraction is yeast at $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041$ and the weakest is \emph{S. solfataricus} at $M^{QTP}=0.0185066856$; no organism has a negative mediated fraction or a fraction outside $[0,1]$.  The result therefore enters the paper as a controlled mediation matrix
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i \longrightarrow T_j \longrightarrow P_k,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $T_j$ is modeled per-protein tAI and $P_k$ is matched PAXdb abundance.  It does not license a direct $Q_i\to P_k$ protein-realization claim when the translation readout is omitted, and it does not establish observed ribosome occupancy, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, perturbational mechanism, organismal phenotype, selection, or a universal biological law.  Those stronger assertions require their own layer-matched measurements and their own finite controlled statistics.
