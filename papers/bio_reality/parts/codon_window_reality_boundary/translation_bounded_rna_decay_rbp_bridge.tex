\subsection{Translation-bounded RNA decay RBP bridge}\label{sec:q6-translation-bounded-rna-decay-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite matched RNA/CDS window panel whose rows carry $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate labels, synonymous-window sequence fields, a same-row translation readout when present, an RNA half-life readout, matched mRNA abundance, and externally attached columns for prespecified decay-factor RBP occupancy and controls. The admissible comparison is not a naked contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows. It is the bounded question of whether an RNA half-life difference remains coordinate-associated only through a measured decay-factor RBP occupancy surface after amino-acid composition, local sequence composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, local RNA-structure proxies, splice or isoform state, batch, and the same-row translation boundary have been accounted for.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout separation.}
For a row $i$ in the finite panel, write $q_i$ for the residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate class, $t_i$ for the attached translation readout if it exists, $a_i$ for matched mRNA abundance, $h_i$ for RNA half-life, and $o_i$ for measured occupancy by the nominated decay-factor RBP. The intended statistic has the schematic form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{H} &= T\bigl(h_i \mid q_i = \mathrm{high},\ t_i,\ a_i,\mathrm{controls}\bigr)
        - T\bigl(h_i \mid q_i = \mathrm{low},\ t_i,\ a_i,\mathrm{controls}\bigr),\\
\Delta_{O} &= T\bigl(h_i \mid o_i = \mathrm{occupied},\ q_i,\ t_i,\ a_i,\mathrm{controls}\bigr)
        - T\bigl(h_i \mid o_i = \mathrm{unoccupied},\ q_i,\ t_i,\ a_i,\mathrm{controls}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $T$ denotes the declared finite-row summary used by the experimenter, such as a matched mean, rank statistic, stratified contrast, or regression coefficient. The record is positive only if the coordinate-associated half-life contrast is explained by the measured RBP-occupancy contrast under coordinate/occupancy decoupling, not merely by sequence geometry, transcript abundance, or translation readout availability.

\paragraph{Perturbation gate.}
A cross-layer RNA-decay bridge is admitted only under a stricter perturbation pattern. Target-RBP loss must collapse the half-life effect that had been attributed to the occupied windows. Wild-type rescue must restore that effect. Binding-defective rescue and non-target controls must fail to restore it. The target-RBP condition is therefore part of the finite claim itself, not an optional annotation. If high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels continue to predict half-life when measured occupancy is absent, unchanged, or experimentally inseparable from coordinate state, the row set supports at most a bounded RNA-stability association. If the same-row translation readout is missing, the record cannot be promoted into a translation-conditioned cross-layer statement.

\paragraph{Available row evidence.}
The attached finite contacts are enough to define the boundary of the claim but not enough to close a positive RBP-decay result. The RNA half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with curated half-life estimates and dataset provenance. The matched mRNA contact supplies abundance rows used to separate transcript quantity from translation efficiency. The ribosome-profiling contact supplies per-CDS ribosome occupancy or translation-efficiency rows where $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$ can be computed. These contacts establish the row grammar for a future joined panel, but the supplied payload explicitly lacks same-window decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target control columns.

\paragraph{Calibration against other finite surfaces.}
Three linked cross-layer surfaces give scale for the finite-row standard used here. The degron terminal-motif stability surface reports $5303$ C-terminal rows over $5303$ proteins with runtime $40.3029$ seconds in run $1a932ef5684e$. The human charge-patterning thermal-stability surface reports $6961$ rows with a deterministic runtime bucket of $70.0$ seconds in run $8873440304f9$. The ubiquitylation acidic-microenvironment surface reports $10410$ sites across $2883$ proteins with $2000$ permutations in run $0b96a8e4da3c$. These numbers do not prove the RNA-decay RBP bridge; they only show the finite-row evidentiary scale expected for a positive cross-layer surface. The present RNA-decay claim would need its own joined row count, declared statistic, occupancy contrast, perturbation contrast, and rescue contrast before receiving the same status.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null states that, after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, amino-acid composition, and the same-row translation readout, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA windows are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. In that case the coordinate remains a finite descriptor of synonymous-window geometry. If an RNA half-life contrast survives but does not track measured decay-factor RBP occupancy under decoupling, it is an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP bridge. If occupancy tracks the contrast but target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue fail the stated controls, the record remains associative.

\paragraph{Local limits.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone. No protein output, folding state, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, broad biological rule, or decay pathway is inferred from the coordinate statistic. Translation can enter only through the same-row translation readout and its matched mRNA control. RBP involvement can enter only through measured occupancy plus loss/rescue behavior with binding-defective and non-target controls. Protein-layer or organism-level conclusions require independent layer-matched contacts rather than reinterpretation of the RNA half-life rows.
