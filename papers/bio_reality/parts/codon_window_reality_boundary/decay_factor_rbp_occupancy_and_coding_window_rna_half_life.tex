\subsection{Decay-factor RBP occupancy and coding-window RNA half-life}\label{sec:decay-factor-rbp-rna-half-life-translation-boundary}\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row is a bounded observation tuple: a synonymous coordinate label, the matched amino-acid-preserving window identity, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, transcription-rate or nascent-RNA control, mRNA abundance control, local RNA-structure covariates, motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, perturbation state, rescue state, and a declared stopping condition for refusal.  The intended contrast is not a sequence-wide law.  It is the narrower question of whether high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels, after amino-acid composition control, separate RNA half-life only through a measured decay-factor RBP surface that is present in the same finite rows.

\paragraph{Licensed statistic.}
A positive row-level statistic has the following finite form.  Let $W$ be the retained window set, $c(w)$ the synonymous coordinate class, $T(w)$ the translation readout, $H(w)$ the RNA half-life readout, $O(w)$ the local occupancy of the chosen decay-factor RBP, and $X(w)$ the vector of transcription, mRNA abundance, RNA structure, motif density, GC, dinucleotide, codon-pair, isoform, transcript-abundance, and batch controls.  The admissible claim is bounded by the joint comparison
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(w) &\sim c(w) + O(w) + T(w) + X(w),\\
O(w) &\sim c(w) + X(w),
\end{aligned}
$$
read only inside the edited-window panel.  The same panel must also contain the intervention columns: target-RBP present versus loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue or control, and non-target control.  The coordinate-to-half-life reading is therefore accepted only if the half-life effect tracks the measured occupancy contrast, collapses when the target RBP is lost, returns under wild-type rescue, and is not restored by binding-defective or non-target controls.

\paragraph{Reality contact already present.}
The supplied finite evidence is not yet the requested same-window RBP perturbation panel, but it does show that the paper's RNA-stability boundary is not empty.  In the m6A half-life boundary dataset, $5837$ joined genes were retained; the static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log2 half-life was $-0.388929$, the causal Spearman association between m6A load and $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$, the length-partial Spearman statistic was $0.227683$, and the two-sided partial-permutation value was $p=0.000999001$ under the stated coverage rule.  A separate cross-organism human m6A contact reports $135300$ sites across $15845$ genes, $1000$ permutations, runtime $338.238$ seconds, and sign concordance in $3$ checked organism-scoped comparisons.  These figures support the existence of finite RNA half-life contacts with declared statistics; they do not by themselves identify the decay-factor RBP mechanism required here.

\paragraph{Neighboring RNA-stability checks.}
Two additional row-counted contacts constrain the interpretation.  The transcript-surveillance position-rule boundary retained $38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations with runtime $55.602$ seconds.  The UTR3 AU-rich-element deletion boundary reports $226$ BEAS2B rows and $2242$ Jurkat rows, with $1000$ actual permutations, $2000$ grading permutations, a corrected pooled-label permutation over deletions, runtime $6.93$ seconds, and a sign-concordant result.  These contacts show that RNA-stability readouts can be handled as finite, row-keyed boundary statistics across distinct biological surfaces.  They are deliberately weaker than the present RBP bridge because they do not supply the same-window decay-factor occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective rescue columns required for this claim.

\paragraph{Refusal conditions.}
The chapter refuses the bridge if high-coordinate and low-coordinate windows become exchangeable for RNA half-life after controlling transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch.  It also refuses the bridge if RNA half-life differs while measured decay-factor RBP occupancy does not, if target-RBP loss fails to collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or if binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA structure, motif density, transcriptional change, isoform instability, abundance artifacts, or batch effects reproduce the same pattern.  In those cases the rows may still record an RNA-stability association, but the coordinate remains only a bounded descriptor rather than an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Layer boundary.}
The mandatory translation readout is a boundary column, not a license to infer translation realization from RNA half-life.  No RNA half-life contrast in this chapter entails protein output, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism, or universality unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that information.  Even a successful decay-factor RBP pattern would remain a local finite relation among synonymous coding-window coordinates, measured RBP occupancy, translation-boundary rows, and RNA half-life under the declared controls.
