\subsection{Decay-factor RBP occupancy as a bounded RNA half-life relation}\label{bio:decay-factor-rbp-rna-half-life-with-translation-boundary-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.} The object considered here is a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows. Each row is a single window under one declared perturbation and rescue state, not a transcript-wide or organism-wide summary. The row must contain a synonymous coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local measured occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, transcription-rate and mRNA-abundance controls, isoform state, local RNA-structure and motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and an explicit break condition. The intended comparison is between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after amino-acid composition is fixed, but the comparison is not licensed by the coordinate label alone. The finite row is admissible only when the RNA half-life statistic remains attached to the translation-readout boundary and to controls that separate transcription, abundance, local sequence composition, local structure, isoform ambiguity, and batch.

\paragraph{Statistic.} For a row $i$, let $C_i$ be the synonymous coordinate class, $T_i$ the matched translation readout, $H_i$ the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ the measured local occupancy of the named decay-factor RBP, $P_i$ the target-RBP perturbation state, $W_i$ the wild-type rescue state, $B_i$ the binding-defective or non-target control state, and $X_i$ the declared vector of transcription, mRNA abundance, isoform, local-structure, motif-density, composition, codon-pair, and batch covariates. A positive finite claim has the following form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&H_i \text{ separates by } C_i \text{ only through a matched change in } O_i,\\
&\text{the separation collapses under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue,}\\
&\text{and is not reproduced by binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, or } X_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same-row translation readout $T_i$ is a boundary variable, not a promotion rule. It prevents an RNA half-life result from being restated as translation realization. A half-life shift without the required translation row is therefore only an RNA-stability observation, even if it is coordinate-associated.

\paragraph{Observed support from neighboring RNA-stability contacts.} The current finite evidence does not yet contain the complete decay-factor RBP panel demanded above. It does, however, contain several RNA-layer contacts that show why the boundary must be kept narrow. In the m6A and half-life contact, the joined table retained $5837$ genes under the coverage rule $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{STM2457})$ at least the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes. The static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$, while the perturbation-oriented Spearman value for m6A load against $\Delta\log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$. After length control, the partial Spearman value was $0.227683$, with two-sided partial-permutation value $0.000999001$. This is an RNA half-life boundary statistic; it is not by itself an RBP occupancy, rescue, translation, protein, or function result.

\paragraph{Independent RNA-layer calibration.} Two other contacts give finite row counts for RNA-stability-adjacent boundaries without supplying the present decay-factor RBP mechanism rows. The NMD position-rule contact kept $38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations, with runtime $55.602$ seconds. The ARE-deletion stability contact used a corrected pooled label permutation over deletions, with $1000$ actual permutations, $2000$ grading permutations, $226$ BEAS-2B rows, $2242$ Jurkat rows, and sign concordance recorded as true. These contacts support the practice of reporting finite RNA-layer statistics with row counts and permutation design, but they do not substitute for local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, or wild-type rescue inside the synonymous coding-window panel.

\paragraph{Required positive pattern.} The coordinate-to-half-life relation is accepted only if three separations occur in the same finite panel. First, high- and low-coordinate synonymous labels must differ in RNA half-life after amino-acid composition, transcription rate, mRNA abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch are controlled. Second, the half-life difference must track measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP under a coordinate-occupancy decoupling design, so that the coordinate label is not merely standing in for motif density or local folding. Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the effect and wild-type rescue must restore it, while binding-defective rescue and non-target controls must fail to restore it. If any one of these requirements is absent, the result remains a bounded RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Local refusal rule.} No translation realization, protein output, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, or universal sequence law follows from RNA half-life alone. A translation claim requires its own translation contact; a protein-state claim requires a protein-state contact; a function or phenotype claim requires a layer-matched function or phenotype contact. Even a strong coordinate-associated half-life shift is not enough. If measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is unchanged, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, if binding-defective rescue or non-target controls reproduce it, or if transcription, mRNA abundance, isoform state, motif density, local structure, sequence composition, codon-pair context, or batch explain it, the proposed cross-layer reading is refused.
