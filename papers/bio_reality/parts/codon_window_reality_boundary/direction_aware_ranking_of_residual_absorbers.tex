\subsection{Direction-aware ranking of residual absorbers}
\label{sec:q6-h-ranking-direction-aware}

\paragraph{Finite ledger.}
The object in this section is a finite per-organism table, not a biological mechanism.  For each organism $o$ with a computable modeled-translation-complement residual, the ledger stores the residual energy $P_{Q,\perp T}^{(o)}$, its nine-coordinate support vector $a_{\perp}^{(o)}$, each available readout support $a_H^{(o)}$, the absorbed fraction $A_H^{(o)}$, the direction cosine $C_H^{(o)}$, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Score}(o,H) &= A_H^{(o)} C_H^{(o)} - \lambda \operatorname{DL}(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
\operatorname{DL}(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}(H)).
\end{aligned}
$$
For one-column readouts this gives $\operatorname{DL}=1$, while the twelve-column localization readout has $\operatorname{DL}=\log_2(13)=3.700439718141092$.  The table also records a degree-of-freedom-matched Bonferroni null when it is available.  Rank and score never replace the null result: a row can have high absorbed energy, a favorable direction, and still fail the stated penalized criterion.

\paragraph{Residual scale before ranking.}
The complement residual is large in several organisms.  In the ranking run, the modeled-complement fraction $d$ is $0.874628721049266$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.6629960038578534$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $0.9400341806302775$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $0.9208949355679455$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $0.21173219572706714$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  The associated complement energies include $1005.3614101653926$ for danio, $287.00467177017873$ for \emph{E. coli}, $695.5324705841902$ for gallus, $1262.2237678771598$ for human, and $186.88597748267557$ for yeast.  The residual readback always lives in the same nine coordinates: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.

The leading danio row is measured translation efficiency: $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_H C_H=0.011566051846174291$, $\operatorname{Score}=0.001566051846174291$, $n=13140$, and the Bonferroni threshold is $0.000719994971498518$.  Danio mRNA is close behind with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.000734588577977589$.  The danio tRNA-copy tAI row absorbs more raw energy, $A_H=0.02091194580949614$, but its direction cosine is only $0.33399841523960977$, so the raw directional product falls to $0.006984556759948309$ and the penalized score is negative.

For human, the best one-column directional row is complex membership: $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.0034339698846599055$ at $n=18335$.  Human mRNA is similar, with $A_H=0.007858222847072393$, $C_H=0.7921354915786852$, and $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$.  Human PTM density has the largest raw energy among these one-column rows, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its cosine is only $0.3604440741749213$, which demotes the directional product to $0.0053651472982577525$.  This is the central point of the direction filter: energy absorption alone is not allowed to define support in the relevant coordinate direction.  Human measured TE is an even sharper warning case: it exceeds its Bonferroni threshold as an absorption row, but $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$ and $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$, so it is wrong-direction for this ranking.

Yeast has a smaller upstream residual.  Its top row is mRNA, with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.008853737772559931$ at $n=5128$.  The yeast localization row absorbs $A_H=0.011225105608516642$, but it uses twelve columns and has $C_H=0.4247910216132792$, so the description-length term drives the score to $-0.03223607310225219$.  In \emph{E. coli}, only mRNA and measured TE rows are available in the assembled table; mRNA has $A_H=0.0017706954554909877$, $C_H=0.8639529831258776$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.008470202379021127$, while measured TE has $A_H=0.0010982956489728746$, $C_H=0.5041139753888787$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.009446333814243976$.  Gallus is feature-only in this table: complex membership is the top row, $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$, $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.00575848521855683$.

\paragraph{No shared absorber.}
The per-organism best absorber is not stable across organisms.  The assembled $H_o^\ast$ values are measured TE for danio, complex membership for gallus, complex membership for human, and mRNA for yeast; \emph{E. coli} has no positive-score best row under the stated penalized criterion, and several organisms have no computable H table at all.  Thus the table does not support a universal readout channel.  This negative result is informative rather than defective: the same residual geometry can be replayed against the available measured contacts, yet the resulting best absorber depends on organism and data coverage.

\paragraph{Null and data coverage checks.}
The ranking is tied to finite rows.  Danio measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, mRNA, and PTM density exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, whereas danio localization, domain count, transmembrane count, and complex membership do not.  Human complex membership, localization, measured TE, mRNA, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their Bonferroni thresholds; human measured tRNA-copy tAI, domain count, and turnover do not.  Yeast mRNA and localization exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, while yeast tRNA-copy tAI, measured TE, turnover, complex membership, PTM density, transmembrane count, and domain count do not.  The joined row counts are materially different across contacts: human turnover has $8057$ joined rows and fails its own null with $A_H=3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ against null $95\%$ value $0.0004342285066808464$; yeast turnover has $3640$ joined rows, a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, and $A_H=0.0009086031889966327$; localization joins have $1984$ danio rows, $16008$ human rows, and $4656$ yeast rows.

\paragraph{Relation to translation and organism summaries.}
Measured TE does not by itself resolve the residual.  In the measured mediation table, the measured mediated fractions are $0$ for danio after clipping a raw value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The organism-specific summary likewise does not find a clean global driver: the dominant-coordinate versus domain permutation test has $n=12$, $\chi^2=9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and right-tail permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$.  The cross-organism Window6 boundary set has a strong avoided abundance-preference signature, with $15$ avoided and $0$ preferred among $15$ directional non-ties, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$; that result supplies a finite abundance-preference signature for the boundary set, not an H mechanism for the residual ranking.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The rank table supports a narrow descriptive statement: no available fetchable readout strongly and uniformly absorbs the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual in the relevant coordinate direction across organisms.  The best raw directional products remain small, with the largest reported value $0.011566051846174291$ for danio measured TE and the next $0.01073458857797759$ for danio mRNA.  After the declared description-length penalty, only the two danio one-column rows remain positive, and they do not generalize.  A separate compressibility test reaches the same broad descriptive conclusion: median compressibility across computed organisms is $0.026$, with final unexplained fractions $0.9756319042010854$ for danio, $0.9942823785118229$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.9736442326306781$ for human, and $0.8676586345294415$ for yeast.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
The table is a bounded statistical projection ledger.  It does not establish translation realization beyond the specific attached translation readouts, and it does not establish folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, pathway structure, regulation, mechanism, or universality.  The quantities $A_H$, $C_H$, $A_HC_H$, score, rank, $H_o^\ast$, and any cross-organism best-readout comparison are descriptive outputs of the finite projection policy.  A stronger biological statement would require an independent, layer-matched measured contact and a separate statistic designed for that layer.
