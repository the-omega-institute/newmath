\subsection{Cross-organism CUN/UUR Leu gate at the translation-statistic layer}\label{sec:cross-organism-cun-uur-leu-gate-translation-realization}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Packet scope.}
This record treats the leucine codon split between the $CUN$ block and the $UUR$ block as a finite organism-level statistic, not as a direct observation of ribosome motion or protein output.  The carrier is the residual comparison of the two synonymous leucine blocks, paired with organism labels and with a second coordinate supplied by annotated tRNA-Leu gene-copy spectra.  The admissible readout is the sign and magnitude of $\log(\#CUN/\#UUR)$ after the amino-acid composition issue has been kept local to the synonymous class.  The packet therefore asks a bounded question: whether the organism-level codon-usage imbalance is non-uniform across organisms and whether it is aligned with the corresponding tRNA-Leu copy-number ratio under the stated control.

\paragraph{Finite codon row.}
The supporting code-row fact fixes the relevant $M$ row as a finite list of $20$ codons.  The recorded list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&AAA,AAG,ACA,ACG,AGA,AGG,AUA,AUG,CUA,CUC,\\
& CUG,CUU,UAA,UAG,UCA,UCG,UGA,UGG,UUA,UUG\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same run reports $M\_cardinality\_equals\_20=20$ and $size=20$.  Within that finite row, the leucine comparison uses the four $CUN$ codons $CUA,CUC,CUG,CUU$ against the two $UUR$ codons $UUA,UUG$.  This supplies the finite coordinate on which the cross-organism statistic is evaluated; it does not by itself supply a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Organism-matched statistic.}
The validation row loaded three organism labels: $homo\_sapiens$, $saccharomyces\_cerevisiae$, and $escherichia\_coli\_k12$.  For these three rows the recorded log-ratios were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\;-0.24869078488829677,\;1.0086640520051702),
\end{aligned}
$$
and the corresponding signs were
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1,\;-1,\;1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the sign is not uniform across the measured organisms: the recorded field $sign\_uniform$ is false.  The same validation row records $pearson=1.0$ for the paired statistic and a positive partial correlation entry with $n=3$ and $partial\_pearson=1.0$ for the tRNA-Leu ratio after the stated amino-acid composition control.  On this finite table, the claimed layer is therefore limited to a cross-organism translation-statistic contact: the CUN/UUR residual changes sign across the sampled organisms and, in the recorded three-row table, is perfectly ordered with the annotated tRNA-Leu ratio under the declared control.

\paragraph{Support and break conditions.}
The support condition is satisfied only at the level of the supplied finite rows: codon-usage summaries and tRNA-Leu gene-copy annotations are present for the three named organisms, the sign vector is non-uniform, and the partial correlation field is positive with value $1.0$.  The packet would fail under either of two local counter-readings: if all measured organisms had the same CUN/UUR sign regardless of tRNA-Leu spectrum, or if sign reversal occurred but the controlled paired statistic lost its positive association with the tRNA-Leu ratio.  The present row does not show either failure mode, so the conjectural record can be stated as ready for this bounded finite-data claim.

\paragraph{Local non-promotion rule.}
The result is deliberately narrow.  The codon-usage contact reports sequence-composition summaries, and the tRNA contact reports genome-annotation copy-number summaries.  These contacts permit the organism-level CUN/UUR versus tRNA-Leu statistic above; they do not establish a ribosome-mediated dynamical account, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, or a universal biological law.  No broader translation process, molecular mechanism, folded structure, functional phenotype, or cross-organism universality follows unless another independently specified contact supplies that evidence.  In particular, the negative yeast sign and positive human and bacterial signs are treated as context-dependent finite observations, not as a rule that must persist outside the measured rows.
