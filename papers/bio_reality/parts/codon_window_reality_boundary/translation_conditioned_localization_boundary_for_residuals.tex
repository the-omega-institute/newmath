\subsection{Translation-conditioned localization boundary for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals}
\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-protein-localization-boundary-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
This record concerns a finite CDS-indexed surface with four kinds of coordinates: synonymous $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates $q_i$, matched translation readouts $T_j$, mRNA controls, and a downstream protein-localization readout slot $L_k$. The object is not a direct codon-to-localization law. It is a gated cross-layer question: whether a residual coordinate that survives the amino-acid quotient can be read through a translation-layer boundary before any localization interpretation is considered. The admissible readback has the form $q_i \to T_j \to L_k$, and the localization leg is interpreted only after the translation-conditioned boundary is passed.

The finite distinction is therefore local and ordered. First, a $q_i$ coordinate must survive as a synonymous residual coordinate after amino-acid composition has been quotiented away. Second, a translation readout must be measured or modeled separately from any localization outcome. Third, localization language is withheld unless the residual coordinate either survives against the translation readout or is explicitly conditioned through that translation boundary. In symbolic form, the admissible relation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i &\in B^{\ast}_{Q6},\\
T_j &\in \{\hbox{translation-efficiency or codon-adaptation readouts}\},\\
L_k &\in \{\hbox{matched protein-localization labels}\},\\
q_i \leadsto L_k &\quad \hbox{is readable only through } q_i \leadsto T_j \leadsto L_k.
\end{aligned}
$$
This ordering prevents synonymous residual geometry from being promoted into protein localization, trafficking, compartment identity, or mechanism by notation alone.

\paragraph{Measured boundary test.}
The available finite run for this boundary used standard-library linear discriminant analysis with train-fold $z$-scoring and a ridge shared covariance estimate. The run identifier was \texttt{ab861f8b1269}; the recorded seed was \texttt{sha256:93467a1602ce6c3d589b8b5452f233689fb87fa1bbc0f686694db4bb01da7b1f}; the fold count was $5$; and the run status was recorded as passed. The joined localization rows were finite and organism-scoped: $1908$ CDS records for \emph{Danio rerio}, $10187$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, and $4304$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}. Thus the test is a finite held-out classification contact, not a universal biological assertion.

For the direct $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ localization-discrimination comparison, the observed held-out accuracy increments ranged from $-0.008385744234800874$ to $0.045548247766761585$, with mean $0.022068418153776248$ and median $0.029042750929368033$. Two of the three organism panels had positive accuracy increment. The macro-AUC increments were uniformly positive across the three panels, with minimum $0.073410178078059$, maximum $0.08299262296923093$, mean $0.07848683797295208$, and median $0.07905771287156632$. This supports a finite discriminatory contact between $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates and localization labels, but it does not by itself identify a translation mechanism or a localization mechanism.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned increment.}
The relevant boundary is the translation-conditioned increment. Under that comparison, the accuracy increments were positive in all three organism panels: minimum $0.017295597484276726$, maximum $0.02927509293680297$, mean $0.023049495216764453$, and median $0.02257779522921366$. The macro-AUC increments were also positive in all three panels: minimum $0.015494311048138809$, maximum $0.02573588939607574$, mean $0.021063283115180725$, and median $0.021959648901327622$. The finite call counts were $1$ panel crossing to the localization layer and $2$ panels remaining partial. Accordingly, the chapter-level verdict is partial rather than closed: the boundary has finite positive held-out signal, but the organism-level calls do not collapse into a stable universal rule.

A stricter conditioned comparison against translation, length, amino-acid composition, and GC-related controls weakens the localization leg. In that comparison, the accuracy increment ranged from $-0.010482180293501064$ to $0.010455390334572545$, with mean $-0.000008929986309506402$ and median $0.0$; only one of the three organism panels was positive by accuracy. The macro-AUC increments were all non-positive, with minimum $-0.002467684182081209$, maximum $-0.0008777321759119161$, mean $-0.001407768101771234$, and median $-0.000877887947320577$. This is the decisive boundary record: the data allow a partial finite localization-discrimination signal, but the signal does not survive the stronger conditioned test as an organism-stable localization claim.

\paragraph{Local null statement.}
The null boundary is not a claim that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residuals are biologically irrelevant. It says only that the available finite contact supports a translation-conditioned boundary and a partial held-out localization signal, while refusing a direct localization conclusion. Codon-usage tables, tAI or stAI weights, ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, and matched mRNA abundance controls occupy different layers. Codon usage records sequence composition; tAI or stAI supplies modeled codon-adaptation weights; ribosome profiling can supply translation-efficiency readouts when paired with mRNA abundance; and mRNA abundance separates transcript-layer signal from translation-layer signal. None of these contacts alone supplies folded structure, mature protein function, physical admissibility, trafficking mechanism, compartment mechanism, or a global biological law.

The permitted finite reading is therefore narrow. A $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate may be studied as a candidate contributor to localization only after the amino-acid quotient, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, and mRNA controls are respected, and only through a translation-readout boundary. Protein abundance is not a substitute for matched localization. A modeled tAI or stAI association is not ribosome occupancy. A positive held-out classifier increment is not a mechanism. The present record says that the translation-conditioned boundary is empirically nonempty in the finite run and that the downstream localization interpretation remains partial, controlled, and needs stronger matched localization contact before it can be promoted beyond the boundary statement.
