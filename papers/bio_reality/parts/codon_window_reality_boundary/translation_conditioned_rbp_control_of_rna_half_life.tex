\subsection{Translation-conditioned RBP control of RNA half-life}\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-rbp-rna-half-life-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite claim.}
The record concerns a finite panel of synonymously editable coding RNA windows.  Each row contains an amino-acid-matched RNA/CDS window, a residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a measured local occupancy state for a prespecified decay-factor RBP, a perturbation condition, and the matched transcriptional and mRNA controls needed to keep the half-life statistic from being read as a translation statistic.  The intended positive claim is deliberately narrow: a coordinate-associated RNA half-life residual may be treated as a bounded cross-layer relation only if it survives the translation-readout boundary and the matched controls, tracks measured decay-factor RBP occupancy when coordinate and occupancy are experimentally separated, collapses under loss of the target RBP, returns under wild-type rescue, and is not restored by a binding-defective rescue or by a non-target control.

\paragraph{Row schema.}
A usable row has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
  x_i = (&w_i, q_i, t_i, o_i, c_i, h_i, z_i, T_i),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $w_i$ is the matched RNA/CDS window, $q_i$ is the high-coordinate or low-coordinate synonymous residual label inside an amino-acid-matched editable window, $t_i$ is the same-row translation readout, $o_i$ is measured occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP in the local window, $c_i$ is the perturbation or rescue condition, $h_i$ is the controlled RNA half-life residual, $z_i$ is the block of transcription, abundance, structure, motif, GC and dinucleotide, codon-pair, splice or isoform, batch, and translation-readout covariates, and $T_i$ is the declared matched-null statistic.  The null comparison exchanges high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels after these controls.  Under that null, the coordinate label is not allowed to explain half-life once transcription rate, transcript abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, base-composition context, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, batch, amino-acid composition, and same-row translation readout have been absorbed.

The present finite-row bundle has one quantitative verified comparison attached under the identifier $\texttt{h3.cross\_layer\_relation.recombination\_hotspot.prdm9\_motif\_dsb\_crossdataset\_pratto2014}$.  Its recorded run is $\texttt{a5e9df27ecd0}$, verified at $2026$-$06$-$23$ $01{:}45{:}58$ UTC, with row counts $n_a = 21636$ and $n_c = 21512$, and runtime $187.044$ seconds.  Those numbers are not evidence for the RBP/RNA-half-life mechanism itself; they serve only as a concrete finite-row precedent for a different cross-layer comparison in the linked record family.  The RBP half-life claim identified by $\texttt{q6.translation-conditioned.rbp-rna-half-life.cross-layer}$ therefore remains a conjectural row specification unless a same-window RBP-occupancy and perturbation panel is attached.

\paragraph{Permitted readout.}
The RNA half-life contact can supply externally curated per-gene or per-transcript half-life values, keyed by organism-specific identifiers and dataset provenance.  In this chapter it functions as a half-life boundary condition, not as a direct mechanism assay.  The ribosome-profiling contact can supply per-CDS ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, with translation efficiency treated as $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$ when matched mRNA abundance is available.  The matched mRNA abundance contact separates transcription-layer signal from translation-layer signal by giving the denominator or covariate needed for the translation-efficiency residual.  These contacts jointly specify what must be present before a coordinate-associated RNA decay statistic can be considered: RNA half-life, same-row translation readout, matched mRNA abundance, and the declared covariate block must all be aligned to the same finite window or to an explicitly justified organism-specific identifier join.

\paragraph{Decoupling requirement.}
A positive statistic must distinguish coordinate association from RBP-mediated decay.  Let $H$ denote the controlled half-life residual and let $O$ denote measured local occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP.  The test cannot stop at
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H \not\perp q \mid z,
\end{aligned}
$$
because that statement is only a finite RNA-stability association.  The stronger cross-layer claim requires the effect to follow occupancy when coordinate and occupancy are experimentally decoupled:
$$
\begin{aligned}
  H &\not\perp O \mid q,z,t, \\
  H &\text{ loses the coordinate-associated contrast under target-RBP loss}, \\
  H &\text{ regains that contrast under wild-type rescue}, \\
  H &\text{ does not regain it under binding-defective or non-target control.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The binding-defective and non-target conditions are essential because an expression-level rescue, stress response, or generic RNA-binding perturbation could otherwise mimic a coordinate-conditioned half-life shift.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, RBP mechanism, or universal biological rule follows from RNA half-life alone.  A translation readout may constrain the boundary between RNA decay and translation, but it does not by itself establish protein output.  A modelled tAI or stAI value, a residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate, or an organism-level codon table is likewise not an observed ribosome-occupancy measurement.  If measured decay-factor RBP occupancy is absent, if target-RBP loss does not collapse the effect, or if wild-type rescue does not restore it while the binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it, the result must remain a finite RNA-stability association inside the assayed panel.

\paragraph{Decision status.}
The chapter is not ready as a positive BioReality claim because the attached finite numbers do not include the needed RBP/RNA-half-life panel.  What is presently licensed is the experimental shape: high-coordinate versus low-coordinate synonymous residual labels can be compared inside amino-acid-matched editable RNA windows, but promotion to a cross-layer RBP-decay bridge requires same-window half-life residuals, translation readouts, measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective or non-target controls, and a declared matched-null statistic.  Until those rows are present, the claim remains a well-specified conjecture with a clear failure mode rather than an established biological mechanism.
