\subsection{Rank-admissible gate for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation survival}
\label{subsec:q6-rank-admissible-translation-survival-gate-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object studied here is not a protein-level assertion.  It is a finite residual design problem built from synonymous $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates, organism-indexed readout columns, and control columns $Z$.  The available finite coupling computation used nine synonymous residual coordinates, namely $\mathrm{Arg\_AGR}$, $\mathrm{Ile\_AUA}$, $\mathrm{K\_AAA}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR}$, $\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG}$, $\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY}$, $\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY}$, and $f3\_\mathrm{stress}$, inside the $61$-codon count space after keeping the amino-acid quotient explicit.  The organisms loaded for that coupling readback were $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, and $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12}$.  Against the proxy tRNA-Leu $\mathrm{CUN}$-versus-$\mathrm{UUR}$ log-copy spectrum, all nine entries were nonzero at the $0.01$ coupling threshold and the largest absolute entry was $1882464.1106377328$.  This is evidence for a finite contact between codon-residual geometry and an organism-level proxy spectrum; it is not yet a survival statement for translation.

\paragraph{Rank test.}
For a proposed survival entry $S^{QT}_{ij}$, let $q_i$ be one synonymous residual coordinate, let $T_j$ be a translation-layer readout, and let $Z$ contain the baseline controls: amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, and M-density.  The gate first removes the control span and only then asks whether the surviving row space can falsify the entry on held-out rows.  In matrix terms the admissibility certificate is the tuple of residual rank, remaining degrees of freedom, readout identity, and a refusal flag for saturated designs:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde q_i &= (I-P_Z)q_i,\\
\widetilde T_j &= (I-P_Z)T_j,\\
X_{ij} &= [\,Z\ \widetilde q_i\,],\\
\mathrm{df}_{ij} &= n-\mathrm{rank}(X_{ij}),\\
\mathrm{admissible}_{ij} &= \bigl(\mathrm{rank}(X_{ij})<n\bigr)\wedge \bigl(\mathrm{df}_{ij}>0\bigr)\wedge \bigl(\widetilde T_j\neq 0\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
If this predicate fails, a positive in-sample score, a perfect fitted $R^2$, or an $A^{QT}$ coupling entry remains a projection artifact or a coupling-only contact.  The local rule is deliberately asymmetric: it can reject promotion from codon-topology residuals to translation survival, but it cannot by itself prove ribosome occupancy, protein abundance, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, mechanism, phenotype, or a universal biological law.

\paragraph{What the current finite rows do support.}
The strongest direct finite support presently available is the coupling matrix contact.  Its break condition was defined as null when all nine $q_i$ have $|A_{ij}|<0.01$ against the proxy readout; that break condition was not met.  The result therefore says that the proxy contact is not empty.  The same run explicitly records that true survival would require ribosome profiling, stAI or tAI, or protein-abundance readouts under controls, and that the codon-count plus tRNA-copy proxy does not establish ribosome-mediated realization.  Thus the present chapter uses the coupling computation as a warning case: large or nonzero $A^{QT}$ entries are exactly the situation where the rank gate is needed, because the fitted contact can look decisive while the residualized held-out survival design is still inadmissible.

\paragraph{Cross-readout context.}
The broader finite panel shows that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual structure can persist after several readout attempts.  In the residual-basis extraction run, four organisms were computed and two were marked as needing data.  The computed persistent organisms were $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12\ MG1655}$, $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, and $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$; $\mathrm{Gallus\ gallus}$ and $\mathrm{Mycobacterium\ smegmatis\ str\ MC2\ 155}$ lacked sufficient joined support.  The joined row counts were $14410$ for $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $3415$ for $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12\ MG1655}$, $16008$ for $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, and $3046$ for $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$.  The final residual fractions stayed high: $0.9791828971490305$ for $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, $0.9979478314995476$ for $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12\ MG1655}$, $0.9723341658092314$ for $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, and $0.9798667977250413$ for $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, with a reported floor of $0.97$ and no organism classified as compressible.  The regularizer used in that extraction was $\lambda=0.01$ with description length penalty $\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.

\paragraph{Null comparisons already visible.}
The same residual-basis run gives concrete degrees-of-freedom-matched null comparisons for selected readout blocks.  In $\mathrm{Danio\ rerio}$, the measured tRNA-tAI step had increment $0.02081710285096942$ against null $95$th percentile $0.0002306374780841125$.  In $\mathrm{Escherichia\ coli\ K12\ MG1655}$, the mRNA step had increment $0.0017706954554909888$ against $0.0013371669456573104$, while the measured translation-efficiency step had increment $0.0002819723320884449$ against $0.0010272364744326006$ and did not clear the null.  In $\mathrm{Homo\ sapiens}$, measured tRNA-tAI appears at step $4$ with increment $0.0005032876020244136$ against $0.00020173994173736226$.  In $\mathrm{Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae}$, measured translation efficiency appears at step $1$ with increment $0.003750101033636422$ against $0.0011272729395622515$, while measured tRNA-tAI appears at step $6$ with increment $0.0008239697005207827$ against $0.0012089302784270603$ and does not clear the null.  These numbers are read as local finite-row diagnostics.  They do not replace the per-entry rank certificate for $S^{QT}_{ij}$, because the survival gate is entrywise and must be evaluated after the chosen $Z$ controls are fixed.

\paragraph{Protein and regulatory contacts kept separate.}
A yeast execution-selection computation supplies a useful negative comparator.  It used $5684$ active genes, $5815$ base rows, $5810$ ordered CDS joins, and $5684$ occupancy-dwell joins.  Its active-gene-set baseline $R^2$ was $0.30732228260918726$, the full-gene-set baseline $R^2$ was $0.29837624994140827$, and the finite norms were $\|P_Q\|^2=864.0124192674945$ and $\|P_{\mathrm{residual}}\|^2=2811.4213259512776$.  With five folds and $240$ target permutations, the verdict was $\mathrm{composition\_artifact}$ rather than a boundary crossing.  Separately, a human and mouse mRNA stability contact reported $n_{\mathrm{human}}=16500$, $n_{\mathrm{mouse}}=18924$, and raw $\rho=0.126224$ under a non-CSC tAI circularity guard.  A translation-initiation contact reported $41862$ fixed rows, $16739$ varying rows, and two-sample concordance.  A branchpoint motif contact reported $10584$ rows with expression controlled by log-transformed median GTEx tissue expression.  These contacts show that finite biological readouts exist at several layers, but they remain layer-specific controls and comparisons; none licenses a shortcut from codon residual rank to realized protein-level effect.

\paragraph{Chapter verdict.}
The admissible conclusion is a rank-gated protocol, not a positive translation-survival result.  A proposed $S^{QT}_{ij}$ may be reported only when the residualized row space is non-saturated, the post-control degrees of freedom are positive, the readout is held out or otherwise falsifiable, and the improvement survives the agreed baseline ablation.  When those conditions are absent, the entry is recorded as coupling-only or needs-data even if the in-sample fit is positive, visually strong, or numerically perfect.  The current finite evidence therefore supports the necessity of the gate and documents several concrete contacts, while withholding translation realization until a matched, rank-admissible readout supplies it.
