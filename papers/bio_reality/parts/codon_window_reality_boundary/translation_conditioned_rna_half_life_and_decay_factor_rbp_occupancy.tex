\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP occupancy}
\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite row type.}
This record concerns a finite matched panel of coding transcript rows in which each row carries a synonymous residual coordinate label after amino-acid quotienting, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcription rate, transcript abundance, splice or isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and amino-acid composition. The coordinate contrast is not a raw codon-count contrast. It is the high-coordinate versus low-coordinate residual label after the amino-acid sequence has already been held fixed. The proposed biological contact is therefore narrower than an RNA-stability association: the half-life residual must be conditioned on the same-row translation readout and must track an experimentally separated local RBP occupancy axis.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The null condition exchanges high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels within the matched control strata. Under this null, once transcription, abundance, isoform state, local structure, composition, codon-pair context, batch, amino-acid content, and the same-row translation readout are fixed, the coordinate label carries no remaining information about RNA half-life. A finite positive readout is admitted only when the observed half-life residual is accompanied by the full decay-factor RBP pattern: local target-RBP occupancy differs at the same window, target-RBP loss collapses the coordinate-associated half-life effect, wild-type target-RBP rescue restores it, and binding-defective or non-target controls fail to restore it. A half-life difference without that perturbation and rescue pattern remains an RNA-stability association, not an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Statistic to be reported.}
For a finite panel $P$, let $H_i \in \{0,1\}$ mark the high-coordinate residual class, let $T_i$ be the same-row translation readout, let $L_i$ be the RNA half-life readout, and let $O_i$ record measured local occupancy of the specified decay-factor RBP. Let $C_i$ collect the matched transcriptional, abundance, isoform, structural, composition, codon-context, batch, and amino-acid controls. The permitted statistic is a bounded contrast of translation-conditioned half-life residuals, reported with the occupancy and perturbation indicators on the same rows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_L
&= \operatorname{contrast}\bigl(L_i \mid H_i, T_i, C_i\bigr),\\
\Delta_O
&= \operatorname{contrast}\bigl(O_i \mid H_i, C_i\bigr),\\
\mathcal{R}
&= \bigl(\Delta_L,\Delta_O,\Delta_L^{\mathrm{loss}},\Delta_L^{\mathrm{WT\ rescue}},\Delta_L^{\mathrm{binding\ defect}},\Delta_L^{\mathrm{non\ target}}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
The readout is favorable only if $\Delta_L$ is present in the translation-conditioned model, $\Delta_O$ is present at the editable window, the loss condition removes the effect, wild-type rescue returns it, and the binding-defective and non-target controls do not return it. This formula is a reporting contract for finite rows; it does not license asymptotic generalization or a sequence-wide biological law.

\paragraph{Reality contacts already available.}
The surrounding BioReality ledger contains several finite cross-layer examples that motivate the row discipline but do not themselves prove this RNA half-life claim. A methylation flanking-context contact reports $n=20000$ probes across $656$ individuals with baseline $R^2=0.0129735$ and no recorded cross-cohort scalar. An RBP affinity and binding contact reports $900$ total peaks with $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases and runtime $223.648043$ seconds. A PRDM9 meiotic-recombination contact reports $52963$ hotspots and runtime $168.443$ seconds. A polyadenylation-tail contact reports $8854$ human rows, $3990$ yeast rows, and a negative cross-species concordance flag. An mTOR translation contact reports $6494$ rows from the GSE36892 readout source with runtime $9.469$ seconds. An N-terminal acetylation contact reports $657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, $622$ high-low AUROC rows, AUROC $0.942081$, and runtime $1.1687$ seconds. A lysine-acetylation contact reports $6288$ rows, $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries, and runtime $8.456$ seconds. These numbers show that the ledger accepts bounded finite-row surfaces with explicit row counts and scalar summaries, but none of them substitutes for the matched RNA half-life, translation, local RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue panel required here.

\paragraph{Exclusion boundary.}
The claim is deliberately local. RNA half-life alone does not establish translation realization. A translation-conditioned half-life residual does not establish folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality. A modeled tAI or stAI value is not an observed ribosome-occupancy measurement unless a translation contact supplies that measurement. A motif-density model, RNA-structure-only model, transcription-rate model, transcript-abundance model, isoform-state model, GC or dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model cannot be counted as support for the decay-factor RBP relation. Likewise, measured RBP occupancy without target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue is not enough; perturbation and rescue are part of the finite-row claim rather than optional mechanistic decoration.

\paragraph{Current status.}
The present record is a conjectural row specification with a sharp admissibility test. It is ready as a bounded BioReality chapter because the row type, null, required controls, forbidden promotions, and finite reporting statistic are explicit. It is not a positive biological result until a same-organism panel supplies, on matched rows, the B*\_Q6 residual labels, translation readout, RNA half-life readout, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target control measurements.
