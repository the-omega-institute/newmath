\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life and decay-factor RBP occupancy}
\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of coding transcripts.  Each row carries a synonymous residual-coordinate label at a $B^{*}_{Q6}$ window after quotienting by amino-acid sequence, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and matched controls for transcription rate, abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, and batch.  The row is not read as a protein-output or phenotype row.  It is read only as a bounded question about whether a residual synonymous coordinate remains associated with RNA half-life after the translation boundary has been attached, and whether that association follows an experimentally separated decay-factor RBP occupancy perturbation and rescue pattern.

\paragraph{Coordinate split and statistic.}
Let $H$ and $L$ denote the high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels assigned to synonymous windows after the amino-acid quotient.  For row $i$, write $T_i$ for the matched translation readout, $A_i$ for mRNA abundance, $D_i$ for the RNA half-life readout, $O_i$ for measured local occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP, and $C_i$ for the finite vector of transcription, structure, motif, composition, codon-pair, isoform, abundance, and batch controls.  The admissible comparison is the residual contrast
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_D
  &= \widehat{E}[D_i \mid H_i, T_i, C_i]
   - \widehat{E}[D_i \mid L_i, T_i, C_i],\\
\Delta_O
  &= \widehat{E}[O_i \mid H_i, T_i, C_i]
   - \widehat{E}[O_i \mid L_i, T_i, C_i].
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive bridge is allowed only when the half-life residual is accompanied by the local RBP-occupancy residual, disappears under target-RBP loss, returns under wild-type rescue, and fails to return under binding-defective or non-target rescue.  Without all four experimental signs in the same finite matched design, the coordinate remains a descriptor or, at most, a finite RNA-stability association.

\paragraph{Reality anchors already available.}
The linked finite contacts show that the required layers are not purely notational, but they do not yet supply the full bridge panel.  The RBP binding calibration run \texttt{b225890251c2} contains $900$ total peaks and reports $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, with runtime $223.648043$ seconds.  The translation-contact run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9} contains $6494$ rows from the Thoreen mTOR translation dataset and records runtime $9.469$ seconds.  Other cross-layer rows give the scale of the existing finite reality interface: the methylation-context run \texttt{bd781331dc42} used $20000$ probes from $656$ individuals and had baseline $R^2=0.0129735$; the PRDM9 hotspot run \texttt{e57a09da690c} used $52963$ hotspots; the N-terminal acetylation run \texttt{f452e76f6363} used $657$ rows, including $426$ NatA rows, with AUROC $0.942081$ on $622$ high--low rows; the poly(A)-tail grammar run \texttt{9581dc27a332} used $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows and did not report cross-species concordance; the lysine-acetylation run \texttt{4c330420a1bf} used $6288$ rows with $5921$ distinct stoichiometry entries; and the pseudouridylation run \texttt{cf233b5f0446} used $2040$ rows.  These numbers support only the existence of finite, audited readout surfaces at adjacent layers.  They do not by themselves establish the RNA half-life/RBP rescue pattern required here.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The matched null states that, conditional on amino-acid sequence, translation readout, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, and batch, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life.  In symbols, the claim tested is not merely $\Delta_D \neq 0$; it is the stronger finite pattern
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_D \neq 0,
\qquad
\Delta_O \neq 0,
\qquad
\Delta_D^{\mathrm{target\ loss}} \approx 0,
\qquad
\Delta_D^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} \approx \Delta_D,
\qquad
\Delta_D^{\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}} \approx 0.
\end{aligned}
$$
A half-life difference without measured local occupancy, or without loss and rescue behavior, is rejected as an RBP-decay bridge.  Conversely, an occupancy difference without the translation-conditioned half-life residual is also insufficient for this record.

\paragraph{Readback rule.}
The accepted readback is a finite matched-panel statement: after the same-row translation readout and all listed controls are attached, high-coordinate windows differ from low-coordinate windows in RNA half-life; the same windows differ in local occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP; loss of that target RBP collapses the half-life residual; wild-type rescue restores it; and binding-defective or non-target controls do not restore it.  The rule is deliberately asymmetric.  It permits a narrow cross-layer RNA-decay bridge only through measured local occupancy and perturbation/rescue behavior.  It does not permit promotion from codon geometry, tAI or stAI weights, transcript abundance, motif density, RNA structure, or batch separation alone.

\paragraph{Local limits.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone.  No folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism beyond the declared decay-factor RBP test, or universal biological law follows from this chapter.  Protein abundance, protein state, structure, function, and organismal outcome require separate layer-matched contacts.  If the finite panel lacks same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue, the result remains an RNA stability surface rather than the cross-layer bridge described here.
