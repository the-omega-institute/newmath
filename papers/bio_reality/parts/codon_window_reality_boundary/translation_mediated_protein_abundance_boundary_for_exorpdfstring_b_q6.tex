\subsection{Translation-mediated protein-abundance boundary for  exorpdfstring{$B^*_{Q6}$}{B*Q6}}
\label{sec:q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The subject is the finite mediation surface whose rows have the form $(q_i,T,P)$ after residualization against the declared controls.  The coordinate side contains the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The translation readout $T$ is the per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as the codon-count-weighted average of organism-specific tAI weights from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The protein readout $P$ is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ from measured PAXdb abundance joined to real CDS codon counts.  The control block has rank $23$ in every organism-specific fit and includes the intercept, CDS length, amino-acid composition, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline; the fitted $Q$ block has rank $9$, the translation block has rank $1$, and the joint $(Q,T)$ block has rank $10$ in each reported organism.

\paragraph{Mediated statistic.}
For each organism, let $c=R^2(P\sim Q\mid Z)$ and $c'=R^2(P\sim Q\mid Z,T)$, both read after the same $Z$-residualization.  The reported scalar is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{c-c'}{c},\\
\Delta^{QTP} &= c-c'.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a finite statistical decomposition over matched CDS, modeled translation, and protein-abundance rows.  It is not a causal effect.  In the main powered run the surface covers twelve organisms with joined protein counts $4042$, $11661$, $14410$, $13295$, $3739$, $12418$, $19053$, $19395$, $4129$, $13322$, $5815$, and $1656$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} 168, \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \emph{Danio rerio}, \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, \emph{Gallus gallus}, human, mouse, \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} mc2 155, rat, budding yeast, and \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}, respectively.  The corresponding mediated fractions are $0.608459$, $0.487227$, $0.315705$, $0.616517$, $0.612922$, $0.197690$, $0.272063$, $0.154888$, $0.083976$, $0.174897$, $0.949155$, and $0.018507$.  All twelve are in the unit interval, so the finite statistic is internally ranked and numerically interpretable on the declared panel.

\paragraph{Organism-level readback.}
The strongest fraction occurs in budding yeast, where $c=0.2983762499$, $c'=0.0151709094$, and $c-c'=0.2832053406$ over $5815$ joined proteins.  The large yeast value is not read as a universal law, because the same computation gives smaller but positive finite fractions in human $(0.272063)$, mouse $(0.154888)$, rat $(0.174897)$, chicken $(0.197690)$, zebrafish $(0.315705)$, fly $(0.616517)$, worm $(0.487227)$, \emph{E. coli} $(0.612922)$, \emph{B. subtilis} $(0.608459)$, \emph{M. smegmatis} $(0.083976)$, and \emph{S. solfataricus} $(0.018507)$.  The residual degrees of freedom after controls range from $1633$ in \emph{S. solfataricus} to $19372$ in mouse.  The per-protein tAI construction used total all-tRNA copy counts $86$, $581$, $8874$, $290$, $90$, $288$, $432$, $407$, $49$, $417$, $275$, and $47$ over the same organism order; geometric-mean filling for zero raw wobble weights occurred in only four organism panels, with counts $3$, $1$, $1$, and $1$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, \emph{E. coli}, \emph{M. smegmatis}, and \emph{S. solfataricus}.

\paragraph{Translation contact before protein readout.}
The direct residual-to-protein association is not treated as an admissible protein-realization claim unless the residual first survives a translation-readout step.  A separate modeled-tAI boundary run computed the same nine residual coordinates on three organisms and found $81$ entries above the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximum absolute modeled coupling $3801961.031607896$.  That run used $61$ sense codons and found no zero raw weight fallback in \emph{E. coli} K12, human, or budding yeast.  Its own report explicitly remains underpowered for a strong survival claim because $3$ organisms are below the required multi-organism powered condition.  The present twelve-organism mediation panel therefore supports a controlled $Q\to T\to P$ abundance decomposition with modeled translation weights; it does not convert modeled tAI into observed ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance interpretation.}
The claim supported here is narrow: after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, CDS length, and $M$-density controls, the finite rows admit a positive translation-conditioned attenuation of the $B^*_{Q6}$ residual association with matched protein abundance.  The result is strongest as a cross-sectional decomposition over joined CDS, tAI, and PAXdb rows.  It does not by itself establish translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, pathway mechanism, phenotype, selection, or universality.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control route, not an independent translation readout; PAXdb abundance is a protein-level measurement but is not isolated from mRNA level, degradation, localization, post-translational modification, folding state, or pathway context.

\paragraph{Adjacent finite checks.}
Several neighboring finite rows constrain the reading.  A yeast five-way structure mediation panel joined $4759$ genes and found $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.1377396770$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.1375901121$, with structure-only fraction $0.0032845645$ and structure-incremental fraction $0.0005507779$; this keeps the protein-abundance result separate from a folded-structure claim.  A dynamic RPF-protein closure audit on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals reported $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628202$, $R^2_{L1}=0.9964106289$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443378$, $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568$, $\Delta R^2=-0.0758443809$, $p_{TEbin}=0.6831683168$, and $p_{timeshuffle}=0.9306930693$, so that route does not promote the mediation row into a mass-balance closure law.  A turnover audit on $3290$ genes reported $R^2_{L2}=0.0557408516$, $R^2_{L3}=0.0502788722$, $\Delta R^2=-0.0054619794$, $p_{matchedbin}=0.8855721393$, and $p_{recoded}=1.0$, which blocks a degradation-rate interpretation.  An evolutionary-imprint run over $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species had $\Delta R^2=0.0325051502$ but leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477730$ and null95 $0.0542715707$, so it reports codon-bias expression correlation rather than a strong selection-imprint result.  A separate observable-execution decomposition in budding yeast used $3289$ genes and did find at least one rate proxy slot passing two null classes; that confirms that rate-like subblocks can be finite readouts, but it is not a license to infer a mechanism from the present abundance statistic.

\paragraph{Boundary verdict.}
The finite-row verdict is therefore positive only at the controlled mediation level.  The admissible readback is a matrix of residual coordinates passing through modeled translation weights before reaching matched protein abundance, with the organism-level $M^{QTP}$ values listed above.  The cannot-claim boundary is local to this chapter: no observed translation mechanism, ribosome dynamics, protein folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, system phenotype, evolutionary law, or universal biological principle follows unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies that measurement and a separately declared finite statistic supports it.  The phase-level genetic-code contact, with $40/40$ successes for both boundary and independent replication tests, binomial upper-tail probability $9.094947017729282\times10^{-13}$, and $0/20$ unexpected successes in both negative and ablation controls, remains a codon-table contact; it does not promote this protein-abundance mediation result above its measured layer.
