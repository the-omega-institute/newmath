\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein-stability boundary for $B^{*}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-protein-stability-boundary}

\paragraph{Finite panel.}
This record concerns a finite organism-by-coding-sequence panel in which the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinates are residual coordinates, not amino-acid composition proxies.  The coordinate family has nine entries: $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The regression controls used before any downstream interpretation are an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions computed from real CDS codon counts, GC3, and an $M$-density baseline defined on the union support of the nine projected $B^{*}_{Q6}$ vectors.  Thus each admissible row has the schematic form $q_i \to T_j \to P_k$, where $q_i$ is a residual codon-coordinate entry, $T_j$ is a matched translation readout, and $P_k$ is a stability, degradation, turnover, or abundance-side readout when such a contact exists.

\paragraph{Translation gate.}
The downstream stability question is not opened from $B^{*}_{Q6}$ alone.  It is opened only after a translation contact survives the same row-matched control discipline.  In the structural-order companion computation, the row join was nearly complete for the two tested organisms: $3736$ matched proteins out of $3739$ codon-joined entries in \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, with hit rate $0.999198$, and $5804$ matched proteins out of $5815$ codon-joined entries in \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, with hit rate $0.998108$.  The controlled design retained rank $9$ for the nine residual coordinates in both organisms.  The control matrix had $24$ columns, rank $23$, and the residual degrees of freedom after controls were $3713$ in \emph{E. coli} and $5781$ in yeast.  The associated predicted-structure-confidence associations were finite but not mechanistic: $R^2_{SO}=0.03059081709413562$ in \emph{E. coli} and $R^2_{SO}=0.0715885627979685$ in yeast, both interpreted only as cross-sectional associations with AlphaFold mean pLDDT on the $0$ to $100$ scale.

\paragraph{Stability-side contact.}
The protein-stability boundary computation used five folds, minimum joined row count $300$, and seed $\mathrm{sha256}:1deaaafe98cd16982d810af72f6389a20be0dc19cbd46bc966593dd507bad122$.  Three organism-level stability joins were available.  In \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655 the primary stability-side readout was mRNA half-life, with $2517$ rows.  In \emph{Homo sapiens} the primary readout was protein turnover, with $6697$ protein-turnover rows and $11322$ mRNA-half-life rows available.  In yeast the primary readout was protein turnover, with $3537$ protein-turnover rows and $4759$ mRNA-half-life rows available.  Before translation conditioning, the direct $B^{*}_{Q6}$ stability-side predictive summary was positive across the three joined panels: minimum $0.018837736273789707$, median $0.027727034742559664$, mean $0.02753157569086806$, and maximum $0.03602995605625481$.

\paragraph{Conditioned verdict.}
After full controls and translation conditioning, the aggregate stability-side increment was not a robust positive effect.  The full-control $\Delta R^2$ summary over the three organism panels had minimum $-0.004953854025603666$, median $-0.0033500708066446228$, mean $-0.0025553576666936064$, maximum $0.0006378518321674687$, two negative entries, and one positive entry.  The weighted full-control $\Delta R^2$ was $-0.001572140012981015$, and the sign-flip probability for being greater than zero was $0.946441650390625$.  A permuted full-control comparison was also negative throughout, with minimum $-0.004840148172275405$, median $-0.002926202164063474$, mean $-0.002753469686061827$, and maximum $-0.0004940587218466019$.  The organism calls were two partial entries and one mediated-blocked null entry, yielding the finite verdict ``partial'' rather than a stability mechanism.

\paragraph{Compression check.}
The same caution is reinforced by the residual-compressibility computation.  The tested residual source was not highly compressed by the measured downstream readout family.  The organism-specific compressibilities were $0.024368095798914635$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $0.005717621488177116$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast; the cross-organism median was reported as $0.026$.  The corresponding final residual fractions were $0.9756319042010854$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9736442326306781$, and $0.8676586345294415$.  Each organism-level permutation value was $0.000999000999000999$, but the small compression values mean that the measured readout family does not absorb most of $P_Q$.  The energy and row scales were $605.0092168669606$ over $13140$ full rows for zebrafish, $225.84556319953032$ over $2517$ rows for \emph{E. coli}, $286.3673197177688$ over $11250$ rows for human, and $229.86450794006544$ over $4759$ rows for yeast.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The admissible conclusion is therefore narrow.  $B^{*}_{Q6}$ may be carried into a protein-stability or degradation-rate table only as a translation-conditioned residual association in a matched finite panel.  It is not evidence, by itself, for translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, mature protein function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or a universal biological rule.  A modeled codon-adaptation score is also not survival evidence for protein stability.  Any stronger sentence must be supplied by a separate contact at the corresponding layer, and the direct $q_i \to P_k$ association must be reported together with the conditioned $q_i \to T_j \to P_k$ comparison.
