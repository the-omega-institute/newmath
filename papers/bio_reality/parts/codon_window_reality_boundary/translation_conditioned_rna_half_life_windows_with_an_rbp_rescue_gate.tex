\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life Windows with an RBP Rescue Gate}
\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or coding-sequence rows carrying a synonymous residual coordinate label $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, a local occupancy measurement for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and perturbation controls for that same protein.  The coordinate contrast is not a codon-usage claim by itself: high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows are compared only after amino-acid identity has been quotiented away and after transcription rate, abundance, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, isoform state, and batch have been placed on the same matched panel.  The object being tested is therefore a bounded cross-layer RNA-stability relation: a residual coding-coordinate contrast may be read as an RBP-linked half-life effect only when the half-life residual is conditioned on the translation row and follows the declared RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern.

\paragraph{Null row exchangeability.}
The finite null is the row permutation that preserves the transcript/CDS joins, the amino-acid quotient, the same-row translation readout, and all declared controls while exchanging the high-coordinate and low-coordinate residual labels.  In that null, the coordinate label has no remaining relation to the RNA half-life residual.  A half-life difference that survives ordinary covariate control but lacks the RBP bridge remains only an RNA-stability association.  In compact notation, the intended statistic has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}\mid\mathrm{TE}}
  &= \operatorname{stat}\bigl(\mathrm{HL}_{i}, B^{\ast}_{Q6,i} \mid \mathrm{TE}_{i}, X_{i}\bigr),\\
X_i
  &= \{\mathrm{transcription},\mathrm{abundance},\mathrm{structure},\mathrm{motif},\mathrm{GC},
       \mathrm{dinucleotide},\mathrm{codon\ pair},\mathrm{isoform},\mathrm{batch}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The statistic is not promoted unless the same rows also show experimentally decoupled local occupancy of the target decay-factor RBP, loss of the effect after target-RBP depletion or disruption, restoration by wild-type target-RBP rescue, and failure of restoration under binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Bridge criterion.}
The admissible positive pattern is conjunctive.  First, the coordinate-associated half-life residual must be visible only after the matched translation readout has been attached, so that RNA half-life alone is not mistaken for translation realization.  Second, the local RBP occupancy measurement must change at the editable RNA window in the direction required by the half-life contrast.  Third, removing or disabling the target RBP must collapse the coordinate-conditioned half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type target-RBP rescue must return the effect.  Fifth, a binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to return it.  These clauses make the record a finite cross-layer test rather than a descriptive enrichment statement.  Formally, the bridge predicate is the conjunction
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{B}_{Q6}^{\mathrm{RBP}}
  &= \Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}\mid\mathrm{TE}} \neq 0\\
  &\quad {}\wedge\ \Delta\mathrm{Occ}_{\mathrm{target\ RBP}} \neq 0\\
  &\quad {}\wedge\ \operatorname{collapse}(\mathrm{target\ loss})\\
  &\quad {}\wedge\ \operatorname{return}(\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue})\\
  &\quad {}\wedge\ \neg\operatorname{return}(\mathrm{binding\ defective\ or\ non\ target\ rescue}).
\end{aligned}
$$
If any conjunct is absent, the row panel may still report a finite RNA half-life boundary, but it does not license the RBP-decay interpretation.

\paragraph{Calibrating finite contacts.}
The attached finite evidence ledger shows why the present chapter uses a strict rescue gate.  Other cross-layer panels in the same reality program already have concrete row counts or scalar readouts: the methylation flanking-context panel contains $20{,}000$ probes from $656$ individuals with a baseline $R^2=0.0129735$; the RBNS/eCLIP binding contact reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases; the mTOR translation readout contains $6{,}494$ rows; the N-terminal acetylation contact has $657$ rows, including $426$ NatA rows, with AUROC $0.942081$ on $622$ high/low cases; the poly(A)-tail grammar contact carries $8{,}854$ human rows and $3{,}990$ yeast rows with cross-species concordance recorded as false; the lysine-acetylation stoichiometry contact has $6{,}288$ rows and $5{,}921$ distinct stoichiometry entries; the pseudouridylation contact has $2{,}040$ rows; the m6A stoichiometry contact has $60{,}000$ rows, $49{,}668$ distinct sites, cross-condition concordance true, and coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$; the m6Am Crown-seq contact has $31{,}886$ rows, $19{,}406$ distinct sites, $3{,}230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, $20{,}942$ variable-subset rows, and a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$.  In the m6Am finite statistic, the recorded GC-matched null p-value is $0.000833$, the full partial correlation is $0.268094$, the low-tail partial correlation is $0.098666$, and the variable-subset partial correlation is $0.168335$.  The recombination contact supplies $52{,}963$ hotspots, and the DNA-cyclizability run records runtime $196.505904$ seconds.  These numbers do not prove the present RNA half-life/RBP claim; they calibrate the expected standard for a row-bounded biological contact: a named panel, declared joins, explicit controls, finite scalars, and a stated failure mode.

\paragraph{Local contacts and present limitation.}
For the present RNA half-life bridge, the available contacts identify transcript or gene rows with curated mRNA half-life estimates, matched mRNA abundance controls, and ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency surfaces.  Those contacts can support the boundary that a half-life readout is being compared within organism-scoped transcript rows and that a translation-efficiency residual can be separated from mRNA abundance.  They do not by themselves provide same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective/non-target rescue behavior.  Consequently, the chapter records the finite test that would distinguish a coordinate-conditioned RBP-decay bridge from a mere RNA half-life association, while refusing to treat the half-life rows alone as a completed mechanism.

\paragraph{Cannot-claim boundary.}
No translation realization follows from an RNA half-life association unless the same-row translation readout is present and used as a conditioning boundary.  No decay-factor RBP mechanism follows unless local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue are all measured on the relevant finite panel.  No RNA-structure-only model, motif-density-only model, transcription-rate model, isoform-state model, abundance model, GC/dinucleotide model, codon-pair-context model, or batch model counts as positive evidence for the RBP bridge.  Finally, this record makes no claim about protein output, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, organismal phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality; each of those would require its own matched contact at the appropriate layer.
