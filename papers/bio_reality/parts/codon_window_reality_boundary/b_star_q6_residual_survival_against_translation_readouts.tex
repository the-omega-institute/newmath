\subsection{B-star-Q6 residual survival against translation readouts}
\label{sec:bioreality-translation-survival-b-star-q6-survival-matrix}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record tests whether the nine synonymous residual coordinates of $B^*_{Q6}$ retain predictive mass after the amino-acid quotient and the standard context controls are imposed.  The carrier is a $9 \times K$ matrix whose rows are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN/UUR},\ Leu_{UUA/UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR/AGY},\ Ser_{UCA/UCG},\ Thr_{ACR/ACY},\ f3\text{-stress}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The column index ranges over translation-facing readouts: modeled tAI or stAI weights, measured translation-efficiency rows when available, ribosome-occupancy summaries when available, and protein-abundance readouts only when they are kept as downstream abundance contacts.  For a coordinate $q_i$ and a readout $r_j$, the statistic is the controlled survival entry $S_{ij}=S_{q_i}(r_j\mid Z)$, where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism context, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline.  The local decision rule is strict: the matrix is supported only if at least one entry remains positive after the declared residualization and the simpler-codon-baseline ablation.  A nonzero coupling $A_{ij}$ is not enough; the entry must survive the $Z$-controlled partial-$R^2$ or description-length test.

\paragraph{Finite construction.}
The main powered row uses 40 organisms and a modeled per-codon tAI readout built from GtRNAdb anticodon copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  The nine $B^*_{Q6}$ residual coordinates were computed and the modeled tAI readout was projected into the same nine-coordinate synonymous residual basis.  The control design had 24 columns, rank $23$, and residual degrees of freedom $17$.  The residualized coordinate design had rank $9$, so the row is not the three-organism saturated surface that produced earlier all-one correlations.  The resulting modeled translation matrix has shape $9\times 9$ and total controlled survival
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579,\\
\operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 23,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X) &= 9,\\
\operatorname{df}_{\mathrm{res}} &= 17.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positive-entry threshold used for the powered modeled-tAI row was $10^{-12}$.  On this bounded modeled readout, $B^*_{Q6}$ is therefore not absorbed by the amino-acid, organism, $GC3$, length, and $M$-density model.  This is a translation-facing survival result for modeled adaptation weights, not a measured ribosome-occupancy result.

\paragraph{Proxy coupling and why it is weaker.}
A separate three-organism proxy contact computed a coupling matrix between the nine residual coordinates and a tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum.  The data rows were Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K-12.  All nine coordinates had nonzero proxy couplings above the stated $0.01$ threshold, and the largest absolute coupling was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}| &= 1882464.1106377328.
\end{aligned}
$$
The recorded break condition for that proxy row was false: not all coordinates had $|A_{ij}|<0.01$.  This is useful geometry, but it is deliberately kept below survival status.  The proxy row uses codon counts and tRNA-copy summaries; it does not contain ribosome profiling, measured elongation speed, protein output, folding, function, or a population-level law.

\paragraph{Cross-organism Leu sign check.}
The CUN/UUR Leu gate gives a concrete finite-row warning against reading directionality too strongly.  With the same three organisms, the partial Pearson value between the CUN/UUR usage ratio and the tRNA-Leu copy ratio was $1.0$ at $n=3$, but the signs of the organism-level log ratios were not uniform:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.3514980212896148,\ -0.24869078488829677,\ 1.0086640520051702).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the row supports the existence of a finite contact, not a universal positive direction.  The open eight-organism extension remains a required stress test: the three-organism Pearson value can be a small-row artefact unless the association remains positive under a nontrivial null after additional organisms are joined.

\paragraph{Organism sweep for modeled tAI.}
A broader modeled-tAI sweep covered 41 organisms with at least 500 genes where the data were usable.  The per-organism $R^2_{QT}$ distribution was finite and nondegenerate:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min R^2_{QT} &= 0.1579271837024072,\\
q_{25} &= 0.26353954700074694,\\
\operatorname{median} &= 0.33934845811598124,\\
\operatorname{mean} &= 0.38466555440501393,\\
q_{75} &= 0.5143951545826744,\\
\max R^2_{QT} &= 0.8213457220900503.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sweep used 1000 permutations and met the target of 40 organisms.  Its role is to show that the modeled readout is not confined to the original small finite row.  It still remains modeled tAI from gene-copy and wobble rules, not direct ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance rows as downstream contacts.}
Several abundance rows test whether the same residual coordinates also explain protein-level readouts, while remaining outside the direct translation-survival claim unless a translation step is explicitly present.  In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the condition-specific protein-abundance comparison joined 2209 shared proteins across YEPD and SD.  The controlled protein-abundance survival values were
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{YEPD}) &= 0.3280825603424665,\\
R^2_{QP}(\mathrm{SD}) &= 0.3050385538305022,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $f3$-stress dominant in both conditions, Pearson similarity $0.9983880256003688$, cosine similarity $0.9993775935182967$, and 1000 permutations.  In human tissue-specific protein-abundance rows, the joined counts and controlled $R^2_{QP}$ values were $9244$ and $0.03626117434944674$ for adult B cells, $6468$ and $0.02320896321347296$ for adult heart, and $8664$ and $0.026133456228248052$ for adult liver.  The dominant coordinates were $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $Ile_{AUA}$ respectively.  These rows show downstream abundance associations under the same coordinate grammar, but they do not by themselves certify measured translation.

\paragraph{Modeled-translation complement.}
For six organisms with protein abundance and modeled tAI contacts, the abundance component explained by $B^*_{Q6}$ was decomposed into the part aligned with modeled tAI and the complement.  The complement fractions $d$ and modeled-mediated fractions $m$ were recorded as
$$
\begin{aligned}
(d,m)_{\mathrm{Danio}} &= (0.874628721049266,\ 0.12537127895073397),\\
(d,m)_{\mathrm{Ecoli}} &= (0.6629960038578532,\ 0.3370039961421468),\\
(d,m)_{\mathrm{Gallus}} &= (0.9400341806302775,\ 0.05996581936972245),\\
(d,m)_{\mathrm{Human}} &= (0.9208949355679455,\ 0.07910506443205445),\\
(d,m)_{\mathrm{Mycobacterium}} &= (0.8972672018746489,\ 0.10273279812535108),\\
(d,m)_{\mathrm{Yeast}} &= (0.21173219572706703,\ 0.788267804272933).
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding controlled protein-abundance $R^2_{QP}$ values were $0.04613529805760884$, $0.10767526667768725$, $0.03980793595345331$, $0.04454565117192484$, $0.032090091625816136$, and $0.29837624987287115$.  These numbers make the downstream landscape explicit: yeast is strongly aligned with modeled tAI in this decomposition, while several metazoan and bacterial rows leave most of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component outside modeled tAI.

\paragraph{Supplementary readout ranking.}
The non-translation residual ranking used the direction-aware score $A_H C_H-\lambda DL_H$ with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$.  Danio rerio selected measured translation efficiency as the top auxiliary readout: $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, score $0.001566051846174291$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$, null $p95=0.0003297458343745959$, and $n=13140$.  Homo sapiens selected complex membership by the same ranking table, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, score $-0.0034339698846599055$, Bonferroni threshold $0.0002646171322365322$, null $p95=0.0002334419901244703$, and $n=18335$.  Saccharomyces cerevisiae selected mRNA, with $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, score $-0.008853737772559931$, Bonferroni threshold $0.0013014749238591248$, null $p95=0.0004745820831535835$, and $n=5128$.  Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 and Mycobacterium smegmatis did not receive a stable selected auxiliary readout in this table.  The ranking is descriptive support accounting, not a pathway assignment.

\paragraph{mRNA stability comparisons.}
The same nine-coordinate design was also tested against mRNA half-life or consensus stability readouts.  In yeast, the Neymotin 4tU half-life row joined $5085$ records, with a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, and cosine $0.8040935468384264$ against the same-join protein-abundance vector.  In E. coli K-12 MG1655, the Esquerre row at growth rate $0.40\ h^{-1}$ joined $2720$ records, with hit rate $0.9978649586335735$, $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, dominant coordinate $Arg_{AGR}$, and cosine $-0.7784923375569599$ against the same-join protein-abundance vector.  In human, the Agarwal and Kelley consensus row joined $16165$ records, with hit rate $0.9779294769799313$, $54$ dataset columns, $R^2_{QM}=0.010503670362051522$, dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, and cosine $0.6351183883924848$ against the human protein-abundance vector.  Each of these mRNA rows used 1000 permutations with right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$ where reported.  They show that the residual coordinates also meet transcript-stability readouts, but mRNA stability is not itself a translation measurement.

\paragraph{Yeast mRNA mediation scalar.}
The yeast abundance-residual mediation row decomposed the protein-abundance association after conditioning on residualized mRNA half-life.  On the same $5085$ joined records, the direct abundance association was $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, the conditional value was $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, and the absorbed component was $0.027596042074056304$.  The resulting mediation fraction was
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_M &= 0.09994436477872035,
\end{aligned}
$$
with permutation null mean $0.00019674487584783138$, null $p95=0.0014571003414947088$, and right-tail value $0.000999000999000999$.  This is a variance-partition statement about an observational yeast dataset.  It is not a causal codon-to-decay-to-abundance mechanism.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The matrix claim is ready in a bounded form.  The strongest positive entry is the powered modeled-tAI survival matrix: $40$ organisms, $9\times 9$ readout shape, rank-$9$ residual coordinate design, rank-$23$ controls, and $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$.  The broader organism sweep gives a finite distribution of modeled-tAI survival across $41$ organisms.  The proxy coupling, protein-abundance, complement, auxiliary-readout, and mRNA-stability rows provide surrounding constraints and downstream context, but they are not substitutes for a measured ribosome or TE survival matrix unless those rows are explicitly joined and recomputed.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
This chapter does not infer measured translation, ribosome-mediated mechanism, protein folding, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, or a universal biological law from the modeled-tAI matrix, the tRNA-copy proxy, the protein-abundance rows, the mRNA rows, or the coordinate geometry.  A measured translation statement requires an organism- or CDS-matched ribosome-profiling, TE, or equivalent direct translation contact with the same $Z$ controls.  A protein-output mediation statement requires a same-row $Q_i\to T_j\to P_k$ computation.  Structure, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, and mechanism each require their own layer-matched contact and their own statistic.
