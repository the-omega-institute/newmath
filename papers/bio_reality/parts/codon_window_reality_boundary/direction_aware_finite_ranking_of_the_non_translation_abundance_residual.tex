\subsection{Direction-aware finite ranking of the non-translation abundance residual}
\label{sec:q6-h-ranking-table-direction-aware-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record studied here is a finite per-organism, per-readout ledger for the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual over the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates.  The coordinate list is $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  Every base fit uses the same class of controls: an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline.  In the assembled ranking table these controls give rank $23$ from $24$ control columns for each base organism row.  For each candidate readout $H$, the table stores absorbed energy $A_H$, direction cosine $C_H$, raw directional support $A_H C_H$, a Bonferroni-adjusted degree-of-freedom matched null status, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda\,DL(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus one-column readouts have $DL=1$, while the twelve-column localization readout has $DL=3.700439718141092$.

\paragraph{Residual size before ranking.}
The residual target is not uniform across organisms.  In the complement computation, the modeled-translation-complement fraction $d$ is $0.874628721049266$ for Danio rerio, $0.6629960038578534$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.9400341806302775$ for Gallus gallus, $0.9208949355679455$ for Homo sapiens, $0.21173219572706714$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, $0.7852113562436636$ for Bacillus subtilis 168, $0.739922597382677$ for Caenorhabditis elegans, $0.769859745412206$ for Drosophila melanogaster, $0.9469912232040104$ for Mus musculus, $0.8972672018746484$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis, $0.9573445453007974$ for Rattus norvegicus, and $0.988614641876908$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus.  The six-organism complement support readout also shows that the residual coordinate support differs by organism: Danio is led by $f3\_stress$, $Arg\_AGR$, and $Ile\_AUA$; human by $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, and $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$; yeast by $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, and $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$; and E. coli by $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, $Arg\_AGR$, and $Ile\_AUA$.  The table therefore ranks readouts against an organism-specific residual direction rather than against a single shared biological axis.

This is the local reason for using the direction filter.

Human has several readouts exceeding the Bonferroni absorption threshold, but none survive the description-length penalty. Bacillus, Caenorhabditis, Drosophila, Mus, Mycobacterium, Rattus, and Sulfolobus have no ranked $H$ rows in this finite panel, and those empty rows are missing-data entries rather than negative biological evidence.

\paragraph{Cross-organism verdict.}
The best raw directional support in the computed ranking never exceeds $0.011566051846174291$.  The organism-level winners are not shared: Danio selects measured TE, human selects complex membership, Gallus selects complex membership on a feature-only join, yeast selects mRNA, and E. coli has no Bonferroni-positive winner.  The shared absorber $H_{\mathrm{shared}}^{\ast}$ is therefore absent in this finite panel.  This absence is a positive descriptive outcome: the non-translation residual is organism-specific and remains largely unexplained by the fetched readouts.  The separate minimal-compilation test agrees with this interpretation.  Its residual fractions after the measured dictionary are $0.9756319042010854$ for Danio, $0.9942823785118229$ for E. coli, $0.9736442326306781$ for human, and $0.8676586345294415$ for yeast, with median compressibility $0.026$ and the stated verdict that the low-dimensional measured readout set does not compress the residual across organisms.

\paragraph{Contact diagnostics.}
Several contact rows are usable but join-sensitive.  Measured TE and mRNA rows use $n=13140$ for Danio, $3415$ for E. coli, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast; the corresponding full CDS joins are $14410$, $3739$, $19053$, and $5815$.  The modeled-complement self-check therefore fails the $0.02$ tolerance for Danio and human in the TE and mRNA joins, while E. coli and yeast remain within tolerance.  Localization uses $1984$ joined Danio proteins, $16008$ human proteins, and $4656$ yeast proteins from $2821$, $16406$, and $4831$ localization records, respectively.  The human turnover row has $8057$ joined proteins, a CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$, and absorbed energy $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$, below its null $p95$ value $0.0004342285066808464$.  Yeast turnover has $3640$ joined proteins, join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, and $A_H=0.0009086031889966327$.

The measured-mediation rows also show why the present table should not be read as a translation mechanism.  The measured TE mediation fractions are $0$ for Danio after a raw suppressive value $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  Measured tRNA-copy tAI absorbs $0.020817102845637092$ in Danio, $5.426229300865148\times 10^{-5}$ in human, and $0.001530747712165342$ in yeast, using $8607$, $422$, and $268$ usable measured-tAI records.  These are projection statistics on matched rows, not perturbation or causal measurements.

\paragraph{Background boundary signal.}
The fixed Window6 boundary set supplies an independent abundance-preference diagnostic, not a mechanism.  Across $15$ computed organisms, the $R$ set is avoided in all $15$ non-tie directional cases, with one-sided avoided-binomial value $3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided value $6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  Six organisms are individually significant at the stated two-sided $0.05$ level: human, Danio, Gallus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAO1, Mus musculus, and Rattus norvegicus.  This boundary signal supports that the coordinate packet contacts abundance data, but it does not identify a universal $H$ channel.

\paragraph{Scientific boundary.}
The ranking is a finite descriptive readout of statistical projections.  $A_H$ is absorbed variance-like energy, $C_H$ is coordinate-support alignment, and $\mathrm{Score}$ is a stated penalty rule; none of these is a biological cause, molecular pathway, localization mechanism, folding statement, physical-admissibility statement, function statement, phenotype statement, or universal law.  Missing rows remain unavailable rows.  Bonferroni status is not rewritten by rank, score, or organism-level winner labels.  A stronger biological interpretation would require a separate layer-matched contact with its own statistic, independent of the residual target and independent of this ranking output.
