\subsection{A wobble-broken translation readout for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ $f_3$ stress coordinate}\label{sec:q6-f3-stress-wobble-broken-translation-readout-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite coordinate surface.}
This record concerns the finite coding-sequence surface obtained by restricting the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual basis to the third-position $f_3$ stress coordinate and then pairing that coordinate with organism-matched translation readouts.  The carrier is not a protein-level object.  It is a row-wise residual coordinate on coding sequences, quotiented by amino acid identity, and it is allowed to speak only where a matched translation row is present.  The intended distinction is the survival of an $f_3$-paired synonymous-codon imbalance after amino-acid projection, after organism-level controls, and after sequence covariates such as GC3, length, $M$-density, and mRNA abundance have been accounted for.

The verified finite saturation row fixes the codon support used by the coordinate.  In run $76\mathrm{d}3\mathrm{dc}408\mathrm{a}57$, the set recorded as $M=\operatorname{Med}_{R}\cup\{\mathrm{ACA}\}$ has $20$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding saturated $f_3$ row has $18$ codons:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R)=\{&\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite row difference is exactly the two-codon exclusion $\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$, and the recorded $f_3$ boundary list is empty, with cardinality $0$.  These numbers matter because the present claim is not made from a free-form codon story: it is tied to an explicit $20\to18$ finite-row restriction and to the absence of a leftover boundary entry in that run.

\paragraph{Wobble-broken trigger.}
The trigger is local asymmetry in the translation readout.  Let $c$ be a synonymous codon in the finite row and let $c^{f_3}$ denote its third-position $f_3$ partner where that partner is defined inside the restricted support.  The coordinate is biologically silent unless the organism or context supplies a translation weight with
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The weights may come from organism-scoped tAI or stAI annotations, from tRNA or wobble-weight summaries, or from ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency measurements when the latter are matched to mRNA abundance.  Codon topology alone is insufficient.  The $18$-row saturated support can identify which finite codon contrasts are available, but it does not by itself assert that ribosomes distinguish the two partners.  The cross-layer claim begins only when the external translation row breaks the $f_3$ symmetry.

\paragraph{Controlled survival score.}
For each organism and each admissible translation readout $T_j$, the bounded readout is the controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$.  This entry is interpreted as survival only if the $f_3$ residual coordinate improves the controlled translation model after amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, and mRNA controls have already been included.  A convenient local form is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0
\end{aligned}
$$
under the full baseline and after ablation against simpler codon summaries.  A rank-degenerate matrix, including an all-$1.0$ score table across a small organism panel, is treated as a null boundary rather than as evidence.  The finite $20$-codon $M$ row, the $18$-codon saturated row, and the $0$-entry boundary record supply the coordinate side of the test; they do not supply the translation side.

\paragraph{Reality contacts.}
The required contacts are separated by what each can actually test.  Per-organism codon-usage tables can test coding-sequence composition and support the construction of the residual coordinate, but they do not observe ribosome dynamics.  tRNA-Leu gene-copy counts can test organism-level anticodon-family asymmetry, but copy number is still an annotation summary rather than a direct translation-rate measurement.  tAI or stAI weights can provide a bounded translation-layer weight, provided they remain modelled annotations rather than observed ribosome occupancy.  Ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency data can test a per-CDS translation row when paired with matched mRNA abundance, typically through $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$.  The matched mRNA contact is needed so that the claimed signal is not merely a transcription-layer residue.

\paragraph{Support and falsification.}
The claim is supported only if at least one controlled organism-readout entry is positive and non-degenerate for the $f_3$ stress coordinate, with the translation row supplied by a valid contact.  It is falsified at this layer if every controlled $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}$ is non-positive, or if the available readout has insufficient rank to distinguish survival from a table artifact.  Negative survival would not prove that the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate is biologically irrelevant; it would only close this particular translation-readout route.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization, folded-structure statement, physical admissibility claim, protein function, mechanistic law, phenotype assertion, or universality conclusion follows from the finite codon rows alone.  Even a positive controlled survival entry would remain a translation-layer result unless a separate contact supplies protein abundance, folding, localization, stability, function, or phenotype.  Conversely, modelled tAI or stAI coupling must not be promoted to observed ribosome dynamics unless ribosome-profiling or equivalent translation-efficiency data are present.  The chapter therefore records a narrow cross-layer conjecture: the $20\to18$ finite $f_3$ saturation surface is allowed to become dynamic only in contexts where the translation-readout weights break the paired third-position symmetry and the controlled survival entry remains positive.
