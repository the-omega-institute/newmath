\subsection{Decay-Factor RBP Occupancy and RNA Half-Life Under a Translation Boundary}\label{sec:decay-factor-rbp-rna-half-life-translation-boundary}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record considered here is a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row carries a synonymous coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP perturbation state, wild-type rescue state, binding-defective or non-target control state, transcription-rate control, mRNA abundance control, isoform state, local RNA-structure covariates, motif-density covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, batch, and a declared break condition.  The intended comparison is not a free comparison between arbitrary coding sequences.  It is a matched contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after amino-acid composition control, with the local decay-factor RBP occupancy read on the same finite windows and with translation measured before any interpretation is allowed to move beyond the RNA row.

\paragraph{Coordinate and readout.}
Let $i$ range over the retained coding-RNA windows.  A row has coordinate class $C_i$, translation readout $T_i$, RNA half-life $H_i$, local RBP occupancy $O_i$, transcription-rate control $A_i$, mRNA abundance control $B_i$, local-structure and motif-density covariates $S_i$ and $D_i$, sequence-composition covariates $G_i$, codon-pair context $P_i$, isoform state $I_i$, batch $K_i$, perturbation state $L_i$, and rescue state $W_i$.  The chapter licenses only a bounded cross-layer relation of the following form: after the row has been guarded by $T_i$, the half-life contrast may be associated with the coordinate only when the contrast tracks measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and is controlled by the perturbation and rescue pattern.  A schematic finite statistic is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H &= \operatorname{contrast}(H_i \mid C_i=\mathrm{high})-\operatorname{contrast}(H_i \mid C_i=\mathrm{low}),\\
\Delta_O &= \operatorname{contrast}(O_i \mid C_i=\mathrm{high})-\operatorname{contrast}(O_i \mid C_i=\mathrm{low}),
\end{aligned}
$$
computed only inside rows matched for $A_i,B_i,S_i,D_i,G_i,P_i,I_i,$ and $K_i$.  The statistic is not a claim about protein output or biological function.  It is a row-level test of whether an RNA half-life contrast remains coupled to a measured decay-factor RBP occupancy contrast after the declared controls are attached.

\paragraph{Perturbation gate.}
The positive pattern requires four finite observations on the same design.  First, the high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels must remain distinguishable for RNA half-life after transcription rate, RNA abundance, local structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, and batch have been controlled.  Second, the half-life contrast must follow measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP, not merely the coordinate label.  Third, target-RBP loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect while binding-defective rescue and non-target controls must fail to restore it.  In symbols, the accepted pattern has the row-level shape
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{target\ loss}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{wild\ type\ rescue}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{present}},\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{binding\ defective\ rescue}} &\approx 0,\\
\Delta_H^{\mathrm{non\ target\ control}} &\approx \Delta_H^{\mathrm{present}}\ \text{only when the control preserves the target RBP state.}
\end{aligned}
$$
These equations are not universal laws; they describe the finite acceptance pattern for this particular row design.  If the same pattern is reproduced by RNA structure, motif density, transcription, isoform stability, abundance, or batch, the RBP bridge is refused.

\paragraph{Observed finite support.}
The available verified scalar support attached to this record is not itself an RBP perturbation panel.  The cited verified contact is the transcript-surveillance boundary record $h3.cross\_layer\_relation.transcript\_surveillance.nmd\_position\_rule\_reality\_boundary\_teran2021$, which reports experiment run $c362df6daa98$ with $38576$ retained rows, $1000$ actual permutations, and runtime $55.602$ seconds at verification time 2026-06-21T17:49:33+00:00.  Those numbers establish that a same-family transcript-surveillance contact has a finite row count and permutation audit, but they do not by themselves measure local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, or non-target behavior for the present claim.  Thus the finite support currently functions as a reality-contact precedent for transcript-layer boundary discipline, not as a completed positive assay for the decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Decision rule.}
The claim is accepted only if the finite panel supports all of the following row-local separations.  High-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels must not be exchangeable for RNA half-life after the declared controls.  The half-life contrast must move with measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy.  Coordinate and occupancy must be decoupled enough to show that the coordinate label alone is not being used as a surrogate for the RBP measurement.  Target-RBP loss must remove the effect.  Wild-type rescue must restore it.  Binding-defective rescue and non-target controls must not restore it in a way that explains the original contrast.  The same rows must also carry the translation readout so that the RNA half-life statement is not silently promoted into translation realization.

\paragraph{Refusal cases.}
If high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable for RNA half-life after the controls, the coordinate remains only a bounded descriptor.  If RNA half-life changes but measured decay-factor RBP occupancy does not change, the result is an RNA-stability association rather than an RBP-linked bridge.  If the target-RBP loss and wild-type rescue do not control the effect, no mechanism-level RBP reading is licensed.  If binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform state, transcript abundance, or batch reproduces the pattern, the chapter refuses the RBP bridge.  If the translation readout is absent, the row may still describe RNA half-life, but it cannot be used as a guarded cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Local cannot-claim boundary.}
No RNA half-life difference in this chapter entails translation realization, ribosome dynamics, protein abundance, protein folding, structural order, physical admissibility, cellular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism beyond the specified RBP perturbation pattern, or a universal biological law.  The same-row translation readout is a boundary condition, not a promotion rule.  Protein-layer, structure-layer, function-layer, phenotype-layer, and universality statements require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite rows and controls.  The present chapter therefore records a conditional RNA-stability relation: a coordinate-associated half-life effect may be read as a bounded cross-layer relation only when measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and the perturbation-rescue design carry the effect inside the finite matched panel.
