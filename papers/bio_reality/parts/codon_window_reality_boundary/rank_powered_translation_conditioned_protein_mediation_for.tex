\paragraph{Finite matrix surface.}
The record considered here is the finite cross-layer surface obtained by placing nine synonymous residual coordinates against three families of matched readouts: translation readouts $T$, protein-abundance readouts $P$, and transcript or stability controls $M$. The nine coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$. In every reported regression surface these coordinates are residualized against the same control design: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ vectors. The rank diagnostics are non-degenerate on the reported powered rows: the control rank is $23$ with $24$ control columns, and the residualized $Q$ design has rank $9$ wherever the full coordinate panel is used.

\paragraph{Measured translation survival.}
The measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency panel supplies a separate $Q \to T$ surface in four organisms. Translation efficiency is read as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$, with $\mathrm{TE}$ taken from footprint divided by mRNA in the local ribosome-profiling files. The matched row counts are $13140$ for Danio rerio, $3415$ for Escherichia coli K12, $11554$ for Homo sapiens, and $5128$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with reported ribosome-TE join hit rates $0.9118667592$, $0.9133458144$, $0.6064136881$, and $0.8818572657$. All four rows retain the full nine surviving coordinates. This is the first layer at which the direct protein-omics signal can be compared with an observed translation readout rather than with a modeled codon-adaptation score.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned mediation readback.}
The measured $Q \to T \to P$ decomposition is computed on the same three-way joins. The protein readout is $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\ ppm})$, and the translation readout is the measured ribosome-profiling $\log_{10}(\mathrm{TE})$. The three-way row counts are $13140$ for Danio rerio, $3415$ for Escherichia coli K12, $11554$ for Homo sapiens, and $5128$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The direct survival and measured mediation scalars are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Danio}:&\quad S_{QP}=0.0492657845,\quad S_{QT}=0.0403421761,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0,\quad M^{raw}=-0.0480148605,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\quad S_{QP}=0.1094153595,\quad S_{QT}=0.0093942668,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0409115834,\\
\mathrm{Human}:&\quad S_{QP}=0.0428846500,\quad S_{QT}=0.0188379113,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.0027966485,\\
\mathrm{Yeast}:&\quad S_{QP}=0.3146818503,\quad S_{QT}=0.1368193134,\quad M^{QTP}_{meas}=0.4793069656.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the panel separates four finite outcomes. Yeast gives a strong measured translation-conditioned absorption of the direct protein-abundance signal. Escherichia coli gives a small but positive measured absorption. Human gives a very weak positive measured absorption. Danio has positive $Q \to T$ and positive $Q \to P$ entries, but the measured translation conditioning is suppressive before clipping and therefore does not count as a mediated route in this finite readback.

\paragraph{Modeled-tAI four-case comparison.}
A separate two-organism modeled-tAI discriminant classifies the same nine coordinates by $S^{QT}$, $S^{QP}$, and whether conditioning reduces the direct $Q \to P$ signal. In the modeled-tAI version, Saccharomyces cerevisiae has $R^2_{QT}=0.8213457221$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.2983762499$, with all nine coordinates in case $D$. Escherichia coli K12 has $R^2_{QT}=0.5416736994$ and $R^2_{QP}=0.1076752668$, with seven coordinates in case $D$ and two positive-positive coordinates without a mediation drop. This modeled surface is useful as a sign-and-conditioning contrast, but it is not substituted for the measured ribosome-profiling mediation surface above.

The $f3_{stress}$ component also has a local ribosome-occupancy dwell signal: on $n=1257$ joined rows, base $R^2$ is $0.1855578758$, full $R^2$ is $0.2041226152$, held-out $\Delta R^2$ is $0.0185647394$, the permutation null $95\%$ level is $0.0013115362$, and the $f3$-dwell correlation is $0.1615441451$ with approximate $p=7.8739160572\cdot 10^{-9}$. The positional split localizes the dominant segment to the $5'$ window: the $5'$ ramp passes and the $3'$ body window does not.

\paragraph{Non-translation alternatives tested so far.}
Coarse localization and human turnover were tested as possible carriers of the residual protein-abundance component rather than as causal explanations. In localization, the joined row counts are $1984$ for Danio rerio, $16008$ for Homo sapiens, and $4656$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, with $12$ localization columns. The absorbed localization fractions are $0.0095088645$, $0.0092549133$, and $0.0112251056$, compared with dof-matched null $95\%$ levels $0.0113213287$, $0.0013758521$, and $0.0049581170$. Thus human and yeast exceed the localization null, while the danio localization join does not. In the human turnover test, the matched turnover-abundance-CDS join has $8057$ rows; the modeled complement has $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.6490492$, $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ norm squared $1262.2237679$, and $d_{modeled}=0.9208949356$. The turnover absorption is only $3.57407649\cdot 10^{-6}$, below the null $95\%$ level $0.0004342285$, so protein half-life does not explain the large human modeled-tAI complement in this finite test.

\paragraph{Conclusion and local boundary.}
The rank-powered surface supports the translation-conditioned claim in a qualified finite sense. There is broad direct $Q \to P$ survival across fifteen protein-omics organism rows, measured $Q \to T$ survival across four ribosome-profiling rows, and a measured $Q \to T \to P$ absorption signal that is strong in yeast, small in Escherichia coli, weak in human, and absent after clipping in Danio. The stronger four-way decomposition shows that measured TE and transcript stability together absorb substantial fractions in yeast, Escherichia coli, and human, but the relative share is organism-dependent. None of these rows licenses translation universality, folded structure, physical admissibility, biological function, causal mechanism, perturbation rescue, or evolutionary direction. Those conclusions would require distinct contacts at the relevant layer. The present chapter records only finite matrix survival, conditioning, and residual-readout partitioning on the stated organism joins.
