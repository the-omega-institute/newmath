\subsection{Rank-powered translation-conditioned protein mediation for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$}
\label{sec:q6-rank-powered-translation-conditioned-protein-mediation-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
The record is a finite matrix surface whose rows are $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates and whose columns are matched readouts for translation, transcript behavior, and protein abundance.  The row basis has nine coordinates,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \{&K_{AAA},\ Arg_{AGR},\ Ile_{AUA},\ Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR},\ Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG},\\
&Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY},\ Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG},\ Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY},\ f3_{stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every reported entry is computed after removing the sibling control space $Z$: intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, $GC3$, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  In the powered protein-abundance matrix the control design has $24$ columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, and the residualized $Q$ design has $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde Q)=9$ in every listed organism.  Thus the observed surface is not the degenerate $n=3$ all-one boundary; it is a rank-supported panel with thousands of per-CDS rows in each organism.

\paragraph{Measured translation survival.}
The measured translation contact uses ribosome-profiling translation efficiency, $TE=\mathrm{footprint}/\mathrm{mRNA}$, so the translation readout is already mRNA-normalized within each source.  Four powered organism joins are available.  Their measured $Q\to T$ survival values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{D.\ rerio}:&\ n=13140,\ R^2_{QT,meas}=0.040342,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\ n=3415,\ R^2_{QT,meas}=0.009394,\\
\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}:&\ n=11554,\ R^2_{QT,meas}=0.018838,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\ n=5128,\ R^2_{QT,meas}=0.136819.
\end{aligned}
$$
The corresponding ribosome-TE join hit rates are $0.911867$ for danio, $0.913346$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.606414$ for human, and $0.881857$ for yeast.  All four joins retain the nine-dimensional $Q$ row rank after control residualization.  The finite reading is therefore that $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ survives not only into measured protein abundance but also into measured translation-efficiency readouts in rank-supported panels.

\paragraph{Translation-conditioned protein mediation.}
The mediation test conditions the direct $Q\to P$ association on measured $TE$ rather than on modeled tAI.  The four measured $M^{QTP,meas}$ entries are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{D.\ rerio}:&\ S_{QP}=0.049266,\ S_{QT}=0.040342,\ M^{QTP,meas}_{raw}=-0.048015,\ M^{QTP,meas}=0,\\
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\ S_{QP}=0.109415,\ S_{QT}=0.009394,\ M^{QTP,meas}=0.040912,\\
\mathrm{H.\ sapiens}:&\ S_{QP}=0.042885,\ S_{QT}=0.018838,\ M^{QTP,meas}=0.002797,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\ S_{QP}=0.314682,\ S_{QT}=0.136819,\ M^{QTP,meas}=0.479307.
\end{aligned}
$$
The three-way joined row counts are $13140$ for danio, $3415$ for $E.\ coli$, $11554$ for human, and $5128$ for yeast.  The interpretation is asymmetric.  Yeast supplies a strong measured-translation absorption of the direct protein-abundance association, $M^{QTP,meas}=0.479307$.  $E.\ coli$ and human supply weaker but positive measured-translation-conditioned reductions, $0.040912$ and $0.002797$.  Danio has positive $Q\to T$ and positive $Q\to P$ entries but a suppressive raw mediation fraction, clipped to $0$ for the reported nonnegative mediated share.  Therefore the finite verdict is not universal translation mediation; it is a mixed cross-layer surface with one strong measured translation-mediated row, two weak positive rows, and one translation-surviving but nonabsorbing row.

The yeast row is translation-dominant: measured $TE$ accounts for most of the reduction of the $Q\to P$ association.  The $E.\ coli$ and human rows are stability-dominant: mRNA-stability conditioning accounts for a larger share than measured $TE$.  In all three rows the joint share is close to the sum of the two single-readout shares within the fixed tolerance $0.05$, with joint-minus-single sums $-0.009809$, $0.010265$, and $-0.012862$ respectively.

\paragraph{Modeled-translation four-case readback.}
The modeled-tAI four-case discriminant is computed for $E.\ coli$ and yeast using per-protein mean modeled tAI from GtRNAdb tRNA copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  The modeled $Q\to T$ values are large, $R^2_{QT}=0.541674$ in $E.\ coli$ and $R^2_{QT}=0.821346$ in yeast, while the paired protein-abundance values are $R^2_{QP}=0.107675$ and $0.298376$.  The finite classifications are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{E.\ coli}:&\ A=0,\ B=0,\ C=0,\ D=7,\ \mathrm{positive\mbox{-}positive\ without\ drop}=2,\\
\mathrm{S.\ cerevisiae}:&\ A=0,\ B=0,\ C=0,\ D=9,\ \mathrm{positive\mbox{-}positive\ without\ drop}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This confirms that the modeled translation layer is aligned with the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual rows, but the modeled readout is not interchangeable with ribosome profiling.  It supplies a classification surface, not a direct measurement of ribosome occupancy.

\paragraph{Measured-readout compression and residual structure.}
The yeast dictionary certificate tests whether a small set of measured readouts can cover the abundance residual.  With $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, $n=1800$, five folds, and $120$ permutations, the best selected family drawn from measured $TE$, mRNA stability, and turnover has raw coverage $0.274500$.  After selection-aware calibration the certified coverage is
$$
\begin{aligned}
C^{\ast}=0.245382,\qquad \mathrm{selection\ null}_{95}=0.029149,\qquad \mathrm{unexplained}=0.754618.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the residual is real and only partially reducible to this three-readout dictionary.  A separate yeast dwell certificate focuses on the $f3_{stress}$ abundance component.  On $n=1257$ joined rows, with five folds and $120$ deterministic row-label permutations, the base model has $R^2=0.185558$, the dwell-alone model has $R^2=0.034337$, the full model has $R^2=0.204123$, and the held-out added $R^2$ from dwell is $0.018565$, above the permutation $95$th percentile $0.001312$.  The $f3_{stress}$--dwell correlation is $0.161544$ with approximate $p=7.8739\times 10^{-9}$.  The positional dwell split localizes the leading signal to the $5'$ window: the $5'$ segment passes while the $3'$ segment does not.

\paragraph{Non-translation candidates and structural-order guardrail.}
Localization, turnover, and structural-order tests keep the protein-abundance residual from being overread as a single mechanism.  Localization absorption over twelve UniProt categories gives absorbed fractions $0.009509$ in danio, $0.009255$ in human, and $0.011225$ in yeast.  The corresponding degree-matched null $95$th percentiles are $0.011321$, $0.001376$, and $0.004958$ over $200$ trials; hence human and yeast exceed their category nulls, while danio does not.  Human turnover is much smaller: with $8057$ joined rows, $R^2_{Q,Tturn}=0.004615$ but the absorbed fraction is only $3.5741\times 10^{-6}$, below the null $95$th percentile $0.000434$.  The positional structural-order test has a positive $f3$ ramp value at the primary $50$-codon window, $\Delta R^2_{ramp}=0.001117$, with body value $-0.000262$ and ramp sign-flip $p=0.047852$; however the paired ramp-body localization gate does not pass, with paired $p=0.058594$.  The correct finite label is therefore ramp-positive but not spatially resolved as a body-separated structural-order mechanism.

\paragraph{Scientific verdict.}
The rank-powered matrix supports the cross-layer claim in a bounded form.  At least one $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinate family survives to measured translation readouts and to measured protein abundance under the shared controls, and yeast supplies a strong measured $Q\to T\to P$ absorption with $M^{QTP,meas}=0.479307$.  The broader panel shows heterogeneity: $E.\ coli$ and human have weak measured-TE mediation but stronger transcript-stability co-conditioning, while danio has positive $Q\to T$ and $Q\to P$ survival without a positive measured-TE absorption of the direct protein association.  The allowed conclusion is a finite, readout-conditioned cross-layer relation, not a biological law.

\paragraph{Local limits.}
No translation mechanism follows from codon geometry alone.  No folded structure, physical admissibility, mature protein function, cellular phenotype, causal pathway, edit-rescue claim, or organism-universal rule follows from any $Q\to P$ association unless the relevant layer is measured and separately conditioned.  Direct $S^{QP}$ is a protein-omics association; modeled tAI is a translation proxy; ribosome-profiling $TE$ is condition-specific and cross-sectional; mRNA stability, turnover, localization, dwell, and structural-order readouts are descriptive projections unless perturbation or rescue data are supplied.  The chapter therefore records a positive finite mediation surface with explicit nonpromotion beyond the measured contacts.
