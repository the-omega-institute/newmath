\subsection{Rank-powered translation-conditioned protein mediation for $B^*_{Q6}$}\label{sec:q6-rank-powered-translation-conditioned-protein-mediation-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite data surface.}
The record is a finite matrix experiment on nine synonymous residual coordinates $q_i$ over matched coding-sequence rows.  The coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  Each fit first removes a control space $Z$ containing an intercept, log CDS length, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions from real codon counts, GC3, and an M-density baseline on the union support of the projected $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates.  In the protein-abundance survey this gives $24$ control columns, rank $Z=23$, rank $\widetilde X=9$, and positive rank sufficiency in every listed organism.  The residual protein readout is $\log_{10}$ PAXdb abundance in ppm after joining measured protein abundance to real CDS codon counts.

\paragraph{Direct protein-abundance survival.}
The direct $Q\to P$ surface is not rank-degenerate.  The joined protein rows are $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $6974$ for \emph{Dictyostelium discoideum}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $1747$ for \emph{Halobacterium salinarum}, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $5006$ for \emph{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The corresponding join hit rates include $0.997532$ for \emph{B. subtilis}, $0.891377$ for \emph{C. elegans}, $1.000000$ for zebrafish, $0.946911$ for \emph{Dictyostelium}, $0.995656$ for fly, $0.997865$ for \emph{E. coli}, $1.000000$ for chicken, $1.000000$ for \emph{Halobacterium}, $0.977929$ for human, $0.980833$ for mouse, $1.000000$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, $0.994438$ for \emph{P. aeruginosa}, $0.823159$ for rat, $0.915604$ for yeast, and $1.000000$ for \emph{S. solfataricus}.  Every organism has all nine coordinates surviving at the positive partial-$R^2$ threshold $10^{-12}$.  The observed $R^2_{QP}$ list is $0.061906$, $0.102179$, $0.046135$, $0.081418$, $0.077095$, $0.107675$, $0.039808$, $0.019257$, $0.044546$, $0.049828$, $0.032090$, $0.064647$, $0.027640$, $0.298376$, and $0.063975$ in the same organism order.  These are direct abundance associations after controls, not translation-rate measurements.

\paragraph{Measured translation contact.}
Four organisms also have measured ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency rows.  The measured TE readout is $\log_{10}(TE)$ with $TE=RPF/mRNA$ in the local source files, so the transcript abundance normalization is built into the readout.  The measured-TE joins use $13140$ zebrafish genes with hit rate $0.911867$, $3415$ \emph{E. coli} genes with hit rate $0.913346$, $11554$ human genes with hit rate $0.606414$, and $5128$ yeast genes with hit rate $0.881857$.  After the same control scheme, rank $\widetilde X=9$ and rank $Z=23$ hold in all four cases.  The measured translation survival values are $R^2_{QT,meas}=0.040342$ for zebrafish, $0.009394$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.018838$ for human, and $0.136819$ for yeast, with all nine coordinates surviving in each organism.  Thus the translation layer is genuinely contacted, but only as cross-sectional TE association.

\paragraph{Conditioned mediation readback.}
The measured $Q\to T\to P$ decomposition joins $13140$ zebrafish rows, $3415$ \emph{E. coli} rows, $11554$ human rows, and $5128$ yeast rows across CDS, measured TE, and protein abundance.  Its scalar readback is the joint nine-coordinate mediated fraction after conditioning the direct protein-abundance signal on measured TE:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{zebrafish}) &= 0.000000, & S_{QP} &= 0.049266, & S_{QT} &= 0.040342,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\textit{E. coli}) &= 0.040912, & S_{QP} &= 0.109415, & S_{QT} &= 0.009394,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{human}) &= 0.002797, & S_{QP} &= 0.042885, & S_{QT} &= 0.018838,\\
M^{QTP}_{meas}(\text{yeast}) &= 0.479307, & S_{QP} &= 0.314682, & S_{QT} &= 0.136819.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw zebrafish fraction is suppressive, $-0.048015$, and is clipped to zero for the bounded fraction report.  Yeast is the strongest measured translation-conditioned case: its TE conditioning removes about $47.93\%$ of the direct nine-coordinate abundance signal under this readout.  \emph{E. coli} and human remain positive but small on this scalar.

The joint mediated fractions are $0.240467$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.168907$ for human, and $0.519518$ for yeast, with right-tail permutation $p=0.000999$ in all three organisms.  The TE-only fractions are $0.019755$, $0.005495$, and $0.469719$; the stability-only fractions are $0.230521$, $0.153147$, and $0.062662$.  The overlap readback is approximately independent in each case, with joint-minus-single-sum values $-0.009809$, $0.010265$, and $-0.012862$ under the fixed tolerance $0.05$.

\paragraph{Four-case modeled-tAI contrast.}
The modeled-tAI discriminant is kept separate from measured ribosome profiling.  It uses per-protein mean modeled tAI from GtRNAdb copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  In \emph{E. coli}, $n=3739$ rows give $R^2_{QP}=0.107675$ and $R^2_{QT}=0.541674$; the case counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=7$, with $2$ positive-positive coordinates lacking a mediation drop.  In yeast, $n=5815$ rows give $R^2_{QP}=0.298376$ and $R^2_{QT}=0.821346$; the case counts are $A=0$, $B=0$, $C=0$, $D=9$.  This modeled-tAI classification supports a strong translation-readout alignment in these two organisms, but it is not an observed ribosome-occupancy result.

\paragraph{Residual readout dictionary and $f3_{stress}$.}
The yeast dictionary compression run uses base $n=4759$, active join $n=1800$, fold count $5$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, and $120$ permutations.  The best selection-aware coverage by at most three measured readouts is $C^*=0.245382$, above the selection-aware null $95\%$ value $0.029149$, leaving unexplained fraction $0.754618$.  The raw best coverage over the candidate family is $0.274500$, while the naive fixed-subset null $95\%$ is $0.005984$.  Thus the residual is partly reducible to measured readouts, but most of the yeast abundance signal remains outside that compact dictionary.

The $f3_{stress}$ component has a local ribosome-occupancy dwell contact.  On yeast, with $n=1257$, base $n=1800$, fold count $5$, and $120$ deterministic row-label permutations, the base model has $R^2=0.185558$ and the base+dwell model has $R^2=0.204123$, so the held-out increment is $\Delta R^2=0.018565$.  The null $95\%$ value is $0.001312$, and the approximate $f3$--dwell correlation $p$-value is $7.87\times 10^{-9}$ with correlation $0.161544$.  Positional splitting identifies the $5'$ window as the leading segment: the $5'$ ramp passes, the $3'$ body does not, and the dominant segment is $5prime$.  A separate structural-order ramp test reports conclusion $ramp\_positive\_but\_not\_localized$: for the primary $50$-codon ramp, the weighted ramp increment is $0.001117$, the weighted body increment is $-0.000262$, the paired ramp-minus-body increment is $0.001378$, the ramp sign-flip probability for being positive is $0.047852$, and the paired ramp-body localization gate does not pass at $0.058594$.

\paragraph{Non-translation alternatives.}
Localization and turnover are tested as downstream descriptive absorbers rather than mechanism declarations.  The localization projection uses $12$ multi-label categories.  Human joins $16008$ CDS rows and $16406$ localization records, with absorption $0.009255$ against a degree-matched null $95\%$ value $0.001376$; yeast joins $4656$ rows and $4831$ localization records, with absorption $0.011225$ against null $95\%=0.004958$; zebrafish joins $1984$ rows and $2821$ localization records, with absorption $0.009509$ below its null $95\%=0.011321$.  The human turnover test joins $8057$ rows from the Mathieson primary-cell half-life sheet to abundance and CDS rows.  The modeled complement magnitude is large, with $P_Q$ norm squared $1370.649049$, $P_{Q\perp Tmod}$ norm squared $1262.223768$, and $d_{modeled}=0.920895$, but log half-life absorbs only $3.5741\times 10^{-6}$ and does not exceed its degree-matched null $95\%=0.000434$.

\paragraph{Verdict and scope.}
The finite row data support the rank-powered cross-layer claim in a bounded sense.  $B^*_{Q6}$ survives direct protein-abundance residualization across fifteen organisms, survives measured TE residualization across four organisms, and has measured translation-conditioned abundance mediation in yeast, with weaker positive measured fractions in \emph{E. coli} and human and a clipped null in zebrafish.  Joint TE plus mRNA-stability conditioning explains a substantial fraction of the abundance association in \emph{E. coli}, human, and yeast, while dictionary compression and localization show that other measured readouts carry part of the residual.  The irreducibility certificate run, with $n=1800$, $\lambda_{DL}=0.01$, $\rho_{join}=0.05$, fold count $5$, and $120$ permutations, therefore should be read as a test of reducibility families, not as proof that the residual is primitive.

No stronger biological conclusion is licensed by these rows.  Direct $Q\to P$ survival is not a translation mechanism.  Measured TE conditioning is an observational variance partition, not a causal mediation effect.  Modeled tAI is an annotation-derived translation proxy, not ribosome profiling.  None of the reported matrices establishes folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, evolutionary direction, perturbational rescue, universality, or a global biological rule.  Such claims would require an additional matched contact at the relevant layer and a separate positive finite test for that layer.
