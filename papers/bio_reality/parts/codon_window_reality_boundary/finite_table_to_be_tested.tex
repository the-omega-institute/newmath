\subsection{Finite table to be tested}
\label{sec:q6-f3-stress-position-resolved-table}

\paragraph{Row carrier.}
The object of this chapter is a finite coding-sequence window table, not a continuous biological law.  A row is keyed by organism, transcript or CDS identifier, codon position, and a fixed local window.  Its internal coordinate is the $f_3$-stress residual after the amino-acid quotient: codons that encode the same amino acid are compared only after the amino-acid composition channel has been removed from the primary coordinate.  The row must also carry local synonymous codon counts, GC3, length, methionine density, amino-acid or position-bin controls, a bounded wobble-weight context, a matched mRNA abundance control, and a position-resolved ribosome-footprint or translation-efficiency readout on the same CDS window.

\paragraph{Asymmetry condition.}
The chapter only asks whether the $f_3$ coordinate has residual survival when context-specific wobble weights distinguish the observed codon surface from its $f_3$-shifted comparison surface.  The local condition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
  w_c &\neq w_c^{f_3},\\
  T_{\mathrm{pos}} &= \text{same-window ribosome-occupancy or translation-efficiency readout},\\
  Z &= \text{amino-acid, position, GC3, length, methionine, mRNA, and baseline controls}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The survival entry is written $S_{f_3}(T_{\mathrm{pos}}\mid Z)$.  It is positive only when the held-out, rank-powered statistic improves over the controls and over simpler codon-usage baselines.  The coordinate therefore remains a bounded cross-layer candidate until the same rows contain all control and readout fields.
