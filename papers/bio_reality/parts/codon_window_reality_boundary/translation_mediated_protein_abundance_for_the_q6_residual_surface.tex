\subsection{Translation-Mediated Protein Abundance for the Q6 Residual Surface}\label{sec:q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite mediation surface.}
The subject of this record is the finite matrix of triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$ in which $q_i$ ranges over the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates, $t_j$ is a translation-side readout, and $p_k$ is a matched protein-abundance readout.  The computed coordinate list is $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The allowed readback is not a direct $q_i\to p_k$ association.  A protein-abundance entry is admitted here only through a controlled attenuation calculation in which the residual coordinate first has a translation-side slot and the direct residual-to-protein term is re-estimated after conditioning on that slot.  Amino-acid composition, organism identity, GC3, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline remain control variables, not new promoted coordinates.

\paragraph{Modelled translation contact.}
The first finite contact used a modelled tAI readout derived from GtRNAdb tRNA gene-copy counts and dos Reis wobble weights.  In the bounded-coupling audit, the codon-count space had $61$ sense codons, three organisms were present, and the nonzero-contact threshold was $0.01$.  The matrix had $81$ entries above threshold and a maximum absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  The three organism panels each had complete standard amino-acid-family coverage: Escherichia coli K-12 had $89$ FASTA headers with $87$ matched and $2$ unmatched, Homo sapiens had $432$ matched headers, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae had $275$ matched headers.  All three modelled tAI readouts covered $61$ sense codons with zero raw-weight fallback count $0$.  This establishes a bounded codon-adaptation contact, not observed ribosome motion.  The run itself records that three organisms are insufficient for powered survival and that the result is modelled-tAI coupling rather than ribosome-profiling or proteomics validation.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance mediation rows.}
The main finite panel joins real CDS codon counts, modelled per-protein tAI, and measured PAXdb protein abundance across twelve organism-specific surfaces.  The joined protein counts are $4042$ for Bacillus subtilis subsp. subtilis str. 168, $11661$ for Caenorhabditis elegans, $14410$ for Danio rerio, $13295$ for Drosophila melanogaster, $3739$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for Gallus gallus, $19053$ for Homo sapiens, $19395$ for Mus musculus, $4129$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. MC2 155, $13322$ for Rattus norvegicus, $5815$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and $1656$ for Sulfolobus solfataricus.  In every organism, the residualized control design has $24$ control columns, rank $23$ for the control block, rank $9$ for the $Q6$ coordinate block, rank $1$ for the translation block, and rank $10$ after adjoining $Q6$ to the translation block.  The powered-rank diagnostic is true in every organism, so the finite-row matrix is numerically admissible for the declared statistical decomposition.

\paragraph{Mediation fractions.}
The mediation scalar is reported as $M^{QTP}=(c-c')/c$, where $c$ is the residualized $R^2$ of protein abundance on the nine $Q6$ coordinates and $c'$ is the residualized direct $R^2$ after conditioning on modelled per-protein tAI.  The organism-level fractions all lie in the unit interval.  The largest finite entry is in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, where $c=0.29837624994140827$, $c'=0.0151709093608293$, the indirect component is $0.28320534058057895$, and $M^{QTP}=0.9491551041216973$.  Other large mediated fractions are Drosophila melanogaster with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 with $0.612922252793667$, Bacillus subtilis with $0.6084594553474505$, and Caenorhabditis elegans with $0.4872268357227724$.  The smallest entries are Sulfolobus solfataricus with $0.01850668564589326$, Mycobacterium smegmatis with $0.08397602919861291$, Mus musculus with $0.15488799907323988$, and Rattus norvegicus with $0.17489681798543982$.  Thus the finite-row statement is positive as a statistical mediation decomposition, but heterogeneous across organisms.

\paragraph{Yeast as the sharpest row.}
The yeast row is the strongest protein-abundance mediation example in this packet.  Its joined panel has $5815$ proteins in the twelve-organism abundance run, and the separate five-way yeast structure--translation--mRNA--abundance join has $4759$ rows with reported join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$ and CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$.  In that five-way decomposition, $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$, $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and $R^2_{QP\mid T,M,S}=0.13759011208318542$.  The structure-only fraction is only $0.003284564452662019$, while the structure-incremental fraction beyond translation and mRNA stability is $0.0005507778820193332$.  The $Q6$-to-structure statistic has $S_Q$ structure $R^2=0.003774248455422586$ with right-tail permutation value $0.027972027972027972$, whereas the structure-incremental right-tail value is $0.1258741258741259$.  These numbers keep the yeast row focused on protein abundance and modelled translation attenuation; they do not turn the abundance result into a folding or structure claim.

\paragraph{Coordinate identity and nearby negative checks.}

\paragraph{Selection and execution context.}
The cross-ortholog selection-imprint run reports $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species.  Its residual statistic has $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$, description-length change $-258.6010084301837$ bits, leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$, and null $95$ percent maximum $0.05427157066522958$; the positive selection-imprint gate is false.  This permits the weaker reading as codon-bias expression correlation but blocks promotion to a strong evolutionary mechanism.  By contrast, the observable execution decomposition in Saccharomyces cerevisiae uses $3289$ genes and reports an observable execution subblock, meaning that at least one measured rate-proxy slot passed both effective null classes.  The genetic-code boundary check is also strong at its own lower layer, with $40$ successes in $40$ independent replication trials, one-sided binomial upper-tail value $9.094947017729282\times 10^{-13}$, and zero unexpected successes in $20$ negative-control and $20$ ablation trials.  These context checks help locate the protein-abundance result without changing its layer.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The finite verdict is therefore: the $Q6$ residual surface has a ready protein-abundance mediation record through modelled per-protein tAI, with all twelve organism mediation fractions finite and in range, and with the strongest row in yeast.  The result is not a causal claim.  It does not by itself show observed translation dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, mechanism, organismal phenotype, adaptation, or a universal biological law.  Those stronger statements require their own matched contacts and their own finite statistics: ribosome profiling or translation-efficiency rows for observed translation, structural or physical assays for folded or admissible states, perturbation and rescue rows for mechanism, and multi-organism controls plus simpler-baseline ablations for population-level laws.
