\subsection{Translation-conditioned decay-factor occupancy and RNA half-life}
\label{subsec:q6-translation-conditioned-decay-rbp-rna-half-life-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns a finite panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row carries a residual $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinate label, an amino-acid-composition control, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair controls, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, perturbation state, rescue state, and batch labels.  The biological object is not a whole-transcript law and not a protein-level claim.  It is a bounded relation among four row-local quantities: residual coordinate class, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the same RNA window, perturbation or rescue state of that factor, and RNA half-life.

\paragraph{Claim form.}
The conjectural assertion is that a coordinate-associated RNA half-life effect may be reported as a cross-layer relation only under a translation-conditioned and occupancy-conditioned design.  Let $q(x)$ denote the high-or-low residual coordinate label of window $x$, let $t(x)$ denote the matched translation readout attached to the same row, let $o(x)$ denote measured local occupancy of the nominated decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and let $h(x)$ denote the RNA half-life readout.  The positive pattern is not the marginal association $q(x) \mapsto h(x)$.  It is the finite statistic that remains after the conditioning variables have been attached:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6,RBP}
  &= \operatorname{Stat}\bigl(q(x), o(x), t(x), h(x), p(x), b(x) \mid Z(x)\bigr),\\
Z(x)
  &= \{\text{transcription rate, local structure, motif density, GC, dinucleotide profile,}\;\text{codon-pair context, isoform state, abundance, batch}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $p(x)$ records target-factor presence, target-factor loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and non-target controls, and $b(x)$ records the matched batch structure.  The statistic is finite-row and window-local.  It does not license a statement about protein abundance, folding, function, adaptation, or a general sequence law.

\paragraph{Matched null.}
The null model exchanges high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous RNA-window labels after the declared controls have been imposed.  In row language, the null says that, conditional on amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, same-row translation readout, and batch, the coordinate label carries no additional half-life information:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0:
\quad h(x) \perp q(x) \mid \{t(x), Z(x), b(x)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mechanism-conditioned alternative is narrower.  It requires that the half-life contrast track measured target-factor occupancy under coordinate/occupancy decoupling, disappear when the target RNA-binding protein is lost, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective rescue or non-target controls.  A half-life contrast without the occupancy and perturbation pattern remains an RNA-half-life association, not an RBP-decay mechanism.

\paragraph{Support rule.}
A supporting finite panel must satisfy all of the following tests in the same matched row system.  First, the coordinate contrast must survive amino-acid-composition control and same-row translation-readout control.  Second, measured local occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein must change in the direction needed to mediate the half-life contrast, and this must remain visible when coordinate and occupancy are experimentally decoupled.  Third, target-factor loss must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life effect.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must restore the effect.  Fifth, binding-defective rescue and non-target controls must not restore it.  Sixth, the pattern must not be reproduced by RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform stability, GC content, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, or batch.  These requirements make the permitted readout deliberately small: the chapter records a finite relation among coordinate, translation-conditioned row state, measured RBP occupancy, perturbation/rescue state, and RNA half-life.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The RNA half-life contacts available to this record provide externally curated per-gene or per-transcript half-life estimates with organism-specific join keys.  Those contacts can delimit an RNA half-life boundary, but they cannot by themselves measure target-RBP occupancy, target-factor loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, non-target control behavior, or an RNA decay mechanism.  Likewise, a ribosome-profiling or translation-efficiency contact can condition the row against translation-layer confounding, but it does not itself show that an RNA half-life association is a translation realization.  No translation claim, protein-folding claim, physical-admissibility claim, biological-function claim, causal mechanism claim, adaptive claim, or universal biological law follows from this record unless an independent contact at that layer supplies the missing measurement.

\paragraph{Failure modes.}
The conjecture fails as a mechanism claim if the half-life contrast persists while measured target-factor occupancy does not follow the coordinate-conditioned pattern.  It also fails if target-factor loss does not collapse the contrast, if wild-type rescue does not restore it, if binding-defective rescue restores it, or if a non-target control reproduces it.  It fails as a bounded coordinate relation if the same-row translation readout is absent, if the transcription and local-sequence controls are not attached, or if high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are not exchangeably matched under the declared null.  Under any of these failures, the remaining admissible statement is only that a finite RNA half-life contact exists for the organism-scoped rows; the cross-layer RBP-decay interpretation is not licensed.
