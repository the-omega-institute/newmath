\subsection{Direction-aware ranking of the non-translation abundance residual}
\label{sec:q6-h-ranking-table-direction-aware-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite readout object.}
The record considered here is a finite per-organism ranking table for the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual of the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ panel.  For each organism with enough attached rows, the table keeps the residual support vector $a_\perp$, a candidate-readout support vector $a_H$, the absorbed energy $A_H$, the support-direction cosine $C_H$, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda\operatorname{DL}(H),\\
  \lambda &= 0.01,\\
  \operatorname{DL}(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Single-column candidates therefore have $\operatorname{DL}=1$, while the 12-column localization readout has $\operatorname{DL}=3.700439718141092$.  The classification of the packet is a cross-layer relation: it compares codon-coordinate residuals with measured or curated readouts, but the table itself is a descriptive projection ledger rather than a mechanism.

\paragraph{Coordinate target.}
The residual target is always read in the same nine coordinates, namely $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  The base complements show that the modeled translation readout leaves large fractions of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component outside the modeled translation channel in several organisms: $d=0.874628721049266$ in danio, $d=0.6629960038578534$ in \emph{E. coli}, $d=0.9400341806302775$ in gallus, $d=0.9208949355679455$ in human, $d=0.8972672018746484$ in \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $d=0.21173219572706714$ in yeast.  The same table records full-row sizes such as $n=14410$ for danio, $n=3739$ for \emph{E. coli}, $n=12418$ for gallus, $n=19053$ for human, $n=4129$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $n=5815$ for yeast, with 24 control columns and rank $23$ in each of these base joins.

\paragraph{Direction filter.}
The key separation is between energy absorption and alignment with the residual-coordinate direction.  In danio, measured TE has $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.001566051846174291$; mRNA follows with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.000734588577977589$.  Both exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, respectively $0.000719994971498518$ and $0.000587830414454545$.  Danio measured tRNA-copy tAI has more raw absorption, $A_H=0.02091194580949614$, but only $C_H=0.33399841523960977$, giving $A_HC_H=0.006984556759948309$ and a negative score $-0.003015443240051691$.  This is why the table is not an absorption-only ranking.

\paragraph{Human rows.}
Human gives the cleanest example of the direction-aware penalty against a high-energy but poorly aligned row.  The strongest raw absorber among single-column protein features is PTM density, with $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its coordinate cosine is only $C_H=0.3604440741749213$, so $A_HC_H=0.0053651472982577525$ and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.004634852701742248$.  Complex membership has lower absorbed energy, $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, but much better direction, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, giving $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$ and the best single-column human score, $-0.0034339698846599055$.  Human mRNA is close, with $A_H=0.007858222847072393$, $C_H=0.7921354915786852$, $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.0037752227820994546$.  Human measured TE is a warning case: it exceeds its Bonferroni threshold as an absorber, but has $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, so $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$ and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.01031302107580362$.

\paragraph{Yeast, \emph{E. coli}, and gallus rows.}
Yeast has a much smaller upstream complement fraction than human or danio, and its best raw direction-aware single-column entry is mRNA: $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.008853737772559931$.  Yeast localization absorbs more energy, $A_H=0.011225105608516642$, but the 12-column description length pushes the score to $-0.03223607310225219$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the available table is narrow: mRNA gives $A_H=0.0017706954554909877$, $C_H=0.8639529831258776$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.008470202379021127$, while measured TE gives $A_H=0.0010982956489728746$, $C_H=0.5041139753888787$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.009446333814243976$; neither exceeds its Bonferroni threshold.  Gallus has feature rows but no broad matched panel: complex membership is the table leader with $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$, $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.00575848521855683$, while PTM density has $A_H=0.023360446890988432$ but $C_H=0.10469593979758543$, again showing the demotion of wrong-direction energy.

\paragraph{Null and missingness policy.}
The per-organism best absorber is not a universal readout.  The assembled table marks $H_o^*$ as measured TE in danio, complex membership in human and gallus, mRNA in yeast, and null in organisms where no candidate panel was attached or no row survived the finite policy.  The cross-organism shared absorber is therefore absent.  Empty rankings for bacillus, \emph{C. elegans}, drosophila, mouse, \emph{M. smegmatis}, rat, and \emph{S. solfataricus} are missing rows, not zero effects and not evidence against future measurements.  Bonferroni status and score are also kept separate: a row can exceed a degrees-of-freedom-matched Bonferroni threshold and still have a negative penalized score, as with human PTM density, human measured TE, yeast localization, and gallus PTM density.

\paragraph{Layer contacts and row quality.}
The table uses several measured-contact routes with different row losses.  Matched TE and mRNA rows have three-way joins of $13140$ in danio, $3415$ in \emph{E. coli}, $11554$ in human, and $5128$ in yeast.  tRNA-copy tAI is computed on $14410$ danio rows, $19053$ human rows, and $5815$ yeast rows, with anticodon table sizes $57$, $47$, and $41$.  Localization is a 12-column multi-label readout with $1984$ joined danio proteins, $16008$ human proteins, and $4656$ yeast proteins.  Feature rows are more coverage-sensitive: danio has $2242$ joined feature rows from $3180$ feature records, human has $18335$ from $18842$, and yeast has $5682$ from $6156$.  Yeast turnover has a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$ over $3640$ joined rows; human turnover has CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$ but only $8057$ joined turnover rows.  These counts are part of the interpretation, because a high score on a shifted join would not be a stable cross-organism explanation.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation mediation.}
The measured TE mediation list is not a mechanism proof; it is a comparison readout.  The measured mediation fractions are $0.0$ for danio after clipping a raw value of $-0.04801486048721136$, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  These values are organism-specific and do not align with a single universal translation-mediated explanation.  The larger residual-ranking table therefore reads the non-translation component as mostly unexplained by the attached panel, not as proof of a hidden pathway.

\paragraph{Independent geometric signals.}
The same dataset family contains a separate Window6 boundary test in which the fixed $R$ set is avoided in all 15 computed organisms: avoided count $15$, preferred count $0$, zero count $0$, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  The avoided organisms are \emph{E. coli} K-12 MG1655, \emph{M. smegmatis}, yeast, human, danio, gallus, \emph{P. aeruginosa} PAO1, \emph{B. subtilis} 168, mouse, \emph{C. elegans}, drosophila, \emph{H. salinarum}, \emph{S. solfataricus}, \emph{D. discoideum}, and rat.  This supports the existence of a finite abundance-preference signature for that fixed set, but it does not identify an H-readout mechanism for the residual-ranking table.

\paragraph{Cross-organism diagnostics.}
The organism-specificity summary reinforces the negative shared-H result.  Among 12 complete feature organisms, the dominant-coordinate versus domain contingency has Pearson chi-square $9.399999999999999$, Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$, and permutation right-tail $p=0.5814185814185814$, so domain enrichment is not resolved by this finite panel.  The modeled mediation fraction has weak, nonsignificant associations with GC3, proteome size, and tRNA gene count: $r=-0.3062616500469447$ with GC3 and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, $r=-0.033628115789504955$ with log proteome size and $p=0.913086913086913$, and $r=0.06029405206037823$ with log tRNA gene count and $p=0.8621378621378621$.  The minimal compilation summary reaches the same descriptive conclusion: final unexplained fractions are $0.9756319042010854$ in danio, $0.9942823785118229$ in \emph{E. coli}, $0.9736442326306781$ in human, and $0.8676586345294415$ in yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.

\paragraph{Local interpretation.}
The result is therefore a bounded negative finding.  The strongest raw direction-aware products remain small: danio measured TE has $A_HC_H\approx0.01157$, danio mRNA $\approx0.01073$, human complex membership $\approx0.00657$, human mRNA $\approx0.00622$, yeast mRNA $\approx0.00115$, and \emph{E. coli} mRNA $\approx0.00153$.  Description-length scoring leaves only the danio single-column translation and mRNA rows slightly positive, and no cross-organism readout is shared.  The table is useful exactly because it prevents a high-energy but low-alignment row from being promoted and because it keeps missing rows visibly missing.

\paragraph{Limits.}
No translation realization, folding state, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mechanism, shared pathway, or universal biological law follows from $A_H$, $C_H$, $A_HC_H$, $\operatorname{Score}$, rank, $H_o^*$, or the absence of $H_{\mathrm{shared}}^*$.  Those quantities are finite projection and ranking readbacks over attached rows.  Any stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched contact that measures the proposed object directly and a statistic designed for that object, not a reinterpretation of the residual-coordinate ranking.
