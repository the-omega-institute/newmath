\subsection{Direction-aware H-candidate ranking for the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{sec:q6-h-ranking-table-direction-aware-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record treats the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual as a finite cross-layer ranking problem.  For each organism $o$ with a computable residual, the residual coordinate vector $a_{\perp,o}$ is read in the nine-coordinate basis
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  A candidate readout $H$ supplies an absorbed-energy scalar $A_H$, an $H$-projected coordinate-support vector $a_H$, and the direction cosine
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_H = \frac{\langle a_H,a_{\perp}\rangle}{\lVert a_H\rVert\,\lVert a_{\perp}\rVert}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reported ranking uses the declared regularized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
Score(H) = A_H C_H - \lambda DL(H), \qquad \lambda=0.01, \qquad DL(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw product $A_H C_H$ is therefore kept visible beside the penalized score; the penalty is a stated ranking convention, not a biological law.

\paragraph{Base residual scale.}
The complement computation covers twelve organisms with finite base rows.  The modeled complement fractions include $d=0.9208949355679455$ for human on $n=19053$ rows, $d=0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish on $n=14410$ rows, $d=0.6629960038578534$ for E. coli on $n=3739$ rows, $d=0.21173219572706714$ for yeast on $n=5815$ rows, $d=0.9400341806302775$ for chicken on $n=12418$ rows, and $d=0.9573445453007974$ for rat on $n=13322$ rows.  The same table also records high residual fractions for mouse, Sulfolobus, and Mycobacterium, but no corresponding $H$ ranking rows were available for those organisms.  Thus the table is not a universal panel; it is a finite ledger with explicit missingness.

\paragraph{Null and coverage diagnostics.}
The finite rankings separate three questions: whether $A_H$ exceeds a dof-matched Bonferroni null, whether $C_H$ points in the residual direction, and whether the penalized score remains positive.  Zebrafish measured TE, zebrafish mRNA, zebrafish tRNA-copy tAI, and zebrafish PTM density exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, but only measured TE and mRNA remain positive after the stated penalty.  In human, complex membership, mRNA, PTM density, transmembrane count, localization, and measured TE exceed their Bonferroni thresholds; direction and penalty still demote measured TE and all penalized scores remain below zero.  In yeast, mRNA and localization exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, but all penalized scores are negative.  In E. coli neither mRNA nor measured TE exceeds its Bonferroni threshold.  The human turnover row is especially small: the Mathieson half-life join has $n=8057$, the turnover table reports a CDS join hit rate $0.9779294769799313$, but the absorbed fraction is only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ and does not exceed the dof-matched null $p95=0.0004342285066808464$.  Yeast turnover has $n=3640$, a reported turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$, a CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$, and absorbed energy $0.0009086031889966327$, again weak in the ranking.

\paragraph{Related finite checks.}
The broader $B^*_{Q6}$ contact ledger is not empty.  The fixed Window6 boundary $R$ set has ten sense codons and shows a cross-organism avoided abundance-preference signature: $15$ avoided directions out of $15$ directional non-ties, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$; six organisms are individually significant at the stated two-sided threshold.  This supports an association-level abundance preference for the fixed boundary set, but it does not identify a shared $H$ readout.  The measured TE mediation audit also remains organism-specific: the measured mediated fractions are $0.4793069656345167$ in yeast, $0.04091158340950673$ in E. coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ in human, and $0$ in zebrafish after clipping a raw negative value $-0.04801486048721136$. These diagnostics point to heterogeneity rather than a resolved cross-domain mechanism.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The ranking table is a bounded descriptive result.  Across the computed organisms, the best raw aligned values remain small: zebrafish measured TE reaches $A_HC_H\approx 0.01157$, human complex membership reaches $0.00657$, chicken complex membership reaches $0.00424$, yeast mRNA reaches $0.00115$, and E. coli mRNA reaches $0.00153$.  The organism-wise best absorbers are not shared, and several rows that absorb energy are directionally weak or wrong-direction.  With the declared penalty $\lambda=0.01$, only the top two zebrafish rows remain positive, while human, yeast, E. coli, and chicken have negative penalized scores.  The minimal-compilation audit is consistent with this interpretation: the measured low-dimensional readout dictionary leaves residual fractions $0.9756319042010854$ in zebrafish, $0.9942823785118229$ in E. coli, $0.9736442326306781$ in human, and $0.8676586345294415$ in yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation mechanism, folding claim, physical admissibility claim, function claim, pathway claim, phenotype claim, or universality claim follows from this table.  The entries report finite projections, direction cosines, null comparisons, missingness, and ranks under declared policies.  A future stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and statistic for that stronger statement.  In particular, $C_H$ is a coordinate-support cosine, not a molecular mechanism; $Score$ is a bookkeeping rule, not causal evidence; missing rows are not zeros; and the absence of a shared universal $H$ is a negative finite-panel result rather than a proof that no biological explanation exists.
