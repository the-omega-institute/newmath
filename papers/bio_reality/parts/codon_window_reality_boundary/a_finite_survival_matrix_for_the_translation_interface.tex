\subsection{A finite survival matrix for the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ translation interface}
\label{sec:bior-translation-survival-b-star-q6-survival-matrix}

\paragraph{Question.}
This record asks whether the nine synonymous-residual coordinates collected under $B^{*}_{Q6}$ retain any predictive mass after the amino-acid quotient and ordinary compositional controls are imposed. The tested coordinates are $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA/UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA/UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR/ACY}$, and the antisymmetric $f_3$-stress coordinate. For each residual coordinate $q_i$ and each translation-linked readout $r_j$, the intended statistic is a controlled survival score $S_{ij}=S_{q_i}(r_j\mid Z)$, with $Z$ containing amino-acid composition, organism context, $GC3$, coding-sequence length, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine coordinates. The claim is not that codon geometry alone realizes translation. The claim is the narrower finite-row statement that at least one coordinate remains visible to a translation-layer readout after these controls.

\paragraph{Coordinate and proxy coupling check.}
The first finite contact computed the nine $B^{*}_{Q6}$ synonymous residuals in the $61$-codon count space and paired them with a proxy tRNA-Leu CUN-versus-UUR log-copy spectrum for Homo sapiens, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and Escherichia coli K-12. The break rule for this proxy stage was null if every coordinate had $|A_{ij}|<0.01$. The computed matrix did not meet that null condition: all $9$ entries were nonzero above the stated threshold, with maximum absolute coupling $1882464.1106377328$. This is only a coupling audit,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ij} &= q_i^{T}\Sigma\ell_j^{\perp},\\
\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}| &= 1882464.1106377328,\\
\#\{(i,j): |A_{ij}|\geq 0.01\} &= 9,
\end{aligned}
$$
and it is not counted as survival by itself. It shows that the synonym-residual basis is not numerically orthogonal to the proxy tRNA-copy readout in the three-organism row, while leaving the controlled $S_{ij}$ question open for direct translation readouts.

\paragraph{Modeled translation survival.}
A powered cross-organism modeled-tAI contact used $40$ organisms, organism-level codon usage, GtRNAdb anticodon copy counts, and dos Reis wobble weights. The readout was the modeled per-codon tAI vector projected onto the same nine residual coordinates. The design matrix retained rank after the controls: the control block had $24$ columns, $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $17$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$. The resulting $9\times 9$ modeled survival matrix had
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2_{QT} &= 0.9003849000036579,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X) &= 9,\\
\operatorname{rank}(Z) &= 23,\\
\operatorname{df}_{\mathrm{res}} &= 17.
\end{aligned}
$$
The positivity threshold was $10^{-12}$, so this finite-row contact supports a positive modeled translation-readout survival entry and, at the matrix level, a large controlled $Q\to T$ signal. Its classification remains modeled translation realization: it uses tRNA gene-copy and wobble-weight readbacks, not measured ribosome occupancy, measured elongation speed, or protein output.

\paragraph{Three-organism Leu sign audit.}
The CUN/UUR Leu gate gives a useful warning against overreading signs. In the three-organism row the partial Pearson correlation between $\log(CUN/UUR)$ and the tRNA-Leu copy ratio was $1.0$ with $n=3$, but the organism signs were not uniform. The observed log-ratios were $1.3514980212896148$, $-0.24869078488829677$, and $1.0086640520051702$, giving signs $+,-,+$. Thus the finite contact records a positive association in the small controlled table while refusing a universal direction. A larger organism panel would be needed before treating the Leu coordinate as a population-level directionality statement.

\paragraph{Protein-abundance contacts and translation complement.}
Several downstream contacts test whether the same nine-coordinate block aligns with abundance or with the part of abundance not absorbed by modeled tAI. In the six-organism complement computation, the modeled-tAI-complement fraction $d$ of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component was $0.874628721049266$ for Danio rerio, $0.6629960038578532$ for Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655, $0.9400341806302775$ for Gallus gallus, $0.9208949355679455$ for Homo sapiens, $0.8972672018746489$ for Mycobacterium smegmatis str. mc2 155, and $0.21173219572706703$ for Saccharomyces cerevisiae. The complementary modeled-mediated fractions $m=1-d$ were therefore $0.12537127895073397$, $0.3370039961421468$, $0.05996581936972245$, $0.07910506443205445$, $0.10273279812535108$, and $0.788267804272933$. The same computation reported $R^2_{QP}=0.04613529805760884$, $0.10767526667768725$, $0.03980793595345331$, $0.04454565117192484$, $0.032090091625816136$, and $0.29837624987287115$ in the same organism order. These numbers place yeast at the opposite end of the modeled-tAI complement spectrum from the vertebrate rows: in yeast, much more of the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ abundance component is aligned with modeled translation than in human, chicken, zebrafish, or M. smegmatis.

\paragraph{Supplementary readout ranking.}
The direction-aware ranking table used a regularized score with $\lambda=0.01$ and $DL_H=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{col}})$, while also reporting raw $A_H C_H$. Danio rerio selected measured translation efficiency as $H_o^*$: $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, score $0.001566051846174291$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000719994971498518$, null $p95=0.0003297458343745959$, and $n=13140$ joined rows. In the same organism, mRNA was also positive by the score rule, with $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, score $0.000734588577977589$, Bonferroni threshold $0.000587830414454545$, and null $p95=0.00023560090511497663$. Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 had no selected $H_o^*$; its mRNA and measured-TE scores were $-0.008470202379021127$ and $-0.009446333814243976$ despite positive raw alignments. Gallus gallus and Homo sapiens selected complex membership as the top listed readout, not translation efficiency, while Saccharomyces cerevisiae selected mRNA. This ranking is therefore evidence about where the modeled-tAI complement points after projection, not a translation mechanism.

\paragraph{Abundance rows.}
Protein-abundance survival is finite and organism- or condition-specific. In yeast GFP-tagged PAXdb condition files, the shared YEPD-versus-SD join contained $2209$ proteins. The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ block explained $R^2_{QP}=0.3050385538305022$ in SD and $R^2_{QP}=0.3280825603424665$ in YEPD after the same $24$-column control design, with $\operatorname{rank}(Z)=23$, residual degrees of freedom $2186$, and $f_3$-stress dominant in both conditions. The two condition-specific survival profiles were almost identical: cosine $0.9993775935182967$ and Pearson $0.9983880256003688$ over $1000$ permutations. In human tissue proteomics, the adult B-cell, heart, and liver joins had $9244$, $6468$, and $8664$ rows, with $R^2_{QP}=0.03626117434944674$, $0.02320896321347296$, and $0.026133456228248052$. The dominant coordinates were $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ser_{UCR/AGY}$, and $Ile_{AUA}$, respectively, and the pairwise cosines were $0.6883655232187615$, $0.8990093450963941$, and $0.9176793373754919$. These abundance contacts are downstream statistical projections; they do not establish a ribosome-mediated path unless a surviving $Q\to T$ step is separately present in the same matched design.

\paragraph{mRNA stability and mediation checks.}
The yeast mRNA half-life join used $5085$ rows, with CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ against the Neymotin table. The $B^{*}_{Q6}$ block explained residualized mRNA half-life with $R^2_{QM}=0.05179429044975085$, dominant coordinate $Leu_{CUN/UUR}$, cosine $0.8040935468384264$ against the same-join protein-abundance profile, and permutation $p=0.000999000999000999$ over $1000$ permutations. On the same yeast join, $R^2_{QP}=0.2761140373962526$, $R^2_{QP\mid M}=0.24851799532219632$, and the absorbed part was $0.027596042074056304$, giving mediation fraction $0.09994436477872035$; the permutation null mean was $0.00019674487584783138$, the null $p95$ was $0.0014571003414947088$, and the right-tail permutation value was again $0.000999000999000999$. The Escherichia coli mRNA half-life row used $2720$ joined rows at growth rate $0.40\,h^{-1}$, with hit rate $0.9978649586335735$, $R^2_{QM}=0.02298556640056076$, $R^2_{QP}=0.11632311854858134$ on the same join, dominant coordinate $Arg_{AGR}$, cosine $-0.7784923375569599$, and permutation value $0.000999000999000999$. The yeast and E. coli rows therefore agree that an mRNA-stability association exists, while disagreeing in orientation and dominant coordinate.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite evidence supports the bounded statement that $B^{*}_{Q6}$ is not fully absorbed by amino-acid composition, organism context, $GC3$, length, and the $M$-density baseline when tested against modeled translation weights: the powered $40$-organism modeled-tAI matrix has $R^2_{QT}=0.9003849000036579$ with nondegenerate rank. Additional abundance, mRNA-stability, condition, tissue, complement, and ranking rows show that the same coordinate block remains statistically visible in several downstream readout panels, but those panels are not substitutes for measured translation survival. No translation mechanism, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, biological function, pathway claim, phenotype claim, or universal law follows from this chapter unless a separate layer-matched contact supplies the relevant rows and the corresponding controlled statistic. In particular, tRNA gene-copy counts are not tRNA abundance or charging, modeled tAI is not measured ribosome occupancy, protein abundance is not folding or function, and a positive proxy coupling $A_{ij}$ is not a positive survival entry unless the controlled partial-$R^2$ or description-length survival score is explicitly computed.
