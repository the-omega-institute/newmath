\subsection{Translation-conditioned protein abundance mediation for the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual surface}
\label{subsec:q6-translation-mediated-protein-abundance-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite surface.}
The record considered here is the finite mediation table whose rows are triples $(q_i,t_j,p_k)$.  The first coordinate $q_i$ ranges over the nine computed $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q=\{&\mathrm{K\_AAA},\mathrm{Arg\_AGR},\mathrm{Ile\_AUA},\mathrm{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\mathrm{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\mathrm{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\mathrm{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\mathrm{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\mathrm{f3\_stress}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The second coordinate is a translation readout.  In the powered protein-abundance run the readout is the per-protein mean modeled tAI, computed as the codon-count-weighted mean of GtRNAdb all-tRNA copy-number weights with dos Reis wobble coefficients.  The third coordinate is matched protein abundance, read as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$ after joining PAXdb abundance rows to real coding sequences.  The row is therefore not a direct $q_i \to p_k$ assertion.  It is admissible only as a controlled decomposition in which the $q_i$ residuals first meet a translation-readout coordinate and only then are compared with downstream protein abundance.

\paragraph{Residualization and rank checks.}
For every organism in the powered table the residualization controls include an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{CDS\ length})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions, GC3, and the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors.  The control matrix has $23$ effective rank and $24$ columns in each organism-specific fit.  The translation coordinate has rank $1$ after control residualization, the residual $Q$ block has rank $9$, and the joined $(Q,T)$ block has rank $10$.  Thus the positive mediation fractions are not produced by an algebraically collapsed design: the diagnostic flag \emph{powered rank sufficient} is true for all twelve organisms.  The finite row counts are substantial: $4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis}, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for human, $19395$ for mouse, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis}, $13322$ for rat, $5815$ for yeast, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  These counts make the chapter a finite cross-layer table, not a symbolic promotion from codon geometry alone.

\paragraph{Mediated fractions.}
The measured quantity is a statistical decomposition of the controlled $Q \to P$ association by conditioning on modeled translation.  In each organism the total controlled association $c=R^2(Q,P)$ is compared with the direct remainder $c'=R^2(Q,P\mid T)$, and the reported mediated fraction is $(c-c')/c$ when the fraction lies in $[0,1]$.  All twelve organism entries pass the unit-interval check.  The strongest finite entry is yeast, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= 0.29837624994140827,\\
c' &= 0.0151709093608293,\\
c-c' &= 0.28320534058057895,\\
M^{QTP} &= 0.9491551041216973.
\end{aligned}
$$
Other large mediated fractions occur in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster} with $M^{QTP}=0.6165167748739756$, \emph{Escherichia coli} with $M^{QTP}=0.612922252793667$, and \emph{Bacillus subtilis} with $M^{QTP}=0.6084594553474505$.  Intermediate entries include \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans} at $0.4872268357227724$, \emph{Danio rerio} at $0.3157053953614641$, human at $0.27206289160119157$, chicken at $0.1976903964724546$, rat at $0.17489681798543982$, and mouse at $0.15488799907323988$.  The smallest positive finite entries remain nonnegative: \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} has $M^{QTP}=0.08397602919861291$, and \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus} has $M^{QTP}=0.01850668564589326$.  The result is therefore not a single-organism sign claim.  It is a bounded twelve-organism decomposition in which modeled translation absorbs a positive part of the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual-to-protein-abundance association in every computed row.

\paragraph{Translation contact status.}
The same residual basis had an earlier modeled-tAI survival table over three organisms.  That table computed all nine residual coordinates against modeled tAI weights for \emph{Escherichia coli} K12, human, and yeast, using $61$ sense codons and local GtRNAdb payloads covering all $20$ standard amino-acid families.  It produced $81$ entries above the bounded coupling threshold $0.01$, with maximal absolute entry $3801961.031607896$.  The run explicitly marked this as modeled-tAI bounded coupling, not ribosome-profiling validation, because only $3$ organisms were present and powered survival was not allowed without at least $40$ matched species.  The present twelve-organism protein-abundance table improves the downstream row count and mediation surface, but it inherits the same translation-contact limitation: modeled tAI is a codon-adaptation readout, not an observed ribosome occupancy measurement.

\paragraph{Relation to yeast structure and rate subblocks.}
The yeast structure-mediation row provides an important local comparison.  In a five-way yeast join with $4759$ genes, the controlled $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ association with protein abundance was $R^2_{QP}=0.2715520808117803$.  Conditioning on the $5'$ coding-region MFE proxy alone changed this only to $R^2_{QP\mid S}=0.2706601505000995$, giving structure-only fraction $0.003284564452662019$.  Conditioning on measured translation efficiency plus mRNA stability reduced the association to $R^2_{QP\mid T,M}=0.13773967696311287$, and adding the MFE proxy changed it only to $0.13759011208318542$.  The structure incremental fraction was therefore $0.0005507778820193332$, while the $Q$-to-structure readout itself had $S_Q$ structure $R^2=0.003774248455422586$ with right-tail permutation $p=0.027972027972027972$.  This comparison supports a narrow interpretation: the protein-abundance signal is much more naturally read through translation and abundance covariates than through a folding or structural-order claim.  A separate observable-execution run in yeast also produced a rate-slot subblock over $3289$ genes, reporting that at least one observed rate proxy slot passed both effective null classes; it does not by itself identify a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Negative controls around higher layers.}
Several surrounding finite runs block stronger interpretations.  A dynamic mass-balance test on $120$ genes and $718$ intervals had $R^2_{L0}=0.9960628201518571$, $R^2_{L1}=0.996410628851051$, $R^2_{L2}=0.9712443377743347$, and $R^2_{L3}=0.8953999568421264$, giving $\Delta R^2=-0.07584438093220836$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-668.8046070381928$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{TEbin}}=0.6831683168316832$, and $p_{\mathrm{timeshuffle}}=0.9306930693069307$.  A turnover audit on $3290$ genes similarly gave $R^2_{L2}=0.055740851626933985$, $R^2_{L3}=0.050278872193227464$, $\Delta R^2=-0.005461979433706483$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=-13.68819707902732$ bits, $p_{\mathrm{matchedbin}}=0.8855721393034826$, and $p_{\mathrm{recoded}}=1.0$.  A chaperone-client absorption test in yeast gave held-out $\Delta R^2=-0.025094043993987934$ with $p=0.8059701492537313$ and verdict composition artifact.  A cross-ortholog selection-imprint test over $80$ orthogroups, $240$ rows, and $3$ species reported $\Delta R^2=0.03250515015613468$ but leave-clade-out $\Delta R^2=-0.0021477729853302424$ and maximum null95 $0.05427157066522958$, so the strong selection-imprint gate was false.  These finite negatives keep the chapter from becoming a folding, turnover, chaperone, mass-balance, or evolutionary-selection assertion.

\paragraph{Perturbation context.}
The separate synonymous-perturbation table supplies one positive protein-output contact in \emph{E. coli} mRFP variants: $1459$ variants, base $R^2=0.12648386762689323$, full $R^2=0.1798829276988051$, $\Delta R^2=0.05339906007191191$, $\Delta\mathrm{DL}=19.089327473282296$ bits, and matched and within-null $p=0.004975124378109453$.  This row shows that a $B^{\ast}$ window can sometimes survive a perturbational protein-output test.  It is not substituted for the twelve-organism modeled-tAI mediation table, because the perturbation design, coordinate window, and organism-specific assay differ.  It functions only as nearby evidence that protein-output contacts can be made finite when the assay itself supplies the needed layer.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The chapter supports the following bounded statement.  On the finite table of real CDS rows, modeled per-protein tAI rows, and PAXdb protein-abundance rows, the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual block has a positive statistical mediation decomposition through the modeled translation coordinate in every computed organism, with unit-interval mediated fractions from $0.01850668564589326$ to $0.9491551041216973$.  The result is strongest in yeast and remains positive across bacteria, archaea, fungi, invertebrates, fish, bird, and mammals.  The admissible conclusion is a controlled cross-layer association of the form $Q \to T \to P$ on finite rows.  It is not a direct $Q \to P$ biological law, and it is not a claim that synonymous residual coordinates cause translation, folding, function, phenotype, selection, or universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
No translation realization follows from this chapter unless a separate ribosome or translation-efficiency contact supplies observed RPF or TE rows.  No folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, causal mechanism, or universality follows from modeled tAI, protein abundance, or codon residual geometry.  Matched mRNA abundance is a control surface, not a protein-output readout.  The finite claim remains at the level of controlled statistical mediation through a modeled translation coordinate, with higher biological layers requiring their own layer-matched measurements and their own null tests.
