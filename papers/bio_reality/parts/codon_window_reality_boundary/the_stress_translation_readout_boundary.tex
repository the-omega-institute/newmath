\subsection{The $f_3$-Stress Translation-Readout Boundary}
\label{sec:q6-f3-stress-wobble-broken-translation-readout-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
The object of this record is the finite coding-sequence residual surface obtained after amino-acid quotienting, restricted to the $B^*_{Q6}$ $f_3$-stress coordinate and to matched organism-level translation readouts. The coordinate is not treated as a protein-level or phenotype-level variable. It is a residual synonymous-codon coordinate whose admissible biological question is narrower: whether the $f_3$-paired imbalance remains visible in a bounded translation row after the usual sequence and organism controls have been applied. The finite row fixes three distinctions. First, synonymous codons are compared only after amino-acid projection, so the coordinate cannot be explained by amino-acid composition alone. Second, the row is active only where context tRNA or wobble weights distinguish a codon from its third-position $f_3$ partner, written locally as $w_c \neq w_{c^{f_3}}$. Third, the permitted readout is survival after controls for amino-acid composition, organism, GC3, length, $M$-density, and mRNA abundance.

\paragraph{Saturation data.}
The finite saturation row gives an explicit codon boundary for the $f_3$ operation. The measured set
$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}
\end{aligned}
$$
has $20$ codons. The saturated $f_3$ image removes exactly the two codons $\mathrm{ACA}$ and $\mathrm{ACG}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Sat}_{f_3}(R) &= \{\mathrm{AAA},\mathrm{AAG},\mathrm{AGA},\mathrm{AGG},\mathrm{AUA},\mathrm{AUG},\mathrm{CUA},\mathrm{CUC},\mathrm{CUG},\mathrm{CUU},\\
&\qquad \mathrm{UAA},\mathrm{UAG},\mathrm{UCA},\mathrm{UCG},\mathrm{UGA},\mathrm{UGG},\mathrm{UUA},\mathrm{UUG}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite boundary is empty in the verified row, $\partial_{f_3}=\varnothing$, while the recorded loss from $M$ to the saturated row is the two-element pair $\{\mathrm{ACA},\mathrm{ACG}\}$. This is a coordinate fact, not by itself a translation fact.

\paragraph{Translation row.}
For each organism and translation readout $T_j$, the permitted bounded readback is the controlled survival entry
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j} = \Delta R^2_{\mathrm{partial}}(B^*_{Q6,f_3};T_j\mid Z),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Z$ contains amino-acid composition, organism indicators, GC3, length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA abundance whenever the readout is ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency. A positive row means only that the residual coordinate contributes to the translation-layer readout beyond the baseline; it does not say that the coordinate controls downstream abundance, structure, localization, stability, function, or phenotype. The conjecture is broken if the powered panel remains non-positive or rank-degenerate after these controls. In particular, an all-$1.0$ rank matrix on three organisms is a null boundary for this purpose, not survival.

\paragraph{Powered rows already present.}
The surrounding $B^*_{Q6}$ finite rows justify why this conjecture is worth testing but do not settle it. In a powered axis-strength spectrum with $11$ computed organisms out of $16$ requested, five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns, the captured spectra were led by $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ in all $11$ per-organism dominance calls, followed on average by $d_{\mathrm{tRNA}}$; the largest recorded GC3 rank effect was $d_{\mathrm{usage}}$ with $\rho=0.136$. A boundary-set assay over $40$ computed organisms out of $46$ requested, with $6$ skipped organisms and $1000$ permutations at $\alpha=0.05$, found that $R$ sense codons are systematically tRNA-poor under GtRNAdb tGCN plus dos Reis wobble weights. That result supports a tRNA-supply explanation for $R$ avoidance, while the present $f_3$-stress claim still requires the translation-readout survival row.

\paragraph{Axis constraints.}
The broader powered geometry also records a nontrivial residual structure. A two-axis closure assay over $11$ of $16$ organisms, five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns concluded that a third axis is present because $d_{\perp}$ has systematically positive held-out $R^2$ and same-signed directions across organisms. A third-axis identity assay on the same organism and fold counts aligned $d_{\perp}$ to within-family usage frequency, with mean cosine $0.128$ and one-sided sign-test $p=0.000488$. A separate mechanism assay kept the $d_{\perp}$ signal after GC orthogonalization and reported the finite classification ``selection-not-GC; not-wobble-clustered; CSC/positional identity needs external evidence.'' These rows sharpen the local question: $f_3$-stress becomes a translation-layer distinction only where the wobble or tRNA context actually breaks the $f_3$ symmetry and where the translation row remains positive after controls.

\paragraph{Residual optimality background.}
Additional powered rows constrain possible overreadings. The universal core tRNA-adaptation assay computed $11$ of $16$ organisms under five folds with $41$ synonymous design columns. The non-tRNA residual optimality assay on the same finite design identified $f3\_translation\_selection$ as the dominant residual axis. The three-axis sufficiency assay concluded that a fourth mechanism is present, while the mRNA-stability identity assay, also using five folds, at least $500$ proteins per organism, and $41$ synonymous design columns, concluded ``fourth\_axis\_not\_mrna\_stability'' because the yeast half-life direction had low alignment with abundance $d_{\mathrm{resid4}}$ under the fixed gates. These rows allow the paper to say that an $f_3$-related residual signal is structurally plausible in the coordinate system. They do not allow the paper to claim that the present coordinate has already survived against a matched translation-efficiency row.

\paragraph{Reality contacts.}
The finite contact list separates sequence summaries from translation measurements. Per-organism codon-usage tables provide codon-count or codon-frequency summaries. Organism-level tRNA-Leu gene-copy resources provide anticodon-family copy-number readbacks. tAI or stAI tables provide modelled codon-adaptation weights and may be used only as bounded translation-layer annotations, not as observed ribosome dynamics. Ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contacts, when downloaded and matched to the same CDS identifiers, provide per-CDS ribosome occupancy or $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$. Matched mRNA abundance is required to separate translation-layer signal from transcription-layer signal. Without those matched rows, the coordinate remains a finite residual hypothesis rather than an observed translation effect.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The chapter therefore records a conditional cross-layer conjecture. Support requires at least one controlled entry $S_{f3\text{-}stress,T_j}>0$ above the agreed partial-$R^2$ threshold under baseline ablation. Failure occurs if all entries are non-positive, or if the matrix is rank-degenerate after the required controls. No translation realization, folding conclusion, physical admissibility claim, biological function, mechanistic interpretation, or universality statement follows from the coordinate geometry alone; each such statement would require its own matched contact at that layer. The present record asserts only the finite test: $f_3$-stress should become visible to translation readouts precisely where tRNA or wobble weights break third-position $f_3$ symmetry, and it should disappear as evidence when that controlled survival row is absent.
