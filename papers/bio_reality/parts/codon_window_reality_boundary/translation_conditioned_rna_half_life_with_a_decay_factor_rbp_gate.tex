\subsection{Translation-Conditioned RNA Half-Life With a Decay-Factor RBP Gate}\label{subsec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record treats the claimed object as a finite matched table, not as a genome-wide law.  Each admissible row contains a coding-transcript or CDS identifier, an amino-acid-quotiented $B^*_{Q6}$ residual-coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy information for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and controls capable of separating transcription, abundance, isoform state, local RNA composition, and codon context.  The coordinate contrast is the high-label versus low-label split after amino-acid identity has been quotiented away.  The biological contrast is not merely whether half-life differs across that split, but whether the half-life residual tracks an experimentally separated RBP-occupancy change at the editable RNA window and whether the effect is removed and restored by the appropriate perturbation and rescue rows.

\paragraph{Local null.}
The null model is exchangeability of high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels with respect to RNA half-life after conditioning on amino-acid sequence, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and the mandatory same-row translation readout.  In symbols, the intended finite comparison has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0 &: \Delta_{1/2}(B^*_{Q6}\text{ high},B^*_{Q6}\text{ low}\mid T,A,C,I,S,G,D,P,M,L)=0,\\
H_1 &: \Delta_{1/2}\neq 0\text{ and the signed residual follows the prespecified RBP occupancy, loss, and rescue pattern.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $T$ denotes the translation readout, $A$ the amino-acid quotient, $C$ transcription and abundance controls, $I$ isoform state, $S$ local RNA structure, $G$ GC and dinucleotide composition, $D$ codon-pair context, $P$ batch and platform factors, $M$ motif-density controls, and $L$ the local RBP-occupancy/loss/rescue panel.  A half-life difference without the $L$ pattern remains an RNA-stability association, not an RBP-mediated cross-layer relation.

\paragraph{Observed anchors from finite rows.}
The available finite-row evidence supports the feasibility of the separate contacts but does not itself close the RBP-decay bridge.  The RBP-binding anchor is a bounded RBNS/eCLIP comparison with $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases in run \texttt{b225890251c2}.  The translation-readout anchor is represented by the mTOR translation table with $n=6494$ rows in run \texttt{0c2bbfd3e2d9}.  RNA-modification rows show that finite cross-layer tables can carry continuous stoichiometry and condition checks: the m6Am table has $n=31886$, $19406$ distinct entries, $3230$ low-tail rows below $0.8$, $20942$ rows in the variable subset, a saturated-at-least-$0.95$ fraction of $0.343223$, and a GC-matched null value $p=0.000833$ with partial correlations $0.268094$, $0.098666$, and $0.168335$ in the reported full, low-tail, and variable subsets.  The m6A stoichiometry table has $n=60000$, $49668$ distinct entries, cross-condition concordance marked true, and a coverage-confound correlation of $-0.135922814$.  Additional finite readout scales include $1176$ transcription-elongation rows with $1137$ distinct entries, $6820$ mutational-tolerance rows with $6768$ distinct entries, and $657$ N-terminal acetylation rows with AUROC $0.942081$ over $622$ high/low cases.  These numbers constrain the kind of finite table the present claim would require: the bridge must be row-matched, explicitly conditioned, and incapable of promoting a coordinate association into a mechanism without the missing perturbation/rescue structure.

\paragraph{Bridge test.}
A positive instance requires five row-level events to cohere in the same matched panel.  First, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows must remain distinguishable after the listed controls and after conditioning on the same-row translation readout.  Second, the signed RNA half-life residual must follow a measured local change in occupancy of the prespecified decay-factor RBP.  Third, loss of the target RBP must collapse the coordinate-associated half-life residual.  Fourth, wild-type rescue must return the residual in the expected direction.  Fifth, binding-defective rescue or a non-target control must fail to restore the effect.  The finite readback is therefore not the scalar half-life difference alone; it is the joint pattern consisting of translation-conditioned half-life residual, local RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss collapse, wild-type rescue return, and failed binding-defective or non-target rescue.

\paragraph{Exclusions.}
The record does not license translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It also does not license protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, structural order, function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or universality.  RNA-structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, abundance, GC/dinucleotide, codon-pair, and batch explanations remain live unless the matched controls and the RBP loss/rescue panel remove them.  A binding-defective rescue, a non-target rescue, or unchanged local RBP occupancy counts against the RBP interpretation rather than for it.  The most that can be concluded without the complete panel is a bounded descriptor of synonymous-coordinate RNA stability, optionally conditioned on translation readout, but not a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Decision.}
The chapter records a ready finite conjectural specification because the carrier, null, control set, and failure modes are explicit.  The positive biological claim remains conditional on a future same-window table that joins $B^*_{Q6}$ residual labels, translation readouts, RNA half-life measurements, local decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls in the same rows.  Until those rows exist, the scientifically admissible output is the test design and the negative boundary: coordinate-associated half-life is not promoted to an RBP bridge merely because an RNA half-life residual is observed.
