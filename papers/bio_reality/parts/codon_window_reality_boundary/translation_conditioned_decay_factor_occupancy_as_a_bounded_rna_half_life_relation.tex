\subsection{Translation-conditioned decay-factor occupancy as a bounded RNA half-life relation}\label{sec:q6-translation-conditioned-decay-rbp-rna-half-life-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The object of this record is a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row carries a residual $B^{*}_{Q6}$ coordinate label, an amino-acid-composition control, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the same local window, transcription-rate controls, local RNA-structure controls, motif-density controls, GC and dinucleotide controls, codon-pair controls, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, perturbation state, rescue state, and batch labels.  The row is not a whole-gene biological explanation.  It is a bounded comparison unit in which a high-coordinate and a low-coordinate synonymous label may be exchanged only after the listed controls have been attached.  The intended readback is the finite statistic relating coordinate label, local target-RBP occupancy, perturbation and rescue state, same-row translation readout, and RNA half-life.

\paragraph{Claim form.}
The claim is conditional.  A coordinate-associated RNA half-life difference is licensed as a cross-layer relation only when the half-life contrast follows measured local occupancy of the declared decay-factor RBP after coordinate and occupancy have been experimentally separated.  In addition, the contrast must collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective rescue or a non-target control.  The matched null is that, after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC composition, dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, same-row translation readout, and batch are conditioned on, the high- and low-coordinate labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life.  A positive row statistic therefore has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \Delta h\bigl(B^{*}_{Q6}{\rm\ label}, O_{\rm RBP}, P_{\rm loss}, P_{\rm rescue}, U_{\rm transl}, Z\bigr),\\
Z &= ({\rm transcription}, {\rm structure}, {\rm motifs}, {\rm GC}, {\rm dinucleotides}, {\rm codon\ pairs}, {\rm isoform}, {\rm abundance}, {\rm batch}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $h$ is the RNA half-life readout, $O_{\rm RBP}$ is measured target-RBP occupancy at the same window, $P_{\rm loss}$ and $P_{\rm rescue}$ encode perturbation and rescue state, and $U_{\rm transl}$ is the attached translation readout.  The statistic is not allowed to ignore $U_{\rm transl}$, because the conjecture concerns a translation-conditioned RNA-decay relation rather than a direct map from residual coordinate geometry to RNA stability.

\paragraph{Available finite contacts.}
The repository-local half-life contact supplies organism-scoped transcript or gene rows with externally curated mRNA half-life estimates or normalized consensus half-life readouts.  Its resolution is per-gene or per-transcript before any same-window RBP-decay statistic is computed.  The contact is useful as a half-life boundary, but by itself it lacks same-window decay-factor occupancy and perturbation or rescue rows.  The translation contact supplies per-CDS ribosome occupancy or translation efficiency, with translation efficiency computed from ribosome-protected fragments and matched mRNA abundance.  The matched mRNA contact supplies per-CDS read counts or TPM in the same experimental condition as the ribosome-profiling run, allowing a separated translation-efficiency residual rather than a transcript-abundance surrogate.

\paragraph{Empirical calibration from related finite rows.}
Three verified finite analyses calibrate the level of numerical evidence expected for this cross-layer row.  In a yeast repair-persistence panel with $12055$ one-kilobase windows, adding sequence information to the $0$ hour dipyrimidine and GC baseline increased held-out $R^{2}$ from $0.1878$ to $0.1989$, giving incremental held-out $R^{2}=0.0111$ for the RSC-dependent readout under $1000$ permutations with $p=0.003$ and runtime $284.4$ seconds.  The same run reported SWI/SNF specificity incremental held-out $R^{2}=0.0246$ but classified the result as not RSC-specific, recorded as $\mathrm{rsc\_specific}=\mathrm{false}$.  In a separate in-vitro nucleosome occupancy panel with $36247$ three-hundred-base-pair windows, the composition-only baseline held-out $R^{2}$ was $0.6173$ and the structure-augmented held-out $R^{2}$ was $0.6925$, so the structure increment was $0.0752$ under $500$ permutations with $p=0.002$ and runtime $402.8$ seconds.  A powered yeast observation-ceiling run for $B^{*}_{Q6}$ cross-condition observation completed on organism $\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}$ with runtime $497.03086400032043$ seconds.  These numbers do not prove the present RNA half-life claim.  They fix a useful standard: finite biological rows can carry small but measurable held-out increments, and module labels must remain local when the specificity test is negative.

\paragraph{Support and failure conditions.}
Support requires four simultaneous finite-row outcomes.  First, after all controls, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous windows must differ in RNA half-life only in the stratum where measured target-RBP occupancy differs at the same local window.  Second, coordinate and occupancy must be experimentally decoupled; a coordinate-only half-life contrast without corresponding target-RBP occupancy movement is not enough.  Third, target-RBP loss must erase the coordinate-associated half-life effect, and wild-type rescue must restore it.  Fourth, binding-defective rescue, non-target controls, RNA-structure alternatives, motif-density alternatives, transcription-rate alternatives, isoform-state alternatives, GC or dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, and batch must not reproduce the full pattern.  If any one of these tests fails, the row may still report an RNA half-life association, but it cannot be read as the proposed decay-factor bridge.

\paragraph{Local negative boundary.}
This record does not infer translation realization from RNA half-life alone.  It does not infer protein abundance, protein folding, physical admissibility, cellular function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, or a universal sequence rule.  It also does not infer an RNA-decay mechanism unless measured same-window target-RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target controls are actually present.  The existing half-life payloads, ribosome-profiling contacts, and matched mRNA controls are necessary pieces of the row design, but they are not sufficient for a positive RBP-decay conclusion without the occupancy and perturbation/rescue panel.  Thus the output of the chapter is a bounded finite statistic over matched RNA-window rows, not a general law about synonymous sequence.
