\subsection{Direction-aware ranking of the non-translation residual}\label{sec:q6-h-ranking-table-direction-aware-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.} The record studied here is a finite per-organism ledger for the modeled-translation-complement abundance residual over the nine $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ coordinates
$K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$. For each organism $o$ and fetchable readout $H$, the table stores the absorbed residual energy $A_H$, the residual coordinate-support cosine $C_H$, the unpenalized directional product $A_H C_H$, a description length $DL(H)$, and the penalized score
$$
\begin{aligned}
Score(o,H) &= A_H(o,H) C_H(o,H) - \lambda DL(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_H).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $n_H$ is the number of readout columns. Single-column readouts therefore have $DL=1$, while the twelve-column localization readout has $DL=\log_2(13)=3.700439718141092$. The coordinate-support cosine is not a biological process; it is the finite comparison between the residual support $a_{\perp}$ and the $H$-projected support $a_H$.

\paragraph{Residual scale before the ranking.} The modeled-translation-complement residual is large in several animals and small in yeast. On the full complement joins, $d_{modeled}=\|P_{Q\perp Tmod}\|^2/\|P_Q\|^2$ is $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.874628721049266$ for danio, $0.9400341806302775$ for gallus, $0.6629960038578534$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.21173219572706714$ for yeast, $0.8972672018746484$ for $M.\ smegmatis$, $0.7852113562436636$ for $B.\ subtilis$, $0.739922597382677$ for $C.\ elegans$, $0.769859745412206$ for drosophila, $0.9469912232040104$ for mouse, $0.9573445453007974$ for rat, and $0.988614641876908$ for $S.\ solfataricus$. The readout is therefore not a weak numerical artifact of a vanishing residual: in human, danio, gallus, mouse, rat, and $S.\ solfataricus$ the modeled translation channel leaves most of $P_Q$ outside $Tmod$.

\paragraph{Ranking rows.} The strongest unpenalized directional products are small. In danio, measured TE gives $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, and $Score=0.001566051846174291$; mRNA gives $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and $Score=0.000734588577977589$. These two danio rows are the only listed single-readout entries with a positive score after the stated $0.01$ penalty. Danio measured tRNA-copy tAI absorbs more energy, $A_H=0.02091194580949614$, but its direction cosine is only $C_H=0.33399841523960977$, so $A_HC_H=0.006984556759948309$ and $Score=-0.003015443240051691$.

\paragraph{Human rows.} Human has several Bonferroni-exceeding readouts, but none survive the description-length penalty. The top single-column row by the assembled score is complex membership, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, $Score=-0.0034339698846599055$, and a Bonferroni threshold $0.0002646171322365322$. mRNA follows with $A_H=0.007858222847072393$, $C_H=0.7921354915786852$, $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$, and $Score=-0.0037752227820994546$. PTM density has larger absorbed energy, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but the direction filter lowers it to $C_H=0.3604440741749213$, $A_HC_H=0.0053651472982577525$, and $Score=-0.004634852701742248$. Human measured TE is a useful negative control for directionality: it exceeds its Bonferroni threshold, yet $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, so $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$ and $Score=-0.01031302107580362$.

\paragraph{Yeast, bacteria, and gallus rows.} Yeast ranks mRNA first, but the value is small: $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and $Score=-0.008853737772559931$. Yeast localization absorbs $A_H=0.011225105608516642$, exceeds its Bonferroni threshold, and has $C_H=0.4247910216132792$, but the twelve-column description length gives $Score=-0.03223607310225219$. In $E.\ coli$, mRNA is the top row with $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$ and $Score=-0.008470202379021127$, while measured TE has $A_HC_H=0.0005536661857560242$ and $Score=-0.009446333814243976$; neither exceeds its Bonferroni threshold. Gallus has feature-only rows on the available table: complex membership gives $A_H=0.0074424743012025205$, $C_H=0.5699065404576333$, $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$, and $Score=-0.00575848521855683$, while PTM density absorbs $A_H=0.023360446890988432$ but has only $C_H=0.10469593979758543$.

\paragraph{No shared absorber.} The best per-organism absorber $H_o^{\ast}$ is organism-specific: danio selects measured TE, human selects complex membership, gallus selects complex membership, and yeast selects mRNA. $E.\ coli$ and the organisms without assembled $H$ rows have no positive finite-table winner under the declared scoring rule. There is therefore no shared universal $H$ in this finite panel. This is an informative negative result: the ranking supports a bounded table of weak, organism-specific projections, not a common biological channel.

\paragraph{Null and support discipline.} The nulls are degree-of-freedom matched and Bonferroni-adjusted at the row level. Examples show why score, direction, and null status must remain separate. Human localization exceeds its Bonferroni threshold with $A_H=0.009254913329269913$ and $C_H=0.7450003925723202$, yet its $DL=3.700439718141092$ gives $Score=-0.030109483117882037$. Danio PTM density exceeds its Bonferroni threshold but has $Score=-0.0006084600436587102$. Human turnover has $A_H=0.0005945344095654461$, $C_H=0.6818556702481351$, and $Score=-0.009594613341680174$, and the separate human turnover test reports only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ absorbed energy against a null $95$th percentile of $0.0004342285066808464$. A rank does not rewrite the null call, and a null call does not create a mechanism.

\paragraph{Contact and coverage checks.} The table is tied to finite measured rows rather than inferred biology. Measured TE joins $13140$ danio, $3415$ $E.\ coli$, $11554$ human, and $5128$ yeast rows; mRNA uses the same organism-level row counts. Measured tRNA-copy tAI is available for $14410$ danio rows with $8846$ tRNA genes, $19053$ human rows with $431$ tRNA genes, and $5815$ yeast rows with $273$ tRNA genes. Localization joins $1984$ danio, $16008$ human, and $4656$ yeast proteins over $12$ localization columns. Feature rows join $2242$ danio, $18335$ human, and $5682$ yeast proteins; danio feature coverage shifts the residual target substantially, with $d_{modeled}$ moving from the full-join reference $0.874628721049266$ to $0.7361678489976374$. Yeast turnover joins $3640$ proteins, with a reported CDS join hit rate $0.9156038419146592$ and turnover join hit rate $0.8818572656921754$. Human turnover joins $8057$ proteins, while $10996$ CDS rows lack a turnover match.

\paragraph{Relations to adjacent finite tests.} Several supporting rows constrain the interpretation. The Window6 boundary $R$ set shows an avoided abundance-preference signature across all $15$ computed organisms: avoided count $15$, preferred count $0$, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$. This is a boundary association, not a translation, structure, function, or causal claim. The measured-TE mediation table gives measured mediated fractions $0$ for danio, $0.04091158340950673$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast. The cross-organism meta table reports no resolved domain preference for dominant residual coordinate: the domain-by-coordinate permutation test has $p=0.5814185814185814$ with $n=12$, and $M^{QTP}$ correlations with GC3, proteome size, and tRNA gene count have two-sided permutation $p$ values $0.35264735264735264$, $0.913086913086913$, and $0.8621378621378621$. The minimal compilation test is also negative across the computed organisms, with median compressibility $0.026$ and organism values $0.024368095798914635$ for danio, $0.005717621488177116$ for $E.\ coli$, $0.02635576736932188$ for human, and $0.13234136547055853$ for yeast. The optimal-codon direction test is only clustered, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ over $15$ computed organisms.

\paragraph{Local boundary.} The scientific content of this section is limited to a finite, reproducible ranking of attached readouts against a residual coordinate ledger. It does not say that measured TE, mRNA, tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, localization, PTM density, transmembrane count, domain count, or complex membership causes the residual. It does not infer translation realization beyond the specific translation readout rows, does not infer folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, pathway, trafficking, compartment mechanism, or universality, and does not turn $C_H$ into biology. Missing rows remain missing rather than zero, negative, or within-null evidence. A stronger biological statement would require a separate layer-matched contact and a statistic designed for that stronger layer.
