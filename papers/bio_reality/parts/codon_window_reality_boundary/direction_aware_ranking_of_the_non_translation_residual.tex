\subsection{Direction-Aware Ranking of the Non-Translation Residual}\label{sec:q6-h-ranking-table-direction-aware-cross-layer}
\origin{ai}

\paragraph{Finite object.}
This record concerns a cross-layer relation, not a mechanism claim.  For each organism $o$ and each available readout $H$, the finite row stores the absorbed residual energy $A_H$, the coordinate-support direction cosine $C_H$, the raw directional alignment $A_H C_H$, the description-length penalty, and the resulting score.  The score used in the table is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Score}(o,H) &= A_H(o,H) C_H(o,H)-\lambda DL(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\\
DL(H) &= \log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}(H)).
\end{aligned}
$$
Single-column readouts therefore have $DL(H)=1$, whereas the twelve-column localization readout has $DL(H)=\log_2(13)=3.700439718141092$.  The direction cosine is used only as a coordinate readback filter:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_H &= \frac{\langle a_H,a_{\perp}\rangle}{\lVert a_H\rVert\lVert a_{\perp}\rVert}.
\end{aligned}
$$
It compares the externally supplied readout-support direction $a_H$ with the residual coordinate support $a_{\perp}$ over the nine $B^*_{Q6}$ coordinates $K\_AAA$, $Arg\_AGR$, $Ile\_AUA$, $Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR$, $Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG$, $Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY$, $Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG$, $Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY$, and $f3\_stress$.  It is not a biological process variable.

\paragraph{Base residual and row coverage.}
The base complement computation supplies a nine-dimensional $a_{\perp}$ row for twelve organisms with complete base joins: $n=4042$ for \emph{Bacillus subtilis} 168, $11661$ for \emph{Caenorhabditis elegans}, $14410$ for \emph{Danio rerio}, $13295$ for \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $12418$ for \emph{Gallus gallus}, $19053$ for \emph{Homo sapiens}, $19395$ for \emph{Mus musculus}, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} mc2 155, $13322$ for \emph{Rattus norvegicus}, $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and $1656$ for \emph{Sulfolobus solfataricus}.  The residual fraction after modeled tAI removal is small in yeast and large in metazoan rows: $d=0.21173219572706714$ for yeast, $0.6629960038578534$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.874628721049266$ for zebrafish, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.9400341806302775$ for chicken, $0.9469912232040104$ for mouse, and $0.9573445453007974$ for rat.  Thus the ranking table is primarily a finite ledger of what measured readouts absorb from a residual that remains substantial in many organisms.

\paragraph{Direction-aware ranking results.}
The largest raw directional term in the assembled table is zebrafish measured TE, with $A_H=0.017594290015070787$, $C_H=0.6573753096184687$, $A_HC_H=0.011566051846174291$, $n=13140$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.001566051846174291$.  Zebrafish mRNA is close behind, with $A_H=0.011973033879985887$, $C_H=0.896563785384548$, $A_HC_H=0.01073458857797759$, and $\operatorname{Score}=0.000734588577977589$.  Zebrafish measured tRNA-copy tAI has higher absorbed energy, $A_H=0.02091194580949614$, but weak alignment, $C_H=0.33399841523960977$, so its raw directional term falls to $0.006984556759948309$ and its score is negative.

Human shows the same separation between absorption and direction.  The human top-ranked single-column readouts by raw direction are complex membership, with $A_H=0.007527199987780841$, $C_H=0.8723071163246565$, and $A_HC_H=0.006566030115340095$, and mRNA, with $A_H=0.007858222847072393$, $C_H=0.7921354915786852$, and $A_HC_H=0.006224777217900546$.  Human PTM density has the largest absorbed energy among those three, $A_H=0.014884825920744861$, but its direction cosine is only $0.3604440741749213$, so the direction-filtered term is $0.0053651472982577525$.  Human measured TE is an explicit wrong-direction row: $A_H=0.002894714925796667$, $C_H=-0.10813537216189718$, and $A_HC_H=-0.00031302107580362116$, even though its dof-matched Bonferroni flag is true.

Yeast has weak aligned single-column rows and a larger but penalized localization row.  Yeast mRNA has $A_H=0.001897605472160837$, $C_H=0.6040571890503664$, $A_HC_H=0.0011462622274400686$, and $\operatorname{Score}=-0.008853737772559931$.  Yeast localization has $A_H=0.011225105608516642$ and $C_H=0.4247910216132792$, but the twelve-column penalty gives $\operatorname{Score}=-0.03223607310225219$.  In \emph{E. coli}, the available table contains only mRNA and measured TE rows: mRNA has $A_HC_H=0.0015297976209788734$ and measured TE has $0.0005536661857560242$, with both Bonferroni flags false.  Chicken has feature rows only: complex membership gives $A_HC_H=0.004241514781443171$, while PTM density again illustrates high energy in a poor direction, $A_H=0.023360446890988432$, $C_H=0.10469593979758543$, and $A_HC_H=0.0024457439413436166$.

\paragraph{Null and selection interpretation.}
The dof-matched Bonferroni entries are kept separate from rank and score.  Zebrafish measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, mRNA, and PTM density exceed their dof-matched thresholds, but localization, transmembrane count, domain count, and complex membership do not.  Human complex membership, localization, measured TE, mRNA, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed the dof-matched threshold; human measured tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, and domain count do not.  Yeast mRNA and localization exceed the threshold, while yeast measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, complex membership, PTM density, domain count, and transmembrane count do not.  These flags do not identify a shared readout channel: the per-organism best absorber is measured TE for zebrafish, complex membership for human and chicken, mRNA for yeast, and unresolved for organisms whose ranking list is empty or whose available rows do not pass the combined scoring interpretation.

\paragraph{Relation to measured translation and boundary rows.}
The measured-TE mediation table supplies the translation-side context.  The raw measured mediated fractions are $-0.04801486048721136$ for zebrafish, $0.04091158340950673$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast, with the negative zebrafish value clipped to $0$ in the unit-interval readback.  The modeled-complement table gives $m=0.12537127895073397$ for zebrafish, $0.3370039961421468$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.07910506443205445$ for human, $0.05996581936972245$ for chicken, $0.10273279812535108$ for \emph{M. smegmatis}, and $0.788267804272933$ for yeast.  These numbers show that measured TE and modeled tAI are not interchangeable readouts.  The Window6 boundary set also has an independent abundance-preference signature: among fifteen computed organisms, all fifteen are in the avoided direction and none are preferred or zero, with one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$ and two-sided $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$; among individually significant organisms, the avoided count is six of six.  This supports a bounded abundance association for that fixed boundary set, not an explanation of the H-ranking residual.

\paragraph{Cross-organism verdict.}
No universal $H$ is selected by these finite rows.  The largest raw direction-aware terms remain below $0.012$, and the positive description-length-penalized scores occur only in zebrafish measured TE and zebrafish mRNA.  The minimal-compression calculation reaches the same descriptive conclusion: the final unexplained fractions are $0.9756319042010854$ for zebrafish, $0.9942823785118229$ for \emph{E. coli}, $0.9736442326306781$ for human, and $0.8676586345294415$ for yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.  The direction-aware ordered dictionary emits no positive H readout in its organism table for yeast, \emph{E. coli}, human, or zebrafish, leaving residual fractions $0.8676586345294415$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9798666209022441$, and $0.9756319042010854$.  The organism-specific meta table is also heterogeneous rather than universal: complete-feature rows cover twelve organisms, the dominant-coordinate versus domain permutation test has $p=0.5814185814185814$, and the measured $M^{QTP}$ fraction has weak continuous associations with GC3, proteome size, and tRNA gene count, with two-sided permutation $p$ values $0.35264735264735264$, $0.913086913086913$, and $0.8621378621378621$.  The codon-conservation summary is clustered, with mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ over fifteen computed organisms and forty-one synonymous design columns, but it does not resolve a shared H readout.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
The table is therefore ready as a finite descriptive chapter: it records which attached readouts absorb residual energy, whether their coordinate support points in the same direction as the residual, and how the stated penalty changes the ranking.  It does not state that any readout causes the residual, realizes translation, determines folding, proves physical admissibility, encodes function, identifies a pathway, supplies a phenotype, or establishes universality.  Missing rows remain unavailable rather than negative evidence.  A stronger biological statement would require a separate same-organism contact and statistic for the target layer being claimed, with the H-side support supplied independently of the residual coordinates and independently of the ranking output.
