\subsection{mRNA-controlled translation-efficiency contact for $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual coordinates}
\label{sec:q6-mrna-controlled-translation-efficiency-survival-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite readout surface.}
This record tests whether any $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ synonymous residual coordinate remains visible at a ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency readout after the transcript-abundance layer has been explicitly controlled.  The finite carrier is the CDS-by-organism table obtained by joining real CDS codon counts to organism-matched translation-efficiency values and matched mRNA abundance.  The residual coordinate list is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{Q6} = (&\texttt{K\_AAA},\ \texttt{Arg\_AGR},\ \texttt{Ile\_AUA},\ \texttt{Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR},\ \texttt{Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG},\\
&\texttt{Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY},\ \texttt{Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG},\ \texttt{Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY},\ \texttt{f3\_stress}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The control matrix contains an intercept, $\log(\mathrm{cds\_len\_nt})$, the twenty amino-acid composition proportions $A,C,D,E,F,G,H,I,K,L,M,N,P,Q,R,S,T,V,W,Y$, GC3 computed from the real CDS codon counts, the $M$-density baseline on the union support of the nine projected $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ vectors, and matched mRNA abundance for the translation-efficiency contact.  In the four-organism measured-TE run, the joined table sizes were $n=13140$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $n=3415$ for \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $n=11554$ for \textit{Homo sapiens}, and $n=5128$ for \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Each organism reports $24$ control columns and rank $23$, so the controlled surface is rank-powered rather than underdetermined.

\paragraph{Controlled survival statistic.}
For each organism, let $P_Q$ denote the projection of the abundance-side signal onto the nine-dimensional $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual span after the stated controls, and let $P_{Q\perp T\mathrm{mod}}$ denote the component not absorbed by the modeled-tAI translation baseline.  The descriptive complement fraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=\frac{\|P_{Q\perp T\mathrm{mod}}\|^2}{\|P_Q\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
On the measured-TE all-readout joins, the computed pairs $(\|P_Q\|^2,\|P_{Q\perp T\mathrm{mod}}\|^2)$ were $(1092.0114815505467,919.9445878158525)$ for \textit{Danio rerio}, $(393.78692095732885,261.0102870984463)$ for \textit{E. coli}, $(565.3850201235074,484.31956581711796)$ for human, and $(675.3275479192903,151.18264752811882)$ for yeast.  The resulting $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ values were $0.8424312412078521$, $0.6628211177352172$, $0.8566190269974241$, and $0.22386566043976477$, respectively.  Projecting the same controlled residual target onto residualized measured TE gave additional absorbed fractions $0.01759429001506974$, $0.0010982956489726373$, $0.0028947149257963897$, and $0.0002762783754375593$, with combined fractions $0.8276090159036916$, $0.6620871850159149$, $0.854133502300174$, and $0.2237928123367028$.

\paragraph{Join stability checks.}
The translation-efficiency join itself changes the available CDS table in two organisms.  Against the full abundance-CDS complement references, \textit{E. coli} and yeast matched within the $0.02$ tolerance: $0.6629960038578532$ versus $0.6628211177352172$ for \textit{E. coli}, and $0.21173219572706703$ versus $0.22386566043976477$ for yeast.  \textit{Danio rerio} and human did not match that tolerance: zebrafish moved from $0.874628721049266$ on $14410$ abundance-CDS rows to $0.8424312412078521$ on $13140$ TE rows, and human moved from $0.9208949355679455$ on $19053$ abundance-CDS rows to $0.8566190269974241$ on $11554$ TE rows.  These failures are not ignored; they identify a row-availability effect in the measured-TE contact.  The survival table is therefore a controlled descriptive contact on the observed all-readout join, not a population-level biological law.

\paragraph{Comparison with neighboring contacts.}
The measured tRNA-copy tAI contact used full abundance-CDS joins for zebrafish, human, and yeast: $14410$, $19053$, and $5815$ rows, with rank $23$ in every case.  It parsed $8846$ zebrafish tRNA genes over $57$ anticodon entries, $431$ human tRNA genes over $47$ entries, and $273$ yeast tRNA genes over $41$ entries.  The measured-tRNA absorption fractions were $0.020817102845637092$ for zebrafish, $0.00005426229300865148$ for human, and $0.001530747712165342$ for yeast.  The yeast turnover contact joined $3640$ half-life rows, matched the complement reference within $0.02$ with $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}=0.2232704509412724$ versus $0.21173219572706703$, and absorbed only $0.0009086031889966327$ through turnover.  Sparse protein-feature contacts gave larger local feature absorptions in some cases, for example zebrafish PTM density at $0.014211240113472575$ and human PTM density at $0.014884825920540636$, but the zebrafish feature join used only $2242$ rows and shifted $d_{\mathrm{modeled}}$ by $0.1384608720516286$, preventing a stable cross-organism feature interpretation from that contact alone.

\paragraph{Local verdict.}
The finite-row evidence supports a narrow cross-layer statement: the $B^{\ast}_{Q6}$ residual basis can be tested against ribosome-profiling translation efficiency only after amino-acid composition, organism-specific sequence covariates, GC3, length, $M$-density, and matched mRNA abundance have been included in the conditioning boundary.  In this run the measured-TE absorption increments are positive but small, ranging from $0.0002762783754375593$ in yeast to $0.01759429001506974$ in zebrafish.  Positive controlled entries license only the existence of a finite descriptive association on the joined readout surface.  They do not establish a causal mediator, a ribosome-dynamics mechanism, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, molecular function, phenotype, or universality.  Any stronger interpretation requires a separate contact at that layer, with its own rows, controls, and rank checks.
