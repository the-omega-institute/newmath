\subsection{Decay-factor RBP occupancy as a bounded RNA half-life bridge}
\label{subsec:decay-factor-rbp-rna-half-life-with-translation-boundary-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite row object.}
The record concerns a finite matched panel of synonymously editable coding-RNA windows.  Each row carries a synonymous coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, measured local occupancy of one prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, target-RBP perturbation status, wild-type rescue status, a binding-defective or non-target control state, transcription-rate control, mRNA abundance control, isoform annotation, motif-density and local-structure covariates, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, transcript abundance, batch, and a declared failure condition.  The intended comparison is not a free comparison of codons or transcripts.  It is a matched finite contrast between high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels after amino-acid composition control, crossed with high versus low measured local decay-factor RBP occupancy and with the perturbation/rescue states needed to separate an occupancy-mediated RNA-stability bridge from an ordinary expression association.

\paragraph{Admissible statistic.}
For a finite set of windows $W$, the licensed statistic is a row-wise contrast in RNA half-life that remains attached to its translation boundary and to its RBP occupancy measurement.  A schematic form is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}}
&= \operatorname{median}_{w \in W_{\mathrm{high}}} \log T_{1/2}(w)
 - \operatorname{median}_{w \in W_{\mathrm{low}}} \log T_{1/2}(w),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{occ}}
&= \operatorname{median}_{w \in W_{\mathrm{high}}} O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(w)
 - \operatorname{median}_{w \in W_{\mathrm{low}}} O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(w),\\
B_{\mathrm{RBP}}
&= \{\Delta_{\mathrm{HL}},\Delta_{\mathrm{occ}},\mathrm{translation}(w),\mathrm{transcription}(w),
\mathrm{mRNA}(w),\mathrm{structure}(w),\mathrm{motif}(w),\mathrm{isoform}(w),\mathrm{batch}(w)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $T_{1/2}(w)$ is the RNA half-life readout and $O_{\mathrm{RBP}}(w)$ is the measured local occupancy of the selected decay-factor RBP.  The statistic is interpreted only when the high-coordinate and low-coordinate rows are not exchangeable for RNA half-life after transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and mRNA abundance have been accounted for.  The same-row translation measurement is mandatory, but it is a boundary variable: it prevents promotion of an RNA-stability observation into a translation claim.

\paragraph{Perturbation rule.}
A positive bridge requires a specific finite pattern.  The RNA half-life contrast must track measured local occupancy of the selected decay-factor RBP under coordinate--occupancy decoupling.  The contrast must collapse under target-RBP loss, return under wild-type rescue, and fail to return under binding-defective rescue or a non-target control.  If the half-life contrast remains but occupancy does not change, the row set supports only a finite RNA-stability association.  If the binding-defective rescue, non-target control, local RNA structure, motif density, transcription rate, isoform state, transcript abundance, or batch reproduces the same pattern, the RBP bridge is refused.  This makes the object narrower than a general codon-usage or transcript-stability statement: the bridge is a finite condition on matched rows, not a sequence-wide biological law.

\paragraph{Existing RNA-stability contacts.}
The supplied finite-row evidence already shows that RNA half-life boundaries can be measured in external datasets, but those contacts do not by themselves supply the decay-factor RBP bridge.  In the human m6A half-life boundary run $7\mathrm{cd}189\mathrm{f}15\mathrm{c}4\mathrm{b}$, $5837$ genes passed the joined coverage rule $\min(\mathrm{Mean\ T\ coverage\ DMSO},\mathrm{STM2457})$ at or above the twentieth percentile among valid joined genes.  The static Spearman association between m6A load and DMSO log half-life was $-0.388929$; the perturbation-oriented Spearman value for m6A load against $\Delta \log_2$ half-life was $0.245804$; the length-partial Spearman value was $0.227683$; and the two-sided partial permutation value was $0.000999001$.  In the cross-organism human m6A contact $\mathrm{d}7949\mathrm{df}4\mathrm{a}751$, the finite surface contained $135300$ sites over $15845$ genes, used $1000$ permutations, and reported $3$ sign-concordant components.  These values justify treating RNA half-life as an experimentally reachable layer, not as proof of a decay-factor RBP mechanism.

\paragraph{Neighboring transcript-stability surfaces.}
Other supplied rows sharpen the boundary.  The NMD position-rule run $\mathrm{c}362\mathrm{df}6\mathrm{daa}98$ kept $38576$ rows and used $1000$ permutations.  The synthetic ARE deletion run $\mathrm{a}41440\mathrm{ab}2792$ used $226$ BEAS2B rows, $2242$ Jurkat rows, $1000$ actual permutations, and a $2000$-permutation grading surface; the sign was concordant under a corrected pooled label permutation over deletions.  The endogenous $3'$ UTR transfer run $\mathrm{e}96\mathrm{bf}6\mathrm{e}626\mathrm{b}$ used $14933$ genes and $2000$ permutations, with baseline $R^2=0.0657771$, test $R^2=0.0827561$, $\Delta R^2=0.016979$, ARE coefficient $-0.0607953$, and permutation value $0.00049975$.  These contacts demonstrate finite RNA-stability readouts at several transcript-layer interfaces.  They do not contain the same-window measured decay-factor RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, binding-defective rescue, and same-row synonymous coordinate decoupling required here.

\paragraph{Local refusal conditions.}
The chapter therefore licenses only a conditional cross-layer relation.  RNA half-life cannot be read as translation realization, protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, function, phenotype, adaptation, mechanism, or universality unless an additional layer-matched contact supplies that information.  The translation readout in the row is a guard against over-reading, not a permission to infer protein output.  The mRNA abundance and transcription controls are separation variables, not substitutes for RBP occupancy.  A successful finite panel would show that the coordinate-associated half-life effect follows the measured decay-factor RBP occupancy and the target-RBP perturbation/rescue logic while remaining separated from composition, local structure, motif density, isoform state, transcript abundance, codon-pair context, and batch.  Without that complete finite pattern, the correct conclusion is a bounded RNA-stability association or an RNA half-life boundary, not an RBP-mediated bridge.
