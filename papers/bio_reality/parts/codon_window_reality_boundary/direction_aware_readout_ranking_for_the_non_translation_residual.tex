\subsection{Direction-aware readout ranking for the $B^*_{Q6}$ non-translation residual}
\label{subsec:q6-h-ranking-table-direction-aware-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite object under review.}
This chapter records a finite direction-aware ranking experiment for the $B^*_{Q6}$ modeled-translation-complement abundance residual.  For each organism $o$ and each available readout $H$, the row contains an absorbed-energy scalar $A_H$, a coordinate-support direction cosine $C_H$, a raw directional product $A_H C_H$, a Bonferroni-calibrated null comparison, and the penalized score used for ranking.  The construction is deliberately finite: it ranks the readouts that are available on matched rows and leaves missing readouts absent rather than treating them as zero or negative evidence.  The biological layer assigned to the packet is a cross-layer relation, not a mechanistic claim.

\paragraph{Scoring rule.}
The ranking uses the nine-coordinate $B^*_{Q6}$ residual support vector $a_\perp$ and the readout-projected support vector $a_H$.  The direction filter is
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_H &= \frac{\langle a_H,a_\perp\rangle}{\lVert a_H\rVert\,\lVert a_\perp\rVert},\\
\operatorname{raw}(H) &= A_H C_H,\\
\operatorname{Score}(H) &= A_H C_H - \lambda\,DL(H),\\
\lambda &= 0.01,\qquad DL(H)=\log_2(1+n_{\mathrm{columns}}).
\end{aligned}
$$
For one-column readouts $DL(H)=1$, while the twelve-column localization readout has $DL(H)=\log_2(13)=3.700439718141092$.  The score is therefore a stated regularized ranking statistic.  It is not a likelihood, a causal effect, or a biological mechanism.

\paragraph{Residual coordinate ledger.}
The residual ledger uses the same nine coordinates wherever a complement could be computed: $K_{AAA}$, $Arg_{AGR}$, $Ile_{AUA}$, $Leu_{CUN\,vs\,UUR}$, $Leu_{UUA\,vs\,UUG}$, $Ser_{UCR\,vs\,AGY}$, $Ser_{UCA\,vs\,UCG}$, $Thr_{ACR\,vs\,ACY}$, and $f3_{stress}$.  In the base complement computation, the modeled-translation-complement fraction $d$ was $0.874628721049266$ for danio, $0.6629960038578534$ for $E.$ coli K-12 MG1655, $0.9400341806302775$ for gallus, $0.9208949355679455$ for human, $0.8972672018746484$ for $M.$ smegmatis, and $0.21173219572706714$ for yeast.  These numbers say how much of the $B^*_{Q6}$ abundance component remains after the modeled translation readout is removed; they do not identify a downstream biological pathway.

\paragraph{Null calibration and row counts.}
The ranking table reports Bonferroni-calibrated dof-matched null comparisons separately from score.  Danio measured TE, mRNA, measured tRNA-copy tAI, and PTM density exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, while danio localization, complex membership, domain count, and transmembrane count do not.  Human complex membership, localization, measured TE, mRNA, PTM density, and transmembrane count exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, whereas turnover, measured tRNA-copy tAI, and domain count do not.  Yeast mRNA and localization exceed their Bonferroni thresholds, while yeast measured TE, measured tRNA-copy tAI, turnover, complex membership, PTM density, transmembrane count, and domain count do not.  These comparisons are not overwritten by rank or by score: a row can exceed the null and still be directionally weak, penalized, or organism-specific.

The row counts also delimit the evidence.  The base ranking complement used $14410$ danio proteins, $19053$ human proteins, $5815$ yeast proteins, $3739$ $E.$ coli proteins, and $12418$ gallus proteins.  Individual readouts often use smaller joins: danio localization used $1984$ joined proteins, danio feature rows used $2242$, human turnover used $8057$, yeast turnover used $3640$, and gallus feature rows used $1906$.  For yeast turnover, the join hit rate reported for the protein half-life source was $0.8818572656921754$, with $3640$ turnover proteins and $2175$ CDS records skipped for lack of a turnover match.  For human turnover, $8057$ rows were joined from a reported $8164$ half-life proteins, but the absorbed fraction was only $3.5740764900116538\times 10^{-6}$ and did not exceed its dof-matched null, whose $95$th percentile was $0.0004342285066808464$.

\paragraph{Cross-organism outcome.}
No universal readout $H$ is supported by the finite panel.  The best absorber is organism-specific: measured TE for danio, complex membership for human and gallus, mRNA for yeast, and no positive selected row for $E.$ coli under the penalized table.  Several organisms in the complement ledger have no ranking rows at all in this packet: $B.$ subtilis, $C.$ elegans, $D.$ melanogaster, mouse, $M.$ smegmatis, rat, and $S.$ solfataricus have empty $H$ panels.  The absence of a shared readout is an informative negative finite result, not a failed mechanism discovery.

This conclusion agrees with the broader cross-organism summaries.  The measured ribo-seq mediation fractions were $0$ for danio, $0.04091158340950673$ for $E.$ coli, $0.0027966485036786744$ for human, and $0.4793069656345167$ for yeast.  The minimal measured-readout compression test reported residual fractions $0.9756319042010854$ for danio, $0.9942823785118229$ for $E.$ coli, $0.9736442326306781$ for human, and $0.8676586345294415$ for yeast, with median compressibility reported as $0.026$.  The direction-aware ordered table likewise emitted no positive $H$ readout in its final dictionary rows for yeast, $E.$ coli, human, or danio after its ordered and penalized procedure, leaving unexplained fractions $0.8676586345294415$, $0.9942823785118229$, $0.9798666209022441$, and $0.9756319042010854$ respectively.

\paragraph{Independent reality contacts and negative controls.}
The same dataset family contains one strong finite abundance signature that should not be confused with an $H$ mechanism.  The Window6 boundary $R$ set was avoided in all $15$ computed organisms, with $15$ avoided, $0$ preferred, $0$ zero, majority fraction $1.0$, one-sided binomial $p=3.0517578125\times 10^{-5}$, and two-sided binomial $p=6.103515625\times 10^{-5}$.  Six organisms were individually significant at the stated threshold: human, danio, gallus, $P.$ aeruginosa PAO1, mouse, and rat.  This is an abundance-preference association for a fixed codon-window set, not a proof of translation, folding, function, or mechanism.

The optimal-codon conservation summary is also descriptive: it computed $15$ organisms out of $16$ requested, used $41$ synonymous design columns, and reported mean pairwise cosine $0.2629282074265475$ with the call clustered.  The organism-specificity meta-table had $12$ complete-feature organisms and $16$ total rows, with a dominant-coordinate-by-domain permutation $p=0.5814185814185814$ and Cramer's $V=0.6258327785172862$.  Its continuous tests were likewise weak: $M_{QTP}$ fraction versus GC3 had $r=-0.3062616500469447$ and two-sided permutation $p=0.35264735264735264$, versus log protein count had $r=-0.033628115789504955$ and $p=0.913086913086913$, and versus log tRNA gene count had $r=0.06029405206037823$ and $p=0.8621378621378621$.

\paragraph{Local interpretation boundary.}
The admissible statement is narrow.  The finite table says that, for the available matched organism-readout rows, the non-translation abundance residual has only weak direction-aware absorption by the tested readouts, and the leading readout is not shared across organisms.  $A_H$ is an absorbed projection fraction; $C_H$ is a coordinate-support cosine; $A_HC_H$ is a directional projection product; and $\operatorname{Score}$ is a regularized descriptive ranking statistic with $\lambda=0.01$.  None of these quantities establishes translation realization, ribosome dynamics, folding, physical admissibility, protein function, phenotype, regulatory mechanism, pathway membership, or a universal biological law.  Any such stronger conclusion would require a separate same-row biological contact with its own statistic, not merely a high rank, a positive score, a Bonferroni flag, a contact name, or a residual-coordinate geometry.
