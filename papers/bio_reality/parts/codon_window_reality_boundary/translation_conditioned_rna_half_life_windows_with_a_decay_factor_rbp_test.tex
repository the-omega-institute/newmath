\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life windows with a decay-factor RBP test}
\label{subsec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite rows.}
The record is a finite matched-panel conjecture about coding-transcript windows labelled by the synonymous residual coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ after quotienting by amino-acid identity.  A row is admissible only when the same transcript or coding sequence carries the coordinate label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy for a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein, and controls that keep transcription rate, transcript abundance, splice or isoform state, GC and dinucleotide content, codon-pair context, local RNA structure, motif density, batch, and amino-acid composition attached to the same comparison.  The high-coordinate and low-coordinate labels are therefore not being compared as sequence symbols alone; they are compared only after the translation readout has been made part of the conditioning boundary.

\paragraph{Statistic.}
For a matched row $i$, write $q_i \in \{0,1\}$ for the residual-coordinate bin, $t_i$ for the translation readout, $h_i$ for the RNA half-life readout, $o_i$ for measured local occupancy of the target decay-factor RBP, and $c_i$ for the finite vector of transcription, abundance, isoform, structure, composition, codon-context, amino-acid, and batch controls.  The admissible contrast has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6} &= \widehat{E}(h_i \mid q_i=1,t_i,o_i,c_i)-\widehat{E}(h_i \mid q_i=0,t_i,o_i,c_i),\\
\Delta_{loss} &= \Delta_{Q6}\text{ after target-RBP loss},\\
\Delta_{WT} &= \Delta_{Q6}\text{ after wild-type target-RBP rescue},\\
\Delta_{def} &= \Delta_{Q6}\text{ after binding-defective or non-target rescue}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A positive cross-layer RBP reading requires a nonzero translation-conditioned half-life contrast, a matched local occupancy shift in the editable RNA window, collapse under loss of the target RBP, return under wild-type rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue.  If any of these finite tests is absent, the same rows may still describe an RNA-stability association, but they do not support the RBP-decay reading.

\paragraph{Reality anchors.}
The surrounding BioReality ledger already contains finite precedents showing why the present packet must demand layer-matched contacts rather than promote a coordinate association by itself.  The RBP-binding comparison between RBNS affinity and ENCODE eCLIP binding reports $900$ total peaks and $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases, which is enough to show a measurable RBP-binding surface but not enough to identify a decay mechanism in this RNA half-life packet.  The translation contact based on the mTOR readout uses $n=6494$ rows, giving a finite translation layer against which same-row conditioning can be required.  RNA-modification examples show how strong a layer-specific surface can be without licensing a different layer: the m6Am Crown-seq record has $n=31886$, $19406$ distinct sites, $3230$ low-tail rows with tail value below $0.8$, $20942$ rows in the variable subset, a saturated fraction $0.343223$ at threshold $0.95$, and a GC-matched null probability $p=0.000833$ with partial correlations $0.268094$, $0.098666$, and $0.168335$ in its three reported subsets.  Likewise, the m6A exon-architecture record has $n=60000$, $49668$ distinct rows, cross-condition concordance true, and coverage-confound correlation $-0.135922814$.  These numbers are cited here as finite-row discipline: they show that a strong local molecular readout remains local to its measured layer unless the requested bridge rows are actually present.

\paragraph{Negative controls.}
The null model exchanges high- and low-coordinate labels after conditioning on the same-row translation readout and the declared controls.  A half-life difference that survives this exchange test is still insufficient for the stated RBP reading unless it also tracks measured target-RBP occupancy and passes the loss-and-rescue sequence.  RNA-structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC or dinucleotide, codon-pair, and batch explanations are not auxiliary inconveniences; they are local alternatives that must remain live until the finite matched rows remove them.  The poly(A)-tail example, with $8854$ human rows, $3990$ yeast rows, and cross-species concordance reported as false, is a useful warning that a grammar-like RNA signal need not transport across biological context.  The transcription-elongation record, with $1176$ rows and $1137$ distinct entries, gives a separate transcription-layer surface rather than an RNA-decay mechanism.  The DNA-methylation flanking-context record has $20000$ probes across $656$ individuals with baseline $R^2=0.0129735$, again illustrating that a finite association can be real while remaining bounded to its measured layer.

\paragraph{Readback condition.}
The chapter licenses only the following finite readback: in a matched transcript panel, the $B^{*}_{Q6}$ residual label may be related to a translation-conditioned RNA half-life residual through a prespecified decay-factor RBP if and only if the local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target control pattern are jointly observed in the same comparison frame.  Additional finite surfaces in the ledger reinforce the separation rule.  N-terminal acetylation has $657$ rows, $426$ NatA rows, and AUROC $0.942081$ over $622$ high-low cases; lysine acetylation has $6288$ rows and $5921$ distinct stoichiometry values; pseudouridylation has $2040$ rows; DNA cyclizability records runtime only in this packet; and the PRDM9 hotspot record lists $52963$ hotspots.  None of these scalar contacts supplies the missing target-RBP perturbation panel for this conjecture.  They are included only to mark the project-wide standard that finite molecular measurements remain tied to their own observable surface.

\paragraph{Local boundary.}
No translation realization follows from RNA half-life alone, and no RNA half-life contrast becomes an RBP-decay bridge merely because it aligns with a synonymous residual coordinate.  No conclusion about protein abundance, folding, physical admissibility, biochemical function, phenotype, adaptation, causation, mechanism in general, or universality is licensed here.  Such conclusions would require separate layer-matched contacts with their own finite rows and controls.  The present chapter is therefore a bounded cross-layer conjecture: it is ready as a precise finite-row target, while its positive biological verdict depends on obtaining same-window RBP occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, and binding-defective or non-target rescue measurements joined to the same translation-conditioned RNA half-life panel.
