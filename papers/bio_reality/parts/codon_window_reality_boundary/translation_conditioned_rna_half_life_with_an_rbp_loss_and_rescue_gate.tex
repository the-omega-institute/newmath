\subsection{Translation-conditioned RNA half-life with an RBP loss-and-rescue gate}
\label{subsec:q6-translation-conditioned-rna-half-life-rbp-bridge-cross-layer}

\paragraph{Finite carrier.}
This record concerns finite matched transcript or CDS rows in which a synonymous residual coordinate label is attached after quotienting by amino-acid identity. Each row must carry a high-coordinate or low-coordinate $B^{*}_{Q6}$ label, a same-row translation readout, an RNA half-life readout, local occupancy of a prespecified decay-factor RNA-binding protein at the editable RNA window, and matched controls for transcription rate, transcript abundance, splice or isoform state, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, amino-acid composition, and batch. The biological question is not whether synonymous geometry alone predicts decay. The question is narrower: after the translation readout has been joined and conditioned in the same row, can a residual-coordinate half-life contrast be read as a bounded cross-layer RBP bridge?

\paragraph{Statistic to be read.}
For a finite panel $P$, the admissible statistic separates the coordinate contrast from the mechanistic gate. A schematic form is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}\mid\mathrm{TE}}
  &= \widehat{\mathrm{HL}}(B^{*}_{Q6}=\mathrm{high}\mid \mathrm{TE}, C)
   - \widehat{\mathrm{HL}}(B^{*}_{Q6}=\mathrm{low}\mid \mathrm{TE}, C), \\
G_{\mathrm{RBP}}
  &= \bigl(\mathrm{occupancy\ shift},\ \mathrm{target\ loss\ collapse},\ \mathrm{wildtype\ rescue},\ \mathrm{defective\ rescue\ failure}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $C$ denotes the declared finite controls. The coordinate may be promoted from a bounded RNA-stability association to the proposed cross-layer relation only when $\Delta_{Q6}^{\mathrm{HL}\mid\mathrm{TE}}$ is accompanied by measured local RBP occupancy, disappearance of the effect after target-RBP loss, return under wild-type target-RBP rescue, and failure of binding-defective or non-target rescue. A half-life contrast without this pattern remains a finite association at the RNA-stability surface.

\paragraph{Reality contacts already present.}
The present evidence table supplies boundary contacts rather than the decisive perturbation panel. RNA half-life contacts are available as organism-scoped gene or transcript rows from repo-local parsed payloads for \textit{Escherichia coli}, \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, and \textit{Homo sapiens}. The translation side is represented by same-condition mRNA abundance controls and ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency contacts, with translation efficiency interpreted as $\mathrm{TE}=\mathrm{RPF}/\mathrm{mRNA}$ when the identifiers are matched. These contacts are sufficient to state the required row shape and to reject any inference from RNA half-life alone to translation realization. They are not sufficient to establish measured local occupancy, target-RBP loss, wild-type rescue, or binding-defective rescue behavior for the same editable window.

\paragraph{Calibration from neighboring finite checks.}
Several verified cross-layer runs show the scale of finite contact already tolerated by the surrounding BioReality ledger. The methylation flanking-context check used $20000$ probes from $656$ individuals and reported a baseline $R^{2}=0.0129735$, with no cross-cohort value recorded. The PRDM9 meiotic recombination contact carried $52963$ hotspots. The mTOR translation readout contained $6494$ rows from the GSE36892 source and ran in $9.469$ seconds. The polyadenylation contact carried $8854$ human rows and $3990$ yeast rows, with cross-species concordance recorded as false. The RBP-binding contact carried $900$ total peaks and recorded $3$ cross-RBP concordant cases. These numbers are not evidence for the present RBP-decay bridge; they delimit the finite-row style in which such a bridge would have to be supplied.

\paragraph{Null model.}
The local null states that, after amino-acid composition, transcription rate, local RNA structure, motif density, GC and dinucleotide composition, codon-pair context, splice or isoform state, transcript abundance, batch, and same-row translation readout are held fixed, high-coordinate and low-coordinate synonymous labels are exchangeable with respect to RNA half-life. A positive half-life difference breaks only this exchangeability. It does not by itself identify a decay-factor RBP route. If measured occupancy is unchanged, if target-RBP loss fails to collapse the effect, if wild-type rescue fails to restore it, or if binding-defective or non-target rescue restores it, the proposed RBP bridge is rejected for this finite panel.

\paragraph{Interpretive boundary.}
This chapter licenses no claim about protein abundance, protein output, folding, structural order, physical admissibility, biological function, phenotype, adaptation, general sequence law, or universal mechanism. It also licenses no translation realization from an RNA half-life table unless the same-row translation readout is attached and conditioned. RNA-structure-only, motif-density-only, transcription-rate, isoform-state, transcript-abundance, GC or dinucleotide, codon-pair, and batch explanations remain live alternatives unless removed by the declared matched controls and by the RBP loss-and-rescue gate. A separate layer-matched contact is required for any assertion beyond the bounded RNA half-life and measured RBP-occupancy surface.
