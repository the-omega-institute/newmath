\paragraph{Shared-coordinate test.}
The control asks a specific methodological question: after partitioning the clocks into shared and clock-specific CpGs, does restriction to the shared $60$-CpG coordinate set make the late clock sign split disappear as a coverage artifact? The answer in the verified row is no. Full Horvath2 coverage coexists with no late reversal under the skin-and-blood clock, while the pan-tissue Horvath353 readback carries the late reversal readback in the finite contrast. Because the shared subset is scored separately under each clock's own coefficient weights, the comparison is not a claim that the two clocks measure the same biological variable. It is only a check that the discrepancy survives a controlled coefficient-coverage decomposition.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The local verdict is passed for the age-signature layer: GSE165179, reached through GSE165180, supports a finite clock-dependence coverage control in which $|I|=60$, $|U_{H1}|=293$, $|U_{H2}|=331$, full Horvath2 coverage is present, and the coverage explanation for the late split is false. The result therefore rules out one narrow methodological explanation, namely that the observed clock disagreement is simply due to absent Horvath2 CpGs in the processed methylation matrix. It does not certify a clock-independent biological age state. The output remains a DNAm-clock method certificate over a small donor-paired MPTR contrast with three donor pairs and known donor-level heterogeneity, including one donor whose treated-minus-baseline DNAm-age delta is positive.
