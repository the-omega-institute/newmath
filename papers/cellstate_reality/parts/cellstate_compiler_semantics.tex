\section{Cell-State Compiler Semantics}
\label{sec:cellstate-compiler-semantics}

The Yamanaka-factor contacts in CellStateReality force a compiler reading of the BEDC cell-state object. A cell-state certificate is not a linear chain from a history to one final name; it is a typed compilation graph whose probes, phases, layers, joins, and gaps remain visible in the certificate.

The old linear form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Hist}\to\mathsf{Probe}\to\mathsf{Sig}\to\mathsf{NameCert}
\end{aligned}
$$
is too small for the MPTR and OSKMLN records. The evidence instead has the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Hist}\to\mathsf{Program}\to\mathsf{MultiProbeFrontend}\to\mathsf{LayeredIR}\\
\to
\mathsf{SameScopeJoin}\to\mathsf{PromotionFrontier}\to\mathsf{GapGeometry}\to\mathsf{NameCert}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The section records that upgrade as a semantic constraint induced by the existing empirical witnesses.

\subsection{The Yamanaka-Factor Program Object}

\begin{definition}[Yamanaka-factor program]
\label{def:yamanaka-factor-program}
A Yamanaka-factor program is the finite compilation object
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{YFProgram}=
(\mathsf{FactorSet},\mathsf{Carrier},\mathsf{Delivery},\mathsf{Dose},\mathsf{Schedule},
\mathsf{Withdrawal},\mathsf{ProbeSuite}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The factor set names the reprogramming factors; the carrier names the cell or tissue substrate; the delivery field records the generator by which the factors enter the carrier; the dose and schedule fields record exposure geometry; the withdrawal field records whether and when the generator is removed; and the probe suite records the frontends allowed to compile the program into readback signatures.
\end{definition}

Under this definition, OSKM, OSKMLN, and MPTR are not naked biological labels. They are programs with distinct compilation contracts. The Sarkar OSKMLN instance uses OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, c-MYC, LIN28, and NANOG, delivered by non-integrating mRNA for a four-day transient protocol over human fibroblast and endothelial carriers. The Gill MPTR instance is a lentiviral OSKM program with a methylation timecourse at days $10$, $13$, $15$, and $17$, and with GSE165177 RNA contacts available for day-10 donor-matched MPTR fibroblasts.

The program object matters because the same factor vocabulary does not determine the same certificate. A four-day non-integrating mRNA OSKMLN contact and a lentiviral MPTR timecourse may both be partial-reprogramming programs, but they compile through different carriers, schedules, probe suites, and phase boundaries. BEDC therefore has to name the program before it names a cell-state result.

\subsection{Probe Frontends and the Layered IR}

\begin{definition}[Probe frontend]
\label{def:probe-frontend}
A probe frontend is a typed compiler
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{ProbeFrontend}_P:\mathsf{Hist}\times\mathsf{YFProgram}\to \mathsf{IR}_P
\end{aligned}
$$
which transforms the same carrier history into a probe-indexed intermediate representation. The probe is not merely an observation window. It selects coordinates, coefficients, thresholds, normalization conventions, and the admissible layer in which the resulting signature can be read.
\end{definition}

The layered intermediate representation used here has five layer components. $S_A$ is the age-signature layer; $S_I$ is the cell-identity layer; $S_F$ is the function marker-direction layer; $S_Q$ is the pluripotency or safety-boundary layer; and $S_R$ is the recovery or post-withdrawal layer. A methylation clock is a frontend for a methylation carrier. An RNA marker panel is a transcriptome frontend. They may be applied to the same biological program, but they do not compile to the same type.

This is the compiler reason that one cannot read a methylation-clock pass as a cell-state pass. The methylation frontend can produce $S_A$ when it has beta values, clock coefficients, sample annotations, and pairing. The transcriptome frontend can produce $S_I$, $S_F$, or $S_Q$ when it has gene-expression rows, marker panels, thresholds, and donor pairing. The layered IR keeps those outputs separate until a same-scope join makes a cross-layer object.

\subsection{Phase as Type}

\begin{definition}[Phase type]
\label{def:phase-type}
The phase type of a cell-state program is drawn from
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{PhaseType}=\{
&\mathsf{baseline},\mathsf{transient\mbox{-}induction},\mathsf{late\mbox{-}transient},
\mathsf{withdrawal},\\
&\mathsf{post\mbox{-}withdrawal\mbox{-}recovered},\mathsf{stable\mbox{-}propagated}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A signature has type $S_L[\phi]$, where $L$ is its layer and $\phi$ is its phase. A transient-phase signature and a recovered-phase signature are different typed objects even when they refer to the same program family.
\end{definition}

The compiler analogy is useful but bounded. Transient induction behaves like speculative IR: it exposes aggressive state movement under an active generator. Withdrawal is the rollback or commit test: it asks what remains when the generator is removed. Recovered output is a stable-output candidate: it requires post-withdrawal evidence rather than a transient sample relabelled as mature.

This typing rule also blocks cross-organism substitution. A mouse recovery contact is not a human recovered-phase Horvath353 and RNA same-scope contact. Likewise, a human transient MPTR contact is not a human post-withdrawal recovered contact. Phase is part of the type, not metadata that can be erased after a favorable readback.

\subsection{Five Theorems Induced by the Reality Contacts}

\begin{proposition}[Probe-indexed signature]
\label{prop:probe-indexed-signature}
The expression $S(H)$ is ill-typed for a cell-state signature. The well-typed expression is $S(H,P)$, where $P$ is the probe frontend that compiled the carrier history.
\end{proposition}

The witness is \texttt{cellstate.age-clock-shift.clock-dependence.gse165180}, recorded as a passed clock-dependence certificate over the same GSE165179 MPTR donor-paired methylation carrier reached through GSE165180. The carrier is read through two clock frontends: pan-tissue Horvath1 and skin-and-blood Horvath2. The witness records opposite late-phase mean treated-minus-baseline directions for the two frontends: pan-tissue day $15$ and day $17$ are $+0.84$ and $+6.60$ years, while skin-and-blood day $15$ and day $17$ are $-3.26$ and $-4.18$ years. The linked coverage-control witness \texttt{cellstate.age-clock-shift.clock-dependence.coverage-control.gse165180} records full skin-and-blood coverage at $391/391$ CpGs and \texttt{reversal\_explained\_by\_coverage=false}. Thus the age signature is a property of the pair $(H,P)$, not of $H$ alone.

\begin{proposition}[Layer-local support does not promote]
\label{prop:layer-local-support-non-promotion}
Age-layer support does not imply joint support for identity, function, and safety:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Support}(S_A)\not\Rightarrow
\mathrm{Support}(S_I\wedge S_F\wedge S_Q).
\end{aligned}
$$
\end{proposition}

The age-side witness is \texttt{cellstate.age-clock-shift.gse142439}, recorded as passed: GSE142439 supplies $16$ EPIC methylation samples arranged as $8$ normal/treated pairs, with $334$ covered Horvath353 CpGs out of $353$ and a mean paired DNAm-age shift below zero. The identity-side witness \texttt{cellstate.identity-samesig.gse165177-mptr} is failed because the transient day-10 MPTR RNA contact crosses the pluripotency boundary; the donor-resolved decomposition records three donor-matched pairs and a failed support value for the sameSig$_I$ break. The function-side witness \texttt{cellstate.function-signature-shift.gse165177-mptr-human} remains \texttt{needs\_data}: the registry records $\Delta\mathrm{Sen}=0.268456$ after LMNB1 reverse correction and $\Delta\mathrm{SASP}=-0.707862$ over $n=3$ donor-matched pairs, so both required panel directions are not negative. Therefore the age layer may be supported while identity, function, and safety remain blocked or open.

\begin{proposition}[Same-scope join]
\label{prop:same-scope-join}
Every cross-layer cell-state claim must compile through a same-scope join certificate:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{CrossLayerClaim}\Rightarrow \mathsf{SameScopeJoinCert}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The implication is not a biological entailment from one layer to another; it is a typed join obligation over auditable keys and non-imputed modalities.
\end{proposition}

The witness is \texttt{cellstate.promotion-frontier.gse165180}, recorded as negative. Its carrier joins GSE165179 methylation and GSE165177 RNA by exact donor, day, condition, and experiment, with no imputed modality. It classifies each scope into no-age-gain, clock-only, identity-or-safety-break, or identity-safe age-reset candidate cases and reports no Case $3$ candidate. The companion \texttt{cellstate.identity-preserving-age-reset.samescope.gse165180} is also failed: its support condition requires O1/O2/O3 to join across age and identity contacts, every joined methylation treated-minus-baseline DNAm-age delta to be negative, identity RMS to stay within threshold, and every pluripotency exclusion condition to hold. The typed join exposes the failure rather than allowing age-layer evidence to imply an identity-preserving age reset.

\begin{proposition}[Phase-type separation]
\label{prop:phase-type-separation}
Transient-phase IR is not isomorphic to recovered-phase IR:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{TransientPhase}\not\simeq\mathsf{RecoveredPhase}.
\end{aligned}
$$
\end{proposition}

The witness is the maturation packet \texttt{identity-safe-age-reset.maturation-needs-data}. It records that recovered-phase Case $3$ remains open because the scan did not locate an accession with explicit post-withdrawal or recovered samples, human EPIC or 450k methylation values usable with Horvath353, and processed RNA marker expression for the same recovered-phase scope. Its cannot-claim boundary is phase-typed: transient-phase MPTR samples are not recovered-phase evidence, mouse recovery samples are not human Horvath353 EPIC or 450k evidence, and a literature summary without processed recovered-phase methylation and RNA marker values is not Case $3$ support.

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate fragility]
\label{prop:coordinate-fragility}
A verdict over a signature may be top-$K$ coordinate fragile:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Verdict}(S)\ \text{may depend on a small high-weight coordinate subset.}
\end{aligned}
$$
\end{proposition}

The witnesses are the passed records \texttt{cellstate.age-clock-shift.reversal-decomposition.gse165180}, \texttt{cellstate.age-clock-shift.reversal-leave-top20-out.gse165180}, and \texttt{cellstate.age-clock-shift.clock-dependence.coverage-control.gse165180}. The decomposition record reports that the day-17 Horvath353 reversal is carried by the top $20$ day-17-versus-day-10 CpGs, which account for $56.06\%$ of the positive z-space displacement; their mean absolute Horvath weight is $0.475877$, about $3.65$ times the covered-coordinate median absolute weight $0.130244$. The leave-top-20-out record removes those $20$ coordinates and rescoring leaves $314$ covered CpGs; day-17 $z$ changes from $0.314273$ to $-0.616288$, so the reversal disappears. The coverage-control record rules out a simple missing-CpG explanation by recording full skin-and-blood coverage at $391/391$ CpGs and \texttt{reversal\_explained\_by\_coverage=false}. Thus the gap geometry is coordinate-local and frontend-sensitive rather than a global state verdict.

\subsection{The Compiler Atlas}

The compiler atlas has seven owners. $\mathsf{ProgramSpec}$ owns factor set, delivery, dose, schedule, withdrawal, and probe suite. $\mathsf{CarrierSpec}$ owns the finite biological substrate and row provenance. $\mathsf{ProbeFrontend}$ owns the transformation from carrier rows to probe-indexed IR. $\mathsf{LayeredIR}$ owns $S_A$, $S_I$, $S_F$, $S_Q$, and $S_R$ as separate typed components. $\mathsf{SameScopeJoin}$ owns donor, day, condition, experiment, phase, and modality keys. $\mathsf{PromotionFrontier}$ owns the finite classifier that decides whether a joined scope is no-age-gain, clock-only, broken, or a candidate. $\mathsf{GapGeometry}$ owns the residual obstacles: missing recovered phase, coordinate fragility, frontend dependence, and non-promoted layers.

These owners make CellStateReality a scientific-object compiler rather than a prose ledger. Each owner has a bounded contract, and the output NameCert must preserve which owner supplied each part of the claim. When a witness fails or remains \texttt{needs\_data}, the compiler does not erase the object. It emits a typed gap: phase gap, join gap, layer gap, or coordinate gap.

The Yamanaka-factor program is therefore a pressure test for BEDC. Real cell-state science is not a single-probe linear certificate. It is a multi-frontend, multi-layer, multi-phase, multi-join compilation graph, and the certificate is credible only when that graph remains auditable.
