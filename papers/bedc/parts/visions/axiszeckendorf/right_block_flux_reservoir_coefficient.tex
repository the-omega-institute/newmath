\begin{principle}[Right-block flux-reservoir coefficient]
\label{prin:zeckendorf-right-block-flux-reservoir-coefficient}
The phase-reservoir coefficient also has an edge-flux normalization
inside Window six. Let \(U_R\) be the right edge-flux block in the
Window-six partition of the binary cube. The edge-flux skeleton supplies
$$
  |U_R|=6,
  \qquad
  |\partial U_R|=32.
$$
Since \(Q_6\) is \(6\)-regular, the normalized probability of leaving
the right block is
$$
  P(\mathrm{leave}\ U_R\mid U_R)
  =
  \frac{|\partial U_R|}{6|U_R|}
  =
  \frac{32}{36}
  =
  \frac89.
$$
Thus the same coefficient \(8/9\) is not only the capacity ratio
\(|C_\partial|/N_{\geq4}(6)\) and the light-to-heavy fiber ratio; it is
also the canonical boundary escape flux of the right edge-flux block.
The fold-gauge mismatch mean gives the one-way companion value
$$
  E[g_t]=\frac49,
  \qquad
  2E[g_t]=\frac89,
$$
so the reservoir coefficient may also be read as the two-sided boundary
flux associated with the one-way mismatch rate. This differs from the
global cut-flux density
$$
  \Phi_R=\frac{|\partial U_R|}{64\cdot6}=\frac1{12},
$$
which is normalized by the full directed-edge budget of the cube and is
not a local residual coefficient in \(D_{6,\alpha}^{\star}\).
This is the coefficient relevant to impedance or coupling readouts. By
\autoref{thm:zeckendorf-static-right-boundary-source-green-zero}, the
same static right-boundary cut-flux source is a Dirichlet-exact
cut-source and has zero $571$-Green class, so the infrared impedance
selection does not require a separate $\varphi^{-37}$ correction inside
the static flux-reservoir layer. The dynamic Green-relaxation layer is a
different readout mode, governed by a centered relaxation source rather
than by the static cut vector. The $2/3$ branch records the direct
six-element phase image and the $4/9$ branch records the
curvature-residual quotient.
\end{principle}
