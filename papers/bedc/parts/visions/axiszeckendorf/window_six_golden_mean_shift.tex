\begin{definition}[Golden-mean shift kernel]
\label{def:axiszeckendorf-golden-mean-shift-kernel}
The finite-type transition kernel of $X_\infty^Z$ is the two-state matrix
$$
K_Z
:=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
where the entry $K_Z(a,b)$ is admissible exactly when the adjacent pair
$ab$ is not $11$.  The left shift
$$
\sigma:X_\infty^Z\to X_\infty^Z,
\qquad
\sigma(c_1,c_2,c_3,\ldots)=(c_2,c_3,\ldots)
$$
therefore makes $X_\infty^Z$ the one-sided golden-mean subshift.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Golden growth of finite windows]
\label{prop:axiszeckendorf-golden-growth-finite-windows}
The finite windows satisfy
$$
|X_m|=F_{m+2},
$$
and the transition kernel of
\autoref{def:axiszeckendorf-golden-mean-shift-kernel} has characteristic
equation
$$
\lambda^2=\lambda+1.
$$
Thus the growth scale of the inverse-limit carrier is the golden scale
$\varphi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The no-adjacent grammar splits each nonempty word by its first symbols:
words beginning with $0$ contribute $X_{m-1}$, while words beginning with
$1$ must begin with $10$ and contribute $X_{m-2}$.  Hence
$$
|X_m|=|X_{m-1}|+|X_{m-2}|.
$$
With the initial values $|X_0|=1$ and $|X_1|=2$, this gives
$|X_m|=F_{m+2}$.  The displayed matrix has characteristic polynomial
$\lambda^2-\lambda-1$, whose positive root is $\varphi$.
\end{proof}
