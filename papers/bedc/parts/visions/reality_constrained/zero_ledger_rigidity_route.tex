\section{Zero-ledger rigidity route}
\label{sec:zero-ledger-rigidity-route}

The closure route of
\autoref{prin:closure-plus-exclusion-rh-route} separates location from
exclusion.  Full-constraint closure places a legal zeta packet inside the
self-similar tower.  The fixed-half conclusion still requires a certificate
that the tower-internal zero ledger cannot coexist with an off-fixed radial
defect.

\begin{principle}[Finite induction and global coinduction]
\label{prin:finite-induction-global-coinduction}
Finite readout layers may be closed by ordinary induction over displayed
depths, windows, or recursive constructors.  The full self-similar tower is
not used as an ordinary well-founded chain with an absolute bottom.  It is
a closed compatibility carrier whose projections and residual fibers can
restart the same tower structure.  Global statements about the tower are
therefore invariant statements: one proves that the relevant row is
preserved by the boundary readouts, fiber readbacks, compatibility maps,
and the self-similar operation.  In the zeta direction, the invariant of
interest is fixed-half zero-ledger rigidity.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Self-similar zeta coordinate row]
\label{def:self-similar-zeta-coordinate-row}
Let $\mathcal D$ be a self-similar diagram with zeta projection
$$
\pi_\zeta:T_{\mathcal D}\to R_\zeta.
$$
A self-similar zeta coordinate row assigns to each tower point
$x\in T_{\mathcal D}$ an information-channel readout
$$
s_x=\pi_\zeta(x)=\frac{1}{2}+\delta_x+i\tau_x,
$$
together with the transport rows connecting $\mathsf{ZetaZero}(s_x)$,
$\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)$, and
$\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)$ to the RH normal forms of
\autoref{thm:rh-information-channel-normal-form}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Zero-ledger atom]
\label{def:zero-ledger-atom}
For a tower point $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$ with zeta coordinate $s_x$, a
zero-ledger atom is the packet row
$$
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x)
:=
\mathsf{ZeroLedger}(\pi_\zeta(x)).
$$
Equivalently, it is a zeta-value evaluation packet
$$
(s_x,z_x,\mathsf{ZetaVal}(s_x,z_x),z_x\hsame 0)
$$
together with the zero classifier, source ledger, transport rows, and
audit rows that make the zero event public.  Thus a zero atom is not a
bare complex point.  The phrase that a zeta zero is generated by the
self-similar operation means generation at the zeta-zero ledger layer:
there is a tower point whose zeta projection is admitted by the zero
ledger.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Zeta zero-ledger payload]
\label{def:zeta-zero-ledger-payload}
The zeta zero-ledger payload of a tower point $x$ is the displayed packet
of rows
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{ZetaZeroPayload}_{\mathcal D}(x)
:=
(&s_x,\ z_x,\ \mathsf{ZetaVal}(s_x,z_x),\
z_x\hsame0,\
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x),\\
&\mathsf{CompletedZetaCarrier}(\pi_\zeta(x)),\
\mathsf{ZeroLedger}(\pi_\zeta(x)),\
\mathsf{Ledger}_{\zeta}(x)).
\end{aligned}
$$
The payload records the point coordinate only as one field of a larger
carrier.  Its public status depends on evaluation, classifier, completed
carrier, ledger, and audit rows.  Therefore the zero-ledger route never
treats $\mathsf{ZetaZero}$ as a subset of bare complex points.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Coinductive zero-ledger invariant]
\label{def:coinductive-zero-ledger-invariant}
A coinductive zero-ledger invariant for a self-similar diagram
$\mathcal D$ is a predicate $\mathcal R$ on $T_{\mathcal D}$ such that
for every tower point $x$:
$$
\mathcal R(x)
$$
is stable under the zeta boundary readout, the residual fiber readback,
the compatibility maps of the diagram, and the operation
$\Omega_{\mathcal D}$.  The invariant is RH-facing when it contains the
zero-rigidity implication
$$
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)
\Longrightarrow
\delta_x=0.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Atomization boundary for zeta zeros]
\label{prin:zeta-zero-atomization-boundary}
Atomization at the zero-ledger layer means minimality with respect to the
displayed zero classifier: the packet is a basic auditable zero event in
the ledger.  It does not assert that the analytic zero is simple, and it
does not provide a finite listing of all non-trivial zeros.  A fixed zeta
value can be approximated through its evaluation rows, while exact
zero-ledger admission is a constructive packet obligation that is not
generally decided by finite approximation alone.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Non-trivial zero atom and counterpacket]
\label{def:nontrivial-zero-atom-counterpacket}
The non-trivial zero atoms of the self-similar zeta coordinate are
$$
\mathsf{NTZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x)
:=
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x).
$$
The corresponding tower-internal RH counterpacket type is
$$
\mathsf{CounterAtom}_{\mathsf{RH},\mathcal D}
:=
\sum_{x:T_{\mathcal D}}
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x)
\times
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)
\times
\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta_x).
$$
This is the constructive disproof shape of \autoref{prop:rh-constructive-form}
read through the self-similar tower: a zero-ledger atom in the information
band, together with a positive off-fixed witness.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Zero-ledger fiber geometry]
\label{def:zero-ledger-fiber-geometry}
The zero geometry carried by the self-similar zeta coordinate is the
zero-ledger fiber
$$
\mathbb Z_{\zeta,\mathcal D}
:=
\sum_{x:T_{\mathcal D}}
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x).
$$
Its non-trivial part is
$$
\mathbb Z^{\mathsf{nt}}_{\zeta,\mathcal D}
:=
\sum_{x:T_{\mathcal D}}
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x)
\times
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x).
$$
The RH target is the assertion that this non-trivial zero-ledger fiber
projects into the fixed-half surface, equivalently into the fixed locus of
the information-channel mirror.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Self-similar zero rigidity]
\label{def:self-similar-zero-rigidity}
For such a zeta coordinate row, the self-similar zero-rigidity statement
is
$$
\mathsf{ZeroRigidity}_{\mathcal D}
:=
\forall x\in T_{\mathcal D},\,
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)
\Longrightarrow
\delta_x=0.
$$
Equivalently, by \autoref{thm:rh-information-channel-normal-form}, every
tower-internal zero-ledger atom in the information band has unit amplitude
on all displayed prime channels.  This is a rigidity condition on
zero-ledger atoms, not a procedure for listing the atoms.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Self-similar off-fixed exclusion]
\label{def:self-similar-off-fixed-exclusion}
The off-fixed exclusion form is
$$
\mathsf{NoOffFixedZero}_{\mathcal D}
:=
\forall x\in T_{\mathcal D},\,
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta_x)
\Longrightarrow
\bot.
$$
It is the tower-internal version of the exclusion target in
\autoref{def:off-fixed-zero-exclusion-target}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Off-fixed exclusion gives zero rigidity]
\label{thm:off-fixed-exclusion-gives-zero-rigidity}
If $\mathsf{NoOffFixedZero}_{\mathcal D}$ holds and the real-part readout
is located with tight apartness, then
$$
\mathsf{ZeroRigidity}_{\mathcal D}
$$
holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$ and assume
$$
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x).
$$
To prove $\delta_x=0$, the located real-part readout reduces the task to
ruling out every positive apartness witness for $\delta_x$.  If
$\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta_x)$ were present, the three displayed rows
would inhabit the premise of
$\mathsf{NoOffFixedZero}_{\mathcal D}$ and hence give $\bot$.  Therefore no
positive off-fixed displacement remains, and tightness of the located
readout gives $\delta_x=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Prime-window radial defect]
\label{def:prime-window-radial-defect}
For a finite prime window $W\subset_{\mathrm{fin}}\PrimeUp$, the
prime-window radial defect of a tower point $x$ is a displayed defect row
$$
\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x)
$$
whose amplitude ledger reads
$$
|\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x)|
=
|\delta_x|\sum_{p\in W}\log p.
$$
For $W\neq\varnothing$, a positive value of this row records a visible
finite-prime trace of off-fixed radial displacement.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Defect-zero incompatibility certificate]
\label{def:defect-zero-incompatibility-certificate}
The defect-zero incompatibility certificate for $\mathcal D$ is the
conditional row
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{DefectZeroIncompat}_{\mathcal D}
:=
\forall x\in T_{\mathcal D},\,&
\mathsf{CompletedZetaCarrier}(\pi_\zeta(x))
\wedge
\sum_{W\subset_{\mathrm{fin}}\PrimeUp}
\bigl(W\neq\varnothing\bigr)
\times
\bigl(|\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x)|>0\bigr)
\\
&\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{Apart}_{0}(\zeta(s_x)).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus a completed zeta carrier with a positive finite-window radial defect
is separated from the zero row.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Off-fixed-to-defect certificate]
\label{def:off-fixed-to-defect-certificate}
The off-fixed-to-defect certificate is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{OffFixedToDefect}_{\mathcal D}
:=
\forall x\in T_{\mathcal D},\,&
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta_x)
\\
&\Longrightarrow
\sum_{W\subset_{\mathrm{fin}}\PrimeUp}
\bigl(W\neq\varnothing\bigr)
\times
\bigl(|\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x)|>0\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
It supplies the finite prime window in which a nonzero radial displacement
becomes visible to the ledger.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Zero-ledger soundness row]
\label{def:zero-ledger-soundness-row}
The zero-ledger soundness row is
$$
\mathsf{ZeroLedgerSound}_{\mathcal D}
:=
\forall x\in T_{\mathcal D},\,
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)
\Longrightarrow
\zeta(s_x)\hsame 0.
$$
It says that the cancellation row used by the information-channel normal
form is sound for the completed zeta value carried by the same tower
point.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Zero-ledger soundness and completeness boundary]
\label{prin:zero-ledger-soundness-completeness-boundary}
The exclusion route requires zero-ledger soundness:
$$
\mathsf{ZeroLedger}(\pi_\zeta(x))
\Longrightarrow
\zeta(s_x)\hsame0.
$$
The converse direction,
$$
\zeta(s_x)\hsame0
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{ZeroLedger}(\pi_\zeta(x)),
$$
is a separate zero-ledger completeness target for the completed carrier.
It is not needed to refute an off-fixed zero-ledger atom.  Soundness is
the row that turns $\mathsf{Apart}_{0}(\zeta(s_x))$ into a contradiction
with zero-ledger admission.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Same-source zeta defect dictionary]
\label{def:same-source-zeta-defect-dictionary}
A same-source zeta defect dictionary for $\mathcal D$ is a certificate
that, for each $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$, the following rows are projections
of the same tower point and are transported by the same zeta classifier:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x),\qquad
\mathsf{CompletedZetaCarrier}(\pi_\zeta(x)),\\
\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x),\qquad
\mathsf{ArchimedeanRow}(\pi_\zeta(x)),\qquad
\mathsf{FunctionalEquationRow}(\pi_\zeta(x)).
\end{gathered}
$$
This dictionary prevents a boundary defect and a bulk zero row from being
read as unrelated objects with the same printed coordinate.  The
finite-prime boundary, Archimedean row, functional-equation row, and
zero-ledger row must all be fields of the same completed zeta payload.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Projection-recovery defect obligation]
\label{def:projection-recovery-defect-obligation}
The projection-recovery defect obligation states that a positive
finite-prime radial defect obtained from the information-channel boundary
survives as a defect row of the completed zeta carrier:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{DefectRecovery}_{\mathcal D}
:=
\forall x\in T_{\mathcal D},\,&
\sum_{W\subset_{\mathrm{fin}}\PrimeUp}
\bigl(W\neq\varnothing\bigr)
\times
\bigl(|\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x)|>0\bigr)
\\
&\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{CompletedDefectRow}_{\mathcal D}(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
It is the carrier-level version of saying that analytic continuation,
Archimedean completion, functional equation, and branch transport do not
erase the source of the displayed radial defect.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Defect incompatibility gives off-fixed exclusion]
\label{thm:defect-incompatibility-gives-off-fixed-exclusion}
Assume the completed-carrier row is present for every zero-ledger tower
point.  If
$$
\mathsf{DefectZeroIncompat}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathsf{OffFixedToDefect}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathsf{ZeroLedgerSound}_{\mathcal D}
$$
hold, then
$$
\mathsf{NoOffFixedZero}_{\mathcal D}
$$
holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$ and assume
$$
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta_x).
$$
The last two rows, through
$\mathsf{OffFixedToDefect}_{\mathcal D}$, give a finite nonempty prime
window $W$ with
$$
|\mathsf{RadDef}_{W}(x)|>0.
$$
Together with the completed-carrier row, this is the premise of
$\mathsf{DefectZeroIncompat}_{\mathcal D}$, hence
$$
\mathsf{Apart}_{0}(\zeta(s_x)).
$$
The zero-ledger soundness row applied to
$\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)$ gives
$$
\zeta(s_x)\hsame 0.
$$
The apartness row and the zero row cannot both be inhabited.  Therefore
the assumed off-fixed zero-ledger atom yields $\bot$, uniformly in $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Defect route gives zero rigidity]
\label{cor:defect-route-gives-zero-rigidity}
Assume the completed-carrier row is present for every zero-ledger tower
point.  If
$$
\mathsf{DefectZeroIncompat}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathsf{OffFixedToDefect}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathsf{ZeroLedgerSound}_{\mathcal D}
$$
hold and the real-part readout is located with tight apartness, then
$$
\mathsf{ZeroRigidity}_{\mathcal D}
$$
holds.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \autoref{thm:defect-incompatibility-gives-off-fixed-exclusion}, the
three certificate rows give
$\mathsf{NoOffFixedZero}_{\mathcal D}$.  Then
\autoref{thm:off-fixed-exclusion-gives-zero-rigidity} gives
$\mathsf{ZeroRigidity}_{\mathcal D}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closure and zero rigidity give the BEDC RH target]
\label{thm:closure-zero-rigidity-give-bedc-rh-target}
Assume every legal non-trivial zero-ledger atom has a tower-internal
position as in \autoref{cor:legal-rh-counterexample-tower-internal}, and
assume the zeta coordinate row transports the BEDC zero and strip
predicates to
$\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)$ and
$\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x)$.  If
$$
\mathsf{ZeroRigidity}_{\mathcal D}
$$
holds, then the BEDC RH predicate of \autoref{def:rh-predicate} holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be the zeta coordinate of a legal non-trivial zero-ledger atom.  By
tower-internal location, there is $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$ whose zeta
projection carries the same zero and critical-strip data.  The transport
rows give
$$
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta_x,\tau_x)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x).
$$
Zero rigidity gives $\delta_x=0$.  By
\autoref{thm:rh-information-channel-normal-form}, this is the critical-line
form of the same packet.  Transporting back through the zeta classifier
gives $\mathsf{OnCritLine}(s)$, which is the BEDC RH conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Coinductive defect route gives the BEDC RH target]
\label{thm:coinductive-defect-route-gives-bedc-rh-target}
Assume every legal non-trivial zero-ledger atom has a tower-internal
position in the full self-similar carrier.  Assume also that the zeta
coordinate row is a same-source row: its zero ledger, completed zeta
carrier, finite prime-window defects, and information-channel readouts are
all read from the same tower point and transported through the same
classifier.  If
$$
\mathsf{DefectZeroIncompat}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathsf{OffFixedToDefect}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathsf{ZeroLedgerSound}_{\mathcal D}
$$
hold, and the real-part readout is located with tight apartness, then the
BEDC RH predicate of \autoref{def:rh-predicate} holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be the zeta coordinate of a legal non-trivial zero-ledger atom.
Tower closure supplies $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$ carrying the same zero and
information-band rows.  If this atom were off-fixed, then
$\mathsf{OffFixedToDefect}_{\mathcal D}$ would supply a finite nonempty
prime window whose radial defect is positive.  The same-source condition
places that defect and the zero-ledger row in the same completed zeta
carrier.  By
\autoref{thm:defect-incompatibility-gives-off-fixed-exclusion}, the three
certificate rows exclude the off-fixed zero atom.  Hence
\autoref{cor:defect-route-gives-zero-rigidity} gives
$\mathsf{ZeroRigidity}_{\mathcal D}$.  Applying
\autoref{thm:closure-zero-rigidity-give-bedc-rh-target} yields the BEDC RH
target.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Constructive RH witness extracted from exclusion]
\label{def:constructive-rh-witness-extracted-exclusion}
The witness extracted from the zero-ledger exclusion route is the function
schema
$$
\Phi_{\mathcal D}:
\mathbb Z^{\mathsf{nt}}_{\zeta,\mathcal D}
\to
\operatorname{Fix}_{1/2},
$$
where $\operatorname{Fix}_{1/2}$ denotes the fixed-half output surface of
\autoref{thm:rh-information-channel-normal-form}.  On an input
$(x,\mathsf{ZeroAtom}_{\mathcal D}(x),\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta_x))$, the
function first excludes $\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta_x)$ by the
defect-incompatibility route and then uses located real readback to return
the witness $\delta_x=0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Exact boundary of the self-similar route]
\label{prin:exact-boundary-self-similar-rh-route}
Self-similar closure contributes tower location.  It excludes an external
place for a legal RH counterexample but does not by itself exclude an
off-fixed tower-internal zero-ledger atom.  The certificate that performs the
exclusion is the defect-zero incompatibility row together with
off-fixed-to-defect, zero-ledger soundness, completed-carrier, and located
real readback data.  In witness form, the route is precisely the chain
recorded in \autoref{cor:witness-form-closure-route}.
\end{principle}
