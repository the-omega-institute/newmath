\section{Self-similar operations and exponential readouts}
\label{sec:self-similar-exponential-readouts}

The compatibility-limit operation of
\autoref{def:compatibility-limit-operation} is a packet operation. It acts
on the full carrier $T_{\mathcal D}$, whose rows include readouts,
classifiers, constraints, ledgers, certificates, and audit surfaces. A
scalar constant belongs to a later analytic readout layer. The two objects
therefore have different types even when the scalar is a faithful shadow of
the operation under a chosen projection.

\begin{definition}[Operation--readout type boundary]
\label{def:operation-readout-type-boundary}
Let $\mathcal D$ be a self-similar readout diagram and let
$$
\Omega_{\mathcal D}:T_{\mathcal D}\to T_{\mathcal D}
$$
be its compatibility-limit operation. A scalar analytic readout is a
displayed map
$$
\chi:T_{\mathcal D}\to A
$$
into a scalar carrier $A$, together with the classifier, normalization,
analytic structure, and ledger rows that make $\chi$ public. A scalar
$c\in A$ has the type of a readout value, while $\Omega_{\mathcal D}$ has
the type of a packet operation. Thus the expression
$$
\Omega_{\mathcal D}=c
$$
has no packet-level meaning. The meaningful claims are typed readout
claims such as
$$
\chi(\Omega_{\mathcal D}(b))=c
$$
or flow readout claims such as
$$
\chi(\Omega_t(b))=c(t).
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Constants are not packet operations]
\label{thm:constants-not-packet-operations}
For any scalar readout $\chi:T_{\mathcal D}\to A$, no scalar constant
$c\in A$ is identical with the packet operation
$\Omega_{\mathcal D}:T_{\mathcal D}\to T_{\mathcal D}$. In particular, the
analytic constant $e$ can only occur as a scalar readout value or as the
value of a scalar readout function; it is not the operation
$\Omega_{\mathcal D}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The operation $\Omega_{\mathcal D}$ has domain and codomain
$T_{\mathcal D}$. A scalar constant $c$ is an element of $A$. Unless a
separate coding row embeds operations into scalar values and back with
displayed inverse data, these types are distinct. The interface packet
does not contain such a coding row. It contains only readout maps from the
packet carrier to visible carriers. Therefore a scalar can be compared
only with a scalar readout of the operation, not with the operation itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Continuous packet flow]
\label{def:continuous-packet-flow}
A continuous packet flow over $\mathcal D$ is a displayed family of packet
operations
$$
(\Omega_t)_{t\in\mathbb R},
\qquad
\Omega_t:T_{\mathcal D}\to T_{\mathcal D},
$$
with ledgered rows
$$
\Omega_0=\operatorname{id}_{T_{\mathcal D}},
\qquad
\Omega_{t+u}=\Omega_t\circ\Omega_u.
$$
The flow is a packet object only when the same classifier, constraint,
ledger, certificate, and audit rows remain visible along the parameter.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Normalized self-change readout]
\label{def:normalized-self-change-readout}
Let $(\Omega_t)_{t\in\mathbb R}$ be a continuous packet flow and let
$\chi:T_{\mathcal D}\to\mathbb R$ be a scalar analytic readout. For
$b\in T_{\mathcal D}$, write
$$
y_b(t):=\chi(\Omega_t(b)).
$$
The readout is normalized self-changing along $b$ when its analytic ledger
contains
$$
y_b(0)=1,
\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}y_b(t)=y_b(t).
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exponential readout shadow]
\label{thm:exponential-readout-shadow}
If $y_b$ is a normalized self-change readout, then
$$
y_b(t)=\exp(t).
$$
Consequently,
$$
y_b(1)=e.
$$
Thus $e$ is the unit-time value of this scalar readout, not the
compatibility-limit operation itself.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define
$$
z(t):=\exp(-t)y_b(t).
$$
The displayed derivative row gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
z'(t)
&=
-\exp(-t)y_b(t)+\exp(-t)y_b'(t)\\
&=
\exp(-t)(y_b'(t)-y_b(t))\\
&=
0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $z$ is constant in the analytic readout layer. Since
$$
z(0)=\exp(0)y_b(0)=1,
$$
we have $z(t)=1$ and therefore $y_b(t)=\exp(t)$. At $t=1$ this gives
$y_b(1)=e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Discrete scale readout]
\label{def:discrete-scale-readout}
A discrete scale readout of the packet operation is a scalar sequence
$$
x_n:=\chi(\Omega_{\mathcal D}^n(b))
$$
whose displayed ledger contains
$$
x_{n+1}=\lambda x_n.
$$
The scalar $\lambda$ is a readout scale for that coordinate, not a
replacement for $\Omega_{\mathcal D}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Analytic interpolation of a scale readout]
\label{prop:analytic-interpolation-scale-readout}
If $x_{n+1}=\lambda x_n$ and the analytic readout layer supplies a chosen
branch of $\log\lambda$, then the scalar function
$$
x(t):=\exp(t\log\lambda)x_0
$$
interpolates the displayed discrete readout at integer times:
$$
x(n)=x_n.
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The recurrence gives $x_n=\lambda^n x_0$. For each integer $n$, the chosen
logarithm branch supplies
$$
\exp(n\log\lambda)=\lambda^n.
$$
Therefore
$$
x(n)=\exp(n\log\lambda)x_0=\lambda^n x_0=x_n.
$$
\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[Constants classify readout modes]
\label{prin:constants-classify-readout-modes}
The constants attached to self-similar phenomena classify readout modes.
The normalized continuous self-change readout gives $e$. A Fibonacci
second-order recursive readout gives the golden scale $\varphi$ as in
\autoref{def:axiszeckendorf-golden-mean-self-similar-object}. A prime
Euler-product readout gives the local channel $p^{-s}$ as in
\autoref{def:rh-centered-information-channel}. These constants are
coordinate shadows of packet-level generation; none is the packet
operation itself.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Prime exponential kernel readout]
\label{def:prime-exponential-kernel-readout}
Let a prime-occupancy row be a finite-support function
$$
k:\PrimeUp\to\mathbb N,
$$
with associated integer packet
$$
n(k)=\prod_{p\in\PrimeUp}p^{k_p}.
$$
The logarithmic energy readout is
$$
E(k):=\log n(k)=\sum_p k_p\log p.
$$
For a complex parameter $s$, the exponential kernel readout is
$$
W_s(k):=\exp(-sE(k)).
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exponential kernel gives Dirichlet weight]
\label{prop:exponential-kernel-dirichlet-weight}
For every displayed prime-occupancy row $k$,
$$
W_s(k)=n(k)^{-s}.
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $E(k)=\log n(k)$. Therefore
$$
W_s(k)=\exp(-s\log n(k))=n(k)^{-s}.
$$
\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[Zeta readout boundary]
\label{prin:zeta-readout-boundary}
The zeta-facing expression $n^{-s}=\exp(-s\log n)$ is the analytic
readout by which prime multiplicative occupancy enters the Dirichlet and
Euler-product surfaces. It does not identify $\zeta$, $e$, or any Euler
factor with $\Omega_{\mathcal D}$. It says that when the zeta carrier is a
coordinate of the self-similar diagram, the zeta coordinate reads the
packet through an exponential kernel, Euler gluing, continuation,
functional-equation, pole, zero-ledger, and transport surface.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Holographic readout analogy]
\label{def:holographic-readout-analogy}
A holographic readout analogy for a self-similar diagram $\mathcal D$ is a
records-side dictionary between a boundary packet
$$
\partial\mathbb G_{\mathcal D}
$$
and a bulk packet
$$
\mathbb G_{\mathcal D}.
$$
The boundary packet consists of finite visible readouts such as finite
windows, prime windows, prime fingerprints, and channel codes. The bulk
packet is the compatibility-limit carrier $T_{\mathcal D}$ together with
the operation $\Omega_{\mathcal D}$, its projection rows, constraint
rows, residual fibers, continuation rows, ledgers, certificates, and audit
surface. The analogy is only this records-side dictionary. It is not a
claim that the packet is a physical anti-de Sitter spacetime, a conformal
field theory, a gravitational theory, or a string-theoretic duality.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Boundary--bulk dictionary]
\label{prin:boundary-bulk-readout-dictionary}
The holographic readout analogy is governed by the following dictionary:
finite boundary windows correspond to coordinate projections of
$T_{\mathcal D}$; extension depth corresponds to the ledgered passage from
finite readout to compatibility-limit carrier; window defects correspond
to obstruction rows in the bulk packet; unit-amplitude boundary channels
correspond to fixed-surface constraints in the bulk packet; and
non-unit radial factors in exponential channels correspond to off-fixed
displacement rows. Each correspondence is a typed readout or ledger
relation, not an equality of physical theories.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Zeta radial and phase coordinates]
\label{def:zeta-radial-phase-coordinates}
In the zeta information-channel coordinate
$$
s=\frac{1}{2}+\delta+i\tau,
$$
the centered prime channel of
\autoref{def:rh-centered-information-channel} is
$$
U_p(\delta,\tau)=p^{-\delta}e^{-i\tau\log p}.
$$
The factor $e^{-i\tau\log p}$ is the phase-clock readout, while
$p^{-\delta}=e^{-\delta\log p}$ is the radial exponential readout. The
fixed surface is the row $\delta=0$, equivalently the unit-amplitude row
$$
|U_p(\delta,\tau)|=1
$$
for every displayed prime channel.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Off-fixed packets carry radial exponent]
\label{prop:off-fixed-packets-carry-radial-exponent}
If a zeta information-channel packet has $\delta\neq0$, then every
displayed prime row $p>1$ carries the non-unit radial factor
$$
e^{-\delta\log p}.
$$
If $\delta=0$, the same channel is purely phase:
$$
U_p(0,\tau)=e^{-i\tau\log p}.
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \autoref{def:zeta-radial-phase-coordinates},
$$
U_p(\delta,\tau)=e^{-\delta\log p}e^{-i\tau\log p}.
$$
When $\delta=0$, the first factor is $\exp(0)=1$, so the channel is the
phase factor $e^{-i\tau\log p}$. When $\delta\neq0$ and $p>1$, the
exponent $-\delta\log p$ is nonzero, so the displayed channel contains a
non-unit radial exponential factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Zero-ledger exponential rigidity target]
\label{def:zero-ledger-exponential-rigidity-target}
The zero-ledger exponential rigidity target is the statement that a zeta
zero-ledger packet in the information band admits only pure phase prime
channels:
$$
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta,\tau)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta)
\Longrightarrow
\forall p\in\PrimeUp,\ |U_p(\delta,\tau)|=1.
$$
Equivalently, in the boundary--bulk dictionary, a zero-ledger boundary row
cannot extend to a bulk packet with a nonzero radial exponent.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exponential rigidity is the fixed-half obligation]
\label{thm:exponential-rigidity-fixed-half-obligation}
The zero-ledger exponential rigidity target is equivalent to the
fixed-half form of the RH information-channel target in
\autoref{thm:rh-information-channel-normal-form}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The rigidity target is exactly the third displayed form in
\autoref{thm:rh-information-channel-normal-form}. That theorem proves it
equivalent to
$$
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta,\tau)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta)
\Longrightarrow
\delta=0,
$$
which is the fixed-half form after substituting
$s=1/2+\delta+i\tau$.
\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[Holographic proof boundary]
\label{prin:holographic-proof-boundary}
The boundary--bulk analogy identifies the shape of the remaining
obligation but does not discharge it. To prove the zeta fixed-half target,
one must derive zero-ledger exponential rigidity from the displayed
Dirichlet row, Euler row, analytic-continuation row, Archimedean
completion row, functional-equation row, zero ledger, and transport
ledger. Stating that an off-fixed packet has a radial factor is only the
readout diagnosis; the missing theorem is the incompatibility between
that radial factor and the completed zero-ledger packet.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Full-constraint self-similar closure]
\label{prin:full-constraint-self-similar-closure}
For a self-similar diagram $\mathcal D$, the operation
$\Omega_{\mathcal D}$ generates only objects with a position inside the
compatibility-limit carrier and its displayed projections. A legal object
therefore has no tower-external position: it must be read as a coordinate,
fiber, section, ledger row, certificate row, or audit row of some
$x\in T_{\mathcal D}$. This closure principle is a discipline on
admissibility. It does not say that every coordinate is fixed by every
involution or that every zeta zero row satisfies the fixed-half condition.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Tower-internal position]
\label{def:tower-internal-position}
Let $y$ be a visible record in a carrier $R_i$ of the diagram. The record
$y$ has a tower-internal position when there exists $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$
such that
$$
y\sim_i\pi_i(x).
$$
When the public use of $y$ depends on more than the visible record, its
tower-internal position also includes the source, projection, constraint,
ledger, certificate, and audit rows through which this comparison is
made.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Legal records have tower-internal position]
\label{thm:legal-records-tower-internal-position}
If a record is legal only by being generated, read out, constrained, and
ledgered by the self-similar diagram $\mathcal D$, then it has a
tower-internal position.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the stated legality condition, the record occurs in some displayed
carrier $R_i$ together with the source, constraint, projection,
certificate, ledger, and audit rows that make it public. Those rows are
part of a compatible packet $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$. The visible record is
therefore classified by the $i$th projection $\pi_i(x)$, which is exactly
the tower-internal position condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A legal RH counterexample would be tower-internal]
\label{cor:legal-rh-counterexample-tower-internal}
If a counterexample to the fixed-half zeta target were a legal BEDC
object, it would be a zeta or zero-ledger projection of some
$x\in T_{\mathcal D}$. It could not be a tower-external object.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:legal-records-tower-internal-position} to the zeta
zero-ledger carrier and its critical-strip readout rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Closure does not imply fixedness]
\label{prop:closure-does-not-imply-fixedness}
A closed self-similar carrier may contain non-fixed points.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $X=\{a,b\}$ and let $\Omega:X\to X$ exchange the two elements:
$$
\Omega(a)=b,
\qquad
\Omega(b)=a.
$$
The carrier $X$ is closed under $\Omega$, and every element lies in an
$\Omega$-orbit. If $J=\Omega$, then
$$
J(a)=b\neq a.
$$
Thus closure under a self-similar operation does not imply membership in
the equalizer of $\operatorname{id}$ and $J$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Off-fixed zero exclusion target]
\label{def:off-fixed-zero-exclusion-target}
In zeta information-channel coordinates
$s=1/2+\delta+i\tau$, the off-fixed zero exclusion target is
$$
\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta,\tau)
\wedge
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta)
\wedge
\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta)
\Longrightarrow
\bot.
$$
Its constructive strengthening is the local-global exclusion row
$$
\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta)
\wedge
\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{Apart}_0(\zeta(1/2+\delta+i\tau)).
$$
This target says that an off-fixed packet may be a tower-internal
candidate, but it cannot be inhabited as a zero-ledger packet.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Off-fixed exclusion gives fixed-half zero ledger]
\label{thm:off-fixed-exclusion-gives-fixed-half-zero-ledger}
If the off-fixed zero exclusion target holds and the real-part readout is
located, then every legal zero-ledger packet in the information band lies
on the fixed-half surface.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let a legal zero-ledger packet have information coordinates
$(\delta,\tau)$ and assume $\mathsf{InfoBand}(\delta)$. To prove the
fixed-half conclusion it suffices, by locatedness of the real-part
readout, to rule out every positive apartness witness for $\delta$.
Assume such a witness, namely $\mathsf{ApartNonFix}(\delta)$. Together
with the zero-ledger row
$\mathsf{Cancel}_{\mathcal P}(\delta,\tau)$ and the information-band row,
the off-fixed zero exclusion target gives a contradiction. Hence no
positive off-fixed displacement remains, so $\delta=0$ and the packet is
on the fixed-half surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[Closure-plus-exclusion form of the RH route]
\label{prin:closure-plus-exclusion-rh-route}
The self-similar closure route has two logically separate parts. First,
full-constraint closure places every legal object, including any proposed
RH counterexample, inside the tower. Second, off-fixed zero exclusion
rules out the tower-internal packets whose zeta readout is both
zero-ledger and off-fixed. The first part supplies admissibility and
location; the second part is the substantive analytic obstruction. The
remaining proof obligation is therefore the incompatibility between
off-fixed displacement and the completed zero-ledger carrier, not the
absence of tower-external objects.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Internal closure witness]
\label{def:internal-closure-witness}
For a claim $A$ whose public use depends on a self-similar diagram
$\mathcal D$, an internal closure witness is a packet
$$
\mathsf{Wit}_{\mathcal D}(A)
$$
consisting of a tower point $x\in T_{\mathcal D}$, a displayed projection
$\pi_i(x)$, a carrier certificate, a readback certificate, an invariant
transport certificate, and an audit row showing how these data inhabit the
claim surface for $A$. Such a witness is internal because its evidence is
read through the tower; it is still a witness because the claim is not
licensed by tower membership alone.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closure does not cancel witnesses]
\label{thm:closure-does-not-cancel-witnesses}
Full-constraint closure does not turn every legal claim into an automatic
theorem. It only locates the objects that a legal claim may mention. A
claim still requires a witness packet for its carrier, readback,
invariant, exclusion, or audit obligations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \autoref{thm:legal-records-tower-internal-position}, a legal record has
a tower-internal position. This supplies a source and projection surface,
but it does not by itself prove any predicate over that surface. To prove
a predicate one must also display the relevant classifier preservation,
readback, invariant transport, exclusion, or audit row. These rows are
exactly the components listed in
\autoref{def:internal-closure-witness}. Therefore closure relocates the
witness into the tower but does not remove the witness obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Object-layer closure and truth-total reflection]
\label{def:object-layer-closure-truth-total-reflection}
Object-layer closure is the claim that legal objects are generated,
projected, and ledgered inside $T_{\mathcal D}$. Truth-total reflection
would be a stronger claim: an internal truth classifier for all statements
represented by the diagram. Formally, it would require a predicate
assigning truth to every represented sentence together with an audit
certificate that this assignment is correct for the whole represented
fragment.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Object-layer closure is not truth closure]
\label{prin:object-layer-closure-not-truth-closure}
The self-similar closure used in the RH route is object-layer closure. It
is not truth-total reflection. It may say where legal zeta packets, zero
ledgers, defects, and exclusion certificates live; it may not supply a
universal internal truth predicate for all claims about those packets.
This is the same stratification recorded in
\autoref{ch:visions-reflection-and-limits}: the primitive loop may close,
while truth-total reflection remains outside the internal object layer.
\end{principle}

\begin{corollary}[Witness form of the closure route]
\label{cor:witness-form-closure-route}
The RH route through $\Omega_{\mathcal D}$ has the form
$$
\text{tower location}
+
\text{zero-ledger soundness}
+
\text{off-fixed defect production}
+
\text{defect-zero incompatibility}
+
\text{located real readback}.
$$
The first term prevents tower-external escape. The remaining terms are
internal witness obligations. In particular, the substantive certificate
is still the exclusion of tower-internal off-fixed zero packets.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Tower location is supplied by
\autoref{cor:legal-rh-counterexample-tower-internal}. Off-fixed exclusion
gives fixed-half zero ledger by
\autoref{thm:off-fixed-exclusion-gives-fixed-half-zero-ledger}. The
definition of the off-fixed zero exclusion target names the needed
defect-zero incompatibility form. Located real readback converts absence
of positive off-fixed displacement into $\delta=0$. These are separate
witness rows, not consequences of closure alone.
\end{proof}
