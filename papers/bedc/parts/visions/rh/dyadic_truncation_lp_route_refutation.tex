\chapter{Dyadic Truncation Laguerre--P\'olya Route Refutation}
\label{ch:visions-rh-dyadic-truncation-lp-route-refutation}
\origin{ai}

The completed zeta function admits the de Bruijn--Newman Fourier
representation
$$
\xi(z)=\int_0^{\infty}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
$$
with $\Phi$ the standard super-exponentially decaying kernel. This suggests a
tempting finite-cutoff route to the Riemann Hypothesis: dyadically truncate
the integral, show that every truncation lies in the Laguerre--P\'olya class,
and pass to the limit by Hurwitz's theorem. The present chapter records that
this route does not close, because its central premise is false for sharp
cutoffs. It does not prove or disprove the Riemann Hypothesis; it isolates,
with a finite reproducible counterexample, why the route cannot be completed.

\begin{definition}[Sharp dyadic truncation]
\label{def:rh-dyadic-sharp-truncation}
For a cutoff $a_K>0$ write the sharp dyadic truncation
$$
\xi_K(z):=\int_0^{a_K}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,
$$
and let $\kappa_K$ denote the number of non-real zeros of $\xi_K$ in the upper
half plane. Since every Laguerre--P\'olya entire function has only real zeros,
any certificate $\kappa_K>0$ refutes the all-$K$ Laguerre--P\'olya premise for
this sharp-cutoff family.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[The conditional Hurwitz bridge]
\label{prin:rh-dyadic-hurwitz-bridge}
Since $\xi_K\to\xi$ uniformly on compact sets and the Laguerre--P\'olya class is
closed under locally uniform limits, the implication
$$
\bigl(\forall K,\ \kappa_K=0\bigr)\ \Rightarrow\ \xi\in\mathrm{LP}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathsf{RH}
$$
is valid. The implication is sound, and that is precisely why it carries no
content on its own: all of the difficulty is shifted into its premise.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[The premise is false: a finite counterexample]
\label{prin:rh-dyadic-premise-false}
The premise $\forall K,\ \kappa_K=0$ fails. The reproducible experiment
\texttt{papers/bedc/experiments/dyadic\_truncation\_lp\_refutation.py}
exhibits non-real zeros of small-cutoff truncations $\xi_K$, each certified by
an argument-principle winding count together with a Newton residual check, so
that $\kappa_K>0$ for those cutoffs. The all-$K$ Laguerre--P\'olya premise
therefore cannot hold, and the route above cannot be discharged.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Forced bifurcation mechanism]
\label{prin:rh-dyadic-forced-bifurcation}
The failure is structural rather than incidental. Write the
boundary-corrected truncation as a homotopy $F_t=Q_K+tP_K$ running from a
Robin characteristic function $Q_K$, whose zeros are all real and simple, to
the truncation at $t=1$. For large cutoffs the first Robin zeros cluster near
the origin and move under the homotopy with opposite-sign velocities, so they
are forced to collide before the endpoint, producing a real double zero from
which a non-real pair is born. Hence the no-bifurcation sub-route is
impossible: real-rootedness of the limit cannot be secured merely by keeping
every truncation inside the Laguerre--P\'olya class, and any salvage would
require a separate birth-and-death cancellation argument across the homotopy.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Scope and provenance of the refutation]
\label{prin:rh-dyadic-route-scope}
The obstruction is that a sharp domain cutoff lacks the Laguerre--P\'olya
preservation enjoyed by the smooth de Bruijn--Newman heat deformation
$e^{tu^2}\Phi$. The same failure mode is classical: the proven failure of the
Tur\'an partial-sum route, the Szeg\H{o}-curve behaviour of the partial sums of the
exponential whose zeros stay complex even though the limit is zero-free, and
the insensitivity of Jensen-polynomial hyperbolicity to off-line zeros. The
route is recorded here as not-claimed. It makes no positive or negative
statement about the Riemann Hypothesis itself; it removes one tempting but
unsound bridge so that effort is not spent re-deriving it.
\end{principle}
