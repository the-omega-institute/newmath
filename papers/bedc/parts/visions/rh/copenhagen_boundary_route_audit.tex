\chapter{Copenhagen Boundary Route Audit}
\label{ch:visions-rh-copenhagen-boundary-route-audit}
\origin{ai}

The Copenhagen no-scale route is a route-audit chapter, not a proof of
$\mathsf{RH}$.  Its purpose is to keep three attractive reformulations on
the honest side of the boundary.  A completed-xi route may speak in
Copenhagen tower energy, in diagonal Jensen hyperbolicity, or in
Gomboc--Stieltjes positivity; none of these languages supplies the missing
source-side positivity by being renamed.  A closed proof still needs an
independent global row that is cofinal in all visible cutoffs and is
constructed before the zero divisor is read.

\section{Faithful Reformulations and Leverage}
\label{sec:rh-copenhagen-faithful-reformulations}

\begin{principle}[Faithful reformulations are not leverage]
\label{prin:rh-faithful-reformulations-not-leverage}
The following rows are faithful reformulations of the same completed-xi
barrier unless an independent source-side positivity or uniform-estimate
row is supplied:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathsf{NoFarEndAbsorption},\qquad
\mathsf{LiPositivity},\qquad
\mathsf{WeilPositivity},\\
\mathsf{StieltjesHerglotzPositivity},\qquad
\mathsf{FullJensenHyperbolicity}.
\end{gathered}
$$
They may be excellent coordinates for the proof obligation.  They do not
decrease the proof obligation merely by translating it into a positivity,
moment, hyperbolicity, or no-scale dialect.
\end{principle}

The distinction is between a source row and a posterior readback.  If a
positive spectral measure is built from an already fixed-half divisor, it
records what $\mathsf{RH}$ would make visible; it does not generate
$\mathsf{RH}$.  If Li, Weil, Herglotz, Stieltjes, or Jensen data are proved
from prime-side or operator-side rows without consuming the zero-placement
conclusion, then the route has leverage.  The audit question is therefore
not whether these formulations are equivalent to the target.  The question
is whether their positive object is produced on the source side.

\section{The Three Projections of the Same Wall}
\label{sec:rh-copenhagen-three-projections}

\begin{principle}[Copenhagen boundary]
\label{prin:rh-copenhagen-boundary}
At the completed-xi level, the Copenhagen energy tower, the
diagonal-Jensen row, and the Gomboc row are three projections of the same
global positivity wall.  In schematic form the closed target is
$$
\begin{gathered}
\forall D,n\; \mathsf{JensenHyperbolic}(D,n)\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists \nu\ge0\; \mathsf{StieltjesXi}(\nu)\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall \varphi\; \mathsf{WeilPSD}(\varphi).
\end{gathered}
$$
The Copenhagen energy expression is the no-scale face of the same wall:
an off-half real scale $r$ creates a positive factor $r^2$ multiplying a
cofinal weight.  Bounded energy can then rule out positive apartness of
$r^2$, but only after the cofinal bounded-energy capture has been supplied.
\end{principle}

The bridge just stated is a boundary principle, not a new theorem of this
chapter.  Its force is negative: it prevents a proof from treating one
projection as a free source for another.  A finite table of Jensen
polynomials, a finite Weil Gram table, a finite spectral measure
approximation, or a finite Copenhagen tower prefix sees only a bounded
window.  A global result must be cofinal in degree, truncation, support,
height, and packet depth.

\begin{principle}[Finite visibility is incomplete]
\label{prin:rh-finite-visibility-incomplete}
Finite phase data, finite Jensen radius data, finite spectral data, and
finite positive-semidefinite tables do not determine the complete zero
set.  A finite window may be matched while additional zeros are inserted
outside the visible radius by a Blaschke-type outer-window modification.
Therefore finite success cannot be promoted to completed-xi closure unless
there is a cofinal uniform theorem or an independent source-side
construction that controls every later window.
\end{principle}

The consequence is operational.  If the argument asks to check all
degrees $D$, all Jensen levels $n$, all spectral cutoffs $K$, or all
compact supports one by one, then it has become an enumeration protocol
rather than a proof.  A proof needs a row whose statement is uniform
before the enumeration starts.

\section{Checked Finite Brick}
\label{sec:rh-copenhagen-checked-finite-brick}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded energy excludes positive scale apartness]
\label{thm:rh-copenhagen-bounded-energy-excludes-positive-scale-apartness}
Let $r$ be a rational scale parameter, let
$W:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q$ be nonnegative and unbounded, and let $B$ be a
rational bound.  If
$$
\forall n,\qquad r^2 W_n\le B,
$$
then $0<r^2$ is impossible.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.CopenhagenNoScale.boundedEnergy\_apart\_absurd}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The formal proof chooses, by unboundedness of $W$, an index $n$ with
$$
W_n>B/r^2
$$
under the temporary assumption $0<r^2$.  Multiplication by the positive
rational $r^2$ gives
$$
B<r^2W_n,
$$
contradicting the assumed bound $r^2W_n\le B$.  This is the abstract
Archimedean no-scale brick.  It is checked in Lean with no additional
axiom.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite normal-defect ledger]
\label{thm:rh-normal-defect-ledger}
Let $h_1,\dots,h_m$ be rational heights and $r$ a rational normal
parameter, with per-height defect $d_a=-rh_a$, total reading
$D=\sum_a d_a$, and energy $E=\sum_a d_a^{2}$.  Then
$$
D=-r\sum_a h_a,\qquad E=r^{2}\sum_a h_a^{2},
$$
the mirror $r\mapsto-r$ flips each first-order defect and fixes the
second-order energy, and if $\sum_a h_a^{2}>0$ then $E>0$ for $r^{2}>0$
while $E=0$ forces $r=0$.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.NormalDefectLedger.ledgerE\_identity}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.NormalDefectLedger.ledgerD\_identity}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.NormalDefectLedger.ledgerE\_neg\_parameter}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.NormalDefectLedger.ledgerE\_positive\_of\_square\_positive}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.NormalDefectLedger.ledgerE\_zero\_forces\_parameter\_zero}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first-order defect changes sign under the function-equation mirror,
while the second-order energy survives the mirror.  Thus a finite
window's energy reading is a faithful detector of an off-line normal
offset at the level of rational ledger data.  This ledger supplies
per-level readings for the Copenhagen tower of
\autoref{thm:rh-copenhagen-bounded-energy-excludes-positive-scale-apartness},
whereas the tower supplies the bounded-energy contradiction.

The ledger does not say that a zeta zero rejects a nonzero normal
parameter.  That rejection principle, whether named as no extra zero
sheet, defect-zero incompatibility, or boundary stability, is the same
$\mathsf{RH}$-equivalent wall as the Copenhagen tower capture obligation.
An independent oracle route, treating an off-line zero as a new type and
expecting boundary-information contradiction, audits to that same wall.
The extractable value before the wall is precisely this finite ledger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite jets underdetermine the polynomial]
\label{thm:rh-finite-visibility-additive-jet-underdetermination}
For every polynomial $p$ over the rationals and every $N$, there is an
infinite injective family of polynomials, each sharing the first $N+1$
coefficients, the degree-$N$ jet, with $p$, obtained by adding a single
high-degree monomial beyond the visible window.  Hence a finite jet does
not determine the polynomial.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.FiniteVisibility.fvFamily\_injective}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.FiniteVisibility.fvFamily\_shares\_jet}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.FiniteVisibility.jet\_extendAt\_invisible}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This checked brick is the additive core of
\autoref{prin:rh-finite-visibility-incomplete}.  The family changes only a
coefficient beyond the visible degree-$N$ jet, so the finite window still
reads the same polynomial data while the resulting polynomials are pairwise
different.  The Blaschke-type outer-window modification in
\autoref{prin:rh-finite-visibility-incomplete}, where one keeps the disk
window fixed while inserting a zero outside it, is the multiplicative and
geometric version of the same boundary principle.  That version remains a
paper-level principle here and is not claimed as Lean checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Degree-two Turan certificate for Jensen positivity]
\label{thm:rh-jensen-turan-degree2}
Let $P(X)=a+2bX+cX^{2}$ be the degree-two Jensen polynomial with
$c>0$.  Then $P$ takes a nonpositive value for some rational $X$ if and
only if the Turan inequality $b^{2}\ge ac$ holds.  The proof completes
the square,
$$
c\,P(X)=(cX+b)^{2}+(ac-b^{2}),
$$
and uses the witness $X=-b/c$.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.JensenTuranDegree2.jensen2\_dips\_iff\_turan}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.JensenTuranDegree2.jensen2\_complete\_square}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.JensenTuranDegree2.jensen2\_nonpos\_forces\_turan}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is an unconditional finite algebraic certificate over the rational
carrier used by the Lean packet.  It isolates the structural core of the
Turan inequality
$$
\gamma_{n+1}^{2}\ge \gamma_n\gamma_{n+2}
$$
for the Taylor coefficients that would feed the degree-two Jensen
polynomial of $\xi$.  The certificate is strictly weaker than
$\mathsf{RH}$: only Jensen hyperbolicity for all degrees $d$ and all
levels $n$ is the P\'olya--$\mathsf{RH}$ equivalent statement.  It does
not constrain $\xi$ zeros without the coefficient-enclosure obligation;
turning the rational certificate into a statement about $\xi$ would also
require certified rational enclosures for the relevant Taylor
coefficients $\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cubic discriminant identity and the higher-Turan condition]
\label{thm:rh-jensen-turan-degree3}
Let $J(X)=g_0+3g_1X+3g_2X^2+g_3X^3$ be the degree-three Jensen
polynomial and set $A=g_1^2-g_0g_2$, $B=g_2^2-g_1g_3$,
$C=g_1g_2-g_0g_3$, and $T_3=4AB-C^2$.  Then the discriminant of $J$
satisfies the pure ring identity
$$
\Delta(J)=27\,T_3,
$$
and, over the ordered rationals, $T_3\ge 0$ if and only if $A\ge 0$,
$B\ge 0$, and $C^2\le 4AB$.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.JensenTuranDegree3.cubic\_discriminant\_eq\_27\_T3}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.JensenTuranDegree3.T3\_nonneg\_iff}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.JensenTuranDegree3.subdiscriminant\_A}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Lean certificate is finite, unconditional, and algebraic over the
rational carrier.  The discriminant formula is a pure ring identity, and
the subdiscriminant layer supplies the SOS-type identity
$$
4A^3=H_A^2+g_0^2T_3
$$
with the corresponding $B$-side relation used by the ordered-rational
corollary.  Thus the nontrivial direction from $T_3\ge0$ to $A\ge0$ and
$B\ge0$ is obtained without real closure.

This is not the real-root bridge.  The implication
$$
\Delta\ge0\Longleftrightarrow J \hbox{ has three real roots}
$$
is a separate real-closure or Sturm obligation, and is not part of this
certificate.  Over the ordered rationals, nonnegative discriminant does
not even supply rational roots: $X^3-3X+1$ has discriminant $81>0$ and no
rational root.  Nor is $T_3$ an unconditional coefficient-SOS polynomial:
at $(g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3)=(1,0,0,1)$ one has $T_3=-1$.

The fixed degree-three certificate is strictly weaker than
$\mathsf{RH}$ and is not $\mathsf{RH}$-equivalent.  P\'olya equivalence
requires Jensen hyperbolicity in every degree and every level.  Applying
this finite algebraic brick to $\xi$ would still require certified
enclosures for $\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$, and the brick alone
does not constrain the zero set of $\xi$.
\end{proof}

The conditional tower interface is also encoded:
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.CopenhagenNoScale.CopenhagenTowerData}
and
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.CopenhagenNoScale.CopenhagenCaptureObligation}.
The field
$$
\mathsf{energy\_bounded}:
\exists B,\ \forall n,\ r^2W_n\le B
$$
inside $\mathsf{CopenhagenTowerData}$ is a capture input.  It is not
inhabited here for completed zeta zeros.  The checked conditional theorem
$$
\mathsf{CopenhagenTowerData}(r)
\Longrightarrow
\neg(0<r^2)
$$
is recorded as
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.CopenhagenNoScale.copenhagenTower\_forces\_not\_apart}.
Thus the Lean contribution is a true finite brick plus a conditional
handoff.  It does not assert $r=0$ by trichotomy, does not construct the
xi tower, and does not prove $\mathsf{RH}$.

\section{GAG One-Step Certificate}
\label{sec:rh-copenhagen-gag-one-step-certificate}

The GAG one-step packet is another finite brick in the same audit
discipline.  For a fixed degree parameter $D$, a certificate records
rational root isolation intervals $[r_i^-,r_i^+]$, a rational level
interval $[\lambda^-,\lambda^+]$, and rational signed-lobe intervals
$[S_i^-,S_i^+]$.  Its overshoot interval is computed by the finite
recursion
$$
\begin{gathered}
m_0^-=\lambda^-,
\qquad
m_0^+=\lambda^+,\\
m_{i+1}^- = S_i^- - m_i^+,
\qquad
m_{i+1}^+ = S_i^+ - m_i^-.
\end{gathered}
$$
The checked part is entirely finite and rational: if the true level and
true signed-lobe values lie in the recorded intervals, then the true
overshoot lies in the computed overshoot interval at every step.  If in
addition $\lambda^->0$ and every lower overshoot bound $m_i^-$ is
positive through the finite range, the true overshoots are positive.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.GAGCertificate.GAGCert.gag\_true\_overshoot\_in\_interval}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.GAGCertificate.GAGCert.gag\_true\_overshoot\_pos}

\begin{theorem}[Finite GAG alternation soundness]
\label{thm:rh-gag-finite-alternation-soundness}
For one fixed finite GAG certificate, positive true overshoot at a
signed-lobe step forces the signed-lobe value to cross the level
$\lambda$ in the parity prescribed by the certificate: odd lobes are
strictly above $\lambda$, and even lobes are strictly below $\lambda$.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.GAGCertificate.GAGCert.gag\_signed\_lobe\_alternation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The formal statement is the rational one-step calculation.  In odd
parity the signed-lobe relation is $v=\lambda+m$; in even parity it is
$\lambda=v+m$.  The hypothesis $m>0$ gives the corresponding strict
inequality.  The interval theorem above supplies the positive overshoot
input when the recorded finite certificate passes its lower-bound tests.
\end{proof}

This theorem is not a root-count theorem.  The root intervals are
included in the packet because they are the intended handoff surface for
a later constructive located-IVT bridge.  The finite Lean theorem checks
the rational alternation soundness only: interval inclusion gives
positive overshoot, and positive overshoot gives strict alternation
across the level.

\begin{principle}[Nonvacuous one-step witness]
\label{prin:rh-gag-nonvacuous-one-step-witness}
The certificate layer is not an empty shell.  The encoded $D=1$ instance
uses $\lambda=1/2$, a root ledger at $-1$, and the single signed-lobe
value $1$.  It passes because $1/2>0$ and $1-1/2=1/2>0$.
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.GAGCertificate.GAGCert.gagCert\_D1\_passes}
\end{principle}

This is the formal analogue of the hand calculation behind
$I_1=1+X$ and the one-step hyperbolic successor $I_2$ in the toy oracle
case.  It shows that the certificate format has an inhabited finite
example.  It does not by itself prove a general Jensen--P\'olya step.

\begin{principle}[Located-IVT root count is an obligation]
\label{prin:rh-gag-located-ivt-obligation}
Passing finite GAG alternation is not yet the assertion that
$I_{D+1}$ has $D+1$ located negative real roots.  That conclusion needs a
constructive located-IVT root-count bridge: monotone crossing, located
root existence, and endpoint sign crossing have to be supplied as
located-real witnesses.  In Lean this bridge is deliberately recorded as
an obligation structure, not as an inhabited theorem:
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.GAGCertificate.GAGCert.GAGRootCountObligation}.
The conditional handoff
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.GAGCertificate.GAGCert.gag\_onestep\_hyperbolic\_of\_rootcount}
only packages the conclusion after those obligation witnesses and the
root-count function have already been supplied.
\end{principle}

Thus the checked GAG contribution is exactly the finite rational
certificate soundness plus a nonvacuous $D=1$ witness.  It does not prove
the located-IVT bridge, does not count roots of $I_{D+1}$, and does not
assert unconditional hyperbolicity for any completed Jensen--P\'olya
family.

\begin{principle}[Uniform GAG generator is the frontier]
\label{prin:rh-gag-uniform-generator-frontier}
For each fixed $D$, the GAG format gives a finite certificate target:
one may provide rational isolating intervals, a rational level interval,
signed-lobe bounds, and finite lower-bound checks for the overshoot
recursion.  Closing all degrees would require a
$\mathsf{UniformGAGCertificateGenerator}$ producing the certificates
uniformly in $D$ and proving
$$
\forall D\;\forall i\le D,\qquad m_i^-(D)>0.
$$
The natural decomposition is a finite-part lower-bound proof, a tail
upper-bound proof such as a prolate or Hilbert--Schmidt estimate, and a
stability proof for the overshoot recursion.  If such a uniform generator
were attached to the full Jensen--P\'olya family, it would cross the
$\mathsf{RH}$ boundary.  It is therefore recorded here as an
$\mathsf{RH}$-level frontier, not as a theorem and not as evidence that
the all-$D$ problem is already closed.
\end{principle}

\section{Gomboc Conditional Comparison}
\label{sec:rh-copenhagen-gomboc-conditional-comparison}

The Gomboc route has the same logical shape.  Its encoded xi-facing
container
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.AnalyticGombocForXi.AnalyticGombocForXi}
contains Pick, Herglotz, Stieltjes, equivalence, handoff, and
no-hidden-ballast data.  The handoff theorem
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.AnalyticGombocForXi.rh\_via\_analytic\_gomboc}
is conditional on such a container.  The source-side spectral positivity
field
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.AnalyticGombocForXi.StieltjesPositiveSpectral.positive\_spectral\_measure}
is part of the supplied structure, not a theorem generated in this paper
chapter.

This is the intended comparison.  Copenhagen says that bounded cofinal
energy plus unbounded weights forbid positive scale apartness.  Gomboc
says that a supplied Pick--Herglotz--Stieltjes positive package gives the
constructive RH readout.  Both routes are useful boundary normal forms.
Neither route claims that the required xi-facing package has been
constructed.

\section{Bad Examples as First-Class Boundary Data}
\label{sec:rh-copenhagen-bad-examples}

\begin{principle}[Two-term resonance is off-line]
\label{prin:rh-bad-example-two-term-resonance}
For two exponential terms with distinct heights $h_1\ne h_2$ and
coefficients $a\ne b$, phase cancellation occurs at a shifted line
$$
\delta=\frac{\log(b/a)}{h_2-h_1}\ne0
$$
in general.  Verdict: phase cancellation alone is not enough; exact
balance or a Lee--Yang stability row is required before a resonance may
be used as fixed-half evidence.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Positive kernels do not imply Laguerre--Polya]
\label{prin:rh-bad-example-positive-kernel-not-lp}
Let
$$
\mu=\delta_{-1}+a\delta_0+\delta_1
\qquad (a>2).
$$
The Fourier transform is
$$
\widehat{\mu}(z)=a+2\cos z.
$$
Its zeros need not be real.  Verdict: positivity of a kernel or measure
row does not by itself place an entire function in the
Laguerre--Polya class; a separate real-rootedness-preserving mechanism is
needed.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Mellin projection need not preserve Lee--Yang]
\label{prin:rh-bad-example-mellin-projection}
The finite Mellin projection
$$
P_2(s)=1-2^{-s}
$$
has zeros on $\operatorname{Re}(s)=0$, not on the line
$\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2$.  Verdict: a projection that is natural for
Mellin or prime-window bookkeeping need not preserve the Lee--Yang unit
circle or the fixed-half target.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[All-cutoff dyadic LP is not a reliable premise]
\label{prin:rh-bad-example-all-cutoff-dyadic-lp}
The all-$K$ dyadic Laguerre--Polya premise is not a stable bridge.  Low
corrected Jensen levels for small cutoffs such as $K=2,3$ already exhibit
non-hyperbolic behaviour in the intended finite tests.  Verdict: a
cutoff-by-cutoff Laguerre--Polya route should be treated as a refuted or
unreliable premise unless it supplies a separate uniform preservation
theorem.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Finite visibility cannot certify the tail]
\label{prin:rh-bad-example-finite-visibility}
Any finite table of phases, Jensen coefficients, spectral atoms, Gram
entries, or Copenhagen tower levels leaves an outer region unseen.
Additional zeros or defects may be placed beyond that region without
changing the visible data.  Verdict: finite visibility is evidence about
a window, not a certificate for the completed zero set.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Boundary verdict]
\label{prin:rh-copenhagen-boundary-verdict}
The honest output of the Copenhagen audit is the following separation.
The checked no-scale brick is real: bounded cofinal energy contradicts
positive rational scale apartness.  The capture rows that would attach
that brick to completed zeta zeros are not inhabited here.  The diagonal
Jensen, Weil, Stieltjes--Herglotz, and Gomboc formulations describe the
same global wall.  A proof must add source-side positivity or a cofinal
uniform theorem; otherwise the route remains a boundary normal form and
does not become a proof of $\mathsf{RH}$.
\end{principle}
