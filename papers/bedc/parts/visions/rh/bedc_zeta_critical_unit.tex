\chapter{Zeta BEDC Critical Unit Bridge}
\label{ch:visions-rh-bedc-zeta-critical-unit}
\origin{ai}

This chapter records a conditional ZetaBEDC bridge for the critical-line
route. It is a faithful bridge and a conditional theorem, not an
unconditional proof of RH. The single named hinge is
$$
\mathsf{CriticalUnitSoundness}.
$$
This hinge says that a legally closed zeta-zero residual has unit radial
modulus exactly when its offset from the critical line is zero. In the
classical reading this is the RH-strength assertion
$$
|\widetilde R_{\rho}(u)|=e^{ru}\equiv 1
\Longleftrightarrow
r=0.
$$
It is named as an obligation only. No resident of
$\mathsf{CriticalUnitSoundness}$ or of the total
$\mathsf{ZetaBEDCClosure}$ package is supplied here.

\section{Euler Core}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-euler-core}

\begin{definition}[Euler core packet]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-euler-core}
The Euler core packet is the finite prime-window reading of the Euler
product in a half-plane where the classical product is available:
$$
\zeta(s)=\prod_p (1-p^{-s})^{-1},
\qquad
\Re(s)>1.
$$
At a finite prime bound $P$ the packet records
$$
E_P(s):=\prod_{p\leq P}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
$$
and the logarithmic derivative layer
$$
-\frac{E_P'(s)}{E_P(s)}
=
\sum_{p\leq P}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\log p)p^{-ks}.
$$
This finite packet is a BEDC-addressable prime ledger: every displayed
prime, exponent, and coefficient is a finite row. The infinite Euler
product and its analytic continuation are not kernel inputs.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Classical Euler correspondence]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-classical-euler}
The identity between the Euler product and $\zeta(s)$ in $\Re(s)>1$ is
a classical analytic number theory statement. In this chapter it supplies
the external correspondence row for the finite Euler core. It is not
proved inside the BEDC Lean kernel.
\end{principle}

The bridge role of this core is only address discipline. A zero packet
cannot be moved by a finite Euler layer unless the finite layer carries a
visible residual row. The global question is therefore pushed to the tail
ledger and to the residual normalization in
\autoref{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-zero-residual-functor}.

\section{Eta Bridge}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-eta-bridge}

\begin{definition}[Eta bridge packet]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-eta-bridge}
The eta bridge uses the alternating Dirichlet series
$$
\eta(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-s}
$$
and the classical identity
$$
\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s).
$$
In the BEDC bridge this is not a replacement for the Euler core. It is a
continuation address: finite alternating prefixes, tail ledgers, and the
factor $1-2^{1-s}$ determine where a zeta-zero claim is being read.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Classical eta continuation row]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-eta-classical}
The analytic continuation of $\eta(s)$ to the half-plane needed for the
critical strip, and the identity
$$
\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s),
$$
are classical analytic statements. They are recorded here as paper-level
bridge facts. They are not imported into Lean as complex-analysis
theorems, and no BEDC kernel theorem depends on their internal proof.
\end{principle}

The eta bridge fixes the reading of a nontrivial zero packet. A zero
packet $\rho$ in the strip is admissible only after the pole at $s=1$,
trivial zeros, the eta factor, and the continuation branch ledger have
been separated. This keeps the later residual functor from smuggling a
branch choice into the critical-unit assertion.

\section{Xi Normalization}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-xi}

\begin{definition}[Xi normalization packet]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-xi}
The xi packet records the classical completed zeta expression
$$
\xi(s)
=
\frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
$$
and the symmetry
$$
\xi(s)=\xi(1-s).
$$
For a nontrivial zero $\rho$, write
$$
\rho=\frac12+r+i\gamma.
$$
The functional equation pairs $\rho$ with $1-\rho$ and therefore changes
$r$ to $-r$ while keeping the critical offset as the single radial
coordinate to be tested.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Classical xi symmetry row]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-xi-classical}
The meromorphic continuation of $\zeta$, the Gamma factor, and the
functional equation for $\xi$ are classical analytic statements. This
chapter uses them only as paper-level bridge rows, not as Lean kernel
proof obligations.
\end{principle}

The xi packet makes the target coordinate unambiguous. The BEDC
conditional theorem does not try to locate or enumerate zeros; it only
uses the already scoped `RatComplex` located-zero predicate and asks
whether its real part has offset zero from the rational half.

\section{Zero Residual Functor}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-zero-residual-functor}

\begin{definition}[Principal zero residual]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-principal-residual}
For a zero $\rho=1/2+r+i\gamma$ of multiplicity $m_{\rho}$, the
classical logarithmic derivative has local principal part
$$
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
\sim
-\frac{m_{\rho}}{s-\rho}.
$$
After the logarithmic height readout $x=e^u$, the principal residual
has the form
$$
R_{\rho}(u)=-m_{\rho}e^{u/2}e^{ru}e^{i\gamma u}.
$$
The normalized residual divides out the visible base size
$m_{\rho}e^{u/2}$ and keeps
$$
\widetilde R_{\rho}(u)=e^{ru}e^{i\gamma u}.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Classical logarithmic-derivative residual row]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-log-derivative-classical}
The expansion of $-\zeta'/\zeta$ at a nontrivial zero and its height
readout are classical analytic number theory statements. BEDC records
their finite address and residual ledger roles. Lean does not prove the
Laurent expansion, does not import complex analysis, and does not reason
about unrestricted complex exponentials.
\end{principle}

The critical-unit hinge is now visible:
$$
|\widetilde R_{\rho}(u)|=e^{ru}.
$$
Thus unit modulus for all $u$ is equivalent, in the classical reading,
to $r=0$. This is precisely why the hinge must remain an obligation. To
prove it for every legal closed zero packet would be to prove the
critical-line part of RH in this route.

\section{Finite-Tail Addresses}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-finite-tail-addresses}

\begin{definition}[Finite-tail address ledger]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-finite-tail-address-ledger}
For a finite prime bound $P$ the logarithmic derivative splits as
$$
-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
=
\sum_{p\leq P}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\log p)p^{-ks}
+
\mathsf{Tail}_{>P}(s),
$$
where the second term records every prime address not yet exposed by the
finite window. The BEDC reading stores the first term as finite prime
data and the second term as a tail obligation with branch, support, and
cofinality rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Classical finite-layer pole separation]
\label{lem:rh-zeta-critical-unit-finite-layer-pole-separation}
At a nontrivial zero $\rho$ in the open critical strip, every finite
Euler logarithmic layer
$$
\sum_{p\leq P}\frac{\log p}{p^s-1}
$$
is holomorphic at $\rho$. Therefore the principal part of
$-\zeta'/\zeta$ at $\rho$ lies in the infinite tail ledger.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is a classical analytic statement. For a fixed prime $p$, the poles
of
$$
\frac{\log p}{p^s-1}
$$
occur where $p^s=1$, equivalently at
$$
s=\frac{2\pi i n}{\log p}
\qquad (n\in\mathbb Z).
$$
These poles lie on the imaginary axis. A finite sum has no other poles,
so it is holomorphic at every nontrivial zero in the open critical strip.
Hence a nonzero principal residual at $\rho$ cannot be cancelled by the
finite layer; it must be assigned to the infinite tail ledger.
\end{proof}

This separation is still not a proof of RH. It says only where the
residual is stored. The obstruction remains the soundness claim that a
legal BEDC tail closure cannot hide the radial factor $e^{ru}$ unless
$r=0$.

\section{Boundary Stability}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-boundary-stability}

\begin{principle}[Classical boundary stability row]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-boundary-stability}
Let a contour box avoid zeros and poles on its boundary. Under a
sufficiently small perturbation of the analytic data, the winding number
of the boundary image is unchanged:
$$
\Delta_{\partial B}\arg f
=
\Delta_{\partial B}\arg g.
$$
The standard proof uses Rouch\'e-type boundary estimates and the
argument principle. This row is a classical complex-analysis statement,
not a BEDC Lean theorem.
\end{principle}

Boundary stability supplies the paper-level reason that finite rational
boxes can serve as stable addresses for located zero packets. Once a box
has a certified no-boundary-zero margin, finite refinements may preserve
the zero count. The BEDC kernel does not prove the analytic argument
principle here; it only sees finite carriers and named obligations.

\section{Finite Ledger Lift}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-finite-ledger-lift}

\begin{definition}[Ledger lift and recurrence pressure]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-ledger-lift}
The finite ledger lift maps a zero packet, finite prime window, contour
address, eta continuation address, and xi symmetry address into one
finite BEDC package. Its visible rows are
$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathsf{EulerCore},\quad
\mathsf{EtaBridge},\quad
\mathsf{XiSymmetry},\quad
\mathsf{ZeroResidual},\\
\mathsf{TailAddress},\quad
\mathsf{BoundaryStability},\quad
\mathsf{FiniteWindowReadback}.
\end{gathered}
$$
The pigeonhole role is only finite recurrence pressure: among finitely
many windows, classifiers, and branch ledgers, a stable residual pattern
must be routed through one visible row or through the named tail
obligation.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[No hidden analytic inhabitant]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-no-hidden-analytic-inhabitant}
The finite ledger lift does not construct an analytic object from a
finite pigeonhole argument. It only prevents the bridge from losing the
address of the radial residual. If the residual is not visible in a
finite row, it is recorded in the tail obligation that later becomes
$\mathsf{CriticalUnitSoundness}$.
\end{principle}

Thus the finite lift is a bookkeeping theorem in the paper bridge, not a
replacement for the missing RH-strength analytic rigidity.

\section{Positive Boundary Energy}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-positive-boundary-energy}

\begin{definition}[Rational radial energy]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-radial-energy}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.radialEnergy}
For rational offset $r$ and positive rational interval length $|I|$, the
finite radial energy is
$$
E_I(r)=r^2|I|.
$$
In Lean this is the rational expression
$$
\mathsf{radialEnergy}(r,\ell)
=
r\cdot r\cdot \ell.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite rational energy detects zero offset]
\label{thm:rh-zeta-critical-unit-radial-energy-zero-iff}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.radialEnergy\_zero\_iff}
If $\ell>0$, then
$$
\mathsf{radialEnergy}(r,\ell)=0
\Longleftrightarrow
r=0
$$
in the rational BEDC equality relation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is a finite rational theorem, checked by Lean without complex
analysis. The proof uses positivity of the length row, multiplicative
apartness for nonzero rationals, and the fact that a rational square can
vanish only when its base vanishes. It proves only the arithmetic energy
step
$$
r^2\ell=0\Longleftrightarrow r=0
\qquad(\ell>0).
$$
It does not prove that a legal zeta closure supplies $E_I(r)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonzero rational offset gives nonzero energy]
\label{thm:rh-zeta-critical-unit-radial-energy-nonzero}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.radialEnergy\_not\_zero\_of\_ne}
If $r$ is apart from zero and $\ell>0$, then
$$
\mathsf{radialEnergy}(r,\ell)\neq 0
$$
in the rational BEDC equality relation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The checked proof is the apartness form of the previous finite fact:
nonzero $r$ gives nonzero $r\cdot r$, positive $\ell$ is apart from zero,
and multiplicative apartness gives an energy value apart from zero.
\end{proof}

This is the only part of the bridge where the positive radial energy is
proved in the Lean kernel. All analytic reasons why a zeta residual would
force a particular energy value remain outside the finite kernel.

\section{Critical Unit Closure}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-critical-unit-closure}

\begin{definition}[CriticalUnitSoundness]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-soundness}
\leanstmt{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.CriticalUnitSoundness}
The named obligation
$$
\mathsf{CriticalUnitSoundness}
$$
states that every located nontrivial zeta zero accepted by the legal
ZetaBEDC closure has zero rational offset:
$$
\forall s,\quad
\mathsf{NontrivialZetaZero}(s)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{offsetOf}(s)=0.
$$
The offset is
$$
\operatorname{offsetOf}(s)=\Re(s)-\frac12.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Hinge equivalence]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-hinge-equivalence}
In the classical residual reading, the obligation is exactly the
critical-unit assertion
$$
|\widetilde R_{\rho}(u)|=e^{ru}\equiv 1
\Longleftrightarrow
r=0.
$$
For all legal nontrivial zeta-zero packets, this is RH-strength content.
It is therefore not proved here, not supplied as a structure field value,
and not approximated by a trivial inhabitant.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[ZetaBEDCClosure]
\label{def:rh-zeta-critical-unit-closure}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.ZetaBEDCClosure}
The Lean structure
$$
\mathsf{ZetaBEDCClosure}
$$
has the field
$$
\mathsf{criticalUnitSoundness}:
\mathsf{CriticalUnitSoundness}.
$$
It is a conditional interface. This chapter never constructs a canonical
value of this structure.
\end{definition}

The separation is deliberate: the structure lets Lean verify the final
conditional handoff while keeping the RH-strength hinge visible and
undischarged.

\section{Splicing}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-splicing}

\begin{theorem}[Energy zero reads as zero offset]
\label{thm:rh-zeta-critical-unit-offset-zero-of-energy-zero}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.offset\_zero\_of\_energy\_zero}
For a located rational-complex zero packet $s$, if $\ell>0$ and
$$
\mathsf{radialEnergy}(\operatorname{offsetOf}(s),\ell)=0,
$$
then
$$
\operatorname{offsetOf}(s)=0.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This theorem calls
\autoref{thm:rh-zeta-critical-unit-radial-energy-zero-iff}. It is a
finite rational statement. It does not prove that zeta closure gives
energy zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ZetaBEDC closure implies located ConstructiveRH]
\label{thm:rh-zeta-critical-unit-closure-implies-constructive-rh}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.BedcZetaCriticalUnit.zetaBEDC\_closure\_imply\_constructiveRH}
Given a value
$$
Z:\mathsf{ZetaBEDCClosure},
$$
the located rational-complex RH predicate
$$
\mathsf{ConstructiveRH}
$$
follows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The checked proof is only a conditional reduction. For a located
nontrivial zero $s$, the field
$$
Z.\mathsf{criticalUnitSoundness}
$$
gives
$$
\operatorname{offsetOf}(s)=0.
$$
Since
$$
\operatorname{offsetOf}(s)=\Re(s)-\frac12,
$$
the rational subtraction lemma reads this as
$$
\Re(s)=\frac12,
$$
which is exactly the local \texttt{OnCriticalLine} predicate used by the
existing \texttt{ConstructiveRH} statement.
\end{proof}

No proof of $Z:\mathsf{ZetaBEDCClosure}$ is supplied. Therefore this
theorem does not prove RH. It proves only that the explicitly named
RH-strength field is sufficient for the located BEDC target.

\section{CriticalUnitSoundness Obligation}
\label{sec:rh-zeta-critical-unit-obligation}

\begin{principle}[The single undischarged hinge]
\label{prin:rh-zeta-critical-unit-single-hinge}
The only hinge left open by this bridge is
$$
\mathsf{CriticalUnitSoundness}.
$$
It asserts that a legal closed zeta-zero residual cannot carry the
non-unit radial factor $e^{ru}$ unless $r=0$. Any proof of this row for
all legal nontrivial zeta-zero packets would have RH strength in the
classical reading.
\end{principle}

Candidate routes to the hinge must therefore be treated as RH-strength
routes. Examples include a solenoid terminal-object theorem forcing every
visible phase to lie in the unit torus, or an Archimedean small-support
positivity theorem that refuses hidden radial mass. Until such a theorem
is actually proved, the hinge remains a named obligation.
