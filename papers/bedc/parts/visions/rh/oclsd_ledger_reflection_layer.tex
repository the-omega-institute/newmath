\section{Prime--Zeckendorf register arithmetic}

The OCLSD register is a finite list of $(\text{prime}, \text{Fibonacci-index})$
atoms. Its per-prime weight and the integer it encodes are the arithmetic carrier
of the ``register norm squared equals log-time'' bookkeeping.

\begin{definition}[Per-prime Fibonacci weight]
\label{def:oclsd-register-fib-weight}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDRegisterWeight.primeFibWeight}
For a prime $p$ the weight of a register $R$ is the sum of $F_k$ over the atoms of
$R$ whose prime equals $p$,
$$
w_p(R) \;=\; \sum_{(p,k)\in R} F_k,
$$
where $F_k$ is the $k$-th Fibonacci number. For a Zeckendorf register this reads
back the prime valuation $v_p$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Zeckendorf carry is an isometry of the register weight]
\label{thm:oclsd-register-carry-isometry}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDRegisterWeight.primeFibWeight\_carry}
Replacing two adjacent atoms $(p,k),(p,k+1)$ by $(p,k+2)$ preserves every per-prime
weight, because $F_k+F_{k+1}=F_{k+2}$. The register's per-prime Fibonacci content is
therefore carry-invariant: a well-defined valuation, not an artifact of the digit
representation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Encoded integer]
\label{def:oclsd-register-encoded-integer}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDRegisterWeight.encodedNat}
The integer a register encodes is
$$
n(R) \;=\; \prod_{(p,k)\in R} p^{F_k}.
$$
Grouped by prime this is $n(R)=\prod_p p^{w_p(R)}$, so the register norm squared
$\sum_{(p,k)} F_k\log p=\log n(R)$ is the log-time $T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The ledger is a submonoid of the integers]
\label{thm:oclsd-register-ledger-monoid}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDRegisterWeight.encodedNat\_carry}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDRegisterWeight.encodedNat\_append}
The Zeckendorf carry preserves $n(R)$, so the log-time is a carry-invariant of the
register. Register concatenation multiplies the encoded integer,
$n(R_1 \mathbin{+\!\!+} R_2)=n(R_1)\,n(R_2)$, so the encoding is a monoid homomorphism
from the ledger onto the multiplicative monoid of $S$-smooth integers; the log-time
is additive under concatenation.
\end{theorem}

\section{Finite reflection positivity as a PSD classification}

A single reversible tick carries a located transition weight $q$. Its
Osterwalder--Schrader reflection form, in the manifestly completed-square shape, is
$$
\Phi_q(f_0,f_1) \;=\; q\,(f_0+f_1)^2 \;+\; (1-2q)\,(f_0^2+f_1^2).
$$

\begin{definition}[Reflection form]
\label{def:oclsd-reflection-form}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDReflectionForm.osForm}
$\Phi_q$ is the two-state reversible reflection quadratic form. On the antisymmetric
mode $(1,-1)$ the first term vanishes and $\Phi_q(1,-1)=(1-2q)\cdot 2$: the reflection
form on that mode is exactly the small transition eigenvalue.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Reflection positivity classification]
\label{thm:oclsd-reflection-psd}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDReflectionForm.reflection\_holds}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDReflectionForm.reflection\_fails}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDReflectionForm.ratSq\_nonneg}
If $0\le q$ and $2q\le 1$ then $\Phi_q(f_0,f_1)\ge 0$ for every $f_0,f_1$ --- the
transition operator is positive semidefinite and the reflection form is a sum of two
nonnegative terms. If $2q>1$ then $\Phi_q(1,-1)<0$: an explicit indefinite
counterexample. Reflection positivity is thus a \emph{classification condition} on the
transition weight, discharged by the located square-nonnegativity fact $0\le x^2$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Reflection positivity of the OCLSD tick input}

The classification is parameterized by the actual OCLSD tick.

\begin{definition}[Tick reflection kit]
\label{def:oclsd-tick-reflection-kit}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDTickReflection.TickReflectionKit}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDTickReflection.tickReflectionForm}
A tick reflection kit assigns to each raw generation step $g$ a located transition
weight $q(g)$ with $0\le q(g)$; the tick's reflection form is $\Phi_{q(g)}$. The tick
is \emph{reflection-positive} when $2q(g)\le 1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Antisymmetric mode decides reconstructability]
\label{thm:oclsd-tick-reflection-characterization}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDTickReflection.tick\_reflection\_holds}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDTickReflection.tick\_reflection\_fails}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDTickReflection.reflectionPositive\_of\_flip\_nonneg}
A tick is reflection-positive if and only if its reflection form is nonnegative on the
antisymmetric mode $(1,-1)$. A reflection-positive tick has $\Phi_{q(g)}\ge 0$ on every
mode (a unitary Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction is available); a
reflection-anticorrelated tick is strictly negative on the antisymmetric mode (no such
reconstruction). A single located-rational check on one mode therefore decides the
tick's reconstructability.
\end{theorem}

\section{Carrier-relative Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction}

The reflection-positivity classification is the \emph{hypothesis} of the reconstruction theorem:
from a reflection-positive transition operator $P$ one builds a positive self-adjoint generator
$H=-\Delta T^{-1}\log P$ and the unitary flow $e^{itH}$. That passage is analytic ---
located operator logarithm, monotone spectral calculus, a GNS construction --- none of which the
finite located-rational layer builds. It is encoded honestly as a \emph{carrier contract}: the
analytic content is posited as an obligation, never faked.

\begin{definition}[Reconstruction carrier]
\label{def:oclsd-os-reconstruction-carrier}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OSReconstructionContract.OSReconstructionKit}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OSReconstructionContract.OSReconstruction}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OSReconstructionContract.OSReconstructionCarrier}
A reconstruction kit posits the analytic carrier: a located time axis, transfer/generator/unitary
operator types, the self-adjoint, nonnegative and unitary predicates, and the operations
$P\mapsto\log P$, $H\mapsto e^{-tH}$, $H\mapsto e^{itH}$. A reconstruction of a tick $g$ is a
generator $H$ with its self-adjointness, nonnegativity and unitary-flow certificates together
with the heat-kernel identity $e^{-\Delta T\cdot H}=P_g$ recovering the tick's transfer operator.
A carrier bundles the obligation as a map sending a reflection-positive tick to its reconstruction.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The finite check feeds the reconstruction]
\label{thm:oclsd-os-reconstruction-bridge}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OSReconstructionContract.theorem\_8\_4\_from\_finite\_RP}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OSReconstructionContract.theorem\_8\_4\_carrier\_relative}
Against any reconstruction carrier, a tick whose reflection form is $\ge 0$ on the antisymmetric
mode --- a single located-rational inequality --- admits an Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction.
The finite classification discharged above is exactly the hypothesis the carrier consumes. No
concrete carrier is supplied: the analytic construction remains an obligation, and the statement
``reflection positivity yields a reconstruction carrier'' is deliberately \emph{not} asserted, since
a degenerate all-trivial carrier would fake it. The junction is honest, not the reconstruction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{principle}[The classification closes; the reconstruction does not]
\label{prin:oclsd-reflection-boundary}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.OCLSDTickReflection.trivialKit\_reflectionPositive}
The reflection-positive tick class is inhabited over any dynamics type (the trivial
$q\equiv 0$ kit is reflection-positive), so the classification is not vacuous. What the
finite rational layer supplies is exactly the positivity gate: which tick inputs admit
a unitary reconstruction. What it does \emph{not} supply is the reconstruction itself
--- the passage from a positive transition operator to the located generator
$H=-\Delta T^{-1}\log P$ and its unitary group needs located operator logarithm and
monotone spectral calculus, which this layer does not build. That analytic step, and
the spectral zero identity of
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-free-zero-generation-initiality}, remain the walls; nothing here
constructs either.
\end{principle}
