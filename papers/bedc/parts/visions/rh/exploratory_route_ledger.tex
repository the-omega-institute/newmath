\chapter{Exploratory RH Route Ledger}
\label{ch:visions-rh-exploratory-route-ledger}
\origin{ai}

This chapter records the exploratory RH route developed in a 169-turn
working session.  The purpose is archival and diagnostic.  It separates
ideas that are already covered by existing BEDC formal surfaces from
heuristic geometry, conjectural bridge obligations, and full-strength
restatements.  It does not prove the Riemann Hypothesis, does not assert
proximity to a proof, and does not introduce Lean targets for philosophical
or analogy-driven material.

\section{Audit Convention}
\label{sec:rh-exploratory-ledger-audit-convention}

Each item below is assigned one of four statuses.

\begin{definition}[Route ledger status]
\label{def:rh-exploratory-route-ledger-status}
A route item is \emph{covered} when its formal kernel is already represented
by an existing RH chapter or by an existing
$\mathsf{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute}$ module.  It is \emph{heuristic} when it gives
geometric, physical, or ontological guidance without a present theorem.  It
is \emph{conditional} when it gives a valid implication from an explicit
bridge obligation to $\mathsf{RH}$ or to a zero-exclusion target.  It is
\emph{full-strength restatement} when the proposed bridge is, after
quantification strong enough to do work, another form of the
no-off-line-zero problem.
\end{definition}

The four statuses are intentionally not arranged as a progress scale.
``Covered'' does not mean that $\mathsf{RH}$ is proved; it means that the
particular small datum has already been formalized or recorded.  ``Heuristic''
does not mean false; it means that no certificate-bearing mathematical
statement is being claimed.  ``Conditional'' records an honest theorem shape
whose hypotheses remain open.  ``Full-strength restatement'' records a useful diagnostic
translation rather than a reduction of difficulty.

\section{Three Boundaries and the Prime-Unitary Surface}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-three-boundaries-prime-unitary}

The earliest stable correction in the exploration was the separation of
three objects:
$$
\operatorname{Fix}(J),
\qquad
\prod_s \mathsf{Decidable}(\mathsf{ZetaZero}(s)),
\qquad
\mathsf{RH}.
$$
The first is the geometric fixed surface of the functional-equation mirror
$$
J(s)=1-\overline{s}.
$$
The second is an exact zero decision operator.  The third is the global
rigidity statement that a certified critical-strip zero lands on the fixed
surface.

Status: covered.  The fixed-surface computation is the subject of
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-three-boundary-separation}, whose Lean-side kernel is
the module $\mathsf{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.FunctionalEquationSymmetry}$.  That
module verifies the fixed-surface calculation identifying $J$-fixedness with
the critical-line predicate.  The same chapter also records why exact zero
decidability is a different object from the RH proposition.

The prime-unitary readout was also stabilized.  For a displayed base
$a>1$,
$$
|a^{1/2-s}|=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Re(s)=1/2.
$$
For primes this is the centered Euler-channel readout
$U_p(s)=p^{1/2-s}$.  The point of the ledger is the negative boundary: this
calculation is a critical-line reparameterization, not an RH mechanism by
itself.  It applies for every displayed $a>1$ and says nothing about whether
$s$ carries a zeta-zero packet.

Status: covered.  The prime-local surface is recorded in
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-prime-unitary-balance-surface} and
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-three-boundary-separation}.  Its Lean-side carrier is
the existing $\mathsf{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.UnitaryBalance}$ module.  The
route becomes RH-facing only after a zero-ledger bridge supplies a reason why
an off-line zero cannot coexist with the displayed prime-channel defect.

\section{Located Process Geometry and Type Boundaries}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-located-process-geometry}

The session repeatedly used a geometric language in which a rational value
axis is distinguished from a process axis.  In BEDC terms, the strict content
is this: located real data are not bare rational points but approximation
processes with explicit moduli.  A rational value is the degenerate case with
a constant approximation and trivial modulus.  Comparing two located reals is
therefore comparison of two approximation processes to a displayed tolerance,
not an unrestricted trichotomy of exact completed points.

Status: heuristic as imagery, covered as infrastructure.  The language of
orthogonal axes, shadows, and process directions is only a visualization.
The certificate content is the ordinary BEDC discipline already used by the
located real and zeta-box modules: finite tolerance, apartness, and explicit
moduli stay inside the constructive fragment; exact global comparison and
unrestricted point extraction belong to a separate boundary.

The same discussion produced a useful caution about
$\mathsf{Classical.choice}$, $\mathsf{Quot.sound}$, and
$\mathsf{propext}$.  The honest statement is not that classical mathematics
is erroneous, nor that these names are proved necessary for zeta.  The
statement is only that such constants measure implementation footprints for
particular classical encodings: choice extracts data from existence,
quotients identify processes as points, and propositional extensionality
collapses proof distinctions.  BEDC avoids those collapses in the kernel.

Status: heuristic and governance-facing.  The transcript's strongest
versions of this point are philosophy of implementation, not RH lemmas.  No
Lean target is attached to them here.

\section{Common Carrier and Zeta as a Projection Chain}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-common-carrier-projection-chain}

The middle part of the session organized triangles, circles, elliptic
normalizations, Fourier and Laplace transforms, and zeta as a chain of
compatible projections rather than as a literal holomorphic continuation
from one object to the next.  The valid BEDC reading is:
$$
\text{shared carrier rows}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\text{compatible algebraic, geometric, spectral, and analytic readouts}.
$$
For zeta this means that the Dirichlet series, Euler product, eta row,
analytic continuation, archimedean completion, functional equation, pole
ledger, zero ledger, and branch-transport rows are compatible readings of a
recovered carrier.

Status: covered.  The recovered-carrier discipline is already recorded in
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-prime-unitary-balance-surface},
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-zero-skew-eventflow-target}, and
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-zeta-self-similar-recovered-carrier}.  The taxonomy
side is represented by the existing
$\mathsf{BEDC.Derived.RHRoute.ZetaLikeProjectionTaxonomy}$ module.  This
does not imply that properties of a triangle, a circle, a finite elliptic
model, or a Fourier transform transfer automatically to Riemann zeta.  Such
transfer requires displayed carrier maps, overlap laws, and tail or
continuation estimates.

The finite quartic spectral-curve example was used as a guide for what a
closed finite model looks like: recurrence data, spectral polynomial,
normalization, projection, dominant branch, and endpoint zero law are all in
the same finite ledger.  The honest lesson for RH is negative as much as
positive.  Riemann $\xi$ is an order-one entire transcendental function with
infinitely many zeros; a finite algebraic branch argument does not transfer
through the finite-to-infinite boundary without a separate convergence,
dominance, or positivity theorem.

Status: heuristic for RH, useful as finite-model discipline.  It is not a
proof template that can be applied by analogy alone.

\section{Object Induction, Three Invariants, and No-Split Zeros}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-object-induction-three-invariants}

Another arc described a triad of object-level invariants:
observation/time, distinction/prime locality, and symmetry/transport.  In
BEDC vocabulary this can be read as a route through histories and
continuation, gap or apartness data, and $\hsame$-respecting transport.  The
proposed RH reading was that a zero fiber should have no additional
same-layer normal degree of freedom.

Status: covered as a conditional route, heuristic as philosophy.  Existing
modules already record the usable conditional kernel:
$\mathsf{ObjectRealityInductionRoute}$ packages the object-induction route;
$\mathsf{ZeroGenerationInitiality}$ packages the initiality route; and
$\mathsf{ZeroSignatureFaithfulness}$ records signature-faithfulness
obligations.  The paper-side references are
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-free-zero-generation-initiality} and the neighboring
zero-generation chapters.

The missing theorem is not a slogan about there being only three
invariants.  It is a zero-fiber exactness theorem.  One possible form says:
every accepted critical-strip zero packet is generated by pre-RH
constructors, and those constructors preserve the fixed-half subcarrier.
Another possible form says: a mirror-split off-line zero would expose a
defect row that the completed carrier cannot accept.  Both are conditional
bridges; neither is currently closed for Riemann zeta.

Status: conditional.  The session's ``pigeonhole'' language is best read as
classifier faithfulness.  It is not a proof that two mirror-related off-line
zeros must be the same point.

\section{Finite Prime Windows, Odd Defects, and Even Energy}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-prime-window-energy}

The most concrete non-analytic core of the session was the normal-defect
calculation.  Write
$$
s=\frac12+\delta+i\tau.
$$
Then each centered prime channel satisfies
$$
|p^{1/2-s}|=p^{-\delta}.
$$
Consequently a nonzero $\delta$ produces a coherent amplitude defect in
every displayed nonempty prime window.  A mirror pair sends
$\delta$ to $-\delta$; signed first-order defect can cancel between the two
mirror reads, but the even energy $\delta^2$ cannot be removed by that sign
change.

Status: covered as finite readout.  The window and defect apparatus is
already recorded in \autoref{ch:visions-rh-finite-prime-window-gluing},
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-prime-skew-boundary-defect-refutation}, and
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-prime-window-recursive-tower}.  Existing Lean modules
cover the finite machinery:
$\mathsf{FinitePrimeWindow}$, $\mathsf{PrimeSkewDefect}$,
$\mathsf{OddZeroDefect}$, $\mathsf{EvenDefectEnergy}$, and
$\mathsf{FiniteToInfinitePhaseEnergyTower}$.

The finite conclusion is not RH.  It says that off-line bias is locally
visible.  The missing analytic carrier theorem is
$$
\mathsf{ZetaZero}(s)
\times
\mathsf{PositiveNormalDefect}(s)
\to
\bot,
$$
or an equivalent completed-carrier exclusion.  This is the same hinge
isolated in the local-global exclusion chapters.  It is recorded here as
conditional, not as a checked theorem.

\section{Far-End, Solenoid, and Lee--Yang Readings}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-far-end-solenoid-lee-yang}

The far-end discussion produced two separate items that must not be
confused.  First, it gave a useful discipline: the infinite end is not a
point, a warehouse, or an untyped place where a finite defect can disappear.
Second, when expressed as ``no far-end absorption for zeta,'' it becomes a
restatement of the no-off-line-zero problem unless an independent positivity
or domination theorem is supplied.

Status: full-strength restatement when quantified strongly enough for zeta.  The
existing $\mathsf{OnticBoundaryLedger}$ module already records this boundary
as a ledger entry rather than a proof.  The far-end unity and energy socket
modules record finite normalization interfaces, but they do not discharge
the zeta-specific zero-exclusion theorem.

The solenoid and Lee--Yang turn was more precise.  For a finite prime set
$S$, an $S$-localized solenoid has a visible circle and a compact
$p$-adic fiber.  A finite Lee--Yang stable resonance can force its zeros to
land on the unit boundary.  In the $s$-coordinate, a local factor of the
form
$$
z=e^{-(s-1/2)H},
\qquad H>0,
$$
lands on the unit circle only when $\Re(s)=1/2$.

Status: conditional.  Finite solenoid or Lee--Yang mechanisms are genuine
mathematical structures, and existing RHRoute ledgers contain finite
solenoid-bound and source-gap records.  The missing bridge is to show that
Riemann $\xi$ admits a zero-relevant solenoid Lee--Yang capture:
on each off-line compact region, a stable solenoid main term dominates a
tail term, or a sequence of finite Lee--Yang stable approximants converges
to $\Xi$.  Without that bridge, the mechanism does not prove RH.

\section{Dyadic Truncations, Jensen, Hilbert--Polya, and Weil Positivity}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-classical-route-audit}

The final technical arc audited several standard route families.
All of them can be made honest by expressing their bridge obligations
directly.

\begin{principle}[Dyadic Laguerre--Polya bridge]
\label{prin:rh-ledger-dyadic-lp-bridge}
If a family of completed dyadic truncations $\Xi_K$ converges locally
uniformly to $\Xi$ and every $\Xi_K$ belongs to the
Laguerre--Polya class, then Hurwitz-type reasoning gives real zeros for
$\Xi$ and hence RH.
\end{principle}

Status: bridge sound, premise false for the sharp truncation route recorded
in this paper.  \autoref{ch:visions-rh-dyadic-truncation-lp-route-refutation}
records why the tempting all-$K$ sharp-cutoff Laguerre--Polya premise does
not hold.  Thus the dyadic arc is preserved as a negative route ledger, not
as a pending proof.

\begin{principle}[Jensen and Laguerre--Polya audit]
\label{prin:rh-ledger-jensen-lp-audit}
Showing all Jensen polynomials attached to $\Xi$ are hyperbolic is a
classical Laguerre--Polya route to RH.  Fixed-degree high-shift
hyperbolicity is informative, but the all-degree all-shift family is the
hard object.
\end{principle}

Status: full-strength restatement as a global criterion.  It is not a BEDC
shortcut.

\begin{principle}[Hilbert--Polya and spectral determinant audit]
\label{prin:rh-ledger-hilbert-polya-audit}
A self-adjoint or unitary spectral realization whose determinant is
Riemann $\xi$ would force the required real-line or unit-circle zero
placement.  Finite CMV, Jacobi, and determinant interfaces demonstrate the
shape of such mechanisms but do not construct the required infinite
operator for $\xi$.
\end{principle}

Status: conditional.  The finite spectral interface is useful; the
determinant-equals-$\xi$ theorem is not present.

\begin{principle}[Weil and Li positivity audit]
\label{prin:rh-ledger-weil-li-audit}
Weil positivity and Li coefficient positivity are faithful RH criteria.
They are valuable because they identify the correct positive form or
coefficient sequence.  They become proof mechanisms only when the positivity
is derived from an independent structure rather than assumed in a form
coextensive with the fixed RH target.
\end{principle}

Status: full-strength restatement as global positivity; potentially useful as a
target if an independent semi-local domination theorem is produced.  The
last turns of the session therefore correctly moved toward semi-local Weil
positivity, finite matrix criteria, Birman--Schwinger bounds, and
low/high-frequency estimates.  Those are bridge obligations, not checked
results in this paper.

\section{Lean Decision}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-lean-decision}

No Lean module accompanies this chapter.  The session contains many
phrases that can be given formal-looking names, but the only parts that are
currently suitable for 0-axiom BEDC formalization are already represented by
existing RHRoute modules: the functional-equation fixed surface, the
prime-unitary readout, finite prime-window gluing, odd and even defect
rows, object-induction and zero-generation conditional routes, route-closure
separation, zeta projection taxonomy, and finite solenoid-bound ledgers.

The remaining items require one of the following before they become Lean
targets:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\text{a zeta-specific defect-zero incompatibility theorem},\\
\text{a zero-presentation exactness theorem for a pre-RH generator},\\
\text{a solenoid Lee--Yang capture theorem for }\Xi,\\
\text{a full Laguerre--Polya or Jensen hyperbolicity proof},\\
\text{an independent Weil or Li positivity theorem},\\
\text{or a spectral realization whose determinant is }\xi.
\end{gathered}
$$
None of these is available in the repository as a closed 0-axiom theorem.
They are therefore recorded as route obligations and not as Lean stubs.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:rh-ledger-summary}

The session is valuable as a route ledger because it repeatedly found the
same boundary from different directions.  The critical line is already the
functional-equation fixed surface.  Prime-unitary balance and finite windows
read that surface cleanly, but do not by themselves carry a zeta-zero
exclusion.  Geometric language about common carriers, process axes,
far-ends, resonances, and no-hair principles is useful only when translated
into displayed carrier maps, stability, positivity, or tail domination.
When such translation is strong enough to rule out off-line zeros, it is
exactly where the RH difficulty lives.

Thus the honest current state is:
$$
\boxed{
\text{finite and conditional RHRoute kernels are already recorded;}
}
$$
$$
\boxed{
\text{the session contributes no separate 0-axiom theorem beyond existing coverage;}
}
$$
$$
\boxed{
\text{the unresolved work is analytic rigidity, not terminology.}
}
$$
