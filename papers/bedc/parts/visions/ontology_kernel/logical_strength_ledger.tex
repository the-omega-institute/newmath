\chapter{Logical Strength Ledger of the Kernel Layers}
\label{ch:visions-ontology-kernel-logical-strength-ledger}
\origin{ai}

The generating acts of \autoref{ch:visions-ontology-kernel-categorical-generation-spine}
are not uniform in logical strength.  The arithmetic and algebraic base ---
term generation, relation quotient, and localization --- is finitistic and
constructively innocent.  The completion, inverse-limit, spectrum, and
globalization acts import genuine non-constructive strength, and it is exactly
there that choice fragments and impredicative comprehension enter.  This chapter
grades each layer against standard proof-theoretic and reverse-mathematical
benchmarks and pins the two places where the kernel's own zero-choice discipline
is crossed.  The ledger is a strength survey; it is not a proof that any layer
is dispensable, and it does not certify any layer as constructive beyond the
benchmark cited.

\label{sec:ontology-kernel-strength-stratification}

produces, a strength benchmark sufficient to develop it, and its constructive
status.  Benchmarks name the weakest standard system in which the stated
development is routine; a heavier consumer of the same object may need more.

\begin{principle}[The base is choice-free and finitistic]
\label{prin:ontology-kernel-base-finitistic}
Everything through the localization layer --- and therefore the full arithmetic
payload of the split-prime law $P=a^2+b^2\iff P\equiv1\pmod4$, unique
factorization, and the Godel and Zeckendorf codings --- is developed with
bounded induction and totality of exponentiation.  No completed infinite object,
no impredicative comprehension, and no choice principle is used.  This is the
part of the kernel that matches a strictly constructive reading.
\end{principle}

\section{The two choice red-points}
\label{sec:ontology-kernel-choice-red-points}

Two later acts are not merely infinitary; they are equivalent to fragments of
choice, which the kernel's zero-choice discipline forbids.

\begin{principle}[Spectrum existence is a prime-ideal choice principle]
\label{prin:ontology-kernel-spectrum-choice}
For an arbitrary nonzero commutative ring, the statement that a prime ideal
exists --- so that $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is nonempty --- is equivalent to the
Boolean prime ideal theorem.  Strengthening ``prime'' to ``maximal'' raises the
equivalence to the full axiom of choice.  The spectrum functor of the ontology
kernel therefore silently uses a choice fragment on general rings.  The rings
that actually occur in the arithmetic spine --- $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Z[i]$,
finite fields $\mathbb F_P$, and $\mathbb Z_p$ --- have explicitly exhibited
primes and need no choice; the cost appears only when the functor is asked to
range over all rings.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Global compactness is Tychonoff-strength]
\label{prin:ontology-kernel-global-compactness}
The finite-window to global passage uses compactness through the finite
intersection property.  For the specific profinite spaces of the arithmetic
tower, such as $\mathbb Z_p$ and Cantor-type symbol spaces, this sits at the
$\mathrm{WKL}_0$ level.  For an arbitrary indexed family the compactness of the
product is Tychonoff's theorem, which is equivalent to the axiom of choice.  A
globalization act stated for arbitrary families therefore also crosses the
zero-choice line.
\end{principle}

These two points are the precise locations where a faithful low-strength reading
of the kernel must either restrict the objects --- to rings with exhibited
primes and to profinite or otherwise effectively compact spaces --- or else
declare the imported choice fragment.  The escape-recording discipline for the
non-compact case is the finite-window principle of
\autoref{ch:visions-rh-fixed-window-dominance-certificate}: a conclusion proved
inside a fixed window may not be upgraded to a growing-window conclusion without
a separate compactness or tail certificate.

\section{The universe caveat}
\label{sec:ontology-kernel-universe-caveat}

A last cost is not about any single object but about the meta-level collection.

\begin{principle}[``Category of all $X$'' wants a universe]
\label{prin:ontology-kernel-universe-caveat}
The object-level constructions --- a given free algebra, quotient,
localization, completion, or spectrum --- are innocent for a standard set
theory.  But the categorical spine's ambient categories, ``all
$\Sigma$-algebras'', ``all commutative rings'', ``all finite-dimensional
representations'', are proper classes.  Making the spine fully rigorous as a
category of everything wants a Grothendieck universe, which is a mild large-set
assumption beyond plain $\mathrm{ZFC}$, or a two-sorted class theory.  The
generative reading avoids this only by staying with set-indexed or small
diagrams; the moment one quantifies over the category of all interpretations,
the meta-level strength rises.
\end{principle}

\section{Reading of the ledger}
\label{sec:ontology-kernel-ledger-reading}

The ledger locates a single boundary.  Below the completion layer the kernel is
finitistic and choice-free, and its arithmetic-geometric payload --- culminating
in $a^2+b^2$ as the minimal split-prime classifier --- lives entirely in that
region.  At and above completion the kernel begins to consume completed infinite
objects; at the spectrum and globalization acts it consumes choice fragments
outright; and at the meta-level of ``all interpretations'' it consumes a
universe assumption.  A kernel that advertises a zero-choice, constructive
discipline is faithful to that discipline exactly on the base, and the ledger
names where and by how much it departs.

\begin{principle}[The ledger is descriptive, not eliminative]
\label{prin:ontology-kernel-ledger-descriptive}
Recording that a layer needs the Boolean prime ideal theorem or Tychonoff's
theorem does not show that the corresponding object could be rebuilt without it,
nor that it should be removed.  The ledger reports strength; it neither lowers
nor licenses it.  Any claim that a red-point object admits a genuinely
choice-free construction on a restricted domain must be proved separately, on
that domain, with the restriction displayed.
\end{principle}
