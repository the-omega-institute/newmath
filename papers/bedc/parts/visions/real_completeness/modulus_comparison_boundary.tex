\section{Modulus comparison boundary}
\label{sec:real-completeness-modulus-comparison-boundary}

The located-real comparison surface is not a comparison of two completed
host points.  It is a comparison of two presented processes through finite
windows selected by explicit moduli.  This point is already visible in the
Lean-side shape of $\mathsf{LReal}$: a located real carries a rational
sequence, a monotone modulus, and a Cauchy witness.  The pointwise relation
$\mathsf{lrLe}$ is deliberately stronger and should not be read as the
general comparison interface.  The finite comparison interface is instead
the pair formed by $\mathsf{LRealClose}$ and $\mathsf{lrApart}$.

\begin{definition}[Modulated located real]
\label{def:real-completeness-modulated-located-real}
Fix a rational metric kit $K$ with rational closeness, apartness, and order
rows.  A modulated located real is a triple
$$
x=(x_{\mathrm{seq}},x_{\mathrm{mod}},x_{\mathrm{cau}})
$$
where $x_{\mathrm{seq}}:\NatUp\to\RatUp$,
$x_{\mathrm{mod}}:\NatUp\to\NatUp$, and
$$
x_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)\le m\wedge x_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)\le n
\Longrightarrow
K.\mathsf{close}(x_{\mathrm{seq}}(m),x_{\mathrm{seq}}(n),k).
$$
The rational embedding is the degenerate case
$$
\eta(q)_{\mathrm{seq}}(n)=q,
\qquad
\eta(q)_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)=0.
$$
Thus a rational comparison is a comparison between a stationary process and
the other displayed process.  A comparison between two non-rational
presentations is a comparison between two nontrivial moduli.
\end{definition}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.LocatedReal.LReal}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.LocatedReal.lrLe}

\begin{definition}[Finite tolerance comparison]
\label{def:real-completeness-finite-tolerance-comparison}
For located reals $a,b$ and a tolerance $k$, the finite tolerance relation
is a comparison datum $D$ with its own modulus
$D_{\mathrm{mod}}:\NatUp\to\NatUp$ such that
$$
D_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)\le m\wedge D_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)\le n
\Longrightarrow
K.\mathsf{close}(a_{\mathrm{seq}}(m),b_{\mathrm{seq}}(n),k).
$$
This is the role of $\mathsf{LRealEqData}$ and
$\mathsf{LRealClose}$.  The comparison data may use a common schedule
separate from the two endpoint moduli, but it still inspects only finite
rational windows.
\end{definition}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.LocatedReal.LRealEqData}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.LocatedReal.LRealClose}

\begin{definition}[Finite apartness window]
\label{def:real-completeness-finite-apartness-window}
The apartness surface for two located reals is the existence of a finite
precision $k$ such that the two endpoint moduli already expose a rational
separation:
$$
\exists k:\NatUp,\,
K.\mathsf{apart}
\bigl(a_{\mathrm{seq}}(a_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)),
b_{\mathrm{seq}}(b_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)),k\bigr).
$$
This is the role of $\mathsf{lrApart}$.  The witness is a finite separation
window, not a decision that every pair of located reals has already been
classified as left, equal, or right.
\end{definition}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.LocatedReal.lrApart}

\begin{theorem}[Finite comparison is process-level]
\label{thm:real-completeness-finite-comparison-process-level}
Every finite tolerance comparison or apartness witness between located reals
factors through the two sequences, their moduli, a comparison modulus when
present, and rational-level close or apartness rows.  It does not require a
host real point or quotient representative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For finite tolerance, unfold
\autoref{def:real-completeness-finite-tolerance-comparison}.  A tolerance
request $k$ selects finite thresholds through $D_{\mathrm{mod}}$, and the
only numerical read is the rational comparison
$K.\mathsf{close}(a_{\mathrm{seq}}(m),b_{\mathrm{seq}}(n),k)$.

For apartness, unfold
\autoref{def:real-completeness-finite-apartness-window}.  The witness
selects one finite precision $k$ and reads the two rational observations
picked by $a_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)$ and $b_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)$.  The only
separation claim is the displayed rational apartness row at that precision.
Neither construction asks for a completed host point, a quotient stream, or
a selected representative outside the visible moduli.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison moduli compose]
\label{thm:real-completeness-comparison-moduli-compose}
If $a$ is finitely close to $b$ at tolerance $k+1$, and $b$ is finitely
close to $c$ at tolerance $k+1$, then $a$ is finitely close to $c$ at
tolerance $k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $D_{ab}$ and $D_{bc}$ be the two comparison data.  Use the common
comparison modulus
$$
j\longmapsto
\max(D_{ab,\mathrm{mod}}(j+1),D_{bc,\mathrm{mod}}(j+1)).
$$
At this shared finite threshold, the first datum gives
$K.\mathsf{close}(a,b,j+1)$ and the second gives
$K.\mathsf{close}(b,c,j+1)$ at the corresponding rational observations.
The rational triangle row in $K$ composes the two tolerance-$j+1$ comparisons
into a tolerance-$j$ comparison from $a$ to $c$.  This is the proof pattern
implemented by $\mathsf{LRealClose\_triangle}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Exact comparison boundary]
\label{def:real-completeness-exact-comparison-boundary}
An exact comparison operator for arbitrary located reals is a total operation
of the form
$$
\prod_{x,y}
\bigl(x<y\bigr)
+
\bigl(x=y\bigr)
+
\bigl(y<x\bigr),
$$
where equality is full located-real equality and the strict branches are
point-level order branches, not finite tolerance or apartness windows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact comparison has omniscience strength]
\label{thm:real-completeness-exact-comparison-omniscience}
A total operator of
\autoref{def:real-completeness-exact-comparison-boundary}, supplied for
arbitrary located-real presentations, would decide the limited principle of
omniscience for binary streams.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Given a binary stream $\alpha:\NatUp\to\{0,1\}$, form the nonnegative
located real presentation
$$
x_\alpha=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha(n)2^{-(n+1)}
$$
by its rational partial sums and the usual geometric tail modulus.  Apply
the exact comparison operator to $x_\alpha$ and $0$.

The branch $x_\alpha<0$ is incompatible with the nonnegative construction.
If $x_\alpha=0$, then no coefficient can be $1$, since any displayed
$\alpha(n)=1$ supplies a positive dyadic lower bound.  Hence
$\forall n,\alpha(n)=0$.  If $0<x_\alpha$, the strict branch supplies a
positive separation threshold; inspecting the finite prefix needed by that
threshold yields some $n$ with $\alpha(n)=1$.  The exact comparison operator
therefore returns
$$
\bigl(\forall n,\alpha(n)=0\bigr)
+
\bigl(\exists n,\alpha(n)=1\bigr).
$$
This is $\mathsf{LPO}$ for binary streams.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Located comparison is not trichotomy]
\label{cor:real-completeness-located-comparison-not-trichotomy}
The constructive comparison surface of $\LocatedRealUp$ consists of finite
tolerance comparison, finite apartness witnesses, comparison-modulus
composition, and Real-seal readback through displayed rows.  It does not
export arbitrary exact trichotomy.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \autoref{thm:real-completeness-finite-comparison-process-level}, the
constructive comparison surface remains at the level of finite observations
and explicit moduli.  By
\autoref{thm:real-completeness-exact-comparison-omniscience}, a total exact
trichotomy operator for arbitrary located-real presentations would decide
$\mathsf{LPO}$.  Such an operator is therefore outside the axiom-pure
finite-window comparison interface recorded in this chapter.
\end{proof}
