\chapter{Cataphatic Process Names and Apophatic Far Ends}
\label{ch:visions-apophatic-cataphatic-process-far-end}
\origin{ai}

This chapter separates two boundary readings that are easy to confuse. A
located irrational real is not a rational point on the value axis, but it is
still a positively displayed process-name: it is carried by a concrete
generation axis with a stream, modulus, ledger, classifier, and provenance
surface. The apophatic far end $\mathsf{T}$, also written
$\infty_{\mathrm{apo}}$ or $\Omega$ when the boundary image is emphasized, has
the opposite status. It is not a further process-name, not a point at the end
of a process, and not a special real. It is the shared non-local far-end role
of admissible finite-readout fibers.

The distinction is not between constructible and nonconstructible objects. It
is between a cataphatic process-name that remains localized on a displayed
axis and an apophatic socket that may be named only by refusal of
internalization. The located-real packets of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-real-namecert} and the
irrational-real packets of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-irrational-real-namecert}
belong to the first side. The far-end socket packets of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-apophaticfarendsocket-namecert} and the fiber
far-end packets of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-apophaticfiberfarend-namecert}
belong to the second side.

\section{Value axes and generation axes}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-value-generation-axes}

The positive readout of a constructive real may pass through a value surface
such as $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, or a finite table of observation rows.
Such a surface is a value axis: it is where finite comparisons, intervals,
readbacks, and classifier images are inspected. A value axis is not by itself
the complete process that produces a real-name packet.

\begin{definition}[Generation axis]
\label{def:cataphatic-process-generation-axis}
A \emph{generation axis} $Z_\alpha$ is a localized process surface indexed by
$\alpha$ and displayed as
$$
Z_\alpha :=
(\mathsf{source}_\alpha,
\mathsf{schedule}_\alpha,
\mathsf{modulus}_\alpha,
\mathsf{ledger}_\alpha,
\mathsf{classifier}_\alpha).
$$
The index $\alpha$ records which concrete process is being read. The entries
are paper-side names for BEDC rows: source data, finite read schedules,
progress or modulus data, provenance and refusal ledgers, and classifier
output.
\end{definition}

The point of the notation is not to add a host object named $Z_\alpha$. It is
to mark the axis on which a packet is positively generated. A located real
uses a value axis for readout, but its certificate is carried by a generation
axis.

\begin{definition}[Cataphatic process-name]
\label{def:cataphatic-process-name}
A term $r$ is a \emph{cataphatic process-name} when there exists a generation
axis $Z_\alpha$ such that every legitimate read of $r$ factors through the
displayed rows of $Z_\alpha$. Schematically,
$$
r \rightsquigarrow
(\mathsf{approximation}_\alpha,
\mathsf{modulus}_\alpha,
\mathsf{regularity}_\alpha,
\mathsf{classifier}_\alpha,
\mathsf{ledger}_\alpha).
$$
The name is cataphatic because the process supplies positive data: readbacks,
budgets, regularity or locatedness certificates, classifier rows, and
provenance.
\end{definition}

For $\LocatedRealUp$, the carrier is exactly of this form. Its public surface
contains a regular rational source, dyadic interval rows, a located enclosure,
a $\RealUp$ seal, transport rows, replay rows, package provenance, and a local
\NameCert{} row. It contains no Dedekind cut, quotient-real carrier,
choice-selected representative, host completion point, or trichotomy
decision. Hence it is positive without being a host point.

\section{Located irrationality}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-located-irrationality}

The irrationality of a located real is a refusal of rational value-axis
collapse. It is not a refusal of positive generation.

\begin{definition}[Located irrational process-name]
\label{def:located-irrational-process-name}
A \emph{located irrational process-name} is a paper-side predicate
$$
\mathsf{IrrLocatedReal}(r)
:\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{LocatedRealCarrier}(r)
\wedge
\neg\exists q:\RatUp,\ r\sim_{\RealUp} q.
$$
It says that $r$ is carried by a located-real surface while no displayed
rational candidate is certified as the same $\RealUp$ value.
\end{definition}

The negated existential only blocks the rational-point reading. It does not
remove the stream, modulus, dyadic ledger, regularity row, or classifier. In
the BEDC reading, an irrational real is therefore not a mystery point beyond
construction. It is a process-name whose rational value-axis collapse is
refused.

\begin{theorem}[Located irrationality remains localized]
\label{thm:located-irrationality-remains-localized}
If $\mathsf{IrrLocatedReal}(r)$ holds, then $r$ is carried by some generation
axis:
$$
\mathsf{IrrLocatedReal}(r)
\Longrightarrow
\exists \alpha,\ \mathrm{Carry}(Z_\alpha,r).
$$
The conclusion does not assert that $r$ is a rational value-axis point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The first conjunct of \autoref{def:located-irrational-process-name} states
that $r$ is a located-real carrier. By
\autoref{def:cataphatic-process-name}, any legitimate located-real read
factors through a displayed process surface consisting of source, schedule,
modulus, ledger, and classifier rows. This supplies a generation axis
$Z_\alpha$. The second conjunct only refuses a rational candidate $q:\RatUp$
with $r\sim_{\RealUp}q$. It does not delete the displayed located-real
surface. Thus located irrationality remains localized.
\end{proof}

\section{The apophatic far-end role}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-role}

The far-end role has a different grammar. It is not carried by a generation
axis. It is the common boundary name for the far side of all admissible
finite-readout fibers.

\begin{definition}[Apophatic far-end role]
\label{def:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-role}
An \emph{apophatic far-end role} is a boundary name $\mathsf{T}$ satisfying
the paper-side schema
$$
\forall \alpha,\quad
\mathrm{FarEnd}(Z_\alpha)\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}\mathsf{T}.
$$
The relation $\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}$ is apophatic sameness. It records a
shared boundary role without introducing object-level equality, kernel data,
or a carrier element.
\end{definition}

The role is therefore defined together with its refusals. The following
writings are not alternate encodings of the same idea; they are exactly the
collapses the far-end discipline bars:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{refuse }&\mathsf{T}:\mathsf{Hist},\\
\text{refuse }&\mathsf{T}:\mathsf{State},\\
\text{refuse }&\mathsf{T}:X,\\
\text{refuse }&\mathsf{T}\in X,\\
\text{refuse }&f(\mathsf{T}),\\
\text{refuse }&\mathrm{FarEnd}(Z_\alpha)=\mathsf{T}.
\end{aligned}
$$

\begin{definition}[Process/far-end non-collapse]
\label{def:cataphatic-process-non-collapse}
For a generation axis $Z_\alpha$ and the apophatic far-end role
$\mathsf{T}$, the condition
$\mathsf{NoCollapse}(Z_\alpha,\mathsf{T})$ means:
\begin{enumerate}
\item objects carried by $Z_\alpha$ are read only through source, schedule,
modulus, ledger, and classifier rows;
\item $\mathsf{T}$ is marked only through apophatic far-end sameness and
non-internalization rows;
\item no operation casts $\mathsf{T}$ into a $\mathsf{Hist}$, state, value
axis element, function argument, or object-level equality target.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This is the common discipline behind the far-end diagrammatics of
\autoref{ch:visions-apophatic-far-end-diagrammatics}. Boundary arrows may
point toward $\mathsf{T}$, but they are not functions whose codomain contains
$\mathsf{T}$ as an element.

\section{Level separation}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-level-separation}

\begin{theorem}[Cataphatic process and apophatic far end are level-separated]
\label{thm:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-level-separation}
Let $r$ be a located irrational process-name and let $\mathsf{T}$ be the
apophatic far-end role of \autoref{def:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-role}.
Then
$$
\mathsf{IrrLocatedReal}(r)
\Longrightarrow
\exists \alpha,\ \mathrm{Carry}(Z_\alpha,r),
$$
while
$$
\neg\exists \alpha,\ \mathsf{T}\in Z_\alpha.
$$
The only admissible relation between the two levels is
$$
\mathrm{FarEnd}(Z_\alpha)\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}\mathsf{T}.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The first display is \autoref{thm:located-irrationality-remains-localized}.
The located irrational has a positive carrier even though it refuses rational
candidate collapse.

For $\mathsf{T}$, use \autoref{def:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-role}
and \autoref{def:cataphatic-process-non-collapse}. If
$\mathsf{T}\in Z_\alpha$, then $\mathsf{T}$ is readable as a coordinate of a
generation axis. It could then be consumed by the axis classifier, transported
by the axis ledger, passed to a function, or equated with the result of a
far-end operation. Each reading is one of the refused internalizations. Hence
$\mathsf{T}$ occupies no generation axis. The schema
$\mathrm{FarEnd}(Z_\alpha)\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}\mathsf{T}$ remains legitimate
because it is boundary sameness rather than object membership.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Apophatic infinity is not a located real]
\label{cor:apophatic-infinity-not-located-real}
The boundary name $\infty_{\mathrm{apo}}$ is not another irrational real and
not a special $\LocatedRealUp$ packet:
$$
\neg\exists \alpha,\ \infty_{\mathrm{apo}}\in Z_\alpha.
$$
The permitted reading is only
$$
\forall \alpha,\quad
\mathrm{FarEnd}(Z_\alpha)\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}\infty_{\mathrm{apo}}.
$$
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By \autoref{thm:hash-like-apophatic-infinity-is-T},
$\infty_{\mathrm{apo}}$ is the $\mathsf{T}$-far-end reading of finite readout
fibers. Applying \autoref{thm:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-level-separation}
to that reading gives the claim. The conclusion is not a statement about the
size or completion of a value axis; it is a non-internalization statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The far end is a socket, not an element]
\label{cor:far-end-socket-not-element}
$\mathsf{T}$ may occur as an apophatic socket, boundary row, refusal marker,
or far-end name. It may not occur as an element to be quantified over, passed
to a function, compared by an internal relation, or equated with a kernel
term.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
\autoref{thm:apophatic-far-end-socket-nonescape} states that an accepted
$\ApophaticFarEndSocketUp$ carrier exports only displayed rows and no kernel
object named $\mathsf{T}$. The present corollary is the vision-level reading
of that non-escape theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Compactification points}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-compactification-points}

Classical mathematics often makes infinity available by adding a point at
infinity, compactifying a space, or completing a carrier. BEDC can discuss
such moves as external or bridged constructions, but they are not the
apophatic far-end role.

\begin{principle}[Compactification points are external bridge objects]
\label{prin:compactification-points-external-bridge-objects}
If a theory introduces an internal object $\infty:X$ and permits
$$
f(\infty),\qquad
\infty\in X,\qquad
R(\infty,x),\qquad
\infty=\lim_n x_n,
$$
then that object is a compactification or completion datum, not BEDC's
apophatic far-end role.
\end{principle}

The difference is structural. A compactification point is admitted into a
carrier and then becomes available to eliminators, relations, and functions.
The apophatic far end is named precisely where such availability is refused.
Thus a point at infinity may be studied by a separate bridge, but it cannot be
identified with $\mathsf{T}$ without collapsing the socket into an element.

\section{Axiom-purity boundary}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-axiom-purity-boundary}

The separation above gives a compact reading of the local axiom-purity
boundary. There are two refusals, each aimed at a different kind of collapse.

\begin{principle}[Two refusals at the process/far-end boundary]
\label{prin:two-refusals-process-far-end-boundary}
At this boundary, BEDC axiom-purity requires:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for cataphatic process-names, refusal to collapse a displayed
generation axis $Z_\alpha$ into a host completion point or quotient
representative;
\item for the apophatic far-end role, refusal to internalize $\mathsf{T}$ as
an object, state, global frame, function argument, or equality target.
\end{enumerate}
\end{principle}

\begin{theorem}[Process/far-end separation preserves axiom purity]
\label{thm:process-far-end-separation-preserves-axiom-purity}
Suppose every located-real or irrational-real read factors through its
displayed process rows, and every far-end occurrence is recorded only through
apophatic sameness and non-internalization rows. Then the separation between
cataphatic process-names and the apophatic far-end role does not add an axiom
to the BEDC substrate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The process-name side uses only displayed carrier, classifier, modulus,
ledger, transport, replay, package, and local naming rows. It does not
consume a host real, quotient representative, choice function, or completion
point. The far-end side uses only boundary names and refusal rows. It does not
consume $\mathsf{T}$ as a kernel object, state, $\mathsf{Hist}$ constructor,
global frame, or equality witness. Thus neither side supplies unsupported
content to the substrate. The reading is therefore an instance of the
externalized-not-assumed discipline of
\autoref{prin:apophatic-naming-externalized-not-assumed}.
\end{proof}

\section{Closure}
\label{sec:cataphatic-process-closure}

The compressed form is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{IrrLocatedReal}(r)
&\Longrightarrow
\exists \alpha,\ \mathrm{Carry}(Z_\alpha,r),\\
\forall \alpha,\quad
\mathrm{FarEnd}(Z_\alpha)
&\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}\mathsf{T},\\
\neg\exists \alpha,\quad
\mathsf{T}&\in Z_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus a located irrational is a positive generation on a definite axis, while
$\mathsf{T}$ is the common non-local far-end role of admissible axes. The
former may be named by its stream, modulus, regularity, classifier, and
ledger. The latter may only be named as socket, boundary, and refusal of
objecthood.
