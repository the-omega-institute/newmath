\chapter{Information Strata and Non-Collapse}
\label{ch:visions-apophatic-information-strata-noncollapse}
\origin{ai}

The word ``axis'' is useful as geometric prose, but it is not the strict
object of the theory.  The strict object is a non-collapse relation between
information strata.  A stratum may be a finite value layer, a process layer
with stream and modulus data, a finite algebraic extension layer, a
transcendental expression layer, or an apophatic far-end role.  These strata
are not made honest by drawing them as orthogonal coordinates.  They are made
honest by recording which carrier, classifier, ledger, and transport rows are
available, and by refusing the retraction that would identify a higher
stratum with a lower one.

\section{Value, process, and far-end strata}
\label{sec:information-strata-value-process-far-end}

\begin{definition}[Information stratum]
\label{def:information-stratum}
An \emph{information stratum} is a BEDC presentation layer together with the
rows needed to consume it.  The basic strata used here are:
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_0
&:=\text{finite rational or dyadic value readback},\\
L_1
&:=\text{stream, modulus, regularity, location, and classifier data},\\
T/\Omega
&:=\text{the apophatic far-end role of finite-readout fibers}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first two are cataphatic strata: their objects are read by displayed
rows.  The last is apophatic: it is named by boundary sameness and
non-internalization, not by a carrier element.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finite shadow]
\label{def:information-stratum-finite-shadow}
For a process presentation $x\in L_1$ with sequence and modulus rows, its
$k$-th finite shadow is
$$
\pi_k(x):=
x_{\mathrm{seq}}\bigl(x_{\mathrm{mod}}(k)\bigr)\in L_0.
$$
The map $\pi_k$ is a finite readout request.  It is not a retraction from the
process stratum to the value stratum.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Non-collapse to a lower stratum]
\label{def:information-stratum-noncollapse}
Let $A$ be a stratum whose public reads include shadows in a lower stratum
$B$.  The predicate $\mathsf{NonCollapse}_B(a)$ means that no displayed
$B$-object recovers $a$ up to the classifier of $A$.  For located real
presentations over rational values, this specializes to
$$
\mathsf{NonCollapse}_{L_0}(x)
:\Longleftrightarrow
\neg\exists q:\RatUp,\ x\sim_{\RealUp}\eta(q),
$$
where $\eta(q)$ is the degenerate located-real presentation with constant
sequence $q$ and modulus $0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Located irrationality is process non-collapse]
\label{thm:located-irrationality-process-noncollapse}
A located irrational real has all of its finite rational shadows in $L_0$,
but it is not recovered by any single $L_0$ value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The located-real carrier supplies the stream and modulus rows.  Therefore
\autoref{def:information-stratum-finite-shadow} gives, for each $k$, a finite
rational or dyadic observation in $L_0$.

Irrationality is the refusal of a rational value-axis collapse: there is no
$q:\RatUp$ whose degenerate located-real presentation classifies with the
given located real under the $\RealUp$ classifier.  Thus every finite shadow
exists, while no lower-stratum point is a lossless recovery of the process.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Projection without retraction]
\label{cor:information-strata-projection-without-retraction}
The finite shadow maps $\pi_k:L_1\to L_0$ are constructive, but the existence
of these maps does not supply a general retraction of $L_1$ onto $L_0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each $\pi_k$ is just a displayed finite read request.  A retraction would
assert that every process presentation can be recovered from a lower value
object, contradicting
\autoref{thm:located-irrationality-process-noncollapse} for located
irrational presentations.
\end{proof}

\section{Algebraic and transcendental directions}
\label{sec:information-strata-algebraic-transcendental}

Algebraic extension language gives one place where the geometric word
``direction'' can be made literal.  Even there, the literal structure is
linear independence over a base field, not automatic orthogonality.  An inner
product or bilinear form would have to be supplied separately before
orthogonality could be asserted.

\begin{definition}[Finite algebraic direction]
\label{def:finite-algebraic-direction}
Let $F$ be a field and let $\alpha$ be algebraic over $F$ with minimal
polynomial degree $d$.  The algebraic direction generated by $\alpha$ is the
finite extension carrier
$$
F(\alpha)=
\left\{
a_0+a_1\alpha+\cdots+a_{d-1}\alpha^{d-1}
\mid a_i\in F
\right\}.
$$
Its strict content is the $F$-linear basis
$1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Algebraic non-collapse is finite-dimensional]
\label{thm:algebraic-noncollapse-finite-dimensional}
If $\alpha$ has degree $d>1$ over $F$, then $F(\alpha)$ does not collapse to
$F$, but it is still finitely generated as an $F$-vector space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The minimal polynomial degree gives the standard basis
$1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$ for $F(\alpha)$ over $F$.  If
$\alpha\in F$, the minimal polynomial would have degree $1$, contrary to
$d>1$.  Hence the extension is genuinely larger than $F$.  Since the
displayed basis has $d$ members, the non-collapse is finite-dimensional.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Transcendental direction]
\label{def:transcendental-direction}
If $\tau$ is transcendental over $F$, the expression layer $F(\tau)$ contains
the infinite family
$$
1,\tau,\tau^2,\tau^3,\ldots .
$$
This family is read as a transcendental direction only through its finite
polynomial tests and expression ledgers; it is not the apophatic far-end
role.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Transcendental non-collapse is not the far end]
\label{thm:transcendental-noncollapse-not-far-end}
For transcendental $\tau$, the family
$1,\tau,\tau^2,\ldots$ is $F$-linearly independent, but the resulting
expression layer is still a carrier layer, not $T/\Omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Any finite linear dependence among $1,\tau,\ldots,\tau^n$ would be a nonzero
polynomial $p\in F[X]$ with $p(\tau)=0$, contradicting transcendence.  Thus
the expression layer has infinitely many independent finite polynomial
shadows.

Nevertheless $F(\tau)$ remains a mathematical carrier: its elements may be
formed as expressions, compared by suitable relations, and transported by
displayed ledgers.  The apophatic far-end role has the opposite grammar.  By
\autoref{def:cataphatic-process-apophatic-far-end-role}, it is named only by
$\equiv_{\mathrm{apo}}$ and refuses object-level equality, membership, and
function application.  Hence transcendental non-collapse is not the far end.
\end{proof}

\section{Allowed folding and forbidden flattening}
\label{sec:information-strata-folding-flattening}

\begin{definition}[Dimension as information stratum]
\label{def:dimension-as-information-stratum}
In this chapter, \emph{dimension} means irreducible information stratum.  A
presentation has higher information dimension than a lower layer when its
carrier, classifier, ledger, or transport obligations cannot be reconstructed
from the lower layer's displayed objects.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Finite folding is not infinite collapse]
\label{prin:finite-folding-not-infinite-collapse}
Finite quotient-like folds, such as residue classes modulo a displayed
natural number, are not the same operation as a completion, supremum, or
host-point collapse.  A finite fold has finite representatives, a visible
normal form, and a classifier ledger.  An unwitnessed completion asks for an
endpoint or point extracted from an infinite process or predicate family.
\end{principle}

\begin{theorem}[Classical point-line reading is a bridge]
\label{thm:classical-point-line-reading-is-bridge}
Reading value shadows, located processes, algebraic extensions,
transcendental expression layers, and completion endpoints as points of one
classical line is a useful bridge reading, not a kernel-local identification.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The preceding results separate the strata.  Located irrationality gives
finite shadows without a rational-value retraction.  Algebraic extension
gives finite-dimensional non-collapse over a field.  Transcendental
extension gives infinitely many finite polynomial shadows while remaining a
carrier layer.  The apophatic far end refuses carrier membership altogether.

If all of these are consumed as points of one host line, several
cross-stratum maps have been supplied: process to point, classifier to
equality, predicate or process family to endpoint, and socket to element.
Such maps may be studied as bridge data, but they are not present in the
finite kernel merely because the geometric drawing uses one axis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Axis prose is subordinate to non-collapse]
\label{cor:axis-prose-subordinate-to-noncollapse}
Geometric axis language is legitimate only when the underlying
NonCollapse, carrier, classifier, ledger, and bridge status have already
been specified.  In particular, algebraic extension supplies linear
independence over the base field; orthogonality is an additional geometric
structure, not an automatic consequence of adjoining a root.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \autoref{thm:algebraic-noncollapse-finite-dimensional}, adjoining an
algebraic element of degree $d>1$ supplies a finite basis over the base
field.  The proof uses linear independence and spanning, not an inner
product.  By \autoref{thm:classical-point-line-reading-is-bridge}, any
geometric compression of the strata must be read as bridge structure.
Therefore axis prose is acceptable as orientation, but the invariant is
non-collapse of information strata.
\end{proof}
