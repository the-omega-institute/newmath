\section{BEDC--Langlands Ramified Local State Closure}
\label{sec:unified-reality-bedc-langlands-ramified-local-state-closure}

The unramified layer is closed by Satake coordinates. At a ramified
place there is no spherical Satake quotient, so the finite detector is
not merely $(v,r)$. It is a local testing row
$$
(v,\mathfrak b,f_v),
$$
where $\mathfrak b$ is a Bernstein or packet window and $f_v$ is a
compactly supported local Hecke test function. The role of a Satake
point is played by a stable local character distribution attached to the
local parameter packet.

\begin{definition}[Ramified local parameter ledger]
\label{def:unified-reality-ramified-local-parameter-ledger}
Let $F_v$ be a local field and let $G_v=G(F_v)$. A ramified local
parameter is a Weil--Deligne row
$$
\phi_v:W'_{F_v}\to{}^LG.
$$
Its local ledger records
$$
\Lambda_{\phi_v}
:=
(I_v\text{-type},N_v,\epsilon_v,\gamma_v,
\mathsf{Conductor},\mathsf{Depth},\mathsf{ArthurData},
\mathsf{Endoscopy},\mathsf{Normalization}).
$$
Here $N_v$ is monodromy, and the normalization row includes transfer
factors and local measure conventions.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ramified local packet certificate]
\label{def:unified-reality-ramified-local-packet-certificate}
A ramified local packet certificate for $\phi_v$ consists of a finite or
controlled local packet
$$
\Pi_{\phi_v}=\{\pi_{v,1},\ldots,\pi_{v,m}\}
$$
and a stable local distribution
$$
S\Theta_{\phi_v}
:=
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_i\Theta_{\pi_{v,i}},
$$
where $\Theta_{\pi_{v,i}}$ is the Harish--Chandra character
distribution and at least one coefficient $a_i$ is nonzero. The
certificate carries a local packet ledger for normalization, transfer
factors, endoscopic labels, component-group characters, and local
$L,\epsilon,\gamma$ factors. If the packet or stable distribution is not
available, the failure is a row in the local parameter ledger.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ramified local three-properties rows]
\label{def:unified-reality-ramified-local-three-properties}
The ramified local time row is generated by parabolic induction,
Bernstein block decomposition, and conductor or depth filtration. The
unity row separates a tempered unitary part from real-exponent or
modulus rows. The ledger row records ramification, monodromy, depth,
endoscopy, transfer factors, normalization, and wild ramification tails
without allowing any of them to masquerade as an unramified Satake
readout.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ramified local finite detector]
\label{def:unified-reality-ramified-local-finite-detector}
A ramified local finite detector is a tuple
$$
(v,\mathfrak b,f_v)
$$
where $\mathfrak b$ is a fixed Bernstein block or finite local packet
window, and
$$
f_v\in C_c^\infty(G_v)
$$
is a local Hecke test function. For an admissible representation
$\pi_v$, the detector reads
$$
\Theta_{\pi_v}(f_v)=\operatorname{tr}\pi_v(f_v).
$$
For a parameter packet, it reads
$$
S\Theta_{\phi_v}(f_v).
$$
The local residual is
$$
D_{v,f_v}(\pi_v,\phi_v)
:=
\Theta_{\pi_v}(f_v)-S\Theta_{\phi_v}(f_v).
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Local character separation]
\label{thm:unified-reality-local-character-separation}
Fix a Bernstein or packet window containing finitely many irreducible
admissible representations
$$
\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_M.
$$
If their character distributions are pairwise distinct on the window,
then finite linear combinations of local test functions separate the
corresponding character rows. In particular, for any displayed target
row one can construct a local test function that kills any prescribed
finite set of nonmatching candidates and is nonzero on the target row,
outside declared local cancellation rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The character distributions $\Theta_{\pi_i}$ are linear functionals on
the local test-function space. Pairwise distinctness means that for
each pair $i\ne j$ the difference
$$
\Theta_{\pi_i}-\Theta_{\pi_j}
$$
is a nonzero linear functional, hence is nonzero on some local test
function. Since the candidate set is finite, finitely many such
separating functions suffice. Taking finite linear combinations gives a
projector on the finite list, with any local cancellation explicitly
recorded in the ledger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ramified local state nonzero]
\label{thm:unified-reality-ramified-local-state-nonzero}
If $\phi_v$ has a ramified local packet certificate and
$S\Theta_{\phi_v}$ is not the zero distribution, then there exists
$$
f_v\in C_c^\infty(G_v)
$$
such that
$$
S\Theta_{\phi_v}(f_v)\ne0.
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The stable character $S\Theta_{\phi_v}$ is a linear functional on the
local test-function space. A nonzero linear functional is nonzero on at
least one test function.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Zero local state is a ledger row]
\label{cor:unified-reality-zero-local-state-ledger-row}
If
$$
S\Theta_{\phi_v}(f_v)=0
$$
for every local test function $f_v$, then $S\Theta_{\phi_v}$ is the zero
distribution. A declared legal ramified parameter with only the zero
stable distribution has a local parameter ledger defect.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A linear functional that vanishes on every vector of its domain is the
zero functional. If the parameter is nevertheless declared as a local
state, the failure of nonzero state data must be recorded in
$\Lambda_{\phi_v}$; otherwise the local object has no state readout and
violates the three-properties ledger row.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ramified local no-hidden residual]
\label{thm:unified-reality-ramified-local-no-hidden-residual}
Let $(\pi_v,\phi_v)$ be a ramified local BEDC--Langlands row with a
ramified local packet certificate, local character separation, complete
local ledger discipline, and closedness against unresolved local ledger
rows. Then no ramified local mismatch remains hidden: any mismatch is
detected by some finite local detector $(v,\mathfrak b,f_v)$ or is a
local ledger row, and neither unresolved alternative is compatible with
closedness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the mismatch is visible in a fixed Bernstein or packet window, then
the difference
$$
\Theta_{\pi_v}-S\Theta_{\phi_v}
$$
is a nonzero linear functional on the local test space. Hence there is
some $f_v$ with
$$
D_{v,f_v}(\pi_v,\phi_v)\ne0.
$$
This is a finite local detector. Ledger discipline requires the
detected residual to be recorded; carrying it unresolved violates
closedness, while omitting it violates the ledger row.

If no fixed local window detects the mismatch, then it is not a
character-distribution mismatch on the displayed packet window. It lies
in normalization, transfer factors, endoscopy, monodromy, depth,
component-group labels, or wild ramification tails. These are precisely
the local ledger rows. Again, unresolved ledger data are incompatible
with closedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Ramified global parameter state]
\label{def:unified-reality-ramified-global-parameter-state}
For a global parameter $\phi=(\phi_v)_v$ and a finite window
$$
\mathcal W=(S_{\mathrm{unr}},S_{\mathrm{ram}},B),
$$
the global parameter state with ramification is
$$
\Theta_{\phi,\mathcal W}^{\mathrm{st}}
:=
\left(\bigotimes_{v\in S_{\mathrm{unr}}}
\Theta_{\phi_v}^{\mathrm{unr}}\right)
\otimes
\left(\bigotimes_{v\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}}
S\Theta_{\phi_v}\right)
\otimes
\Theta_{\phi,\infty}.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Global parameter state with ramification]
\label{thm:unified-reality-global-parameter-state-with-ramification}
Assume every unramified local state is the Satake evaluation state,
every ramified local packet state present in the window is nonzero,
the archimedean state is nonzero or its failure is ledgered, and the
test function factors as
$$
f_{\phi,\mathcal W}
=
\bigotimes_{v\in S_{\mathrm{unr}}}f_{\phi_v}^{\mathrm{unr}}
\otimes
\bigotimes_{v\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}}f_{\phi_v}^{\mathrm{ram}}
\otimes
f_{\phi,\infty},
$$
with each local factor normalized to value $1$ under its corresponding
state. Then
$$
\Theta_{\phi,\mathcal W}^{\mathrm{st}}(f_{\phi,\mathcal W})=1.
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the tensor product definition,
$$
\Theta_{\phi,\mathcal W}^{\mathrm{st}}(f_{\phi,\mathcal W})
=
\prod_{v\in S_{\mathrm{unr}}}
\Theta_{\phi_v}^{\mathrm{unr}}(f_{\phi_v}^{\mathrm{unr}})
\cdot
\prod_{v\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}}
S\Theta_{\phi_v}(f_{\phi_v}^{\mathrm{ram}})
\cdot
\Theta_{\phi,\infty}(f_{\phi,\infty}).
$$
Each factor is $1$ by the hypotheses, so the product is $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local-to-global parameter ledger dichotomy]
\label{thm:unified-reality-local-global-parameter-ledger-dichotomy}
If a global parameter state has no nonzero fixed-window test function,
then at least one local parameter ledger row is present, or an
archimedean, endoscopic, transfer-factor, or global normalization ledger
row is present.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If all unramified local states are present, their projectors give
nonzero evaluation readouts. If every ramified packet state is nonzero,
one can choose a local test function on which it is nonzero and rescale
that local factor to value $1$. If the archimedean state is also present
and the global transfer and normalization rows are closed, the tensor
test function of
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-global-parameter-state-with-ramification}
has nonzero global state. Therefore total failure of all fixed-window
readouts forces a missing or zero local state, or a global normalization,
transfer, archimedean, or endoscopic defect. Those are exactly the
ParameterLedger rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ramified parameter state and trace realization]
\label{thm:unified-reality-ramified-parameter-state-trace-realization}
Assume the global parameter state with ramification is nonzero on the
declared test function, the stable trace formula certificate holds, the
finite Hecke and local projectors are constructed, and all non-target
trace ledger rows are separated or discharged. Then the fixed window
contains an automorphic packet matching the parameter on all displayed
unramified and ramified local readouts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The nonzero global parameter state gives a nonzero stable geometric-side
readout. The stable trace formula certificate transports it to a nonzero
spectral-side readout. The unramified finite Hecke projector kills all
displayed unramified nonmatching packets, and local character separation
supplies the ramified packet-window projectors. Hence a nonzero spectral
contribution must come from a packet matching the parameter on the whole
fixed window.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ramified BEDC--Langlands closure]
\label{thm:unified-reality-ramified-bedc-langlands-closure}
Assume unramified Satake closure, ramified local packet certificates,
local character separation, global parameter state nonzero, the stable
trace formula certificate, finite-window projector certificates, cofinal
automorphic tail closure, no-hidden Langlands residual, and closedness.
Then every legal global parameter has a global automorphic realization
$\Pi_\phi$ whose unramified Hecke--Frobenius readouts match $\phi$ and
whose ramified local packet identities, local factors, and transfer data
are ledgered and closed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The global parameter state and stable trace formula give a nonzero
spectral readout in every fixed window. The unramified and ramified
projector certificates force a matching packet in each fixed window.
The cofinal automorphic tail closure globalizes the window realizations
unless they enter a tail ledger; closedness excludes unresolved tail
rows. Thus a global packet $\Pi_\phi$ is obtained.

Any remaining unramified mismatch is detected by a finite
Hecke--Frobenius detector. Any remaining ramified mismatch is detected by
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-ramified-local-no-hidden-residual} or lies
in a local ledger row. No-hidden residual and closedness exclude both
unresolved alternatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The abelian ramified check]
\label{cor:unified-reality-abelian-ramified-check}
For $G=GL_1$, a ramified local packet is a local character
$$
\chi_v:F_v^\times\to\mathbb C^\times
$$
with conductor ledger. The local state
$$
\Theta_{\chi_v}(f_v)
=
\int_{F_v^\times}f_v(x)\chi_v(x)\,d^\times x
$$
is nonzero on a conductor-compatible compact coset test function after
normalization. For the quadratic character of $\mathbb Q(i)/\mathbb Q$,
the prime $2$ is the ramified conductor row, while odd primes are the
unramified detectors of
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-gl1-quadratic-splitting-detector}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose a compact coset on which the local character is constant and let
$f_v$ be its normalized characteristic test function. The integral is
then the chosen character value times the normalized volume, so it can
be scaled to $1$. For $\mathbb Q(i)/\mathbb Q$, the only ramified prime
is $2$, and the odd-prime splitting detector remains unramified.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ramified local state boundary]
\label{thm:unified-reality-ramified-local-state-boundary}
Ramified local state closure consumes local packet certificates,
character distribution separation, transfer-factor normalization, and
local factor compatibility. It does not construct those traditional
local Langlands and endoscopic inputs; when they are absent, the absence
remains a local ParameterLedger obligation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proofs above are linear-functional and ledger arguments once the
local packet and stable character distribution are supplied. The
existence of ramified packets, transfer identities, component-group
labels, and character identities is separate local Langlands data. BEDC
records that data as explicit certificates rather than hiding it inside
the unramified Satake layer.
\end{proof}
