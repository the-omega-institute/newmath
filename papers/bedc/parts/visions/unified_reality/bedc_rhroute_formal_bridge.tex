\section{BEDC RHRoute Formal Bridge}
\label{sec:unified-reality-bedc-rhroute-formal-bridge}

The preceding taxonomy identifies the analytic source theorem that would
feed the RH fixed-half section.  The Lean-side RHRoute layer should be
read in the same way: it supplies a finite formal route discipline and
several checked algebraic carriers, but it does not by itself construct
the zeta-specific source family for every completed critical-strip zero.
The bridge point is therefore not the fixed-half conclusion itself, but
the map from completed zero rows to zero-relevant finite certificates.

\begin{definition}[RHRoute formal layer]
\label{def:unified-reality-rhroute-formal-layer}
The \emph{RHRoute formal layer} is the collection of finite BEDC
carriers that record:
$$
\mathsf{ConstructiveZetaEval},
\qquad
\mathsf{MirrorFixedAlgebra},
\qquad
\mathsf{FinitePrimeWindow},
$$
$$
\mathsf{PrimeSkewDefect},
\qquad
\mathsf{LocalGlobalExclusion},
\qquad
\mathsf{RouteClosureSeparation}.
$$
The first row gives rational-complex evaluation packets for the
eta-to-zeta readout.  The mirror row proves the finite algebraic
equivalence between $J$-fixedness and the critical-line readout.  The
prime-window rows record finite prime supports, unitary-balance packets,
and skew-defect certificates.  The route-separation row records that a
finite route closure certificate is not uniformly the same thing as an
arbitrary fixed statement.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Formal bridge packet]
\label{def:unified-reality-rhroute-formal-bridge-packet}
For a critical-strip zero coordinate $s$, an \emph{RHRoute formal bridge
packet} consists of:
$$
\mathsf{CritZero}(s),
\qquad
\mathsf{EvalPacket}(s),
\qquad
\mathsf{MirrorOrbit}(s),
$$
$$
\mathsf{PrimeWindowSource}(s),
\qquad
\mathsf{SkewOrUnitaryBoundary}(s),
\qquad
\mathsf{RouteSeparationAudit}(s).
$$
The packet is a formal carrier for the downstream proof route.  It is
not yet a source theorem unless the prime-window source is produced from
the completed zero ledger by active capture, dominance, tail control,
and solenoid or Lee--Yang stability.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[RHRoute source bridge]
\label{def:unified-reality-rhroute-source-bridge}
The \emph{RHRoute source bridge} is the zeta-specific row
$$
\mathsf{RHRouteSourceBridge}_{\zeta}
:=
\prod_{s:\ComplexUp}
\left(
\mathsf{CritZero}(s)
\to
\sum_{P:\mathsf{RHRouteFormalBridgePacket}(s)}
\mathsf{ZeroRelevant}(P,s)
\right).
$$
Here $\mathsf{ZeroRelevant}(P,s)$ means that the finite prime-window or
resonance data in $P$ controls a local zero island of the completed
zeta readout, rather than merely displaying a visible defect or a
compatible finite route.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The formal layer is not the source bridge]
\label{thm:unified-reality-formal-layer-not-source-bridge}
The RHRoute formal layer does not by itself imply
$$
\mathsf{RHRouteSourceBridge}_{\zeta}.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The formal layer contains finite algebraic and route-theoretic carriers:
evaluation packets, mirror-fixed readbacks, prime-window supports,
unitary-balance surfaces, skew-defect certificates, and finite route
closure decisions.  Each of these is a legal finite BEDC object.
However, none of them asserts that every completed critical-strip zero
has a zero-relevant dominant source packet.  In particular, route
closure and fixed-statement transport are separated by the route
separation row: a closed finite route is not a uniform proof of an
arbitrary fixed statement, and a fixed statement is not uniformly a
finite route closure certificate.  Therefore the missing row is still
the source bridge from the zero ledger to zero-relevant finite data.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[BEDC RHRoute certificate]
\label{def:unified-reality-bedc-rhroute-certificate}
A \emph{BEDC RHRoute certificate} for $s$ is an RHRoute formal bridge
packet $P$ together with the following additional rows:
$$
\mathsf{ActiveCapture}(P,s),
\qquad
\mathsf{DominantSource}(P,s),
\qquad
\mathsf{BoundaryStability}(P,s),
$$
$$
\mathsf{DefectExtraction}(P,s),
\qquad
\mathsf{FixedHalfReadback}(P,s).
$$
Active capture and dominance make $P$ zero-relevant for the completed
zeta readout.  Boundary stability prevents the packet from carrying a
noncompact real scale.  Defect extraction identifies the normal
coordinate read by the prime-window or resonance channel.  Fixed-half
readback transports the resulting $J$-fixed row to
$\mathsf{OnCritLine}(s)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BEDC RHRoute certificates are sufficient]
\label{thm:unified-reality-bedc-rhroute-certificates-sufficient}
If every completed critical-strip zero carries a BEDC RHRoute
certificate, then the RH fixed-half section exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $z:\mathcal Z_{\zeta}$ have coordinate $s$.  By hypothesis, $s$
carries a BEDC RHRoute certificate $P$.  The active-capture and
dominance rows say that $P$ is zero-relevant for the completed zeta
readout.  The defect-extraction row reads the normal coordinate
$$
\delta(s)=\Re(s)-\frac12
$$
through the finite prime-window or resonance channel.  The boundary
stability row excludes a nonzero value of that normal coordinate on a
zero-relevant packet; otherwise the same packet would carry a visible
prime skew or an unbounded normal energy incompatible with the boundary
row.  Hence $\delta(s)=0$.  The mirror-fixed algebra then reads this as
$J$-fixedness, and the fixed-half readback gives
$$
\mathsf{OnCritLine}(s).
$$
Varying $z$ gives the fixed-half section.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The remaining theorem is source construction]
\label{cor:unified-reality-bedc-rhroute-remaining-source-construction}
The unclosed content of the RHRoute route is the implication
$$
\mathsf{CritZero}(s)
\to
\sum_{P:\mathsf{RHRouteFormalBridgePacket}(s)}
\left(
\mathsf{ActiveCapture}(P,s)
\times
\mathsf{DominantSource}(P,s)
\times
\mathsf{BoundaryStability}(P,s)
\right).
$$
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
All rows after source construction are structural.  Mirror-fixed
readback is the algebraic equivalence between $J$-fixedness and the
critical-line coordinate.  Prime-window skew and normal-energy rows
detect nonzero off-half displacement once a zero-relevant packet has
been supplied.  Route separation prevents these finite certificates from
being confused with the target statement itself.  Thus the proof load is
the zeta-specific construction of $P$: active capture, dominance, tail
control, and boundary stability for the completed zero ledger.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compatibility with the triadic route taxonomy]
\label{cor:unified-reality-rhroute-compatible-with-triadic-taxonomy}
The RHRoute formal bridge is a finite certificate interface for the same
proof target named by the no-ghost, stable-packet, and triadic-closure
rows of \autoref{sec:unified-reality-triadic-route-taxonomy}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The no-ghost row asks that every completed zero enter the closed
generability carrier.  The stable-packet row asks for a canonical local
zero island with Rouch\'e capture and Lee--Yang stability.  The
triadic-closure row asks for an inverse-limit packet whose parity or
energy certificate kills the normal coordinate.  An RHRoute certificate
is the finite formal interface through which these rows can be audited:
it displays the evaluation packet, the mirror algebra, the finite
prime-window source, the skew or unitary boundary, and the route
separation audit.  It therefore refines the taxonomy without replacing
the remaining source theorem.
\end{proof}
