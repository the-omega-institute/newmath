\section{Parity Closure Tower Certificate}
\label{sec:unified-reality-parity-closure-tower-certificate}

The recursive parity tower displays the normal defect at every finite
prime-adic depth, but that visibility is not yet a zero-exclusion
theorem.  The missing statement can be sharpened by separating two
notions that the functional equation tends to blur.  Mirror symmetry
gives paired zero packets; it does not by itself identify a packet with
its mirror inside the same routing closure.  A parity closure tower is
the stronger object: the mirror packet lies in the same recursive
routing closure, while the normal defect is routing-invariant and
parity-odd.

\begin{definition}[Abstract parity-routing packet]
\label{def:unified-reality-abstract-parity-routing-packet}
An \emph{abstract parity-routing packet} consists of a topological
space $X$, a homeomorphism
$$
\mathscr R:X\to X,
$$
an involution
$$
P:X\to X,
\qquad
P^2=\operatorname{id},
$$
and a continuous real readout
$$
\Delta:X\to\RealUp
$$
such that
$$
P\mathscr R P=\mathscr R^{-1},
$$
$$
\Delta(\mathscr R x)=\Delta(x),
$$
and
$$
\Delta(Px)=-\Delta(x).
$$
The map $\mathscr R$ is the packet-routing step, $P$ is parity
reflection, and $\Delta$ is the signed normal defect read by the packet.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Parity closure kills odd defect]
\label{thm:unified-reality-parity-closure-kills-odd-defect}
Let $(X,\mathscr R,P,\Delta)$ be an abstract parity-routing packet.  If
$$
Px\in\overline{\{\mathscr R^m x:m\in\mathbb Z\}},
$$
then
$$
\Delta(x)=0.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The closure hypothesis gives a sequence of integers $m_j$ such that
$$
\mathscr R^{m_j}x\to Px.
$$
By continuity of $\Delta$,
$$
\Delta(Px)=\lim_j\Delta(\mathscr R^{m_j}x).
$$
Routing invariance gives
$$
\Delta(\mathscr R^{m_j}x)=\Delta(x)
$$
for every $j$, hence
$$
\Delta(Px)=\Delta(x).
$$
Parity oddness gives
$$
\Delta(Px)=-\Delta(x).
$$
Thus $\Delta(x)=-\Delta(x)$, and the located real readout gives
$\Delta(x)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Eta return loop and cut coordinate]
\label{def:unified-reality-eta-return-loop-cut-coordinate}
For
$$
\eta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-s},
$$
write
$$
v_N(s):=(-1)^{N-1}N^{-s},
\qquad
P_N(s):=\sum_{n\le N}v_n(s),
\qquad
R_N(s):=\sum_{n>N}v_n(s).
$$
On a zero row $\eta(s)=0$,
$$
P_N(s)+R_N(s)=0.
$$
The local cut coordinate at the $N$th segment is the row
$$
P_{N-1}(s)+\theta_N(s)v_N(s)=0.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Eta cut coordinates satisfy exact routing recursion]
\label{thm:unified-reality-eta-cut-coordinates-exact-routing-recursion}
On an eta zero row, the cut coordinates satisfy
$$
\theta_N(s)
=
1-
\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)^s
\theta_{N+1}(s).
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By
\autoref{def:unified-reality-eta-return-loop-cut-coordinate},
$$
\theta_N(s)=-\frac{P_{N-1}(s)}{v_N(s)}.
$$
Since $P_{N-1}+v_N+R_N=0$,
$$
P_{N-1}=-v_N-R_N,
$$
so
$$
\theta_N(s)=1+\frac{R_N(s)}{v_N(s)}.
$$
Also $R_N=v_{N+1}+R_{N+1}$, and the definition of
$\theta_{N+1}$ gives
$$
R_N=v_{N+1}\theta_{N+1}.
$$
Finally
$$
\frac{v_{N+1}}{v_N}
=
-\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)^s.
$$
Substitution gives the displayed recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Parity closure tower certificate]
\label{def:unified-reality-zeta-parity-closure-tower-certificate}
For a critical-strip zeta zero $s=1/2+\delta+i\tau$, a
\emph{zeta parity closure tower certificate} consists of a canonical
zero-relevant packet $x_s$ in an abstract parity-routing packet
$(X,\mathscr R,P,\Delta)$, together with rows:
$$
\mathsf{ActiveCapture}(x_s,s),
\qquad
\Delta(x_s)=\delta,
$$
$$
P x_s\in\overline{\{\mathscr R^m x_s:m\in\mathbb Z\}},
$$
and a zero-relevance ledger saying that $x_s$ is the packet whose
dominance, endpoint, tail, or solenoid rows control the completed zero.
The equality $\Delta(x_s)=\delta$ is the defect-invariance row; the
closure row is stronger than the statement that $s$ and $1-\overline{s}$
are both zeros.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Parity closure tower certificates are conditionally sufficient]
\label{thm:unified-reality-parity-closure-tower-certificates-conditionally-sufficient}
If every critical-strip zeta zero carries a zeta parity closure tower
certificate, then the RH fixed-half section exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $z:\mathcal Z_{\zeta}$ have complex coordinate
$$
s=\frac12+\delta+i\tau.
$$
The certificate supplies $x_s$ with
$$
\Delta(x_s)=\delta
$$
and
$$
P x_s\in\overline{\{\mathscr R^m x_s:m\in\mathbb Z\}}.
$$
By
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-parity-closure-kills-odd-defect},
$$
\Delta(x_s)=0.
$$
Hence $\delta=0$, so $\Re(s)=1/2$.  Since $z$ was arbitrary,
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-rh-fixed-half-section-equivalence} supplies
the RH fixed-half section.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Functional equation pairing is not packet closure]
\label{cor:unified-reality-functional-equation-pairing-not-packet-closure}
The completed zeta functional equation supplies mirror pairing of zero
coordinates, but the parity closure tower certificate requires a
stronger packet-level identification.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The mirror
$$
J(s):=1-\overline{s}
$$
sends
$$
\frac12+\delta+i\tau
\quad\text{to}\quad
\frac12-\delta+i\tau.
$$
The completed functional equation and conjugation symmetry place zeros
in mirror-related positions.  This gives a paired-zero row
$$
s\longmapsto J(s).
$$
It does not by itself give
$$
P x_s\in\overline{\{\mathscr R^m x_s:m\in\mathbb Z\}},
$$
nor does it identify the active endpoint, Rouch\'e, solenoid, or
dominance packet for $s$ with its mirror inside one recursive closure.
Thus the parity closure certificate isolates the noncircular bridge
left after ordinary functional-equation symmetry has been accounted for.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Relation to endpoint and solenoid certificates]
\label{cor:unified-reality-parity-closure-relation-endpoint-solenoid}
Endpoint bisection and solenoid Lee--Yang rows are candidate mechanisms
for producing the active capture and zero-compatible routing closure
required by a parity closure tower certificate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint bisection certificate of
\autoref{def:unified-reality-zeta-endpoint-bisection-certificate}
identifies a finite endpoint window, a distinguished segment, and a
Rouch\'e ledger.  These are possible sources for
$\mathsf{ActiveCapture}(x_s,s)$.  The solenoid-compatible resonance lift
of
\autoref{def:unified-reality-solenoid-compatible-resonance-lift}
identifies a compact phase carrier and forbids hidden noncompact real
scale.  This is a possible source for the zero-compatible routing
closure.  The parity closure tower certificate records the abstract
consequence common to these mechanisms: once the signed defect is both
routing-invariant and parity-odd inside the same closure, it must vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Parity closure bridge obligations]
\label{def:unified-reality-parity-closure-bridge-obligations}
The parity closure route leaves four bridge obligations:
$$
\mathsf{ActiveCapture},
\qquad
\mathsf{DefectInvariant},
\qquad
\mathsf{ParityClosure},
\qquad
\mathsf{ZeroCompatibleBound}.
$$
Active capture constructs the zero-relevant packet from the completed
zero ledger.  Defect invariance proves that its signed readout is
$\Re(s)-1/2$.  Parity closure places the mirror packet in the same
routing closure.  Zero-compatible bound or compactness prevents the
closure from using a hidden noncompact real scale.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[The bridge obligations are the proof load]
\label{cor:unified-reality-parity-closure-bridge-obligations-proof-load}
The parity closure route proves a conditional RH theorem, but the
zeta-specific proof load is the construction of the four bridge
obligations in
\autoref{def:unified-reality-parity-closure-bridge-obligations}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-parity-closure-kills-odd-defect} is an
abstract topological theorem, and
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-parity-closure-tower-certificates-conditionally-sufficient}
is its zeta-facing conditional use.  Neither theorem constructs the
canonical packet space, its routing, its dominance ledger, or its compact
zero-compatible carrier from the completed zeta row.  Those tasks are
exactly the four bridge obligations.  Consequently the remaining work is
not another restatement of the fixed-half surface, but the construction
of a zero-relevant recursive packet tower satisfying those obligations.
\end{proof}
