\section{Triadic Ontic Invariant Readback}
\label{sec:unified-reality-triadic-ontic-invariant-readback}

The fixed-half invariant of
\autoref{sec:unified-reality-generation-invariant-exclusion-route} is
the zeta-facing readout of a higher BEDC invariant.  It should not be
taken as the primitive invariant itself.  The primitive row is the
closure preservation of the ontic triad: distinction is not lost,
boundary transport remains legal, and recursive generation keeps a
unique auditable interpretation.

\begin{definition}[Triadic ontic invariant]
\label{def:unified-reality-triadic-ontic-invariant}
The \emph{triadic ontic invariant} is the packet
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}
:=
\left(
\mathcal I_{\mathsf P},
\mathcal I_{\mathsf U},
\mathcal I_{\mathsf T}
\right),
$$
where:
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathsf P}
=
\text{distinction, prime charge, and ledger provenance are preserved},
$$
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathsf U}
=
\text{symmetry, unit boundary, and legal gauge are preserved},
$$
and
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathsf T}
=
\text{recursion, finite-window closure, and unique readback are preserved}.
$$
The invariant is not one of these rows in isolation.  It is the closure
condition that the three rows remain compatible under generation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Triadic closed generation rule]
\label{def:unified-reality-triadic-closed-generation-rule}
Let $R$ be a legal generation rule on RHRoute packets.  The rule is
\emph{triadic closed} when it sends packets satisfying
$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$ to packets satisfying
$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$, and the output ledger records how the
three components are transported:
$$
\mathsf{PrimeLedgerOK},
\qquad
\mathsf{BoundaryGaugeOK},
\qquad
\mathsf{RecursiveClosureOK}.
$$
The first row records source provenance, prime-window data, and refusal
of unlicensed collapse.  The second row records mirror, unit-boundary,
and compact gauge discipline.  The third row records the recursive
construction and its unique finite readback.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Triadic closure is preserved by generated packets]
\label{thm:unified-reality-triadic-closure-preserved-by-generated-packets}
If the source packets satisfy $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$ and every
legal generation rule is triadic closed, then every generated packet
satisfies $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is structural induction on the generated packet.  A source packet
satisfies the invariant by hypothesis.  For an inductive step, assume
the input packets satisfy $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$.  Since the
applied rule is triadic closed, its output carries the three transported
rows $\mathsf{PrimeLedgerOK}$, $\mathsf{BoundaryGaugeOK}$, and
$\mathsf{RecursiveClosureOK}$.  Hence the output satisfies
$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$.  Repeating the argument along the finite
generation tree gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Zeta readback of the triadic invariant]
\label{def:unified-reality-zeta-readback-triadic-invariant}
For a zeta-facing packet over
$$
s=\frac12+r+i\gamma,
$$
the local channel at a displayed node of height $h_a>0$ is
$$
U_a(s)=e^{-(s-1/2)h_a}
=
e^{-r h_a}e^{-i\gamma h_a}.
$$
The zeta readback of $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$ is:
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathsf P}
\leadsto
\text{the prime charge fixes }h_a,
$$
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathsf U}
\leadsto
\text{the allowed gauge contains unit phase and compact prime fibres},
$$
$$
\mathcal I_{\mathsf T}
\leadsto
\text{the packet is read through a finite or cofinal recursive tower}.
$$
Under this readback, the real positive factor $e^{-r h_a}$ is legal only
when it is trivial.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Triadic ontic readback gives fixed-half closure]
\label{thm:unified-reality-triadic-ontic-readback-gives-fixed-half}
For a zeta-facing generated packet, preservation of
$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$ implies the fixed-half readout
$$
r=0.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The prime-ledger component supplies a positive height $h_a>0$ for every
active node.  The recursive component supplies the finite or cofinal
readback in which the same node is part of the generated packet.  The
boundary-gauge component allows the unit phase
$e^{-i\gamma h_a}$ and compact prime-register fibres, but it does not
allow an unledgered noncompact real positive scale.  Therefore the
factor
$$
e^{-r h_a}
$$
must be trivial on every zero-relevant generated packet.  Since $h_a>0$,
this gives $r=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-half is a projection, not the primitive invariant]
\label{cor:unified-reality-fixed-half-projection-not-primitive-invariant}
The fixed-half row used by RHRoute is the zeta projection of the triadic
ontic invariant, not the primitive invariant of BEDC.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The primitive invariant is the preservation of distinction provenance,
legal boundary gauge, and recursive readback.  In the zeta projection,
these rows respectively determine the active heights, restrict the
hidden carrier to unit phase and compact prime-register data, and make
the finite or cofinal tower auditable.  Their combined readback excludes
the nontrivial real positive factor $e^{-r h_a}$.  This exclusion is
then read as $r=0$, equivalently as the fixed-half row.  Thus fixed-half
is the output surface of the invariant under the zeta projection.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Generated-zero coverage remains the missing zeta row]
\label{cor:unified-reality-triadic-invariant-coverage-remains-missing}
Even if every generated RHRoute packet preserves
$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{BEDC}}$, the RH fixed-half section still requires
generated-zero coverage for the completed zeta zero ledger.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-triadic-ontic-readback-gives-fixed-half},
a generated zeta-facing packet satisfying the triadic ontic invariant
has fixed-half readout.  To obtain a section over all completed
critical-strip zero rows, each such zero must first be covered by a
generated packet with the same zero readout.  This is precisely the
source row isolated in
\autoref{def:unified-reality-generated-zero-coverage-row}; it is not
supplied by the invariant itself.
\end{proof}
