\section{BEDC--Langlands Window-to-Global Bridge}
\label{sec:unified-reality-bedc-langlands-window-global-bridge}

The trace-window closure of
\autoref{sec:unified-reality-bedc-langlands-trace-window-closure}
uses a compactness row. This section separates that row into a finite
complexity certificate and an explicit automorphic tail ledger. The
point is to prevent a fixed-window realization theorem from being read
as a growing-window theorem without an intervening stability witness.

\begin{definition}[Automorphic complexity]
\label{def:unified-reality-automorphic-complexity}
An automorphic packet $\Pi$ has automorphic complexity
$$
\kappa(\Pi)
:=
\kappa_{\mathrm{level}}(\Pi)
+\kappa_{\mathrm{cond}}(\Pi)
+\kappa_{\infty}(\Pi)
+\kappa_{\mathrm{ram}}(\Pi)
+\kappa_{\mathrm{endo}}(\Pi)
+\kappa_{\mathrm{mult}}(\Pi).
$$
The six summands record, respectively, compact-open or level
complexity, conductor complexity, archimedean infinitesimal-character or
weight complexity, ramified local type complexity, endoscopic or Arthur
packet label complexity, and multiplicity or packet bookkeeping
complexity. The expression is a ledgered height: changing the
automorphic category may change its implementation, but not the
obligation that every escape mode represented by a hidden name be
addressable.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bounded automorphic finiteness certificate]
\label{def:unified-reality-bounded-automorphic-finiteness-certificate}
For $B<\infty$ set
$$
\mathsf{Aut}_{\le B}(G)
:=
\{\Pi:\kappa(\Pi)\le B\}.
$$
A bounded automorphic finiteness certificate says that
$\mathsf{Aut}_{\le B}(G)$ is finite up to the declared closed
automorphic equivalence relation for every finite $B$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cofinal window realization]
\label{def:unified-reality-cofinal-window-realization}
Let
$$
\mathcal W_1\subset \mathcal W_2\subset \cdots
$$
be a cofinal Hecke window tower in the sense of
\autoref{def:unified-reality-cofinal-hecke-window-tower}. A cofinal
window realization of an allowed parameter packet $\phi^{\uparrow}$ is a
sequence of automorphic packets $(\Pi_n)$ such that
$$
\lambda_{\mathcal W_n}(\Pi_n)=a_{\mathcal W_n}(\phi)
$$
for every $n$. Equivalently, for every detector $(v,r)$ lying in
$\mathcal W_n$,
$$
\lambda_{v,r}(\Pi_n)
=
\operatorname{tr}r(\phi(\operatorname{Frob}_v)).
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Automorphic tail ledger]
\label{def:unified-reality-automorphic-tail-ledger}
The automorphic tail ledger is the refinement of
\autoref{def:unified-reality-automorphic-compactness-ledger} that names
the concrete ways a cofinal window realization can fail to stabilize:
$$
\mathsf{LevelConductorEscape},\quad
\mathsf{ArchimedeanParameterEscape},\quad
\mathsf{RamifiedLocalTypeEscape},
$$
$$
\mathsf{ContinuousSpectrumEscape},\quad
\mathsf{EndoscopicSwitching},\quad
\mathsf{BoundaryEisensteinResidualEscape}.
$$
A complete automorphic tail ledger says that every unbounded realization
sequence has at least one of these rows, or a declared refinement of one
of them, as its obstruction to global stabilization.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded subsequence gives global realization]
\label{thm:unified-reality-bounded-subsequence-global-realization}
Let $(\Pi_n)$ be a cofinal window realization of
$\phi^{\uparrow}$. Assume the bounded automorphic finiteness
certificate. If there are $B<\infty$ and an infinite subsequence
$n_j\to\infty$ such that
$$
\kappa(\Pi_{n_j})\le B
$$
for all $j$, then there is an automorphic packet $\Pi_{\phi}$ satisfying
$$
\lambda_{v,r}(\Pi_{\phi})
=
\operatorname{tr}r(\phi(\operatorname{Frob}_v))
$$
for every certified unramified finite Hecke--Frobenius detector
$(v,r)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By bounded automorphic finiteness, the packets of complexity at most
$B$ represent only finitely many closed automorphic equivalence classes.
Hence one class occurs for infinitely many terms of the subsequence.
Choose a representative $\Pi_{\phi}$ of that recurring class.

Let $(v,r)$ be any certified unramified finite detector. Cofinality of
the tower gives $N(v,r)$ such that $(v,r)\in\mathcal W_n$ for all
$n\ge N(v,r)$. Since the recurring class appears along infinitely many
$n_j$, choose one with $n_j\ge N(v,r)$. The packet $\Pi_{n_j}$ realizes
$\phi$ on $\mathcal W_{n_j}$, so
$$
\lambda_{v,r}(\Pi_{n_j})
=
\operatorname{tr}r(\phi(\operatorname{Frob}_v)).
$$
Closed automorphic equivalence preserves the declared Hecke readouts, so
the same equality holds for $\Pi_{\phi}$. Since $(v,r)$ was arbitrary,
$\Pi_{\phi}$ matches the parameter on the full cofinal detector scope.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unbounded realization sequence enters the tail ledger]
\label{thm:unified-reality-unbounded-realization-tail-ledger}
Let $(\Pi_n)$ be a cofinal window realization of
$\phi^{\uparrow}$. If no infinite subsequence has bounded automorphic
complexity, then the realization sequence enters the automorphic tail
ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The absence of a bounded-complexity infinite subsequence means that for
every finite $B$, only finitely many $n$ satisfy
$$
\kappa(\Pi_n)\le B.
$$
Thus the realization sequence escapes every finite complexity sublevel.
By the definition of $\kappa$, this escape must occur through level or
conductor growth, archimedean parameter growth, ramified local type
growth, endoscopic or Arthur packet switching, multiplicity bookkeeping
growth, or movement of mass into continuous, boundary, Eisenstein, or
residual spectrum. These are exactly the rows named by
\autoref{def:unified-reality-automorphic-tail-ledger}. Completeness of
the ledger records the observed escape as an automorphic tail row.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Window-to-global alternative]
\label{thm:unified-reality-window-global-alternative}
Assume that every window in a cofinal Hecke window tower has an
automorphic realization of $\phi^{\uparrow}$, that bounded automorphic
finiteness holds, and that the automorphic tail ledger is complete.
Then either $\phi^{\uparrow}$ has a global automorphic realization on
the certified unramified finite detector scope, or a cofinal realization
sequence enters the automorphic tail ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose one realizing packet $\Pi_n$ for each window $\mathcal W_n$. If
some infinite subsequence of $(\Pi_n)$ has bounded automorphic
complexity, then
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-bounded-subsequence-global-realization}
produces a global automorphic packet $\Pi_{\phi}$ matching all certified
unramified finite detectors. If no such subsequence exists, then
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-unbounded-realization-tail-ledger} places
the sequence in the automorphic tail ledger. These two cases exhaust the
sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed window-to-global realization]
\label{thm:unified-reality-closed-window-global-realization}
Assume the hypotheses of
\autoref{thm:unified-reality-window-global-alternative}. If closed
accepted Langlands packets cannot carry unresolved automorphic tail
ledger rows, then $\phi^{\uparrow}$ has a global automorphic realization
on the certified unramified finite detector scope.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the window-to-global alternative, either the global realization is
already present or the chosen cofinal realization sequence enters the
automorphic tail ledger. In the second case, the tail row is either
unrecorded, which violates ledger discipline, or recorded but
unresolved, which violates closedness. Hence the tail alternative is
not available for a closed accepted packet. The global realization
alternative remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Trace-window existence gives global realization]
\label{cor:unified-reality-trace-window-global-realization}
Let $\phi^{\uparrow}$ have parameter stable distribution certificates,
finite-window projector certificates, and stable trace-window
certificates on a cofinal Hecke window tower. Assume bounded automorphic
finiteness, a complete automorphic tail ledger, and closedness against
unresolved automorphic tail rows. Then $\phi^{\uparrow}$ has a global
automorphic realization.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The fixed-window certificates give an automorphic realization on every
window by
\autoref{cor:unified-reality-fixed-window-automorphic-realization}. The
closed window-to-global realization theorem applies to the resulting
cofinal realization sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Global reciprocity after no-hidden residuals]
\label{cor:unified-reality-window-global-reciprocity}
Under the hypotheses of
\autoref{cor:unified-reality-trace-window-global-realization}, assume
also the no-hidden Langlands residual condition of
\autoref{def:unified-reality-no-hidden-langlands-residual}. Then the
global automorphic packet $\Pi_{\phi}$ satisfies
$$
\lambda_{v,r}(\Pi_{\phi})
=
\operatorname{tr}r(\phi(\operatorname{Frob}_v))
$$
on the certified unramified finite detector scope, and every residual
mismatch is either detected or rejected by closedness.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The preceding corollary supplies the global automorphic packet. If a
Hecke--Frobenius mismatch remained on the certified scope, the
no-hidden residual condition would make it a finite detector residual or
a ramified, endoscopic, continuous-spectrum, boundary, or normalization
ledger residual. A finite detector residual contradicts the cofinal
matching; an unresolved ledger residual contradicts closedness. Hence no
residual mismatch remains in a closed accepted packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Functorial window-to-global transfer]
\label{thm:unified-reality-functorial-window-global-transfer}
Let $(\Pi_H,\phi_H)$ be a closed $H$-side packet, and let
$$
\eta:{}^LH\to{}^LG
$$
preserve the declared Langlands packet rows. Put
$$
\phi_G:=\eta\circ\phi_H.
$$
If $\phi_G$ satisfies the hypotheses of
\autoref{cor:unified-reality-window-global-reciprocity} on the $G$-side,
then there is a $G$-side automorphic packet $\Pi_G$ with
$$
\lambda_{v,r}(\Pi_G)
=
\operatorname{tr}r(\eta(\phi_H(\operatorname{Frob}_v)))
$$
on the certified unramified finite detector scope.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply
\autoref{cor:unified-reality-window-global-reciprocity} to
$\phi_G$. The resulting packet $\Pi_G$ matches the Frobenius readout of
$\phi_G$ on the certified detector scope. Substituting
$\phi_G=\eta\circ\phi_H$ gives the displayed equality. Any remaining
failure would be a no-hidden residual or an unresolved automorphic tail
row, both incompatible with the closed hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Categorical analogue]
\label{thm:unified-reality-categorical-window-global-analogue}
In a geometric Langlands setting, the same bridge has the following
categorical form. If a certified Langlands functor sends every finite
Hecke modification window of a spectral object into the automorphic
category, and if the functor is essentially surjective modulo a complete
categorical boundary ledger, then the spectral object has an
automorphic realization unless it enters that boundary ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite Hecke modification windows play the role of the tower
$\mathcal W_n$. Essential surjectivity replaces bounded automorphic
finiteness, and the categorical boundary ledger replaces the
automorphic tail ledger. The same dichotomy applies: a cofinally stable
image gives an automorphic realization, while failure of stability is a
declared boundary row. Closedness excludes unresolved boundary rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Window-to-global boundary]
\label{thm:unified-reality-window-global-boundary}
The window-to-global bridge proves only a conditional passage from
finite Hecke windows to a global automorphic packet. It does not prove
bounded automorphic finiteness, stable trace-window formulae, parameter
stable distributions, or geometric essential surjectivity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each step consumes one displayed certificate. Fixed-window existence
comes from the trace-window machinery; repetition inside a finite
complexity sublevel comes from bounded automorphic finiteness; exclusion
of unbounded escape comes from the automorphic tail ledger and
closedness. The bridge therefore identifies the precise obstruction to
globalization: missing finite-window realization, missing bounded
finiteness, or an unresolved automorphic tail row.
\end{proof}
