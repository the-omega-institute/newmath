\chapter{Bundle-Inspired Section-Comparison Boundary}
\label{ch:visions-section-comparison-section-bundle-boundary}
\origin{ai}

This chapter records a BEDC-internal bundle-inspired reading of the
observer-centered section dynamics of
\autoref{ch:visions-observer-centered-logarithmic-section-dynamics}. It
does not derive quantum mechanics, general relativity, or quantum gravity.
Its purpose is to state an interface boundary in which fixed-context
internal-state data and inter-context comparison data are kept distinct.
The terminology is schematic only: it does not assert an Ehresmann
horizontal distribution, a physical reference frame, a spacetime metric,
a Lorentzian causal structure, an electromagnetic gauge field, or
Einstein equations. In a later physical model, quantum-theoretic data
might be represented in the fibre-indexed layer and relativistic
covariance might constrain the base-indexed comparison layer, but those
identifications require additional physical structures.

\section{Fibre-Indexed and Base-Comparison Faces}
\label{sec:section-comparison-fibre-base-layer-faces}

\begin{definition}[Section-bundle packet]
\label{def:section-comparison-section-bundle-packet}
A \emph{section-bundle packet} consists of a reference-context base
$\mathcal R$, a frame-indexed Hilbert family
$$
\{\mathcal H_R\}_{R\in V_{\mathcal R}},
$$
phase-coordinate fibres $\Sigma_{S,R}$ over displayed context rows,
context reshaping rows
$$
\Phi_{R'\leftarrow R}:\mathcal H_R\to\mathcal H_{R'},
$$
and ledger rows recording projection, residual, visible readout, and
transport data. Its typical fibre over $R$ is read schematically as
$$
\mathcal H_R\otimes\Sigma_{S,R}.
$$
The word Hilbert is literal only when the family carries complex Hilbert
space structure and the comparison rows are specified accordingly. It is
not the Hilbert space of the physical universe and supplies no Born rule,
observable algebra, Hamiltonian, or quantum-field dynamics by itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fibre-indexed state face]
\label{def:section-comparison-vertical-quantum-face}
The \emph{fibre-indexed state face} of a section-bundle packet is the
collection of rows internal to one chosen context fibre:
$$
\psi_R\in\mathcal H_R,\qquad
\widetilde t_R\in\Sigma_{S,R},
$$
orthogonal axis packets, phase rows, projection rows, Born-weight rows
when displayed, and section-style measurement updates. It is the
fibre-internal grammar already constrained by
\autoref{sec:quantum-measurement-generation-interface}. This face is
Hilbert-style bookkeeping, not quantum mechanics itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Base-comparison covariance face]
\label{def:section-comparison-horizontal-relativistic-face}
The \emph{base-comparison covariance face} of a section-bundle packet is
the collection of rows comparing neighbouring complete sections:
$$
R'\sim R,
\qquad
\Phi_{R'\leftarrow R},
\qquad
B_{R'}-\Phi_{R'\leftarrow R}(B_R),
$$
together with visible stability bounds, inter-history invariants,
maximum-rate rows when displayed, and any continuous comparison operator
or loop-obstruction row introduced over the context base. It is not a
physical signal inside one fibre; it is the comparison discipline between
context-relative sections. The base-comparison language is not an
Ehresmann horizontal distribution unless a connection with horizontal
lifts is separately constructed.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[State layer and comparison layer]
\label{prin:section-comparison-fibre-base-layer-reading}
In this packet language, the Hilbert-style grammar is placed in the
fibre-indexed layer: phase, superposition-like rows, projection, and
orthogonal residual admission inside a chosen context. The
frame-comparison grammar is placed in the base-indexed comparison layer:
comparison operators, visible-rate bounds, comparison costs, and loop
obstructions governing how neighbouring complete sections are compared.
Vertical and horizontal terminology, when used informally, is only a
bundle-diagram mnemonic.
\end{principle}

\section{Hilbert-Style Fibre Reading}
\label{sec:section-comparison-quantum-fibre-reading}

\begin{definition}[Context-relative state row]
\label{def:section-comparison-frame-relative-quantum-state}
A \emph{context-relative state row} is a state row
$$
\psi_R\in\mathcal H_R
$$
together with the phase and ledger rows required by the chosen
Hilbert-style axis packet. If a displayed orthogonal family
$$
E=\{e_a:a\in A\}
$$
is available, the state may be written at that resolution as
$$
\psi_R=\sum_{a\in A}c_a e_a.
$$
The basis elements are admissible readout directions inside the selected
context fibre, not absolute local facts outside the packet. A physical
quantum interpretation would additionally require projective states,
admissible observables or effects, probability-readout rules, and
dynamics.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Log-time phase transport schema]
\label{prin:section-comparison-unitary-log-time-transport}
When a multiplicative accumulation row
$$
A_{n+1}=\lambda_{n+1}A_n
$$
is read through
$$
T_n=\log A_n,
$$
a context-fibre Schrödinger-shaped row is a log-time phase transport
schema:
$$
i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial T}=H\psi.
$$
Equivalently, on the multiplicative scale,
$$
i\hbar A\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial A}=H\psi.
$$
This is a coordinate reading of the time parameter. It does not supply a
physical clock, proper time, a quantum time observable, the Hamiltonian,
Hilbert-space physical laws, or empirical calibration rows.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Measurement as fibre projection with residual ledger]
\label{def:section-comparison-measurement-fibre-projection-residual}
For a displayed projection family $\{P_a\}$ in frame $R$, a measurement
read at outcome $a$ has the section-style form
$$
\psi_R
\longmapsto
\frac{P_a\psi_R}{\|P_a\psi_R\|}
\oplus
\eta_a
$$
when the denominator condition, post-outcome carrier, residual row, and
ledger replay are displayed. The residual must satisfy the appropriate
orthogonality row against the prior accepted ledger.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Collapse is not a propagation row]
\label{prin:section-comparison-collapse-not-propagation}
The section-style update of
\autoref{def:section-comparison-measurement-fibre-projection-residual} is a
licensed update of the context-relative description. It is not, by itself,
a controllable signal propagating inside the selected world-section. A
claim of physical signalling requires an internal propagation row of the
kind refused by
\autoref{prin:observer-centered-reshaping-not-propagation} unless a
separate bridge is supplied.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Entanglement as non-product section residue]
\label{prin:section-comparison-entanglement-nonproduct-section-residue}
An entangled pair is read as a non-product residue of one complete
section, not as two independently complete worlds joined by a hidden
signal. This refines
\autoref{def:quantum-entanglement-nonproduct-residue}: local outcomes may
be displayed, but the residual fibre need not split as a product of
local residual fibres.
\end{principle}

\section{Frame-Comparison Reading}
\label{sec:section-comparison-relativistic-base-reading}

\begin{definition}[Residual comparison cost]
\label{def:section-comparison-residual-metric-candidate}
For neighbouring context rows $R'\sim R$ and a section $B_R$, define the
comparison residual row
$$
\eta_R(R')
:=
B_{R'}-\Phi_{R'\leftarrow R}(B_R)
$$
whenever both sections and the reshaping row are displayed. A
\emph{residual comparison cost} is a visible quadratic read
$$
g_R(\delta R,\delta R)
:=
\langle
\eta_R(\delta R),\eta_R(\delta R)
\rangle_{\mathrm{vis}}
$$
after the packet supplies the visible projection, signature convention,
and locality certificate needed for the read. This cost is not a
spacetime metric, not Lorentzian by name alone, and supplies no causal
cones, geodesics, or Einstein equations.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Light-cone bounds are internal propagation bounds]
\label{prin:section-comparison-light-cone-internal-propagation}
Relativistic maximum-rate bounds constrain visible propagation rows
inside or between displayed histories. They do not constrain bare context
rewriting as a coordinate or ledger comparison. Thus a global rewrite of
a description is not a superluminal signal unless it also supplies a
controllable internal propagation row.
\end{principle}

\begin{definition}[Context comparison operator and loop obstruction]
\label{def:section-comparison-frame-connection-curvature-row}
In a continuous context-base reading, a context comparison row is an
operator $\nabla_\mu$ transporting fibre data over the base. Its
loop-obstruction row is the noncommutativity
$$
[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]=\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}.
$$
The row says that transport around an infinitesimal loop can return with
nontrivial holonomy. It is the comparison obstruction to globally
flattening the context reshaping discipline. It is connection-like only
when identity, composition, and typed transport laws have been supplied.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Local comparison flattening]
\label{prin:section-comparison-equivalence-local-gauge-flattening}
The local flattening reading is that near a context row $R$ one may
choose a presentation in which the first-order reshaping row is
trivial:
$$
\Phi_{R+dR\leftarrow R}=I+O(dR^2).
$$
Loop obstruction remains visible through holonomy; local presentation
flattening does not erase the comparison-defect row. This is not the
equivalence principle of general relativity unless a spacetime model and
its metric geometry are separately supplied.
\end{principle}

\section{Unified Boundary}
\label{sec:section-comparison-unified-boundary}

\begin{definition}[Combined comparison schema]
\label{def:section-comparison-combined-connection-row}
A speculative combined comparison schema over a section-bundle packet has
the schematic form
$$
D_\mu
:=
\partial_\mu+\Gamma_\mu+\frac{i}{\hbar}A_\mu+\mathcal A_\mu^{\mathrm{sol}},
$$
where $\Gamma_\mu$ is a context-reshaping comparison row, $A_\mu$ is a
visible phase-coordinate row, and $\mathcal A_\mu^{\mathrm{sol}}$ is a
prime-register phase-fibre comparison row. Its loop defect decomposes
schematically as
$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]
=
\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}
+
\frac{i}{\hbar}F_{\mu\nu}
+
\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{sol}}.
$$
This is an interface target, not a closed physical theory, a principal
connection, an electromagnetic vector potential, or an Aharonov--Bohm
prediction.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[Conflict of times becomes a bundle-coordinate problem]
\label{prin:section-comparison-time-conflict-bundle-coordinate-problem}
The usual tension between a fixed quantum time parameter and dynamical
relativistic time is read here as a coordinate mismatch. The quantum time
row is a log-time parameter along a selected fibre evolution schema; the
relativistic time row would be part of the visible geometry of a physical
base only after an external spacetime interpretation is added. Neither
row is an external absolute time object in the packet.
\end{principle}

\begin{theorem}[Section-bundle separation of state and comparison roles]
\label{thm:section-comparison-section-bundle-role-separation}
In any section-bundle packet, fibre-indexed rows can state phase, projection,
Born-weight, and non-product residue obligations without specifying a
base loop-obstruction row. Base-comparison rows can state context
reshaping, visible stability, maximum-rate, comparison-cost, and
commutator-defect obligations without choosing a Born-weight layer. A
physical unification claim therefore requires bridge rows relating the
two faces; it is not obtained merely by naming both in one packet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fibre-indexed face of
\autoref{def:section-comparison-vertical-quantum-face} is defined using rows
inside one fibre over a context. Those rows do not include the
base-comparison operator or loop-obstruction data of
\autoref{def:section-comparison-horizontal-relativistic-face}. Conversely, the
base-comparison face is defined from neighbouring context comparisons and visible
stability data; those rows do not include a Born-weight layer or a
measurement-update section. Therefore each face is well typed without the
other. A unification claim needs additional compatibility rows, such as
the combined connection target of
\autoref{def:section-comparison-combined-connection-row}.
\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[Boundary status]
\label{prin:section-comparison-section-bundle-boundary-status}
The chapter licenses one compressed interpretation: Hilbert-style state
data may be placed in the fibre-indexed grammar of phase, superposition,
projection, and orthogonal residual ledger inside a selected context
fibre; covariance constraints may be placed in the base-comparison
grammar of context comparison, comparison operators, cost readout, and
loop obstruction over the base. A quantum-gravity interpretation would
require a certificate showing that these grammars share one ledgered
discipline with empirical prediction rows and with the relevant physical
Hilbert, spacetime, gauge, metric, and field-equation structures.
\end{principle}
