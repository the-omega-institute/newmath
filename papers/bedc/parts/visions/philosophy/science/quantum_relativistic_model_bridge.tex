\subsection{Quantum and Relativistic Model Bridges}
\label{subsec:philosophy-quantum-relativistic-model-bridge}

The probability-projection reading has two immediate physical neighbours.
Quantum mechanics gives the direct Hilbert-projection model: state, observable,
spectral projection, and scalar probability are all already visible as finite
certificate surfaces. Relativistic geometry gives the complementary
no-global-frame model: locality, rate bounds, metric-facing geometry, and
curvature are read only through local ledgers and observer-stable transport.
The two readings are not fused into a single physical theory; they share the
same refusal of hidden global objects.

\begin{definition}[Born-rule bridge packet]
\label{def:philosophy-born-rule-bridge-packet}
A Born-rule bridge packet is a finite scientific bridge
$$
B_Q=(Q,O,M,r,L)
$$
where $Q$ is a displayed $\QuantumStateUp$ packet, $O$ is a displayed
$\ObservableUp$ packet over the same Hilbert source, $M$ is the
$\SpectralMeasureUp$ projection-family row consumed by the observable, $r$ is
a $\RealUp$ scalar window, and $L$ records the comparison
$$
r\sim_R\langle \psi,\Pi_A(E)\psi\rangle .
$$
Here $\psi$ is read only through the state ray of $Q$, $A$ only through the
self-adjoint observable endpoint of $O$, and $\Pi_A(E)$ only through the
finite projection row of $M$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Born probability is a certified projection shadow]
\label{thm:philosophy-born-probability-certified-projection-shadow}
An accepted Born-rule bridge packet reads a quantum measurement probability
as a finite Hilbert projection shadow. It does not expose a hidden wavefunction
representative, host operator equality, unrestricted spectral theorem,
unlogged measurement primitive, or ambient probability oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the packet $B_Q=(Q,O,M,r,L)$. The $\QuantumStateUp$ row supplies the
Hilbert source, unit-vector endpoint, projective endpoint, global-phase
classifier, and provenance. The $\ObservableUp$ row supplies the displayed
self-adjoint endpoint and its expectation-facing row. The
$\SpectralMeasureUp$ row supplies the event-to-projection ledger and finite
scalar-window boundary.

The ledger $L$ then records the only cross-surface comparison needed for the
measurement read:
$$
r\sim_R\langle \psi,\Pi_A(E)\psi\rangle .
$$
Thus the public probability is the displayed scalar endpoint $r$ classified
with the Hilbert projection expression named by the bridge. Since no field of
$B_Q$ is a selected vector representative beyond the projective state row, a
host operator equality, an unrestricted spectral-theorem object, an unlogged
measurement operation, or a detached probability source, none of those objects
is exported by the bridge.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Phase and density boundaries]
\label{cor:philosophy-phase-density-boundaries}
Pure-state measurements consume the projective state classifier, not host
vector equality. Mixed-state measurements consume the $\DensityMatrixUp$
trace-class, positivity, trace-one, and finite affine ledger rows before any
trace-form scalar window is read.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a pure state, the carrier of $\QuantumStateUp$ identifies public state
content through the projective endpoint and global-phase classifier. A
measurement bridge cannot distinguish two representatives that the public
state row has already classified.

For a mixed state, the $\DensityMatrixUp$ carrier exposes only positivity,
trace-class membership, trace-one readback, restricted classifier comparison,
and finite affine-mixture ledger data over the Hilbert source. A trace-form
measurement read therefore consumes those rows before it reaches the scalar
window. Spectral decompositions, purifications, or hidden ensembles are not
part of that public surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quantum-channel preservation of the measurement interface]
\label{cor:philosophy-quantum-channel-preserves-measurement-interface}
A $\QuantumChannelUp$ row preserves the type of data needed by the
Born-rule bridge: a density-matrix input is sent to a density-matrix output,
so downstream scalar windows remain measurement-interface rows rather than
untyped state updates.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The $\QuantumChannelUp$ packet carries a completely positive trace-preserving
linear-map row between density-matrix spaces. Its public image theorem sends
accepted density-matrix rows to accepted density-matrix rows. Consequently a
post-channel measurement bridge still starts from the same public interface:
Hilbert source, density carrier, observable row, spectral projection ledger,
and $\RealUp$ scalar window.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Relativistic pre-geometry bridge]
\label{def:philosophy-relativistic-pre-geometry-bridge}
A relativistic pre-geometry bridge is a finite model bridge whose displayed
rows may include $\ManifoldUp$, $\RiemannianMetricUp$, $\ConnectionUp$,
$\CurvatureUp$, $\LeviCivitaConnectionUp$, $\CrossHistCausalConeUp$,
$\LorentzFrameRateUp$, and $\RelationalPhysicsUp$ packets. Its public content
is the local geometric, curvature, causal, observer-symmetry, model-audit, and
no-global-frame ledger carried by those packets.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Relativistic bridge is pre-geometry, not completed field equation]
\label{thm:philosophy-relativistic-bridge-pregeometry-boundary}
The current relativistic bridge supports a certificate pre-structure for
local geometry, curvature-facing rows, causal cones, observer-symmetric rate
readback, and relational physics. It does not close a Lorentzian metric,
Ricci tensor, scalar curvature, Einstein tensor, stress-energy tensor, or
Einstein field-equation certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The manifold, Riemannian-metric, connection, curvature, and
Levi-Civita-connection packets expose local carrier, fibre, derivative,
commutator, torsion-free, metric-compatibility, curvature-facing, transport,
and provenance rows. These are geometry-facing certificate rows, not a
Lorentzian spacetime object or a field equation.

The causal and relational packets expose finite multi-history locality,
maximum-rate, observer-symmetry, no-global-frame refusal, invariant readback,
physical-model audit, and admissible rate or refusal rows. Their public
boundaries explicitly exclude global frames, synchronized clocks, ambient
spacetime, metric tensors as primitive inputs, and detached physical truth.

No listed packet supplies a Lorentzian metric carrier, curvature contractions
to Ricci and scalar curvature, an Einstein tensor row, a stress-energy tensor
row, or an empirical field-equation bridge tying those rows together. The
current claim is therefore the pre-geometry boundary stated above, not a
completed general-relativity certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common physical bridge discipline]
\label{thm:philosophy-common-physical-bridge-discipline}
Quantum and relativistic model bridges share a certificate discipline: the
world is not closed as a global object; only finite public projection,
geometry, causal, invariant, empirical, and failure ledgers are exported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the quantum bridge, unobserved state detail is not exported as a hidden
complete object. Measurement passes through a state row, an observable row, a
spectral projection row, and a scalar window.

In the relativistic bridge, global frame data is not exported as a primitive
object. Physical structure passes through local geometry rows,
cross-history causal rows, observer-symmetric rate rows, invariant readback,
and model-audit ledgers.

Both bridges therefore replace hidden totality by finite public certificates.
They differ in geometry and physical interpretation, but they obey the same
BEDC rule: a scientific bridge exports only displayed rows and their
classifier-stable transport, not an unlogged global world.
\end{proof}
