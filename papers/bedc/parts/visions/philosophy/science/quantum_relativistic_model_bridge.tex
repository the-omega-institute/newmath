\subsection{Quantum and Relativistic Model Bridges}
\label{subsec:philosophy-quantum-relativistic-model-bridge}

The probability-projection reading has two immediate physical neighbours.
Quantum mechanics gives the direct Hilbert-projection model: state, observable,
spectral projection, and scalar probability are all already visible as finite
certificate surfaces. Relativistic geometry gives the complementary
no-global-frame model: locality, rate bounds, metric-facing geometry, and
curvature are read only through local ledgers and observer-stable transport.
The two readings are not fused into a single physical theory; they share the
same refusal of hidden global objects.

\begin{definition}[Born-rule bridge packet]
\label{def:philosophy-born-rule-bridge-packet}
A Born-rule bridge packet is a finite scientific bridge
$$
B_Q=(Q,O,M,r,L)
$$
where $Q$ is a displayed $\QuantumStateUp$ packet, $O$ is a displayed
$\ObservableUp$ packet over the same Hilbert source, $M$ is the
$\SpectralMeasureUp$ projection-family row consumed by the observable, $r$ is
a $\RealUp$ scalar window, and $L$ records the comparison
$$
r\sim_R\langle \psi,\Pi_A(E)\psi\rangle .
$$
Here $\psi$ is read only through the state ray of $Q$, $A$ only through the
self-adjoint observable endpoint of $O$, and $\Pi_A(E)$ only through the
finite projection row of $M$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Born probability is a certified projection shadow]
\label{thm:philosophy-born-probability-certified-projection-shadow}
An accepted Born-rule bridge packet reads a quantum measurement probability
as a finite Hilbert projection shadow. It does not expose a hidden wavefunction
representative, host operator equality, unrestricted spectral theorem,
unlogged measurement primitive, or ambient probability oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the packet $B_Q=(Q,O,M,r,L)$. The $\QuantumStateUp$ row supplies the
Hilbert source, unit-vector endpoint, projective endpoint, global-phase
classifier, and provenance. The $\ObservableUp$ row supplies the displayed
self-adjoint endpoint and its expectation-facing row. The
$\SpectralMeasureUp$ row supplies the event-to-projection ledger and finite
scalar-window boundary.

The ledger $L$ then records the only cross-surface comparison needed for the
measurement read:
$$
r\sim_R\langle \psi,\Pi_A(E)\psi\rangle .
$$
Thus the public probability is the displayed scalar endpoint $r$ classified
with the Hilbert projection expression named by the bridge. Since no field of
$B_Q$ is a selected vector representative beyond the projective state row, a
host operator equality, an unrestricted spectral-theorem object, an unlogged
measurement operation, or a detached probability source, none of those objects
is exported by the bridge.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Phase and density boundaries]
\label{cor:philosophy-phase-density-boundaries}
Pure-state measurements consume the projective state classifier, not host
vector equality. Mixed-state measurements consume the $\DensityMatrixUp$
trace-class, positivity, trace-one, and finite affine ledger rows before any
trace-form scalar window is read.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a pure state, the carrier of $\QuantumStateUp$ identifies public state
content through the projective endpoint and global-phase classifier. A
measurement bridge cannot distinguish two representatives that the public
state row has already classified.

For a mixed state, the $\DensityMatrixUp$ carrier exposes only positivity,
trace-class membership, trace-one readback, restricted classifier comparison,
and finite affine-mixture ledger data over the Hilbert source. A trace-form
measurement read therefore consumes those rows before it reaches the scalar
window. Spectral decompositions, purifications, or hidden ensembles are not
part of that public surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quantum-channel preservation of the measurement interface]
\label{cor:philosophy-quantum-channel-preserves-measurement-interface}
A $\QuantumChannelUp$ row preserves the type of data needed by the
Born-rule bridge: a density-matrix input is sent to a density-matrix output,
so downstream scalar windows remain measurement-interface rows rather than
untyped state updates.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The $\QuantumChannelUp$ packet carries a completely positive trace-preserving
linear-map row between density-matrix spaces. Its public image theorem sends
accepted density-matrix rows to accepted density-matrix rows. Consequently a
post-channel measurement bridge still starts from the same public interface:
Hilbert source, density carrier, observable row, spectral projection ledger,
and $\RealUp$ scalar window.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Relativistic pre-geometry bridge]
\label{def:philosophy-relativistic-pre-geometry-bridge}
A relativistic pre-geometry bridge is a finite model bridge whose displayed
rows may include $\ManifoldUp$, $\RiemannianMetricUp$, $\ConnectionUp$,
$\CurvatureUp$, $\LeviCivitaConnectionUp$, $\CrossHistCausalConeUp$,
$\LorentzFrameRateUp$, and $\RelationalPhysicsUp$ packets. Its public content
is the local geometric, curvature, causal, observer-symmetry, model-audit, and
no-global-frame ledger carried by those packets.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Relativistic bridge is pre-geometry, not completed field equation]
\label{thm:philosophy-relativistic-bridge-pregeometry-boundary}
The current relativistic bridge supports a certificate pre-structure for
local geometry, curvature-facing rows, causal cones, observer-symmetric rate
readback, and relational physics. It does not close a Lorentzian metric,
Ricci tensor, scalar curvature, Einstein tensor, stress-energy tensor, or
Einstein field-equation certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The manifold, Riemannian-metric, connection, curvature, and
Levi-Civita-connection packets expose local carrier, fibre, derivative,
commutator, torsion-free, metric-compatibility, curvature-facing, transport,
and provenance rows. These are geometry-facing certificate rows, not a
Lorentzian spacetime object or a field equation.

The causal and relational packets expose finite multi-history locality,
maximum-rate, observer-symmetry, no-global-frame refusal, invariant readback,
physical-model audit, and admissible rate or refusal rows. Their public
boundaries explicitly exclude global frames, synchronized clocks, ambient
spacetime, metric tensors as primitive inputs, and detached physical truth.

No listed packet supplies a Lorentzian metric carrier, curvature contractions
to Ricci and scalar curvature, an Einstein tensor row, a stress-energy tensor
row, or an empirical field-equation bridge tying those rows together. The
current claim is therefore the pre-geometry boundary stated above, not a
completed general-relativity certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[General-relativity coordination bridge]
\label{def:philosophy-general-relativity-coordination-bridge}
A general-relativity coordination bridge is a relativistic bridge packet whose
displayed rows include observer-coordinate frames, local clock-budget reads,
observer-locality cells, cross-history causal-cone rows, Lorentz-frame-rate
rows, Minkowski-rate geometry rows, and a $\CurvedSpacetimeUp$ curved-gluing
row. Its public object is the compatibility of these rows under componentwise
$\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and no-global-frame
refusal.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[General relativity is interface coordination]
\label{thm:philosophy-general-relativity-interface-coordination}
An accepted general-relativity coordination bridge reads the relativistic
model as a minimal compatibility geometry among local clocks, local frames,
causal cones, inter-Hist coordination rows, metric-facing distance-shape
ledgers, and curvature or transition ledgers. It does not export a pre-given
four-dimensional host container, a global observer frame, a shared external
time scalar, a primitive metric tensor, or an observer-independent event point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the coordination bridge. The observer-coordinate frame and
local-clock-budget rows supply observer-local chart and clock reads, not a
global frame. The observer-locality-cell and cross-history causal-cone rows
supply finite inter-Hist coordination and bounded causal comparison, not a
host event object. The Lorentz-frame-rate and Minkowski-rate geometry rows
supply observer-symmetric rate and distance-shape readback, not a primitive
metric tensor. The $\CurvedSpacetimeUp$ row then records chart transition,
curvature, transport, replay, and provenance data over the same finite
surface.

Thus the bridge exports only the compatibility of displayed interfaces. Any
coordinate system anchored at one observer history is a gauge-facing interface
choice; the invariant physical content is the classifier-stable transport of
the rows across the displayed histories. Since the packet has no coordinate
for a host spacetime container, shared clock, primitive tensor, or detached
event point, none of those objects is exported.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Events and metrics are coordination readbacks]
\label{cor:philosophy-events-metrics-coordination-readbacks}
Within a general-relativity coordination bridge, an event is public only as a
licensed inter-Hist coordination of local records, and a metric expression is
public only as a bridge readback compressing local-clock, causal-cone,
distance-shape, transition, and curvature ledgers.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The locality-cell and causal-cone rows identify which local records may be
read by the same downstream consumer. That identification is the public event
surface; no point of an ambient manifold is present in the carrier.

The metric-facing read is likewise downstream from the displayed clock, rate,
distance-shape, transition, and curvature rows. It can serve as a host
geometric expression only after a bridge-preservation row relates that
expression back to the finite packet. Hence both event and metric are
coordination readbacks rather than primitives.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Geometric forcing certificate]
\label{def:philosophy-geometric-forcing-certificate}
A geometric forcing certificate is a finite scientific bridge packet
$$
\mathcal{F}=(\Lambda,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{P},\Gamma,R,S,U,M,A)
$$
where $\Lambda$ is an observation ledger, $\mathcal{G}$ is the declared
candidate geometry class, $\mathcal{P}$ is the audited probe class, $\Gamma$
is the candidate bridge, $R$ records realization of $\Lambda$ by $\Gamma$
within the declared tolerance, $S$ records the separation tests for candidates
not observationally equivalent to $\Gamma$, $U$ records the gauge or
representation freedom left invisible to $\mathcal{P}$, $M$ records
minimality of the exported structure, and $A$ records residual gaps and
no-host-leak boundaries.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ledger forcing up to gauge]
\label{def:philosophy-ledger-forcing-up-to-gauge}
For a geometric forcing certificate, write
$$
\Lambda\Vdash_{\mathcal{G},\mathcal{P}}[\Gamma]
$$
when $\Gamma$ realizes $\Lambda$, and every candidate
$\Delta\in\mathcal{G}$ that also realizes $\Lambda$ is observationally
equivalent to $\Gamma$ on all probes in $\mathcal{P}$, up to the gauge
or representation freedom recorded by $U$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Geometric forcing is not empty-kernel derivation]
\label{thm:philosophy-geometric-forcing-not-empty-kernel-derivation}
A geometric forcing certificate does not derive general relativity, quantum
mechanics, or any physical geometry from the empty BEDC kernel. It derives a
relative claim: given an observation ledger, an admissible candidate class,
an audited probe class, realization rows, separation rows, gauge rows,
minimality rows, and residual-gap rows, the surviving candidates collapse to
one observational equivalence class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The forcing judgment in \autoref{def:philosophy-ledger-forcing-up-to-gauge}
is parameterized by $\Lambda$, $\mathcal{G}$, and $\mathcal{P}$. Without these
fields there is no candidate comparison and no probe surface on which
surviving candidates could be tested.

Assume the certificate fields are present. The realization row $R$ admits
$\Gamma$ as a candidate compatible with the ledger. For any
$\Delta\in\mathcal{G}$, if $\Delta$ fails realization, the ledger excludes it.
If $\Delta$ realizes the ledger but is not observationally equivalent to
$\Gamma$ on $\mathcal{P}$, the separation row $S$ supplies a displayed probe
that distinguishes the two and records the conflict with $\Lambda$. Hence
every surviving candidate is equivalent to $\Gamma$ on the audited probes,
modulo the gauge row $U$. This proves only the relative forcing claim, not an
unconditional derivation of a host physical theory.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Forced geometry is a gauge class]
\label{cor:philosophy-forced-geometry-gauge-class}
The public output of a geometric forcing certificate is a gauge or
representation equivalence class, not a privileged coordinate presentation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The row $U$ records transformations that preserve all audited probe readouts.
No ledger row can distinguish two presentations related by such a
transformation. Therefore the certificate can export only the invariant
class $[\Gamma]$, together with the residual gaps recorded by $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Physical geometry as invariant compression]
\label{cor:philosophy-physical-geometry-invariant-compression}
In this layer, physical geometry is the invariant compression of observation
ledgers. A relativistic packet compresses clock, causal, lightlike,
free-route, transition, and curvature ledgers into Lorentzian-facing
geometry when those rows separate all non-equivalent alternatives in the
declared class. A quantum packet compresses preparation, measurement,
probability, interference, symmetry, composition, and update ledgers into
Hilbert/projective/operator-facing geometry under the same forcing pattern.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:philosophy-geometric-forcing-not-empty-kernel-derivation}
with the relevant observation ledger and candidate class. In the relativistic
case, the admissible probes inspect causal order, local clocks, causal-cone
or lightlike rows, route comparison, transition, and curvature data. In the
quantum case, the admissible probes inspect preparation equivalence,
measurement partitions, probability weights, interference, symmetries,
composition, and update rows. When the corresponding separation row excludes
all non-equivalent candidates, the exported structure is the unique invariant
compression of those ledgers up to the recorded gauge or representation row.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Constraint container]
\label{def:philosophy-constraint-container}
A constraint container for an observation ledger $\Lambda$ is a derived
bridge-facing packet
$$
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}=(Q,C,I,T,U,G,A)
$$
where $Q$ is the current observational quotient induced by the displayed
probes, $C$ is the surviving candidate class compatible with the ledger, $I$
is the invariant readback stable across the surviving candidates, $T$ is the
transport row between local interfaces, $U$ is the gauge or representation
freedom still invisible to the probes, $G$ is the residual gap surface, and
$A$ is the audit row recording source, classifier, witness, ledger, and
boundary data. The packet is a container only as a stabilized closure of
constraints; it is not a primitive host arena.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Constraint closure becomes container]
\label{thm:philosophy-constraint-closure-becomes-container}
Suppose a refinement sequence of observation ledgers keeps adding displayed
records and probes, so that the observational quotient is refined and the
surviving candidate class is restricted. If the surviving class stabilizes
to one gauge or representation class $[\Gamma]$, and the corresponding packet
has stable invariants, transport across local interfaces, compression of the
ledger with residual gaps exposed, prediction rows for further probes, and
auditable source/classifier/witness/boundary rows, then the stabilized packet
is licensed as a constraint container.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each displayed record adds a constraint to the ledger. A new probe can only
refine the observational quotient by making distinctions that were previously
unavailable to the packet; it cannot by itself add an unrecorded global arena.
Likewise, a new compatibility row can only restrict the declared candidate
class unless it is accompanied by a new residual-gap row.

If every non-equivalent candidate is eventually excluded by displayed
separation evidence, the surviving class is $[\Gamma]$ in the sense of
\autoref{def:philosophy-ledger-forcing-up-to-gauge}. The additional stability,
transport, compression, prediction, and audit rows state exactly that this
class can be reused as a public interface for later reads. That reuse is the
container role. Since the role is obtained only after refinement and
candidate restriction, the container is the stabilized closure of
observational constraints rather than a premise into which the records were
placed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Containers delimit future extensions]
\label{cor:philosophy-containers-delimit-future-extensions}
The container role of a stabilized geometric packet is its restriction of
admissible future extensions. It says which further records, routes, probes,
and bridge reads remain compatible with the current ledger, and which have
already been excluded by the boundary rows.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The stabilized packet contains the surviving candidate class, invariant
readback, transport, gauge, residual-gap, prediction, and audit rows. A
future extension is admissible only if it preserves those rows or records a
declared gap or boundary pressure. Thus the container is not a box holding
events; it is the public shape of the still-admissible continuation space
after the ledger has excluded incompatible alternatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Relativistic and quantum containers are derived]
\label{cor:philosophy-relativistic-quantum-containers-derived}
The spacetime-facing container in a relativistic bridge and the
Hilbert-facing container in a quantum bridge are derived interfaces. The
former is licensed only after clock, causal, lightlike, route, transition,
and curvature rows stabilize; the latter only after preparation,
measurement, probability, interference, symmetry, composition, and update rows
stabilize. Neither is a BEDC kernel primitive.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:philosophy-constraint-closure-becomes-container} to the two
candidate classes named by \autoref{cor:philosophy-physical-geometry-invariant-compression}.
In the relativistic case, the stable rows are the local physical ledgers that
support Lorentzian-facing readback. In the quantum case, the stable rows are
the operational probability ledgers that support Hilbert/projective/operator
readback. In both cases the public container appears only after the forcing
and audit rows are present.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Route-delay bridge packet]
\label{def:philosophy-route-delay-bridge-packet}
A route-delay bridge packet is a finite records-side comparison
$$
\mathcal{D}=(R_{1},R_{0},E,K,\Delta,A)
$$
where $R_{1}$ and $R_{0}$ are two displayed routes with the same emitted
and received locality-cell endpoints, $E$ is the endpoint-equivalence
certificate, $K$ is a shared local-clock and accumulation-edge
certificate, $\Delta$ is the route-pair clock-read token, and $A$ records
the refusal of a global clock, host event point, ambient metric, or
unlisted host theorem. The delay row is first a comparison of two
certified route reads, not an external real-valued time difference.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Route delay is not host proper time]
\label{thm:philosophy-route-delay-not-host-proper-time}
An accepted route-delay bridge packet does not identify BEDC clock reads
with relativistic proper time, coordinate time, Shapiro delay, or
Wigner--Smith scattering delay unless the relevant host correspondence
is present as a displayed bridge-preservation row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The packet $\mathcal{D}$ supplies only two routes, endpoint equivalence,
a shared local-clock certificate, an accumulation-edge read, and an
audit row. These rows make the BEDC delay token well formed as a
records-side comparison. Proper time, coordinate time, Shapiro delay,
and Wigner--Smith delay are host-side readings involving additional
metric, scattering, or asymptotic-state data. Since the audit row refuses
host leakage and unlisted host theorems, such readings can enter only by
a bridge-preservation row that relates the host expression back to the
displayed route-pair token.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scattering delay is a bridge reading]
\label{cor:philosophy-scattering-delay-bridge-reading}
A scattering-delay expression may be used in this layer only as a
host-side reading of a route-delay packet whose incoming route, outgoing
route, free reference route, interaction ledger, clock certificate, and
no-leak boundary are displayed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Scattering delay compares an interacting route with a free reference
route. On the BEDC side, such a comparison is meaningful only after
same-endpoint evidence and a shared clock certificate have been supplied
by \autoref{def:philosophy-route-delay-bridge-packet}. The scattering
formula belongs to the host model; the BEDC export is the finite
route-pair token together with the bridge row that preserves it.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Bridge-alignment learner]
\label{def:philosophy-bridge-alignment-learner}
A bridge-alignment learner is a derived machine-learning packet that
reads a records-side family $\mathcal R$, encodes it into a displayed
representation $Z_i$, and applies a predictor or adapter
$A_{\hat\theta}:Z_i\to Y$ to a declared readout family $Y$. Its effect
claim is accepted only through the source, split, prediction, loss,
NoLeak, stability, failure-surface, and NameCert rows of the
machine-learning effect certificate.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Readout alignment]
\label{def:philosophy-readout-alignment}
Representations $Z_i$ and $Z_j$ are readout-aligned over a readout
family $Y$ when there are accepted bridge-alignment learners
$A_i:Z_i\to Y$ and $A_j:Z_j\to Y$ whose certified errors lie inside the
same declared budget. This is an alignment of observable reads, not an
identity of the representation carriers.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Readout alignment is not latent equivalence]
\label{thm:philosophy-readout-alignment-not-latent-equivalence}
If metric-facing, scattering-facing, and information-clock-facing
representations all predict the same route-delay readout within a
declared budget, BEDC may record readout alignment. It may not identify
the latent representations without an additional bridge certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The accepted learners prove only that the composites
$A_i:Z_i\to Y$ preserve the declared readout within the stated budget.
Many non-equivalent representation carriers can admit predictors with
the same readout behavior. Latent equivalence requires more data:
translation in both directions, classifier preservation, return-comparison
witnesses, theorem or invariant preservation where claimed, and a
NoLeak ledger for the translation. Those rows are not supplied by
readout alignment alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bridge learning is evidence, not bridge closure]
\label{cor:philosophy-bridge-learning-evidence-not-closure}
A machine-learning adapter from a metric, scattering, or information
representation to a delay readout is evidence for a bridge candidate,
not a completed standard bridge.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The adapter may certify useful prediction from one representation to the
declared readout. A standard bridge additionally requires displayed
encoders and decoders, preservation rows, return-comparison witnesses,
NoLeak boundaries, and failure-surface accounting between the
interfaces being bridged. Prediction effectiveness is therefore a
records-to-readout certificate, not representation-level bridge closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common physical bridge discipline]
\label{thm:philosophy-common-physical-bridge-discipline}
Quantum and relativistic model bridges share a certificate discipline: the
world is not closed as a global object; only finite public projection,
geometry, causal, invariant, empirical, and failure ledgers are exported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the quantum bridge, unobserved state detail is not exported as a hidden
complete object. Measurement passes through a state row, an observable row, a
spectral projection row, and a scalar window.

In the relativistic bridge, global frame data is not exported as a primitive
object. Physical structure passes through local geometry rows,
cross-history causal rows, observer-symmetric rate rows, invariant readback,
and model-audit ledgers.

Both bridges therefore replace hidden totality by finite public certificates.
They differ in geometry and physical interpretation, but they obey the same
BEDC rule: a scientific bridge exports only displayed rows and their
classifier-stable transport, not an unlogged global world.
\end{proof}
