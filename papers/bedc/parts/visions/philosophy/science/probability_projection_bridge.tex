\subsection{Probability Projection Bridge}
\label{subsec:philosophy-probability-projection-bridge}

The probability interfaces already present in the concrete layer do not
license the claim that a probability space, a Hilbert space, a Fourier
analysis interface, a conditional-expectation row, a belief ledger, and a
spectral-measure packet are one object. They license a narrower reading:
each interface exposes finite scalar readbacks through named certificates,
and a scientific bridge may compare those readbacks only when the comparison
row itself is recorded.

\begin{definition}[Probability scalar shadow]
\label{def:philosophy-probability-scalar-shadow}
Let $P$ be a displayed $\ProbSpaceUp$ packet with total event $\Omega$,
event ledger $\mathcal A$, $\RealUp$-valued measure readback $\mu$, and
total-mass row $\mu(\Omega)\sim_R 1_R$. For a displayed event row
$A\in\mathcal A$, the probability scalar shadow of $A$ is the exported
$\RealUp$ endpoint
$$
\operatorname{Shadow}_{P}(A):=\mu(A).
$$
It is a one-dimensional public readback of the event row, not a complete
description of the internal structure that produced the event.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hilbert projection shadow]
\label{def:philosophy-hilbert-projection-shadow}
Let $H$ be a displayed $\HilbertUp$ packet whose public surface includes an
inner-product probe row. For a carried endpoint $v$ and a displayed observable
direction $u$, the Hilbert projection shadow along $u$ is the scalar endpoint
named by the finite probe
$$
\operatorname{ProjShadow}_{H}(v;u):=\langle v,u\rangle_H .
$$
When the packet also carries a norm or energy readback, that readback remains
a separate ledger row and is not inferred from an unrecorded ambient Hilbert
space.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Prob-Hilbert projection bridge]
\label{def:philosophy-prob-hilbert-projection-bridge}
A Prob-Hilbert projection bridge is a finite bridge packet
$$
B_{PH}=(P,H,A,\chi_A,\mathbf 1,\pi,L)
$$
where $P$ is a $\ProbSpaceUp$ packet, $H$ is a $\HilbertUp$ packet,
$A$ is a displayed event row of $P$, $\chi_A$ is an indicator row exposed to
the Hilbert side, $\mathbf 1$ is the total-event direction, $\pi$ is the
inner-product probe endpoint, and $L$ is the ledger that records the
classifier comparison
$$
\mu(A)\sim_R\langle \chi_A,\mathbf 1\rangle_H .
$$
The packet is public only to the extent that $A$, $\chi_A$, $\mathbf 1$,
$\pi$, the two scalar endpoints, the comparison row, and their provenance
are all displayed in the same finite certificate surface.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Probability readback as finite projection]
\label{thm:philosophy-probability-readback-finite-projection}
Every accepted Prob-Hilbert projection bridge makes the probability scalar
shadow of its displayed event readable as a Hilbert projection shadow along
the displayed total-event direction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The bridge carrier includes the event row $A$, the indicator row $\chi_A$,
the direction $\mathbf 1$, the probability readback $\mu(A)$, the Hilbert
probe $\langle \chi_A,\mathbf 1\rangle_H$, and the comparison row
$$
\mu(A)\sim_R\langle \chi_A,\mathbf 1\rangle_H .
$$
By \autoref{def:philosophy-probability-scalar-shadow}, the left endpoint is
$\operatorname{Shadow}_{P}(A)$. By
\autoref{def:philosophy-hilbert-projection-shadow}, the right endpoint is
$\operatorname{ProjShadow}_{H}(\chi_A;\mathbf 1)$. Thus the bridge reads the
probability endpoint as the named Hilbert projection endpoint. No further
ambient $L^2$ equality is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Fourier projection ledger]
\label{def:philosophy-fourier-projection-ledger}
Let $H$ be a displayed Hilbert-side packet and let
$\Phi=\{\phi_i:i\in I\}$ be a finite family of observable probe directions.
A Fourier projection ledger for a carried endpoint $f$ is the finite packet
$$
\mathcal F_I(f)=\{(i,c_i,L_i):i\in I,\ c_i=\langle f,\phi_i\rangle_H\},
$$
where $L_i$ records the source of $\phi_i$, the inner-product probe, the
integration or coefficient witness, the truncation window, classifier
evidence, and package provenance attached to that coefficient row.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Fourier coefficients are projection shadows]
\label{thm:philosophy-fourier-coefficients-projection-shadows}
In a Fourier projection ledger, each displayed coefficient is a Hilbert
projection shadow. The probability scalar shadow is the special case in
which $f=\chi_A$ and the probe direction is the total-event direction
$\mathbf 1$, provided the corresponding Prob-Hilbert bridge row is present.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each retained index $i$, the ledger defines the coefficient endpoint as
$$
c_i=\langle f,\phi_i\rangle_H .
$$
This is exactly the endpoint
$\operatorname{ProjShadow}_{H}(f;\phi_i)$ from
\autoref{def:philosophy-hilbert-projection-shadow}. If the finite family
contains the total-event direction and the bridge packet supplies the
comparison
$$
\mu(A)\sim_R\langle \chi_A,\mathbf 1\rangle_H ,
$$
then \autoref{thm:philosophy-probability-readback-finite-projection}
identifies the probability readback with that one coefficient row. The
argument does not use Plancherel, inversion, convergence closure, or a
complete orthonormal basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional expectation is a projected update surface]
\label{thm:philosophy-conditional-expectation-projected-update-surface}
A $\CondExpUp$ packet may be read as a projected update surface only through
its displayed random-variable readback, Hilbert projection witness, residual
continuation row, and ledger exactness rows. It does not export a completed
sub-$\sigma$-algebra projection theorem beyond those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The conditional-expectation carrier is presented in
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-condexp-namecert} through a random-variable
total-readback certificate, a Hilbert projection witness, and a continuation
ledger. A scientific projection bridge may therefore read the packet as an
update by projection only by projecting those displayed rows. Since the packet
has no public coordinate for a completed measurable-space quotient or an
unrestricted closed subspace, the bridge cannot promote the row to an
unbounded host theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Belief updates consume probability projections]
\label{cor:philosophy-belief-updates-consume-probability-projections}
When a $\BeliefUp$ packet consumes a probability row, the consumed datum is
the displayed probability scalar shadow or a bridge-recorded projection
shadow, together with the prior, observation, update trace, posterior endpoint,
and evidence ledger. The belief packet does not gain access to hidden
probability structure.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The belief carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-belief-namecert} exposes
only the prior row, observation row, update trace, probability row, posterior
endpoint, evidence ledger, and provenance rows. If the probability row is
supplied by a projection bridge, then its public content is the scalar
comparison recorded in that bridge. All other probability or Hilbert data are
outside the belief ledger and therefore unavailable to the consumer.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projection bridge cannot-claim boundary]
\label{thm:philosophy-projection-bridge-cannot-claim-boundary}
The projection bridge does not identify probability spaces with Hilbert
spaces, Fourier theory, conditional expectation, belief dynamics, or spectral
measures. It identifies only displayed scalar readbacks whose comparison rows
are present in a finite certificate ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each participating interface has its own carrier and classifier: probability
exports event-measure rows, Hilbert exports inner-product and projection
rows, Fourier exports finite coefficient probes, conditional expectation
exports random-variable readback and projection rows, belief exports
observation-update ledgers, and spectral measure exports projection-family
and scalar-window rows. A bridge can compare endpoints across these surfaces
only by displaying the relevant source rows, probe rows, scalar endpoints,
classifier comparison, continuation replay, and provenance. Therefore the
shared content is the recorded projection readback, not an ontology-level
fusion of the participating interfaces.
\end{proof}
