\begin{definition}[Menger theorem carrier]
\label{def:menger-theorem-carrier}
A $\MengerTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(G,s,t,\Pi,S,K,F,Q,L,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the $\GraphUp$ source row, carrying the finite vertex inventory, edge inventory, incidence data, and endpoint-adjacency checks.
\item $s$ and $t$ are the two distinguished vertex rows, with the endpoint-separation witness that keeps the cut-path question nontrivial inside $G$.
\item $\Pi$ is the internally disjoint path-family row, represented by finite $\ListUp$ data whose entries are paths from $s$ to $t$ and whose internal vertex ledgers are pairwise disjoint.
\item $S$ is the separator row, recording a finite vertex cut that meets every displayed $s$--$t$ path while excluding the two distinguished endpoints.
\item $K$ is the $\NatUp$ cardinality ledger, recording the common finite size comparison between the path family and the separator.
\item $F$ is the $\NetworkFlowUp$ handoff row, reading the path and cut data through the finite unit-capacity flow boundary rather than through a host max-flow theorem.
\item $Q$ is the $\MatchingUp$ handoff row, recording the auxiliary bipartite matching view used by finite alternating-route consumers.
\item $L$ records the $\ListUp$ accounting discipline for path inventories, separator inventories, and duplicate checks.
\item $A$ records the $\NatUp$ accounting discipline for lengths, family size, separator size, and equality of the two displayed finite cardinals.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for graph source, endpoints, path-family, separator, cardinality, flow, matching, list, natural-number, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a cut-path duality query through $G,s,t,\Pi,S,K,F,Q,L,A,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MengerTheoremUp$, $\GraphUp$, $\NetworkFlowUp$, $\MatchingUp$, $\ListUp$, $\NatUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MengerTheoremUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for an infinite graph, a host max-flow/min-cut theorem, quotient equality of paths, selected path families outside the packet, or choice over separators.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Menger theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:menger-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MengerTheoremUp$ carrier $M=(G,s,t,\Pi,S,K,F,Q,L,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MengerTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of graph source admission through $G$, endpoint exposure through $s,t$, internally disjoint path-family exposure through $\Pi$, separator exposure through $S$, cardinality-ledger exposure through $K$, network-flow handoff through $F$, matching handoff through $Q$, list accounting through $L$, natural-number accounting through $A$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:menger-theorem-carrier}. The graph and endpoint rows $G,s,t$ fix the finite source of the cut-path question before either side of the duality can be read.

The path-family row $\Pi$ and separator row $S$ are the two visible witnesses. The list ledger $L$ records path inventories, duplicate checks, and separator enumeration, while the natural-number ledger $A$ records the finite sizes inspected by the cardinality row $K$.

The handoff rows $F$ and $Q$ keep the same data readable by network-flow and matching consumers. They do not replace the packet by an ambient max-flow/min-cut theorem or by a selected matching oracle; they replay the displayed graph, path, cut, and counting rows.

Transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming are exactly $H,C,P,N$. Since no further coordinate stores infinite graph data, quotient path equality, a choice-selected path family, or a host theorem, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Menger theorem cut-path duality handoff]
\label{thm:menger-theorem-cut-path-duality-handoff}
Every cut-path duality consumer of an accepted $\MengerTheoremUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
\GraphUp,\ \ListUp,\ \NatUp,\ \NetworkFlowUp,\ \MatchingUp
\longmapsto
G,s,t,\Pi,S,K,F,Q,L,A,H,C,P,N .
$$
The handoff states only that the displayed internally disjoint path-family row and displayed separator row meet in the same finite cardinality ledger. It does not export a theorem for infinite graphs, quotient equality of path objects, selected witnesses outside the packet, arbitrary host max-flow/min-cut, or countable choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with \autoref{thm:menger-theorem-namecert-obligations}. A consumer must expose the finite graph source and the two endpoint rows before it can inspect either the internally disjoint path family or the separator.

Next read the list and natural-number ledgers. The path-family row $\Pi$ is a finite $\ListUp$ inventory of paths, the separator row $S$ is a finite $\ListUp$ inventory of vertices, and $K,A$ record the $\NatUp$ equality between their displayed sizes.

Finally read the flow and matching handoffs. The $\NetworkFlowUp$ row presents the same data as a unit-capacity cut-flow boundary, while the $\MatchingUp$ row presents the same finite incidence discipline to alternating-route consumers. Both handoffs remain attached to $G,s,t,\Pi,S,K,L,A$.

Thus the duality consumer receives a finite cut-path certificate and no stronger host theorem. The refused endpoints remain outside the carrier because no row supplies infinite graph data, quotient path equality, selected external witnesses, arbitrary max-flow/min-cut, or countable choice.
\end{proof}
