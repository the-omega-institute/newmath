It sits after the charted source carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-manifold-namecert}, the local-trivialization rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bundle-namecert}, the circle boundary source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-s1-namecert}, the horizontal-lift ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ehresmann-connection-namecert}, and the cycle-read surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-homology-namecert}. The chapter names only displayed singular chart rows, punctured-neighbourhood rows, circle-base rows, fibre-bundle rows, connection-lift rows, homology readback, monodromy replay, provenance, transport, and local naming; it does not assert analytic Milnor existence, choose a global tube, identify fibres by host diffeomorphism, or import a Picard--Lefschetz formula.

\begin{definition}[Milnor fibration carrier]
\label{def:milnor-fibration-carrier}
A $\MilnorFibrationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(X,U,S,F,B,E,H,L,T,R,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\ManifoldUp$ charted source row carrying the inspected singular neighbourhood and its chart ledger.
\item $U$ is the punctured-neighbourhood boundary row, naming the deleted singular point and the finite boundary window used by the read.
\item $S$ is the $\SOneUp$ base-circle row through which phase or argument reads are exposed.
\item $F$ is the fibre row over the displayed base-circle observation, with no host fibre quotient or selected representative.
\item $B$ is the $\BundleUp$ local-trivialization row for the displayed map over the visible circle window.
\item $E$ is the $\EhresmannConnectionUp$ horizontal-lift row used only after the bundle and base-circle rows have been read.
\item $H$ is the $\HomologyUp$ cycle-read row on the displayed fibre boundary.
\item $L$ is the monodromy ledger, recording the finite continuation route from one circle read to the next through $E$ and $H$.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,U,S,F,B,E,H,L$.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for the fibration, lift, cycle, and monodromy reads.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MilnorFibrationUp$, $\ManifoldUp$, $\BundleUp$, $\SOneUp$, $\EhresmannConnectionUp$, $\HomologyUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MilnorFibrationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for analytic existence of the Milnor tube, host smooth-fibre equality, selected global trivialization, quotient vanishing-cycle object, or Picard--Lefschetz transformation.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Milnor fibration NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:milnor-fibration-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\MilnorFibrationUp$ packet $M=(X,U,S,F,B,E,H,L,T,R,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MilnorFibrationUp}$ surface is exhausted by carrier admission for $M$, manifold-source exposure through $X,U$, circle-base exposure through $S$, fibre and local-trivialization exposure through $F,B$, horizontal-lift exposure through $E$, homology-cycle exposure through $H$, monodromy-ledger exposure through $L$, componentwise transport through $T$, displayed replay through $R$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. A consumer cannot inspect a monodromy or vanishing-cycle boundary before those rows have been displayed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted packet to the carrier of \autoref{def:milnor-fibration-carrier}. Its coordinates list exactly the manifold, punctured-neighbourhood, circle-base, fibre, bundle, connection, homology, monodromy, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The $\NameCert$ surface is therefore the local package over those displayed rows. The bundle row cannot be read before the manifold and circle rows, the connection row cannot be read before the bundle row, and the homology row cannot be consumed by monodromy before the fibre boundary has been displayed.

Since the carrier contains no host analytic tube, no global trivialization coordinate, and no quotient vanishing-cycle object, any accepted downstream monodromy read is exhausted by the listed rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Milnor fibration monodromy route]
\label{thm:milnor-fibration-monodromy-route}
Every monodromy-facing consumer of an accepted $\MilnorFibrationUp$ packet must read the dependency route
$$
\ManifoldUp \longmapsto \SOneUp \longmapsto \BundleUp \longmapsto \EhresmannConnectionUp \longmapsto \HomologyUp .
$$
The manifold and punctured-neighbourhood rows delimit the inspected singular boundary, the circle row supplies the base loop, the bundle row supplies the local fibre handoff, the Ehresmann row supplies the finite horizontal-lift replay, and the homology row supplies the cycle endpoint read by the monodromy ledger. The route exports no arbitrary analytic fibration theorem, no selected fibre equivalence, and no Picard--Lefschetz formula.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the source boundary. The rows $X$ and $U$ expose the charted neighbourhood and deleted-point boundary before any circle or fibre read is available.

Next read the base and bundle rows. The $\SOneUp$ row $S$ supplies the visible loop coordinate, and the $\BundleUp$ row $B$ supplies only the local fibre handoff over that displayed loop.

Finally route continuation through $E,H,L$. The $\EhresmannConnectionUp$ row gives the horizontal-lift replay, the $\HomologyUp$ row gives the cycle endpoint on the displayed fibre, and the monodromy ledger records only this finite continuation. Therefore the consumer sees exactly the stated dependency route and none of the excluded host analytic data.
\end{proof}
