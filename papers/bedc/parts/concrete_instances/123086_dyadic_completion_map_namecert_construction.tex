\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DyadicCompletionMapUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real-number completion, the $\DyadicCompletionMapUp$ packet records the finite map object by which dyadic rational source data are read into Real-sealed regular Cauchy names. It sits over the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream-window discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a map surface from dyadic source rows to sealed regular names; it does not quotient streams, choose selected limits, use countable choice, or assert Real completeness.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic completion map carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-completion-map-carrier}
A $\DyadicCompletionMapUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(D,S,R,E,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ source and tolerance row, recording the dyadic rational input, radius, slack, and finite comparison budget.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row generated from the dyadic source and the requested precision windows.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback attached to the same stream windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal reached only after $D,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $W$ is the map witness row, recording that the dyadic source window and the regular-rational readback use the same finite tolerance ledger.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for dyadic source, stream window, regular readback, real seal, map witness, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from dyadic source data through stream windows and regular readback to the sealed real-name boundary.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicCompletionMapUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicCompletionMapUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for quotient streams, selected limits, countable choice, host equality of completed names, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic completion map NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-completion-map-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicCompletionMapUp$ carrier
$$
M=(D,S,R,E,W,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicCompletionMapUp}$ surface consists exactly of dyadic source admission through $D$, finite-window generation through $S$, regular rational readback through $R$, terminal sealing through $E$, map-witness exactness through $W$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. Every Real-facing or completion-facing consumer must read \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} in that order before consuming the map boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-completion-map-carrier}. The displayed tuple gives exactly the dyadic source row $D$, stream window row $S$, regular readback row $R$, Real seal row $E$, map witness row $W$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Read the analytic route in order. $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ supplies the finite dyadic tolerance ledger, $\StreamNameUp$ supplies the requested finite windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ supplies the regular rational readback attached to those windows, and $\RealUp$ is reached only as a terminal seal after the regular route has been displayed.

The witness row $W$ binds the dyadic ledger to the regular readback, while $H,C,P,N$ preserve transport, replay, provenance, and local naming for the same route. Since the packet contains no quotient stream, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host completed-name equality, or ambient Real-completeness row, none of those principles can become a local NameCert obligation.
\end{proof}
