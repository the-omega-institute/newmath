Following Bourbaki, Kelley, and Bishop-style completion functoriality, the $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$ packet records the L10 route that transports uniformly continuous metric maps across the displayed completion surface. It specializes the metric-completion carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, the functorial transport surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completionfunctor-namecert}, and the Cauchy-name functor packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletionfunctor-namecert}. \begin{definition}[MetricCompletionFunctor carrier]
\label{def:metric-completion-functor-carrier}
A $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(M_0,M_1,G,E,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M_0$ is the source $\MetricCompletionUp$ row, including the displayed metric source, its Cauchy-filter or Cauchy-net cofinal evidence, finite readback, and separated-limit comparison row.
\item $M_1$ is the target $\MetricCompletionUp$ row with the same kind of displayed completion evidence and readback surface.
\item $G$ is the metric-map row whose finite modulus and graph ledger send source observations to target observations before completion-facing reads are taken.
\item $E$ is the extension row inherited from the $\CompletionFunctorUp$ and $\CauchyCompletionFunctorUp$ surfaces, routing the displayed map through the two completion rows.
\item $T$ is the functoriality ledger comparing identity and composition reads by finite $\hsame$ transport over $M_0$, $M_1$, $G$, and $E$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the two metric-completion rows, the map row, the extension row, the functoriality ledger, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
M_0,G,M_1 \longmapsto E \longmapsto T,H,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CompletionFunctorUp$, $\CauchyCompletionFunctorUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\CauchyNetUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricCompletionFunctorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for quotient completion equality, selected limits, host function equality, a host completed metric space, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

Every obligation is read from the displayed rows $M_0,M_1,G,E,T,H,C,P,N$ and their finite $\hsame$ and $\Cont$ routes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:metric-completion-functor-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes exactly the two metric-completion rows, the displayed map ledger, the extension row, functoriality ledger, transport rows, replay rows, provenance, and local naming row.

The source and target exactness clauses require every completion-facing read to pass through $M_0$ or $M_1$ before the extension row is inspected. The map-modulus clause keeps the graph ledger $G$ attached to the finite source observations and target observations already visible in those rows.

Extension exactness is the coordinate $E$. It is read from the existing completion-functor and Cauchy-completion-functor surfaces, so no second extension route or host function equality is available.

Identity and composition are the public roles of $T$, transported componentwise by $H$ and replayed through $C$. The provenance and local naming rows then exhaust the packet, leaving no coordinate for quotient completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient completeness.

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFunctor L10 route]
\label{thm:metric-completion-functor-route}
Every L10 completion-route consumer of an accepted $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$ packet factors through the finite chain
$$
M_0,G,M_1 \longmapsto E \longmapsto T,H,C,P,N .
$$
The route first reads the source $\MetricCompletionUp$ packet, then the displayed map ledger, then the target $\MetricCompletionUp$ packet, and only then the extension and functoriality rows supplied by the completion-functor surfaces. It therefore connects $\MetricCompletionUp$ to $\CompletionFunctorUp$ and $\CauchyCompletionFunctorUp$ by displayed finite data rather than by a quotient completion object or a host-level completed metric functor.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-functor-carrier}. The first completion-facing coordinate is $M_0$, so the consumer begins with the source metric-completion evidence, including its filter or net branch and finite readback row.

The next analytic coordinate is the map ledger $G$. Its finite modulus and graph rows move displayed source observations to target observations before any extension read is taken.

The target completion row $M_1$ supplies the completion-facing landing surface. The extension row $E$ then repacks the displayed map through the completion-functor and Cauchy-completion-functor interfaces, while the functoriality ledger $T$ records identity and composition comparisons as finite transport.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name the same route. Since the carrier has no quotient, selected-limit, host-function-equality, or ambient-completeness coordinate, every L10 consumer receives only the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFunctor map-modulus compatibility]
\label{thm:metric-completion-functor-map-modulus-compatibility}
For an accepted $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier
$F=(M_0,M_1,G,E,T,H,C,P,N)$, every consumer read of map-modulus compatibility
is forced through the displayed map row $G$ of
\autoref{def:metric-completion-functor-carrier}. The source observations
come from $M_0$, the target observations land in $M_1$, and the modulus row is
read before the extension row $E$ can be consumed. No consumer may replace
this finite row by host uniform continuity or a function-space equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier coordinates. The only row that relates source observations
to target observations is $G$, and it is displayed between the source and
target completion rows $M_0$ and $M_1$.

The extension row $E$ is downstream of that graph and modulus data. A consumer
therefore cannot request an extension read until the finite modulus and graph
ledger in $G$ have already been exposed.

Transport and replay are supplied by $H$ and $C$, with provenance and naming
through $P$ and $N$. Since no host function-space row exists in the carrier,
the compatibility claim is exactly the visible finite row.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFunctor extension-ledger exactness]
\label{thm:metric-completion-functor-extension-ledger-exactness}
For an accepted $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier
$F=(M_0,M_1,G,E,T,H,C,P,N)$, every extension read is exactly the displayed
row $E$ named in \autoref{def:metric-completion-functor-carrier}. The row $E$
must be read after the source completion row $M_0$, the metric-map row $G$,
and the target completion row $M_1$, and it may only export the completion
handoff already visible through $\CompletionFunctorUp$ and
$\CauchyCompletionFunctorUp$ rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the route in \autoref{thm:metric-completion-functor-route}. The
extension coordinate is not an independent host map; it is the displayed row
encountered after the two completion packets and the map ledger have been
read.

Exactness means that a consumer cannot choose another extension witness or
compare two extensions by host function equality. The only usable extension
data are the entries of $E$, transported by $H$ and replayed by $C$.

The provenance row $P$ records the source of the completion-functor handoff,
and $N$ names the local certificate. These rows exhaust the extension ledger
visible to downstream completion consumers.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFunctor identity and composition functoriality]
\label{thm:metric-completion-functor-identity-composition-functoriality}
For an accepted $\MetricCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier
$F=(M_0,M_1,G,E,T,H,C,P,N)$, every identity or composition read is forced
through the functoriality ledger $T$ displayed in
\autoref{def:metric-completion-functor-carrier}. Identity reads compare the
visible completion row with its own displayed map ledger, and composition
reads compare two displayed map rows only after their extension rows have
been read. The comparison is finite $\hsame$ transport, not host equality of
completed maps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The route theorem places $T$ after the extension row $E$. Thus identity and
composition are not prerequisites for constructing a host functor; they are
ledger reads over already displayed completion and extension data.

For identity, $T$ records the finite transport comparing the source completion
row with the identity map ledger. For composition, $T$ records the finite
transport comparing the two-stage displayed map read with the displayed
composite read after their extension rows are available.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name those comparisons.
Because the carrier has no host completed-map equality coordinate, the
functoriality surface is exactly the finite ledger $T$.
\end{proof}
