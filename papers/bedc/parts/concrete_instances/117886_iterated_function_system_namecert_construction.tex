\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\IteratedFunctionSystemUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-iterated-function-system-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Hutchinson on iterated function systems, the $\IteratedFunctionSystemUp$ packet records a finite family of contraction maps on a compact complete metric carrier as BEDC naming data. It sits after the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the complete-metric limit surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, the compact-metric boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the map interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the contraction route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-contractionmapping-namecert}, the tolerance schedules of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchymodulus-namecert}, and the real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only the finite contraction-family surface and its dependency rows; it does not construct an attractor, a hyperspace completion, a Hausdorff metric fixed point, or a measure on the limit set.

\begin{definition}[IteratedFunctionSystem carrier]
\label{def:iterated-function-system-carrier}
An $\IteratedFunctionSystemUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(X,M,K,F,L,R,Q,S,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following rows:
\begin{itemize}
\item $X$ is the displayed $\MetricUp$ source carrier.
\item $M$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ limit-witness surface for Cauchy reads in $X$.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ total-bounded and closed-boundary row used by finite cover consumers.
\item $F$ is a finite ledger of $\ContinuousMapUp$ rows whose sources and targets are $X$.
\item $L$ is the finite Lipschitz ledger, with each entry routed through a $\ContractionMappingUp$ row.
\item $R$ is the $\CauchyModulusUp$ threshold ledger used by the contraction estimates.
\item $Q$ is the $\RealUp$ seal-facing row for the displayed contraction constants.
\item $S$ is the finite index and family-membership row naming which maps are in the system.
\item $A$ is the attractor-obligation placeholder row: it records the route a later fixed-set consumer must inspect, but it is not itself an attractor construction.
\item $H,C,P,N$ are the componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert_{\IteratedFunctionSystemUp}$ rows.
\end{itemize}
Two carriers are classified only by the componentwise rows $X,M,K,F,L,R,Q,S,A,H,C,P,N$. The classifier has no coordinate for quotient hyperspaces, selected fixed compact sets, ambient classical compactness, choice of a point in the attractor, or host equality of functions.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[IteratedFunctionSystem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:iterated-function-system-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\IteratedFunctionSystemUp$ carrier
$$
I=(X,M,K,F,L,R,Q,S,A,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\IteratedFunctionSystemUp}$ surface is exhausted by five obligations: the carrier is inhabited by finite displayed rows; classifier equality is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive by componentwise $\hsame$ over those rows; and classifier transport preserves the metric, complete-metric, compact-metric, continuous-map, contraction, modulus, Real-seal, finite-index, attractor-obligation, replay, provenance, and naming coordinates. No obligation may be discharged by a host hyperspace quotient, selected attractor, global choice principle, or ambient compactness theorem outside the displayed packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:iterated-function-system-carrier}. Every row is a finite $\BHist$ coordinate, and the classifier compares only those coordinates through componentwise $\hsame$.

Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are therefore the corresponding componentwise $\hsame$ laws for $X,M,K,F,L,R,Q,S,A,H,C,P,N$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet.

The excluded objects are not carrier coordinates. A NameCert consumer can inspect only the displayed metric, compactness, map-family, contraction, modulus, Real-seal, and structural rows, so no host hyperspace quotient or selected attractor can become a local obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[IteratedFunctionSystem contraction route]
\label{thm:iterated-function-system-contraction-route}
Every contraction-family request served by an accepted $\IteratedFunctionSystemUp$ carrier
$$
I=(X,M,K,F,L,R,Q,S,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
must first read the metric source $X$, the continuous-map ledger $F$, the finite family row $S$, the contraction ledger $L$, and the modulus threshold ledger $R$. It may then use the $\CompleteMetricUp$ row $M$, the $\CompactMetricUp$ row $K$, and the $\RealUp$ seal row $Q$ only through the same componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows $H,C,P,N$. Thus the contraction route is a finite displayed BEDC route, not a direct appeal to a Hutchinson attractor theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a contraction-family request. The carrier exposes maps only through the finite continuous-map ledger $F$ and records family membership only through $S$, both over the metric source $X$.

Read the contraction ledger $L$ and threshold ledger $R$ next. These rows are the only places where Lipschitz bounds and requested tolerances are stored before a later compact or complete-metric consumer can use them.

Finally pass through $M,K,Q,H,C,P,N$. The complete-metric, compact-metric, and Real-seal rows are consumed through the same displayed packet, while $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name that packet. Since the carrier does not contain a hyperspace fixed-set row, the route cannot claim an attractor construction.
\end{proof}
