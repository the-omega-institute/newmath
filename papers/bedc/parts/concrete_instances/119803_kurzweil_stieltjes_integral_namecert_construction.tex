Following Kurzweil, Henstock, and Stieltjes, the $\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp$ packet records a finite gauge-controlled Stieltjes integration surface in which a displayed integrand is read against a bounded-variation integrator through tagged gauges and regulated handoff rows. It sits after the Riemann--Stieltjes packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riemannstieltjes-namecert}, the gauge-integral surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gauge-integral-namecert}, the bounded-variation ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedvariation-namecert}, the regulated-integral handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulatedintegral-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only a finite gauge-variation carrier; it does not assert measure completion, host quotient equality of integrands, a selected primitive, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Kurzweil--Stieltjes integral carrier]
\label{def:kurzweil-stieltjes-integral-carrier}
A $\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the $\GaugeIntegralUp$ row exposing the finite gauge cover, tagged cells, and admissible local radii.
\item $R$ is the $\RiemannStieltjesUp$ row supplying the tagged Stieltjes sum surface already accepted by the Riemann--Stieltjes certificate.
\item $A$ is the $\BoundedVariationUp$ integrator ledger, including transported endpoint jumps and finite variation bounds for the displayed cells.
\item $T$ is the gauge-tagged refinement row tying the cells of $G$ to the Stieltjes tags read by $R$.
\item $I$ is the $\RegulatedIntegralUp$ handoff row reached only after the gauge, Stieltjes, variation, and refinement rows are present.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for the accepted integral value.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the gauge, Stieltjes, variation, refinement, handoff, real seal, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from gauge cells to tags, from bounded-variation jumps to Stieltjes sums, from refinement rows to the regulated handoff, and from the handoff to the $\RealUp$ seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp$, $\RiemannStieltjesUp$, $\GaugeIntegralUp$, $\BoundedVariationUp$, $\RegulatedIntegralUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for measure spaces, completed variation objects, host quotient equality of functions, selected antiderivatives, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kurzweil--Stieltjes integral NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:kurzweil-stieltjes-integral-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp$ carrier $K=(G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp}$ surface consists exactly of gauge admission, accepted Riemann--Stieltjes readback, bounded-variation integrator control, gauge-tagged refinement, regulated-integral handoff, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay, package provenance, and local naming through $G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:kurzweil-stieltjes-integral-carrier}. The rows $G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N$ are the complete public packet, so every accepted read is first a finite gauge read, then a Riemann--Stieltjes tagged-sum read, then a bounded-variation and refinement read.

The regulated handoff row $I$ is downstream of those rows. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve the displayed order, while $P$ and $N$ only record provenance and local naming. Since the carrier contains no measure, quotient, selected primitive, or ambient completeness coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kurzweil--Stieltjes gauge-variation handoff]
\label{thm:kurzweil-stieltjes-integral-gauge-variation-handoff}
Every accepted $\KurzweilStieltjesIntegralUp$ consumer of a terminal $\RealUp$ value must read the route
$$
\GaugeIntegralUp,\ \RiemannStieltjesUp,\ \BoundedVariationUp,\ \RegulatedIntegralUp,\ \RealUp,\ \BHist,\ \hsame,\ \Cont,\ \Pkg,\ \NameCert
$$
through $G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N$ before it treats the output as an integral value. The route consumes existing gauge and bounded-variation rows and does not assert measure completion, host quotient equality, selected primitives, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted carrier $K=(G,R,A,T,I,E,H,C,P,N)$. The gauge row $G$ supplies the only admissible local cells, and the Riemann--Stieltjes row $R$ supplies the only tagged Stieltjes sum surface.

The bounded-variation row $A$ controls the integrator contribution on the same displayed cells. The refinement row $T$ ties the gauge tags to the Stieltjes tags, so a downstream consumer cannot bypass either the gauge surface or the variation ledger.

The gauge-variation handoff consumes the bounded-variation ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedvariation-namecert} and the regulated-function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulated-function-namecert} before it may expose the Kurzweil--Stieltjes $\RealUp$ seal.

The handoff row $I$ then exposes the regulated-integral route, and the terminal seal $E$ is available only after that handoff. Structural transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same order through $H,C,P,N$, leaving no coordinate for the excluded host objects.
\end{proof}
