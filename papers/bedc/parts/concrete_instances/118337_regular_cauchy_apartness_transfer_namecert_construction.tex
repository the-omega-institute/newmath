\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges, \emph{Constructive Analysis}, the $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$ packet records the finite handoff by which apartness data already exposed over regular Cauchy names are transported into later real-name and completion-facing consumers. It sits over the regular rational source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic arithmetic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}; it also consumes the order boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-apartness-order-namecert}, the reflector of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-apartness-reflector-namecert}, the located-apartness route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-located-apartness-namecert}, and the finite budget row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-apartness-budget-namecert}. The packet is a constructive transfer surface for apartness reads, not a trichotomy principle, quotient-real equality test, selected-limit operation, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness assertion.

\begin{definition}[Regular Cauchy apartness transfer carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(X,Y,O,B,F,L,R,W,D,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ and $Y$ are the source $\RegSeqRatUp$ rows whose apartness relation is being transferred.
\item $O$ is the $\RegularCauchyApartnessOrderUp$ comparison row that supplies the order-facing apartness read.
\item $B$ is the $\RegularCauchyApartnessBudgetUp$ positive lower-bound budget required before any strict or apartness-facing read is public.
\item $F$ is the $\RegularCauchyApartnessReflectorUp$ row that reflects the order read into an inspected apartness surface.
\item $L$ is the $\RegularCauchyLocatedApartnessUp$ row that keeps the apartness read located over displayed regular Cauchy data.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after the regular rational and apartness rows have been displayed.
\item $W$ records the finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows inspected by the transfer.
\item $D$ records the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance and lower-bound ledgers attached to those windows.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, order, budget, reflector, located-apartness, seal, window, and dyadic rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay for the same transfer route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RegularCauchyApartnessOrderUp$, $\RegularCauchyApartnessReflectorUp$, $\RegularCauchyLocatedApartnessUp$, $\RegularCauchyApartnessBudgetUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for trichotomy, quotient real equality, selected limits, countable choice, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy apartness transfer NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $T$, source admission for $X,Y$, apartness-order admission through $O$, positive-budget admission through $B$, reflector admission through $F$, located-apartness admission through $L$, terminal real-seal handoff through $R$, finite window and dyadic ledger admission through $W,D$, componentwise transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-carrier}. The listed rows are exactly the available coordinates of $T$.

The apartness-facing obligations are inherited from the sibling packets. The order row $O$ supplies the comparison surface, the budget row $B$ supplies the positive lower-bound evidence, the reflector row $F$ exposes the inspected apartness read, and the located row $L$ keeps the read attached to displayed regular Cauchy data.

The structural rows $W,D,H,C,P,N$ keep the same finite route under window inspection, dyadic readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since no further coordinate is present, the NameCert surface is exactly the displayed transfer packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy apartness transfer real-seal handoff]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-real-seal-handoff}
Every $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$ packet reaches the terminal seal $R$ only after the regular rational sources $X,Y$, the apartness-order row $O$, the budget row $B$, the reflector row $F$, the located-apartness row $L$, the finite stream windows $W$, and the dyadic ledger $D$ have been displayed. The handoff exports a real-facing apartness read, but no quotient equality, selected representative, trichotomy decision, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-namecert-obligations}. They place $R$ downstream of the source, apartness, budget, reflector, located, window, and dyadic rows.

Use the sibling order and budget boundaries. The $\RegularCauchyApartnessOrderUp$ and $\RegularCauchyApartnessBudgetUp$ rows force the positive apartness read through displayed finite lower-bound data before any real-facing seal is public.

Finally use the reflector and located-apartness rows. They preserve the inspected apartness route into the real-facing handoff, while the absent objects are not coordinates of $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy apartness transfer transport]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-transport}
Componentwise $\hsame$ transport of an accepted $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$ carrier preserves the source rows, apartness-order row, budget row, reflector row, located-apartness row, terminal seal, finite windows, dyadic ledger, replay, provenance, and local naming together. A transported packet therefore carries the same apartness-transfer route and cannot detach the reflected apartness read from its displayed budget, window, dyadic, or real-seal boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the transport row $H$ from the carrier. It is componentwise over every displayed coordinate used by the transfer.

Apply the NameCert obligation theorem. The order, budget, reflector, located-apartness, window, dyadic, and seal rows are admitted as one packet surface, so a transport of the packet transports those rows together.

The replay and provenance rows preserve the same route for downstream consumers. Thus transport may rename the finite packet but cannot split the apartness read from its displayed constructive witnesses.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy apartness transfer non-escape]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-apartness-transfer-nonescape}
Every downstream read of an accepted $\RegularCauchyApartnessTransferUp$ packet factors through
$$
X,Y,O,B,F,L,R,W,D,H,C,P,N .
$$
It may inspect regular rational sources, stream windows, dyadic lower-bound data, apartness-order evidence, the apartness budget, reflector and located-apartness rows, the terminal real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row. It cannot inspect trichotomy, quotient real equality, selected limits, countable choice, host real equality, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier definition and the NameCert obligations. They enumerate all rows available to a consumer of the accepted packet.

Apply the real-seal handoff and transport theorems. The handoff keeps the real-facing read after the displayed apartness route, while transport preserves the same finite route component by component.

The excluded principles are absent from the carrier inventory and from the sibling apartness rows used by the transfer. Therefore no downstream consumer can extract them from the packet.
\end{proof}
