\begin{theorem}[Cauchy selection subsequence window]
\label{thm:cauchy-selection-principle-subsequence-window}
Every subsequence-facing read of an accepted $\CauchySelectionPrincipleUp$ carrier $S=(D,W,R,E,Q,H,C,P,N)$ is confined to the displayed finite window $D,W,R$. The read may inspect a dyadic precision request, a bounded StreamName observation family, and the attached RegSeqRat readback, but it receives no selected infinite subsequence and no countable-choice schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The only value-facing rows before the Real seal are the dyadic ledger $D$, the stream-window row $W$, and the regular rational readback $R$.

The continuation row $C$ fixes the order of inspection. A precision request first reads the dyadic budget, then the bounded stream observations, and then the regular rational readback attached to those observations.

The structural rows $H,P,N$ preserve the same displayed route, and $Q$ is only a comparison row for accepted reads. Since no row stores a selected infinite subsequence or a choice schedule, every subsequence-facing read remains inside the finite window.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy selection regularity handoff]
\label{thm:cauchy-selection-principle-regularity-handoff}

The handoff is therefore a regular rational readback, not a quotient stream and not a selected representative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the displayed rows $D$ and $W$. They bind the finite precision request and the bounded observations from which the selection read is made.

The row $R$ consumes exactly those displayed rows as a $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback. Thus the completion-facing handoff is already regular-rational before the Real seal or comparison row can be used.

Rows $E,Q,H,C,P,N$ do not add another value source. They seal, compare, transport, replay, source, and name the same handoff, so the route cannot introduce quotient streams, selected representatives, or countable choice.
\end{proof}
