\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LowerTopologyUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-lower-topology-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Scott and Lawson domain theory, the $\LowerTopologyUp$ packet records the lower-topology component of an ordered carrier as finite NameCert data. It sits between the specialization discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-preorder-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-poset-namecert}, the indexed-open surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the observable-open classifier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sierpinski-space-namecert}, and the domain-topology neighbours \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-scott-topology-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lawson-topology-namecert}. The packet names lower-open reads through displayed order and observable-open rows; it does not import a host powerset topology, a selected family of all opens, quotient point equality, choice over down-closed witnesses, or an ambient compactness theorem.

\begin{definition}[Lower topology carrier]
\label{def:lower-topology-carrier}
A $\LowerTopologyUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(R,Q,T,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the $\PreorderUp$ specialization source row, carrying the finite comparison reads used by lower-open inspection.
\item $Q$ is the $\PosetUp$ separated-order row when the lower topology is read over a displayed antisymmetric order surface.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ indexed-open row that records the finite lower-open neighbourhood requests admitted by the packet.
\item $S$ is the $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$ observable-open row, reading membership in each displayed lower-open request as a finite truth observation.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the order, topology, observable-open, replay, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an order comparison request through the indexed-open row and then through the observable-open classifier.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LowerTopologyUp$, $\PreorderUp$, $\PosetUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LowerTopologyUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these eight rows. It has no coordinate for a host family of all lower opens, arbitrary powersets, selected bases, quotient point equality, countable choice, or a compactness principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lower topology NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lower-topology-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LowerTopologyUp$ carrier $L=(R,Q,T,S,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LowerTopologyUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the preorder or poset row, lower-open topology row, observable-open row, transport row, replay row, provenance row, and local naming row; lower-open membership inspection through $T$ and $S$; order compatibility through $R$ and, when present, $Q$; and provenance exhaustion through $P,N$. No obligation is discharged by a host powerset topology, selected lower-open basis, quotient point equality, countable-choice schedule, Scott-open completeness, or Lawson compactness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier of \autoref{def:lower-topology-carrier}. The only order-facing data are $R$ and $Q$, so every lower-open read must begin with the finite preorder or poset comparisons already present in the packet.

Read the topology and observable-open rows next. The row $T$ records the indexed lower-open requests, while $S$ turns each membership read into displayed Sierpinski truth data. Thus the topology route remains a finite observation surface rather than a host powerset of opens.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$. Componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same row order from comparison data to observable-open data. Since none of the excluded host objects has a carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lower topology specialization scope]
\label{thm:lower-topology-specialization-scope}
Every accepted $\LowerTopologyUp$ consumer that reads specialization information must factor through the finite route
$$
\PreorderUp \longmapsto \PosetUp \longmapsto \TopologyUp \longmapsto \SierpinskiSpaceUp .
$$
The preorder row supplies comparison data, the optional poset row supplies separated-order data, the topology row names lower-open neighbourhood requests, and the Sierpinski row exposes membership observations. The route may be consumed by $\ScottTopologyUp$ and $\LawsonTopologyUp$ as their lower-topology component, but it exports no host powerset topology, selected open basis, quotient order, choice principle, or compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the order rows of \autoref{def:lower-topology-carrier}. A specialization-facing request first reads $R$, and when a separated order surface is present it reads $Q$ as the displayed $\PosetUp$ handoff.

The request then passes to $T$. Lower-open neighbourhoods are indexed rows in the $\TopologyUp$ surface, so the consumer sees only the finite lower-open requests carried by the packet.

The final observable read is $S$. Membership in those indexed opens is exposed through $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$ truth data, after which $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. The Scott and Lawson neighbours may consume this component only through the displayed rows, so no external topology or choice object is exported.
\end{proof}
