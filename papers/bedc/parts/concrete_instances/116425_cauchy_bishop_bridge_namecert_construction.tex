\begin{definition}[Cauchy-Bishop bridge carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-bishop-bridge-carrier}
A $\CauchyBishopBridgeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$

$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-observation row supplying the inspected windows of the rational name.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular Cauchy readback row attached to those windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger recording the positive dyadic budgets used by the readback.
\item $L$ is the $\BishopCauchyLocatorUp$ located-Cauchy request row, recording the finite witness that a requested precision has a displayed tail location.
\item $W$ is the bridge-witness row matching each located request in $L$ with a regular readback and tolerance entry in $R,D$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $S,R,D,L,W$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the stream, readback, tolerance, locator, bridge-witness, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the same finite route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyBishopBridgeUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BishopCauchyLocatorUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyBishopBridgeUp}$ row.

The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,R,D,L,W,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient real equality, a selected limit, countable choice, host real completeness, or an uninspected infinite sequence.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy-Bishop bridge NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-bishop-bridge-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyBishopBridgeUp$ carrier $B=(S,R,D,L,W,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyBishopBridgeUp}$ surface consists exactly of stream-window exposure, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance accounting, located-Cauchy request coverage, bridge-witness matching, terminal Real seal routing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-bishop-bridge-carrier}. The packet exposes precisely the rows $S,R,D,L,W,E,H,C,P,N$, so carrier admission cannot appeal to an external sequence, quotient class, or host completeness fact.

The regular Cauchy side is read through $S,R,D$. A consumer first inspects a finite stream window in $S$, then reads the regular rational evidence in $R$, and then checks the requested precision against the dyadic tolerance ledger $D$.

The Bishop located-Cauchy side is read through $L,W$. Each located request in $L$ must be matched in $W$ to the already displayed regular readback and tolerance rows, so the bridge does not choose a new representative or import countable choice.

Finally $E,H,C,P,N$ seal, transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since no additional coordinate exists, the local NameCert obligations are exhausted by the displayed rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular readback to Bishop located-Cauchy data]
\label{thm:cauchy-bishop-bridge-regular-readback}
Every accepted $\CauchyBishopBridgeUp$ packet turns a displayed $\RegSeqRatUp$ read over $\StreamNameUp$ windows and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerances into a $\BishopCauchyLocatorUp$ read only through
$$
S,R,D,L,W,H,C,P,N.
$$
Thus a located-Cauchy consumer receives finite locator data after the regular readback and dyadic tolerance rows have already been displayed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the regular readback rows $S,R,D$. The stream row supplies the inspected finite windows, the regular-sequence row supplies the rational readback, and the dyadic row supplies the positive tolerance budget.

Now inspect the located request row $L$. For each requested precision, the bridge-witness row $W$ names the displayed window and tolerance entry that covers the requested tail location. This is a finite matching operation inside the packet, not an appeal to an infinite choice of moduli.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same matching data under transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Therefore the located-Cauchy read is available exactly through the bridge rows and not through quotient real equality or host completion.
\end{proof}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

The rows $H,C,P,N$ keep the seal inside the same finite packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for the excluded objects, no Real-seal read can escape the displayed bridge route.
