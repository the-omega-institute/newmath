\section{Prime Spectral Fingerprint Carrier}
\label{sec:prime-spectral-fingerprint-carrier}

\begin{definition}[Prime spectral fingerprint carrier]
\label{def:prime-spectral-fingerprint-carrier}
A $\PrimeSpectralFingerprintUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\mathsf{PSF}(P,Z,D,R,H,C,G,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $P$ is the prime-probe row, listing the finite prime names and local probe windows inspected by the packet.
\item $Z$ is the zeta projection row, recording the visible prime-local channel response consumed from the zeta packet.
\item $D$ is the dyadic response ledger, storing the finite $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ approximants used by the response comparison.
\item $R$ is the rational response ledger, storing the finite $\RatUp$ readback rows used to compare probe amplitudes and phases.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for prime probes, zeta projection, dyadic responses, rational responses, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a fingerprint request through $P,Z,D,R,H,G,N$.
\item $G$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PrimeSpectralFingerprintUp$, $\PrimeUp$, zeta projection rows, $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PrimeSpectralFingerprintUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no host complex equality, no completed zeta continuation, no selected infinite prime product, no RH proof object, and no ambient spectral theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[PrimeSpectralFingerprint NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:prime-spectral-fingerprint-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PrimeSpectralFingerprintUp$ carrier $\mathsf{PSF}(P,Z,D,R,H,C,G,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\PrimeSpectralFingerprintUp}$ surface consists exactly of prime-probe exposure, zeta-projection exposure, dyadic response-ledger exposure, rational response-ledger exposure, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Any accepted prime-local zeta response read must pass through
$$
P,Z,D,R \longmapsto H,C,G,N
$$
before a separation consumer may inspect the fingerprint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:prime-spectral-fingerprint-carrier}. The fingerprint-facing rows are $P,Z,D,R$, and the structural rows are $H,C,G,N$.

The prime-probe row $P$ supplies the finite probe names, while the zeta row $Z$ supplies the corresponding visible channel responses. The dyadic and rational ledgers $D$ and $R$ record only finite approximants and readback rows used by that response comparison.

Transport, replay, provenance, and naming are exhausted by $H,C,G,N$. Since no row records host complex equality, a completed continuation, an infinite Euler product, or an RH proof object, the NameCert obligations are exactly the finite rows listed above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PrimeSpectralFingerprint separation witness]
\label{thm:prime-spectral-fingerprint-separation-witness}
Every separation witness exported by an accepted $\PrimeSpectralFingerprintUp$ carrier factors through the prime-probe row $P$, zeta projection row $Z$, dyadic ledger $D$, rational ledger $R$, transport $H$, replay $C$, provenance $G$, and local naming $N$. The witness may record that two displayed spectral histories differ at a prime-local response row, but it does not identify histories by host complex equality or by an unlisted infinite family of probes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer asking whether two displayed histories are separated by a prime-local response. The packet can inspect only the prime names in $P$ and the zeta-channel response rows in $Z$.

The dyadic and rational ledgers $D$ and $R$ then provide the finite comparison surface for the response readback. They do not add a completed analytic continuation or a global Euler product; they only expose the approximants and rational rows already present in the packet.

Finally $H,C,G,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite witness. Therefore the exported separation row is prime-local and finite, with no host equality or unlisted infinite probe family.
\end{proof}
