Following Kelley and the Arzela--Ascoli compactness theorem, the $\CompactFunctionSpaceUp$ packet records compact-source function-space data as finite BEDC rows before any compact-open or equicontinuity consumer reads a family of maps. It sits over the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the graph surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the tolerance-only modulus handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformmodulus-namecert}, and the Real readback chain through $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\StreamNameUp$. The packet is a carrier for named function-space observations; it is not a quotient function space, a host function equality principle, a selected representative family, compactness by choice, or an ambient theorem asserting that every function family is compact.

\begin{definition}[CompactFunctionSpace carrier]
\label{def:compact-function-space-carrier}
A $\CompactFunctionSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(X,F,M,R,V,U,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the compact source row, read through $\CompactMetricUp$ and its finite-net evidence;
\item $F$ is the displayed family of graph rows, read through $\ContinuousMapUp$;
\item $M$ is the tolerance-only $\UniformModulusUp$ row shared by the family on the compact source;
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ value and distance-read seal used by evaluations;
\item $V$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback row for value windows;
\item $U$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row for finite evaluation schedules;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,F,M,R,V,U$ and for the structural rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact-net probes to graph reads, modulus reads, StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, and Real seals;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactFunctionSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no host function object, no quotient function-space equality, no selected representative of an equivalence class, no global compactness theorem, and no choice principle for extracting functions from the displayed rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CompactFunctionSpace NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-function-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompactFunctionSpaceUp$ packet $Q=(X,F,M,R,V,U,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CompactFunctionSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the compact metric row $X$, continuous graph family $F$, shared uniform-modulus row $M$, Real seal $R$, RegSeqRat readback $V$, StreamName window $U$, componentwise transport $H$, displayed replay $C$, provenance $P$, and local naming $N$. Any consumer preserving the packet must preserve these rows together, and no consumer may replace them by host function equality, quotient-function classes, selected representatives, compactness by choice, or an external function-space compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier definition. The accepted packet exposes exactly the ten displayed rows, so carrier admission is a finite $\BHist$ inventory and not an external space of functions.

The analytic rows are tied together. The compact metric row supplies finite probes, the continuous-map row supplies graph reads, the uniform-modulus row supplies tolerance-only control, and the StreamName, RegSeqRat, and Real rows expose finite value readback.

Finally read $H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, package, and name the same packet. Since the excluded host and quotient objects are absent from the carrier, they cannot appear as NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CompactFunctionSpace finite-net handoff]
\label{thm:compact-function-space-finite-net-handoff}
Every Arzela--Ascoli, compact-open, or uniform-convergence consumer of an accepted $\CompactFunctionSpaceUp$ packet receives the function-space data through
$$
X \to F \to M \to U \to V \to R
$$
with $H,C,P,N$ visible. The finite handoff first reads compact-net probes in $X$, then graph-family data in $F$, then the tolerance-only modulus $M$, and only then the finite StreamName, RegSeqRat, and Real readback rows. It does not supply selected functions, quotient function spaces, host extensional equality, compactness by choice, or an ambient compact-open theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the compact source row $X$. A consumer receives only finite compact-net probes from the displayed packet, so it cannot begin by selecting a host function or a point of an ambient function space.

Read $F$ and $M$ next. The graph-family row and shared uniform-modulus row determine how compact-source probes are turned into finite evaluation windows.

The terminal readback is $U,V,R$ with $H,C,P,N$ visible. StreamName windows, regular rational readbacks, Real seals, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are the whole exported handoff, so the excluded quotient and choice-based objects are outside the route.
\end{proof}
