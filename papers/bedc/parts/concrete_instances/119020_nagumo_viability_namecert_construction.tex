Following Nagumo's invariance theorem and the constructive ODE tradition, the $\NagumoViabilityUp$ packet records the finite boundary-viability surface by which a displayed vector field is read as staying inside a named closed constraint set. It consumes the ODE source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ode-namecert}, the local interval and vector-field rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-peano-existence-namecert}, the initial-data packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-initial-value-problem-namecert}, the continuous-map handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, and the compact interval fixed-point boundary route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactintervalfixedpoint-namecert}. The packet names a finite viability-boundary certificate; it does not select a maximal flow, assert uniqueness, impose host compactness, choose a solution representative, or provide a standard bridge.

\begin{definition}[Nagumo viability carrier]\label{def:nagumo-viability-carrier}
A $\NagumoViabilityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
V=(O,I,F,K,B,T,E,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $O$ is the $\OdeUp$ source row carrying the time-state interface and vector-field request.
\item $I$ is the $\InitialValueProblemUp$ row naming the initial time, initial state, local window, and solution-read surface.
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ vector-field graph and modulus row used for all boundary-facing reads.
\item $K$ is the closed constraint row, read through the existing closed-set and Real boundary surfaces and exposing only the displayed membership and complement-neighborhood tests.
\item $B$ is the Nagumo boundary row, recording the finite tangent or inward-pointing comparison data inspected at the displayed boundary cells of $K$.
\item $T$ is the interval trap row inherited from the Peano local-existence route, keeping Euler polygon and local solution reads inside the certified Real time-state window.
\item $E$ is the $\PeanoExistenceUp$ existence row that exports a local solution read only after $O,I,F,K,B,T$ have been displayed.
\item $Q$ is the compact interval fixed-point comparison row used when a finite interval boundary or residual trap is needed to certify the viability step.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transports for the ODE, initial-data, continuous-map, closed-set, boundary, trap, existence, fixed-point, and local certificate rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from the ODE request through the finite viability-boundary route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\NagumoViabilityUp$, $\OdeUp$, $\InitialValueProblemUp$, $\PeanoExistenceUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\ClosedSetUp$, $\CompactIntervalFixedPointUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\NagumoViabilityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It contains no coordinate for uniqueness, maximal-flow selection, host compactness, a choice-selected solution, quotient trajectory equality, or a standard bridge.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Nagumo viability NameCert obligations]\label{thm:nagumo-viability-obligations}
For every accepted $\NagumoViabilityUp$ carrier
$$
V=(O,I,F,K,B,T,E,Q,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\NagumoViabilityUp}$ obligations are exactly carrier admission for the ODE source, initial-data row, continuous vector-field row, closed constraint row, finite boundary comparison row, Peano interval trap, local existence row, compact interval fixed-point comparison row, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Any viability read must therefore pass through $O,I,F,K,B,T,E,Q,H,C,P,N$ before it can expose an invariant local solution segment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:nagumo-viability-carrier}. The rows $O,I,F$ fix the displayed ODE request, initial data, and continuous vector-field read before any boundary comparison is inspected.

The constraint-facing rows then supply the finite viability data. The closed-set row $K$ exposes the displayed boundary surface, $B$ records the Nagumo comparison at that boundary, and $T$ keeps the Peano polygon and solution reads inside the certified Real time-state window.

The consumer-facing rows $E$ and $Q$ provide only the local existence and compact interval comparison data already routed through the previous rows. Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, package, and name the same finite route. Since no carrier coordinate stores uniqueness, maximal-flow selection, host compactness, a choice-selected solution, quotient trajectory equality, or a bridge schema, none of those objects belongs to the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}
