\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-effective-cauchy-completeness-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, effective Cauchy completeness is read through displayed Cauchy names, explicit moduli, and finite rational approximation ledgers rather than through quotient classes or a host completeness theorem. The $\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp$ packet records that traditional route as NameCert data over the dyadic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the displayed sequence-completeness boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sequentiallycompletemetric-namecert}. It gives Real and Completion consumers a named effective-completeness landing surface without asserting that $\RealUp$ is complete.

\begin{definition}[Effective Cauchy completeness carrier]
\label{def:effective-cauchy-completeness-carrier}
An $\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(D,S,R,M,L,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance and endpoint ledger used for precision requests;
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row for the Cauchy name being completed;
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback of the same windows;
\item $M$ is the effective modulus row assigning each dyadic tolerance a displayed late threshold;
\item $L$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row offered as the candidate landing name;
\item $W$ is the witness ledger saying that each sufficiently late regular-rational window is within the dyadic tolerance of the landing seal;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $D,S,R,M,L,W,C,P,N$;
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay from dyadic tolerance requests through stream windows, regular readback, modulus thresholds, witness rows, and the Real seal;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\SequentiallyCompleteMetricUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no quotient Cauchy class, selected representative, countable-choice schedule, open-cover compactness principle, ambient metric-completeness premise, or proof that $\RealUp$ is complete.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Effective Cauchy completeness NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:effective-cauchy-completeness-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp$ carrier $E=(D,S,R,M,L,W,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed dyadic, stream, regular-rational, modulus, landing, witness, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows; classifier stability under componentwise $\hsame$ transport; modulus coverage for every dyadic tolerance request; witness exactness tying the late regular-rational windows to the terminal $\RealUp$ seal; and ledger non-escape for quotient completion, representative selection, countable choice, open-cover compactness, ambient completeness, and $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:effective-cauchy-completeness-carrier}. The displayed tuple exposes the dyadic tolerance row, finite windows, regular rational readback, effective modulus, landing seal, witness ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

Classifier stability is componentwise. A comparison of accepted packets first transports $D,S,R,M,L,W,C,P,N$ by $H$ before any local read is identified.

Modulus coverage and witness exactness are read in order. A dyadic tolerance request reaches $M$ for a late threshold, then reaches $S$ and $R$ for the inspected windows, and finally reaches $W$ for the comparison with $L$.

The rows of $E$ are exhausted by this route. Since none of them stores a quotient class, selected representative, choice schedule, open-cover principle, ambient complete carrier, or completeness theorem for $\RealUp$, the non-escape clause follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Effective Cauchy completeness modulus handoff]
\label{thm:effective-cauchy-completeness-modulus-handoff}
Every completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp$ packet $E=(D,S,R,M,L,W,H,C,P,N)$ must read the dyadic tolerance ledger $D$, the $\StreamNameUp$ windows $S$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $R$, and the effective modulus row $M$ before it can consume the landing seal $L$. The handoff to $\SequentiallyCompleteMetricUp$ and $\RealUp$ is therefore the finite route
$$
D,S,R,M \longmapsto W,L,H,C,P,N .
$$
It is not a quotient completion, selected limit, countable-choice construction, open-cover compactness argument, ambient completeness assumption, or proof that $\RealUp$ is complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a completion-facing tolerance request. The dyadic row $D$ fixes the requested precision, so the consumer cannot inspect a landing seal before the precision row is displayed.

Read the Cauchy-name rows next. The finite windows $S$ and the regular rational readback $R$ expose the observed tail of the name, while $M$ supplies the late threshold for that tolerance.

Only after this route is displayed can $W$ compare the late windows with $L$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite handoff for $\SequentiallyCompleteMetricUp$ and $\RealUp$ consumers.

No further row exists in the carrier. Hence the handoff cannot provide quotient completion, selected representatives, countable choice, open-cover compactness, ambient completeness, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Effective Cauchy completeness ledger nonescape]
\label{thm:effective-cauchy-completeness-ledger-nonescape}
An accepted $\EffectiveCauchyCompletenessUp$ carrier $E=(D,S,R,M,L,W,H,C,P,N)$ exports only the displayed effective Cauchy route through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\SequentiallyCompleteMetricUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$. Any consumer asking for quotient completion, a selected representative, countable choice, open-cover compactness, ambient metric completeness, or completeness of $\RealUp$ is returned to those finite rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. Its coordinates are precisely the dyadic tolerance ledger, stream windows, regular readback, modulus, landing seal, witness ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming row.

Apply the NameCert obligation surface. It makes every effective-completeness read pass through the displayed modulus and witness rows before the terminal Real seal is visible.

Apply the modulus handoff. The completion-facing consumer receives a finite route to the displayed seal, not a second completed carrier. Since the forbidden objects are absent from the tuple, they cannot be exported by the local ledger.
\end{proof}
