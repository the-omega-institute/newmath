\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FreudenthalCompactificationUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-freudenthal-compactification-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Freudenthal's compactification of locally compact spaces by ends, the $\FreudenthalCompactificationUp$ packet records a source space together with a finite end-filter boundary and a compact-boundary admission route as NameCert data. It reads the end carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-freudenthal-end-namecert}, the one-point boundary discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-one-point-compactification-namecert}, the compact metric route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the locally compact surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locallycompact-namecert}, the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, and the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}. The packet names the compactification boundary through displayed finite end filters rather than through an ambient quotient, selected ideal points, open-cover compactness, or choice-selected escaping rays.

\begin{definition}[FreudenthalCompactification carrier]
\label{def:freudenthal-compactification-carrier}
A $\FreudenthalCompactificationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(X,E,B,J,T,H,Q,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source row, exposing the $\TopologyUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\LocallyCompactUp$, and optional $\ProperMetricUp$ support used by the compactification packet.
\item $E$ is the $\FreudenthalEndUp$ row. It records compact-exhaustion stages, complement-component rows, tail-comparison data, and escaping-prefix ledgers exactly as in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-freudenthal-end-namecert}.
\item $B$ is the finite end-filter boundary row. Each boundary request names the compact stage, the complement-component identifier, and the tail ledger slice that admits the boundary read.
\item $J$ is the source-inclusion row. It records the displayed embedding of the source row into the compactification carrier without adding a quotient host space.
\item $T$ is the compact-boundary admission ledger. It compares finite source neighbourhood reads, one-point compactification boundary discipline, and compact metric support rows before any boundary request is public.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, end-filter, boundary, inclusion, compact-boundary ledger, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $Q$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a compactification request through $X,E,B,J,T,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FreudenthalCompactificationUp$, $\FreudenthalEndUp$, $\OnePointCompactificationUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\LocallyCompactUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FreudenthalCompactificationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an ambient quotient compactification, a selected ideal boundary point, an open-cover compactness proof, a chosen escaping ray, countable-choice enumeration of ends, or a standard bridge.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FreudenthalCompactification NameCert obligation surface]
\label{thm:freudenthal-compactification-namecert-obligation-surface}
For every accepted $\FreudenthalCompactificationUp$ carrier $K=(X,E,B,J,T,H,Q,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FreudenthalCompactificationUp}$ surface consists exactly of source admission, Freudenthal end admission, finite end-filter boundary display, source-inclusion display, compact-boundary ledger readback, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. A compactification consumer must read $E$ before any boundary row $B$, must read $X,J,B$ before the compact-boundary ledger $T$, and must keep the boundary admission route separate from open-cover compactness, quotient equality, selected ideal points, and choice-selected rays.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:freudenthal-compactification-carrier}. The source row $X$ supplies only the displayed topological, metric, locally compact, and optional proper metric data, while the end row $E$ supplies the finite Freudenthal end surface.

The boundary row $B$ is therefore readable only through compact stages, complement-component identifiers, and tail ledger slices already exposed by $E$. The source-inclusion row $J$ records how source points enter the compactification carrier, but it does not create a quotient host space or a selected ideal point.

Next read the compact-boundary ledger $T$. Its only inputs are $X,E,B,J$ together with the cited one-point, compact metric, locally compact, topological, and metric support rows, so it is a finite boundary-admission ledger rather than an open-cover compactness theorem.

Finally transport and replay through $H$ and $Q$, then package and name through $P$ and $N$. Since no displayed coordinate stores quotient compactification equality, selected rays, countable-choice enumeration of ends, or a standard bridge, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}
