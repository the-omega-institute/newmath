Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real sequences, the $\QuasiCauchySequenceUp$ packet records the adjacent-difference boundary of a real sequence as finite BEDC NameCert data. It sits on the finite stream-window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet gives later Real and Completion arguments a sequence frontier where adjacent gaps vanish without asserting the full Cauchy condition, selecting limits, quotienting streams, invoking countable choice, or claiming ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Quasi-Cauchy sequence carrier]
\label{def:quasi-cauchy-sequence-carrier}
A $\QuasiCauchySequenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(S,W,D,R,E,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ source row naming the inspected real sequence by finite windows.
\item $W$ is the adjacent-window schedule row exposing each displayed pair $(x_n,x_{n+1})$.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used to compare adjacent differences.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational difference stream attached to the displayed windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for each inspected adjacent distance value.
\item $A$ is the adjacent-difference ledger, recording that every accepted tolerance request is eventually met by the displayed adjacent rows.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the stream, schedule, dyadic, regular-rational, Real-seal, adjacent-ledger, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a quasi-Cauchy request through $S,W,D,R,E,A,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\QuasiCauchySequenceUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\QuasiCauchySequenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier accepts a sequence read only through $S,W,D,R,E,A,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for arbitrary tail-pair Cauchy comparison, selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Quasi-Cauchy sequence NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:quasi-cauchy-sequence-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\QuasiCauchySequenceUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(S,W,D,R,E,A,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\QuasiCauchySequenceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite stream source, adjacent-window schedule, dyadic tolerance ledger, regular-rational readback, terminal Real seal, adjacent-difference ledger, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming rows; adjacent-window exposure before any difference read; tolerance-readback coherence through $D,R,E$; eventual adjacent-smallness through $A$; and structural preservation through $H,C,P,N$. No quasi-Cauchy obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:quasi-cauchy-sequence-carrier}. The tuple displays the stream source, adjacent schedule, dyadic tolerance ledger, regular-rational readback, Real seal, adjacent-difference ledger, and structural rows.

The adjacent-window exposure clause is exactly the row pair $S,W$. It forces a consumer to inspect displayed neighbouring entries before reading any distance or tolerance datum.

The tolerance coherence clause is stored in $D,R,E$. The dyadic tolerance row supplies the requested precision, the regular-rational row reads the finite rational difference stream, and the Real seal records the accepted adjacent distance as a displayed real-name boundary.

Finally $A,H,C,P,N$ record eventual adjacent-smallness, transport, replay, source, and local naming. Since no carrier coordinate compares arbitrary distant tail pairs or chooses a limit, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface without promoting the packet to a full Cauchy or completion theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quasi-Cauchy adjacent-difference ledger]
\label{thm:quasi-cauchy-sequence-adjacent-difference-ledger}
Every accepted $\QuasiCauchySequenceUp$ packet routes each Real- or Completion-facing adjacent-difference request through
$$
\StreamNameUp \longrightarrow \DyadicRatCoreUp \longrightarrow \RegSeqRatUp \longrightarrow \RealUp
$$
and then through the adjacent ledger $A$ and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. The route certifies only that displayed adjacent differences become small along the inspected schedule; it does not certify the full Cauchy comparison of arbitrary tail pairs, a selected limiting real, quotient stream equality, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted adjacent-difference request. The carrier forces the request to read $S$ and $W$, so the compared entries are the displayed neighbours of the same finite stream schedule.

The request then passes through $D,R,E$. These rows expose the dyadic tolerance, the regular rational readback of the difference stream, and the terminal Real seal before any completion-facing consumer can inspect the ledger.

The row $A$ records the eventual adjacent-smallness claim for exactly those displayed neighbouring entries. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name the same packet.

Because the route never adds a coordinate comparing arbitrary $x_m$ and $x_n$ in the tail, it remains the quasi-Cauchy adjacent-difference ledger and cannot be reread as full Cauchy convergence, selected completion, quotient stream equality, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}
