\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$ packet records a finite constructive upper-envelope read for located rational observations before any Real or Completion consumer may treat the envelope as a located real boundary. It sits beside the regular rational name surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the located upper-bound packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-upper-bound-namecert}, the located-real boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedreal-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet gives later located-real and completion arguments one finite carrier for an inspected upper envelope without importing Dedekind cuts, quotient reals, global supremum existence, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Bishop located upper-envelope carrier]
\label{def:bishop-located-upper-envelope-carrier}
A $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
U=(S,R,D,L,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the finite sample row, recording the inspected rational observations and the index window in which they are read.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row that turns the inspected samples into regular rational name data.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row, storing the dyadic radii and slack comparisons used by the envelope test.
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedUpperBoundUp$ row, recording the displayed upper-bound and locatedness comparisons certified for the sampled window.
\item $Q$ is the $\LocatedRealUp$ and $\RealUp$ handoff row reached only after $S,R,D,L$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the sample, readback, dyadic tolerance, located upper-bound, located-real handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the same finite envelope route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\LocatedUpperBoundUp$, $\LocatedRealUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a Dedekind cut, quotient-real equality, a supremum for arbitrary subsets, countable choice, selected least upper bound outside the packet, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop located upper-envelope NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-located-upper-envelope-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$ carrier $U=(S,R,D,L,Q,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, finite sample exposure, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance accounting, $\LocatedUpperBoundUp$ located upper-bound exposure, $\LocatedRealUp$ and $\RealUp$ handoff sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-located-upper-envelope-carrier}. The analytic rows are $S,R,D,L,Q$, and the structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

Read the finite rows first. The sample row $S$ fixes the inspected window, $R$ supplies regular rational readback, $D$ records the dyadic tolerance ledger, and $L$ exposes the located upper-bound comparison for that same window.

The handoff row $Q$ is downstream of those rows. It may be consumed by located-real and real-name arguments only after the finite readback and located-bound data have been displayed.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no row stores a Dedekind cut, quotient real, arbitrary subset supremum, choice operator, selected least upper bound, or ambient completeness principle, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop located upper-envelope real-located handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-located-upper-envelope-real-located-handoff}
Every $\LocatedRealUp$, $\RealUp$, or completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$ packet factors through the finite route
$$
S,R,D \longmapsto L \longmapsto Q \longmapsto H,C,P,N
$$
before reading the envelope as a located real boundary. The route exports only inspected rational samples, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance accounting, a located upper-bound row, and a terminal located-real or real-name seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-located-upper-envelope-namecert-obligations}. They force the consumer to inspect $S,R,D$ before the located upper-bound row can be used.

Next read $L$. This row is the only envelope-facing upper-bound coordinate, so the consumer sees located upper-bound evidence rather than a host supremum or a Dedekind-cut object.

The handoff row $Q$ then reaches the located-real and real-name boundary. Transport, replay, provenance, and naming through $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same finite route, so no completion-facing consumer receives an off-packet supremum or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop located upper-envelope nonescape]
\label{thm:bishop-located-upper-envelope-nonescape}
An accepted $\BishopLocatedUpperEnvelopeUp$ packet cannot be reread as a Dedekind-cut construction, quotient-real presentation, arbitrary-subset supremum theorem, countable-choice selector, host least-upper-bound principle, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness statement. Every such request is forced back to the displayed rows $S,R,D,L,Q,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier inventory. The only analytic coordinates are the finite sample, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance, located upper-bound, and located-real handoff rows.

Apply the obligation theorem and the real-located handoff theorem. Both routes keep a consumer inside the finite packet before the terminal located-real or real-name seal is read.

Thus a request for a cut, quotient, arbitrary supremum, choice selector, host least upper bound, or completeness principle has no coordinate to inspect. The packet exports only the displayed constructive upper-envelope route.
\end{proof}
