It sits over the convex carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the real comparison boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the vector source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-vecspace-namecert}, and the rational coefficient source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert}. The packet supplies the source row consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-jensen-inequality-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fenchelduality-namecert}; it is a finite NameCert surface, not a host function space or an ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[ConvexFunction carrier]
\label{def:convex-function-carrier}
A $\ConvexFunctionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(D,V,R,A,M,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the convex-domain row, recording the accepted $\ConvexSetUp$ carrier whose elements may be queried.
\item $V$ is the vector-source row, recording the displayed $\VecSpaceUp$ operations used to form affine expressions.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ codomain and readback row, recording the real-valued output names and comparison interface.
\item $A$ is the affine-weight route, recording finite rational weights through $\RatUp$, nonnegative checks, and the displayed finite-sum-to-one row.
\item $M$ is the midpoint or finite affine-combination ledger, recording the source points, weighted vector expression, and the proof row that the result remains inside $D$.
\item $E$ is the epigraph and comparison row, recording the displayed inequality between the function value at the affine point and the corresponding affine combination of output reads.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $D,V,R,A,M,E$ and the local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the domain, vector, rational-weight, real-comparison, transport, provenance, and local name rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConvexFunctionUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\RealUp$, $\VecSpaceUp$, $\RatUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConvexFunctionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $D,V,R,A,M,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host convex function, quotient equality of functions or real names, selected extrema, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ConvexFunction NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:convex-function-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConvexFunctionUp$ carrier $F=(D,V,R,A,M,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ConvexFunctionUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following five obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed convex domain, vector source, real codomain and readback row, affine-weight route, midpoint or finite affine-combination ledger, epigraph and comparison row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item affine-combination accountability, requiring every weight and finite affine expression read by a consumer to pass through $A$ and $M$;
\item convexity comparison exactness, requiring every displayed convexity inequality to be the row recorded in $E$ over the $\RealUp$ readback interface $R$;
\item transport and replay stability, requiring componentwise $\hsame$ transport and $\Cont$ replay to preserve the same domain, vector, rational-weight, real-comparison, provenance, and local naming route;
\item ledger nonescape from host functions, quotient real equality, selected extrema, trichotomy, countable choice, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted packet $F=(D,V,R,A,M,E,H,C,P,N)$. The carrier definition exposes exactly the convex domain, vector source, real codomain, affine-weight, affine-combination, comparison, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The affine data are read before the comparison row. Thus $A$ supplies the rational weights and finite-sum row, while $M$ supplies the displayed midpoint or finite affine expression and its return to the convex domain.

The convexity comparison is then read only through $E$ and $R$. Transport and replay are componentwise through $H$ and $C$, and provenance and naming are carried by $P$ and $N$. Since none of the excluded host objects is a carrier coordinate, the five listed clauses exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ConvexFunction midpoint route]
\label{thm:convex-function-midpoint-route}
Every midpoint or finite affine read of an accepted $\ConvexFunctionUp$ carrier routes through
$$
D,V,A,M \longmapsto R,E \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Consequently Jensen and Fenchel consumers may inspect a convex-function source only after the displayed domain, rational weights, $\VecSpaceUp$ affine route, $\RealUp$ comparison row, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local name have all been read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer read of a midpoint or finite affine expression. By \autoref{def:convex-function-carrier}, the only source rows for such a read are the domain row $D$, vector row $V$, affine-weight row $A$, and affine-combination ledger $M$.

The real-valued convexity statement is downstream of those rows. The codomain row $R$ supplies the displayed real reads, and $E$ supplies the comparison row tying the value at the affine point to the affine combination of output reads.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since the packet contains no separate host-function or quotient-equality coordinate, Jensen and Fenchel consumers cannot bypass this displayed path.
\end{proof}
