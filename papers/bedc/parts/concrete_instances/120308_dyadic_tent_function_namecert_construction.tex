\begin{definition}[Dyadic tent-function carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-tent-function-carrier}
A $\DyadicTentFunctionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(C,S,H,L,R,E,D,A,M,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C$ is the dyadic center row, read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$.
\item $S$ is the dyadic support-window row, recording the left and right dyadic endpoints.
\item $H$ is the height row, normalized as the visible peak value.
\item $L$ and $R$ are the left-slope and right-slope rows, each carried by finite dyadic arithmetic.
\item $E$ records endpoint vanishing and peak agreement for the three displayed dyadic breakpoints.
\item $D$ is the dyadic-to-real readback row consumed before any $\RealUp$ graph evaluation.
\item $A$ records the affine-piece admission rows for the two support halves.
\item $M$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for center, support, height, slopes, endpoint rows, readback, and affine pieces.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\FaberSchauderSystemUp$, $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$, $\PiecewiseLinearApproximationUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicTentFunctionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an infinite coefficient family, an external real-valued function object, a completed approximation theorem, or a selected ambient representative.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicTentFunction NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-tent-function-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-tent-function-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicTentFunctionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for
$$
T=(C,S,H,L,R,E,D,A,M,P,N),
$$
dyadic center display through $C$, support-window display through $S$, height display through $H$, slope display through $L,R$, endpoint and peak display through $E$, dyadic-to-real readback through $D$, affine-piece admission through $A$, componentwise transport through $M$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the finite carrier. The center, support, height, slope, endpoint, readback, and affine-piece rows are all displayed before the provenance and naming rows are read.

The dyadic rows are consumed through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, while the graph-facing handoff to $\RealUp$ occurs only through the readback row $D$. The system and interpolation chapters may reuse this packet as one basis element, but the packet itself does not expose a Faber--Schauder family or a polygonal approximation theorem.

The structural rows preserve the same displayed rows. Thus the listed fields exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicTentFunction modulus handoff]
\label{thm:dyadic-tent-function-modulus-handoff}
Every accepted $\DyadicTentFunctionUp$ packet exposes a finite modulus handoff to dyadic interpolation consumers through the same support-window, slope, endpoint, and dyadic-to-real readback rows
$$
S,L,R,E,D,A,M,P,N.
$$
A $\FaberSchauderSystemUp$, $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$, or $\PiecewiseLinearApproximationUp$ consumer may use the packet as a single tent primitive only after those rows have been displayed, and cannot infer an infinite basis expansion, a global approximation bound, or a hidden real-function representative from the packet alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:dyadic-tent-function-namecert-obligations}. The support, slope, endpoint, readback, and affine-piece rows are separate displayed coordinates of the same carrier.

The interpolation-facing handoff reads $S,L,R,E$ before it reads the real graph route through $D$. Transport through $M$ preserves those rows componentwise, so refining the ambient interpolation context does not detach the tent primitive from its dyadic support and slope data.

The excluded objects are not carrier coordinates. Hence the packet supplies only the finite tent-function primitive and its modulus-facing handoff.
\end{proof}
