Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive completions and the standard unit map into a regular-Cauchy completion, the $\CauchyCompletionEtaUp$ packet records the canonical eta route from a named source element into its completion name. It sits after the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream-name surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational sequence surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}; it also reads the completion-side boundary exposed by $\FastCauchyCompletionUp$ and $\MetricCompletionUp$. The chapter names the source-to-completion unit as finite NameCert data, not as a primitive quotient representative, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[CauchyCompletionEta carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-completion-eta-carrier}
A $\CauchyCompletionEtaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(S,W,D,R,Q,F,M,H,K,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source-name row whose finite observations are read before any completion-side coordinate is exposed.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row for source observations.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row that records the inspected dyadic scales.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular-rational handoff row extracted from the same finite windows and tolerances.
\item $Q$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached by the regular-rational source read.
\item $F$ is the $\FastCauchyCompletionUp$ fast-completion row that names the regular-Cauchy completion candidate produced by the source route.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricCompletionUp$ completion-boundary row that lets completion-facing consumers read the eta image without selecting a quotient representative.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $S,W,D,R,Q,F,M,K,P,N$.
\item $K$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the source-to-completion route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyCompletionEtaUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\FastCauchyCompletionUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyCompletionEtaUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier accepts only packets read through $S,W,D,R,Q,F,M,H,K,P,N$. It contains no quotient-completion equality row, no selected limit representative, no countable-choice selector, no host function-extensionality witness, and no ambient $\RealUp$ completeness field.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CauchyCompletionEta NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-completion-eta-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyCompletionEtaUp$ carrier $E=(S,W,D,R,Q,F,M,H,K,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyCompletionEtaUp}$ surface consists exactly of five obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed finite packet $E$;
\item source readback through $S,W,D,R,Q$, requiring every eta input to pass through $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ before any completion row is read;
\item completion admission through $F$ and $M$, requiring the $\FastCauchyCompletionUp$ row to be visible before the $\MetricCompletionUp$ boundary is consumed;
\item eta-order stability, requiring completion-facing reads to preserve the source row rather than replacing it by a quotient representative or selected limit;
\item structural stability through $H,K,P,N$, requiring transport, replay, provenance, and local naming to package only the displayed source-to-completion route.
\end{enumerate}
These rows do not assert quotient-completion equality, countable choice, host equality of completed objects, selected limits, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-completion-eta-carrier}. Its rows list the source name, stream windows, dyadic tolerance readback, regular-rational handoff, real seal, fast-completion row, metric-completion boundary, and structural data.

Read the source side first. The rows $S,W,D,R,Q$ force any eta input through finite observations, dyadic scales, regular rational readback, and the real-name seal before completion-side data can be consumed.

Next read the completion side through $F$ and $M$. The fast-completion row names the regular-Cauchy completion candidate, while the metric-completion boundary exposes it to completion-facing consumers without a quotient representative or selected limit.

Finally use $H,K,P,N$ for transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since these rows only preserve the displayed packet, they cannot add choice, host equality, quotient equality, selected limits, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CauchyCompletionEta real seal]
\label{thm:cauchy-completion-eta-real-seal}
Every $\RealUp$, $\FastCauchyCompletionUp$, or $\MetricCompletionUp$ consumer of an accepted $\CauchyCompletionEtaUp$ packet must factor through the finite route
$$
\StreamNameUp
\longmapsto
\DyadicRatCoreUp
\longmapsto
\RegSeqRatUp
\longmapsto
\RealUp
\longmapsto
\FastCauchyCompletionUp
\longmapsto
\MetricCompletionUp .
$$
The source row $S$ remains visible throughout the route, so the eta image is read as the displayed source-to-completion unit rather than as a primitive quotient object, selected limit, or ambient real-completeness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the source windows. The rows $W,D,R,Q$ expose exactly the stream, dyadic, regular-rational, and real-seal data available from the source row $S$.

Then consume the completion rows. The fast-completion coordinate $F$ is downstream of the real seal, and the metric-completion boundary $M$ is downstream of $F$, so a completion-facing consumer cannot bypass the source route.

The structural rows $H,K,P,N$ replay the same dependency for all accepted reads. Thus the route seals the eta image as a finite NameCert surface and exports none of the refused completion principles.
\end{proof}
