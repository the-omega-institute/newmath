\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-epigraph-projection-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis and Rockafellar convex analysis, the $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$ packet records the finite projection-facing read of an epigraph without importing a host projection operator, selected minimizer, quotient subset, or ambient completeness theorem. It sits after the epigraph carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-epigraph-namecert}, the lower-semicontinuity packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lowersemicontinuous-namecert}, the metric boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the located-real handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedreal-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the finite data a Real or Completion consumer may inspect before asking whether a displayed epigraph row has a located projection boundary.

\begin{definition}[Constructive epigraph-projection carrier]
\label{def:constructive-epigraph-projection-carrier}
A $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E_{\mathrm{proj}}=(E,L,M,R,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E$ is the $\EpigraphUp$ source row containing the displayed domain, value, lower-bound, and comparison data.
\item $L$ is the $\LowerSemicontinuousUp$ row certifying that the epigraph read is consumed through the lower-semicontinuity boundary.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row supplying the finite distance window in which a projection request is measured.
\item $R$ is the $\LocatedRealUp$ radius and witness row used to read the finite distance comparison constructively.
\item $Q$ is the $\RealUp$ terminal seal row reached only after $E,L,M,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the epigraph, lower-semicontinuity, metric, located-real, real-seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the epigraph source, lower-semicontinuity boundary, metric window, located-real radius, terminal seal, and structural rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$, $\EpigraphUp$, $\LowerSemicontinuousUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\LocatedRealUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for a total projection operator, selected nearest point, quotient epigraph subset, Hilbert projection theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive epigraph-projection NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:constructive-epigraph-projection-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$ carrier $E_{\mathrm{proj}}=(E,L,M,R,Q,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, $\EpigraphUp$ source exposure, $\LowerSemicontinuousUp$ boundary exposure, $\MetricUp$ finite-window exposure, $\LocatedRealUp$ radius readback, $\RealUp$ terminal sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every projection-facing consumer reads those rows through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-epigraph-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lowersemicontinuous-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedreal-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}; no obligation is stored in a host subset, selected minimizer, quotient carrier, or completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:constructive-epigraph-projection-carrier}. The analytic rows are exactly $E,L,M,R,Q$, and the structural rows are exactly $H,C,P,N$.

Read the epigraph and lower-semicontinuity rows first. The source row $E$ supplies only displayed epigraph data, while $L$ forces the consumer through the lower-semicontinuity boundary before any projection request can be interpreted as stable.

Next read the metric and located-real rows. The metric row $M$ supplies the finite distance window, and $R$ supplies the located radius readback through which the distance request is inspected. The terminal row $Q$ is available only after those finite rows have been replayed.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$. Transport and replay preserve the same displayed rows, provenance records the sibling dependency route, and the local name closes the packet. Since no coordinate supplies a total projection operator, selected nearest point, quotient subset, Hilbert projection theorem, or ambient completeness, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive epigraph-projection located handoff]
\label{thm:constructive-epigraph-projection-located-handoff}
Every Real or Completion consumer of an accepted $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$ carrier factors through the finite route
$$
E,L \longmapsto M,R \longmapsto Q \longmapsto H,C,P,N
$$
before reading a projection-facing distance request. The route exports only epigraph data, lower-semicontinuity exposure, a finite metric window, a located-real radius readback, and a terminal real seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:constructive-epigraph-projection-namecert-obligations}. They force $E$ and $L$ to be displayed before any Real-facing or Completion-facing consumer can inspect the projection request.

The handoff then passes through $M$ and $R$. The metric row supplies the distance window, and the located-real row supplies the constructive radius readback for that same window.

The terminal seal $Q$ is downstream of those finite rows. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route, so the consumer sees the projection request only through the displayed finite handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive epigraph-projection nonescape]
\label{thm:constructive-epigraph-projection-nonescape}
A consumer of an accepted $\ConstructiveEpigraphProjectionUp$ packet cannot recover a total projection operator, selected minimizer, quotient epigraph subset, host nearest-point theorem, Hilbert projection theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness from the packet. Any such request is forced back to the displayed rows $E,L,M,R,Q,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier inventory in \autoref{def:constructive-epigraph-projection-carrier}. It lists epigraph, lower-semicontinuity, metric, located-real, real-seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows, and no row for a selected point or total operator.

Apply the obligation theorem and located handoff theorem. Both routes keep every legal consumer inside the same finite packet before a projection-facing distance request is public.

Therefore an off-table request for a host projection, quotient subset, selected minimizer, or completeness endpoint has no carrier coordinate to read. The packet exports only the finite handoff rows $E,L,M,R,Q,H,C,P,N$.
\end{proof}
