\begin{theorem}[Int signed-pair balance stability]
\label{thm:int-signed-pair-balance-stability}
The $\IntUp$ signed-pair classifier of
\autoref{def:int-signed-pair-carrier} is stable at exactly the Nat/Add balance
rows consumed downstream by $\RatUp$ numerator reads and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$
mantissa reads. An accepted comparison of signed pairs preserves the four
displayed $\NatUp$ magnitude rows, the two $\AddUp$ cross-addition histories,
componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance,
and the local $\NameCert$ row. The stability exports no primitive subtraction,
host integer equality, quotient class of integer pairs, ordered-ring law, or
ambient field equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the signed-pair carrier and classifier stability of
\autoref{def:int-signed-pair-carrier} and
\autoref{thm:int-signed-pair-classifier-stability}. The carrier supplies only
the two unary magnitude rows, and the classifier compares signed pairs through
the cross-addition histories $a+d$ and $c+b$.

Expose the exact downstream balance rows. A rational numerator consumer needs
the signed row, both Nat magnitudes, and the Add balance ledger before it can
pair the numerator with a denominator; a dyadic mantissa consumer needs the
same signed row before it reads mantissa and denominator routing.

Finally replay the transported comparison through the structural rows. Since
the carrier and classifier contain no primitive subtraction, host integer
equality, quotient pair, ordered-ring law, or ambient field-equality coordinate,
the balance stability is precisely the finite Nat/Add row consumed downstream.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Int Rat numerator handoff]
\label{thm:int-rat-numerator-handoff}
Every $\RatUp$ numerator read and every $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ mantissa read that
consumes $\IntUp$ data factors through the signed-pair carrier of
\autoref{def:int-signed-pair-carrier}, the $\NatUp$ magnitude rows, the
$\AddUp$ balance ledger, and the Int inverse ledger of
\autoref{thm:int-additive-inverse-ledger} before denominator normalization,
dyadic denominator routing, $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readbacks,
or the terminal $\RealUp$ seal is inspected. The handoff supplies finite signed
numerator and mantissa source data only; it exports no host integer equality,
quotient rational representative, primitive subtraction, selected real limit,
or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply balance stability from
\autoref{thm:int-signed-pair-balance-stability}. It keeps the signed numerator
inside the displayed Nat magnitudes and Add cross-balance rows before any
rational or dyadic consumer can inspect later arithmetic data.

Next use the additive inverse ledger of
\autoref{thm:int-additive-inverse-ledger}. The downstream numerator route may
read sign reversal only as the finite coordinate swap of the signed-pair
carrier, not as primitive subtraction or an ambient group law.

Now route the numerator through the existing rational and dyadic surfaces,
including \autoref{thm:int-rat-signed-numerator-route} and
\autoref{thm:int-dyadic-mantissa-real-route}. These place the accepted
$\IntUp$ row before $\RatUp$ denominator normalization and before
$\DyadicRatCoreUp$ mantissa routing.

The L10 consumers therefore receive only the finite signed source route before
they inspect $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, or $\RealUp$. Since no excluded
host-level, quotient-level, choice-level, or completeness-level coordinate is
present in the handoff, none is exported by the numerator read.
\end{proof}
