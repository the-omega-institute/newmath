\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FoxArtinArcUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-fox-artin-arc-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Fox and Artin, the $\FoxArtinArcUp$ packet records a wild arc in three-dimensional topology by displaying the finite rows a BEDC consumer may inspect: an arc row, an ambient endpoint row, compact-window rows around the wild end, tame-shadow comparison rows, knot-shadow obstruction rows, topology routes, package provenance, and a local naming ledger. The chapter places the classical example beside the topology surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the compact metric boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the knot row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-knot-namecert}, and the continuous-map route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}; it does not assert a host embedding theorem, a selected ambient isotopy, a quotient path equality, or a complete three-manifold classifier.

\begin{definition}[Fox-Artin arc carrier]
\label{def:fox-artin-arc-carrier}
A $\FoxArtinArcUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(A,E,W,T,K,R,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the arc row, recording a compact interval-shaped source and its embedded image as topology-facing data;
\item $E$ is the ambient endpoint row, including the distinguished wild endpoint and the tame endpoint read by local consumers;
\item $W$ is the compact-window ledger around the wild endpoint, with nested compact neighbourhood rows and finite boundary-intersection witnesses;
\item $T$ is the tame-shadow comparison row, naming the finite local test by which a tame arc packet would be recognized away from the wild endpoint;
\item $K$ is the knot-shadow obstruction row, recording the finite knotting data seen in the complement windows;
\item $R$ stores the displayed $\Cont$ and $\hsame$ routes through $\TopologyUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\KnotUp$, and $\ContinuousMapUp$;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over the same topology, compact-metric, knot, continuous-map, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FoxArtinArcUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows and their componentwise transports. It has no coordinate for a host ambient isotopy classifier, a selected homeomorphism of complements, quotient path equality, or a global three-manifold recognition oracle.
\end{definition}
\leanstmt{BEDC.Derived.FoxArtinArcUp}

\begin{theorem}[Fox-Artin arc NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:fox-artin-arc-namecert-obligations}
The local $\NameCert_{\FoxArtinArcUp}$ surface consists of five finite obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed arc, endpoint, compact-window, tame-shadow, knot-shadow, route, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item topology routing through $\TopologyUp$ and $\ContinuousMapUp$, so every arc read exposes its ambient topology and map route before any complement-facing consumer proceeds;
\item compact-window locality through $\CompactMetricUp$, so wild-end neighbourhood data is consumed as finite compact rows rather than as an open-cover compactness theorem;
\item knot-shadow visibility through $\KnotUp$, so obstruction reads remain finite complement-window rows and not a global knot classification;
\item non-escape from host ambient isotopy classification, quotient path equality, selected complement homeomorphisms, and global three-manifold recognition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection of the tuple $F$. The arc, endpoint, compact-window, tame-shadow, knot-shadow, routing, provenance, and naming rows are explicitly listed, so the packet is finite at the point where a BEDC consumer reads it.

The topology route is supplied by $R$. A consumer that asks for the arc image must first pass through the displayed $\TopologyUp$ and $\ContinuousMapUp$ rows, and the same route records componentwise $\hsame$ transport of the source and ambient data.

The compact-window obligation is read through $W$ and the $\CompactMetricUp$ dependency. The nested windows around the wild endpoint are therefore finite compact rows with boundary witnesses, not an appeal to an ambient compactness theorem.

The knot-shadow obligation is read through $K$. It names the complement-window obstruction data seen by the classical Fox-Artin arc, while refusing to export a global knot or three-manifold classifier.

Finally inspect the carrier shape. No coordinate stores an ambient isotopy classifier, selected complement homeomorphism, quotient path equality, or global recognition oracle, so the local NameCert cannot return such data.
\end{proof}
