\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CompactCompletionFunctorUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-compact-completion-functor-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges and Kelley, the $\CompactCompletionFunctorUp$ packet records the finite route by which compact metric source data, total-bounded completion data, and L10 regular-name readback are offered as a functorial completion surface. It consumes the finite-net completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallyboundedcompletion-namecert}, the metric-completion interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, and the compact metric source surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. Its L10 observation rows are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a displayed completion functor route; it does not assert open-cover compactness, quotient-completion equality, selected limits, countable choice, host equality of completed points, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Compact completion functor carrier]
\label{def:compact-completion-functor-carrier}
A $\CompactCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(K,T,C,M,L,S,R,D,E,H,A,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row, displaying the compact metric carrier, metric classifier, and compact-facing finite observation surface before any completion read is exposed.
\item $T$ is the $\TotallyBoundedCompletionUp$ row, recording the finite-net completion route, completion embedding, separated handoff, and total-bounded completion ledger consumed by the functor surface.
\item $C$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ row attached to the completed target, displaying the complete metric seal used by completion-facing consumers.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricCompletionUp$ row, exposing the metric-completion admissibility route and the displayed completion map from the source side to the completed side.
\item $L$ is the $\CompletionUp$ interface row, retaining only the functorial source-target ledger, identity route, composition route, and completion-seal handoff visible to downstream packets.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row opened from compact source names and completed target names before any regular readback is public.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from those finite windows and the dyadic tolerance ledger.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row, carrying the dyadic scales, finite-net radii, comparison ledgers, and precision requests used by $T,S,R$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $K,T,C,M,L,S,R,D$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact metric source, total-bounded completion route, complete metric seal, metric-completion map, completion interface, stream windows, regular readback, dyadic tolerance ledger, Real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $A$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a compact-source tolerance request through the finite-net completion route, completion map, stream windows, regular readback, and Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactCompletionFunctorUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\TotallyBoundedCompletionUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactCompletionFunctorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate asserts exactly that these rows form one finite compact-completion functor packet. It has no coordinate for an open-cover compactness theorem, quotient representatives, a selected limit, countable choice, host equality of completed points, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact completion functor NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-completion-functor-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompactCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier $F=(K,T,C,M,L,S,R,D,E,H,A,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CompactCompletionFunctorUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed compact metric source, total-bounded completion route, complete metric seal, metric-completion map, completion interface, stream windows, regular readback, dyadic tolerance ledger, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item finite-net completion handoff, requiring every completion-facing tolerance request to read $K,T,D$ before $M,L$ are exposed;
\item complete-target seal, requiring every completed metric read to pass through $C$ and then through the terminal $\RealUp$ row $E$;
\item functorial replay, requiring identity and composition reads in $L$ to be replayed through the same $S,R,D,E,H,A,P,N$ rows as the underlying completion request;
\item ledger non-escape, excluding open-cover compactness, quotient-completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, host equality of completed points, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness from the packet.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:compact-completion-functor-carrier}. The projected rows are exactly the compact metric source $K$, total-bounded completion route $T$, complete metric seal $C$, metric-completion map $M$, completion interface $L$, finite windows $S$, regular readback $R$, dyadic tolerances $D$, Real seal $E$, and structural rows $H,A,P,N$.

The finite-net completion handoff is inherited from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallyboundedcompletion-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}. A tolerance request first reads compact metric source data and finite-net radii, then reaches the completion-facing map and interface rows.

The complete-target seal is downstream of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}. The L10 observation spine through $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ keeps every completed metric read behind finite windows, regular readbacks, dyadic tolerances, and the terminal seal.

Functorial replay is the role of $L$ together with $H$ and $A$. Identity and composition reads preserve the displayed completion route and cannot replace it by quotient representatives, selected limits, or host equality. The provenance and naming rows $P,N$ name only this finite route, so the excluded principles have no carrier coordinate from which to escape.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact completion functor finite-net handoff]
\label{thm:compact-completion-functor-finite-net-handoff}
Every completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\CompactCompletionFunctorUp$ carrier $F=(K,T,C,M,L,S,R,D,E,H,A,P,N)$ must first read the compact metric source row, the total-bounded finite-net completion route, the dyadic tolerance ledger, the metric-completion map, the completion interface, the finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal. In route notation, every public completion read factors through
$$
K,D,T \longmapsto M,L \longmapsto S,R \longmapsto C,E \longmapsto H,A,P,N .
$$
Thus the handoff exports the L10 finite-net completion route and not an open-cover compactness principle, a quotient-completion equality, a selected limit, countable choice, host equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted carrier $F=(K,T,C,M,L,S,R,D,E,H,A,P,N)$. The source compact metric packet is visible only through $K$, and the finite-net completion route is visible only through $T$ together with dyadic tolerance data $D$.

Apply \autoref{thm:compact-completion-functor-namecert-obligations}. It requires the finite-net route to be read before the metric-completion map $M$ and completion interface $L$ become public.

The L10 readback then passes through $S,R,D,E$. The finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback carry the displayed precision request, while the $\RealUp$ seal is reached only after the complete metric row $C$ has been displayed.

Finally, $H,A,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since the carrier contains no open-cover compactness row, quotient representative, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host equality row, or ambient Real-completeness theorem, the route shown in the statement is the whole public handoff.
\end{proof}
