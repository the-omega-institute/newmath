\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-regulated-riemann-integrability-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, a regulated real function on a compact interval is controlled by finite step approximations and dyadic mesh refinements before a Riemann-sum consumer may read an integral value. The $\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp$ packet records that integrability certificate as BEDC NameCert data. It sits beside the regulated-function schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulated-function-namecert}, the Darboux finite-sum surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-darboux-sum-namecert}, the Riemann-integral readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riemannintegral-namecert}, the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the dyadic mesh ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicmesh-namecert}. The packet names finite mesh-and-sum evidence only; it does not import measure completion, selected limits, quotient function equality, host integration, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Regulated Riemann integrability carrier]
\label{def:regulated-riemann-integrability-carrier}
A $\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(A,M,G,D,T,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the $\RealUp$ interval-domain row, naming the left endpoint, right endpoint, orientation data, and the compact interval on which the certificate is read.
\item $M$ is the $\DyadicMeshUp$ mesh ledger, recording the dyadic subdivision request, refinement order, and finite mesh width used by the certificate.
\item $G$ is the $\RegulatedFunctionUp$ step-approximation schedule, listing the step functions and oscillation bounds inspected on the dyadic mesh.
\item $D$ is the $\DarbouxSumUp$ finite-sum handoff row, exposing lower and upper finite sums computed from the displayed step-approximation data.
\item $T$ is the $\RiemannIntegralUp$ tagged-mesh read, available only after $A,M,G,D$ have been displayed and the Darboux gap is inside the requested dyadic tolerance.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval domain, mesh ledger, step schedule, finite-sum handoff, tagged-mesh read, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interval request through dyadic mesh refinement, regulated step approximation, Darboux finite sums, and tagged-mesh readback.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp$, $\RealUp$, $\DyadicMeshUp$, $\RegulatedFunctionUp$, $\DarbouxSumUp$, $\RiemannIntegralUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $A,M,G,D,T,H,C,P,L$. It has no coordinate for a measure algebra, host function quotient, selected limiting partition, ambient compactness proof, or external completeness principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regulated Riemann integrability NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regulated-riemann-integrability-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp$ carrier
$$
I=(A,M,G,D,T,H,C,P,L),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following five finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the Real interval domain, dyadic mesh ledger, regulated step-approximation schedule, Darboux finite-sum handoff, tagged-mesh read, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item mesh coverage, requiring $M$ to refine the interval row $A$ before the step schedule $G$ is inspected;
\item regulated approximation exactness, requiring the entries of $G$ to be step data over the mesh cells named by $M$;
\item Darboux handoff exactness, requiring $D$ to compute finite lower and upper sums only from $A,M,G$ and to expose the dyadic gap budget;
\item tagged-mesh non-escape, requiring $T$ to be read only after $A,M,G,D$ and the structural rows $H,C,P,L$ are visible.
\end{enumerate}
Thus an accepted regulated-integrability certificate is a finite NameCert packet over interval data, mesh data, regulated approximation data, finite Darboux sums, tagged-mesh readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier of \autoref{def:regulated-riemann-integrability-carrier}. The tuple lists exactly the interval, mesh, approximation, finite-sum, tagged-read, and structural rows.

First read the source side. The interval row $A$ supplies the $\RealUp$ domain, while $M$ supplies the dyadic mesh ledger on that same interval. The regulated schedule $G$ is therefore inspected only cellwise over displayed mesh data.

Next read the finite-sum side. The Darboux row $D$ is computed from the step schedule and mesh cells already displayed, so its lower and upper sums cannot be replaced by a hidden host integral or selected limiting partition.

Finally read $T,H,C,P,L$. The tagged-mesh row $T$ is downstream of the finite Darboux gap, and the transport, replay, provenance, and local-name rows preserve the same route. Since no further coordinate remains, the five obligations exhaust the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedRiemannIntegrabilityUp}$ surface.
\end{proof}
