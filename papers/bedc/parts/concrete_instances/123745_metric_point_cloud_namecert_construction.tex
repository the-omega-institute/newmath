Following Munkres on metric-topological examples and Burago--Burago--Ivanov on finite metric data, the $\MetricPointCloudUp$ packet records a finite sampled cloud together with its displayed distance table and finite observation windows. It sits between the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the finite metric packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-metric-space-namecert}, the finite-net discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the Hausdorff-distance finite-net route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hausdorff-metric-namecert}, and the terminal real-name surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names sampled metric data for later L10 and compact-metric consumers without importing host finite sets, quotient point clouds, selected nearest points, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Metric point-cloud carrier]
\label{def:metric-point-cloud-carrier}
A $\MetricPointCloudUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(X,D,M,F,T,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the finite point-address row, listing only the sampled addresses visible to the cloud.
\item $D$ is the displayed distance-table row for pairs of addresses in $X$.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ row whose zero, symmetry, and triangle ledgers certify that the table in $D$ is metric-facing.
\item $F$ is the $\FiniteMetricSpaceUp$ handoff row, reading the same finite point row and distance table as a finite metric packet.
\item $T$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row generated by the finite cloud itself.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ distance readback row for displayed distance comparisons and finite Hausdorff-distance estimates.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for point addresses, distance-table entries, metric-law rows, finite-metric handoff, total-bounded row, real readback, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through
$$
X,D \longmapsto M,F,T,R \longmapsto H,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricPointCloudUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\FiniteMetricSpaceUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\HausdorffMetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricPointCloudUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $X,D,M,F,T,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host finite-set membership, quotient equality of clouds, selected nearest points, an ambient hyperspace, open-cover compactness, or ambient completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MetricPointCloud NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:metric-point-cloud-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MetricPointCloudUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(X,D,M,F,T,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\MetricPointCloudUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite point-address row, displayed distance table, $\MetricSpaceUp$ metric-law row, $\FiniteMetricSpaceUp$ handoff row, $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row, $\RealUp$ readback row, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. A consumer may read sampled metric data only through those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-point-cloud-carrier}. The point data are exactly $X$, and the distance data are exactly $D$.

Read the metric and finite-metric rows next. The row $M$ supplies the metric-law ledger, while $F$ records that the same point addresses and distance table are available as a $\FiniteMetricSpaceUp$ packet.

The total-bounded and real readback rows are downstream of the same finite table. The row $T$ is the finite net generated by the cloud, and $R$ records the displayed real-valued distance comparisons used by compact or Hausdorff-distance consumers.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$ to transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since the carrier has no row for host finite-set membership, quotient cloud equality, selected nearest points, open-cover compactness, or ambient completion, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Metric point-cloud Hausdorff sample route]
\label{thm:metric-point-cloud-hausdorff-sample-route}
Every Hausdorff-distance or compact-metric consumer of an accepted $\MetricPointCloudUp$ packet reads the sampled cloud through the finite route
$$
X,D \longmapsto M \longmapsto F,T \longmapsto R,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer first displays the point addresses and distance table, then the $\MetricSpaceUp$ metric-law row, then the $\FiniteMetricSpaceUp$ and $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net rows, and only then the $\RealUp$ readback used by the $\HausdorffMetricUp$ comparison route. The packet exports no host hyperspace, quotient equality of point clouds, selected nearest-point map, selected finite subcover, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the displayed cloud rows $X$ and $D$. They are the only point and distance coordinates available to a Hausdorff-distance consumer.

The metric source row $M$ certifies the table before any finite-net reading is public. The finite metric row $F$ and the total-bounded row $T$ then expose the cloud as a finite metric sample and as its own finite net.

The real readback row $R$ is reached only after those finite rows have been displayed. Hence the $\HausdorffMetricUp$ comparison route receives a finite sampled metric packet rather than a host hyperspace, quotient cloud, selected nearest point, selected subcover, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
