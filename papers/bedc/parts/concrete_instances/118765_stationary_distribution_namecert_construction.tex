\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\StationaryDistributionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-stationary-distribution-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kemeny--Snell finite Markov-chain terminology, the
$\StationaryDistributionUp$ packet records an invariant law for a displayed
finite transition matrix. It sits downstream of the transition-packet surface
of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-markovchain-namecert}, the probability
measure rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert} and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-distribution-namecert}, the finite matrix
carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, and the scalar
seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the
finite invariant-law surface only; it is not an ergodic theorem, limiting
distribution theorem, Perron--Frobenius argument, or host stochastic-process
construction.

\begin{definition}[Stationary distribution carrier]
\label{def:stationary-distribution-carrier}
A $\StationaryDistributionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\Pi=(S,M,\pi,L,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the finite state ledger over a displayed $\ProbSpaceUp$ source.
\item $M$ is the $\MatrixUp$ transition row whose entries are nonnegative
$\RealUp$ scalars and whose displayed rows sum to the probability unit.
\item $\pi$ is the $\DistributionUp$ law row on the same finite state ledger.
\item $L$ is the finite left-invariance ledger recording the row equations
$$
\pi_j=\sum_{i\in S}\pi_i M_{ij}
$$
for every displayed state $j$.
\item $E$ records the endpoint compatibility between the transition row, the
probability source, and the displayed law row.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $S,M,\pi,L,E$ and the
local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the Markov transition packet
through the matrix row and invariant-law equations.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\StationaryDistributionUp$,
$\MarkovChainUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\DistributionUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\RealUp$,
$\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\StationaryDistributionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports no convergence rate, recurrence classifier, irreducibility
argument, uniqueness proof, host limiting law, countable-choice selector, or
standard Markov-kernel bridge.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stationary distribution NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:stationary-distribution-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:stationary-distribution-carrier}, the local
$\NameCert_{\StationaryDistributionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier
admission for $\Pi=(S,M,\pi,L,E,H,C,P,N)$, finite state exposure through $S$,
transition-matrix admission through $M$, invariant-law admission through
$\pi$, finite left-invariance equations through $L$, endpoint compatibility
through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$
replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through
$N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. Its coordinates display the finite state ledger, matrix
transition row, law row, invariance equations, endpoint compatibility,
transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The dependency certificates account for the data-bearing rows. The
$\ProbSpaceUp$ row supplies the source ledger, $\MatrixUp$ supplies the finite
transition entries, $\RealUp$ supplies the scalar seals, and
$\DistributionUp$ supplies the law row $\pi$.

The remaining rows transport, replay, source, and name the same finite data.
Since the carrier contains no convergence, recurrence, uniqueness, limiting,
choice, or standard-kernel coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local
NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stationary distribution invariance scope]
\label{thm:stationary-distribution-invariance-scope}
Every invariant-law read of an accepted $\StationaryDistributionUp$ carrier
factors through the finite route
$$
S,M,\pi,L,E,H,C,P,N.
$$
The read inspects the finite $\MatrixUp$ transition row and the
$\DistributionUp$ law row before it can use the equations in $L$, and those
equations are interpreted only through the displayed $\RealUp$ scalar seals
and the shared $\ProbSpaceUp$ state ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier rows $S,M,\pi$. They fix the finite state ledger, the
transition matrix, and the law whose invariance is being certified.

Next read the equation ledger $L$. Each displayed equation has endpoints in
the same finite state ledger and uses only the transition entries and law
weights already exposed by $M$ and $\pi$.

Finally pass through $E,H,C,P,N$. These rows keep the endpoint compatibility,
transport, replay, provenance, and naming attached to the same finite route.
Thus an invariant-law consumer has no alternate path through a host chain,
limiting distribution, or uniqueness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stationary distribution Markov consumer handoff]
\label{thm:stationary-distribution-markov-consumer-handoff}
Whenever a $\MarkovChainUp$ transition packet consumes an accepted
$\StationaryDistributionUp$ carrier, the handoff reads the displayed finite
state ledger $S$, transition row $M$, invariant law $\pi$, invariance ledger
$L$, endpoint row $E$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. The consumer may treat
$\pi$ as a stationary law for the displayed finite transition packet, but it
does not receive a convergence theorem, recurrence decomposition,
irreducibility witness, uniqueness classifier, or standard Markov-kernel
object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the Markov transition-packet source boundary from
\autoref{thm:markovchain-probspace-randomvar-source-boundary}. It keeps the
probability source, random-variable rows, distribution laws, and transition
rows inside the displayed $\MarkovChainUp$ packet.

Attach the stationary carrier along the common finite transition row. The
matrix row $M$ supplies the finite transition entries, while $\pi$ and $L$
record the invariant law and its finite equations over the same state ledger.

The structural rows then replay the handoff without adding host stochastic
process data. Since neither carrier contains a convergence, recurrence,
irreducibility, uniqueness, or standard-kernel coordinate, the Markov consumer
receives exactly the displayed stationary-law surface.
\end{proof}
