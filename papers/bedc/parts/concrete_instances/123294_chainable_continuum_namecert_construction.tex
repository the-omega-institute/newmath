\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ChainableContinuumUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-chainable-continuum-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Nadler's continuum theory and Kuratowski's compact metric treatment, the $\ChainableContinuumUp$ packet records the traditional chainable-continuum surface as finite BEDC rows. It sits after the compact metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the connected-space carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-connected-space-namecert}, the Peano continuum route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-peano-continuum-namecert}, the locally connected continuum route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-connected-continuum-namecert}, and the Lelek fan boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lelek-fan-namecert}. The packet names finite chain-cover and mesh-refinement data; it does not import full continuum classification, selected covers, open-cover compactness, quotient continua, or classical choice principles.

\begin{definition}[Chainable continuum carrier]
\label{def:chainable-continuum-carrier}
A $\ChainableContinuumUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(K,C,L,M,T,R,H,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row, exposing the compact metric carrier, displayed finite probes, and metric-read windows used by the packet.
\item $C$ is the $\ConnectedSpaceUp$ row, recording that the same source is read through a connectedness surface before chain covers are consumed.
\item $L$ is the finite chain-cover ledger: each displayed cover is linearly ordered, adjacent links overlap through visible membership rows, and non-adjacent links carry the stated separation refusal.
\item $M$ is the refinement and mesh row, recording the requested tolerance, the chosen finite chain cover at that tolerance, and the displayed mesh comparison against the compact metric row.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact metric, connectedness, chain-cover, refinement, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a chainability request through $K,C,L,M,T,P,N$.
\item $H$ is the handoff row to downstream continuum packets, including the Peano continuum, locally connected continuum, and Lelek fan siblings only as cited consumer routes.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ChainableContinuumUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ConnectedSpaceUp$, $\PeanoContinuumUp$, $\LocallyConnectedContinuumUp$, $\LelekFanUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ChainableContinuumUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,C,L,M,T,R,H,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host continuum, an arbitrary open-cover theorem, a selected refinement oracle, quotient equality of continua, continuum classification, or a choice principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Chainable continuum NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:chainable-continuum-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ChainableContinuumUp$ carrier $Q=(K,C,L,M,T,R,H,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ChainableContinuumUp}$ surface consists exactly of compact-metric source admission, connectedness visibility, finite chain-cover ledger exposure, refinement and mesh accountability, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, downstream continuum handoff, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ChainableContinuumUp.ChainableContinuumTasteGate\_single\_carrier\_alignment}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier. The compact metric source is $K$, the connectedness row is $C$, and the chain-cover data are $L,M$.

Read the structural rows next. Transport through $T$ preserves the same displayed rows, replay through $R$ keeps consumers inside the finite route, and $H$ only cites downstream continuum siblings as consumers of the chainable surface.

The provenance row $P$ and local name $N$ bind the packet. Since no further coordinate exists in \autoref{def:chainable-continuum-carrier}, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations and no host continuum object can be read from the certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chainable continuum finite chain refinement]
\label{thm:chainable-continuum-finite-chain-refinement}
Every chainability read exported by an accepted $\ChainableContinuumUp$ packet factors through the finite route
$$
\CompactMetricUp \longmapsto \ConnectedSpaceUp \longmapsto L,M \longmapsto T,R,H,P,N .
$$
The refinement row $M$ may shrink mesh by choosing a displayed finite chain cover already recorded in $L$, but it cannot invoke a selected open-cover refinement, a quotient continuum, a host compactness theorem, or a classification of chainable continua.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ChainableContinuumUp.ChainableContinuumCarrier\_finite\_chain\_refinement}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the compact metric and connectedness rows. The source is read through $K$ and $C$ before any chain cover is available.

The finite chain-cover ledger $L$ supplies only displayed links and adjacency data. The refinement row $M$ then records the tolerance and mesh comparison for one displayed chain cover from that ledger.

Finally transport and replay the same rows through $T,R,H,P,N$. Because selected covers, quotient continua, and host compactness theorems have no carrier coordinate, every refinement read remains inside the finite route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chainable continuum nonescape]
\label{thm:chainable-continuum-nonescape}
No accepted $\ChainableContinuumUp$ packet exports data outside $K,C,L,M,T,R,H,P,N$. In particular, it does not export a full continuum classification, an open-cover compactness principle, a selected refinement oracle, quotient equality of continua, local connectedness, Peano parametrization, or a Lelek fan endpoint theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier of \autoref{def:chainable-continuum-carrier}. It contains compact metric and connectedness rows, finite chain-cover and mesh rows, transport, replay, handoff, provenance, and local naming.

Apply the NameCert obligations and the finite refinement theorem. Each consumer read must pass through the displayed route and therefore cannot recover a datum with no row in the carrier.

The Peano continuum, locally connected continuum, and Lelek fan chapters remain sibling consumers or comparison surfaces. Their stronger structure is not copied into $\ChainableContinuumUp$, so the chainable packet exports only the finite chain-cover surface described here.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ChainableContinuumUp.ChainableContinuumCarrier\_nonescape}
