Following Bishop--Bridges closed interval practice, the $\ClosedIntervalObjectUp$ packet records the finite endpoint-and-window surface by which a displayed real interval is handed to L10 Real and Completion consumers. It sits between the dyadic tolerance source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite observation discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the finite-net interval route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-closed-interval-finite-net-namecert}. The packet names only displayed endpoint order, membership-window, tolerance, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows; it is not an open-cover compactness theorem, quotient interval equality, real trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Closed interval object carrier]
\label{def:closed-interval-object-carrier}
A $\ClosedIntervalObjectUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(a,b,O,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $a$ is the lower endpoint row, read as a displayed $\RealUp$ name through finite windows.
\item $b$ is the upper endpoint row, read as a displayed $\RealUp$ name through the same finite-window discipline.
\item $O$ is the endpoint-order evidence row, recording the displayed comparison that places $a$ before $b$ at the inspected tolerances.
\item $M$ is the membership-window row, listing the inspected interval elements and the endpoint-window comparisons that keep them between $a$ and $b$.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row exposing the requested endpoint and membership reads.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger attached to the endpoint-order and membership-window rows.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row serving the same finite windows with regular rational observations.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row available only after $a,b,O,M,W,D,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint, order, membership, tolerance, readback, seal, provenance, route, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a closed-interval request through $a,b,O,M,W,D,R,E$ and back to the structural rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ClosedIntervalObjectUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\LocatedClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedIntervalObjectUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports only this finite displayed closed-interval object. It contains no coordinate for open-cover compactness, quotient equality of interval names, unrestricted endpoint trichotomy, countable choice, selected finite nets, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closed interval object NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:closed-interval-object-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ClosedIntervalObjectUp$ carrier
$$
K=(a,b,O,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedIntervalObjectUp}$ surface consists exactly of lower endpoint exposure through $a$, upper endpoint exposure through $b$, endpoint-order evidence through $O$, membership-window admission through $M$, finite-window exposure through $W$, dyadic tolerance readback through $D$, regular rational readback through $R$, terminal seal admission through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. Any closed-interval consumer must read those rows before using the interval object as a source for finite-net, compact-interval, or Heine--Borel routes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:closed-interval-object-carrier}. The endpoint rows, order row, membership-window row, stream window, dyadic tolerance ledger, regular rational readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local name are the complete visible packet.

The endpoint route first reads $a,b,O$. Thus the two real-name endpoints and their displayed order evidence are visible before any membership or finite-net request can be consumed.

Next read $M,W,D,R$. The membership row lists the inspected interval elements, the stream row names the finite endpoint and membership windows, the dyadic row supplies the tolerance ledger, and the regular rational row supplies readback for those windows. The seal row $E$ is downstream of that finite route.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since the carrier has no open-cover compactness theorem, quotient interval equality, real trichotomy, countable-choice schedule, selected finite-net row, or ambient completeness coordinate, none of those principles can be exported by the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed interval object endpoint-order window]
\label{thm:closed-interval-object-endpoint-order-window}
For an accepted $\ClosedIntervalObjectUp$ carrier $K=(a,b,O,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, every endpoint-order read factors through the displayed route
$$
\StreamNameUp \longmapsto \DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
using $W,D,R,E$ after the lower endpoint row $a$, upper endpoint row $b$, and order row $O$ have been exposed. The endpoint-order window may be transported and replayed by $H$ and $C$, but it cannot become a host trichotomy principle, quotient equality of real names, selected midpoint, open-cover compactness theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the obligation theorem. It forces an endpoint-order request to expose $a,b,O$ before membership, readback, or Real-facing rows are available.

Use the finite-window dependencies. The row $W$ names the inspected endpoint windows, $D$ supplies the dyadic tolerance comparisons for the order evidence, $R$ gives regular rational readback, and $E$ seals the Real-facing observation.

Finally use the structural rows. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay preserve the same finite endpoint-order route, while $P$ and $N$ source and name it. No remaining row supplies trichotomy, quotient equality, a selected midpoint, compactness, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed interval object nonescape]
\label{thm:closed-interval-object-nonescape}

$$

The handoff supplies a finite closed-interval object surface, not open-cover compactness, arbitrary subcover choice, quotient interval equality, real trichotomy, countable choice, selected endpoints, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:closed-interval-object-namecert-obligations}. They place lower endpoint exposure, upper endpoint exposure, endpoint-order evidence, and membership-window admission before any compactness-facing consumer can use the interval object.

Apply the endpoint-order window theorem \autoref{thm:closed-interval-object-endpoint-order-window}. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that same packet under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Since no row remains for open-cover compactness, arbitrary subcover choice, quotient interval equality, trichotomy, countable choice, selected endpoints, or ambient completeness, every downstream handoff stays inside the finite closed-interval object surface.
