\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegulatedStepFunctionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-regulated-step-function-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on regulated functions and Riemann integration, the $\RegulatedStepFunctionUp$ packet records the finite step ledger that sits between the single-step partition surface and the regulated or Riemann integration consumers. It refines the finite $\StepFunctionUp$ route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-step-function-namecert}, remains readable by the regulated-function approximation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulatedrealfunction-namecert}, and supplies the step-integral primitive consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulatedintegral-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riemannintegral-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[Regulated step function carrier]
\label{def:regulated-step-function-carrier}
A $\RegulatedStepFunctionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(I,S,A,J,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the interval source row, naming the closed domain, endpoint order data, and finite cell boundary reads used by every step request.
\item $S$ is a finite $\StepFunctionUp$ schedule, read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-step-function-namecert}, listing the displayed partition cells, carried $\RealUp$ values, and constant-on-cell admission data.
\item $A$ is the $\RegulatedRealFunctionUp$ approximation row, read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulatedrealfunction-namecert}, stating that the same finite step schedule is accepted as a regulated-function approximation surface.
\item $J$ is the primitive step-integral ledger, computing finite cell sums from $I$ and $S$ without importing a limiting integration theorem.
\item $D$ is the Darboux/Riemann compatibility row, recording that the primitive ledger fits the Darboux gap route and the tagged-mesh route before any integration-facing handoff is exported.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the displayed finite sums and tolerance windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal reached only after $I,S,A,J,D,Q$ are displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval, schedule, regulated approximation, primitive step-integral, compatibility, readback, seal, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from interval reads to step schedule reads, from step schedules to primitive sums, from compatibility rows to Darboux and Riemann consumers, and from readback rows to the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegulatedStepFunctionUp$, $\StepFunctionUp$, $\RegulatedRealFunctionUp$, $\RegulatedIntegralUp$, $\RiemannIntegralUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedStepFunctionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only by these rows. It has no coordinate for a host function quotient, selected refinement limit, measure completion, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regulated step function NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regulated-step-function-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegulatedStepFunctionUp$ carrier $K=(I,S,A,J,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedStepFunctionUp}$ surface consists exactly of interval source admission, finite $\StepFunctionUp$ schedule exposure, $\RegulatedRealFunctionUp$ approximation admission, primitive step-integral computation, Darboux/Riemann compatibility, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regulated-step-function-carrier}. The rows $I,S,A$ display the interval source, the finite step schedule, and the regulated-function approximation surface before any integration-facing consumer can inspect a value.

The integration rows are downstream of that displayed surface. The primitive ledger $J$ computes only finite cell sums from $I$ and $S$, while $D$ records that the same ledger is readable by Darboux and Riemann consumers.

The readback and structural rows preserve the same route. The rows $Q,E,H,C,P,N$ add regular rational readback, terminal Real sealing, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming, but no hidden function quotient, selected refinement, measure-completion object, compactness proof, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regulated step function Riemann handoff]
\label{thm:regulated-step-function-riemann-handoff}
Every Riemann-facing or regulated-integral-facing read of an accepted $\RegulatedStepFunctionUp$ packet factors through
$$
\StepFunctionUp,\quad \RegulatedRealFunctionUp,\quad \RegulatedIntegralUp,\quad \RiemannIntegralUp
$$
by first reading the finite interval and step schedule rows $I,S$, then the regulated approximation row $A$, then the primitive step-integral ledger $J$, then the Darboux/Riemann compatibility row $D$, and finally the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback and $\RealUp$ seal $Q,E$. The packet exports a finite regulated step primitive, not a quotient function, a selected mesh limit, a measure-completion integral, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite step side. The $\StepFunctionUp$ schedule $S$ exposes the cell partition and carried values over the interval row $I$, so the primitive sum ledger has a finite source.

Next read the regulated-function side. The row $A$ makes the same finite schedule available to the $\RegulatedRealFunctionUp$ approximation surface, and no integration row is present before this approximation row is displayed.

Finally read the integration side. The primitive ledger $J$ supplies finite sums, the compatibility row $D$ routes those sums through the regulated-integral and Riemann boundaries, and $Q,E$ provide regular rational readback and the terminal Real seal. Since every remaining coordinate is structural, no consumer can replace this handoff by a host quotient, selected mesh limit, measure completion, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{proof}
