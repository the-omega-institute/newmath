\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\InjectiveModuleUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-injective-module-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Baer and Cartan--Eilenberg, the $\InjectiveModuleUp$ packet records the finite homological-algebra interface in which maps out of a displayed submodule inclusion extend into a chosen module row. It sits over the ring and module surfaces of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-module-namecert}, reads linear maps through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-linearmap-namecert}, and keeps its exactness boundary compatible with the exact-sequence obligation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-abeliancat-namecert} and the displayed $\ExactSequenceUp$ handoff in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-universal-coefficient-namecert}. The packet is an extension-lifting certificate; it is not a host injective object, a derived-functor computation, a quotient representative, or an ambient choice principle.

\begin{definition}[Injective module carrier]
\label{def:injective-module-carrier}
An $\InjectiveModuleUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(R,M,S,F,E,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the scalar $\RingUp$ row and $M$ is the target $\ModuleUp$ row over that scalar source.
\item $S$ is the submodule inclusion row, recording a displayed inclusion $A\hookrightarrow B$ inside the same module classifier window.
\item $F$ is the source hom row, read as a $\LinearMapUp$ map from the submodule row $A$ into $M$.
\item $E$ is the extended hom row, read as a $\LinearMapUp$ map from the ambient row $B$ into $M$.
\item $B$ is the exact-sequence boundary row, recording that the restriction of $E$ along $S$ is the displayed source hom row $F$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $R,M,S,F,E,B,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an extension request through the module, inclusion, source hom, extended hom, and boundary rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\InjectiveModuleUp$, $\RingUp$, $\ModuleUp$, $\LinearMapUp$, $\ExactSequenceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\InjectiveModuleUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $R,M,S,F,E,B,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host category of modules, a selected quotient representative, a global Ext calculation, an arbitrary splitting, or an unstated choice of extension.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Injective module NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:injective-module-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\InjectiveModuleUp$ carrier
$$
I=(R,M,S,F,E,B,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\InjectiveModuleUp}$ surface consists exactly of scalar-ring admission, target-module admission, submodule-inclusion exposure, source-hom exposure, extended-hom exposure, exact-sequence boundary exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every extension-facing consumer must expose $R,M,S,F,E,B$ before it may consume transport, replay, provenance, or naming rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:injective-module-carrier}. The scalar row $R$ and target row $M$ are the only module data available to the classifier, and the inclusion row $S$ is the only displayed submodule input.

The source hom row $F$ and extended hom row $E$ are both read through the $\LinearMapUp$ surface over the same scalar source. The boundary row $B$ records their restriction contact, so an extension request cannot bypass $S,F,E$, or the exact-sequence boundary by appealing to a host quotient or an external lifting theorem.

Transport and replay preserve the same finite route componentwise, while provenance and the local naming row bind it to $\InjectiveModuleUp$. Since no other coordinates occur in the carrier, these rows exhaust the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Injective module extension lifting]
\label{thm:injective-module-extension-lifting}
Every extension-lifting read of an accepted $\InjectiveModuleUp$ packet factors through the displayed route
$$
\RingUp,\ModuleUp,\LinearMapUp,\ExactSequenceUp \longmapsto \InjectiveModuleUp .
$$
Concretely, a consumer must read the scalar ring row $R$, target module row $M$, submodule inclusion row $S$, source hom row $F$, extended hom row $E$, and exact-sequence boundary row $B$ before it may claim that the source map has been extended. The exported lift is the displayed row $E$ together with its boundary contact $B$; it is not a host injective-object theorem, Baer criterion over all ideals, Ext-vanishing statement, selected splitting, quotient representative, or ambient choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:injective-module-namecert-obligations}. It forces every extension-facing consumer to expose the ring, module, inclusion, source hom, extended hom, and boundary rows before any structural support row is read.

The lift is therefore not a separate existence principle. It is the row $E$ already present in the finite carrier, checked against $F$ through the boundary row $B$ and replayed through the same $\Cont$ route.

The sibling chapters supply the route components: $\RingUp$ and $\ModuleUp$ provide the scalar and target module rows, $\LinearMapUp$ provides the hom rows, and the exact-sequence surfaces keep the restriction boundary visible. The missing host coordinates exclude global injectivity, Ext computations, arbitrary splittings, quotient representatives, and choice-based extension selection.
\end{proof}
