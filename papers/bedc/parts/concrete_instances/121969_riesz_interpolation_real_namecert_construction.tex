Following Riesz's interpolation property for ordered groups and vector lattices, the $\RieszInterpolationRealUp$ packet records a finite interpolation witness for two displayed lower real names and two displayed upper real names inside the constructive Real algebraic-order surface. It sits between the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the Real algebraic-order boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-alg-order-namecert}, and the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}. The packet names the dyadic comparison route that witnesses one interpolant $w$ with $a_{0},a_{1}\leq w\leq b_{0},b_{1}$; it does not assert trichotomy, quotient real equality, selected representatives, order completion, choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[RieszInterpolationReal carrier]
\label{def:riesz-interpolation-real-carrier}

$$
I=(R,A,D,L,U,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row naming the four endpoint real names and the candidate interpolant real name.
\item $A$ is the $\RealAlgOrderUp$ operation-and-order row exposing the constructive nonnegative-difference comparisons used by the packet.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison ledger recording the finite dyadic radii, slack rows, and lower-upper comparison budget.
\item $L$ is the lower-pair row, displaying the two lower endpoints $a_{0},a_{1}$ and their dyadic comparison requests against the upper endpoints.
\item $U$ is the upper-pair row, displaying the two upper endpoints $b_{0},b_{1}$ and the finite evidence that each lower endpoint is below each upper endpoint through $A$ and $D$.
\item $W$ is the interpolation-witness row, naming a displayed real name $w$ together with the four comparison reads $a_{0}\leq w$, $a_{1}\leq w$, $w\leq b_{0}$, and $w\leq b_{1}$ through the same RealAlgOrder and dyadic ledger rows.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the Real seal, RealAlgOrder row, dyadic ledger, lower pair, upper pair, witness row, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a real-order interpolation request through $R,A,D,L,U,W,H$ back to the same finite comparison surface.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RieszInterpolationRealUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RealAlgOrderUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RieszInterpolationRealUp}$ row binding the interpolation packet.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $R,A,D,L,U,W,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host real order, trichotomy, quotient representatives, a selected order-completion element, countable choice, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RieszInterpolationReal dyadic witness]
\label{thm:riesz-interpolation-real-dyadic-witness}
For every accepted $\RieszInterpolationRealUp$ carrier
$$
I=(R,A,D,L,U,W,H,C,P,N),
$$
every real-order interpolation consumer must read the finite route
$$
R,A,D,L,U \longrightarrow W \longrightarrow H,C,P,N
$$
before using the interpolant. The route supplies the four endpoint real seals, the RealAlgOrder comparison row, the shared dyadic comparison ledger, the lower-pair row, the upper-pair row, and the displayed witness row; it supplies no trichotomy branch, quotient equality of real names, selected representative, order-completion principle, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:riesz-interpolation-real-carrier}. Its analytic rows are exactly the Real seal row $R$, the RealAlgOrder row $A$, the dyadic comparison ledger $D$, the lower-pair row $L$, the upper-pair row $U$, and the interpolation witness row $W$.

The comparison premises are finite. The lower and upper rows are accepted only through the displayed RealAlgOrder comparisons and the dyadic ledger, so the request cannot switch to a host order relation or to a quotient equality of real names.

The witness row is read after those premises. Its four comparisons reuse the same $A$ and $D$ rows, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name this same packet. Since none of the excluded order-completion or choice principles appears as a carrier coordinate, the listed route exhausts the consumer-facing witness surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RieszInterpolationReal ledger stability]
\label{thm:riesz-interpolation-real-ledger-stability}
Let $I=(R,A,D,L,U,W,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RieszInterpolationRealUp$ carrier. If a downstream $\RealAlgOrderUp$ consumer refines the dyadic tolerance rows inside the same ledger $D$ while preserving the displayed lower-pair, upper-pair, and witness comparisons, then the accepted interpolation read still factors through
$$
R,A,D,L,U,W,H,C,P,N .
$$
The refinement may narrow the comparison radii, but it cannot add a new host real-order coordinate, a trichotomy selector, quotient real equality, countable choice, a selected completion point, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the dyadic witness route of \autoref{thm:riesz-interpolation-real-dyadic-witness}. It identifies the only rows available to any real-order interpolation consumer.

A refinement inside $D$ changes only the displayed dyadic radius and slack entries carried by the same ledger. The lower-pair row $L$, upper-pair row $U$, and witness row $W$ remain tied to the same Real seal $R$ and RealAlgOrder row $A$.

Transport and replay are already recorded by $H$ and $C$, while $P$ and $N$ keep the provenance and local naming fixed. Therefore the refined read is still the same finite NameCert route, with no additional analytic or choice-dependent coordinate.
\end{proof}
