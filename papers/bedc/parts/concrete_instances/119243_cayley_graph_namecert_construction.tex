\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CayleyGraphUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-cayley-graph-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Cayley and standard algebraic graph theory, the $\CayleyGraphUp$ packet names the finite graph generated by a displayed group row and a finite generator list as BEDC NameCert data. It sits after the finite graph surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-graph-namecert}, the algebraic group surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-group-namecert}, and the list spine of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-list-namecert}. The packet records vertices, directed generator-labelled edges, finite walk replay, transport, provenance, and local naming; it does not import quotient graph equality, a selected group presentation, ambient connectedness, or an unbounded word problem oracle.

\begin{definition}[CayleyGraph carrier]
\label{def:cayley-graph-carrier}
A $\CayleyGraphUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\Gamma=(G,S,V,E,L,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the consumed $\NameCert_{\GroupUp}$ row, retaining the multiplication, unit, inverse, and finite support rows visible to graph construction.
\item $S$ is the finite generator-list row, read through the $\ListUp$ spine and carrying the displayed generator entries and membership tags.
\item $V$ is the vertex row, naming exactly the group elements admitted from $G$ as graph vertices.
\item $E$ is the edge-incidence row, recording triples $(g,s,g\cdot s)$ for visible $g$ in $V$ and visible $s$ in $S$.
\item $L$ is the generator-label ledger, attaching each edge in $E$ to its unique displayed generator row from $S$.
\item $W$ is the walk and replay row, storing finite generator words, their successive vertex rows, and the displayed endpoint replay.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the group, generator list, vertices, edges, labels, walks, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from any graph request through $G,S,V,E,L,W,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CayleyGraphUp$, $\GraphUp$, $\GroupUp$, $\ListUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CayleyGraphUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $G,S,V,E,L,W,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotienting group elements, unlabelled host graph equality, selected presentations, Cayley-complex cells, ambient connectedness, or an unbounded word problem solver.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CayleyGraph NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cayley-graph-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CayleyGraphUp$ carrier
$$
\Gamma=(G,S,V,E,L,W,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CayleyGraphUp}$ surface consists exactly of group-row admission, finite generator-list admission, vertex exposure, edge-incidence exposure, generator-label exactness, finite walk replay, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every graph-facing consumer must expose $G,S,V,E,L,W$ before the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ can be consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cayley-graph-carrier}. The rows $G,S,V,E,L,W$ are the only graph-building coordinates, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name those coordinates.

The group row $G$ supplies the multiplication, unit, inverse, and visible element rows. The finite list row $S$ supplies the only generator entries available to edge construction.

The graph rows are then forced: vertices come from $V$, edge incidences from $E$, generator labels from $L$, and finite walks from $W$. Since no other coordinate appears in the carrier, a consumer cannot obtain quotient graph equality, a hidden presentation, or ambient connectedness from the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CayleyGraph edge-label exactness]
\label{thm:cayley-graph-edge-label-exactness}
Let $\Gamma=(G,S,V,E,L,W,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\CayleyGraphUp$ carrier. Every public edge read factors through one visible vertex row from $V$, one visible generator row from $S$, one multiplication read from $G$, and the edge-incidence row $E$ before the label ledger $L$ is consumed. Thus an accepted edge cannot be relabelled by a generator outside $S$ or detached from the group multiplication row that produced its endpoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the edge-incidence row $E$. By \autoref{def:cayley-graph-carrier}, each displayed edge is a triple $(g,s,g\cdot s)$ over the vertex row $V$, generator list $S$, and multiplication row of $G$.

Read the label ledger $L$ only after this incidence row is visible. The ledger attaches the edge to the same generator row $s$, so it cannot substitute a generator outside the finite list.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:cayley-graph-namecert-obligations}. Transport and replay preserve the displayed incidence and label rows componentwise, so an accepted public edge retains both its group multiplication endpoint and its generator label.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CayleyGraph graph-group route]
\label{thm:cayley-graph-graph-group-route}
Every accepted $\CayleyGraphUp$ graph consumer factors through the route
$$
\GroupUp,\quad
\ListUp,\quad
\GraphUp,\quad
\BHist,\quad
\NameCert .
$$
The $\GraphUp$ surface sees only vertices, labelled edge incidences, and finite walk replay generated from the consumed group row and finite generator list. It does not receive quotient group equality, an unlabelled host graph, an infinite presentation, a selected normal form, or an ambient connectedness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project $G$ and $S$ from the carrier. These rows provide the algebraic source and finite generator list before any graph row is read.

Next expose $V,E,L,W$. This is the graph-facing surface: vertices are admitted group elements, edges are multiplication incidences by listed generators, labels retain generator identity, and walks replay finite generator words.

The route ends with $H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Because the carrier has no row for quotient group equality, unlabelled graph equality, infinite presentations, selected normal forms, or connectedness, no accepted graph consumer can read such data from $\Gamma$.
\end{proof}
