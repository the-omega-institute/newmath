\label{def:darboux-sums-carrier}

$$

\item $P$ is the $\PartitionUp$ row naming the ordered finite cell list, adjacency data, and partition coverage.
\item $L$ and $U$ are lower-value and upper-value ledgers over the cells of $P$, with every value read through the same displayed partition route.

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DarbouxSums NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:darboux-sums-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DarbouxSumsUp$ carrier $D=(I,P,L,U,SL,SU,G,R,E,H,C,Q,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DarbouxSumsUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the $\RealUp$ interval source, finite $\PartitionUp$ row, lower and upper value ledgers, lower and upper finite sum rows, upper-minus-lower gap row, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, terminal $\RealUp$ seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming. Every obligation is read through the displayed $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ cell-width arithmetic and the existing $\DarbouxSumUp$ comparison boundary before any $\RiemannIntegralUp$ consumer is available.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:darboux-sums-carrier}. The interval, partition, lower-value, upper-value, finite-sum, gap, readback, and seal rows are the only analytic rows carried by the packet.

The partition and arithmetic obligations are finite. Row $P$ supplies the ordered cell list, and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ support in $Q$ supplies the displayed cell-width arithmetic used by $SL$ and $SU$.

The Darboux comparison obligation is read through $G$. It is downstream of $P,L,U,SL,SU$ and agrees with the single $\DarbouxSumUp$ boundary cited by the provenance row.

Finally $R,E,H,C,Q,N$ read back, seal, transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since no coordinate stores quotient functions, host suprema, selected limits, countable choice, or real completeness, no NameCert obligation can export those objects.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DarbouxSums real partition scope]
\label{thm:darboux-sums-real-partition-scope}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

Use the finite sum rows $SL$ and $SU$ together with the gap row $G$. These rows compute lower and upper Darboux sums over the same displayed cells and route the comparison through the $\DarbouxSumUp$ boundary.

The structural rows $H,C,Q,N$ preserve this order, so no Riemann or Real-facing consumer can bypass the finite partition route or import a refused host principle.
\end{proof}
