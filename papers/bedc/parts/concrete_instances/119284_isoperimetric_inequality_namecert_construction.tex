\begin{definition}[Isoperimetric inequality carrier]
\label{def:isoperimetric-inequality-carrier}

$$

\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ row for the displayed planar distance comparisons used by edge lengths and compact containment.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactUp$ row naming the finite containment window for the inspected polygonal region.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ comparison row used for convex hull and support-line checks.

\begin{theorem}[Isoperimetric inequality NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:isoperimetric-inequality-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\IsoperimetricInequalityUp$ carrier $I=(R,M,K,V,Q,A,L,B,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\IsoperimetricInequalityUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed real scalar, metric, compact, convex, polytope, polygonal area, finite perimeter, isoperimetric budget, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. A certificate may replay the finite inequality route, but it cannot read measure completion, a selected minimizing region, quotient equality of domains, host Euclidean equality, open-cover compactness, or an ambient compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:isoperimetric-inequality-carrier}. The only analytic rows available to a consumer are the scalar row $R$, metric row $M$, compact window $K$, convex comparison row $V$, polytope ledger $Q$, area row $A$, perimeter row $L$, and budget row $B$.

Read the polygonal rows first. The polytope ledger supplies the finite vertices, oriented edges, and faces; the compact row bounds the inspected region; the convex row supplies only the displayed comparison checks; and the metric and real rows turn the same edge data into length and scalar comparisons.

Transport and replay then preserve the finite route. The rows $H$ and $C$ move and replay the same polygonal area, perimeter, and budget ledgers, while $P$ and $N$ source and name the packet. Since the forbidden objects have no carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Isoperimetric inequality finite perimeter route]
\label{thm:isoperimetric-inequality-finite-perimeter-route}

The route exports no completed perimeter measure, selected optimizer, quotient region, host plane equality, or classical minimizer principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier. The row $B$ is downstream of $Q,A,L$ and therefore cannot be read until the finite polytope, area, and perimeter ledgers have been displayed.

The sibling rows supply exactly the required inputs. $\PolytopeUp$ gives the finite boundary ledger, $\MetricSpaceUp$ supplies the edge-length comparisons, $\RealUp$ seals the scalar area and perimeter reads, $\CompactUp$ bounds the inspected region, and $\ConvexSetUp$ records the displayed convex comparison surface.

Finally replay the route through $H,C,P,N$. These rows preserve and name the same finite data, so the inequality read remains a polygonal finite-perimeter route rather than a measure-completion theorem, a selected minimizer, or an ambient Euclidean principle.
\end{proof}
