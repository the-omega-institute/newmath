\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-stream-merge-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, a finite merge of two lawlike source windows is a useful construction only when the two finite schedules, the merge order, and the downstream rational readback remain visible. The $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$ packet sits between the finite schedule surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-stream-namecert}, the parity interleaving surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stream-interleaving-namecert}, the stream-name readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic arithmetic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It names a two-source finite merge certificate without replacing either source by host sequence equality or an unrecorded choice of positions.

\begin{definition}[Finite stream merge carrier]
\label{def:finite-stream-merge-carrier}
A $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(F_0,F_1,M,S,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F_0$ is the first $\FiniteStreamUp$ source schedule, including its finite request row, list row, and window boundary.
\item $F_1$ is the second $\FiniteStreamUp$ source schedule, with the same finite request/list/window discipline.
\item $M$ is the finite merge-order row, recording which displayed output positions read from $F_0$ and which read from $F_1$.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ handoff row obtained after the two finite schedules and the merge-order row have been displayed.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the source windows and the merged output window.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row used to compare the rational reads at the requested finite precision.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal boundary, available only after $F_0,F_1,M,S,Q,D$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for both finite sources, the merge order, stream-name handoff, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance, Real seal, provenance, replay, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from source-window inspection through merge-order selection, StreamName handoff, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore comparison, and Real sealing.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$, $\FiniteStreamUp$, $\StreamInterleavingUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteStreamMergeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for host sequence equality, countable choice over positions, an unbounded stream merge, a hidden subsequence selector, or a Real value that bypasses the displayed finite merge order.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite stream merge NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-stream-merge-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$ carrier
$$
G=(F_0,F_1,M,S,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteStreamMergeUp}$ surface consists exactly of first finite-source schedule exposure, second finite-source schedule exposure, finite merge-order exposure, StreamName handoff, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance comparison, Real-seal boundary, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. No obligation row may erase either finite source, reorder the merge outside $M$, replace the route by host sequence equality, or export a Real-facing read before the finite merge certificate is displayed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-stream-merge-carrier}. The first two rows are the source schedules $F_0$ and $F_1$, each read through the finite request/list/window discipline of $\FiniteStreamUp$.

The merge-order row $M$ is the only coordinate that combines the two sources. It records the finite output positions, so the packet cannot appeal to countable choice over later positions or to equality of host sequences.

The downstream rows $S,Q,D,R$ expose StreamName handoff, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance comparison, and Real sealing only after $F_0,F_1,M$ have been displayed. Thus every analytic read remains attached to the finite merge route.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the excluded objects are absent from the carrier, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite stream merge StreamName handoff]
\label{thm:finite-stream-merge-streamname-handoff}
Every $\StreamNameUp$ or $\StreamInterleavingUp$ consumer of an accepted $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$ packet receives the handoff row $S$ only through the two finite source schedules $F_0,F_1$ and the finite merge-order row $M$. The consumer may compare with the interleaving packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stream-interleaving-namecert}, but it may not replace the displayed merge certificate by a private stream selector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read $F_0$ and $F_1$ first as finite schedules. Each source exposes only a finite request, list, and window boundary, so no infinite host stream is available to the consumer.

Next read the merge-order row $M$. This row determines which source supplies each displayed output position before any $\StreamNameUp$ handoff is issued.

The handoff row $S$ is therefore downstream of the same finite merge certificate. The parity split route of \autoref{thm:stream-interleaving-parity-window-split} is a sibling consumer of this discipline: it may read the finite source windows, but it still receives the output through a displayed selection row rather than through hidden sequence equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite stream merge Real nonescape]
\label{thm:finite-stream-merge-real-nonescape}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\FiniteStreamMergeUp$ packet factors through
$$
(F_0,F_1,M)\longmapsto S\longmapsto Q\longmapsto D\longmapsto R
$$
with the same $H,C,P,N$ rows attached. A Real-facing read cannot bypass the two finite source schedules, the merge-order row, the StreamName handoff, the regular rational readback, or the dyadic tolerance comparison.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The Real seal row $R$ is displayed after the finite sources $F_0,F_1$, the merge order $M$, the StreamName handoff $S$, the RegSeqRat readback $Q$, and the DyadicRatCore tolerance row $D$.

A regular rational consumer must therefore read $Q$ as a readback of the displayed source and output windows, not as an independent rational sequence. The dyadic row $D$ then checks the requested precision against that same finite data.

Finally the Real consumer reads $R$ only after the route above has been replayed through $C$ and transported through $H$. Provenance $P$ and local naming $N$ keep the same certificate attached, so a Real-facing value has no route around the finite merge packet.
\end{proof}
