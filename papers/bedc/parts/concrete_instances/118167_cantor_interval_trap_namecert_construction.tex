\begin{definition}[Cantor interval trap carrier]
\label{def:cantor-interval-trap-carrier}
A $\CantorIntervalTrapUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(I,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$

\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the nested interval row, listing a finite prefix of closed dyadic intervals together with the inclusion checks read from the $\DyadicNestedIntervalSelectorUp$ and $\CantorIntersectionTheoremUp$ siblings.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ radius ledger, recording endpoint comparison, midpoint, and radius-shrink data for each displayed interval row.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row that records which finite interval prefixes are inspected for each requested tolerance.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the displayed interval centers and dyadic radius bounds.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row, exposed only after the nested interval prefix, dyadic radius ledger, stream window, and regular rational readback have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for nested interval rows, dyadic radius rows, window rows, readback rows, Real seal rows, provenance rows, and the local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from interval-prefix requests to dyadic radius checks, from those checks to stream windows, and from stream windows through the regular readback to the Real seal.

\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CantorIntervalTrapUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted traps only through these displayed rows. It has no coordinate for a selected host limit, quotient equality of streams, trichotomy, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cantor interval trap NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cantor-interval-trap-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CantorIntervalTrapUp$ carrier $T=(I,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CantorIntervalTrapUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the nested interval prefix, dyadic radius ledger, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, terminal Real seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every completion-facing consumer must read $I,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$ in that finite route before it can consume the trapped Real boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cantor-interval-trap-carrier}. The nested interval data are exactly $I$, and every radius or endpoint comparison available to the packet is stored in the dyadic ledger $D$.

Next read the finite observation route. The window row $W$ determines the inspected interval prefix for a requested tolerance, while $R$ records the regular rational readback obtained from the displayed centers and radius bounds.

Finally expose the Real seal $E$ through the structural rows. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are exactly $H,C,P,N$, so the certificate cannot export a selected host limit, quotient stream equality, trichotomy, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cantor interval trap Real handoff]
\label{thm:cantor-interval-trap-real-handoff}

$$
\DyadicNestedIntervalSelectorUp \longmapsto \CantorIntersectionTheoremUp \longmapsto \DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
