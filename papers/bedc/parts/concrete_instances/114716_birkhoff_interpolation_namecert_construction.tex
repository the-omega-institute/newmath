Following Birkhoff and de Boor, the $\BirkhoffInterpolationUp$ packet records the finite interpolation route in which only a displayed lacunary subset of derivative conditions is imposed. It sits beside the ordinary node-value route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lagrange-interpolation-namecert} and the confluent jet route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hermite-interpolation-namecert}; the present carrier names the mask that selects derivative orders rather than requiring every derivative level below a jet height. Its construction consumes the coefficient-list surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-polynomial-namecert}, the displayed derivative surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-derivative-namecert}, the finite matrix ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, and the linear-system route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-linearmap-namecert}.

The packet is a finite $\BHist$ ledger. It displays nodes, a derivative-order mask, the lacunary value table, a polynomial basis, a finite matrix and linear-system ledger, assembly, transport, replay, provenance, and a local $\NameCert$ row before any consumer can read a Birkhoff interpolant. It is not ordinary Lagrange interpolation, because the mask may request derivative rows; it is not confluent Hermite interpolation, because the mask need not contain every lower derivative order at each node.

\begin{definition}[Birkhoff interpolation carrier]
\label{def:birkhoff-interpolation-carrier}
A $\BirkhoffInterpolationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(X,M,V,P,A,L,S,H,C,R,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is a finite node row, with pairwise-distinctness data for the displayed nodes.
\item $M$ is the derivative-order mask row, recording a finite set of pairs $(x_i,k)$ at which a derivative condition is requested.
\item $V$ is the lacunary value table, assigning one scalar value to each selected pair in $M$.
\item $P$ is the polynomial-basis row, displaying the finite $\PolynomialUp$ coefficient lists used for the unknown interpolant.
\item $A$ is the finite matrix ledger whose entries are obtained by applying the requested $\DerivativeUp$ orders from $M$ to the basis row $P$ and evaluating at the nodes $X$.
\item $L$ is the $\LinearMapUp$ linear-system row, recording the matrix map, right-hand side, solvability witness, and uniqueness witness when the Birkhoff pattern is poised.
\item $S$ is the assembly row, transporting the solved coefficient vector back to a $\PolynomialUp$ coefficient list and recording the requested derivative matches against $V$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for node, mask, value, basis, matrix, linear-system, assembly, replay, provenance, and local-name rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for derivative evaluation, matrix assembly, linear-system solution, polynomial assembly, and lacunary derivative checking.
\item $R$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BirkhoffInterpolationUp$, $\LagrangeInterpolationUp$, $\HermiteInterpolationUp$, $\PolynomialUp$, $\DerivativeUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\LinearMapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffInterpolationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate is $\mathsf{BirkhoffInterpolationLedger}(X,M,V,P,A,L,S,H,C,R,N)$. Two accepted packets classify together exactly when the eleven displayed rows are transported by $H$ and replay through the same routes named by $C$ and $R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff interpolation NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:birkhoff-interpolation-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\BirkhoffInterpolationUp$ packet $B=(X,M,V,P,A,L,S,H,C,R,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffInterpolationUp}$ obligation surface consists exactly of the finite carrier and classifier rows of \autoref{def:birkhoff-interpolation-carrier}, the node row $X$, derivative-order mask $M$, lacunary value table $V$, polynomial-basis row $P$, matrix ledger $A$, linear-system row $L$, assembly and derivative-match row $S$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $R$, and the local naming row $N$. No host differentiability oracle, unstated determinant inverse, quotient polynomial equality, complete Hermite jet table, ordinary Lagrange-only table, or ambient completeness datum is exported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier of \autoref{def:birkhoff-interpolation-carrier}. The rows $X,M,V$ supply the finite interpolation request: nodes, the selected derivative orders, and one displayed value for each selected request.

The construction rows are $P,A,L,S$. The polynomial-basis row gives coefficient-list unknowns, the matrix row evaluates the selected derivative orders on those basis elements, the linear-system row records solvability and uniqueness for the displayed finite matrix, and the assembly row returns the solved coefficient vector as a polynomial satisfying the lacunary table.

The structural rows $H,C,R,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only this displayed packet. Since the carrier has no further coordinate for a host derivative oracle, determinant inverse outside the matrix ledger, quotient polynomial equality, complete Hermite table, or Lagrange-only interpolation table, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff interpolation lacunary derivative route]
\label{thm:birkhoff-interpolation-lacunary-derivative-route}
Every derivative-match read exported by an accepted $\BirkhoffInterpolationUp$ packet factors through the finite route
$$
X,M,V,P \longmapsto A \longmapsto L \longmapsto S \longmapsto N .
$$
The route first displays the node row and derivative-order mask, then evaluates the requested derivative orders against the polynomial basis to form a finite $\MatrixUp$ ledger, solves the displayed $\LinearMapUp$ system, assembles the polynomial, checks the selected derivative rows against the lacunary value table, and exposes the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffInterpolationUp}$ row. A consumer cannot replace this route with ordinary Lagrange interpolation, a full Hermite jet table, an ambient smooth-function argument, or an unstated matrix inverse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted packet $B=(X,M,V,P,A,L,S,H,C,R,N)$. The mask row $M$ determines exactly which derivative requests are visible, and $V$ supplies values only for those selected requests.

Next inspect $P$ and $A$. The basis row is a finite $\PolynomialUp$ coefficient-list row, and $A$ is obtained by applying the displayed $\DerivativeUp$ orders from $M$ to that basis and evaluating at the nodes in $X$.

Use the linear-system row $L$ and assembly row $S$. The solved finite system supplies the coefficient vector, while $S$ repacks it as a polynomial and checks precisely the derivative rows selected by $M$.

Finally transport and replay through $H,C,R,N$. These rows preserve the same node, mask, value, basis, matrix, linear-system, assembly, provenance, and local name, so every exported lacunary derivative read follows the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}
