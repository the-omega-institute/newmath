\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC candidate-normalization scope]
\label{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-candidate-normalization-scope}
Every candidate-normalization consumer of an accepted $\MetaCICParallelDiamondFrontierUp$ packet must expose the full frontier spine
$$
P,K,J,R,S,C,B,O,H,T,G,N
$$
before it reads the candidate row. The consumer may use $J$ only as the displayed candidate-join row of a candidate-mediated residual route, and only while the residual-substitution premise row $P$ and obstruction row $O$ remain visible. Thus the candidate-normalization read is not a premise-free confluence theorem, not a completed residual-substitution theorem, not full normalization, not subject reduction, not evaluator equality, not quotient equality of terms, and not a kernel-consistency certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the candidate-mediated residual route of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-candidate-mediated-residual-route}. It permits a local peak to reach the candidate row $J$ only through the displayed critical-pair row $K$ and residual-frontier row $R$, with the retained premise row $P$ still attached.

Next apply substitution nonescape, \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-substitution-nonescape}. That theorem keeps substitution data confined to $P$ and $R$ through the typed-window local join, so the candidate-normalization consumer cannot turn $J$ into a discharged substitution result.

Use residual pasting, \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-residual-pasting}. Even after two typed-window residual reads are pasted through $J$, the pasted read remains the finite residual packet under $P$ and still carries $S,C,B,O,H,T,G,N$.

Finally apply the normalization-frontier handoff of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-normalization-frontier-handoff}. The handoff exports only the conditional spine $P,K,J,R,S,C,B,O,H,T,G,N$ to normalization consumers, so $P$ and $O$ cannot be hidden when $J$ is consumed.
\end{proof}
