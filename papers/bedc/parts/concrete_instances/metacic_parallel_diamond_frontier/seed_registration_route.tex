\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC parallel-diamond frontier seed registration]
\label{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-seed-registration}
Every seed read of an accepted $\MetaCICParallelDiamondFrontierUp$ packet that presents a conditional parallel-diamond handoff must first expose the residual-substitution premise row $P$, critical-pair row $K$, candidate join $J$, residual frontier $R$, closed checker $S$, closed-normal fragment $C$, bounded check $B$, obstruction $O$, and structural rows $H,T,G,N$. The handoff is public only at this finite row surface; it does not claim a premise-free diamond, full Church--Rosser theorem, subject reduction, evaluator equality, quotient equality of terms, or kernel consistency.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-obligations}. It makes $P,K,J,R,S,C,B,O,H,T,G,N$ the visible rows of the accepted frontier packet, with $P$ retained as an actual residual-substitution premise.

Next use the route theorem \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-route}. A conditional diamond handoff cannot be consumed until the critical-pair, residual-frontier, closed-checker, closed-normal, bounded-check, and obstruction rows have been read alongside the structural rows.

Finally apply premise retention, \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-premise-retention}. The premise row remains visible through the local-diamond route, so the seed registration records the finite frontier interface rather than any of the refused confluence or kernel endpoints.
\end{proof}
