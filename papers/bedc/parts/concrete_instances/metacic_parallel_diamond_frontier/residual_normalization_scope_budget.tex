\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC parallel-diamond residual-normalization scope]
\label{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-residual-normalization-scope}
Any residual-join read from an accepted $\MetaCICParallelDiamondFrontierUp$ packet must consume the critical-pair envelope, candidate-normalization row, residual-substitution row, finite residual witness, and candidate-SN handoff before it exports a diamond-frontier budget. The exported budget is the finite route through
$$
P,K,J,R,S,C,B,O,H,T,G,N ,
$$
with the residual-substitution premise and obstruction rows still visible; it is not a premise-free parallel diamond, full normalization theorem, subject-reduction theorem, evaluator equality, quotient equality of terms, or kernel-consistency certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First read the critical-pair envelope of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-critical-pair-envelope}. It fixes the local pair inside the displayed rows $P,K,J,R,S,C,B,O,H,T,G,N$ and keeps the bounded overlap surface visible.

Next consume the candidate-normalization scope of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-candidate-normalization-scope} together with the residual-substitution dependency route of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-residual-substitution-dependency-route}. These rows place $J$ under the retained premise $P$ rather than turning candidate normalization into an unconditional confluence result.

Now apply finite residual witness consumption, \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-finite-residual-witness-consumption}. The finite witness must read the typed window, residual frontier, substitution-boundary row, checker rows, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming before a closed parallel-diamond route can inspect it.

Finally use the candidate-SN handoff of \autoref{thm:metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-candidate-sn-handoff}. The handoff packages the same conditional spine as a diamond-frontier budget, so the exported object still retains $P$ and $O$ and cannot be reread as full normalization, subject reduction, evaluator equality, quotient equality, or kernel consistency.
\end{proof}
