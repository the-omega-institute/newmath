\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CountableMetricNameUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-countable-metric-name-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis and represented-space computable analysis, the $\CountableMetricNameUp$ packet names the finite interface by which a countably represented source is read as metric-name data without leaving the BEDC carrier discipline. It sits between the finite observation schedules of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the metric carrier surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular rational readback route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the completion-facing countable metric route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-countable-metric-completion-namecert}. The packet records a named handoff from certified countable observations to metric-name data; it is not a quotient stream equality, a selected limit, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Countable metric name carrier]
\label{def:countable-metric-name-carrier}
A $\CountableMetricNameUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(S,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the represented source row, naming the countable source, its displayed enumeration window, and the admitted source tokens read by the metric-name request.
\item $M$ is the metric row, exposing the source-side distance classifier, finite ball ledger, and equality boundary only through the displayed metric packet.
\item $W$ is the family of finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows used to inspect source tokens, distance requests, and metric-name prefixes.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row for requested radii, comparison budgets, slack witnesses, and refusal rows.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the displayed stream windows and dyadic tolerance data.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the finite distance comparisons that survive regular rational readback.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the represented source, metric row, stream windows, dyadic tolerance, regular readback, Real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from any metric-name request through $S,M,W,D,R,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CountableMetricNameUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CountableMetricNameUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient equality of streams, a selected completion limit, countable choice, host real equality, a hidden distance oracle, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CountableMetricName obligation surface]
\label{thm:countable-metric-name-namecert-obligation-surface}
For an accepted $\CountableMetricNameUp$ packet $A=(S,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CountableMetricNameUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, represented-source exposure, metric-row exposure, finite stream-window scheduling, dyadic tolerance checking, regular rational readback, Real sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and ledger non-escape.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is \autoref{def:countable-metric-name-carrier}: every row read by a consumer is one of $S,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N$.

The source and metric obligations are finite because the represented source is inspected only through $S$ and the metric classifier only through $M$. Stream-name data is reduced to the displayed windows $W$, tolerance data is reduced to $D$, and every numerical readback must pass through $R$ before the Real seal $E$ is consumed.

Transport, replay, and provenance are the remaining exposed routes. The rows $H$, $C$, and $P$ force componentwise identity transport, continuation replay, and package ancestry to remain inside the same displayed packet, while $N$ names exactly this finite surface.

There is no additional obligation for quotient stream equality, a selected limit, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, because none of those objects is a coordinate of the carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CountableMetricName finite observation scope]
\label{thm:countable-metric-name-finite-observation-scope}
Every legal consumer read of a $\CountableMetricNameUp$ carrier factors through a finite observation route
$$
S\longmapsto W\longmapsto D\longmapsto R\longmapsto E
$$
over the displayed metric row $M$, followed only by the transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect a consumer read of the carrier. A request first chooses a displayed source token from $S$ and a finite metric-name prefix from the stream windows $W$; no unbounded source enumeration is exported.

The requested tolerance is then checked by the dyadic row $D$. Any distance or comparison data that survives the tolerance check is read back through the regular rational row $R$ and sealed by $E$ as a finite $\RealUp$ observation.

Finally, the read is transported and replayed only by $H$ and $C$, with provenance recorded by $P$ and the local NameCert row $N$. Since the route never leaves these displayed rows, it cannot recover quotient stream equality, a hidden representative, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CountableMetricName completion handoff]
\label{thm:countable-metric-name-completion-handoff}
A $\CountableMetricNameUp$ carrier exports to completion-facing consumers only the represented source row, metric row, finite stream windows, dyadic tolerance row, regular rational readback, Real seal, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming data. Any later $\CountableMetricCompletionUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer must rebuild completion or real-completeness claims from those displayed rows rather than reading a selected limit, quotient equality, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient completeness from the handoff.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The handoff payload is the carrier tuple $S,M,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N$. A completion-facing consumer may reuse the represented source row $S$, the metric row $M$, the finite windows $W$, the tolerance ledger $D$, the regular readback $R$, and the Real seal $E$.

The same consumer may also transport and replay the packet through $H$ and $C$, and may cite $P$ and $N$ for provenance and local naming. These are finite BEDC rows, so they can be passed to the countable metric completion route without changing the classifier boundary.

Nothing in the packet selects a limit or identifies two streams by quotient equality. Consequently, any completion theorem, separated comparison, or Real completeness claim must be supplied by a later completion certificate; it is not exported by $\CountableMetricNameUp$ itself.
\end{proof}
