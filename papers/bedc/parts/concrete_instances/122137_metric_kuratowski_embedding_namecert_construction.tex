\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp$}

Following Kuratowski and Kelley, the $\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface by which a displayed metric source is embedded into its distance-profile interface before any completion-facing consumer reads a limit object. It sits after the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the separated complete-metric boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, the Cauchy-net handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchynet-namecert}, the finite StreamName windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a distance-profile embedding route; it does not quotient completion names, choose limits, identify host equality, or import ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Metric Kuratowski embedding carrier]
\label{def:metric-kuratowski-embedding-carrier}
A $\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(M,D,W,Q,E,C,F,H,R,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row, including the named point carrier and displayed metric comparison data.
\item $D$ is the distance-profile row, recording finite reads of $x \mapsto d(x,a)$ against named anchor rows $a$ from the source.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row through which each distance profile is observed.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row induced by the displayed windows and dyadic tolerance data already carried by the regular-rational corridor.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for the finite distance-profile values.
\item $C$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ comparison row available when the profile is consumed by a complete-metric target.
\item $F$ is the $\CauchyNetUp$ or $\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff row for completion-facing reads that enter through finite Cauchy data rather than through a selected limit.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source points, anchors, distance-profile rows, windows, readbacks, Real seals, complete-metric comparisons, Cauchy handoffs, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a profile request through $M,D,W,Q,E,C,F,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\CauchyNetUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,D,W,Q,E,C,F,H,R,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient completion, selected representatives, host equality of functions, ambient complete spaces, or a global Real-completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Metric Kuratowski embedding NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:metric-kuratowski-embedding-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp$ carrier
$$
K=(M,D,W,Q,E,C,F,H,R,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the $\MetricUp$ source, distance-profile exposure, finite StreamName observation, RegSeqRat readback, Real sealing, CompleteMetric comparison, CauchyNet or CauchyFilter handoff, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every completion-facing read must expose the route
$$
\MetricUp \to \mathsf{DistanceProfile} \to \StreamNameUp \to \RegSeqRatUp \to \RealUp
$$
and then choose either the $\CompleteMetricUp$ comparison row or the $\CauchyNetUp/\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff row already displayed by the carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-kuratowski-embedding-carrier}. The source and profile rows are $M$ and $D$, the observable analytic route is $W,Q,E$, and the completion-facing handoff rows are $C$ and $F$.

Read the metric side first. The $\MetricUp$ source supplies named points and metric comparisons, while $D$ turns those comparisons into distance-profile rows against the displayed anchors.

The profile then passes through finite windows, regular rational readback, and the Real seal. The rows $W,Q,E$ are the only value-facing route, so a consumer cannot bypass them to obtain a host function or ambient real value.

Finally $C,F,H,R,P,N$ preserve the same finite packet for complete-metric and Cauchy consumers. Since no row stores quotient representatives, selected limits, host function equality, or ambient completeness, the NameCert obligations are exhausted by the displayed carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Metric Kuratowski completion route]
\label{thm:metric-kuratowski-embedding-completion-route}
Every $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\CauchyNetUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\MetricKuratowskiEmbeddingUp$ packet must read the finite profile route through $\MetricUp$ source rows, distance-profile rows, $\StreamNameUp$ finite windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seals, $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ naming. The consumer receives a displayed embedding interface for completion arguments, not quotient completion, selected limits, host equality of profiles, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the accepted carrier $K=(M,D,W,Q,E,C,F,H,R,P,N)$. Carrier projection gives the consumer access only to those rows.

The metric source row $M$ and distance-profile row $D$ fix the embedding interface. A profile value is observable only after the StreamName window $W$, RegSeqRat readback $Q$, and Real seal $E$ have been displayed.

The completion-facing rows $C$ and $F$ then route the same profile either through a complete-metric comparison or through a CauchyNet/CauchyFilter handoff. These rows do not add a selected limit; they only expose the finite comparison path already named by the packet.

The structural rows $H,R,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same data. Therefore the handoff is a finite distance-profile interface and not a quotient completion or ambient completeness claim.
\end{proof}
