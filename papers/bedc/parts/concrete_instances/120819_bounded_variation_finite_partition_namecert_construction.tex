\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp$}

Following Jordan's finite-partition treatment of bounded variation and Rudin's real-analysis presentation of variation sums, the $\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp$ packet records only the displayed partition ledger read by a bounded-variation consumer. It sits between the variation packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedvariation-namecert}, the real-function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bounded-variation-real-function-namecert}, the finite dyadic partition carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-partition-namecert}, the finite mesh and adjacency sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-partition-mesh-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet keeps the classical bounded-variation read at finite means: it names one interval, one finite partition, endpoint readbacks, edge-difference budgets, and local provenance, not a supremum over all partitions, a variation measure, or host equality of functions.

\begin{definition}[BoundedVariationFinitePartition carrier]
\label{def:bounded-variation-finite-partition-carrier}
A $\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(I,\Pi,E,R,D,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the interval row on which the inspected partition is read.
\item $\Pi$ is the finite $\DyadicPartitionUp$ row, listing the ordered dyadic subintervals and their endpoint adjacency ledger.
\item $E$ records the $\RealUp$ endpoint rows attached to the displayed partition points.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the endpoint names used by the partition inspection.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ edge-difference budget, recording the adjacent endpoint comparisons and the finite partial-sum ledger for the inspected partition.
\item $V$ is the $\BoundedVariationUp$ ledger row consumed by a bounded-variation classifier after $I,\Pi,E,R,D$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the interval, partition, endpoint, readback, budget, bounded-variation, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a bounded-variation finite-partition request through $I,\Pi,E,R,D,V$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp$, $\BoundedVariationUp$, $\DyadicPartitionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through the displayed interval, finite partition, endpoint readback, edge-difference budget, bounded-variation ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows. It has no coordinate for total variation as a host supremum, a variation measure, quotient equality of functions, hidden partitions, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BoundedVariationFinitePartition NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bounded-variation-finite-partition-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(I,\Pi,E,R,D,V,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp}$ surface consists exactly of interval admission, finite dyadic partition admission, endpoint admission through $\RealUp$, regular-rational endpoint readback through $\RegSeqRatUp$, dyadic edge-difference budgeting through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, bounded-variation ledger handoff through $\BoundedVariationUp$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bounded-variation-finite-partition-carrier}. The interval row $I$ fixes the domain of the inspected read, and the finite $\DyadicPartitionUp$ row $\Pi$ fixes the ordered adjacent cells whose endpoints may be queried.

Read the endpoint rows next. The $\RealUp$ row $E$ gives the terminal endpoint seal, while the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$ supplies the regular-rational readback used to inspect those endpoint names at finite precision.

The dyadic budget row $D$ then records the adjacent endpoint differences and their finite partial sums through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$. Only after those rows are displayed can the bounded-variation ledger $V$ be consumed as a $\BoundedVariationUp$ row.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier contains no row for a host supremum over all partitions, a variation measure, quotient equality of functions, hidden partition families, or ambient real completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BoundedVariationFinitePartition ledger handoff]
\label{thm:bounded-variation-finite-partition-ledger-handoff}
Every bounded-variation consumer of an accepted $\BoundedVariationFinitePartitionUp$ carrier must read the ordered finite route
$$
\DyadicPartitionUp
\longrightarrow
\RealUp
\longrightarrow
\RegSeqRatUp
\longrightarrow
\DyadicRatCoreUp
\longrightarrow
\BoundedVariationUp
$$
through $I,\Pi,E,R,D,V,H,C,P,N$. The handoff supplies exactly one finite partition ledger to \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedvariation-namecert} and to the real-function surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bounded-variation-real-function-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a bounded-variation request. By \autoref{thm:bounded-variation-finite-partition-namecert-obligations}, the request first enters the displayed interval and finite partition rows, so every edge is one of the adjacent cells listed by $\Pi$.

The endpoint and budget rows then determine the inspected variation contribution. Endpoint values are read through $E$ and $R$, and each adjacent difference is budgeted by the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row $D$.

The $\BoundedVariationUp$ ledger $V$ is downstream of this route. It receives the displayed finite partition and its edge-difference budget, while $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route for transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Thus the consumer obtains one finite partition ledger rather than a global variation object. The displayed partition row consumes the finite mesh and adjacency sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-partition-mesh-namecert}; no row supplies a supremum over all partitions, a variation measure, host function equality, a hidden refinement family, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
