\begin{theorem}[Channel/genetic-code codon stop sibling route]
\label{thm:channel-genetic-code-bridge-codon-window-sibling-route}
Every stop-role read consumed by an accepted $\ChannelGeneticCodeBridgeUp$ packet factors first through the channel termination budget certificate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-channel-termination-budget-certificate-namecert} and the codon-window stop-face classifier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-codon-window-stop-face-classifier-namecert}. Only after those two sibling routes are displayed may the bridge compare channel termination with biological stop-role analogy. The route exports no token identity between channel events and codons, no direct biological stop-codon theorem, and no MetaCIC proof object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the bridge carrier of \autoref{def:channel-genetic-code-bridge-carrier}. The decoded channel row $D$ and stop-role ledger $L$ are the only rows from which a stop-role comparison can be read.

The channel side is read first through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-channel-termination-budget-certificate-namecert}. That sibling supplies the finite termination-budget certificate before the bridge may describe a channel event as termination-facing.

The codon side is then read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-codon-window-stop-face-classifier-namecert}. That sibling supplies the finite codon-window stop-face classifier before the biological stop-role analogy is consumed.

Finally the bridge replays the same route through its structural rows. Since the packet has no coordinate for channel-codon token identity, biological stop-codon proof, or MetaCIC theorem evidence, the sibling route remains a dependency boundary rather than a theorem transfer.
\end{proof}
