Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\BishopRegularIntervalUp$ packet records a regular interval by finite rational endpoint data and explicit tolerance control, not by a quotient of intervals or a choice-selected limiting endpoint. It sits on the L10 route through dyadic rational rows, stream-name windows, regular rational readback, the terminal real-name seal, and located interval containment. The packet is a constructive interval carrier for downstream completion arguments.

\begin{definition}[Bishop regular interval carrier]
\label{def:bishop-regular-interval-carrier}
A $\BishopRegularIntervalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(D,S,R,E,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the dyadic endpoint-and-tolerance row, read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, containing two rational endpoint windows and a visible tolerance budget.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row that records which endpoint and tolerance windows have been inspected.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the regular rational endpoint names and their common tolerance schedule.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for the endpoint readbacks supplied by $R$.
\item $L$ is the located interval containment row, read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-rational-interval-namecert}, recording which displayed rational subinterval contains the accepted endpoint windows.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $D,S,R,E,L$.
\item $C$ is the displayed $\Cont$ replay of the same finite interval evidence.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopRegularIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRegularIntervalUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for quotient interval equality, a selected real endpoint, trichotomy of endpoints, ambient real completeness, or countable choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop regular interval NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-regular-interval-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-regular-interval-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRegularIntervalUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $I=(D,S,R,E,L,H,C,P,N)$, dyadic endpoint-and-tolerance coverage by $D$, finite window coverage by $S$, regular rational readback by $R$, terminal real sealing by $E$, located interval containment by $L$, componentwise transport by $H$, displayed replay by $C$, package provenance by $P$, and local naming by $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the packet. The dyadic row $D$ supplies the only endpoint and tolerance data, and the stream row $S$ records which finite windows are visible to the consumer.

Read the rational endpoint names through $R$ and then through the terminal real seal $E$. This is the same L10-facing route used by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}.

Use the located interval row $L$ to bind the endpoint windows to the displayed containment surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-rational-interval-namecert}. The transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows $H,C,P,N$ carry those same rows and do not add a second interval object.

Since the carrier has no further coordinate, all NameCert obligations are exhausted by the displayed packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop regular interval L10 handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-regular-interval-l10-handoff}
Every L10 consumer of an accepted $\BishopRegularIntervalUp$ packet must read the interval through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint tolerances, $\StreamNameUp$ finite windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, located interval containment, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRegularIntervalUp}$ row. The handoff exports no quotient interval, choice-selected endpoint, endpoint trichotomy, host real equality, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-regular-interval-namecert-obligations}. They identify the visible route as $D,S,R,E,L,H,C,P,N$.

The numerical part of the handoff is forced through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. Thus a consumer cannot skip from a rational endpoint window to an ambient real interval.

The containment part is forced through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-rational-interval-namecert}. It records located containment for the displayed rational interval row and supplies no quotient equality between interval representatives.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, package, and name the same finite evidence. The excluded endpoint-selection and completeness readings have no carrier coordinate, so the L10 handoff is exactly the displayed constructive route.
\end{proof}
