\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MetricCompletionFiniteUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges, \emph{Constructive Analysis}, the $\MetricCompletionFiniteUp$ packet records the finite-window completion boundary that can be inspected before any $\RealUp$ seal is read. It sits downstream of the metric-completion surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, the generated Cauchy-filter basis interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-basis-namecert}, the dyadic precision window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicroundingwindow-namecert}, and the embedding route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completionembedding-namecert}. Its role is to bind those rows to a regular-rational or Real-facing readback without importing quotient completions, selected representatives, countable choice, open-cover compactness, host equality, or an ambient complete metric space.

\begin{definition}[MetricCompletionFinite carrier]
\label{def:metric-completion-finite-carrier}
A $\MetricCompletionFiniteUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(M,B,W,E,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricCompletionUp$ row naming the completion-facing metric source and its finite admissibility boundary.
\item $B$ is the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ row, or the finite CauchyNet branch when the source is presented by nets, from which the completion request is generated.
\item $W$ is the $\DyadicRoundingWindowUp$ precision row that fixes the finite dyadic scale inspected by the request.
\item $E$ is the $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$ row carrying the source object into the completion-facing interface.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ seal readback row exposed only after $M,B,W,E$ have been replayed.
\item $S$ is the local seal selector that records which finite window is consumed before the readback row is public.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric-completion, Cauchy-basis, dyadic-window, embedding, readback, seal-selector, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a completion request through the Cauchy basis, dyadic window, completion embedding, finite seal selector, and readback row.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\DyadicRoundingWindowUp$, $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricCompletionFiniteUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for quotient completion equality, a selected representative, countable-choice selection, open-cover compactness, host equality, or an ambient complete metric space.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFinite total-bounded window]
\label{thm:metric-completion-finite-total-bounded-window}
Every accepted $\MetricCompletionFiniteUp$ packet exposes its finite total-bounded branch through the displayed $\MetricCompletionUp$ row, the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ row or finite CauchyNet branch, the $\DyadicRoundingWindowUp$ precision row, the $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$ row, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ readback row, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row before any completion consumer may read a finite total-bounded window.

The exposed window is exactly the carrier route
$$
M,B,W,E,S \longmapsto R \longmapsto H,C,P,N ,
$$
and it exports no quotient completion equality, selected representative, countable-choice selector, open-cover compactness row, host equality, or ambient complete metric space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-finite-carrier}. Its rows $M,B,W,E,R,S,H,C,P,N$ are the only admitted coordinates, so the finite total-bounded branch must be read through that tuple.

Use the finite-window and readback rows already displayed in the carrier. The Cauchy source row $B$, dyadic window row $W$, embedding row $E$, and local selector $S$ precede the readback row $R$, and the transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows preserve that order.

Finally apply the ledger refusal clause of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-namecert-obligations}. Since none of the excluded completion principles has a carrier coordinate, no consumer can replace the finite window by quotient completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, open-cover compactness, host equality, or an ambient complete metric space.
\end{proof}

\item generated-Cauchy source exposure, requiring $B$ to be read before a completion-facing consumer inspects $W,E,R$, or $S$;

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFinite classifier exactness]
\label{thm:metric-completion-finite-classifier-exactness}
Let $F=(M,B,W,E,R,S,H,C,P,N)$ and $F'=(M',B',W',E',R',S',H',C',P',N')$ be accepted $\MetricCompletionFiniteUp$ packets. The local classifier identifies $F$ with $F'$ exactly when the metric-completion, Cauchy-source, dyadic-window, embedding, readback, seal-selector, transport, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows are componentwise equal along the displayed $\hsame$ route:
$$
M \hsame M',\quad B \hsame B',\quad W \hsame W',\quad E \hsame E',\quad R \hsame R',\quad S \hsame S',\quad H \hsame H',\quad C \hsame C',\quad P \hsame P',\quad N \hsame N' .
$$
There is no additional classifier coordinate for quotient completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, open-cover compactness, host equality, or ambient complete metric spaces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-finite-carrier}. Both accepted packets have exactly the rows $M,B,W,E,R,S,H,C,P,N$ and their primed counterparts, so the classifier can inspect only those coordinates.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-namecert-obligations}. They force carrier admission, source exposure, precision exposure, embedding handoff, and ledger refusal to be read through the same displayed rows.

Finally use the readback route of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-window-readback}. A classifier equality that survives the readback must therefore be componentwise $\hsame$ on those rows, and componentwise $\hsame$ supplies the same finite route in the reverse direction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFinite stability transport]
\label{thm:metric-completion-finite-stability-transport}
If accepted $\MetricCompletionFiniteUp$ packets $F=(M,B,W,E,R,S,H,C,P,N)$ and $F'=(M',B',W',E',R',S',H',C',P',N')$ are classifier-equal in the sense of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-classifier-exactness}, then carrier admission and readback eligibility transport from $F$ to $F'$ and from $F'$ to $F$. In particular, every consumer allowed to read $R$ through $M,B,W,E,S$ is allowed to read $R'$ through $M',B',W',E',S'$ after the componentwise $\hsame$ transport.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the componentwise classifier supplied by \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-classifier-exactness}. It gives transport for each carrier coordinate, including the Cauchy-source row, dyadic-window row, completion-embedding row, seal selector, and readback row.

Carrier admission is one of the obligations of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-namecert-obligations}, so it is preserved by the displayed transport row $H$ and the local naming row $N$.

Readback eligibility is the route of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-finite-window-readback}. Transport each row in the route $M,B,W,E,S \longmapsto R$ to the primed route and repeat the same argument in the opposite direction.
\end{proof}
