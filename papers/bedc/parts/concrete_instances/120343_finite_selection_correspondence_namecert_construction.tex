\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp$}

Following Berge and Aliprantis--Border on finite correspondences and measurable selection language, the $\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp$ packet records only finite displayed selection rows. It sits beside the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the compact metric ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the set-like membership surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-setlike-namecert}, the finite transport-plan route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-wasserstein-namecert}, and the distribution ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-distribution-namecert}. The packet is a finite correspondence interface; it does not export a global selector, countable choice, measurable-selection theorem, optimizer, or ambient completion.

\begin{definition}[Finite selection correspondence carrier]
\label{def:finite-selection-correspondence-carrier}
A $\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(M,K,S,W,R,D,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the displayed $\MetricUp$ source row;
\item $K$ is an optional $\CompactMetricUp$ finite-support or finite-net ledger read by compact consumers;
\item $S$ is the $\SetLikeUp$ graph row listing finite source points and finite admissible value rows;
\item $W$ is the displayed selection-window table, whose entries are finite pairs already present in $S$;
\item $R$ is the readback row consumed by finite $\WassersteinUp$ and $\DistributionUp$ interfaces;
\item $D$ is the refusal ledger recording that no global selector, countable-choice schedule, optimizer, or measurable-selection theorem is exported;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric, compact, graph, window, readback, refusal, provenance, and naming rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from graph rows to finite windows and from windows to downstream finite consumers;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\SetLikeUp$, $\WassersteinUp$, $\DistributionUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets componentwise by $\hsame$ on these rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite selection correspondence obligation surface]
\label{thm:finite-selection-correspondence-obligation-surface}
For every accepted $\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp$ carrier $F=(M,K,S,W,R,D,H,C,P,N)$, the local obligation surface consists exactly of the displayed metric source, compact-support ledger, set-like graph row, finite selection windows, downstream readback row, refusal ledger, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. Any finite transport, compact metric, or distribution consumer must read $S$ and $W$ before consuming $R$, and $D$ remains visible on every route, so no choice principle or selected global function is exported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-selection-correspondence-carrier}. The only admissible correspondence data are the finite graph row $S$ and the finite selection-window table $W$ over the displayed metric source $M$ and optional compact row $K$.

The downstream readback row $R$ is attached to those finite rows. A $\WassersteinUp$ or $\DistributionUp$ consumer may therefore receive a finite support or finite provenance route, but it must receive it through the listed $S,W,R$ coordinates rather than through a hidden selector.

Finally read the structural rows. The componentwise $\hsame$ row $H$, the displayed $\Cont$ replay row $C$, the $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and the local $\NameCert$ row $N$ preserve the same finite route, while $D$ records the refusal of global selection, countable choice, optimizer extraction, and measurable-selection principles. Hence the displayed rows exhaust the finite selection correspondence obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\falsifiablePrediction{An accepted $\FiniteSelectionCorrespondenceUp$ consumer that reads a selected value without first exposing the finite graph row $S$ and finite selection window $W$, or that exports a global selector after reading $D$, is outside the carrier surface.}
