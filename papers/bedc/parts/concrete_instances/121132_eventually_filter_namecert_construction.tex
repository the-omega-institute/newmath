\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\EventuallyFilterUp$}

Following Bishop--Bridges tail arguments for sequences and the Bourbaki--Kelley filter vocabulary, the $\EventuallyFilterUp$ packet records the finite evidence that a sequence claim is read only beyond a displayed threshold. It sits between the cofinite-tail surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-frechetfilter-namecert}, the regular Cauchy tail-filter route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-tail-filter-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a finite eventual-tail certificate, not a quotient filter, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host equality of streams, or ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[EventuallyFilter carrier]
\label{def:eventually-filter-carrier}
An $\EventuallyFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
V=(F,T,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is the cofinite threshold row, naming the displayed index after which the event is inspected.
\item $T$ is the finite complement check, listing the bounded prefix ignored by the eventual read.
\item $B$ is the $\FilterBaseUp$ row whose accepted base elements are exactly the threshold tails generated by $F$ and checked by $T$.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row read only on the displayed tail.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the tail windows and their tolerance ledger.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal reached only after $F,T,B,S,R$ have been exposed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the threshold, finite complement, filter-basis, stream-window, readback, Real seal, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from threshold admission through finite complement checking, filter-basis exposure, tail-window readback, regular rational readback, and the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\EventuallyFilterUp$, $\FrechetFilterUp$, $\RegularCauchyTailFilterUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\EventuallyFilterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $F,T,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient filters, selected limits, countable choice, host equality of streams, hidden infinite complements, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[EventuallyFilter NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:eventually-filter-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\EventuallyFilterUp$ carrier $V=(F,T,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\EventuallyFilterUp}$ surface consists exactly of cofinite threshold admission, finite complement checking, filter-basis exposure, tail $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. No obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The rows $F,T,B,S,R,E$ are the only mathematical data coordinates available to an eventual-tail consumer.

The threshold row $F$ determines the tail, and $T$ records the bounded prefix that is not read by the eventual claim. The filter-basis row $B$ then exposes exactly the cofinite threshold tails generated by those two rows.

The stream and regular-rational rows are downstream of the same basis. The window row $S$ is read only on the displayed tail, and $R$ records the corresponding regular rational readback before the seal $E$ is exposed.

Finally the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name this route. Since the carrier contains no quotient filter, selected-limit row, countable-choice schedule, host stream equality, hidden infinite-complement row, or ambient completeness principle, none can become a local obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[EventuallyFilter tail refinement]
\label{thm:eventually-filter-tail-refinement}
Let $V=(F,T,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\EventuallyFilterUp$ carrier. If a displayed threshold tail is refined by deleting a further finite prefix, every accepted read remains in the same carrier route through the refined $F,T,B$ rows and the downstream $S,R,E,H,C,P,N$ rows. Thus finite prefix deletion changes only the inspected tail window and not the eventual-filter basis seen by completion-facing consumers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the refinement of the threshold row. Deleting a further finite prefix changes the displayed threshold and enlarges the finite complement recorded by $T$.

The filter-basis row $B$ is built from threshold tails, so the refined tail is a displayed base element of the same eventual-filter route. No quotient-filter comparison is needed; the refinement is recorded by the finite prefix ledger.

Read the downstream rows through the refined tail. The $\StreamNameUp$ window $S$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $R$ are replayed by $C$ after the refined $F,T,B$ rows are exposed, while $H$ transports the same componentwise route.

The terminal seal $E$ therefore remains attached to the same eventual-tail certificate. Only the finite window and prefix ledger have changed, and no selected limit, countable choice, host stream equality, or ambient completeness row is introduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[EventuallyFilter Real-facing route]
\label{thm:eventually-filter-real-facing-route}
Every $\RealUp$-facing consumer of an accepted $\EventuallyFilterUp$ packet reaches the seal row only through
$$
F,T,B \longrightarrow S \longrightarrow R \longrightarrow E .
$$
The route factors the cofinite threshold, finite complement check, filter-basis row, StreamName windows, and RegSeqRat readback before any Real-facing completion or Cauchy-tail consumer can inspect the terminal seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a Real-facing read of the accepted packet. The carrier places the seal row $E$ downstream of the stream-window and regular-rational readback rows.

Apply the NameCert obligations. They require the threshold $F$, finite complement check $T$, and filter-basis row $B$ to be displayed before $S$ can be read as a tail window.

The readback $R$ is then obtained from that same tail window, and the continuation row replays the order from the threshold data to the Real seal. The route is exactly the displayed finite chain through $F,T,B,S,R,E$.

The sibling $\FrechetFilterUp$ and $\RegularCauchyTailFilterUp$ chapters may consume this cofinite-tail route, but they receive no quotient filter, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host stream equality, or ambient completeness coordinate from the packet.
\end{proof}
