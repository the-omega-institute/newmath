\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteMetricTreeUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-metric-tree-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Buneman and Dress, finite tree metrics are read from a finite weighted tree by the path-distance ledger on its vertices. The $\FiniteMetricTreeUp$ packet records that classical object as constructive L10 finite-metric witness data: it sits over the metric surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the graph and tree routes of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-graph-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-tree-namecert}, and the finite-net consumer surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-epsilon-net-namecert}. The packet is a finite path-distance certificate, not a quotient metric space, not a selected geodesic in an ambient graph, and not a host compactness theorem.

\begin{definition}[FiniteMetricTree carrier]
\label{def:finite-metric-tree-carrier}
A $\FiniteMetricTreeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(V,E,W,P,M,G,R,H,C,K,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V$ is the finite vertex ledger, listing the accepted vertex names and the finite equality classifier used by later distance reads.
\item $E$ is the finite edge ledger, listing accepted undirected edge incidences between vertices of $V$.
\item $W$ is the edge-length row, assigning a positive $\RealUp$-sealed or rationally witnessed length to every displayed edge.
\item $P$ is the path-distance readback row. For each accepted pair of vertices it records the unique finite tree path through $E$ and the finite sum of edge lengths from $W$.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ row whose distance witness is exactly the path-distance readback $P$ and whose metric laws are checked against the finite path ledger.
\item $G$ is the sibling graph-and-tree route, exposing the $\GraphUp$ adjacency row and the $\TreeUp$ connected-acyclic row before any path-distance readback is consumed.
\item $R$ is the finite four-point comparison ledger. For each displayed quadruple it records the two largest of the three pair sums and the equality comparison required by the tree-metric condition.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for vertices, edges, edge lengths, path distances, metric rows, graph/tree rows, four-point ledgers, continuation rows, provenance, and the local naming row.
\item $C$ stores $\Cont$ replay from a vertex-pair or quadruple request through the finite graph/tree route, path-distance readback, metric row, and four-point ledger.
\item $K$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteMetricTreeUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\GraphUp$, $\TreeUp$, finite-net consumers, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteMetricTreeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $V,E,W,P,M,G,R,H,C,K,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient metric spaces, choice-selected paths outside the finite tree, open-cover compactness, infinite graph completion, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteMetricTree NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-metric-tree-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteMetricTreeUp$ carrier $T=(V,E,W,P,M,G,R,H,C,K,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteMetricTreeUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, finite vertex-ledger admission, edge-ledger admission, positive edge-length readback, graph/tree route exposure, path-distance exactness, metric-law stability, four-point comparison stability, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, local naming, and ledger nonescape.

In particular, any $\MetricUp$, $\GraphUp$, $\TreeUp$, or finite-net consumer must read the displayed finite ledger route
$$
V\longmapsto E\longmapsto W\longmapsto P\longmapsto M\longmapsto G\longmapsto R\longmapsto H,C,K,N
$$
before using the packet as finite tree-metric data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-metric-tree-carrier}. The projection exposes exactly the finite vertex ledger, edge ledger, edge-length row, path-distance readback, metric row, graph/tree route, four-point ledger, and structural rows.

The graph/tree route is read before distance data. The $\GraphUp$ row names the finite adjacency surface, while the $\TreeUp$ row supplies connectedness and acyclicity as displayed finite data; therefore the path row $P$ can read a unique finite route without selecting a path from an ambient graph.

Metric-law stability follows from the same path-distance ledger. Symmetry reverses the finite path, reflexivity reads the empty path, and the triangle comparison concatenates displayed finite paths before returning to the tree path through the metric row $M$.

The four-point comparison row $R$ is downstream of $P$ and $M$. It records quadruple comparisons only from displayed path distances, so componentwise transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve the comparison surface.

Finally, $K$ and $N$ source and name the finite packet. Since no carrier row stores quotient metric spaces, choice-selected ambient paths, open-cover compactness, infinite graph completion, or ambient real completeness, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteMetricTree four-point stability]
\label{thm:finite-metric-tree-four-point-stability}
Let $T=(V,E,W,P,M,G,R,H,C,K,N)$ and $T'=(V',E',W',P',M',G',R',H',C',K',N')$ be accepted $\FiniteMetricTreeUp$ packets. If the vertex ledger, edge ledger, edge-length row, path-distance readback, metric row, graph/tree route, four-point ledger, replay row, provenance row, and local naming row of $T$ are transported componentwise through the displayed $\hsame$ and $\Cont$ routes to the corresponding rows of $T'$, then every finite four-point tree-metric comparison accepted by $R$ is accepted by $R'$ with the same displayed pair-sum equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:finite-metric-tree-namecert-obligations}. The four-point comparison row is not independent data; it is downstream of the finite vertex, edge, edge-length, path-distance, metric, and graph/tree rows.

Transport the carrier componentwise. The displayed $\hsame$ rows carry vertices to vertices, edges to edges, edge lengths to edge lengths, and path-distance readbacks to path-distance readbacks, while the $\Cont$ rows replay the same quadruple request through the transported packet.

The four-point ledger therefore recomputes its three pair sums from transported path distances. Since the accepted comparison in $R$ was read only from those sums, $R'$ accepts the same tree-metric equality after transport.

No external geodesic selector, quotient metric equality, compactness principle, or ambient completion can alter the comparison, because none is a coordinate of either accepted packet.
\end{proof}
