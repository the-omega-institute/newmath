\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bourbaki-completion-filter-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Bishop constructive completion practice, the $\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp$ packet records the filter-based completion surface as finite BEDC data before any quotient-filter carrier or ambient completeness theorem is available. It reads the Cauchy-filter basis of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-basis-namecert}, the reflector discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-completion-reflector-namecert}, the constructive completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-metric-completion-namecert}, the finite stream window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a seed object for the L10 Real and Completion route; it does not construct quotient filters, select limits, use countable choice, assert selected representatives, or claim ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Bourbaki completion filter carrier]
\label{def:bourbaki-completion-filter-carrier}
A $\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(B,M,C,S,R,E,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ row naming the finite Cauchy-filter basis packets and their common-refinement reads.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricCompletionReflectorUp$ row that records the reflector-facing completion request for the same metric source.
\item $C$ is the $\ConstructiveMetricCompletionUp$ route through regular Cauchy names, dyadic tolerances, Cauchy-filter basis data, separated comparison, dense embedding, and uniformly continuous handoff.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row used to read the displayed basis through finite observation schedules.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the stream windows and tolerance ledger.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row, exposed only after $B,M,C,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the basis, reflector, constructive completion route, stream windows, regular readback, and Real seal.
\item $T$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay from the Cauchy-filter basis to the reflector request, from the reflector request to the constructive completion route, from stream windows to the regular readback, and from the regular readback to the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\MetricCompletionReflectorUp$, $\ConstructiveMetricCompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for quotient filters, selected limits, selected representatives, countable choice, host real equality, or an ambient completion object.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BourbakiCompletionFilter NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bourbaki-completion-filter-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp$ carrier $F=(B,M,C,S,R,E,H,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the Cauchy-filter basis, metric-completion reflector, constructive completion route, StreamName window, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local NameCert row. No obligation is stored in a quotient-filter object, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, selected representative, host real equality row, or ambient completion theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bourbaki-completion-filter-carrier}. The displayed rows $B,M,C,S,R,E,H,T,P,N$ are the whole finite packet, and the local NameCert row names only those coordinates.

The route rows bind the basis to the completion surface. The Cauchy-filter basis $B$ feeds the reflector request $M$ and the constructive completion route $C$; the finite StreamName row $S$ and RegSeqRat row $R$ then provide the readback through which the terminal Real seal $E$ is visible.

Transport, replay, and provenance add no hidden coordinate. The rows $H,T,P,N$ only move, replay, source, and name the same displayed packet, so quotient filters, selected limits, countable choice, selected representatives, host real equality, and ambient completion have no row from which a NameCert obligation could be exported.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BourbakiCompletionFilter Cauchy nonescape]
\label{thm:bourbaki-completion-filter-cauchy-nonescape}
Every completion-facing read of an accepted $\BourbakiCompletionFilterUp$ packet factors through
$$
\CauchyFilterBasisUp \longmapsto \MetricCompletionReflectorUp \longmapsto \ConstructiveMetricCompletionUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
The read stays inside the displayed rows $B,M,C,S,R,E,H,T,P,N$ and exports no quotient filters, countable choice, selected limits, selected representatives, detached $\RealUp$ completeness, or ambient completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the basis row $B$. It is the only Cauchy-filter source carried by the packet, and its common-refinement reads are the inputs to the reflector-facing row $M$.

The constructive completion route $C$ is downstream of that basis and reflector surface. The finite window row $S$ and regular rational readback $R$ make the completion read visible as a BEDC route before the terminal seal $E$ is exposed.

Finally, the structural rows $H,T,P,N$ preserve the same sequence of displayed coordinates. Since the carrier excludes quotient-filter objects, choice schedules, selected limits, selected representatives, detached Real completeness, and ambient completion objects, no completion-facing consumer can escape the finite route named in the statement.
\end{proof}
