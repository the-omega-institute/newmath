Following Milman's converse direction in compact convexity, the $\MilmanConverseUp$ packet records the finite boundary route by which a compact convex carrier reads an extreme-window source before exporting a closure-hull handoff. It sits after the compact carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}, the convex-set surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the locally convex observation rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert}, the Choquet boundary packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-choquet-simplex-namecert}, and the finite Krein--Milman extreme witness of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-krein-milman-finite-extreme-namecert}. The chapter names only the compact-convex boundary object needed for a Milman-converse read; it does not assert selected extreme points, strong Hahn--Banach separation, quotient convex-set equality, countable choice, or ambient locally convex completeness.

\begin{definition}[Milman converse carrier]
\label{def:milman-converse-carrier}
A $\MilmanConverseUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(K,V,L,S,E,W,G,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactUp$ source row naming the compact carrier inspected by the boundary request.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ row exposing affine-combination and convex-subcarrier data.
\item $L$ is the $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$ observation row for seminorm and neighbourhood windows used by compact-convex consumers.
\item $S$ is the $\ChoquetSimplexUp$ boundary row when the read is routed through a simplex-facing compact convex source.
\item $E$ is the $\KreinMilmanFiniteExtremeUp$ finite extreme-window row, opened only as displayed candidate-extreme and exposed-face evidence.
\item $W$ is the finite extreme-window ledger recording which boundary observations have been inspected.
\item $G$ is the closure-hull handoff row, available only after $K,V,L,S,E,W$ are visible.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for compact, convex, locally convex, Choquet, finite extreme-window, closure-hull, replay, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a compact-convex boundary request through the same rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MilmanConverseUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$, $\ChoquetSimplexUp$, $\KreinMilmanFiniteExtremeUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MilmanConverseUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,V,L,S,E,W,G,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a selected extreme point, a strong Hahn--Banach separator, quotient convex-set equality, countable-choice search, or ambient locally convex completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Milman converse NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:milman-converse-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MilmanConverseUp$ carrier $M=(K,V,L,S,E,W,G,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MilmanConverseUp}$ surface consists exactly of compact-source admission through $K$, convex-carrier admission through $V$, locally convex observation through $L$, Choquet-boundary admission through $S$, finite Krein--Milman extreme-window admission through $E$, boundary ledger visibility through $W$, closure-hull handoff visibility through $G$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:milman-converse-carrier}. The compact source, convex carrier, locally convex observations, Choquet boundary row, finite extreme-window row, boundary ledger, and closure-hull handoff are exactly the rows $K,V,L,S,E,W,G$.

Read the source rows first. The compact row $K$ and convex row $V$ supply the compact-convex object, while $L$ keeps the locally convex observations finite and visible.

Next attach the boundary rows. The Choquet row $S$ supplies the simplex-facing boundary packet when present, the finite Krein--Milman row $E$ supplies only candidate-extreme and exposed-face data, and $W$ records the inspected extreme-window ledger.

Finally the handoff row $G$ may be read, and $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since no remaining coordinate stores selected extreme points, a strong Hahn--Banach separator, quotient convex-set equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Milman converse compact-convex handoff]
\label{thm:milman-converse-compact-convex-handoff}
Every compact-convex boundary read exported by an accepted $\MilmanConverseUp$ packet factors through
$$
\CompactUp,\quad \ConvexSetUp,\quad \LocallyConvexSpaceUp,\quad \ChoquetSimplexUp,\quad \KreinMilmanFiniteExtremeUp .
$$
The closure-hull handoff row $G$ is available only after the compact carrier, convex carrier, locally convex observation row, Choquet boundary row, finite extreme-window row, and boundary ledger have all been displayed. The handoff exports no selected extreme point, strong Hahn--Banach separation, quotient convex-set equality, countable choice, or ambient locally convex completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with \autoref{thm:milman-converse-namecert-obligations}. It forces any accepted boundary consumer to expose $K,V,L,S,E,W,G,H,C,P,N$ before the local $\NameCert_{\MilmanConverseUp}$ row can be used.

Read the supplying chapters in order. \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert} supplies compact observations, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert} supplies convex carrier data, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert} supplies locally convex observation windows.

Then use the boundary sources. \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-choquet-simplex-namecert} supplies only the displayed Choquet boundary packet, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-krein-milman-finite-extreme-namecert} supplies only finite exposed-face and candidate-extreme evidence.

Therefore the closure-hull handoff is downstream from the displayed compact-convex boundary route. Since the carrier has no selected extreme point, strong Hahn--Banach separator, quotient convex-set equality, countable-choice selector, or ambient completeness row, the handoff cannot export any of those objects.
\end{proof}
