\begin{theorem}[Real diagonal non-surjection metric dependency route]
\label{thm:real-diagonal-non-surjection-metric-dependency-route}
Every $\MetricUp$ consumer that compares outputs of a $\RealDiagonalNonSurjectionUp$ packet must first read the diagonal witness through the $\RegSeqRatUp$ route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. Only after those rows are exposed may the consumer use the metric comparison surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}; the diagonal packet itself exports no direct metric distance row, quotient real equality, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier of \autoref{def:real-diagonal-non-surjection-carrier}. The rows $B$ and $F$ produce the displayed dyadic bit disagreement, but this data is still a finite diagonal observation rather than a metric comparison.

The readback row $R$ converts the displayed bit-flip data into the regular rational route named by \autoref{thm:real-diagonal-non-surjection-regseqrat-dependency-route}. The seal row $S$ is then the only $\RealUp$ endpoint visible to a downstream consumer.

A $\MetricUp$ consumer can compare diagonal outputs only after it has both the regular rational readback and the terminal real seal. Thus the dependency order is
$$
\RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp \longmapsto \MetricUp .
$$

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve this order componentwise. Since no carrier row names a host metric distance directly, the metric consumer cannot bypass the $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ sibling routes.
\end{proof}
