\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CuspNeighborhoodUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-cusp-neighborhood-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Beardon and Ratcliffe on hyperbolic manifolds, the $\CuspNeighborhoodUp$ packet records a finite horoball-based cusp-neighborhood certificate at a displayed parabolic boundary end. It sits beside the horoball surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-horoball-namecert}, the upper-half-plane model of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-poincare-half-plane-namecert}, the discrete action rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fuchsian-group-action-namecert}, the metric readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-metric-namecert}, and the terminal scalar seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only displayed cusp data; it is not a quotient hyperbolic surface, a selected fundamental domain, a completed ideal boundary object, or an ambient finite-volume theorem.

\begin{definition}[Cusp-neighborhood carrier]
\label{def:cusp-neighborhood-carrier}
A $\CuspNeighborhoodUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(B,H,S,M,R,E,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the parabolic boundary-point row, read in the same upper-half-plane boundary surface used by $\PoincareHalfPlaneUp$.
\item $H$ is the $\HyperbolicHoroballUp$ row, recording the horoball attached to $B$ together with its displayed height or Busemann-threshold data.
\item $S$ is the boundary-stabilizer row, recording the finite $\FuchsianGroupActionUp$ word data that preserves the same boundary point and horoball side.
\item $M$ is the $\HyperbolicMetricUp$ metric-threshold readback row used to compare points with the horoball boundary.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for the threshold and metric comparison values.
\item $E$ records the local cusp-neighborhood export row, namely the finite region accepted after the boundary point, horoball threshold, stabilizer word, metric readback, and Real seal have all been displayed.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $B,H,S,M,R,E$ and the local certificate row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the upper-half-plane boundary point through the horoball, stabilizer, metric threshold, Real seal, export, transport, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CuspNeighborhoodUp$, $\HyperbolicHoroballUp$, $\PoincareHalfPlaneUp$, $\FuchsianGroupActionUp$, $\HyperbolicMetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CuspNeighborhoodUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a quotient cusp surface, a selected fundamental domain, global discreteness of the ambient group, finite-volume completion, or a host ideal-boundary object.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cusp-neighborhood NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cusp-neighborhood-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CuspNeighborhoodUp$ carrier
$$
K=(B,H,S,M,R,E,T,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CuspNeighborhoodUp}$ surface consists exactly of boundary-point admission through $B$, horoball and cusp-threshold exposure through $H$, boundary-stabilizer exposure through $S$, hyperbolic metric-threshold readback through $M$, Real sealing through $R$, local cusp-neighborhood export through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $T$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The first five rows expose the parabolic boundary point, the horoball threshold, the stabilizer word data, the metric readback, and the terminal Real seal.

Read the local export row $E$ only after those rows have been displayed. The export is therefore a cusp-neighborhood row over a named boundary end and horoball threshold, not an ambient quotient surface.

Finally read $T,C,P,N$. These structural rows transport, replay, source, and name the same finite data. Since the carrier has no coordinate for quotienting by the full group, selecting a fundamental domain, completing the ideal boundary, or proving finite volume, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cusp-neighborhood horoball handoff]
\label{thm:cusp-neighborhood-horoball-handoff}
Every downstream consumer of an accepted $\CuspNeighborhoodUp$ packet receives the horoball route
$$
\PoincareHalfPlaneUp,\ \HyperbolicHoroballUp,\ \FuchsianGroupActionUp,\ \HyperbolicMetricUp,\ \RealUp
$$
only through the displayed boundary point $B$, horoball row $H$, boundary-stabilizer row $S$, metric-threshold row $M$, Real seal $R$, export row $E$, transport row $T$, replay row $C$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$. The handoff supplies a finite cusp-neighborhood certificate, not a quotient manifold cusp, a selected fundamental region, a global parabolic subgroup theorem, or an ambient finite-volume claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the boundary row $B$ and the horoball row $H$. They are read through the upper-half-plane and horoball sibling certificates before any cusp-neighborhood export is available.

Next read the stabilizer and metric rows. The row $S$ records only displayed action-word data preserving the same boundary side, and $M,R$ record the threshold comparison and its Real seal.

The handoff row $E$ is therefore downstream of $B,H,S,M,R$. The structural rows $T,C,P,N$ preserve the same finite route, so a consumer receives only the listed rows and none of the excluded quotient, selection, global-group, or finite-volume objects.
\end{proof}
