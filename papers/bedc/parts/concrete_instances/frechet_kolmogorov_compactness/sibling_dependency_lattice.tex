\begin{theorem}[Frechet--Kolmogorov compactness sibling route]
\label{thm:frechet-kolmogorov-compactness-sibling-route}
The $\FrechetKolmogorovCompactnessUp$ packet consumes the Arzela--Ascoli finite-modulus route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-namecert} as its equicontinuity-facing compactness control, and it consumes the local seminorm-to-completion route recorded in \autoref{thm:frechet-kolmogorov-locally-convex-boundedness-route} as its locally convex boundedness control. Together with the compact metric surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the total-bounded surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, these sibling reads keep the compact metric, total-bounded, uniform-equicontinuity, locally convex seminorm, Frechet metric, RegSeqRat, and Real-seal rows visible as one finite dependency lattice rather than as an ambient function-space compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the compact metric and total-bounded rows first. The compactness-facing consumer must pass through the displayed compact metric packet and its total-bounded control before any finite-cover or subsequence conclusion is available.

Next read the equicontinuity and local boundedness rows. The Arzela--Ascoli finite-modulus route supplies the uniform-equicontinuity control, while the locally convex boundedness route supplies the seminorm-to-completion control.

Finally read the completion, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ seal rows. Since each consumer opens these sibling rows before the compactness conclusion, the packet exports a finite dependency lattice rather than an ambient function-space compactness principle.
\end{proof}
