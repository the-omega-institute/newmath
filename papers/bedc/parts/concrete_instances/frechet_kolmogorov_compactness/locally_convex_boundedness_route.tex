\begin{theorem}[Frechet-Kolmogorov locally convex boundedness route]
\label{thm:frechet-kolmogorov-locally-convex-boundedness-route}
Every $\FrechetKolmogorovCompactnessUp$ consumer that reads compactness through locally convex bounded sets must factor through the finite route
$$
\LocallyConvexSpaceUp,\FrechetSpaceUp,\CompactMetricUp,\TotallyBoundedUp,\RealUp .
$$
Concretely, the consumer must expose the locally convex seminorm row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert}, the Frechet metric completion row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-frechet-space-namecert}, the compact metric row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the total bounded row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, and the terminal Real seal row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} before it may treat a bounded family as Frechet-Kolmogorov compactness data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the locally convex seminorm row. It supplies the bounded-set requests that a functional-analysis consumer is allowed to ask of the packet.

The Frechet metric completion row then turns those seminorm requests into a displayed metric-completion boundary. The compact metric row and total bounded row refine that boundary into finite compactness data, so the consumer sees nets and metric windows rather than an ambient bounded-set compactness theorem.

Finally the Real seal row records the scalar readback used by the seminorm and metric estimates. Since each step is an exposed row in the displayed route, no consumer can bypass the seminorm, completion, compact metric, total bounded, or Real-seal surfaces by appealing to host boundedness or selected compactness data.
\end{proof}
