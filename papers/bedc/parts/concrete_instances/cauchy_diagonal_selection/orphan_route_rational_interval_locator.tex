\begin{theorem}[Cauchy diagonal selection rational-interval locator route]
\label{thm:cauchy-diagonal-selection-orphan-route}
Every $\RationalIntervalLocatorUp$ consumer that reads a $\CauchyDiagonalSelectionUp$ diagonal window must first display the finite $\StreamNameUp$ observation windows, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback rows, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal carried by the diagonal-selection packet. The route then hands the visible rational lower and upper interval data to \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rational-interval-locator-namecert}; it exports no new selector, quotient stream equality, selected real limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the diagonal-selection packet through its displayed child routes. The only analytic rows available before locator consumption are the finite StreamName windows, regular rational readback rows, dyadic tolerance ledger, and Real seal.

The rational-interval locator may therefore read the selected diagonal window only after those rows are visible. At that point the lower and upper rational interval data are already finite packet data, so the consumer receives a local locator route rather than a new diagonal selector or a completion theorem.

Componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row preserve that same route into \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rational-interval-locator-namecert}. Since no carrier row contains quotient stream equality, a selected real limit, a countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness, the route exports none of those objects.
\end{proof}
