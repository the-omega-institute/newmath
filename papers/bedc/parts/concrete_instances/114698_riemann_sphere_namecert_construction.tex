\label{def:riemann-sphere-carrier}

$$

\begin{enumerate}

\item $P$ is the $\ProjectiveSpaceUp$ homogeneous-line row over the complex scalar carrier.
\item $A$ is the affine-chart admission row, recording the displayed inclusion of complex coordinates into homogeneous coordinates.
\item $I$ is the infinity row $[1:0]$, stored as a named projective endpoint rather than as a quotient class.
\item $Q$ is the compact projective coverage ledger, recording that every inspected point request is routed through the affine chart row or through the infinity row.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for complex, projective, affine-chart, infinity, coverage, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from affine complex reads through the projective line row and from projective endpoint reads through the compact coverage ledger.
\item $G$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RiemannSphereUp$, $\ComplexUp$, $\ProjectiveSpaceUp$, $\ProjectiveVarUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.

The classifier compares accepted packets only through $C,P,A,I,Q,H,T,G,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient equality of projective representatives, a selected atlas isomorphism, a host complex-surface object, or ambient compactness.

\begin{theorem}[Riemann sphere NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:riemann-sphere-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RiemannSphereUp$ carrier $S=(C,P,A,I,Q,H,T,G,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RiemannSphereUp}$ surface consists exactly of complex affine-coordinate exposure, projective homogeneous-line exposure, affine-chart admission, infinity endpoint exposure, compact projective coverage, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every accepted consumer must inspect those rows before treating the packet as the extended complex plane.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:riemann-sphere-carrier}. The complex row $C$ is the only affine source, and the projective row $P$ is the only homogeneous-line source available to the packet.

The affine chart row $A$ and infinity row $I$ then split the public point reads into two displayed routes. The coverage ledger $Q$ records that a point request reaches the carrier only through one of those routes, so no unrecorded quotient representative or host point set is available.

The structural rows $H,T,G,N$ transport, replay, source, and name exactly the same finite packet. Since no row contains host compactness, a selected atlas equality, quotient projective equality, or an ambient complex surface, the listed rows are the full NameCert obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Riemann sphere projective-complex handoff]
\label{thm:riemann-sphere-projective-complex-handoff}
Every $\ComplexUp$ affine read exported by an accepted $\RiemannSphereUp$ carrier factors through the displayed projective line route
$$
\ComplexUp,\ \ProjectiveSpaceUp,\ \ProjectiveVarUp
$$
before a downstream projective or fractional-linear consumer may inspect it. The affine chart row $A$ sends complex coordinates to homogeneous coordinates, the infinity row $I$ supplies the complementary endpoint, and the coverage ledger $Q$ keeps both reads inside $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ data. The handoff does not assert quotient projective equality, a host Riemann-sphere object, or ambient compact complex completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an affine read of the complex coordinate row $C$. The carrier admits it to the projective surface only through the chart row $A$, so a downstream projective consumer must first display the $\ProjectiveSpaceUp$ row $P$.

The projective-side read then passes through the same homogeneous-line and projective-variety route named in the provenance row $G$. The infinity row $I$ is carried separately, and the coverage ledger $Q$ records that affine and infinity reads jointly exhaust the inspected point requests without identifying their representatives by an external quotient.

Finally use $H,T,N$ to transport, replay, and name the packet. Because the excluded host objects have no coordinate in $S$, a $\MobiusTransformUp$ or projective consumer receives only this finite projective-complex handoff.
\end{proof}
