\chapter{The Golden-Mean Zeta Denominator as a Fifth-Cyclotomic Pentagon Certificate}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-window6-golden-mean-cyclotomic-pentagon}
\origin{ai}

The golden-mean reciprocal zeta denominator $1-t-t^2$ has a finite fifth-cyclotomic lift.  Let $\zeta=\zeta_5$ satisfy $\Phi_5(\zeta)=\zeta^4+\zeta^3+\zeta^2+\zeta+1=0$, and put
$$
D_k(t,y)=1-\zeta^k t-y\zeta^{2k}t^2=\det(I-\zeta^k tT_y),
\qquad
T_y=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The certificate below records the primitive fifth-cyclotomic norm of the golden-mean denominator, its scalar $y$-weighted norm, and the pentagon Gram bridge to the golden field.  It is a finite algebraic identity in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$ and in $\mathbb Z[y,t]$, not a numerical coincidence.

\section{The Fifth-Cyclotomic Twisted Norm}
\label{sec:window6-golden-mean-cyclotomic-norm}

Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_5(t,y)={}&1+t+(1+y)t^2+(1+2y)t^3+(1+3y+y^2)t^4\\
&-(y+2y^2)t^5+(y^2+y^3)t^6-y^3t^7+y^4t^8.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then the scalar norm identity is
$$
(1-t-yt^2)Q_5(t,y)=1-(1+5y+5y^2)t^5-y^5t^{10}
=\det(I-t^5T_y^5).
$$
At $y=1$, the primitive cyclotomic twists collapse from $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$ to integer coefficients:
$$
\prod_{k=1}^{4}(1-\zeta^k t-\zeta^{2k}t^2)
=1+t+2t^2+3t^3+5t^4-3t^5+2t^6-t^7+t^8,
$$
and the full fifth norm is
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{4}(1-\zeta^k t-\zeta^{2k}t^2)=1-11t^5-t^{10}.
$$
The number $11$ is the fifth Lucas trace $C_5(1)=\operatorname{tr}(T_1^5)$.

\begin{theorem}[Fifth-cyclotomic twisted norm]
\label{thm:window6-golden-mean-cyclotomic-twisted-norm}
The four primitive $\zeta_5$-twists of $1-t-t^2$ multiply to $Q_5(t,1)$, the five twists multiply to $1-11t^5-t^{10}$, and the bivariate scalar identity
$$
(1-t-yt^2)Q_5(t,y)=1-(1+5y+5y^2)t^5-y^5t^{10}
$$
holds by finite polynomial multiplication.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Pentagon Bridge}
\label{sec:window6-golden-mean-pentagon-bridge}

Let
$$
u=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=\zeta+\zeta^4.
$$
Since
$$
u^2+u-1=\zeta^2+\zeta+1+\zeta^{-1}+\zeta^{-2}
=\zeta^{-2}\Phi_5(\zeta)=0,
$$
the real element $u$ is $2\cos 72^\circ=(\sqrt5-1)/2=1/\varphi$.  Thus $1+u=\varphi$ and $-u=-1/\varphi$, and the golden denominator factors in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5][t]$ as
$$
1-t-t^2=(1-(1+u)t)(1+ut).
$$
Equivalently, the transfer characteristic polynomial
$$
x^2-x-1=(x-(1+u))(x+u)
$$
has spectrum $\{1+u,-u\}=\{\varphi,-1/\varphi\}$.

The same pentagon element appears in the Gram matrix
$$
G_{ij}=\zeta^{i-j}+\zeta^{j-i},
\qquad i,j\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z.
$$
Its first row is
$$
[2,\ u,\ -1-u,\ -1-u,\ u],
$$
and finite multiplication gives
$$
G^2=5G.
$$
The two nontrivial Gram inner products $u=1/\varphi$ and $-1-u=-\varphi$ are exactly the two roots of $1-t-t^2=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Pentagon Gram and golden factorisation]
\label{thm:window6-golden-mean-pentagon-gram}
In $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$, the element $u=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$ satisfies $u^2+u-1=0$, the golden denominator factors as
$$
1-t-t^2=(1-(1+u)t)(1+ut),
$$
and the pentagon Gram matrix $G_{ij}=\zeta^{i-j}+\zeta^{j-i}$ satisfies $G^2=5G$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Scope}
\label{sec:window6-golden-mean-cyclotomic-scope}

This is a finite cyclotomic-norm and pentagon-Gram identity bridging the golden-mean zeta to the fifth cyclotomic field; it is not a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, not about the Riemann xi, and not a zero-line statement.

The precise $\Phi_5$ and pentagon link is canonical at $y=1$: the golden field is the maximal real subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$, and $u=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=1/\varphi$ satisfies $u^2+u-1=0$.  The scalar twisted norm holds for all $y$, but the generic transfer spectrum lies in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{1+4y})$, not canonically in the fifth cyclotomic field.  The pentagon Gram identity $G^2=5G$ also does not by itself prove any reflection-positivity deficit theorem; that would require a separately defined reflection-positivity functional.

\falsifiablePrediction{A direct finite recomputation in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$ that violates $u^2+u-1=0$, the primitive twist product $\prod_{k=1}^{4}D_k(t,1)=Q_5(t,1)$, the full fifth norm $\prod_{k=0}^{4}D_k(t,1)=1-11t^5-t^{10}$, the bivariate scalar norm $(1-t-yt^2)Q_5(t,y)=1-(1+5y+5y^2)t^5-y^5t^{10}$, the factorisation $1-t-t^2=(1-(1+u)t)(1+ut)$, or $G^2=5G$ refutes this packet.}

\independenceWitness{riemann-hypothesis-roadmap, riemann-zeta, reflection-positivity-deficit: this packet is the finite fifth-cyclotomic twisted norm and pentagon Gram certificate for the golden-mean zeta denominator only; it makes no claim about the Riemann $\xi$, the critical line, prime-channel zero statistics, or any reflection-positivity deficit theorem.}
