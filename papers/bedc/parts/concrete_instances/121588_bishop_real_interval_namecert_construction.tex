Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\BishopRealIntervalUp$ packet records a constructive closed real interval as finite endpoint and regularity data rather than as a classical subset of a completed line. It sits after the dyadic rational arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream-window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. \begin{definition}[BishopRealInterval carrier]
\label{def:bishop-real-interval-carrier}
A $\BishopRealIntervalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$

$$
with the following rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the lower endpoint row, displayed as a regular rational name and sealed only through the terminal real-name route.
\item $U$ is the upper endpoint row, displayed as a regular rational name with the same endpoint discipline.
\item $T$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row used to inspect both endpoint names at requested precisions.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the endpoint regular sequences.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance and comparison row recording the finite dyadic scale at which $L$ is below $U$.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ enclosure seal available only after the endpoint windows, regular readbacks, and dyadic comparison row have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the endpoint, stream, readback, dyadic, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interval-enclosure request through the same endpoint and tolerance rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopRealIntervalUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRealIntervalUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for classical interval subsets, quotient real equality, selected endpoint representatives, Heine--Borel compactness, countable choice, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BishopRealInterval NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-real-interval-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopRealIntervalUp$ carrier $I=(L,U,T,Q,D,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRealIntervalUp}$ surface consists exactly of lower endpoint admission through $L$, upper endpoint admission through $U$, finite endpoint-window exposure through $T$, regular rational readback through $Q$, dyadic tolerance and order comparison through $D$, real-enclosure sealing through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ stability through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. A consumer therefore reads the interval only through the finite route
$$
L,U,T,Q,D \longmapsto E,H,C,P,N .
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-real-interval-carrier}. The endpoint rows $L$ and $U$ are not host real numbers; they are displayed regular names whose finite prefixes are exposed by the shared $\StreamNameUp$ row $T$.

Use the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$ and the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison row $D$. These rows supply the regular-sequence and dyadic-order evidence before the interval can be treated as a Real-facing enclosure.

Finally replay the structural rows. The seal $E$, transport $H$, continuation replay $C$, provenance $P$, and local name $N$ preserve the same finite endpoint packet, so the NameCert surface cannot import quotient equality of reals, selected representatives, Heine--Borel compactness, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopRealInterval regular seal route]
\label{thm:bishop-real-interval-regular-seal-route}
Every $\RealUp$-readable enclosure request accepted by a $\BishopRealIntervalUp$ carrier factors through the displayed route
$$
\StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \DyadicRatCoreUp \longrightarrow \RealUp .

The route is an L10 completion interface over \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, not a classical interval subset or an ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-real-interval-namecert-obligations}. They require endpoint admission, stream-window exposure, regular rational readback, and dyadic comparison before any Real-facing interval seal can be consumed.

Read the endpoint rows through the sibling $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ routes. The stream row exposes only finite endpoint prefixes, while the regular rational row supplies the endpoint readback used by completion consumers.

Now apply the dyadic comparison row $D$. Its finite tolerance ledger is the boundary at which the lower and upper endpoint names become an interval enclosure rather than two unrelated real-name seals.

Finally consume $E,H,C,P,N$. The terminal $\RealUp$ seal is reached only after those displayed rows remain visible, so the route supplies a constructive interval enclosure without selected endpoint representatives, quotient real equality, countable choice, Heine--Borel compactness, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
