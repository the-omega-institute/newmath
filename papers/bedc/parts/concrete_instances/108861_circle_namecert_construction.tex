\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CircleUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-circle-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Hatcher and Munkres on the circle as a compact one-dimensional boundary object, the $\CircleUp$ packet records the topological boundary reading of a one-dimensional closed loop without identifying it with the algebraic $\SOneUp$ group certificate. It sits beside $\SOneUp$ of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-s1-namecert}, $\RealUp$ of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, $\MetricSpaceUp$ of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, and $\CompactUp$ of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}: those siblings supply phase multiplication, real-coordinate readback, metric inspection, and compact covering rows, while this packet binds the finite boundary carrier and its NameCert obligations as a topological circle surface.

\begin{definition}[Circle carrier]
\label{def:circle-carrier}
A $\CircleUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
C=(B,R,M,K,S,H,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the boundary-loop row, naming the closed one-dimensional boundary surface.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ coordinate-read row, recording the finite real-coordinate inspection used by local charts or parametrized arcs.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ row, recording the metric neighborhoods through which boundary adjacency is read.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactUp$ row, recording finite cover data for the closed boundary surface.
\item $S$ is the $\SOneUp$ handoff row, available only when a consumer asks for the phase-group reading of the same boundary.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the boundary, real-coordinate, metric, compactness, handoff, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CircleUp$, $\SOneUp$, $\RealUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CircleUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an ambient plane, a selected trigonometric parametrization, a quotient by angle equality, or a global orientation choice.
\end{definition}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CircleUp.CircleCarrier\_boundary\_route\_certificate}

\begin{theorem}[Circle NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:circle-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CircleUp$ carrier
$$
C=(B,R,M,K,S,H,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CircleUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the boundary-loop row, real-coordinate readback, metric-neighborhood row, compact-cover row, optional $\SOneUp$ handoff, componentwise transport, package provenance, and local naming. Any topological-circle consumer must read the closed boundary surface through $B,R,M,K,H,P,N$ before using the algebraic phase handoff $S$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CircleUp.CircleUpTasteGate\_single\_carrier\_alignment}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier of \autoref{def:circle-carrier}. The topological rows are $B,R,M,K$: they name the closed boundary, the real-coordinate readback, the metric neighborhoods, and the compact cover data.

The handoff row $S$ is downstream. It allows a consumer to compare the boundary packet with the $\SOneUp$ phase-group sibling, but it does not replace the boundary carrier by an algebraic group row.

The rows $H,P,N$ transport, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no ambient plane, trigonometric parametrization, quotient-angle equality, or orientation coordinate appears in the carrier, the listed rows exhaust the $\NameCert_{\CircleUp}$ obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Circle and $\SOneUp$ boundary handoff]
\label{thm:circle-sone-boundary-handoff}
Every $\SOneUp$-facing read of an accepted $\CircleUp$ carrier factors through the boundary-loop row $B$, real-coordinate readback $R$, metric row $M$, compact-cover row $K$, and handoff row $S$. The handoff may expose a phase-group consumer, but it cannot make $\SOneUp$ the carrier of topological compact-boundary data and cannot export quotient-angle equality or a selected global parametrization.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CircleUp.CircleSOneBoundaryHandoff}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer asking for the phase-group reading. The $\CircleUp$ carrier first exposes the boundary surface through $B$ and its local real and metric inspections through $R,M$.

Compactness is read through $K$, so the consumer receives a finite cover surface for the closed boundary before any phase handoff is available. Only then can $S$ point to the $\SOneUp$ sibling.

The componentwise transport, provenance, and naming rows $H,P,N$ preserve this order. Because the carrier has no quotient-angle coordinate or selected global parametrization, the handoff remains a boundary-to-phase route rather than an identification of the two packets.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Circle metric subspace carrier obligation]
\label{thm:circle-metric-subspace-carrier-obligation}
For every accepted $\CircleUp$ carrier $C=(B,R,M,K,S,H,P,N)$, the boundary row $B$, $\RealUp$ coordinate row $R$, $\MetricSpaceUp$ neighborhood row $M$, $\CompactUp$ cover row $K$, $\SOneUp$ handoff row $S$, transport row $H$, provenance row $P$, and local $\NameCert_{\CircleUp}$ row $N$ are the full carrier obligation surface. A consumer may not replace these rows by an ambient plane, a selected parametrization, quotient-angle equality, or a global orientation choice.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CircleUp.CircleMetricSubspaceCarrierObligation}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:circle-carrier}. Its analytic boundary surface is exactly $B,R,M,K,S$, and its structural surface is exactly $H,P,N$.

No other coordinate is available for a circle consumer. Since the carrier omits an ambient plane, selected trigonometric map, quotient by angle equality, and orientation row, the displayed coordinates exhaust the carrier obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Circle $\SOneUp$ real seal route]
\label{thm:circle-sone-real-seal-route}
Every phase-group read of an accepted $\CircleUp$ carrier must pass through the $\RealUp$ coordinate row $R$ and the $\SOneUp$ handoff row $S$, with the boundary row $B$, metric row $M$, compact row $K$, transport $H$, provenance $P$, and local naming $N$ still present. This route permits a phase interpretation but does not identify $\CircleUp$ with $\SOneUp$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CircleUp.CircleSOneRealSealRoute}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer that asks for phase-group data. The carrier first exposes the topological boundary and real-coordinate surface through $B$ and $R$.

The handoff row $S$ can then point to the $\SOneUp$ sibling, while $M,K,H,P,N$ keep the same metric, compact, transport, provenance, and naming rows attached to the circle packet. Because $S$ is a handoff coordinate rather than the carrier itself, the route is not an identification of the two chapters.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Circle compact metric boundary]
\label{thm:circle-compact-metric-boundary}
Before any consumer reads $\CircleUp$ as a closed boundary, the packet must expose the compact-cover row $K$, the metric-neighborhood row $M$, the boundary row $B$, the $\RealUp$ coordinate row $R$, transport $H$, provenance $P$, and local naming $N$. The compact-metric boundary does not supply an ambient plane, a selected parametrization, or quotient-angle equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the compact and metric coordinates together. The row $K$ supplies finite cover data for the closed boundary, and $M$ supplies the neighborhood inspection by which boundary adjacency is read.

The rows $B,R,H,P,N$ bind those compact and metric inspections to the circle carrier and its local name. Since no ambient plane or quotient-angle coordinate appears in the packet, a closed-boundary consumer receives only this compact-metric surface.
\end{proof}
