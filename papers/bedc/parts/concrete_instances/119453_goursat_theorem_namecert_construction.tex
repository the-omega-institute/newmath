\begin{definition}[Goursat theorem carrier]
\label{def:goursat-theorem-carrier}
A $\GoursatTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(T,F,S,M,Z,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the triangle-contour row. It records three $\ComplexUp$ vertex histories, three oriented piecewise-linear edge rows, and the socket-facing boundary contour assembled from those edges.
\item $F$ is the holomorphic input row, consumed from $\HolomorphicUp$ on a displayed neighbourhood of the triangle contour and its subdivision windows.
\item $S$ is the finite subdivision ledger. Each entry stores a parent triangle, four child triangles, inherited orientation rows, and the internal-edge pairing visible to the contour socket.
\item $M$ is the Riemann-sum modulus row. It records the unary mesh budget, local oscillation bounds for $F$, and the tolerance comparison attached to the leaves of $S$.
\item $Z$ is the $\ComplexUp$ zero-output row, displaying the target value $\mathsf{0}_{\ComplexUp}$ for the contour-integral socket read.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $T,F,S,M,Z$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the triangle contour request through the holomorphic input, subdivision ledger, Riemann-sum modulus, zero-output row, provenance, and local naming row.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\GoursatTheoremUp$, $\ContourIntegralSocketUp$, $\HolomorphicUp$, $\ComplexUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\GoursatTheoremUp}$ row.

The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,F,S,M,Z,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an ambient contour integral, selected antiderivative, host compactness proof, quotient path equality, or classical-choice schedule.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Goursat theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:goursat-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\GoursatTheoremUp$ carrier $G=(T,F,S,M,Z,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\GoursatTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of triangle-contour exposure, holomorphic-input exposure, subdivision-ledger exposure, Riemann-sum modulus exposure, $\ComplexUp$ zero-output sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. A consumer receives no ambient contour-integral object, no selected primitive, no host compactness argument, no quotient equality of paths, and no classical-choice schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:goursat-theorem-carrier}. The analytic rows are exactly $T,F,S,M,Z$, and the structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

The contour request enters through $T$ and the contour-integral socket named in $P$. The holomorphic input row $F$ supplies only the displayed local data on the triangle neighbourhood and subdivision windows.

The theorem-shaped content is carried by $S,M,Z$. The subdivision ledger exposes the child triangles and internal-edge pairings, the Riemann-sum modulus exposes the finite tolerance comparison on the leaves, and the zero-output row seals the socket read at $\mathsf{0}_{\ComplexUp}$.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that finite route. Since the carrier contains no slot for the excluded ambient objects, the local NameCert surface is exhausted by the rows listed in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Goursat theorem triangle-subdivision route]
\label{thm:goursat-theorem-triangle-subdivision-route}
Every contour-integral consumer that reads an accepted $\GoursatTheoremUp$ packet must factor the read through the finite route
$$
T,F \longmapsto S \longmapsto M \longmapsto Z \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
The internal-edge cancellations are visible only as entries of the subdivision ledger $S$, and the vanishing conclusion is visible only as the $\ComplexUp$ zero-output row $Z$. No consumer may replace this route by a host path integral theorem, a selected primitive, an open-cover compactness proof, quotient path equality, or a classical-choice schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a public contour-integral read. By \autoref{thm:goursat-theorem-namecert-obligations}, the read first exposes the triangle contour row $T$ and holomorphic input row $F$.

The read then enters $S$. Every child triangle and every internal opposite-edge pairing is a displayed ledger entry, so cancellation is inspected as finite subdivision data rather than as an ambient homology or path-quotient theorem.

The mesh and oscillation comparison is read from $M$. Its leafwise Riemann-sum bound is the only analytic estimate available before the conclusion is sealed.

Finally $Z$ supplies the zero-output row, and $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Because no excluded ambient object is a carrier coordinate, the displayed triangle-subdivision route is exhaustive.
\end{proof}
