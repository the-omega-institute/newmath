\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension finite epsilon-net carrier]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-finite-epsilon-net-carrier}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N),
$$
the finite epsilon-net row $E$ is a root cover obligation: every cover sample exported by $C$, every refinement membership read by $R$, and every order-bound count recorded by $O$ must be read through the displayed $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ centers, dyadic tolerance witnesses, and $\BHist$ coverage rows of $E$ as described in \autoref{def:covering-dimension-carrier}. The local NameCert surface of \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations} therefore admits finite cover inspection only through $K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N$; it admits no arbitrary open-cover compactness, selected dense subfamily, or host finite-subcover theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows of \autoref{def:covering-dimension-carrier}. The source row $K$ supplies the compact metric packet, and $E$ supplies the finite $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ of centers and tolerance-indexed coverage witnesses.

The cover row $C$ can be inspected only by reading those displayed finite witnesses. The refinement row $R$ and order-bound row $O$ then operate over the same $\BHist$ cover entries, so their counts and memberships remain finite BEDC rows rather than external compactness data.

The rows $L,H,T,P,N$ replay, transport, package, and name the same route. By \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations}, the NameCert obligation surface has no coordinate for an arbitrary cover or a selected finite subcover, so the finite epsilon-net carrier obligation is root-local.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension compact-cover forward dependency]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-compact-cover-forward-dependency}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N),
$$
the cover-refinement rows $C,R,O$ consume the finite-cover lower-bound ledgers of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-cover-lebesgue-ledger-namecert} before any $\MetricCompletionUp$ or $\RealSeparabilityUp$ consumer may read a dimension bound. Concretely, the Lebesgue-ledger row $L$ must display the same compact finite-net centers, positive radius lower bounds, and finite fold route used by the cover row $C$ and refinement row $R$; only then can $O$ record an order bound and expose it through $H,T,P,N$. Thus the dimension-bound surface is downstream of a substantive $\CompactCoverLebesgueLedgerUp$ finite-cover dependency, not an isolated root cover assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite epsilon-net carrier of \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-finite-epsilon-net-carrier}. It exposes the compact metric source row $K$, probe centers, tolerance witnesses, and finite cover row $C$ before any order-bound count is visible.

The compact-cover lower-bound ledger then enters through row $L$. By \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-cover-lebesgue-ledger-namecert}, this ledger is a finite route through compact finite-net data, positive rational lower bounds, and finite lower-bound folding; it cannot supply an arbitrary open-cover compactness theorem.

Finally use \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-order-refinement-obligation} and \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-real-separability-root-route}. The order row $O$ is read after $C,R,L$, and the MetricCompletion and RealSeparability consumers see that same displayed route before receiving a dimension-bound handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension order refinement obligation]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-order-refinement-obligation}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N),
$$
the order row $O$ is valid only after the refinement row $R$ has displayed, for each refined $\BHist$ cover member, its parent member in the finite cover row $C$ and its containment witness. Thus the order-refinement obligation exposed by \autoref{def:covering-dimension-carrier} and \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations} is a finite computation over $K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\CoveringDimensionUp}$ row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Open the accepted carrier. The finite cover row $C$ is the only list of cover members, and the refinement row $R$ is the only map from refined members back into that list.

Before $O$ may count overlaps, each refined member must therefore pass through the parent and containment data carried by $R$. The count is made over finite $\BHist$ rows visible through $C$ and $R$, not over a choice-selected refinement or an ambient multiplicity oracle.

Finally $H,T,P,N$ preserve the same finite route under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. The NameCert obligations in \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations} require exactly these rows, so the order refinement claim is a root obligation of the displayed carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension compact metric handoff obligation]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-compactmetric-handoff-obligation}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N),
$$
any compact metric consumer that receives a dimension-bound handoff must first read the compact metric source row $K$, the finite epsilon-net row $E$, the finite cover row $C$, the refinement row $R$, the order-bound row $O$, and the Lebesgue-ledger row $L$, and then pass through $H,T,P,N$. Consequently the handoff certified by \autoref{def:covering-dimension-carrier} and \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations} remains a finite BEDC route over $\BHist$, $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$, not a claim of open-cover compactness or choice-selected refinement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the compact metric handoff in row order. The source row $K$ anchors the carrier, while $E$ displays the finite probes used to sample the cover row $C$.

The refinement and order data are then exposed by $R$ and $O$. The Lebesgue-ledger row $L$ ties the same finite cover route to positive radius readback, so the consumer sees a compact metric handoff only after the finite cover-refinement surface has been displayed.

The rows $H,T,P,N$ preserve this route as componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Since the carrier and NameCert obligation theorem list no arbitrary open-cover coordinate or choice-selected refinement coordinate, the handoff obligation is exactly the finite BEDC route $K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension RealSeparability root completion route]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-real-separability-root-route}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N),
$$
any root cover read that samples finite covers through dense real windows must display the $\RealSeparabilityUp$ rational and dyadic windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-separability-namecert} and the completion-facing rows of $\MetricCompletionUp$ in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert} after the finite epsilon-net row $E$ and cover-refinement rows $C,R$, and before the order-bound row $O$ is consumed. Thus the root cover route is the finite chain
$$
K,E,C,R,\RealSeparabilityUp,\MetricCompletionUp,O,L,H,T,P,N,
$$
not a selected dense sequence, arbitrary open-cover compactness principle, or ambient completion theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the compact metric handoff of \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-compactmetric-handoff-obligation}. The rows $K,E,C,R$ display the compact metric source, finite epsilon-net probes, finite cover entries, and refinement memberships before any order count is public.

When dense real samples are used, the sample row must enter through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-separability-namecert}. Those rational and dyadic windows are finite rows, so they can refine the displayed cover samples but cannot supply a selected dense subfamily outside $E,C,R$.

The completion-facing read is then routed through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}. Its completion boundary rows are attached after the separability windows and before $O$, so the order-bound ledger counts only the finite cover-refinement data already exposed through the root cover packet.

Finally $L,H,T,P,N$ replay the same route as the Lebesgue ledger, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, package provenance, and local naming rows. Hence the RealSeparability and MetricCompletion dependency is a substantive root-cover obligation of the finite carrier, not a bare citation or an ambient compactness argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension finite-cover root boundary]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-finite-cover-root-boundary}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N),
$$
every downstream dimension-bound read must display the compact source row $K$, finite epsilon-net row $E$, finite cover row $C$, refinement row $R$, order-bound row $O$, and Lebesgue-ledger row $L$ before any cover consumer can read the order bound. Thus the root boundary for the order row is the finite chain
$$
K,E,C,R,O,L,
$$
together with the structural rows $H,T,P,N$, and not an arbitrary open-cover compactness theorem, selected refinement, or ambient separability argument.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier of \autoref{def:covering-dimension-carrier}. Its dimension-bound surface is a finite row family $K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N$, so an order-bound read has no source outside those coordinates.

Use \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations} and \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-finite-epsilon-net-carrier}. The NameCert obligations require carrier admission through the displayed rows, while the finite epsilon-net carrier theorem makes $E$ the root cover witness for $C,R,O$.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-real-separability-root-route}. Dense real windows and MetricCompletion rows enter only after $E,C,R$ and before $O$ is consumed, so the consumer sees the same finite root boundary $K,E,C,R,O,L$ rather than a hidden compactness or selection principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CoveringDimension refinement order root unblock]
\label{thm:coveringdimension-refinement-order-root-unblock}
For an accepted $\CoveringDimensionUp$ carrier $D=(K,E,C,R,O,L,H,T,P,N)$, refinement containment through $R$ is the only admissible source for the order row $O$. A downstream finite-cover consumer may read $O$ only after $R$ has displayed, for each refined $\BHist$ cover member, its parent member in $C$ and its containment witness over the finite epsilon-net support $E$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the refinement and order rows from \autoref{def:covering-dimension-carrier}. The row $R$ is the only coordinate that relates refined cover members to parent cover members in $C$, and $O$ is the only coordinate that records the resulting order bound.

Apply \autoref{thm:covering-dimension-namecert-obligations}. The local NameCert surface assigns order validity to the displayed refinement route, so no external multiplicity oracle or selected refinement can provide the order row.

Use \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-finite-epsilon-net-carrier} and \autoref{thm:coveringdimension-real-separability-root-route}. The finite epsilon-net row $E$ supplies the root cover support, and any dense real or completion-facing sample is routed through that same support before $O$ is visible. Therefore refinement containment through $R$ is the root source that unblocks the order-bound consumer.
\end{proof}
