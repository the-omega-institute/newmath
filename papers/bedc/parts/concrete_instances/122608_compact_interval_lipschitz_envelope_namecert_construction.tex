\begin{definition}[Compact interval Lipschitz-envelope packet]
\label{def:compact-interval-lipschitz-envelope-packet}
A $\CompactIntervalLipschitzEnvelopeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(K,F,L,M,B,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ compact interval source, including the finite mesh rows accepted by the interval carrier.
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ or $\LipschitzMapUp$ function row whose values are being bounded.
\item $L$ is the finite Lipschitz-bound row, displaying the rational or dyadic bound used to compare values across mesh cells.
\item $M$ is the finite mesh row, read through $\FiniteIntervalMeshRefinementUp$ or an existing compact interval finite-net sibling when the consumer supplies one.
\item $B$ is the envelope row assigning displayed lower and upper rational bounds to each mesh cell.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the displayed envelope bounds.
\item $S$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for envelope values exposed after $K,F,L,M,B,R$ have all been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for compact source rows, map rows, Lipschitz bounds, mesh rows, envelope rows, readback rows, Real seals, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an envelope request through $K,F,L,M,B,R,S,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactIntervalLipschitzEnvelopeUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\LipschitzMapUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactIntervalLipschitzEnvelopeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an uninspected supremum, a selected maximizing point, an open-cover choice principle, quotient function equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact interval Lipschitz-envelope NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-interval-lipschitz-envelope-namecert-obligations}

A consumer may read a compact interval envelope only after these rows expose the finite mesh, Lipschitz bound, uniform-modulus handoff, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and $\RealUp$ seal route.

\begin{proof}

The finite mesh row $M$ is the only place where the interval is inspected. The Lipschitz row $L$ then transports pointwise control across cells, and $B$ records the resulting lower and upper rational envelope bounds.

The readback and seal rows $R,S$ make the envelope usable by regular rational and Real-facing consumers only after the finite rows have been displayed. \end{proof}
