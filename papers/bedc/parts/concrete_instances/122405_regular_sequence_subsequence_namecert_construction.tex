\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp$ packet records the finite route by which a regular sequence remains regular after a displayed increasing subsequence schedule is read. It sits over the regular-sequence source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-sequence-space-namecert}, the finite observation windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the rational readback surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only the subsequence-stability route; it does not choose a host subsequence, identify quotient sequence names, import countable choice, or assert ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[RegularSequenceSubsequence carrier]
\label{def:regular-sequence-subsequence-carrier}
A $\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(S,W,K,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ source row, retaining the source regular-sequence classifier and its admitted observation windows.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row, recording the source observations from which the subsequence is read.
\item $K$ is the increasing subsequence schedule row. It records finite monotone index windows and does not select an infinite host function outside the packet.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row, carrying the dyadic budget used to compare source and subsequence windows.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, exposing the regular rational observations of the selected windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row consumed by Real-facing or Completion-facing readers after $S,W,K,D,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source, stream-window, subsequence schedule, dyadic tolerance, rational readback, Real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a subsequence request through $S,W,K,D,Q,E,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp$, $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,W,K,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host subsequence selector, quotient sequence equality, selected limit, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RegularSequenceSubsequence NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-subsequence-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp$ carrier
$$
R=(S,W,K,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp}$ surface consists exactly of source regular-sequence admission, finite StreamName window exposure, increasing subsequence schedule display, dyadic tolerance readback, RegSeqRat rational readback, terminal Real seal nonescape, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, package provenance, and local naming. Each obligation is read through the finite route
$$
\RegularSequenceSpaceUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-sequence-subsequence-carrier}. The source row $S$ and finite-window row $W$ are the only rows from which a subsequence request may begin.

The schedule row $K$ records monotone finite index windows, while $D$ supplies the dyadic comparison budget for those windows. Thus a subsequence consumer receives a displayed finite route rather than a host-selected infinite map.

The rational readback row $Q$ is downstream of $S,W,K,D$, and the Real seal row $E$ is downstream of $Q$. Componentwise transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve that order, while $P,N$ record provenance and local naming.

Since the tuple contains no coordinate for quotient sequence equality, selected limits, countable choice, host subsequence selectors, or ambient completeness, no NameCert obligation can be discharged outside the displayed packet rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RegularSequenceSubsequence regularity preservation]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-subsequence-regularity-preservation}
Let $R=(S,W,K,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RegularSequenceSubsequenceUp$ carrier. Every $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, or completion-facing consumer that reads the subsequence as regular must first expose the $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ source row $S$, the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row $W$, the increasing schedule row $K$, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row $D$, and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$. Therefore regularity preservation is a finite readback route from the source regular sequence to the subsequence seal, not a host theorem about arbitrary subsequences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-subsequence-namecert-obligations}. It places $S,W,K,D,Q$ before every Real-facing or completion-facing read of the subsequence.

The source regularity is read from $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ through $S$, and the observed subsequence windows are read through $\StreamNameUp$ and $K$. The dyadic row $D$ supplies the tolerance budget that makes the selected windows compatible with the regular rational readback.

Finally the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $Q$ exposes the rational regularity data before the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$ can be consumed. The support rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, package, and name that same finite route, so they cannot add a host subsequence selector, quotient equality, selected limit, countable choice, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
