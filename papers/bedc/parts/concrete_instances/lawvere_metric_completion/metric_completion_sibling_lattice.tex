\begin{theorem}[Lawvere metric-completion sibling lattice route]\label{thm:lawvere-metric-completion-sibling-lattice-route}
Any symmetric metric-completion consumer of an accepted $\LawvereMetricCompletionUp$ packet must first pass through the directed route displayed after $\QuasiMetricUp$, $\YonedaCompletionUp$, $\FormalBallUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$. It may then compare that route with $\MetricCompletionDenseEmbeddingUp$ and $\CauchyCompletionDenseReflectorUp$, but the comparison does not replace the directed source row or the one-sided profile classifier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the namecert construction route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lawvere-metric-completion-namecert}. The packet names completion through the directed $\QuasiMetricUp$, $\YonedaCompletionUp$, and $\FormalBallUp$ rows before any rational readback or terminal Real seal is available.

The symmetric siblings are therefore downstream comparison surfaces. The dense embedding of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-completion-dense-embedding-namecert} and the dense-reflector route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-completion-dense-reflector-namecert} may inspect the same completed-window data, but neither sibling supplies a new directed source row.

Thus the horizontal lattice edge is substantive only after the Lawvere route has been read, and it preserves the one-sided profile classifier of the original packet.
\end{proof}
