Following Bishop and Bridges on regular Cauchy names and constructive real completion, the $\RegularCauchyCommonModulusUp$ packet records a shared finite modulus ledger for joining two regular Cauchy names before any terminal $\RealUp$ seal is read. It sits between the regular rational names of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the interleaving route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regularcauchyinterleaving-namecert}, and the real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The carrier is a finite ledger object, not a quotient equality of Cauchy names, an ambient completeness theorem, or a choice of global representative.

\begin{definition}[RegularCauchyCommonModulus carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-common-modulus-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyCommonModulusUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(A,B,W,R,D,J,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ and $B$ are the two $\RegSeqRatUp$ rows naming the regular Cauchy inputs whose comparison is being joined;
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row exposing the prefixes of both names that are visible to the request;
\item $R$ records the shared regular-readback ledger tying the two names to the same displayed window schedule;
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row containing the dyadic budget used to compare both sides;
\item $J$ is the common-modulus ledger stating that every accepted precision request is answered by the same finite window before the $\RealUp$ seal is available;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $A,B,W,R,D,J$;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for the common-window request and its downstream handoff;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyCommonModulusUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegularCauchyInterleavingUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyCommonModulusUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $A,B,W,R,D,J,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient equality of Cauchy names, selected representatives, hidden countable choice, ambient completeness, or a direct $\RealUp$ equality proof.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyCommonModulus NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-common-modulus-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchyCommonModulusUp$ carrier $G=(A,B,W,R,D,J,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyCommonModulusUp}$ surface is exhausted by five obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier exposure for the two regular Cauchy input rows, finite stream window, shared readback ledger, dyadic tolerance row, common-modulus ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item regular-name admission for $A$ and $B$ through the $\RegSeqRatUp$ rows already visible in the carrier;
\item finite-window exposure through $W,R,D,J$, requiring both names to answer the same displayed dyadic precision request before a consumer may continue;
\item interleaving compatibility, allowing $\RegularCauchyInterleavingUp$ consumers to read the common ledger as a finite join of the two visible regular names;
\item ledger refusal, excluding quotient equality of Cauchy names, selected representatives, hidden choice schedules, ambient completeness, and direct $\RealUp$ equality.
\end{enumerate}
No local NameCert obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier. The only regular-name inputs are $A$ and $B$, and the only shared comparison data are the finite rows $W,R,D,J$.

The regular-name admission row first reads $A$ and $B$ as $\RegSeqRatUp$ data. The window row $W$ then displays the prefixes of both names that the request can inspect.

Next the shared readback and tolerance rows bind the two sides to the same dyadic request. The common-modulus ledger $J$ records that the request is answered by one finite window schedule rather than by two unrelated searches.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name exactly that finite route. Since no carrier coordinate supplies quotient equality, selected representatives, hidden choice, ambient completeness, or direct $\RealUp$ equality, the five rows exhaust the local surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyCommonModulus finite-window handoff]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-common-modulus-finite-window-handoff}

$$
A,B,W,R,D,J \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer receives two regular rational names, a finite stream-window schedule, a shared readback ledger, a dyadic tolerance budget, a common-modulus row, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming before it can read any real-name seal. It receives no quotient equality of Cauchy names, selected representative, hidden choice schedule, ambient completeness theorem, or host real equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier definition. Its Real-facing data are downstream of the two regular-name rows and the common finite-window ledger.

Use the NameCert obligations. The finite-window exposure row requires $W,R,D,J$ to be displayed after $A$ and $B$, and the interleaving-compatibility row allows only that displayed ledger to feed the $\RegularCauchyInterleavingUp$ side.

The structural rows then preserve the same route. Transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$ add no quotient, selected representative, choice schedule, completeness theorem, or host real equality, so every downstream seal read is forced through the finite chain above.
\end{proof}
