\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-reals-completion-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$ packet records the traditional Cauchy-real completion interface as finite BEDC rows rather than as a quotient carrier or a selected representative. It reads the finite stream schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the displayed monad-multiplication boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletionmultiplication-namecert}. The packet is a finite completion-facing interface for the open Real and Completion phase; it does not assert quotient classes, selected representatives, countable choice, ambient completeness, or completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Cauchy reals completion carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-reals-completion-carrier}
A $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(S,Q,D,M,E,J,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window schedule for the regular Cauchy input.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the same scheduled windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used by every admitted precision request.
\item $M$ is the modulus-readback row that sends each unary precision request to a displayed finite window and dyadic tolerance entry.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached after $S,Q,D,M$ have been displayed.
\item $J$ is the $\CauchyCompletionMultiplicationUp$ handoff row used when a completion-of-completion consumer asks to flatten its request before reading the Real-facing seal.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the schedule, readback, tolerance, modulus, seal, join, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
S \longmapsto Q \longmapsto D \longmapsto M \longmapsto E
$$
and of the join route through $J$ when a nested completion request is present.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CauchyCompletionMultiplicationUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyRealsCompletionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,Q,D,M,E,J,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient classes, selected representatives, countable choice, host equality, ambient completeness, completeness of $\RealUp$, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy reals completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-reals-completion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$ carrier
$$
R=(S,Q,D,M,E,J,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyRealsCompletionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed rows, finite-window scheduling through $S$, regular-rational readback through $Q$, dyadic tolerance accounting through $D$, modulus readback through $M$, Real-seal handoff through $E$, nested completion flattening through $J$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

No local obligation is stored in quotient classes, selected representatives, countable-choice schedules, host equality, ambient completeness, or a completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-reals-completion-carrier}. The tuple displays exactly the StreamName schedule, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance ledger, modulus row, Real seal, completion-multiplication handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The scheduling, readback, tolerance, and modulus obligations are read from $S,Q,D,M$. The Real-facing obligation is read from $E$ only after those rows have been displayed.

The nested completion obligation is read through $J$, so a completion-of-completion consumer must use the same finite route already named by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletionmultiplication-namecert}. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are exhausted by $H,C,P,N$.

Since the carrier has no further coordinate, the excluded quotient, representative, choice, host-equality, and completeness objects cannot store a local obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy reals completion modulus readback]
\label{thm:cauchy-reals-completion-modulus-readback}
Let $R=(S,Q,D,M,E,J,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$ carrier. Every unary precision request is answered by the modulus row $M$ as one displayed finite route through a $\StreamNameUp$ window in $S$, a $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback in $Q$, and a $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance entry in $D$ before the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$ is read.

The readback exports no quotient class, selected representative, countable-choice selector, host equality, ambient completeness datum, or completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a precision request at the accepted carrier. The only public request-answering row is $M$, and it is tied in the carrier to the finite schedule $S$.

Read the regular rational observation through $Q$ and the corresponding dyadic tolerance entry through $D$. These rows are the only displayed data that can justify the requested accuracy before the seal row is reached.

The terminal Real-facing read is $E$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route, while $J$ is used only when a nested completion request must first be flattened.

Thus the precision request is answered by the stated finite route. The excluded quotient, representative, choice, host-equality, and completeness data have no row from which they could be read.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy reals completion classifier stability]
\label{thm:cauchy-reals-completion-classifier-stability}
If two accepted $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$ carriers agree componentwise under displayed $\hsame$ transport on their $\StreamNameUp$ schedule, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger, modulus-readback row, $\RealUp$ seal, $\CauchyCompletionMultiplicationUp$ handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows, then they have the same public completion classifier read.

The classifier is stable only over those finite rows and cannot be altered by quotient classes, selected representatives, countable choice, host equality, ambient completeness, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the componentwise transport row $H$ for the two accepted packets. It compares exactly the rows named in \autoref{def:cauchy-reals-completion-carrier}.

Replay the transported rows through $C$. The finite schedule, regular readback, dyadic tolerance ledger, modulus answer, Real seal, and nested completion handoff remain on the same displayed route.

The provenance row $P$ and local name $N$ do not add classifier data; they only source and name the accepted route. Since the carrier exposes no quotient, representative, choice, host-equality, or completeness coordinate, no such object can change the classifier read.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy reals completion Real seal]
\label{thm:cauchy-reals-completion-real-seal}
Every $\RealUp$-facing consumer of an accepted $\CauchyRealsCompletionUp$ carrier reaches the seal row $E$ only after reading the displayed $\StreamNameUp$ schedule, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger, modulus row, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row. If the consumer begins with a nested completion request, it must first pass through the $\CauchyCompletionMultiplicationUp$ handoff row $J$.

The seal is a terminal BEDC row for the displayed route; it is not a proof of ambient completeness and it is not a completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The Real-facing seal coordinate is $E$, and the rows upstream of it are the schedule $S$, readback $Q$, tolerance ledger $D$, and modulus row $M$.

If the request is nested, the handoff row $J$ supplies the completion-multiplication route before the same Real-facing rows are consumed. This keeps the completion-of-completion case inside the displayed BEDC packet.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are carried by $H,C,P,N$. These rows preserve and source the same route without adding a host Real-completeness premise.

Therefore every Real-facing consumer reaches $E$ only through the finite surface listed in the statement, and the terminal seal cannot be read as ambient completeness or as completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{proof}
