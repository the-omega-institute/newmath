\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\GromovBoundaryUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-gromov-boundary-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Gromov's hyperbolic-boundary construction, the $\GromovBoundaryUp$ packet records finite boundary-equivalence data for hyperbolic rays before a visual or cocycle consumer reads the boundary. It sits after the visual-boundary packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-visual-boundary-namecert}, the basepointed product packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-gromov-product-namecert}, the visual-metric packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-visual-metric-namecert}, the Busemann cocycle route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-busemann-cocycle-namecert}, and the boundary-shadow expansion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundary-shadow-expansion-cocycle-namecert}. The packet names a displayed boundary carrier rather than a completed quotient of rays, selected geodesic representatives, host compactification, or ambient hyperbolic space.

\begin{definition}[Gromov boundary carrier]\label{def:gromov-boundary-carrier}
A $\GromovBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(B,P,V,M,C,Q,E,S,T,H,R,N)
$$
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $B$ is the $\HyperbolicVisualBoundaryUp$ boundary row;
\item $P$ is the $\HyperbolicGromovProductUp$ basepointed product row;
\item $V$ is the $\HyperbolicVisualMetricUp$ visual-metric row;
\item $M$ is the $\HyperbolicBusemannCocycleUp$ cocycle row;
\item $C$ is the $\BoundaryShadowExpansionCocycleUp$ boundary-shadow expansion row;
\item $Q$ records finite ray-equivalence comparisons;
\item $E$ records endpoint admission for the accepted boundary token;
\item $S$ records visual-cocycle stability under the displayed equivalence row;
\item $T,H,R,N$ are displayed $\Cont$ replay, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\GromovBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Gromov boundary NameCert obligations]\label{thm:gromov-boundary-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\GromovBoundaryUp$ carrier $G=(B,P,V,M,C,Q,E,S,T,H,R,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\GromovBoundaryUp}$ surface consists exactly of visual-boundary admission, Gromov-product readback, visual-metric comparison, Busemann-cocycle comparison, boundary-shadow expansion, finite ray-equivalence exposure, endpoint admission, visual-cocycle stability, displayed replay, componentwise transport, provenance, and local naming. A consumer that reads a boundary token without one of those rows is outside the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:gromov-boundary-carrier}. The visual boundary, Gromov product, visual metric, Busemann cocycle, and boundary-shadow expansion rows are all explicit coordinates of the accepted packet.

The ray-equivalence row $Q$ and endpoint row $E$ provide the boundary token only as displayed finite data. The packet therefore exposes equivalence information before any visual metric or cocycle consumer receives the boundary.

Finally, $S,T,H,R,N$ record stability, replay, transport, provenance, and naming. Since no carrier row supplies a host quotient of rays, selected geodesic representative, completed compactification, or ambient hyperbolic model, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gromov boundary visual-cocycle stability]\label{thm:gromov-boundary-visual-cocycle-stability}
Every accepted $\GromovBoundaryUp$ packet whose boundary token is consumed by a visual-metric, Busemann-cocycle, or boundary-shadow-expansion consumer must retain the ray-equivalence row $Q$ through the $\HyperbolicGromovProductUp$ row $P$, $\HyperbolicVisualMetricUp$ row $V$, $\HyperbolicBusemannCocycleUp$ row $M$, and $\BoundaryShadowExpansionCocycleUp$ row $C$. The stability row $S$ transports those displayed comparisons without turning the packet into a completed ray quotient or global boundary compactification.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with \autoref{thm:gromov-boundary-namecert-obligations}. It keeps $B,P,V,M,C,Q,E,S,T,H,R,N$ visible for every accepted boundary read.

The product row $P$ and ray-equivalence row $Q$ determine which boundary token is being transported. The visual metric, Busemann cocycle, and boundary-shadow expansion rows then read that token through their own displayed comparison surfaces.

The stability row $S$ preserves those comparisons through $T,H,R,N$. Because the carrier has no coordinate for a selected ray representative or host quotient, the handoff remains a finite BEDC boundary-equivalence packet.
\end{proof}
