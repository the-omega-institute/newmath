The BioReality $Q6$ translation-mediation abundance ledger records a cross-layer statistical decomposition connecting per-protein $B^*_{Q6}$ codon residual coordinates, a modeled translation-adaptation readout, and measured protein abundance.  The carrier name is \texttt{BioRealityQSixTranslationMediationAbundanceLedger}.  The layer is \texttt{cross\_layer\_relation}, and the conjecture identifier is \texttt{q6.translation-mediated.protein-abundance.cross-layer}.  Its statement is intentionally narrow: per-protein $B^*_{Q6}$ codon-residual association with protein abundance is decomposed by conditioning on modeled per-protein tAI, and the decomposition is statistical rather than causal.

The ledger uses three organisms with matched coding-sequence and protein-abundance rows.  The joined row counts are $3739$ for \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655, $4129$ for \emph{Mycobacterium smegmatis} str. MC2 155, and $5815$ for \emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}.  Each row carries real CDS codon counts joined to measured PAXdb abundance as $\log_{10}(\mathrm{abundance\_ppm})$.  The mediator $T$ is the per-protein mean modeled tAI,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_i &= \frac{\sum_c n_{ic} w_c}{\sum_c n_{ic}},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $n_{ic}$ is the CDS count of codon $c$ in protein $i$, and $w_c$ is the modeled tAI codon weight derived from all-tRNA copy counts with dos Reis wobble weights.  This is not a ribosome-profiling measurement.

\begin{definition}[Ledger coordinates]
The $B^*_{Q6}$ residual block $X_Q$ has exactly nine coordinates:
$$
\begin{gathered}
K\_AAA,\quad Arg\_AGR,\quad Ile\_AUA,\quad Leu\_CUN\_vs\_UUR,\quad Leu\_UUA\_vs\_UUG,\\
Ser\_UCR\_vs\_AGY,\quad Ser\_UCA\_vs\_UCG,\quad Thr\_ACR\_vs\_ACY,\quad f3\_stress.
\end{gathered}
$$
The coordinate source is the imported $q$-vector block used for the $B^*_{Q6}$ translation-survival powered computation.  The abundance ledger does not alter those coordinates; it only joins them with per-protein abundance and modeled tAI.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Residualized mediation ledger]
For each organism, let $Y$ be the abundance readout, let $T$ be the scalar modeled tAI readout, let $X_Q$ be the nine-dimensional codon-residual block, and let $Z$ be the control block.  The ledger residualizes $Y$, $T$, and $X_Q$ against $Z$, then computes the joint mediation decomposition
$$
\begin{aligned}
M^{QTP} &= \frac{c-c'}{c},\\
c &= R^2(Y\sim X_Q\mid Z),\\
c' &= R^2(Y\sim X_Q\mid T,Z).
\end{aligned}
$$
The decomposition is joint in the nine $X_Q$ coordinates, with per-coordinate audit kept separate from the scalar mediation fraction.
\end{definition}

The control block has $24$ columns in all three organisms and residual rank $23$ after degeneracy handling.  The residualized design has $9$ $X_Q$ coordinates, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X)=9$, $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{XT})=10$ in all three organisms.  The powered-rank condition is therefore satisfied for the three ledgers.  The residual degrees of freedom after $Z$ are $3716$, $4106$, and $5792$, respectively.

\begin{theorem}[Powered statistical mediation certificate]
The carrier \texttt{BioRealityQSixTranslationMediationAbundanceLedger} certifies a powered cross-layer statistical mediation decomposition for the three matched organism ledgers. \begin{proof}
The certificate is finite.  The nine coordinate names specify the $X_Q$ block.  The row counts, control-column count, residual ranks, residual degrees of freedom, and energy values are listed explicitly above.  For each organism, the reported ranks satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde X_Q)&=9,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde T)&=1,\\
\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{XT})&=10.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the scalar mediator is not collapsed by the residualized control design, and the joint block preserves the nine codon-residual directions together with the mediator direction.  The three recorded mediation fractions are $0.612922252793667$, $0.08397602919861291$, and $0.9491551041216973$; each is declared in-unit-interval by the computation.  These finite records are exactly the data required by the ledger predicate.
\end{proof}
