\begin{theorem}[Real terminal seal Cauchy completeness witness route]
\label{thm:real-terminal-seal-cauchy-completeness-witness}
Every terminal $\RealUp$ seal consumer that claims Cauchy-completeness data factors through $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and the witness surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-completeness-witness-namecert} before exporting completion-facing rows. The route reads finite stream windows, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance ledgers, the displayed $\CauchyCompletenessWitnessUp$ packet, and only then the local terminal Real seal.

Consequently the terminal seal may export completion-facing rows only as a finite NameCert route through those sibling objects. It does not claim quotient stream equality, selected host limits, countable choice, trichotomy, detached completeness of $\RealUp$, or an ambient Real completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a terminal $\RealUp$ seal consumer that claims Cauchy-completeness data. The RealUp L10 input lattice already routes terminal sealing through the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger before the local seal is read.

Next insert the witness chapter. The carrier and Real-seal route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-completeness-witness-namecert} bind the same stream, regular-sequence, dyadic, and Real faces as one displayed completion-witness packet, so a completion-facing consumer must pass through that witness surface before it can treat the seal as carrying Cauchy-completeness data.

The final export is therefore a route projection through the existing RealUp L10 input lattice. Since the route contains only finite stream windows, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerances, the displayed witness packet, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows, it cannot introduce quotient equality, selected limits, countable choice, trichotomy, detached $\RealUp$ completeness, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Real terminal seal no completeness]
\label{thm:real-terminal-seal-no-completeness}
Passing a terminal $\RealUp$ seal through the
$\CauchyCompletenessWitnessUp$ route supplies only finite witness rows:
$\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$
tolerance ledgers, the displayed witness packet, componentwise $\hsame$,
displayed $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and local $\NameCert$ rows. It does not upgrade
$\RealUp$ to an ambient completeness theorem and does not assert quotient
stream equality, selected host limits, countable choice, trichotomy, detached
Real completeness, or a host completion object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply
\autoref{thm:real-terminal-seal-cauchy-completeness-witness}. The cited route
places every completion-facing consumer behind the finite StreamName,
RegSeqRat, DyadicRatCore, and CauchyCompletenessWitness rows before the local
Real seal is exported.

The Real roadmap rule keeps the claim off the ambient statement that
$\RealUp$ is complete. The terminal seal may carry witness rows for a
completion-facing consumer, but those rows are part of a displayed NameCert
route rather than a theorem about all Real names.

The exclusions in the theorem statement are exactly the coordinates absent
from the route: quotient stream equality, selected limits, countable choice,
trichotomy, detached Real completeness, and host completion objects. Since
none is present in the finite witness packet, no Real completeness claim is
created.
\end{proof}
