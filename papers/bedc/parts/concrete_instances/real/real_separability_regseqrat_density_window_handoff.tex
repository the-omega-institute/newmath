\begin{theorem}[RealSeparability RegSeqRat density-window handoff]
\label{thm:real-separability-regseqrat-density-window-handoff}
Every $\RealSeparabilityUp$ density read consumed by a $\RealUp$ or completion-facing route factors through a finite $\StreamNameUp$ window, a $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ approximant row, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and the regular-Cauchy dense embedding route before the consumer may read the separability surface. The handoff is the displayed finite route
$$
\StreamNameUp,\DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
together with the regular-Cauchy dense embedding certificate; it exports no quotient stream equality, selected host dense subset, countable-choice selector, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted $\RealSeparabilityUp$ packet through the existing schedule dependency of \autoref{thm:real-separability-regseqrat-schedule-dependency}. The density request first opens a finite $\StreamNameUp$ window and reads a $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row at that window.

The $\RegSeqRatUp$ row then supplies the regular rational readback attached to the same finite window. This is the only route by which a rational approximant reaches the terminal $\RealUp$ seal in the separability packet.

Finally use the dense embedding route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-dense-embedding-namecert}. A completion-facing consumer receives the already displayed regular-Cauchy dense embedding certificate after the window, dyadic, and readback rows have been fixed.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected host dense subsets, countable-choice selectors, or ambient completeness, every such density read remains inside the finite density-window handoff.
\end{proof}
