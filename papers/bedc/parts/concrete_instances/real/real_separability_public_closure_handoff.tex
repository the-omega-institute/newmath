\begin{theorem}[RealSeparability public closure handoff]
\label{thm:real-separability-public-closure-handoff}
Every public $\RealSeparabilityUp$ countable-dense read is exported through the displayed L10 source route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\quad \StreamNameUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp
$$
inside the separability packet. The handoff may expose dyadic tolerances, finite stream windows, regular rational readback, terminal Real seals, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming, but it does not expose host enumerations of completed reals, selected dense points, quotient real equality, countable choice, completed-real compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the countable dense route of \autoref{lem:real-separability-countable-dense-route} and the public status route of \autoref{thm:real-separability-public-status-route}. Together they force the density consumer to pass through the displayed finite density rows before any public Real-facing read is available.

The dyadic tolerance row is the first public source row. It controls the approximation window and keeps the density read inside the finite $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger rather than inside a host enumeration.

The stream and regular-rational rows then expose the inspected finite windows and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback. Only after those rows are displayed may the consumer inspect the terminal $\RealUp$ seal.

Thus the public handoff is the finite route through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$. Since no row contains a selected dense point, host enumeration, quotient equality, countable-choice selector, compactness theorem, or ambient completeness principle, those objects cannot be exported by the public separability surface.
\end{proof}
