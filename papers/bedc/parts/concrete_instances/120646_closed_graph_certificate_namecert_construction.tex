\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ClosedGraphCertificateUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-closed-graph-certificate-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Banach's closed graph theorem tradition and the Bishop--Bridges reading of metric completeness, the $\ClosedGraphCertificateUp$ packet records graph-closedness as finite BEDC carrier data before it is consumed by the theorem-level route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closed-graph-theorem-namecert}. It sits over the metric observation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the complete-metric boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, the metric-completion interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, the Banach source row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-namecert}, and the finite StreamName, RegSeqRat, and Real readback route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The certificate names the displayed graph-closedness surface itself, not the surrounding boundedness conclusion, open mapping theorem, bounded inverse theorem, quotient function space, or host-level graph equality.

\begin{definition}[Closed graph certificate carrier]
\label{def:closed-graph-certificate-carrier}
A $\ClosedGraphCertificateUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(X,Y,F,G,Q,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source metric or Banach row, read through $\MetricUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, or $\BanachUp$ observations.
\item $Y$ is the target metric or Banach row, read through the same displayed complete-metric vocabulary.
\item $F$ is the finite function-action row, containing the inspected source addresses and their displayed target addresses.
\item $G$ is the graph-membership row, recording the finite source--target pairs accepted as graph data for $F$.
\item $Q$ is the graph-closedness witness row, recording the sequential test: a scheduled source route converges in $X$, its graph targets converge in $Y$, graph membership persists along the schedule, and the target limit is accepted as the graph value for the source limit.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row selecting the finite source, target, graph, and convergence windows used by the test.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for rational metric and convergence estimates on the scheduled windows.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for distance and bound reads consumed by metric or completion-facing consumers.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source, target, function action, graph membership, graph-closedness witness, schedule, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from metric and completion admission through graph inspection, sequential closure, and real-seal handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ClosedGraphCertificateUp$, $\ClosedGraphTheoremUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedGraphCertificateUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a boundedness theorem, an open-mapping theorem, a bounded-inverse theorem, host graph equality, selected limits, quotient function spaces, or ambient completeness beyond the displayed rows $X$ and $Y$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closed graph certificate NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:closed-graph-certificate-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\ClosedGraphCertificateUp$ carrier $K=(X,Y,F,G,Q,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedGraphCertificateUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, source and target metric readback, function-action exposure, graph-membership exactness, sequential graph-closedness, finite schedule and rational readback, real-seal non-escape, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Each obligation is read through the finite route
$$
X,Y,F,G,Q,S,R,E,H,C,P,N .
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:closed-graph-certificate-carrier}. The displayed rows are exactly $X,Y,F,G,Q,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$, so carrier admission and local naming are immediate readings of the packet.

Read the analytic rows first. The source and target rows $X,Y$ supply metric or Banach observations, $F$ supplies the inspected function action, and $G$ supplies the graph-membership pairs accepted for that action.

The graph-closedness row $Q$ then supplies the sequential test. It can be inspected only along the windows selected by $S$, the rational estimates read by $R$, and the real-facing seals in $E$.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since no further coordinate remains, the listed obligations exhaust the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedGraphCertificateUp}$ surface and no theorem-level boundedness conclusion is exported by the certificate alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed graph certificate sequential route]
\label{thm:closed-graph-certificate-sequential-route}
Every completion-facing or theorem-facing consumer of an accepted $\ClosedGraphCertificateUp$ carrier
$$
K=(X,Y,F,G,Q,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
must read graph-closedness through the sequential route
$$
X,Y,F,G,S,R,E,Q,H,C,P,N .
$$
The route may feed the $\ClosedGraphTheoremUp$ packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closed-graph-theorem-namecert}, but it does not by itself assert local boundedness, continuity, a host graph equality, a quotient function-space equality, selected limits, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the graph rows. The consumer sees the function-action row $F$ and graph-membership row $G$ only as displayed finite source--target observations.

Next read the sequential closure data. The schedule $S$ selects the source and target windows, the readback row $R$ supplies rational metric estimates, and the seal row $E$ carries the metric values into a Real-facing read. The row $Q$ accepts the limit graph value only along that same scheduled route.

Use the closed-graph theorem sibling only after this route is visible. \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closed-graph-theorem-namecert} may consume the certificate as its graph-closedness input, while the certificate remains the smaller carrier-level surface named by \autoref{def:closed-graph-certificate-carrier}.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are then supplied by $H,C,P,N$. None of those structural rows adds a boundedness theorem, a quotient function-space object, or a selected host limit, so the sequential graph-closedness route is exactly the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}
