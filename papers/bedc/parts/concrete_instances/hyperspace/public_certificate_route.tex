\begin{theorem}[Hyperspace public certificate route]
\label{thm:hyperspace-public-certificate}
The public $\HyperspaceUp$ interface exports exactly the compact-metric source rows, finite compact-subset net schedules, directed Hausdorff-distance and Vietoris handoff rows, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ naming already visible in the scoped packet. A public reader may consume the route
$$
\CompactMetricUp,\quad
\HausdorffDistanceUp,\quad
\VietorisTopologyUp,\quad
\hsame,\quad
\Cont,\quad
\Pkg,\quad
\NameCert
$$
only through those displayed rows, and receives no quotient hyperspace, host powerset topology, selected compact representative, selected nearest point, open-cover compactness proof, or ambient complete-hyperspace object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted $\HyperspaceUp$ packet through \autoref{def:hyperspace-carrier}. The compact source, finite subset-net schedules, directed coverage ledgers, tolerance readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows are the only coordinates available to a public consumer.

Use \autoref{thm:hyperspace-scoped-closure-route}. It already routes compact-subset, directed Hausdorff, Vietoris, and completion-facing reads through the same compact-source and finite-net dependency lattice before any downstream surface is public.

Finally apply the downstream dependency and Vietoris handoff theorems, \autoref{thm:hyperspace-vietoris-downstream-dependency} and \autoref{thm:hyperspace-vietoris-handoff}. They identify the public Hausdorff-distance and Vietoris handoff rows with the displayed finite ledgers, so the excluded quotient, host-topological, selected-representative, and open-cover objects have no public coordinate.
\end{proof}
