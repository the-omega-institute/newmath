\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ArnoldiIterationUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-arnoldi-iteration-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Arnoldi's 1951 finite orthogonalization method and the standard Krylov-subspace treatment of matrix computations, the $\ArnoldiIterationUp$ packet records the finite basis-and-Hessenberg certificate produced from one displayed operator and one seed vector. It sits after the Krylov carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-krylov-subspace-namecert}, the matrix carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, the vector-space carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-vecspace-namecert}, the linear-map carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-linearmap-namecert}, and the Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gram-schmidt-namecert}. The packet names only a finite iteration ledger; it does not assert floating-point stability, Ritz-value convergence, a chosen host eigenspace, or an ambient equality between the Hessenberg row and the original operator.

\begin{definition}[ArnoldiIteration carrier]
\label{def:arnoldi-iteration-carrier}
An $\ArnoldiIterationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(K,V,M,L,G,q_0,m,Q,H_{\mathrm{hes}},R,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\KrylovSubspaceUp$ row generated by the displayed operator and seed vector.
\item $V$ is the $\VecSpaceUp$ row carrying the finite vector operations and scalar action used by the iteration.
\item $M$ is the common $\MatrixUp/\LinearMapUp$ row for the operator action inspected at every step.
\item $L$ is the finite ledger of raw Krylov iterates before orthogonalization.
\item $G$ is the $\GramSchmidtUp$ handoff row used to orthogonalize the raw ledger inside the displayed vector-space row.
\item $q_0$ is the normalized seed row.
\item $m$ is the finite step bound.
\item $Q=(q_0,\ldots,q_m)$ is the displayed orthonormal vector ledger.
\item $H_{\mathrm{hes}}$ is the upper-Hessenberg coefficient row recording how each $Mq_j$ is expressed against the displayed vector ledger and the next residual.
\item $R$ is the residual row recording the finite next-vector tests and the possible terminal breakdown boundary.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for all displayed rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the seed request through Krylov generation, Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization, coefficient extraction, residual testing, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ArnoldiIterationUp$, $\KrylovSubspaceUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\VecSpaceUp$, $\LinearMapUp$, $\GramSchmidtUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ArnoldiIterationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for an infinite Krylov limit, a numerical roundoff model, a selected spectral basis, a host orthogonal projector, or a convergence theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ArnoldiIteration NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:arnoldi-iteration-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ArnoldiIterationUp$ carrier
$$
A=(K,V,M,L,G,q_0,m,Q,H_{\mathrm{hes}},R,T,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ArnoldiIterationUp}$ surface is exhausted by six obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the Krylov, vector-space, matrix, linear-map, Gram--Schmidt, seed, step, orthonormal-vector, Hessenberg, residual, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item same-action generation, requiring the raw ledger $L$ and the Krylov row $K$ to use the same operator row $M$ and the same seed row $q_0$;
\item orthogonalization handoff, requiring the vector ledger $Q$ to be read only through the displayed $\GramSchmidtUp$ row $G$ over $V$;
\item Hessenberg assembly, requiring $H_{\mathrm{hes}}$ to record only the finite coefficients obtained by projecting each displayed $Mq_j$ against $Q$ and its next residual row;
\item residual boundary, requiring a terminal row in $R$ before a consumer treats a shortened run as a completed finite certificate;
\item ledger exhaustion, requiring downstream Lanczos, Krylov spectral, projection, or Ritz-data consumers to read only the displayed rows before citing the packet.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation for $\ArnoldiIterationUp$ is stored outside those six rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:arnoldi-iteration-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes the Krylov, vector-space, operator, raw-ledger, Gram--Schmidt, seed, step, vector, Hessenberg, residual, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Read the generation and orthogonalization rows next. The raw ledger $L$ is tied to the same $K$ and $M$ rows, while the displayed vector ledger $Q$ is admitted only through the Gram--Schmidt handoff row $G$ over $V$.

The Hessenberg row is not an independent host matrix. It is assembled from the finite coefficient reads of $Mq_j$ against $Q$ and the residual row $R$, so every coefficient remains inside the displayed iteration packet.

Finally $T,C,P,N$ preserve and name the same rows. Since the carrier has no coordinate for spectral convergence, roundoff analysis, a host projector, or an infinite Krylov limit, the listed obligations exhaust the local certificate surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ArnoldiIteration Krylov--Hessenberg handoff]
\label{thm:arnoldi-iteration-krylov-hessenberg-handoff}
Every accepted $\ArnoldiIterationUp$ carrier canonically exposes a finite handoff from the $\KrylovSubspaceUp$ row $K$ to the upper-Hessenberg row $H_{\mathrm{hes}}$ through
$$
K \to L \to G \to Q \to H_{\mathrm{hes}} \to R
$$
with $V,M,T,C,P,N$ visible. The handoff preserves the displayed matrix and linear-map action, vector-space operations, Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization, coefficient ledger, residual boundary, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. It does not add a Lanczos tridiagonalization, a spectral approximation theorem, a host diagonalization, or a convergence claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier row $K$ in \autoref{def:arnoldi-iteration-carrier}. It is already the finite Krylov packet generated by the same seed and operator rows used by the Arnoldi ledger.

Follow the finite route through $L,G,Q,H_{\mathrm{hes}},R$. The raw Krylov ledger is orthogonalized by the displayed Gram--Schmidt row, the vector ledger supplies the coefficient reads, and the residual row records whether the next-vector test continues or terminates.

The structural rows $T,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the route without adding any host-level spectral object. Thus the public handoff is exactly the finite Krylov-to-Hessenberg route stated above.
\end{proof}
