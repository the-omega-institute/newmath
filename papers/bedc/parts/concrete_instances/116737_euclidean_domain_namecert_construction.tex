Following Bourbaki and Lang, the $\EuclideanDomainUp$ packet records the traditional division-with-remainder interface as finite BEDC NameCert data over a displayed ring carrier. It sits after the algebraic operation rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert}, the ideal membership and absorption surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ideal-namecert}, and the Euclidean backtracking witness of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bezout-identity-namecert}. The packet names the carrier, the divisor admissibility row, the Euclidean measure row, the quotient and remainder rows, the strict descent check, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not import a principal-ideal theorem, a selected gcd normalization, host quotient equality, Noetherian induction, or a classical field-of-fractions argument.

\begin{definition}[Euclidean domain carrier]
\label{def:euclidean-domain-carrier}
A $\EuclideanDomainUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(R,D,\nu,Q,M,I,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the consumed $\RingUp$ row, naming the carrier, zero, one, addition, additive inverse, multiplication, and classifier used by the division read.
\item $D$ is the nonzero-divisor admissibility row, recording that division requests are made only for displayed divisors outside the zero row.
\item $\nu$ is the Euclidean measure row, read as a value in a displayed well-founded comparison ledger.
\item $Q$ is the quotient row for a requested pair $(a,b)$ with $b$ admitted by $D$.
\item $M$ is the remainder row for the same request.
\item $I$ is the $\IdealUp$ dependency row, exposing the ring-indexed membership and absorption surface consumed by ideal-facing consequences.
\item $B$ is the $\BezoutIdentityUp$ backtracking row reached by iterating the displayed descent trace.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $R,D,\nu,Q,M,I,B,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a division request through the quotient, remainder, descent, ideal, and Bezout rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\EuclideanDomainUp$, $\RingUp$, $\IdealUp$, $\BezoutIdentityUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\EuclideanDomainUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these displayed rows. It has no coordinate for host quotient equality, selected gcd normalization, a principal-ideal theorem, Noetherian induction, field-of-fractions construction, or ambient classical Euclidean-domain schema.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Euclidean domain NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:euclidean-domain-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\EuclideanDomainUp$ carrier
$$
E=(R,D,\nu,Q,M,I,B,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\EuclideanDomainUp}$ surface consists exactly of ring-source admission, nonzero-divisor admission, Euclidean-measure exposure, quotient-row exposure, remainder-row exposure, strict descent for nonzero remainders, ideal dependency exposure, Bezout backtracking exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every division read must expose $R,D,\nu,Q,M,I,B,H,C,P,N$ before any ideal-facing or Bezout-facing consumer can use the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:euclidean-domain-carrier}. The rows $R,D,\nu,Q,M,I,B,H,C,P,N$ are the complete visible inventory, so the ring operations and classifier are read through the consumed $\RingUp$ surface before any division request is admitted.

For a request with divisor admitted by $D$, the quotient row $Q$ and remainder row $M$ are displayed together. The Euclidean measure row $\nu$ supplies the comparison ledger used to state that a nonzero remainder descends below the divisor.

The ideal and Bezout rows are dependency surfaces, not consequences imported from outside the packet. The $\IdealUp$ row $I$ exposes membership and absorption data over the same ring source, while the $\BezoutIdentityUp$ row $B$ records the finite backtracking route reached by iterating the displayed descent.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no row names host quotient equality, selected gcd normalization, a principal-ideal theorem, Noetherian induction, a field of fractions, or an ambient classical schema, none of those objects belongs to the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Euclidean domain division descent]
\label{thm:euclidean-domain-division-descent}
Every accepted $\EuclideanDomainUp$ carrier exposes division with remainder as the finite route
$$
R,D,\nu,Q,M \longmapsto I,B,H,C,P,N .
$$
For each admitted divisor request, the quotient and remainder rows are read under the ring classifier from $R$; if the remainder is not the displayed zero row, the measure row $\nu$ records a strict descent comparison for that remainder. Iterating only these displayed descent rows may feed the Bezout backtracking row $B$ and the ideal dependency row $I$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a division request admitted by $D$. The ring row $R$ supplies the operations and classifier used to read the requested dividend, divisor, quotient, and remainder.

The quotient and remainder are the rows $Q$ and $M$. The measure row $\nu$ is the only row that compares a nonzero remainder with its divisor, so descent cannot be replaced by a host termination argument or by an external Euclidean algorithm.

The iteration route is then confined to the displayed packet. The Bezout-facing row $B$ may be read only after the descent trace is visible, and the ideal-facing row $I$ may be read only as an $\IdealUp$ dependency over the same ring row.

Transport and replay through $H$ and $C$, with provenance and naming from $P$ and $N$, preserve this finite route. Therefore the division-descent surface exports division data and its finite downstream dependencies without exporting host quotient equality, selected gcd normalization, a principal-ideal theorem, Noetherian induction, or field-of-fractions reasoning.
\end{proof}
