\chapter{Birkhoff Distributive Lattice NameCert Construction}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-birkhoff-distributive-lattice-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Birkhoff and Davey--Priestley, the $\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp$ packet records the finite representation surface for distributive lattices before any Stone-style, Boolean, or order-facing consumer may read the object as a represented lattice. The packet sits after the order carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-poset-namecert}, the meet-join surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lattice-namecert}, the bounded endpoint rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bounded-lattice-namecert}, the Boolean comparison envelope of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-booleanalgebra-namecert}, and the prime-filter dual route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stoneduality-namecert}. It is a finite NameCert reconstruction of the Birkhoff distributive-lattice handoff, not an import of host algebraic representation theorems, choice-selected prime filters, quotient lattice equality, or classical completeness.

\begin{definition}[Birkhoff distributive lattice carrier]
\label{def:birkhoff-distributive-lattice-carrier}
A $\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(P,L,E,J,M,D,F,S,H,C,Q,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $P$ is the $\PosetUp$ order row naming the displayed carrier order.
\item $L$ is the $\LatticeUp$ meet-join row over the same carrier.
\item $E$ is the $\BoundedLatticeUp$ endpoint row exposing the displayed bottom and top entries.
\item $J$ is the finite join-irreducible ledger used by the representation read.
\item $M$ is the finite meet-irreducible ledger used for the dual read.
\item $D$ is the distributivity witness row, read only through the displayed meet and join coordinates.
\item $F$ is the finite down-set representation row attached to $J$ and the order row $P$.
\item $S$ is the $\StoneDualityUp$ and $\BooleanAlgebraUp$ sibling handoff row for Boolean comparison and prime-filter inspection.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $P,L,E,J,M,D,F,S$ and the structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for opening the order, lattice, endpoint, irreducible, representation, and sibling handoff rows.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp$, $\PosetUp$, $\LatticeUp$, $\BoundedLatticeUp$, $\BooleanAlgebraUp$, $\StoneDualityUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier stores no host lattice quotient, no choice-selected prime ideal, no ambient powerset equality, no classical completeness theorem, and no Boolean algebra replacement for the displayed distributive-lattice rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff distributive lattice NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:birkhoff-distributive-lattice-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp$ carrier $B=(P,L,E,J,M,D,F,S,H,C,Q,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, order exposure through $P$, meet-join exposure through $L$, bounded endpoint exposure through $E$, finite irreducible ledgers $J$ and $M$, distributivity inspection through $D$, finite down-set representation through $F$, Boolean and Stone sibling handoff through $S$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $Q$, and local naming through $N$.

No accepted read may replace these rows by host distributive-lattice equality, a selected prime-filter family, classical Stone representation, unbounded ideal completion, or an ambient Boolean algebra quotient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:birkhoff-distributive-lattice-carrier}. Its algebraic rows are the order row $P$, the lattice row $L$, the endpoint row $E$, the irreducible ledgers $J,M$, the distributivity row $D$, the representation row $F$, and the sibling route $S$; its structural rows are $H,C,Q,N$.

The order-facing obligations are supplied by the $\PosetUp$, $\LatticeUp$, and $\BoundedLatticeUp$ sibling chapters. They make the carrier order, meet-join operations, and endpoints visible before any representation read is consumed.

The representation-facing obligations then read $J,M,D,F$ as finite ledgers. Distributivity is inspected through the displayed meet and join rows, while the down-set row remains attached to the finite irreducible ledger rather than becoming an ambient powerset quotient.

Finally the Boolean and Stone sibling handoff $S$ is only a route through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-booleanalgebra-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stoneduality-namecert}. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that finite route, so the packet exports the listed NameCert surface and no host representation theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff distributive lattice sibling handoff]
\label{thm:birkhoff-distributive-lattice-sibling-handoff}
Every order-facing, Boolean-facing, or Stone-facing consumer of an accepted $\BirkhoffDistributiveLatticeUp$ carrier must first factor through
$$
\PosetUp \longrightarrow \LatticeUp \longrightarrow \BoundedLatticeUp \longrightarrow \BooleanAlgebraUp \longrightarrow \StoneDualityUp
$$
as displayed sibling rows attached to $P,L,E,S$. The finite irreducible ledgers $J,M$, distributivity row $D$, down-set representation row $F$, componentwise transport $H$, replay $C$, provenance $Q$, and local name $N$ remain visible throughout the handoff.

The route does not claim a general representation theorem for all host lattices, a choice-selected spectrum, quotient equality of down-sets, classical prime-filter separation, or Boolean replacement of the original carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:birkhoff-distributive-lattice-namecert-obligations}. They require the order, lattice, bounded endpoint, irreducible, distributivity, representation, sibling handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows to be visible together.

For order-facing consumers, project $P,L,E$ and use the sibling chapters \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-poset-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lattice-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bounded-lattice-namecert}. These rows expose order, meet, join, bottom, and top data before a representation row can be read.

For Boolean-facing and Stone-facing consumers, project $S$ together with $J,M,D,F$. The Boolean and Stone sibling chapters provide comparison and duality routes, but the irreducible ledgers and down-set row remain part of the carrier and are not discharged by those routes.

Thus every consumer sees the displayed sibling chain and the local representation data before using the Birkhoff packet. Since no coordinate stores a host quotient, selected spectrum, prime-filter choice, or classical completeness theorem, no such endpoint is exported by the handoff.
\end{proof}
