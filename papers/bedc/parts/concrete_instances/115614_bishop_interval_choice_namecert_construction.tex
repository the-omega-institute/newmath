\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-interval-choice-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on nested interval arguments, the $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$ packet records the finite induction surface used when a closed bounded interval route is refined through lawlike dyadic windows. It sits over the interval carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closedboundedinterval-namecert}, the dyadic arithmetic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the stream-window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It differs from the finite token extraction of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-interval-choice-namecert} and the compact finite-net surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-choice-free-namecert}: the named object is the stepwise nested-window induction route, not a selected finite subcover, a chosen interval point, or an appeal to ambient compactness.

\begin{definition}[Bishop interval choice carrier]
\label{def:bishop-interval-choice-carrier}
A $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(I,D,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ source row, including the lower endpoint, upper endpoint, endpoint-order, containment, stream, readback, and Real-seal data available from that packet.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ halving and containment ledger used to refine one closed bounded interval window to its next nested subwindow.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row that records exactly the finite name coordinates inspected at each displayed induction step.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to those window coordinates.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after the closed interval, dyadic, stream, and regular-rational rows have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the interval source, dyadic ledger, stream windows, regular readbacks, Real seal, replay route, provenance row, and local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of each nested-window induction step through $I,D,S,Q,R,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$, $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopIntervalChoiceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,D,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for Heine--Borel compactness, trichotomy, selected points, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval choice NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-choice-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$ carrier
$$
B=(I,D,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BishopIntervalChoiceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ source row, dyadic nested-window refinement, $\StreamNameUp$ window exposure, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every obligation consumed by the certificate is read from $I,D,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-interval-choice-carrier}. Its only visible rows are the closed bounded interval source, dyadic refinement ledger, stream windows, regular rational readbacks, Real seal, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

The NameCert obligation surface is therefore row exact. Carrier admission reads $I$, nested-window refinement reads $D$, stream and regular-rational exposure read $S$ and $Q$, and the terminal real-name boundary reads $R$ only after those rows have been displayed.

The transport, replay, provenance, and local naming clauses are supplied by $H,C,P,N$. Since no row stores Heine--Borel compactness, trichotomy, selected points, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness, none of those objects can become a certificate obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval choice nested-window induction]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-choice-nested-window-induction}
Every accepted $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$ carrier supports induction over its displayed nested-window route: the base step reads the $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ source row $I$, and each successor step refines the current closed window using the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger $D$ before exposing the corresponding $\StreamNameUp$ window row $S$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$. The induction state remains inside $I,D,S,Q,H,C,P,N$ and does not select an interval point, finite subcover, quotient representative, trichotomy branch, countable-choice family, or ambient completion coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The base step is the source projection. It reads the displayed closed bounded interval row $I$ and no downstream seal or selected point.

For the successor step, use the dyadic ledger $D$. The ledger names the finite halving and containment data needed to pass from one displayed closed window to the next nested window.

The stream and readback rows then expose the successor window. The row $S$ records the inspected finite name coordinates, and $Q$ records the corresponding regular rational readback before any Real-facing consumer may inspect a seal.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same induction route. Thus the induction is a finite nested-window recursion inside the packet, not an invocation of finite subcover selection, quotient equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or Real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval choice Real seal boundary]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-choice-real-seal-boundary}
Every $\RealUp$ or Completion consumer of an accepted $\BishopIntervalChoiceUp$ carrier factors through the closed interval source $I$, dyadic nested-window ledger $D$, $\StreamNameUp$ window row $S$, and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$ before reading the terminal Real seal $R$. The seal exports only the displayed nested-window induction boundary and no Heine--Borel compactness, open-cover compactness, selected finite subcover, selected real point, trichotomy, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-interval-choice-namecert-obligations}. They force every accepted consumer read to begin from the same finite rows $I,D,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N$.

Apply nested-window induction. The consumer route reaches $S$ and $Q$ only by following the source interval and dyadic refinement rows step by step, so the Real seal cannot be detached from the displayed induction boundary.

The seal row $R$ is therefore downstream of closed interval source, dyadic refinement, stream exposure, and regular-rational readback. The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve this boundary as transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Since the carrier contains no coordinate for compactness selection, selected points, trichotomy, quotient equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness, the Real and Completion consumer receives exactly the displayed nested-window seal boundary.
\end{proof}
