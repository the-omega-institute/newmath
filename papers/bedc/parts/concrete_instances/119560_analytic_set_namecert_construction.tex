\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\AnalyticSetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-analytic-set-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Suslin and Luzin in descriptive set theory, the $\AnalyticSetUp$ packet records analytic subsets as finite BEDC data for a projection-coded tree over Baire-space names. It sits after the sequence carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bairespace-namecert}, the Borel-code hierarchy of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-borel-hierarchy-namecert}, the membership surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-setlike-namecert}, the topological source rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The chapter names the projection-code surface used by later descriptive-set and measurable-function consumers; it is not a universal classical separation theorem, a quotient point-set equality, a selected witness branch, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[AnalyticSet carrier]
\label{def:analytic-set-carrier}
An $\AnalyticSetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(X,B,T,S,P,R,H,C,G,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source $\BaireSpaceUp$ row whose finite sequence windows name candidate witnesses.
\item $B$ is the $\BorelHierarchyUp$ code row for the subset of the product space whose projection is under inspection.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ row supplying the product, cylinder, and projection-domain reads used by the Borel code.
\item $S$ is the $\SetLikeUp$ membership row recording finite positive and negative membership probes against the projected set.
\item $P$ is the projection-code row, recording that a displayed Baire witness window and Borel-code read are consumed before the projected membership row is visible.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ boundary row used when the source topology or later measurable-function consumers read real-valued probes.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the Baire-space, Borel-code, topology, membership, projection-code, real-boundary, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a membership request through
$$
X,B,T \longmapsto P \longmapsto S,R .
$$
\item $G$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\AnalyticSetUp$, $\BaireSpaceUp$, $\BorelHierarchyUp$, $\SetLikeUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\AnalyticSetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a selected infinite witness branch, quotient equality of subsets, classical separation, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[AnalyticSet NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:analytic-set-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\AnalyticSetUp$ carrier $A=(X,B,T,S,P,R,H,C,G,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\AnalyticSetUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, Baire-space witness-window exposure, Borel-code exposure, topology compatibility, set-membership readback, projection-code binding, real-boundary nonescape, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming through the displayed rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $A=(X,B,T,S,P,R,H,C,G,N)$. The only analytic-set data available to a consumer are the Baire-space windows, Borel-code row, topology row, membership row, projection-code row, and real-boundary row.

Read $X,B,T$ before projection membership. The Baire-space row supplies finite witness windows, the Borel hierarchy row supplies the code being projected, and the topology row supplies the product and cylinder reads needed to interpret the code.

The projection row $P$ then binds those reads to $S$. A membership consumer may inspect the projected set only through the displayed witness-window and Borel-code route, while $R$ records real-valued topological probes without adding a completion principle.

Finally $H,C,G,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no carrier coordinate stores a selected infinite branch, quotient subset equality, classical separation, countable choice, or ambient real completeness, none of those principles is part of the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[AnalyticSet Suslin projection handoff]
\label{thm:analytic-set-suslin-projection-handoff}
Every descriptive-set or measurable-function consumer of an accepted $\AnalyticSetUp$ packet receives the analytic set only through the finite projection-code route
$$
\BaireSpaceUp,\ \BorelHierarchyUp,\ \TopologyUp \longmapsto P \longmapsto \SetLikeUp,\ \RealUp
$$
with structural support $H,C,G,N$. The handoff records the Suslin projection surface as named BEDC data; it does not export a selected witness branch, a universal separation theorem, quotient equality of subsets, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier definition. The projection-code row $P$ is downstream of the Baire-space witness windows, Borel-code row, and topology rows, so a consumer cannot read projected membership before those rows have been displayed.

Apply the NameCert obligation theorem, \autoref{thm:analytic-set-namecert-obligations}. It identifies carrier admission, Baire-space exposure, Borel-code exposure, topology compatibility, membership readback, projection binding, and real-boundary nonescape as the complete local obligation surface.

The structural rows $H,C,G,N$ then preserve the same route under transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming. They do not add a branch selector, a quotient of subsets, a separation theorem, countable choice, or Real completeness.

Thus the public handoff is exactly a finite Suslin projection-code packet over the named sibling chapters. Downstream consumers may reuse that packet as a descriptive-set boundary, but any stronger classical object would require a coordinate absent from the carrier.
\end{proof}
