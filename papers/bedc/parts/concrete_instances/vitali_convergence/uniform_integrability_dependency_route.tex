\begin{theorem}[Vitali convergence uniform-integrability dependency route]
\label{thm:vitali-convergence-uniform-integrability-dependency-route}
Every accepted $\VitaliConvergenceUp$ consumer that reads the uniform-integrability row $U$ must route it through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniform-integrability-namecert}, the measurable event surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-measure-namecert}, the $L^p$ function-space boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lp-space-namecert}, and the dominated majorant handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dominated-convergence-namecert} before consuming the terminal $\RealUp$ seal. The route keeps the displayed rows
$$
M,I,F,U,L,R,E,D,H,C,P,N
$$
visible, and exports no measure completion, almost-everywhere quotient equality, selected subsequence, countable choice, or detached host convergence theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:vitali-convergence-carrier}. The uniform-integrability row $U$ is available only together with the displayed measure row $M$, integral row $I$, integrand-family row $F$, convergence-in-measure window $L$, tolerance row $R$, real seal $E$, dominated-convergence handoff $D$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Read \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniform-integrability-namecert}. It supplies the tail-cutoff and uniform tail-bound surface that the Vitali packet consumes, while \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-measure-namecert} keeps the event requests attached to the same measurable carrier.

The $L^p$ boundary in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lp-space-namecert} records the norm-facing function-space read without adding representatives or quotient equality. The dominated-convergence sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dominated-convergence-namecert} is then available only through the displayed handoff row $D$.

Thus the dependency route is carrier-local: it preserves the Vitali rows and their sibling citations, but it does not turn uniform integrability into a host measure-completion theorem, a selected-subsequence principle, or an ambient convergence theorem.
\end{proof}
