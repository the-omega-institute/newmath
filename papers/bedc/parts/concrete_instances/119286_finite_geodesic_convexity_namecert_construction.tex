\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp$}

Following Bridson--Haefliger and Burago--Burago--Ivanov on geodesic metric spaces and convex subsets, the $\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface on which a displayed geodesic segment remains inside a displayed convex target. It sits over the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the segment witness of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-geodesic-metric-namecert}, the convex membership carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the CAT(0) comparison packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cat-zero-metric-namecert}, and the terminal scalar readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only endpoint, segment, convex-target, comparison, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows; it does not select global geodesics, quotient paths, ambient closed convex subsets, or real-completeness data.

\begin{definition}[Finite geodesic convexity carrier]
\label{def:finite-geodesic-convexity-carrier}
A $\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(M,E,G,V,Z,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row, exposing only the distance observations and endpoint identifiers inspected by the packet.
\item $E$ is the endpoint row, recording the two displayed endpoints and their admitted membership in the target convex carrier.
\item $G$ is the finite $\GeodesicMetricUp$ segment-witness row joining the endpoints through named segment parameters and metric-distance ledgers.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ target row, exposing the membership and convex-combination obligations that the inspected segment must respect.
\item $Z$ is the comparison ledger, optionally read through $\CatZeroMetricUp$ or a sibling metric-convexity packet when a downstream route needs curvature-facing convexity.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ readback row for segment parameters, distance budgets, and comparison tolerances; it is terminal and exports no ambient real-completeness principle.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric source, endpoints, geodesic witness, convex target, comparison ledger, scalar readback, continuation, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a convexity request through $M,E,G,V,Z,R,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\GeodesicMetricUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\CatZeroMetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,E,G,V,Z,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host path space, selected geodesic family, quotient path equality, global convex hull operator, projection selector, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite geodesic convexity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-geodesic-convexity-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp$ carrier $F=(M,E,G,V,Z,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteGeodesicConvexityUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the metric source, endpoint membership, finite geodesic segment witness, convex target row, comparison ledger, terminal real readback, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every CAT(0), Hadamard, projection, or Alexandrov consumer must read the finite segment-inside-target route through those rows before using the packet as metric-convexity data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-geodesic-convexity-carrier}. The metric and endpoint data are exactly $M$ and $E$, so the packet fixes the inspected source and target endpoints before a segment can be read.

Read the geodesic and convex rows next. The row $G$ supplies the finite segment witness, while $V$ supplies the convex target and membership obligations. The comparison row $Z$ and scalar row $R$ only annotate this same finite segment route with downstream comparison and tolerance data.

Finally inspect the structural rows. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve the same metric, endpoint, segment, convex-target, comparison, and scalar rows, while $P$ and $N$ source and name the accepted packet. Since no row stores a host path space, selected geodesic family, quotient path equality, global convex hull operator, projection selector, or ambient real-completeness principle, the displayed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}
