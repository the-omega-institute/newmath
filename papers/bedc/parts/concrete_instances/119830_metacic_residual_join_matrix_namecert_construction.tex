It sits beside the parallel-diamond frontier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metacic-parallel-diamond-frontier-namecert}, the finite-window confluence packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-window-confluence-namecert}, the residual local-join packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-parallel-diamond-residual-namecert}, the candidate-normalization confluence handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metacic-candidate-normalization-confluence-handoff-namecert}, and the confluence audit packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metacic-confluence-audit-packet-namecert}. The matrix is a concrete NameCert object for the confluence audit boundary; it is not an unconditional confluence theorem.

\begin{definition}[MetaCIC residual-join matrix carrier]
\label{def:metacic-residual-join-matrix-carrier}
A $\MetaCICResidualJoinMatrixUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
J=(P,F,L,R,K,S,A,O,H,C,G,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $P$ is the residual-substitution premise row retained by the confluence audit boundary.
\item $F$ is the $\MetaCICParallelDiamondFrontierUp$ row exposing the finite frontier of local peaks, residual premises, and obstruction rows.
\item $L$ and $R$ are the left and right residual-branch rows, each read through $\ParallelDiamondResidualUp$ data.
\item $K$ is the finite candidate-join row, recording the proposed common target and the branch-local routes to it.
\item $S$ is the finite-window confluence row, read through $\FiniteWindowConfluenceUp$ before any matrix row is exported.
\item $A$ is the candidate-normalization confluence handoff row, read through $\MetacicCandidateNormalizationConfluenceHandoffUp$ when normalization-facing consumers inspect the same matrix.
\item $O$ is the confluence-audit consumer row, read through $\MetaCICConfluenceAuditPacketUp$ after $P,F,L,R,K,S,A$ remain visible.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for premise, frontier, residual-branch, candidate-join, finite-window, handoff, audit-consumer, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a confluence query through $P,F,L,R,K,S,A,O,H$.
\item $G$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetaCICResidualJoinMatrixUp$, $\MetaCICParallelDiamondFrontierUp$, $\FiniteWindowConfluenceUp$, $\ParallelDiamondResidualUp$, $\MetacicCandidateNormalizationConfluenceHandoffUp$, $\MetaCICConfluenceAuditPacketUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetaCICResidualJoinMatrixUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $P,F,L,R,K,S,A,O,H,C,G,N$. It has no coordinate for premise-free parallel diamond, unrestricted closed Church--Rosser, subject reduction, evaluator equality, quotient syntax equality, global normalization, or kernel consistency.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC residual-join matrix NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:metacic-residual-join-matrix-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MetaCICResidualJoinMatrixUp$ carrier
$$
J=(P,F,L,R,K,S,A,O,H,C,G,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\MetaCICResidualJoinMatrixUp}$ surface consists exactly of residual-substitution premise retention through $P$, frontier exposure through $F$, two residual-branch exposures through $L,R$, candidate-join exposure through $K$, finite-window confluence exposure through $S$, candidate-normalization confluence handoff through $A$, confluence-audit consumption through $O$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, provenance through $G$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metacic-residual-join-matrix-carrier}. Its matrix rows are $P,F,L,R,K,S,A,O$, and its structural rows are $H,C,G,N$.

The retained premise row $P$ is read before the frontier row $F$ can be consumed. The two residual rows $L,R$ and the candidate row $K$ then record the local join matrix under the same finite frontier.

The finite-window row $S$ and candidate-normalization handoff row $A$ keep the matrix bounded for confluence and normalization-facing consumers. The audit row $O$ receives the matrix only after those rows remain visible.

Finally $H,C,G,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no excluded global endpoint is a carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC residual-join matrix candidate-diamond boundary]
\label{thm:metacic-residual-join-matrix-candidate-diamond-boundary}
Every candidate-diamond consumer of an accepted $\MetaCICResidualJoinMatrixUp$ packet must expose the residual-substitution premise row $P$, the $\MetaCICParallelDiamondFrontierUp$ row $F$, both $\ParallelDiamondResidualUp$ branch rows $L,R$, the candidate-join row $K$, the $\FiniteWindowConfluenceUp$ row $S$, the $\MetacicCandidateNormalizationConfluenceHandoffUp$ row $A$, and the $\MetaCICConfluenceAuditPacketUp$ row $O$ before any local diamond read is public. The read is a bounded audit-matrix route, not a premise-free parallel diamond or closed Church--Rosser theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:metacic-residual-join-matrix-namecert-obligations}. They force every accepted matrix read through the retained premise, frontier, two residual branches, candidate join, finite window, normalization handoff, audit consumer, and structural rows.

Read the residual side first. The row $P$ keeps the substitution premise visible, while $F,L,R,K$ display the finite local peak, its two residual descendants, and the candidate common target.

Read the bounded-audit side next. The row $S$ restricts the read to a finite confluence window, $A$ records the candidate-normalization handoff for the same matrix, and $O$ supplies the confluence-audit consumer row.

The rows $H,C,G,N$ only preserve that displayed route. Therefore the matrix cannot be cited as a premise-free parallel diamond, closed Church--Rosser theorem, subject-reduction theorem, evaluator equality, quotient syntax equality, global normalization theorem, or kernel-consistency certificate.
\end{proof}
