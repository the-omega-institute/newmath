\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\PackingDimensionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-packing-dimension-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Falconer and Mattila's packing-side metric dimension, the
$\PackingDimensionUp$ packet records the finite disjoint-ball schedules that
measure a compact metric object from the separated-cover side. It is the
packing neighbour of the Hausdorff, Minkowski, and Assouad metric-dimension
certificates in
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hausdorff-dimension-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-minkowski-dimension-namecert}, and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-assouad-dimension-namecert}. The carrier is
grounded in the metric and compact metric surfaces of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert} and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the dyadic radius
ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the
threshold readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It names
only finite packing evidence; it does not assert a host supremum over all
packings, quotient equality of real names, selected limits, or ambient
completeness.

\begin{definition}[Packing dimension carrier]
\label{def:packing-dimension-carrier}
A $\PackingDimensionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
P=(M,K,C,R,N,H,Q,S,T,U,L,O,A)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ distance surface used to test separated centers.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ finite-net source for the compact metric
object whose packing behaviour is being inspected.
\item $C$ is the finite list of packing centers, each read through the compact
metric carrier rather than through a host point set.
\item $R$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ radius ledger, with one positive dyadic
radius attached to each packing window.
\item $N$ records the disjointness checks: the closed metric balls centered at
the displayed entries of $C$ and using the radii in $R$ are pairwise separated
inside the metric row $M$.
\item $H$ records the Hausdorff comparison row, naming the covering-side
threshold from $\HausdorffDimensionUp$ that is compared with the packing
schedule.
\item $Q$ records the Minkowski comparison row, naming the finite covering
and box-counting ledger from $\MinkowskiDimensionUp$.
\item $S$ records the Assouad comparison row, naming the local scale
comparison from $\AssouadDimensionUp$.
\item $T$ is the $\RealUp$ threshold readback for the displayed exponent
bound associated to the packing schedule.
\item $U$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $M,K,C,R,N,H,Q,S,T$
and for the structural rows.
\item $L$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the same finite route from
metric data through disjoint packing centers, dyadic radii, comparison rows,
and the real threshold.
\item $O$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PackingDimensionUp$, $\MetricUp$,
$\CompactMetricUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\HausdorffDimensionUp$,
$\MinkowskiDimensionUp$, $\AssouadDimensionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$,
$\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $A$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PackingDimensionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers by transporting these displayed
metric, compact-metric, packing-center, dyadic-radius, comparison, threshold,
continuation, provenance, and local naming rows to a common finite surface. It
has no coordinate for a host supremum, arbitrary set-family quantification,
quotient real equality, selected limits, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Packing dimension NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:packing-dimension-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PackingDimensionUp$ carrier
$P=(M,K,C,R,N,H,Q,S,T,U,L,O,A)$, the local
$\NameCert_{\PackingDimensionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier
admission, separated-center admission, dyadic radius positivity, disjoint ball
checking, Hausdorff/Minkowski/Assouad comparison readback, $\RealUp$
threshold readback, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay,
package provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:packing-dimension-carrier}. The rows
$M,K,C,R,N$ expose the metric source, compact finite-net data, packing
centers, radius ledger, and pairwise disjointness checks.

Read the comparison rows $H,Q,S$ next. They place the packing schedule beside
the existing Hausdorff, Minkowski, and Assouad metric-dimension certificates
without replacing any of those carrier surfaces.

The real-facing row is $T$. It reads only the displayed threshold associated
to the finite packing schedule, so the route reaches $\RealUp$ after the
metric and radius rows have already been replayed.

Finally $U,L,O,A$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet.
Since no other carrier coordinate exists, the NameCert cannot expose host
suprema, arbitrary set-family quantification, quotient real equality,
selected limits, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Packing dimension scale-packing route]
\label{thm:packing-dimension-scale-packing-route}
Every scale-facing consumer of an accepted $\PackingDimensionUp$ carrier must
read the finite route
$$
\MetricUp\longmapsto\CompactMetricUp\longmapsto(C,R,N)\longmapsto
(\HausdorffDimensionUp,\MinkowskiDimensionUp,\AssouadDimensionUp)
\longmapsto\RealUp\longmapsto(U,L,O,A).
$$
Thus the packing-side dimension evidence is exported only after disjoint
packing centers, dyadic radii, sibling dimension comparisons, and the real
threshold have been displayed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the metric and compact metric rows. The metric row gives the
distance test, and the compact metric row gives the finite-net source through
which centers are admitted.

The packing rows $C,R,N$ are then replayed. They list the centers, attach
positive dyadic radii, and check pairwise disjointness inside the displayed
metric surface.

Next the sibling comparison rows are read. The Hausdorff row supplies the
covering-side threshold, the Minkowski row supplies the finite box-counting
ledger, and the Assouad row supplies the local scale comparison.

Only then is the $\RealUp$ threshold row $T$ available. The structural rows
$U,L,O,A$ preserve and name this order of dependency, so no scale-facing
consumer can bypass the finite packing route through a host supremum,
quotient real equality, selected limit, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
