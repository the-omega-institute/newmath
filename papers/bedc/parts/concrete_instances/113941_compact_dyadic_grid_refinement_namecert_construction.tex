\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp$ packet realizes the finite grid-refinement route of \autoref{ch:visions-compact-uniform-continuity} as concrete NameCert data. It sits over the compact finite-net surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the uniform-modulus consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformmodulus-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet records compact-net rows, dyadic-radius refinements, and tolerance-only handoff data; it does not import open-cover compactness, a host grid topology, selected mesh limits, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Compact dyadic grid-refinement carrier]
\label{def:compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-carrier}
A $\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(X,Q,\Delta,\Gamma,R,U,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the consumed $\CompactMetricUp$ row, including the displayed compact finite-net and located metric support;
\item $Q$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row carrying the input dyadic radius, half-radius, and comparison budget;
\item $\Delta$ is the finite dyadic grid row, listing cells whose centers are read from the compact finite-net surface of $X$;
\item $\Gamma$ is the refinement ledger, recording which dyadic child cells refine which parent cells and which compact-net probes they cover;
\item $R$ is the finite-net coverage row saying that every inspected source point is routed through one displayed grid cell and one compact-net center;
\item $U$ is the $\UniformModulusUp$ consumer handoff row, available only after $Q,\Delta,\Gamma,R$ have been projected;
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only through the displayed dyadic tolerance and uniform-modulus route;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,Q,\Delta,\Gamma,R,U,E$ and the support rows;
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact-net readback to dyadic subdivision, coverage, uniform-modulus handoff, and real seal;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactMetricUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier compares accepted packets only through these rows and stores no open-cover proof, external grid space, quotient real equality, selected mesh limit, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact dyadic grid-refinement NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp$ carrier
$$
G=(X,Q,\Delta,\Gamma,R,U,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the compact metric row, dyadic tolerance row, finite grid row, refinement ledger, coverage row, uniform-modulus handoff, real seal, componentwise transport, replay routes, provenance, and local naming row. The obligation route is compact finite-net readback, dyadic-radius admission, finite grid subdivision, parent-child refinement coverage, uniform-modulus consumer handoff, real-seal nonescape, and then the $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ packaging rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-carrier}. The displayed tuple is the full public surface, so an open-cover proof, host grid topology, selected mesh limit, quotient real equality, and ambient completeness are not available as extra coordinates.

Read $X$ and $Q$ first. The compact metric row supplies finite-net centers and located metric support, while the dyadic tolerance row supplies the visible radius, half-radius, and comparison budget used by every grid cell.

Next build $\Delta,\Gamma,R$. The finite grid row lists dyadic cells, the refinement ledger ties each child to a parent and to the compact-net probes it covers, and the coverage row records the inspected source-point route through one displayed cell and one compact-net center.

Finally expose $U$ and $E$. The uniform-modulus handoff consumes only the compact-net, dyadic-radius, grid, refinement, and coverage rows, and the real seal is placed after that handoff. Repacking through $H,C,P,N$ gives exactly the NameCert surface stated above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact dyadic grid-refinement finite-net route]
\label{thm:compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-finite-net-route}
Every finite-net read of an accepted $\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp$ packet factors through
$$
X,\quad Q,\quad \Delta,\quad \Gamma,\quad R.
$$
The route first consumes the compact finite-net row of $X$, then reads dyadic radii from $Q$, then exposes the displayed grid cells and refinement ledger. No consumer receives a standalone grid topology, an untracked mesh parameter, or a selected cover outside the finite BEDC rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the compact source row. The only centers available to the packet are the finite-net centers carried by $X$.

Use the dyadic tolerance row $Q$ to size the cells. Each grid cell in $\Delta$ is therefore a finite dyadic-radius row, and each parent-child relation in $\Gamma$ is checked against the same comparison budget.

The coverage row $R$ ties every inspected source point to one displayed cell and compact-net center. Since $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, package, and name these same rows, the finite-net read cannot escape to a host grid or selected cover.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact dyadic grid-refinement uniform-modulus consumer]
\label{thm:compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-uniform-modulus-consumer}
Every $\UniformModulusUp$ consumer of an accepted $\CompactDyadicGridRefinementUp$ packet reads the compact-net coverage, dyadic radius, grid subdivision, and refinement rows before it may consume the uniform-modulus handoff row $U$. The subsequent $\RealUp$ seal $E$ is available only downstream of $U$ and the displayed $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and local $\NameCert$ rows. Thus the consumer receives finite compact-net, dyadic-radius, and uniform-modulus handoff data, not an ambient Heine--Cantor theorem or host real-completeness argument.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:compact-dyadic-grid-refinement-finite-net-route}. It fixes the finite route through $X,Q,\Delta,\Gamma,R$ before any uniform-modulus read is exposed.

The row $U$ is downstream of that route in the carrier. It therefore reads a tolerance-only modulus handoff from displayed compact-net and dyadic-grid data rather than from a selected external modulus.

The real seal $E$ comes after $U$. Transport and replay through $H$ and $C$, together with provenance $P$ and local naming $N$, preserve that order, so no consumer can bypass the finite grid-refinement packet to reach ambient completeness or host real equality.
\end{proof}
