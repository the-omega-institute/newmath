Following Doob, the $\BoundedStoppingTimeUp$ packet names the finite stopping interface that sits between the stopping-decision surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stoppingtime-namecert} and the stopped-value surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-optionalstopping-namecert}. It reads martingale windows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-martingale-namecert}, probability sources from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert}, and random-variable endpoint rows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-randomvar-namecert}. The packet records the displayed finite bound, filtration-window visibility, and decision ledger needed before optional stopping or concentration consumers may read a stopped value; it does not assert an unbounded stopping theorem, almost-sure quotient equality, uniform integrability, or measure completion.

\begin{definition}[Bounded stopping time carrier]
\label{def:bounded-stopping-time-carrier}
A $\BoundedStoppingTimeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(T,b,F,D,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the source $\StoppingTimeUp$ row whose decisions are inspected.
\item $b$ is the finite bound row, naming the displayed horizon before any stopped value is read.
\item $F$ is the filtration-window visibility ledger, recording that each decision of $T$ used before $b$ is visible in the corresponding finite filtration window.
\item $D$ is the decision ledger, listing the finite branch outcomes up to $b$ and the first displayed stopping branch when one is present.
\item $V$ is the stopped-value handoff row consumed by $\OptionalStoppingUp$, tying the selected branch of $D$ to the corresponding $\RandomVarUp$ endpoint row inside the martingale window.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $T,b,F,D,V$ and the structural certificate rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a bounded stopping request through the same stopping-time, bound, filtration-window, decision-ledger, and stopped-value rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BoundedStoppingTimeUp$, $\StoppingTimeUp$, $\OptionalStoppingUp$, $\MartingaleUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\RandomVarUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BoundedStoppingTimeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,b,F,D,V,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an unbounded stopping index, optional-stopping expectation theorem, almost-sure quotient, completed filtration, uniform-integrability hypothesis, or ambient measure-completion carrier.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded stopping time NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bounded-stopping-time-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BoundedStoppingTimeUp$ carrier
$$
B=(T,b,F,D,V,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BoundedStoppingTimeUp}$ surface consists exactly of source stopping-time exposure through $T$, finite-bound exposure through $b$, filtration-window visibility through $F$, decision-ledger exactness through $D$, stopped-value handoff through $V$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bounded-stopping-time-carrier}. The rows $T,b,F,D,V$ are the only mathematical rows available to a bounded stopping consumer.

The source stopping-time row $T$ supplies the finite decision surface, and the bound row $b$ cuts that surface to the displayed window. The filtration ledger $F$ records that each inspected decision is visible at the corresponding finite stage.

The decision ledger $D$ then identifies the bounded branch data consumed by the stopped-value handoff $V$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that same finite route. Since the carrier contains no unbounded stopping index, optional-stopping expectation theorem, quotient event equality, completed filtration, uniform-integrability row, or measure-completion row, the listed rows are exactly the local naming obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded stopping time optional-stopping handoff]
\label{thm:bounded-stopping-time-optional-stopping-handoff}
Every $\OptionalStoppingUp$ consumer of an accepted $\BoundedStoppingTimeUp$ carrier
$$
B=(T,b,F,D,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
must read the stopped-value handoff through the finite route
$$
\StoppingTimeUp,\ \BoundedStoppingTimeUp,\ \MartingaleUp,\ \ProbSpaceUp,\ \RandomVarUp,\ \BHist,\ \hsame,\ \Cont,\ \Pkg,\ \NameCert .
$$
The handoff exposes the source stopping row, displayed bound, filtration-window ledger, bounded decision ledger, and stopped-value row before any optional-stopping packet can inspect expectation-facing data. It exports no unbounded stopping branch, no limiting stopping theorem, no almost-sure quotient, and no completed-filtration carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with \autoref{thm:bounded-stopping-time-namecert-obligations}. It forces any consumer to display $T,b,F,D,V,H,C,P,N$ before the local name is consumed.

The optional-stopping side reads only the handoff row $V$ after the finite route has been displayed. The row $V$ is tied to a branch in $D$, to the finite bound $b$, and to the visible filtration windows $F$ over the source stopping-time row $T$.

The martingale, probability, and random-variable rows are therefore consumed only as the finite source data named by $P$ and replayed through $C$. Because the carrier inventory excludes unbounded stopping branches, limiting stopping theorems, almost-sure quotient equality, and completed filtrations, the handoff supplies exactly the bounded-window interface required by $\OptionalStoppingUp$.
\end{proof}
