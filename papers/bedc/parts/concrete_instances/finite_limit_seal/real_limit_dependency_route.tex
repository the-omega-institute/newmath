\begin{theorem}[Finite limit seal real dependency route]
\label{thm:finite-limit-seal-real-dependency-route}
Every public $\FiniteLimitSealUp$ seal consumes its real-facing rows in the displayed dependency order
$$
\RealUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \StreamNameUp,\ \MetricCompletionUp
$$
only through the finite modulus, window, readback, tolerance, and completion-envelope rows of \autoref{def:finite-limit-seal-carrier}. A limit consumer must first read the $\StreamNameUp$ observation windows, then the $\RegSeqRatUp$ rational readback, then the $\RealUp$ seal and $\MetricCompletionUp$ envelope route before the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteLimitSealUp}$ row is public; it receives no selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, trichotomy branch, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier from \autoref{def:finite-limit-seal-carrier}. The only real-facing path is the finite route from the modulus schedule through stream windows, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance, and terminal Real seal.

Apply \autoref{thm:finite-limit-seal-route-nonescape} and \autoref{thm:finite-limit-seal-category-consumer-route}. They force completion-facing consumers to remain behind the displayed $M,W,R,D,E$ rows before any categorical or completion envelope can read the seal.

The added dependency order records the same lattice edge: $\StreamNameUp$ supplies the finite windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ supplies the rational readback, $\RealUp$ supplies the terminal seal, and $\MetricCompletionUp$ consumes that seal only through the completion route. Since the local packet has no coordinate for a selected limit or ambient completeness theorem, the dependency route is exactly the finite readback route already named by the carrier.
\end{proof}
