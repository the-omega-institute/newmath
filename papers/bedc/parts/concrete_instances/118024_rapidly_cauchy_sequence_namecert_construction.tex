It sits on the Real and Completion route through the finite windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. \begin{definition}[Rapidly Cauchy sequence carrier]
\label{def:rapidly-cauchy-sequence-carrier}
A $\RapidlyCauchySequenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$

$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window schedule through which the sequence is inspected.
\item $D$ is the fixed $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row for the dyadic tolerance schedule $n \mapsto 2^{-n}$, including the finite radius and comparison rows read at each displayed window.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback obtained from the same stream windows and dyadic tolerance rows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $S,D,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the stream schedule, dyadic schedule, regular readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from every finite precision request through $S,D,R,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RapidlyCauchySequenceUp$, $\FastCauchySequenceUp$, $\FastCauchyModulusUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.

\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an arbitrary modulus, quotient stream equality, selected limits, countable-choice schedules, host real equality, or a detached completeness principle.

\begin{theorem}[Rapidly Cauchy sequence NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:rapidly-cauchy-sequence-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RapidlyCauchySequenceUp$ carrier $K=(S,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RapidlyCauchySequenceUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed stream schedule, fixed dyadic-rate ledger, regular rational readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local name;
\item fixed-rate discipline, requiring every precision request to read the dyadic schedule row $D$ for the tolerance $2^{-n}$ before $R$ or $E$ may be consumed;
\item StreamName-to-RegSeqRat readback, requiring $S$ and $D$ to determine the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$ without an arbitrary modulus parameter;
\item Real-seal terminality, requiring $E$ to remain downstream of $S,D,R$ and to export no completed host real;
\item ledger nonescape, requiring $H,C,P,N$ to preserve only the displayed packet rows and no quotient stream, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host equality, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:rapidly-cauchy-sequence-carrier}. The packet displays exactly the stream windows, fixed dyadic-rate ledger, regular readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

For fixed-rate discipline, read a precision request. The only tolerance row in the packet is $D$, so the request must use the dyadic schedule $2^{-n}$ before the regular readback $R$ or seal row $E$ can be reached.

The readback obligation follows by the same route. The stream windows $S$ and the dyadic ledger $D$ are the only sources for $R$, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that route componentwise.

Finally, the excluded coordinates are absent from the carrier. Since $K$ has no arbitrary modulus row, quotient-stream coordinate, choice schedule, selected limit, host equality, or completeness witness, no local NameCert obligation can read one.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rapidly Cauchy dyadic schedule nonescape]
\label{thm:rapidly-cauchy-sequence-dyadic-schedule-nonescape}
Every consumer of an accepted $\RapidlyCauchySequenceUp$ carrier $K=(S,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ must read the same displayed route
$$
S \longrightarrow D \longrightarrow R \longrightarrow E
$$
with structural support from $H,C,P,N$. Hence the consumer cannot replace the fixed dyadic schedule by quotient stream equality, a selected limit, a countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:rapidly-cauchy-sequence-carrier}. A downstream read sees only the finite stream schedule $S$, the dyadic tolerance ledger $D$, the regular readback $R$, the seal row $E$, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:rapidly-cauchy-sequence-namecert-obligations}. Its fixed-rate and readback clauses require every precision request to pass through $D$ before $R$ or $E$ can be consumed.

The nonescape conclusion is then immediate by absence of coordinates. None of the listed forbidden principles is a row of $K$, and the transport, replay, provenance, and local name rows preserve only the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}
