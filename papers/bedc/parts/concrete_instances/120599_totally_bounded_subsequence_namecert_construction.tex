\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-totally-bounded-subsequence-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp$ packet records the finite subsequence-extraction surface that is visible once a metric sequence exposes tolerance-indexed finite epsilon-net selector rows. It sits between the total-boundedness carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the finite center packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-epsilon-net-namecert}, and the compact-metric consumer surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. Before any subsequence window is handed to $\StreamNameUp$ (\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}), $\RegSeqRatUp$ (\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}), or the terminal $\RealUp$ seal (\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}), the packet makes the chosen finite-net rows, window bounds, replay rows, and nonescape clauses explicit as BEDC NameCert data.

\begin{definition}[Totally bounded subsequence carrier]
\label{def:totally-bounded-subsequence-carrier}
A $\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(X,\sigma,E,P,C,D,W,H,R,S,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row carrying the point classifier, distance classifier, and metric-law ledger used by the sequence.
\item $\sigma$ is the inspected metric sequence row, read as a finite-window $\StreamNameUp$ schedule whose entries are admitted by $X$.
\item $E$ is the positive tolerance row, read through $\RatUp$ or $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ before a center selector can be used.
\item $P$ is the closed $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ of finite-net centers supplied by the $\FiniteEpsilonNetUp$ surface.
\item $C$ records, for every accepted center of $P$, the corresponding point-carrier admission in $X$.
\item $D$ records the finite-net coverage ledger: for each inspected sequence entry, one accepted center of $P$ and one displayed $\mathsf{MetricDistanceWitness}$ row bounded by $E$.
\item $W$ records the subsequence-window selector. It names a finite source window, a center branch of $P$, and the entries of $\sigma$ that remain in that branch for the next extraction stage.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,\sigma,E,P,C,D,W,R,S,N$.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a subsequence request through the same metric, tolerance, finite-net, coverage, and window rows.
\item $S$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\FiniteEpsilonNetUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these rows. It has no coordinate for countable choice, an infinite pigeonhole functional, an open-cover compactness proof, a selected limit, a host subsequence object, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Totally bounded subsequence finite-net selector]
\label{thm:totally-bounded-subsequence-finite-net-selector}
For every accepted $\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp$ carrier $Q=(X,\sigma,E,P,C,D,W,H,R,S,N)$, the finite-net selector row $W$ can be read only after the metric source $X$, sequence row $\sigma$, positive tolerance $E$, center bundle $P$, center-carrier ledger $C$, and coverage-distance ledger $D$ have been displayed. The selected branch remains a finite $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ branch with admitted centers and bounded metric-distance rows; it does not expose countable choice, an infinite pigeonhole functional, an open-cover compactness proof, a host subsequence object, or a selected $\RealUp$ limit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:totally-bounded-subsequence-carrier}. The selector coordinate $W$ is placed after the metric, sequence, tolerance, finite center bundle, center admission, and coverage-distance rows.

The finite-net data are therefore prerequisites. A branch named by $W$ can be inspected only through membership in $P$, admission in $C$, and the bounded distance rows recorded by $D$.

Transport and replay do not add a new selector source. The row $H$ moves the same displayed coordinates componentwise, and $R$ replays requests through the same ordered route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for countable choice, an infinite pigeonhole functional, open-cover compactness, host subsequences, or selected limits, none of those objects can be exported by the selector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Totally bounded subsequence L10 handoff]
\label{thm:totally-bounded-subsequence-l10-handoff}
Every $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\TotallyBoundedSubsequenceUp$ carrier must read the finite route
$$
X,\sigma,E \longmapsto P,C,D \longmapsto W,H,R,S,N
$$
before it may treat a chosen subsequence window as real-name data. Thus the consumer receives only displayed metric rows, finite epsilon-net centers, bounded coverage witnesses, the subsequence-window selector, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row. The handoff exports no countable-choice schedule, host-level subsequence, quotient stream equality, selected limit, open-cover compactness theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a downstream consumer of $Q$. By \autoref{def:totally-bounded-subsequence-carrier}, entry into the packet first exposes the metric source $X$, the inspected sequence $\sigma$, and the positive tolerance row $E$.

The finite-net selector is opened next. The closed bundle $P$ names the centers, $C$ admits those centers as points of $X$, and $D$ supplies the bounded distance rows that locate inspected sequence entries in center branches.

Apply \autoref{thm:totally-bounded-subsequence-finite-net-selector}. The window selector $W$ can only name branches already justified by $P,C,D$, and the structural rows $H,R,S,N$ preserve the same route when the window is replayed toward $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, or $\RealUp$.

Therefore the L10 consumer sees exactly the displayed finite route. Because the carrier omits countable-choice schedules, host-level subsequence objects, quotient stream equality, selected limits, open-cover compactness proofs, and ambient completeness data, the handoff cannot export any of them.
\end{proof}
