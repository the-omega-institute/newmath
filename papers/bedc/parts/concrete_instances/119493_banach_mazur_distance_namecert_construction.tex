Following Banach and Mazur's finite-dimensional distance formalism, the $\BanachMazurDistanceUp$ packet records a finite distortion comparison between two displayed normed or Banach rows as NameCert data. It sits over the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the norm carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-norm-namecert}, the Banach-facing carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-namecert}, the located-limit route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedlimit-namecert}, and the real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a displayed pair of inverse-candidate rows and a finite $\RealUp$ distortion ledger; it does not assert a host infimum, a quotient isometry class, a Hahn--Banach separation theorem, or a choice-selected optimal isomorphism.

\begin{definition}[Banach--Mazur distance carrier]
\label{def:banach-mazur-distance-carrier}
A $\BanachMazurDistanceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(X,Y,F,G,L,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source space row, read through $\NormedSpaceUp$ or $\BanachUp$ and carrying its displayed norm, metric, finite-dimensional coordinate window, and completion-facing $\LocatedLimitUp$ references when present.
\item $Y$ is the target space row, read through the same normed or Banach-facing surface and carrying its displayed norm, metric, finite-dimensional coordinate window, and located-limit references when present.
\item $F$ is the forward inverse-candidate row from $X$ to $Y$, recording the displayed finite matrix or finite linear-action table, its domain window, and its $\RealUp$ operator-bound ledger.
\item $G$ is the reverse inverse-candidate row from $Y$ to $X$, recording the displayed finite matrix or finite linear-action table, its domain window, and its $\RealUp$ operator-bound ledger.
\item $L$ is the distortion ledger, storing the two forward and reverse norm-bound seals and the displayed product bound as $\RealUp$ data.
\item $R$ is the inverse-replay row, recording that $G \circ F$ and $F \circ G$ return to the displayed source and target windows by finite coordinate checks, not by a host quotient or an ambient isometry class.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source row, target row, forward candidate, reverse candidate, distortion ledger, inverse replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from a distance-facing request through $X,Y,F,G,L,R,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BanachMazurDistanceUp$, $\NormedSpaceUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\LocatedLimitUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BanachMazurDistanceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an external infimum over all isomorphisms, a quotient isometry class, an unrestricted Hahn--Banach separation argument, a selected optimal map, or an appeal to countable choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Banach--Mazur distance classifier]

\begin{theorem}[Banach--Mazur distance NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:banach-mazur-distance-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BanachMazurDistanceUp$ carrier $D=(X,Y,F,G,L,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BanachMazurDistanceUp}$ surface consists exactly of five finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item source and target admission: $X$ and $Y$ are displayed normed or Banach-facing rows with metric and located-limit dependencies available only through their named packets;
\item inverse-candidate admission: $F$ and $G$ are the only forward and reverse rows available to the certificate;
\item distortion-ledger exactness: $L$ stores the displayed $\RealUp$ norm-bound product read from $F$ and $G$;
\item inverse replay: $R$ records the finite coordinate-window checks for $G \circ F$ and $F \circ G$;
\item structural preservation: $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same rows without adding an optimal isomorphism, host infimum, quotient isometry class, Hahn--Banach theorem, or choice principle.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:banach-mazur-distance-carrier}. The source and target rows are $X,Y$, and all norm, metric, Banach, and located-limit dependencies are read through those displayed rows.

Read the inverse-candidate rows next. The packet contains exactly $F$ and $G$ as finite forward and reverse action data, so every distance-facing request must use those two rows before consulting any distortion value.

The distortion row is $L$. It records the two operator-bound reads and their displayed $\RealUp$ product, while the inverse-replay row $R$ records the finite coordinate-window return checks.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same finite packet under transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since the carrier has no row for a host infimum, quotient isometry class, selected optimal map, Hahn--Banach separation, or countable choice, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}
