\chapter{The Golden-Mean Ruelle Zeta: A Finite Fibonacci Transfer Certificate}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-window6-golden-mean-ruelle-zeta}
\origin{ai}

The golden-mean subshift of finite type is the space of two-sided binary sequences with no two adjacent $1$s; its Artin--Mazur/Bowen--Lanford dynamical zeta function is a genuine zeta, built from periodic points and carrying a primitive-orbit Euler product. This chapter records that zeta as a finite Fibonacci transfer certificate: its reciprocal is the weighted spectral determinant $1-t-yt^2$ of the golden-mean transfer matrix, its periodic-point counts obey the Fibonacci trace recurrence, and at unit weight its poles factor over the golden ring $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ into the two golden eigenvalues. This is a real zeta theorem for an infinite dynamical system whose entire zeta is controlled by a finite Fibonacci transfer operator; it is not a statement about the Riemann zeta function and not about the Riemann $\xi$.

The finite transfer rows consume the six-window no-adjacent-one carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-golden-mean-shift-window-six-namecert} before the trace recurrence, determinant readback, primitive-orbit, or pole-factorization rows are interpreted.

\section{The Weighted Periodic-Point Zeta}
\label{sec:window6-ruelle-zeta-object}

Let $\mathrm{CycFib}_n$ be the set of cyclic no-adjacent-$1$ words of length $n$, and weight each word by $y^{\#1}$. With the weighted transfer matrix
$$
T_y=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
the weighted periodic-point count is $C_n(y)=\operatorname{tr}(T_y^n)$, and the dynamical zeta is
$$
\zeta_{\mathrm{GM}}(t,y)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{C_n(y)}{n}\,t^n\right)=\frac{1}{\det(I-tT_y)}=\frac{1}{1-t-yt^2}.
$$
At $y=1$ this is the Bowen--Lanford zeta of the golden-mean shift, with the Euler product $\prod_{d\ge1}(1-t^d)^{-\pi_d}$ over primitive orbit counts; that product is what makes this a zeta rather than a determinant in zeta clothing.

\begin{theorem}[Fibonacci trace recurrence]
\label{thm:window6-ruelle-trace-recurrence}
The weighted periodic-point polynomials obey the two-term recurrence forced by $T_y^2=T_y+yI$,
$$
C_{n+2}(y)=C_{n+1}(y)+y\,C_n(y),
$$
so at $y=1$ the counts are the Lucas trace sequence $C_n(1)=1,3,4,7,11,18,29,47,\ldots$
\end{theorem}

\section{The Finite Zeta Certificate}
\label{sec:window6-ruelle-finite-certificate}

Rather than begin with the analytic exponential, the finite content is the log-derivative window. With $\chi_y(t)=1-t-yt^2$ and $S_N(t,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_n(y)t^{n-1}$, for every $N\ge1$
$$
(1-t-yt^2)\,S_N(t,y)=1+2yt-C_{N+1}(y)\,t^N-y\,C_N(y)\,t^{N+1},
$$
equivalently $S_N\equiv(1+2yt)/(1-t-yt^2)\pmod{t^N}$, which together with $\zeta_{\mathrm{GM}}(0,y)=1$ pins down $\zeta_{\mathrm{GM}}(t,y)=(1-t-yt^2)^{-1}$. The Lean certificate multiplies the truncated trace series by $1-t-yt^2$ as genuine polynomial arithmetic and matches the right-hand side; the $N=6$, $y=1$ instance is
$$
(1-t-t^2)(1+3t+4t^2+7t^3+11t^4+18t^5)=1+2t-29t^6-18t^7.
$$

\begin{theorem}[Finite log-derivative window]
\label{thm:window6-ruelle-finite-window}
The order-six weighted window satisfies the polynomial identity $\chi_y(t)\,S_6(t,y)=1+2yt-C_7(y)t^6-yC_6(y)t^7$ as an equality of polynomials in $y$ and $t$, verified by explicit polynomial multiplication.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Determinant reading]
\label{thm:window6-ruelle-determinant}
The reciprocal zeta equals the weighted spectral determinant of the golden-mean transfer matrix,
$$
\det(I-tT_y)=1-t-yt^2,
$$
so the zeta and the determinant are two readings of the single Fibonacci object $1-t-yt^2$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Golden Pole Factorisation}
\label{sec:window6-ruelle-golden-pole}

Specialising to $y=1$ and working in the golden ring $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ with $\varphi^2=\varphi+1$, the reciprocal zeta polynomial factors into golden linear terms.

\begin{theorem}[Golden pole factorisation]
\label{thm:window6-ruelle-pole-factorisation}
In $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$,
$$
1-t-t^2=(1-\varphi t)\,(1+(\varphi-1)t),
$$
so the poles of $\zeta_{\mathrm{GM}}(t,1)$ are exactly $t=\varphi^{-1}=\varphi-1$ and $t=-\varphi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer spectrum]
\label{thm:window6-ruelle-transfer-spectrum}
The transfer spectrum is exactly the golden pair
$$
\operatorname{spec}T_1=\{\varphi,\;1-\varphi\}=\{\varphi,\;-\varphi^{-1}\},
$$
two simple real spectral roots. This is the honest ``fib-$\mathrm{RH}$'' statement here: the reciprocal zeta polynomial of the golden-mean shift has only simple real spectral roots, controlled by a finite Fibonacci transfer operator of entropy $\log\varphi$.
\end{theorem}
