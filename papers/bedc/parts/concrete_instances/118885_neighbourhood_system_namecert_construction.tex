\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\NeighbourhoodSystemUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-neighbourhood-system-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kelley and Bourbaki, a neighbourhood system is the pointwise local presentation of topology by neighbourhood rows and local bases. The $\NeighbourhoodSystemUp$ packet records that standard presentation as finite BEDC NameCert data: it sits beside the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the filter-basis interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-filterbase-namecert}, the local filter route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-neighborhood-filter-namecert}, the countable-base surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-second-countable-namecert}, the finite window discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a finite local-system interface for topology and completion consumers; it is not a host powerset of neighbourhoods, a quotient filter, a selected limit, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Neighbourhood system carrier]
\label{def:neighbourhood-system-carrier}
A $\NeighbourhoodSystemUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(X,N,B,T,L,W,R,E,H,C,P,Q)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the point row, naming the displayed source point or finite point-window at which the local system is read.
\item $N$ is the neighbourhood-window row, listing finite neighbourhood tokens around $X$ together with their displayed containment and refinement requests.
\item $B$ is the $\FilterBaseUp$ row generated by the finite neighbourhood windows and their meet/refinement witnesses.
\item $T$ is the topology-compatibility row, routing the neighbourhood windows through the open-set carrier and topology provenance of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}.
\item $L$ is the local-basis row, recording the basis tokens that refine each displayed neighbourhood window and the second-countable handoff when a countable-base consumer is present.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row used when local neighbourhood reads are sampled by completion-facing consumers.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to those finite windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after the point, neighbourhood, basis, topology, window, and readback rows have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the point, neighbourhood-window, filter-base, topology, local-basis, window, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a local neighbourhood request through $X,N,B,T,L,W,R,E$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\NeighbourhoodSystemUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\NeighborhoodFilterUp$, $\SecondCountableUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $Q$ is the local $\NameCert_{\NeighbourhoodSystemUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $X,N,B,T,L,W,R,E,H,C,P,Q$. It has no coordinate for host powersets of neighbourhoods, quotient filters, selected limits, countable choice, host equality of topology structures, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Neighbourhood system NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:neighbourhood-system-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\NeighbourhoodSystemUp$ carrier $S=(X,N,B,T,L,W,R,E,H,C,P,Q)$, the local $\NameCert_{\NeighbourhoodSystemUp}$ surface consists exactly of point exposure, neighbourhood-window exposure, filter-base generation, topology compatibility, local-basis refinement, finite-window exposure, RegSeqRat readback, Real-seal nonescape, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every completion-facing read must display
$$
X,N,B,T,L \longmapsto W,R,E,H,C,P,Q
$$
before a Real or topology consumer can inspect the route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:neighbourhood-system-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes exactly the rows $X,N,B,T,L,W,R,E,H,C,P,Q$.

The local-topology rows are upstream. The point row $X$, neighbourhood-window row $N$, filter-base row $B$, topology-compatibility row $T$, and local-basis row $L$ jointly present the neighbourhood system as finite data tied to the sibling topology, filter-base, neighbourhood-filter, and second-countable routes.

The completion-facing rows are downstream. StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, and the terminal Real seal are read only after the local system rows have been displayed. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that same order, so no host powerset, quotient filter, selected limit, countable choice, or ambient Real-completeness coordinate is introduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Neighbourhood system filter-basis route]
\label{thm:neighbourhood-system-filter-basis-route}
Every local-filter or completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\NeighbourhoodSystemUp$ carrier reads the neighbourhood system through the route
$$
\TopologyUp,\NeighbourhoodSystemUp \longmapsto \FilterBaseUp,\NeighborhoodFilterUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp,\RegSeqRatUp,\RealUp .
$$
The route exports finite neighbourhood-window and local-basis data only. It does not export a host powerset of all neighbourhoods, a quotient filter object, a chosen limit, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:neighbourhood-system-namecert-obligations}. They force every accepted consumer to expose the point row, neighbourhood windows, generated filter-base row, topology compatibility, and local-basis refinement before any downstream read.

The local filter route is horizontal. The generated base is consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-filterbase-namecert} and then by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-neighborhood-filter-namecert}; a second-countable consumer may additionally read the displayed local-basis row through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-second-countable-namecert}.

Finally the completion route passes through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. These siblings receive finite windows, regular rational readbacks, and a terminal Real seal only after the neighbourhood-system rows are public, so the route cannot supply host neighbourhood powersets, quotient filters, selected limits, choice schedules, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
