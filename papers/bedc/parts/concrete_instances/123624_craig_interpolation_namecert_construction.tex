\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CraigInterpolationUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-craig-interpolation-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Craig and the standard proof-theoretic interpolation theorem, the $\CraigInterpolationUp$ packet reconstructs the separator route as finite BEDC NameCert data. It sits over the first-order syntax surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-firstorder-namecert}, the derivability and proof-row surfaces used by the model-theoretic chapters, and the transport discipline of $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$. The packet names the finite place where a left formula, a right formula, their shared-symbol ledger, and an interpolant row can be inspected without importing a host completeness theorem, quotient syntax equality, or an ambient semantic compactness argument.

\begin{definition}[Craig interpolation carrier]
\label{def:craig-interpolation-carrier}
A $\CraigInterpolationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(S,L,R,\Sigma,D,J,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\FirstOrderUp$ signature and formula source row.
\item $L$ is the left formula endpoint and $R$ is the right formula endpoint, both read through $S$.
\item $\Sigma$ is the shared-symbol ledger, listing exactly the relation, function, and constant names visible on both sides of the proposed separation.
\item $D$ is the derivability row witnessing the accepted implication or inconsistency route from the left side to the right-side target.
\item $J$ is the interpolant row, naming a formula endpoint whose symbols are all recorded in $\Sigma$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for formula endpoints, shared symbols, derivability rows, interpolant rows, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the derivability row through the interpolant row to any downstream separator consumer.
\item $P$ records $\Pkg$ provenance over the first-order source, derivability row, interpolation row, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and local naming data.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CraigInterpolationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for host proof normalization, semantic completeness, quotient equality of formulas, a selected proof search procedure, or a separator whose symbols are not displayed in $\Sigma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Craig interpolation symbol separation]
\label{thm:craig-interpolation-symbol-separation}
Every accepted $\CraigInterpolationUp$ packet exposes the left formula row $L$, right formula row $R$, shared-symbol ledger $\Sigma$, derivability row $D$, interpolant row $J$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and local $\NameCert$ row $N$ before any interpolation consumer may read the separator. A consumer that cannot display one of these rows has no route from the finite packet to an interpolant formula.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:craig-interpolation-carrier}. The formula endpoints, shared-symbol ledger, derivability row, interpolant row, transport, replay, provenance, and local name are exactly the displayed coordinates of the packet.

The shared-symbol ledger is read before the interpolant row. Thus the separator is not merely a formula endpoint; it is a formula endpoint whose relation, function, and constant names have already been checked against $\Sigma$.

The derivability row is also explicit. It records the proof-theoretic route that licenses the separator read, while $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route under transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Since the carrier contains no coordinate for host proof normalization, semantic completeness, quotient formula equality, selected proof search, or off-ledger symbols, none of those objects can supply the separator. Every interpolation consumer must pass through the listed finite rows.
\end{proof}

\falsifiablePrediction{A claimed $\CraigInterpolationUp$ separator is rejected if it displays $L$ and $R$ but omits the shared-symbol ledger $\Sigma$, the derivability row $D$, the interpolant row $J$, or the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.}
