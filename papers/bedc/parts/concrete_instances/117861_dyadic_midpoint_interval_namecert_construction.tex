\begin{definition}[Dyadic midpoint interval carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-midpoint-interval-carrier}
A $\DyadicMidpointIntervalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(L,R,M,W,D,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the left endpoint row, read as a dyadic rational endpoint supplied by the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger.
\item $R$ is the right endpoint row, read as a dyadic rational endpoint in the same ledger and displayed with $L \leq R$ by finite comparison data.
\item $M$ is the midpoint row, recording the dyadic value obtained from the displayed halving operation on $L$ and $R$.
\item $W$ is the width and tolerance row. It records the original width, the halved width, and the tolerance budget consumed by Real and Completion requests.
\item $D$ is the dyadic ledger. It contains the addition, comparison, halving, and denominator-clearance rows needed to read $L,R,M,W$ without leaving $\DyadicRatCoreUp$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint, midpoint, width, dyadic-ledger, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interval-halving request through $L,R,M,W,D,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicMidpointIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\NestedIntervalUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicMidpointIntervalUp}$ row naming exactly this midpoint-halving certificate.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $L,R,M,W,D,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for Dedekind cuts, host interval equality, open-cover compactness, selected limits, quotient equality, countable choice, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic midpoint interval halving]
\label{thm:dyadic-midpoint-interval-halving}
For every accepted $\DyadicMidpointIntervalUp$ carrier $I=(L,R,M,W,D,H,C,P,N)$, the midpoint row $M$ and width row $W$ display a finite halving route: the original endpoint interval $[L,R]$ is read through the dyadic ledger $D$, the midpoint is the dyadic half-sum recorded in $M$, and each retained child interval has width bounded by the halved width recorded in $W$. Any $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, or $\RealUp$ consumer of this halving route must pass through $D,H,C,P,N$ before reading the resulting finite window or real-name seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-midpoint-interval-carrier}. The only endpoint, midpoint, and width data available to a consumer are the displayed rows $L,R,M,W$ together with the dyadic ledger $D$.

The halving step is read inside $\DyadicRatCoreUp$. The ledger $D$ supplies the endpoint comparison, half-sum row, denominator-clearance row, and width comparison row, so the retained left or right child interval is certified by finite dyadic arithmetic rather than by host interval equality.

Next attach the Real and Completion handoff. The finite windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} may consume the halved interval only after the rows $L,R,M,W,D$ are displayed.

Finally compare with the nested route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nestedinterval-namecert}. A retained nested cell reads this midpoint packet as one finite refinement step, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no excluded object is a carrier coordinate, no Dedekind cut, host interval equality, open-cover compactness, selected limit, quotient equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness principle is exported.
\end{proof}
