It sits after the finite windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the alternating route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regularcauchyinterleaving-namecert}, and the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. \begin{definition}[RegularCauchyBinarySubdivision carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-binary-subdivision-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyBinarySubdivisionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(S,D,R,I,L,U,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row whose entries list the inspected binary subdivision stages.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger recording the midpoint, lower half, upper half, and requested radius for each inspected stage.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the same windows and dyadic ledger.
\item $I$ is the $\RegularCauchyInterleavingUp$ parity row that records how the left and right half-window reads are interleaved.
\item $L$ is the left half-window row, read only through $S,D,R,I$.
\item $U$ is the upper half-window row, read only through $S,D,R,I$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal available only after $L$ and $U$ have been displayed as half-window evidence for the same regular name.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for windows, dyadic ledger, regular readback, interleaving, half-window rows, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a binary-subdivision request through $S,D,R,I,L,U,E$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyBinarySubdivisionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RegularCauchyInterleavingUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyBinarySubdivisionUp}$ row.

The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,D,R,I,L,U,E,H,C,P,N$. There is no coordinate for quotient reals, selected limits, trichotomy, countable-choice schedules, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyBinarySubdivision NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-binary-subdivision-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchyBinarySubdivisionUp$ carrier
$$
K=(S,D,R,I,L,U,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyBinarySubdivisionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed finite windows, dyadic tolerance ledger, regular rational readback, parity interleaving row, left and upper half-window rows, terminal real seal, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. A consumer may read binary subdivision only through the route $S,D,R,I,L,U,E,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-binary-subdivision-carrier}. The visible rows are precisely $S,D,R,I,L,U,E,H,C,P,N$.

The rows $S,D,R,I$ display the inspected stream windows, dyadic midpoint accounting, regular rational readback, and parity route. The half-window rows $L$ and $U$ are downstream of that joint surface, so they cannot be detached from the same regular Cauchy data.

The terminal seal $E$ and the support rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same finite route. Since the carrier has no quotient, selected-limit, trichotomy, choice, host-equality, or ambient-completeness coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyBinarySubdivision window halving]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-binary-subdivision-window-halving}
Let $K=(S,D,R,I,L,U,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RegularCauchyBinarySubdivisionUp$ carrier. Every binary-window request from $K$ first reads the $\StreamNameUp$ row $S$, then the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger $D$, then the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$, and then the $\RegularCauchyInterleavingUp$ parity row $I$ before either half-window row $L$ or $U$ is public. Thus each half-window read is tied to the same displayed regular Cauchy name and the same dyadic subdivision ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the continuation row $C$. Its displayed route sends a binary-subdivision request through the finite window row $S$ and the dyadic ledger $D$ before any half-window row can be inspected.

Next read the regular rational row $R$ and the interleaving row $I$. They bind the left and upper half-window reads to the same regular name and to the same parity schedule.

The rows $L$ and $U$ are therefore not independent interval choices. They are the two displayed halves of the same finite subdivision route, transported and replayed only through $H,C,P,N$.
\end{proof}

The terminal row $E$ is downstream of both half-window rows. A Real-facing consumer therefore receives $E$ only after the displayed binary subdivision route has been replayed.
