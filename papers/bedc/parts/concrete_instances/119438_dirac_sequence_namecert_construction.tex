It sits between the smooth-kernel packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-mollifier-namecert}, the singleton-mass packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dirac-point-mass-namecert}, the terminal real-name surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the continuous-map route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}. \begin{definition}[Dirac sequence carrier]
\label{def:dirac-sequence-carrier}
A $\DiracSequenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(S,R,N,W,E,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the scale-schedule row, recording the finite list of dyadic scale requests and the order in which downstream consumers may inspect them.
\item $R$ is the support-radius decay row, recording the displayed radius bound attached to each scale entry and the finite locality budget consumed by the packet.
\item $N$ is the normalization ledger, exposing unit-mass readback as $\RealUp$ and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparisons rather than as a host integral.
\item $W$ is the convolution-window row, recording the source window, target window, translation request, scale request, and finite compatibility data read by a $\ContinuousMapUp$ or $\MollifierUp$ consumer.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $S,R,N,W$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the schedule, support, normalization, convolution-window, real-seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from an approximate-identity request through $S,R,N,W,E,H,P,L$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DiracSequenceUp$, $\MollifierUp$, $\DiracPointMassUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\RealUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DiracSequenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,R,N,W,E,H,C,P,L$. It has no coordinate for host integration, selected limits, quotient equality of functions or distributions, countable-choice schedules, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dirac sequence NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dirac-sequence-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DiracSequenceUp$ carrier $D=(S,R,N,W,E,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DiracSequenceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed scale schedule, support-radius decay, normalization ledger, convolution-window row, Real seal, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every $\MollifierUp$, $\DiracPointMassUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\RealUp$, or $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ consumer of the packet must read through these rows before treating the sequence as approximate-identity data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dirac-sequence-carrier}. The analytic rows are exactly $S,R,N,W,E$, and the structural rows are exactly $H,C,P,L$.

The scale schedule $S$ and support-radius decay row $R$ expose the finite locality route. The normalization ledger $N$ supplies the unit-mass boundary through displayed real and dyadic comparisons, so no host integration row is needed.

The convolution-window row $W$ is the only row that a regularization or continuous-map consumer can use to relate a source window, target window, translation request, and scale request. The terminal seal $E$ is downstream of that finite route rather than a selected limiting delta object.

Finally $H,C,P,L$ transport, replay, source, and name only the displayed packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for host integration, selected limits, quotient equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dirac sequence Real-seal handoff]
\label{thm:dirac-sequence-real-seal-handoff}
Let $D=(S,R,N,W,E,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DiracSequenceUp$ carrier. Any $\RealUp$ readback exported by the packet factors through the finite route
$$
S \longmapsto R \longmapsto N \longmapsto W \longmapsto E \longmapsto H \longmapsto C \longmapsto P \longmapsto L .
$$
The handoff provides scale-schedule, support-radius, normalization, convolution-window, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming data, but it exports no host integration operator, selected approximate identity limit, quotient function or distribution equality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient real-completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a consumer that asks for a real-valued normalization or regularization readback. Carrier admission first displays the scale schedule $S$ and support-radius decay row $R$, so the request is confined to a finite approximate-identity window.

The normalization ledger $N$ then supplies the real and dyadic comparisons needed for unit-mass readback. The convolution-window row $W$ records the source and target windows, translation request, and scale request before the terminal seal $E$ can be cited.

Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve the same displayed rows, while $P$ and $L$ source and name the local certificate. Thus the $\RealUp$ handoff factors through the listed finite route.

No carrier row stores host integration, a chosen limiting delta object, quotient equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness. Those objects therefore cannot be exported by the Real-seal handoff.
\end{proof}
