\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\PathMetricUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-path-metric-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Burago--Burago--Ivanov on intrinsic metrics and Gromov on metric structures read through finite chains, the $\PathMetricUp$ packet records the path-distance surface obtained from a displayed metric carrier and finite curve-fragment rows. It sits between the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the pointwise and modulus rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the length-space consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-length-space-namecert}, the segment-witness refinement of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-geodesic-metric-namecert}, and circle-source consumers such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-s1-namecert}. The packet names path-distance data without assuming selected geodesics, quotient path spaces, host curve equality, or completion of the ambient metric carrier.

\begin{definition}[PathMetric carrier]
\label{def:path-metric-carrier}
A $\PathMetricUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
P=(M,F,L,D,B,H,C,Q,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the accepted $\MetricSpaceUp$ or $\MetricUp$ source row, carrying the endpoint classifier and the distance observations used by every path-fragment read.
\item $F$ is a finite family of displayed curve-fragment rows, read through $\ContinuousMapUp$ when a fragment is presented by a finite graph or modulus ledger.
\item $L$ is the fragment-length ledger, assigning each displayed fragment a finite length budget over the same metric source.
\item $D$ is the path-distance row, recording the requested endpoint distance as the infimum-facing finite lower-envelope surface generated by the listed fragments.
\item $B$ is the boundary handoff row for $\LengthSpaceUp$, $\GeodesicMetricUp$, and $\SOneUp$ consumers: it records which endpoint, fragment, and circle-source reads are visible before a downstream path-distance claim is used.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric source, fragment family, length ledger, path-distance row, boundary handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from endpoint reads through fragment inspection, length-budget comparison, path-distance exposure, and downstream handoff.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PathMetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\LengthSpaceUp$, $\GeodesicMetricUp$, $\SOneUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PathMetricUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,F,L,D,B,H,C,Q,N$. It has no coordinate for selected geodesics, quotient path spaces, host equality of curves, unlisted path families, or ambient metric completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[PathMetric NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:path-metric-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PathMetricUp$ carrier $P=(M,F,L,D,B,H,C,Q,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\PathMetricUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed metric source, curve-fragment family, fragment-length ledger, path-distance row, boundary handoff, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Every path-distance read must expose $M,F,L,D,B,H,C,Q,N$ before a downstream consumer may use it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:path-metric-carrier}. The metric source $M$, the fragment family $F$, the fragment-length ledger $L$, the path-distance row $D$, and the handoff row $B$ are all displayed before transport and naming are read.

The fragment route is finite. If a fragment is supplied by a graph or modulus row, it is read through the $\ContinuousMapUp$ boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}; its length is then recorded by $L$ over the same metric source.

The path-distance row $D$ is therefore not a quotient of all paths. It is the finite lower-envelope surface generated by the displayed fragment ledger and transported by $H$ and $C$.

Finally $Q$ and $N$ source and name exactly this route. Since selected geodesics, quotient path spaces, host curve equality, unlisted path families, and ambient completion are absent from the carrier, no NameCert obligation can export them.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PathMetric length-space handoff]
\label{thm:path-metric-length-space-handoff}
Every $\LengthSpaceUp$, $\GeodesicMetricUp$, or $\SOneUp$ consumer of an accepted $\PathMetricUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
M,F,L,D \longmapsto B \longmapsto H,C,Q,N
$$
before treating the path-distance row as usable data. The handoff supplies the displayed metric source, curve-fragment rows, length budgets, path-distance exposure, boundary handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it supplies no selected geodesic, quotient path space, host curve equality, unlisted path family, or completed metric carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the consumer boundary. A $\LengthSpaceUp$ read uses the length ledger $L$ and the path-distance row $D$, a $\GeodesicMetricUp$ read may compare a segment witness with the same rows, and an $\SOneUp$ read may use only the circle-source rows exposed through the boundary handoff $B$.

The handoff row $B$ is therefore the only downstream exit. It records which endpoint, fragment, length, and circle-source reads are visible before the consumer uses the path-distance claim.

Transport and replay remain componentwise through $H$ and $C$, while $Q$ and $N$ preserve provenance and local naming. Hence the downstream surface is exactly the displayed route and cannot be replaced by selected geodesics, quotient path spaces, host curve equality, unlisted path families, or completion of the ambient metric carrier.
\end{proof}
