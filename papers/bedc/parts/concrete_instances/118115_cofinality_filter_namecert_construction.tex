\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CofinalityFilterUp$}

Following Kelley and Bourbaki on filters and Bishop--Bridges on constructive Cauchy tails, the $\CofinalityFilterUp$ packet records the finite tail-cobase interface by which a sequence-indexed completion route is read as a filter without importing quotient filters, selected limits, or countable choice. It sits after the directed-tail route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-directed-filter-namecert}, the cofinal sequence interface of $\CofinalSequenceUp$, the Cauchy-filter basis surface of $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, the finite Cauchy-filter row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchyfilter-namecert}, the stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite cobase corridor for Real and Completion consumers; it is not a quotient filter, a chosen convergent subsequence, a selected completed point, or an ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[CofinalityFilter carrier]\label{def:cofinality-filter-carrier}
A $\CofinalityFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(I,L,B,F,U,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the unary tail-index row, recording finite natural-number tail requests for the sequence-indexed route.
\item $L$ is the cofinal lower-bound ledger. For every displayed tail request in $I$, it stores a later index whose inspected window is still inside the same requested tail.
\item $B$ is the cobase row, listing the finite family of tail neighborhoods generated from $I$ and refined by $L$.
\item $F$ is the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ and $\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff row assembled only from the cobase row $B$.
\item $U$ is the Completion corridor row, available only after the directed and filter-facing tail data have been replayed through $I,L,B,F$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached through $\StreamNameUp$ finite windows and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback attached to the same cobase route.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the tail index, lower-bound ledger, cobase, filter-basis handoff, completion corridor, Real seal, replay, provenance, and local name rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the finite route
$$
I,L \longmapsto B \longmapsto F \longmapsto U,E .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CofinalityFilterUp$, $\DirectedFilterUp$, $\CofinalSequenceUp$, $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CofinalityFilterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier compares accepted packets only through $I,L,B,F,U,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient filters, selected limits, countable-choice schedules, host equality of filters, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CofinalityFilter NameCert obligations]\label{thm:cofinality-filter-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CofinalityFilterUp$ carrier $K=(I,L,B,F,U,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CofinalityFilterUp}$ surface consists exactly of the finite obligations for tail-index admission, cofinal lower-bound exactness, cobase generation, Cauchy-filter basis handoff, completion-corridor nonescape, Real-seal readback, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every obligation is read from $I,L,B,F,U,E,H,C,P,N$, and no obligation is stored in a quotient filter, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host equality relation, quotient real equality row, or ambient completeness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cofinality-filter-carrier}. The displayed analytic rows are $I,L,B,F,U,E$, and the structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

Tail-index admission is supplied by $I$. The lower-bound ledger $L$ is then read against the same finite tail requests, so a later window is displayed before any cobase or filter consumer inspects the packet.

The cobase and filter rows are downstream. The row $B$ records exactly the tail neighborhoods generated from the displayed sequence indices, while $F$ presents those neighborhoods through the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ and $\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff surface.

Finally $U,E,H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route for Completion and Real-facing consumers. Since the excluded objects have no carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CofinalityFilter tail cobase handoff]\label{thm:cofinality-filter-tail-cobase-handoff}
Every $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, Completion, or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\CofinalityFilterUp$ carrier must read the unary tail-index row $I$, the cofinal lower-bound ledger $L$, and the cobase row $B$ before the handoff row $F$ is available. The handoff presents finite tail neighborhoods, cofinal lower-bound witnesses, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row; it does not present a quotient filter, selected limit, countable-choice selector, host filter equality, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CofinalityFilterUp.CofinalityFilterCarrier\_tail\_cobase\_handoff}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:cofinality-filter-namecert-obligations}. They place every filter-facing read after the displayed tail-index row $I$ and cofinal lower-bound ledger $L$.

Read $B$ next. Because $B$ is assembled only from the finite tail neighborhoods witnessed by $I$ and refined by $L$, the consumer receives a displayed cobase rather than an external filter object.

The row $F$ hands that cobase to $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ and $\CauchyFilterUp$, and the rows $U,E$ keep Completion and Real-facing reads downstream of the same cobase. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that route, so none of the excluded objects can be recovered from the carrier.
\end{proof}
