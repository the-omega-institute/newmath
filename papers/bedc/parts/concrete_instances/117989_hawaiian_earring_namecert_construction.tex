\begin{definition}[Hawaiian earring carrier]
\label{def:hawaiian-earring-carrier}

$$

$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C$ is the circle-source row, read through S1Up and its $\RealUp$ coordinate surface, naming the reusable unit-circle certificate.
\item $R$ is the radius schedule row, read through $\RegSeqRatUp$, displaying a positive rational radius for each inspected circle and its convergence toward the common tangent point.
\item $S$ is the stream-name row, read through $\StreamNameUp$, recording the finite stream observations by which requested circle indices and rational radii are presented.
\item $T$ is the topology row, read through $\TopologyUp$, naming the subspace and neighborhood interface available at the wedge point and at displayed circle points.
\item $K$ is the compact-metric row, read through $\CompactMetricUp$, exposing only the finite compact-metric observations attached to the displayed radius schedule.
\item $Q$ is the circle-wedge incidence row, recording the shared tangent point and the finite membership routes for the inspected circle pieces.
\item $W$ is the wedge-boundary readback row, recording which displayed circle and radius row supplies a requested local observation.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the circle source, radius schedule, stream name, topology, compact-metric row, incidence row, wedge-boundary readback, replay, provenance, and local naming.

The classifier compares accepted packets only through these eleven rows. It has no coordinate for a full fundamental-group computation, quotient wedge equality, selected points from infinitely many circles, ambient compactness beyond the displayed compact-metric rows, Lean verification, or bridge data.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hawaiian earring NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hawaiian-earring-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\HawaiianEarringUp$ carrier $E=(C,R,S,T,K,Q,W,H,D,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\HawaiianEarringUp}$ obligation surface consists exactly of circle-source admission, regular rational radius scheduling, stream-name readback, topology exposure, compact-metric observation, circle-wedge incidence, wedge-boundary readback, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. A consumer may inspect the compact metric boundary packet only after those rows have been exposed from the same $\BHist$ carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hawaiian-earring-carrier}. The rows $C,R,S,T,K,Q,W,H,D,P,N$ are the only rows exposed by an accepted packet.

Read the source and schedule rows first. The S1Up circle row $C$, regular rational sequence row $R$, and stream-name row $S$ jointly determine which displayed circles and radii may be inspected before any topology or compact-metric consumer enters.

Next read $T,K,Q,W$. These rows expose the topology, compact-metric observation, shared tangent point, finite incidence data, and wedge-boundary readback, while keeping all observations attached to the same displayed schedule.

Finally $H,D,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that packet. Since no carrier coordinate stores a fundamental group, quotient wedge equality, a choice schedule over all circles, ambient compactness beyond $K$, Lean verification, or bridge data, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}
