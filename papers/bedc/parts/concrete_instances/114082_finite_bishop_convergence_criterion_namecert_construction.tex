\begin{theorem}[Finite Bishop convergence window test]
\label{thm:finite-bishop-convergence-criterion-window-test}
Every accepted $\FiniteBishopConvergenceCriterionUp$ carrier $F=(D,W,Q,R,M,I,H,C,P,N)$ exposes a convergence test only through the finite route
$$
D,W \longmapsto Q \longmapsto M,R \longmapsto I,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus a consumer must inspect the dyadic tolerance row and the $\StreamNameUp$ window before it can use regular rational readback, convergence-modulus evidence, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, or the Bishop real-line interface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-bishop-convergence-criterion-carrier}. The first visible stage is the tolerance and window pair $D,W$, so the request radius and finite observation schedule are fixed before any rational readback is available.

Replay the read through $C$. The replay opens $Q$ only after the displayed windows have been inspected, and it opens $M$ and $R$ only after the same readback has been tied to the dyadic budget.

Finally transport through $H$ and source the route through $P,N$. The Bishop interface row $I$ receives exactly the same finite evidence, so no selected limit, quotient stream equality, host equality, choice principle, completeness theorem, Lean target, or bridge row can be exported.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Bishop convergence L10 handoff]
\label{thm:finite-bishop-convergence-criterion-l10-handoff}
Every L10 consumer of an accepted $\FiniteBishopConvergenceCriterionUp$ packet factors through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-modulus-of-convergence-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-real-line-interface-namecert} before it may read convergence-test evidence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier rows in order. The dyadic tolerance row $D$ supplies the request budget, the stream row $W$ supplies the inspected finite window, and the regular rational row $Q$ supplies the readback seen by L10.

The convergence-modulus row $M$ and Real seal $R$ are downstream of that route. They may certify that the displayed finite window is sufficient for the named request, but they do not introduce a host real limit or a quotient equality of streams.

The interface row $I$ packages the same data for Bishop real-line consumers, while $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name it. The L10 handoff is therefore exactly the finite sibling route listed in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Bishop convergence nonescape]
\label{thm:finite-bishop-convergence-criterion-nonescape}
An accepted $\FiniteBishopConvergenceCriterionUp$ carrier cannot export Real completeness, quotient stream equality, selected representatives, selected limits, host real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, Lean verification, or bridge checking. Every public read is exhausted by the rows $D,W,Q,R,M,I,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier coordinates. They are exactly the dyadic tolerance row, finite stream window, regular rational readback, Real seal, convergence-modulus ledger, Bishop interface row, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Each public read must be a projection from those rows or a replay through $C,H,P,N$. None of the excluded objects is a row of the carrier, and none can be created by projection, replay, or componentwise transport.

Therefore the accepted packet is exhausted by the finite NameCert route displayed above. The nonescape claim follows from row exhaustion rather than from any external completeness or choice principle.
\end{proof}
