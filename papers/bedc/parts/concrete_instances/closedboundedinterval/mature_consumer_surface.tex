\begin{theorem}[Closed bounded interval mature consumer surface]
\label{thm:closed-bounded-interval-mature-consumer-surface}
For every accepted $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ carrier $I=(L,U,O,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the public consumer surface of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-public-consumer-surface} and the public bridge boundary of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-public-bridge-boundary} determine the mature $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ consumer route. Every interval-facing read must pass through the displayed endpoint, containment, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real-seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert rows before it can serve a net, located-cover, nested-window, uniform-modulus, or compactness-facing consumer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the public consumer surface. It identifies the available public rows as exactly $L,U,O,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$ and records that no consumer receives open-cover compactness, a selected subcover, quotient real equality, trichotomy, choice, a selected point, or ambient completeness.

Then apply the public bridge boundary. It forces finite-cover, located-cover, uniform-modulus, and compactness-facing reads to factor through the same endpoint, containment, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real-seal, transport, replay, provenance, and NameCert rows.

Thus the mature route exposes no data beyond the displayed finite packet. The consumer may reuse the public surface and its bridge boundary, but it cannot bypass either one or export a completeness-facing coordinate not already present in the carrier.
\end{proof}
