\begin{theorem}[Closed bounded interval public consumer surface]
\label{thm:closed-bounded-interval-public-consumer-surface}
For every accepted $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ carrier $I=(L,U,O,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the carrier of \autoref{def:closed-bounded-interval-carrier}, classifier of \autoref{def:closed-bounded-interval-classifier}, NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-namecert-obligations}, root source ledger of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-source-obligation-package}, source nonescape rows of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-source-nonescape}, \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-finite-cover-nonescape}, and \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-consumer-nonescape-obligations}, and scoped kernel envelope of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-scoped-kernel-envelope} form the public $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ consumer surface. A public consumer may read only the lower endpoint, upper endpoint, endpoint-order, rational containment, dyadic containment, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real-seal, hsame, Cont, Pkg, and NameCert rows displayed by the packet, and receives no open-cover compactness, selected subcover, quotient real equality, trichotomy, choice, selected interval point, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted packet through the carrier and classifier. The public rows are exactly $L,U,O,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$, with endpoint order and containment read through the same displayed transport and replay routes.

Apply the NameCert obligation theorem and the root source ledger. They identify the endpoint, containment, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real-seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows before any consumer-facing route is exposed.

Use the source nonescape and consumer nonescape rows to block external coordinates. Net, finite-cover, nested-window, located-cover, uniform-modulus, and compactness-facing consumers remain inside the displayed finite packet.

Finally use the scoped kernel envelope. It packages the same rows as the public object surface, so the public consumer receives the named finite carrier and none of the refused open-cover, subcover-selection, quotient-equality, trichotomy, choice, point-selection, or completeness data.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ClosedboundedintervalUp.ClosedBoundedIntervalPublicConsumerSurface}

\begin{theorem}[Closed bounded interval public bridge boundary]
\label{thm:closed-bounded-interval-public-bridge-boundary}
For every accepted $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ carrier $I=(L,U,O,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, any finite-cover, located-cover, uniform-modulus, or compactness-facing consumer of $I$ must factor through the displayed lower endpoint, upper endpoint, endpoint-order, rational containment, dyadic containment, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real-seal, hsame, Cont, Pkg, NameCert rows and the public consumer surface of \autoref{thm:closed-bounded-interval-public-consumer-surface} before it can read interval data. The bridge boundary exposes no open-cover compactness theorem, selected subcover data, quotient real equality, trichotomy, choice, selected interval point, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the public consumer surface theorem. It identifies the public rows as exactly $L,U,O,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$ and records the nonescape clauses that block external compactness and choice data.

Route each consumer class through those rows. A finite-cover consumer reads only the displayed endpoint and containment rows; a located-cover consumer also passes through $S,R,E$ before reading a located interval verdict; a uniform-modulus consumer must replay the same Real-sealed route through $H,C$; and a compactness-facing consumer reaches only the package and NameCert boundary $P,N$.

The stated refusals follow because none of the displayed rows stores open-cover compactness, a selected subcover, quotient real equality, trichotomy, choice, a selected point, or ambient completeness. The bridge therefore has no coordinate from which such data can be exported.
\end{proof}
