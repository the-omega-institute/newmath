\begin{theorem}[Locally convex TopVecSpace anchor]
\label{thm:locally-convex-space-topvecspace-anchor}
Every accepted $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$ packet consumes the $\TopVecSpaceUp$ carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topvecspace-namecert} and the $\ConvexSetUp$ neighbourhood rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert} before any seminorm ledger, local-base read, or functional-analysis handoff is available. The local convexity read is therefore a sibling route through displayed vector-topology and convex-neighbourhood data, not an isolated host predicate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:locally-convex-space-carrier}. The row $T$ is the topological-vector-space source, while $B$ is the convex balanced neighbourhood ledger.

The row $T$ must be read first because it supplies the topology, addition-continuity ledger, and scalar-continuity ledger used by every neighbourhood request. The row $B$ is then read through $\ConvexSetUp$ as the displayed convex-neighbourhood evidence.

Only after $T$ and $B$ are visible may the finite seminorm ledger $S$ or a local-base consumer inspect the packet. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming keep the same route, so the chapter records a horizontal dependency through $\TopVecSpaceUp$ and $\ConvexSetUp$ rather than a kernel-only local-convexity predicate.
\end{proof}
