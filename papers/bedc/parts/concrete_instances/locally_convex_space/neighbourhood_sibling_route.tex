\begin{theorem}[Locally convex neighbourhood sibling route]
\label{thm:locally-convex-space-neighbourhood-sibling-route}
Every $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$ neighbourhood read first consumes the $\TopVecSpaceUp$ vector/topology rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topvecspace-namecert} and the $\SeminormedSpaceUp$ finite seminorm rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-seminormed-space-namecert}. Only after those sibling rows are visible may the packet expose convex balanced neighbourhood data through the local rows $B,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:locally-convex-space-carrier}. The topological-vector row $T$ records the linear topology and operation-continuity route, while the seminorm ledger $S$ records the finite probes that generate the accepted neighbourhood reads.

Read those rows through their sibling chapters. The $\TopVecSpaceUp$ route supplies the vector/topology source, and the $\SeminormedSpaceUp$ route supplies the finite seminorm comparison surface before any convex balanced neighbourhood can be used.

The neighbourhood row $B$ is therefore downstream of $T$ and $S$. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route and do not introduce a detached locally convex host predicate.
\end{proof}
