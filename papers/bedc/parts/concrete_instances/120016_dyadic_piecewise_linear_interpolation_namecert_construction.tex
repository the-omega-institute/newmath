\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$ packet records the finite dyadic mesh route by which endpoint rational data is read as a piecewise-linear interpolation graph before any regular Cauchy or Real consumer sees a completion-facing handoff. It sits after the midpoint arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicmidpoint-namecert}, the dyadic rational core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular interpolation corridor of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-interpolation-namecert}, the rational readback surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only displayed finite mesh data; it is not a host function space, an ambient uniform-continuity theorem, a selected real limit, or a completeness principle for $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic piecewise-linear interpolation carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-piecewise-linear-interpolation-carrier}
A $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(M,E,S,G,R,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the finite dyadic mesh row. It lists ordered dyadic knots, endpoint inclusions, adjacent midpoint checks, and the local interval codes read through $\DyadicMidpointUp$ and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$.
\item $E$ is the endpoint-value row. It assigns displayed dyadic rational values to the knots of $M$ and records the equality checks needed at shared segment endpoints.
\item $S$ is the segment-slope row. For every adjacent knot pair it records the displayed dyadic numerator, denominator, nonzero interval-width witness, and rational slope ledger.
\item $G$ is the interpolation graph row. It records the affine value rule on each mesh segment, the endpoint agreement rows, and the segment-membership row used when a downstream reader asks for a graph value.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained by reading the graph on dyadic refinement windows rather than by invoking a host function.
\item $L$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ handoff row, available only after the mesh, endpoint, slope, graph, and regular rational readback rows have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for mesh knots, endpoint values, segment slopes, graph rows, regular rational readback, terminal handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interpolation request through $M,E,S,G,R,L,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$, $\DyadicMidpointUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegularCauchyInterpolationUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,E,S,G,R,L,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an external real-valued function, a host slope field, arbitrary real arithmetic, quotient stream equality, selected limits, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic piecewise-linear interpolation NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-piecewise-linear-interpolation-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$ carrier
$$
I=(M,E,S,G,R,L,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite dyadic mesh admission, endpoint-value admission, segment-slope ledger construction, interpolation graph exposure, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, terminal $\RealUp$ handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-piecewise-linear-interpolation-carrier}. The rows $M,E,S,G$ are the only mesh, endpoint, slope, and graph coordinates, while $R,L,H,C,P,N$ only read back, seal, transport, replay, source, and name those rows.

The arithmetic obligations are local to the dyadic rows. The mesh row reads midpoint and dyadic rational evidence through $\DyadicMidpointUp$ and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, the endpoint row records displayed values at mesh knots, and the slope row stores only the finite rational differences and interval-width witnesses for adjacent knots.

The graph obligation is then a finite segment ledger. The row $G$ chooses a displayed segment by the membership row supplied by $M$, applies the affine rule recorded by $S$, and checks endpoint agreement against $E$.

Finally $R$ reads the same graph through regular rational refinement windows, $L$ exposes the Real-facing seal only after that readback, and $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route. Since no carrier coordinate stores a host function, arbitrary real arithmetic, quotient stream equality, selected limit, or ambient completeness principle, the listed rows exhaust the local certificate obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic piecewise-linear interpolation RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:dyadic-piecewise-linear-interpolation-regseqrat-handoff}
Every regular-rational or Real-and-Completion consumer of an accepted $\DyadicPiecewiseLinearInterpolationUp$ packet reads the interpolation route through
$$
\DyadicMidpointUp,\ \DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \RegularCauchyInterpolationUp \longrightarrow \RegSeqRatUp \longrightarrow \RealUp .
$$
Concretely, the consumer must first expose the dyadic mesh row $M$, endpoint-value row $E$, segment-slope row $S$, and interpolation graph row $G$ before it may read the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff $R$ or terminal seal $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from an accepted packet $I=(M,E,S,G,R,L,H,C,P,N)$. The only source of interval data is the finite mesh row $M$, and the only source of endpoint values is the row $E$ tied to that mesh.

The segment-slope row $S$ and graph row $G$ turn this finite dyadic data into displayed affine segment reads. These rows are exactly the interpolation surface consumed by the regular interpolation corridor; no downstream consumer can bypass them to obtain a host function.

The $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$ is downstream of the graph row and records rational refinement reads of the same finite interpolation ledger. The terminal $\RealUp$ row $L$ is available only after $R$ is displayed.

The transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows preserve this order. Therefore every regular-rational or completion-facing handoff factors through the displayed dyadic mesh, endpoint, slope, graph, and readback rows, and it exports no independent real function, selected limit, quotient stream equality, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
