\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-connected-compact-interval-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive compact intervals, the $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$ packet records the finite route by which a located compact interval supplies connectedness data without appealing to open-cover compactness. It sits after the located endpoint packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}, the compact uniform-continuity packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-uniform-continuity-namecert}, the interval image-connected route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-interval-image-connected-namecert}, the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the finite StreamName windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and the RegSeqRat readback boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}. The packet names compact interval connectedness as displayed finite data; it does not assert open-cover compactness, selected subcovers, quotient real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Connected compact interval carrier]
\label{def:connected-compact-interval-carrier}
A $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(E,N,I,W,Q,R,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E$ is the located interval endpoint row, recording the left endpoint, right endpoint, endpoint order, membership boundary, and located interval probes supplied by $\LocatedIntervalUp$.
\item $N$ is the compact finite-net row, recording the dyadic mesh, finite covering windows, mesh-radius certificate, and endpoint coverage consumed from $\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$.
\item $I$ is the interval image-connected route row, recording the path from interval data to connectedness reads through $\IntervalImageConnectedUp$.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row for endpoints, mesh points, bisection probes, and connectedness challenge reads.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to those finite windows and rational endpoint comparisons.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for endpoint and image-value names, reached only after $E,N,I,W,Q$ are displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoints, finite nets, connectedness route, windows, readbacks, Real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the endpoint, finite-net, connectedness-route, window, readback, Real-seal, transport, provenance, and local certificate rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$, $\IntervalImageConnectedUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $E,N,I,W,Q,R,H,C,P,L$. It has no coordinate for open-cover compactness, selected subcovers, host image subsets, quotient real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, ambient completeness, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Connected compact interval NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:connected-compact-interval-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$ carrier
$$
K=(E,N,I,W,Q,R,H,C,P,L),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for located endpoints, compact finite-net data, interval image-connected routing, finite windows, regular readbacks, Real sealing, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item endpoint exactness, requiring all interval-boundary reads to pass through the displayed $\LocatedIntervalUp$ row $E$;
\item finite-net exactness, requiring all compact interval mesh and coverage reads to pass through $N$ rather than through an open-cover compactness principle;
\item connectedness-route exactness, requiring every connectedness read to pass through the interval image-connected route $I$;
\item window and readback exactness, requiring StreamName windows $W$ and RegSeqRat readbacks $Q$ before the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $R$ is inspected;
\item transport and replay locality, requiring $H$ and $C$ to preserve the same displayed rows componentwise;
\item nonescape, excluding open-cover compactness, selected subcovers, host image connectedness, quotient real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, Lean verification, and bridge checking.
\end{enumerate}
No connected compact interval obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:connected-compact-interval-carrier}. The accepted packet displays one endpoint row, one finite-net row, one connectedness-route row, finite windows, readbacks, a Real seal, and structural support.

Endpoint exactness is supplied by $E$. Every interval-boundary read uses the same located endpoints and membership boundary, so no separate host interval or quotient real representative is introduced.

Finite-net exactness is supplied by $N$. Mesh points, finite covering windows, and radius certificates are read as finite packet data, not as an open-cover compactness theorem or a selected subcover functional.

Connectedness-route exactness is supplied by $I$. The route delegates the connectedness read to the interval image-connected packet after the endpoint and finite-net rows are displayed.

Finally $W,Q,R,H,C,P,L$ impose readback order and local naming. StreamName windows and RegSeqRat readbacks precede the Real seal, and the structural rows transport, replay, source, and name only the displayed carrier rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Connected compact interval route]
\label{thm:connected-compact-interval-route}
Every connectedness read exported by an accepted $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$ carrier factors through
$$
E,N \longmapsto I \longmapsto W,Q,R,H,C,P,L .
$$
The route uses located interval endpoints, compact finite-net data, the interval image-connected handoff, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readbacks, a Real seal, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay, package provenance, and the local NameCert row. It exports no open-cover compactness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted packet $K=(E,N,I,W,Q,R,H,C,P,L)$. The endpoint row $E$ fixes the located interval boundary, and the finite-net row $N$ fixes the compact mesh data available to the interval consumer.

The connectedness read must then pass through $I$. This row is the only connectedness coordinate in the carrier, and it points to the interval image-connected route rather than to a host connectedness theorem.

The downstream rows $W,Q,R,H,C,P,L$ expose finite observations, rational readbacks, the terminal Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since no row stores open-cover compactness or a selected subcover, the exported connectedness claim is exactly the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Connected compact interval Real-seal nonescape]
\label{thm:connected-compact-interval-real-seal-nonescape}
Every Real-facing seal read of an accepted $\ConnectedCompactIntervalUp$ packet is downstream of the located endpoint row, compact finite-net row, interval image-connected route, finite-window row, and RegSeqRat readback row. The terminal $\RealUp$ row cannot be read as ambient Real completeness, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, or a selected-subcover principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier places the Real seal at $R$ after $E,N,I,W,Q$. Thus a Real-facing consumer first reads the located endpoints, finite compact mesh data, connectedness route, finite windows, and rational readbacks.

The structural rows preserve that order. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay move the same packet through provenance and local naming; they add no independent completeness or compactness coordinate.

Therefore the Real seal is a downstream endpoint and image-value seal for the displayed finite route. It is not an escape hatch to open-cover compactness, quotient real equality, selected subcovers, trichotomy, choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
