\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\AmGmInequalityUp$}

Following Hardy, Littlewood, and Polya on classical inequalities, the $\AmGmInequalityUp$ packet records the arithmetic-mean/geometric-mean comparison as finite NameCert data rather than as host arithmetic. It sits over the dyadic radius ledger of $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the rational comparison surface of $\RatUp$ in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert}, the Real radius consumer of $\RealUp$ in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the arithmetic rows of $\AddUp$ and $\MulUp$ in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-add-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-mul-namecert}, and the comparison classifier of $\OrderUp$ in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-order-namecert}. The certificate is a local radius-comparison ledger for nonnegative entries; it is not a quotient equality principle, a host square-root computation, or an ambient completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[AM-GM inequality carrier]
\label{def:am-gm-inequality-carrier}
An $\AmGmInequalityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(D,Q,R,X,Y,S,M,O,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ radius ledger, recording the L10 dyadic tolerance used to read both sides of the comparison.
\item $Q$ is the $\RatUp$ nonnegative rational source row, exposing the two rational entries and their dyadic readbacks.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ radius-consumer row, used only after the rational and dyadic rows have been displayed.
\item $X$ is the $\AddUp$ arithmetic row naming the arithmetic-mean numerator and its radius ledger.
\item $Y$ is the $\MulUp$ arithmetic row naming the product side and its radius ledger.
\item $S$ is the nonnegative square-comparison row, recording the finite certificate that the displayed difference is a square or a dyadic lower bound for a square.
\item $M$ is the mean-comparison row, exposing the inequality route from $X,Y,S$ to the radius comparison.
\item $O$ is the $\OrderUp$ row that turns the radius comparison into an ordered AM-GM judgment.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the dyadic, rational, Real, addition, multiplication, square, mean, order, replay, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from $D,Q$ through $X,Y,S,M,O$ and then to $R$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\AmGmInequalityUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\AddUp$, $\MulUp$, $\OrderUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\AmGmInequalityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $D,Q,R,X,Y,S,M,O,H,C,P,N$. It stores no host real equality, quotient representative, selected square root, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness coordinate.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[AM-GM inequality NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:am-gm-inequality-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\AmGmInequalityUp$ carrier $A=(D,Q,R,X,Y,S,M,O,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\AmGmInequalityUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed rows, nonnegative rational source exposure through $Q$, dyadic radius accounting through $D$, addition routing through $X$, multiplication routing through $Y$, square-comparison exposure through $S$, mean-comparison routing through $M$, order export through $O$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:am-gm-inequality-carrier}. The finite tuple displays the dyadic radius ledger, rational source row, Real consumer row, arithmetic rows, square-comparison row, mean-comparison row, order row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming surface.

Read the route in dependency order. The rational nonnegativity data are first read through $Q$ and measured against the dyadic radius ledger $D$. The addition row $X$ and multiplication row $Y$ then provide the two arithmetic sides of the AM-GM comparison, while $S$ records the square comparison that makes the difference nonnegative.

The order row $O$ exports only the comparison already routed through $M$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that finite route and add no host equality, quotient representative, square-root choice, countable-choice schedule, or ambient Real completeness principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[AM-GM inequality L10 radius handoff]
\label{thm:am-gm-inequality-l10-radius-handoff}
Every Real-radius consumer of an accepted $\AmGmInequalityUp$ carrier reads the comparison through the finite route
$$
D,Q,X,Y,S,M,O,R,H,C,P,N .
$$
In particular, the L10 handoff from dyadic and rational rows to $\RealUp$ estimates is mediated by $\AddUp$, $\MulUp$, and $\OrderUp$ rows before any Real-facing radius estimate is public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:am-gm-inequality-namecert-obligations}. They bind public comparison reads to the dyadic, rational, addition, multiplication, square-comparison, mean-comparison, and order rows.

The Real-radius row $R$ is downstream of that route. A consumer cannot inspect $R$ as an independent Real completeness claim, because the carrier exposes it only after the arithmetic and order rows have already replayed the L10 radius ledger.

The transport and replay rows preserve the same finite route. Since the carrier contains no quotient equality, selected square root, host arithmetic oracle, countable choice, or ambient completeness coordinate, the Real-facing estimate is exactly the displayed AM-GM radius handoff.
\end{proof}
