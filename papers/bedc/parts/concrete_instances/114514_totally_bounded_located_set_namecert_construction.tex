\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-totally-bounded-located-set-namecert}
\origin{human}

Bishop and Bridges use located subsets and finite nets as separate constructive interfaces before compactness arguments are allowed to read them together. The $\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp$ packet records that joint L10 metric-frontier surface over the located-subset carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}, the finite-net carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the ambient metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the rational-window readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal distance seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It supplies later compactness, projection, completion, and uniformity consumers with displayed finite-net plus located-distance data, while making no claim of open-cover compactness, nearest-point choice, quotient subset equality, or full $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Totally bounded located set carrier]
\label{def:totally-bounded-located-set-carrier}
A $\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(X,A,B,Q,R,E,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the ambient $\MetricUp$ source row, including admitted points and metric-distance observations.
\item $A$ is the $\LocatedSetUp$ row for the displayed subset, including admission and located-distance comparisons from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}.
\item $B$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row, including for each accepted tolerance request a finite bundle of centers and coverage witnesses.
\item $Q$ is the located-distance window ledger tying each admitted ambient point, subset read, and net-center request to a displayed rational comparison.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational windows used by both the located-distance and finite-net routes.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the displayed distance value attached to the located-set read.
\item $T$ is the handoff row saying that finite-net coverage and located-distance readback are consumed in the same ambient metric route.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the ambient metric, located subset, finite nets, rational windows, real seal, handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from tolerance and point requests through $X,A,B,Q,R,E,T,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp$, $\LocatedSetUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for open-cover compactness, a selected nearest point, quotient equality of subset presentations, an ambient completion, or full $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Totally bounded located set NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:totally-bounded-located-set-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp$ carrier $K=(X,A,B,Q,R,E,T,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the ambient metric row, located subset row, finite-net row, located-distance windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seal, joint handoff, componentwise transport, continuation replay, package provenance, and local naming. Every compactness, projection, completion, or uniformity consumer must display $X,A,B,Q,R,E,T,H,C,P,N$ before it may read any finite-net or located-distance conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:totally-bounded-located-set-carrier}. Its visible rows are exactly the ambient metric source, located subset, finite nets, distance windows, rational readback, Real seal, joint handoff, and structural rows.

The located-set obligations are read from $A,Q,R,E$. These rows provide the finite distance comparison and real-valued seal while keeping every read downstream of the same ambient metric row $X$.

The total-boundedness obligations are read from $B$ over the same source. The finite bundle and its coverage witnesses are therefore available only as displayed packet data, not as an open-cover compactness theorem or hidden choice of centers outside the carrier.

The rows $T,H,C,P,N$ preserve, replay, source, and name the same joint route. Since no excluded compactness, nearest-point, quotient, completion, or real-completeness coordinate appears in the carrier, the stated rows exhaust the NameCert obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-net located-distance handoff]
\label{thm:totally-bounded-located-set-net-distance-handoff}
Every accepted $\TotallyBoundedLocatedSetUp$ packet exposes a finite-net plus located-distance consumer route
$$
\MetricUp
\longmapsto
\LocatedSetUp
\longmapsto
\TotallyBoundedUp
\longmapsto
\RegSeqRatUp
\longmapsto
\RealUp .
$$
Concretely, a consumer reads the ambient metric row $X$, located-subset row $A$, finite-net row $B$, located-distance windows $Q$, rational readback $R$, and real seal $E$ before it may use the handoff row $T$. The route exports no open-cover compactness, nearest-point choice, quotient subset equality, ambient completion, or full $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with $K=(X,A,B,Q,R,E,T,H,C,P,N)$. The metric row $X$ fixes the admitted ambient point and distance observations used by both the located-distance and finite-net reads.

Read $A$ and $B$ next. The located-set row supplies the subset-facing distance interface, while the totally-bounded row supplies finite bundles and coverage witnesses at accepted tolerance requests over the same metric source.

The numerical readback passes through $Q,R,E$. The window ledger $Q$ records the finite comparison, $R$ exposes the rational readback, and $E$ seals the displayed distance as $\RealUp$ data.

The handoff and structural rows $T,H,C,P,N$ only package that same route for downstream consumers. Thus the packet can feed later compactness and completion arguments with finite-net and located-distance data, but it cannot export any of the excluded principles.
\end{proof}
