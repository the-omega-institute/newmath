\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\AntiSpeckerUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-anti-specker-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Specker and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\AntiSpeckerUp$ packet records the compactness boundary where a displayed stream of located real probes is refuted by finite-net data rather than by a selected convergent subsequence or a host compactness theorem. It sits over the finite observation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the compact-metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, and the finite-net coverage row named by $\TotallyBoundedUp$.

\begin{definition}[Anti-Specker carrier]
\label{def:anti-specker-carrier}
An $\AntiSpeckerUp$ carrier is a finite $\mathsf{BHist}$ packet
$$
A=(K,T,S,R,E,F,G,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row whose compact information is exposed only as finite metric and cover data;
\item $T$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row, carrying the finite centers, radii, and membership alternatives used by the boundary argument;
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ row for the inspected probe stream;
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the requested rational windows of the stream;
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for each displayed probe window after the regular-rational readback has been exposed;
\item $F$ is the finite escape ledger, recording the inspected net cell, the displayed probe window, and the positive separation row for that cell;
\item $G$ is the compact finite-net refusal row, stating that the escape ledger is read as a finite obstruction to a universal compact accumulation claim;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact source, net row, stream row, regular readback, real seal, escape ledger, refusal row, provenance, and local name;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact finite-net inspection through stream windows, regular readback, real sealing, finite escape, and refusal;
\item $P$ records $\Pkg$ provenance over $\AntiSpeckerUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\mathsf{BHist}$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\AntiSpeckerUp}$ registration row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for open-cover compactness, sequential compactness, selected subsequences, countable choice, quotient stream equality, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Anti-Specker NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:anti-specker-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\AntiSpeckerUp$ carrier $A=(K,T,S,R,E,F,G,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\AntiSpeckerUp}$ surface consists exactly of compact-source admission through $K$, finite-net admission through $T$, stream-window exposure through $S$, regular-rational readback through $R$, terminal real-name sealing through $E$, finite escape recording through $F$, compact finite-net refusal through $G$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier. The compact source is read through $K$ and the finite-net row $T$, while the probe stream is read through $S$, $R$, and $E$ before any escape datum is available.

The finite escape ledger $F$ is then a row over the already displayed net cell and probe window. It records a positive separation row for that finite inspection, not a global compactness theorem.

The refusal row $G$ packages this finite escape as the local boundary consumed by compactness-facing readers. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. No remaining coordinate can supply open-cover compactness, sequential compactness, selected subsequences, countable choice, quotient stream equality, or real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anti-Specker finite escape route]
\label{thm:anti-specker-finite-escape-route}
Every compactness-facing consumer of an accepted $\AntiSpeckerUp$ packet must read the route
$$
K,T,S,R,E,F,G,H,C,P,N
$$
in that order: compact finite-net data first, then stream and regular-rational windows, then the terminal real-name seal, then the finite escape and refusal rows. The route provides a constructive compactness boundary over displayed finite-net rows and exports no selected subsequence, no countable-choice schedule, no open-cover compactness theorem, no quotient stream equality, and no $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the local NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:anti-specker-namecert-obligations}. They force any consumer to inspect the compact source and finite-net row before reading a stream window or regular-rational readback.

The real-facing seal $E$ is downstream of $S$ and $R$, so a consumer cannot treat the probe as a detached completed real. The escape row $F$ is attached to that displayed window and to the finite-net cell named by $T$.

Finally the refusal row $G$ is read only after the finite escape has been displayed, and $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name this route. The packet therefore remains a finite constructive boundary rather than a sequential compactness, choice, open-cover, quotient, or completeness principle.
\end{proof}
