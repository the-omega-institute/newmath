The certificate stores a local zero island, half-split coordinate, real-defect ledger, phase-residual row, and refusal boundary; it is not a global zeta theorem, a Lee--Yang theorem, an analytic continuation proof, or a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.

\begin{definition}[Local half-split certificate carrier]
\label{def:local-half-split-certificate-carrier}
A $\LocalHalfSplitCertificateUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(Z,D,A,B,R,S,F,P,H,C,G,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Z$ is the local zero-island row, storing the critical-strip point, local disk, dominant two-branch window, and zero-relevant boundary.
\item $D$ is the dominance row for the two visible branches.
\item $A$ and $B$ are the two complex branch rows read through $\ComplexUp$.
\item $R$ is the Rouch\'e tail-control row for the remainder term.
\item $S$ is the half-split row, storing the equal-length coordinate and the real-defect split formula.
\item $F$ is the phase-residual row, storing the opposite-phase congruence and height-facing residual data after the equal-length row has been read.
\item $P$ is the no-hidden-real-correction row, refusing a cofinal correction that cancels a nonzero real defect outside the displayed local island.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the zero island, dominance, branch, tail, split, phase, refusal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay for the local certificate route.
\item $G$ stores $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocalHalfSplitCertificateUp$, $\ComplexUp$, $\ZetaZeroTypeFactorizationUp$, $\ZetaZerosUp$, $\ZetaBasicUp$, $\QuarticSpectralCommonCarrierUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocalHalfSplitCertificateUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a global zero enumeration, analytic continuation, functional equation proof, Lee--Yang stability theorem, or RH conclusion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Local half-split certificate NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:local-half-split-certificate-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LocalHalfSplitCertificateUp$ carrier $L=(Z,D,A,B,R,S,F,P,H,C,G,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LocalHalfSplitCertificateUp}$ surface consists exactly of seven finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the local zero island, dominance, branch, tail, half-split, phase-residual, refusal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item branch dominance, requiring $D$ to expose the two active local branches before any zero certificate is exported;
\item complex branch readback, requiring $A$ and $B$ to be read through the displayed $\ComplexUp$ branch rows;
\item tail control, requiring $R$ to keep the local zero count inside the same island;
\item half-split exactness, requiring $S$ to expose the equal-length coordinate and real-defect split formula;
\item phase-residual exposure, requiring $F$ to remain present whenever the packet exports a half-split zero certificate;
\item non-escape, requiring $P,H,C,G,N$ to refuse hidden real corrections and to transport, replay, source, and name only the displayed finite packet.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:local-half-split-certificate-carrier}. Its zero-facing rows are exactly $Z,D,A,B,R,S,F,P$, and its structural rows are exactly $H,C,G,N$.

The branch obligations force a consumer to read dominance and the two complex branches before the half-split row is available. The tail-control row then keeps the read inside the same local island.

The half-split row supplies equal length and the real-defect split formula. The phase-residual row remains a separate displayed row, so the certificate records the remaining zero-location data rather than hiding it in the real coordinate.

Finally the refusal and structural rows prevent a hidden real correction, unrelated zeta-zero carrier, global analytic principle, or RH conclusion from entering the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local half-split public certificate]
\label{thm:local-half-split-public-certificate}
Every accepted $\LocalHalfSplitCertificateUp$ packet exports a half-split zero certificate only through the displayed route
$$
Z \longmapsto D \longmapsto A,B \longmapsto R \longmapsto S \longmapsto F \longmapsto P \longmapsto H,C,G,N .
$$
If the phase-residual row $F$ is absent, the packet cannot export a half-split zero certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with carrier admission. The local zero-island row $Z$, dominance row $D$, branch rows $A,B$, tail-control row $R$, half-split row $S$, phase-residual row $F$, and refusal row $P$ are all explicit coordinates of the accepted packet.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:local-half-split-certificate-namecert-obligations}. They require dominance before branch readback, branch readback before tail control, tail control before half-split exactness, and phase-residual exposure before export.

Thus removing $F$ removes one of the required rows in the certificate route. The structural rows $H,C,G,N$ can transport and replay only rows that are present, so the packet cannot export the half-split zero certificate without the phase-residual row.
\end{proof}
