\begin{definition}[Layered relation gate question carrier]
\label{def:layered-relation-gate-question-carrier}

$$

\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ and $B$ are the sourceA and sourceB rows naming the two domains being compared.
\item $U$ and $V$ are the carrierA and carrierB rows naming the carrier families visible at the compared layers.
\item $L$ is the layerMap row pairing source layers, carrier rows, and classifier rows.
\item $P$ is the preserved row listing the relation content the question surface permits a consumer to export.

\begin{theorem}[Layered relation gate question NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:layered-relation-gate-question-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LayeredRelationGateQuestionUp$ carrier $Q=(A,B,U,V,L,P,N,E,F,S,H,C,R,M)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LayeredRelationGateQuestionUp}$ surface consists exactly of sourceA/sourceB exposure, carrierA/carrierB exposure, layerMap display, preserved-row disclosure, notPreserved-row disclosure, exactness display, failureBoundary display, strength display, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. A consumer cannot export same-schema language until each of these rows has been displayed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The coordinates of \autoref{def:layered-relation-gate-question-carrier} are exactly the finite rows named in the statement.

The source and carrier questions are $A,B,U,V$. They bind the compared domains and their carrier families before the layer map $L$ can be consumed.

The relation export questions are $P,N,E,F,S$. The preserved row $P$ records what may be exported, while $N$ and $F$ record the refusal and failure boundary that prevent identity readings.

Finally $H,C,R,M$ transport, replay, source, and name the same question packet. Since no other coordinate exists, a consumer cannot obtain same-schema language without first displaying the listed rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Layered relation gate question refusal surface]
\label{thm:layered-relation-gate-question-refusal-surface}
Every accepted $\LayeredRelationGateQuestionUp$ packet refuses identity-misreadings of cross-domain analogy. If a consumer asks to identify sourceA with sourceB, to transfer empirical content, to transfer proof obligations, to import subject-substance, to assert a global frame, or to claim bridge strength without a bridge row, the read is routed to the notPreserved row $N$ and the failureBoundary row $F$ rather than to the preserved row $P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:layered-relation-gate-question-namecert-obligations}. They require $P,N,E,F,S$ to be displayed together before any same-schema answer is exported.

The preserved row $P$ contains only the relation content allowed by the displayed layer map and exactness row. The notPreserved row $N$ lists identity, empirical transfer, proof transfer, subject-substance, global-frame, hidden bridge, and strength over-reading as refused content.

The failureBoundary row $F$ records the action for those refused reads. Therefore any request for an identity-misreading is routed through $N$ and $F$, while the exported same-schema answer remains confined to $P$ with its exactness and strength rows.
\end{proof}
