\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\HamiltonJacobiUp$}

Following Evans and Fleming, the $\HamiltonJacobiUp$ packet records a finite dynamic-programming and variational-control surface for Hamilton-Jacobi readback. It sits beside the $\OdeUp$ flow rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ode-namecert}, the $\DynSystemUp$ transition rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dynsystem-namecert}, the finite quadratic-control ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lqr-namecert}, the Riccati dependency row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riccati-namecert}, the $\RealUp$ scalar surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the $\MatrixUp$ coefficient rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, and the $\VecSpaceUp$ state rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-vecspace-namecert}. The packet names the finite carrier through which later optimal-control consumers may request a Hamiltonian row, value-slope row, and dynamic-programming replay without importing analytic viscosity solutions, global PDE existence, selected optimal controls, host differentiability, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Hamilton-Jacobi carrier]
\label{def:hamilton-jacobi-carrier}
A $\HamiltonJacobiUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
J=(X,U,F,L,H,V,D,R,Q,A,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\VecSpaceUp$ state row and $U$ is the control-row domain visible to the finite dynamic-programming request.
\item $F$ is the $\DynSystemUp$ or $\OdeUp$ transition row tying a state-control pair to the displayed successor row.
\item $L$ is the $\RealUp$ running-cost row, and $H$ is the Hamiltonian row computed from the displayed transition and cost rows.
\item $V$ is the value-row name and $D$ is the finite slope or subgradient witness row used only as packet data.
\item $R$ is the $\RiccatiUp$ comparison row available when the displayed data come from a quadratic linear-control packet.
\item $Q$ is the $\LQRUp$ dependency row recording the finite dynamic-programming route for the quadratic specialization.
\item $A$ is the $\MatrixUp$ coefficient row used by the linear and quadratic control fragments.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,U,F,L,H,V,D,R,Q,A,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\HamiltonJacobiUp$, $\OdeUp$, $\DynSystemUp$, $\LQRUp$, $\RiccatiUp$, $\RealUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\VecSpaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\HamiltonJacobiUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $X,U,F,L,H,V,D,R,Q,A,T,P,N$ and has no coordinate for analytic viscosity solutions, global PDE existence, selected optimal controls, host differentiability, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hamilton-Jacobi NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hamilton-jacobi-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\HamiltonJacobiUp$ carrier $J=(X,U,F,L,H,V,D,R,Q,A,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\HamiltonJacobiUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, state-control exposure, transition or ODE route admission, running-cost and Hamiltonian row exposure, value and slope-row readback, Riccati and LQR dependency availability, matrix-coefficient exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hamilton-jacobi-carrier}. The state-control rows are $X,U$, the transition side is $F$, the scalar cost and Hamiltonian rows are $L,H$, and the value-readback rows are $V,D$.

The finite control dependencies are displayed separately. Rows $R$ and $Q$ expose the Riccati and LQR routes only when a quadratic linear-control packet is being read, while $A$ records the matrix coefficients used by that comparison.

The structural rows $T,P,N$ transport, source, and name the same packet. Since the carrier contains no additional coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hamilton-Jacobi LQR-Riccati route]
\label{thm:hamilton-jacobi-lqr-riccati-route}
Every downstream $\LQRUp$ or $\RiccatiUp$ consumer of an accepted $\HamiltonJacobiUp$ packet must pass through the displayed state row $X$, control row $U$, transition row $F$, cost row $L$, Hamiltonian row $H$, value row $V$, slope row $D$, matrix row $A$, transport row $T$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$ before it can read the quadratic-control rows $Q$ and $R$. The route exports no analytic viscosity solution, global PDE existence theorem, selected optimal controller, host differentiability, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:hamilton-jacobi-namecert-obligations}. They require the state-control, transition, cost, Hamiltonian, value, slope, matrix, transport, provenance, and local naming rows to be visible in the same finite packet.

The LQR and Riccati rows are dependency rows of that packet, not external solvers. A consumer that wants the quadratic-control face must therefore read $Q$ and $R$ through the displayed Hamilton-Jacobi carrier rather than through a host optimal-control theorem.

Because no carrier coordinate stores analytic viscosity solutions, global PDE existence, selected optimal controls, host differentiability, or ambient completeness, the only available LQR-Riccati route is the finite route displayed by $X,U,F,L,H,V,D,R,Q,A,T,P,N$.
\end{proof}
