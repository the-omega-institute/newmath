\chapter{The Golden-Mean Hankel--Turan Discriminant Identity}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-window6-golden-mean-hankel-turan}
\origin{ai}

The golden-mean weighted trace polynomials $C_n(y)=\operatorname{tr}(T_y^n)$ for
$$
T_y=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
carry a finite Hankel--Turan minor whose scalar value is exactly the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial $t^2-t-y$. The identity is
$$
C_n(y)C_{n+2}(y)-C_{n+1}(y)^2=(-y)^n(1+4y).
$$
At $y=1$, where $L_n=C_n(1)$ is the Lucas trace sequence $1,3,4,7,11,18,29,47,\ldots$, this specializes to
$$
L_nL_{n+2}-L_{n+1}^2=5(-1)^n.
$$
Thus the same $2\times2$ trace minor exposes the golden discriminant $1+4y$, and the unit-weight constant is $5=\sqrt{5}^{\,2}$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite golden-mean Hankel--Turan certificate]
\label{thm:window6-golden-mean-hankel-turan}
For the first eight positive trace indices, the polynomial identity
$$
C_n(y)C_{n+2}(y)-C_{n+1}(y)^2=(-y)^n(1+4y)
$$
is verified by direct coefficient arithmetic, and the same certificate records the Lucas specialization at $n=4$:
$$
L_4L_6-L_5^2=5.
$$
\end{theorem}

This is a finite Hankel--Turan discriminant identity for the golden-mean trace sequence; it is not a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and not about the Riemann xi. Its content is the scalar consequence of the second-order trace recurrence attached to $T_y^2=T_y+yI$, recorded as explicit polynomial arithmetic. The determinant side and the trace side meet through the same characteristic polynomial $t^2-t-y$, but this packet claims only the stated finite discriminant window and the displayed Lucas specialization.
