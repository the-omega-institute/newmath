Following Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the
$\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$ packet records the finite closed-interval
route by which a displayed continuous map receives one source tolerance for a
requested target tolerance. It reads its interval endpoints from
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}, its graph and
pointwise-radius rows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert},
its finite window discipline from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert},
its regular rational readback from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert},
its terminal value boundary from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert},
the fixed-point consumer route of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactintervalfixedpoint-namecert}, and the
separate modulus-object surface of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-uniform-continuity-modulus-namecert}.
The packet names a displayed finite-net modulus route for one located closed
interval; it does not claim general Heine--Borel compactness, sequential
compactness, selected limits, quotient reals, or $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Compact interval uniform-continuity carrier]
\label{def:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-carrier}
A $\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
U=(J,F,N_\varepsilon,M,W,Q,E,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $J$ is the $\LocatedIntervalUp$ closed-interval row, including the
displayed endpoint names and the located endpoint-order evidence consumed by
all later reads.
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ graph and pointwise-radius row for the
displayed map on $J$.
\item $N_\varepsilon$ is the finite interval-net row at the requested target
tolerance, listing the inspected subinterval centers and their covering
radii.
\item $M$ is the rational lower-bound fold over the pointwise source radii
attached to those centers, producing the uniform source tolerance.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window surface for the interval
endpoints, inspected centers, nearby input names, and output reads.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row translating the retained finite
windows into regular rational comparisons.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row used only after $W$ and $Q$ have displayed
the required input and output comparisons.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $J,F,N_\varepsilon,M,W,Q,E$
and the local certificate rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a target tolerance request
through the interval net, centerwise pointwise-continuity rows, lower-bound
fold, window/readback rows, and real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedIntervalUp$,
$\ContinuousMapUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$,
$\CompactIntervalFixedPointUp$, $\CompactUniformContinuityModulusUp$,
$\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier is an interval-specific uniform-continuity certificate over
displayed finite rows. It has no coordinate for general Heine--Borel
compactness, sequential compactness, selected limits, quotient real equality,
open-cover subcover selection, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CompactIntervalUniformContinuity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$ carrier
$U=(J,F,N_\varepsilon,M,W,Q,E,H,C,P,L)$, the local
$\NameCert_{\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp}$ surface consists exactly of
the following obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the located interval, continuous-map row, finite
interval-net row, lower-bound fold, finite windows, regular readback, real
seal, componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local
naming;
\item source lock, requiring $J$ and the source of $F$ to name the same
located closed interval before any pointwise radius is read;
\item finite-net coverage, requiring every input comparison to pass through
one displayed center of $N_\varepsilon$ before the pointwise row in $F$ is
used;
\item lower-bound exactness, requiring the uniform source tolerance to be read
from $M$ and not from a host minimum or selected Lebesgue number;
\item window and seal nonescape, requiring each downstream value comparison to
pass through $W$, $Q$, and $E$ before it can be consumed by fixed-point or
modulus-object users.
\end{enumerate}
No obligation may call on general Heine--Borel compactness, sequential
compactness, selected limits, quotient reals, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of
\autoref{def:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-carrier}. The interval row
$J$ and continuous-map row $F$ are the only sources for endpoint, graph, and
pointwise-radius information.

The finite-net obligation is carried by $N_\varepsilon$. Each input comparison
is routed to one displayed center before the corresponding pointwise radius in
$F$ is used, so no open-cover subcover selection or host compactness step is
available.

The uniform source tolerance is then computed by the finite fold $M$. The
window/readback rows $W$ and $Q$ expose exactly the input and output
comparisons needed by the requested target tolerance, and the real seal $E$ is
read only after those rows have been displayed.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same route. Since
the carrier has no coordinate for the excluded compactness or completion
principles, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact interval uniform-continuity source lock]
\label{thm:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-source-lock}
Every accepted $\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$ carrier
$U=(J,F,N_\varepsilon,M,W,Q,E,H,C,P,L)$ must expose the same located closed
interval row $J$ as the source of the continuous-map row $F$ before any
consumer reads $N_\varepsilon$, $M$, $W$, $Q$, or $E$. Thus the finite-net
radius rows, lower-bound fold, finite windows, regular readback, and real seal
all remain locked to the displayed source interval of $F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of
\autoref{def:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-carrier}. The displayed row
$J$ is the only located closed interval coordinate, and $F$ is explicitly the
continuous-map row for the displayed map on $J$.

Now apply the source-lock obligation in
\autoref{thm:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-namecert-obligations}. Before
any pointwise radius is read, the NameCert surface requires $J$ and the source
of $F$ to name the same located closed interval.

The remaining rows are downstream of that obligation. The finite net
$N_\varepsilon$, the lower-bound fold $M$, the window row $W$, the readback row
$Q$, and the real seal $E$ consume pointwise-radius and comparison data only
after the source lock has been exposed, so none of them can be read from a
different source interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact interval finite-net modulus]
\label{thm:compact-interval-uniform-continuity-finite-net-modulus}
For every accepted $\CompactIntervalUniformContinuityUp$ carrier
$U=(J,F,N_\varepsilon,M,W,Q,E,H,C,P,L)$ and every displayed target tolerance,
the source tolerance exported by the packet is obtained by the finite route
$$
J,F,N_\varepsilon,M,W,Q,E,H,C,P,L .
$$
If two input names are read within that source tolerance through $W$ and $Q$,
then the output comparison is accepted only through the same centerwise
pointwise-continuity rows and the same real-seal row $E$. The route is a
finite-net modulus route, not a general compactness theorem or a selected
limit construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the target tolerance request. The continuation row $C$ sends it to
the displayed interval-net row $N_\varepsilon$, whose centers and covering
radii are finite rows over the same located interval $J$.

For each listed center, the continuous-map row $F$ supplies a pointwise source
radius. The fold $M$ computes one rational lower bound from those displayed
radii, giving the source tolerance exported by the packet.

Input names and output values are then inspected through $W$ and translated
through $Q$. The real seal $E$ can be consumed only after these window and
readback rows have displayed the required comparisons.

Thus the modulus route is finite and internal to the packet. It neither
selects a limit point nor appeals to open-cover compactness, sequential
compactness, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
