\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CompactEmbeddingUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-compact-embedding-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Rellich--Kondrachov compactness in the form used by Brezis and Adams--Fournier, the $\CompactEmbeddingUp$ packet records the finite certificate surface for a compact embedding from displayed Sobolev source windows into a compact complete metric target. It sits after the Sobolev embedding carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sobolev-embedding-namecert}, the compact and complete metric surfaces of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The chapter names only the finite embedding, readback, and compact-window rows available to BEDC consumers.

\begin{definition}[Compact embedding carrier]
\label{def:compact-embedding-carrier}
A $\CompactEmbeddingUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(F,S,K,M,Q,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is the finite source-function window family naming the Sobolev functions, source-domain observations, target observations, and boundedness gauges inspected by this packet.
\item $S$ is the $\SobolevEmbeddingUp$ row that records the displayed embedding comparison for the source windows, including the exponent and codomain route visible to the consumer.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactUp$ row exposing the finite compactness window used for target observations.
\item $M$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ row used only to read Cauchy and limit-window data inside the target surface.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ tolerance and regular readback row attached to the source-function and target-metric windows.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row, reached only after $F,S,K,M,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source windows, Sobolev-embedding rows, compact rows, complete-metric rows, regular rational readbacks, Real seals, continuation rows, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from source-function windows through embedding comparison, compact target inspection, complete-metric readback, regular rational tolerance, Real sealing, transport, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactEmbeddingUp$, $\SobolevEmbeddingUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactEmbeddingUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a selected subsequence, open-cover compactness, quotient equality of functions, host representatives, countable choice, trace theory, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness beyond the displayed seal row.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact embedding NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-embedding-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompactEmbeddingUp$ carrier
$$
E=(F,S,K,M,Q,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CompactEmbeddingUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite source-function windows, SobolevEmbedding row, Compact row, CompleteMetric row, RegSeqRat tolerance readback, Real seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. These rows bind the compact-embedding handoff without adding selected subsequences, open-cover compactness, quotient function equality, host representatives, countable choice, trace theory, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:compact-embedding-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes exactly $F,S,K,M,Q,R,H,C,P,N$.

The analytic route is then read row by row. The source-function windows are in $F$, the embedding comparison is in $S$, the compact target window is in $K$, and the complete-metric readback is in $M$.

The tolerance and endpoint observations are finite. They are read through $Q$ and then sealed through $R$, so a consumer cannot replace the displayed RegSeqRat route by an ambient completeness theorem or a selected limit.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no excluded analytic principle is a carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact embedding finite route]
\label{thm:compact-embedding-finite-route}
Every consumer read exported by an accepted $\CompactEmbeddingUp$ packet factors through the finite route
$$
\SobolevEmbeddingUp,\ \CompactUp,\ \CompleteMetricUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RealUp
$$
using the displayed rows $F,S,K,M,Q,R,H,C,P,N$. The route first exposes source-function windows and the SobolevEmbedding comparison, then compact and complete-metric target windows, then regular rational tolerance readback, and only then the terminal Real seal. It supplies no selected subsequence, open-cover compactness principle, quotient function space, host representative, countable-choice schedule, trace theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a consumer asking for compact-embedding data. Carrier admission confines the request to the finite source-function windows $F$ and the embedding comparison row $S$, so the consumer first sees the same SobolevEmbedding surface cited in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sobolev-embedding-namecert}.

Next read the target side. The rows $K$ and $M$ expose only the compact and complete-metric windows named by this packet; the regular rational tolerances in $Q$ are the only numerical readbacks supplied to the consumer.

The terminal seal $R$ is downstream of those rows. Componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same finite route and do not add a second analytic endpoint.

The L10 completion anchor is therefore the RegSeqRat tolerance readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} together with the displayed Real seal. No quotient equality, choice schedule, selected subsequence, open-cover compactness principle, trace theorem, or Real completeness object can be read from the carrier.
\end{proof}
