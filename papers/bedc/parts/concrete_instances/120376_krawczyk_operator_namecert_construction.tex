\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\KrawczykOperatorUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-krawczyk-operator-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Krawczyk and Moore--Kearfott--Cloud interval analysis, the $\KrawczykOperatorUp$ packet records the finite preconditioned interval operator used to validate and narrow boxes for nonlinear zero searches. It separates the Krawczyk image from the surrounding $\IntervalNewtonUp$ row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervalnewton-namecert}: the operator is a named L10 certificate whose arithmetic is read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervalarithmetic-namecert}, whose Newton comparison route is read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-newtoniteration-namecert}, whose enclosure provenance may use the Taylor rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-taylormodel-namecert}, and whose rational and real readback passes through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[Krawczyk operator carrier]
\label{def:krawczyk-operator-carrier}
A $\KrawczykOperatorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(B,F,J,A,R,M,I,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the input interval box, including the coordinate boxes and their $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ endpoint rows.
\item $F$ is the interval enclosure of the function values on $B$, read only through interval-arithmetic operation rows.
\item $J$ is the interval matrix or slope enclosure for the derivative data over $B$.
\item $A$ is the approximate inverse or preconditioner row attached to the chosen center.
\item $R$ is the center and residual row, recording the displayed center $c$ and the preconditioned residual $A F(c)$.
\item $M$ is the interval matrix row for $I-AJ$, including its interval product and subtraction ledger.
\item $I$ is the Krawczyk image row, recording the displayed enclosure $c-AF(c)+(I-AJ)(B-c)$.
\item $V$ is the containment and narrowing validation row, recording whether $I \subseteq B$, $I \cap B$, or the refused containment boundary is the public result.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for boxes, enclosures, derivative rows, preconditioners, residuals, interval matrices, Krawczyk images, and validation rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from interval arithmetic to the image computation, from validation to narrowing, and from readback rows to the public certificate.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KrawczykOperatorUp$, $\IntervalArithmeticUp$, $\IntervalNewtonUp$, $\NewtonIterationUp$, $\TaylorModelUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KrawczykOperatorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports only the displayed interval operator and its validation ledger. It does not assert host existence of a zero, global uniqueness, floating-point semantics, differentiability outside the supplied slope row, quotient real equality, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Krawczyk operator NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:krawczyk-operator-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\KrawczykOperatorUp$ carrier
$$
K=(B,F,J,A,R,M,I,V,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\KrawczykOperatorUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, box exposure through $B$, function enclosure exposure through $F$, derivative or slope enclosure exposure through $J$, preconditioner exposure through $A$, center and residual exposure through $R$, interval matrix exposure through $M$, Krawczyk image exposure through $I$, containment or narrowing validation through $V$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The only arithmetic rows available to a Krawczyk read are the displayed box, function enclosure, derivative or slope enclosure, preconditioner, residual, interval matrix, image, and validation rows.

The interval-arithmetic route computes $F$, $J$, $M$, and $I$ inside the same finite packet. The row $A$ is only a displayed approximate inverse or preconditioner, and $R$ is only the center-residual ledger attached to that preconditioner.

Validation is read through $V$. It records containment, narrowing by intersection, or refusal of containment without adding a root-existence theorem or a uniqueness theorem.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for host zeros, global uniqueness, floating-point semantics, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Krawczyk operator interval-Newton handoff]
\label{thm:krawczyk-operator-interval-newton-handoff}
Every $\IntervalNewtonUp$ consumer that uses a $\KrawczykOperatorUp$ packet must read the Krawczyk route
$$
B,F,J,A,R,M,I,V,H,C,P,N
$$
before treating a correction row as a validated enclosure. The handoff supplies the named image $I=c-AF(c)+(I-AJ)(B-c)$ and the containment or narrowing validation row $V$ to the interval-Newton packet; it supplies no solver oracle, host zero selection, global uniqueness claim, floating-point semantics, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the interval-Newton correction boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervalnewton-namecert}. That chapter may consume a Krawczyk correction row, but the row is public only after the box, enclosure, derivative, and validation data have been displayed.

The present carrier names those data as $B,F,J,A,R,M,I,V$. The interval-arithmetic rows compute the residual and image, while the $\NewtonIterationUp$ and $\TaylorModelUp$ provenance rows record how the displayed derivative or slope data were obtained.

The rational and real readback remains finite. Any $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer reaches the image and validation rows only through the displayed replay and provenance rows $C,P$ together with the transport and local name $H,N$.

Thus the interval-Newton consumer receives a named finite operator certificate rather than an unnamed correction row. Since no carrier field selects a zero or proves global uniqueness, the handoff remains an interval validation route.
\end{proof}
