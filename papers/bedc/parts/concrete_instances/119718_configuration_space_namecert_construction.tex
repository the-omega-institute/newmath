\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-configuration-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following the classical configuration-space treatment of ordered collision-free point tuples, the $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$ packet records the finite inspection surface for $n$ displayed points in an ambient space before any quotient by permutations, compactification, or path-space completion is available. It sits after the ambient open-set discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the distance and separation rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the finite index spine of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finset-namecert}, and the membership provenance of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-setlike-namecert}. It also exposes the finite ordered-strand boundary consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-braidgroup-namecert}, but it does not claim braid isotopy completeness or unordered configuration quotients.

\begin{definition}[ConfigurationSpace carrier]
\label{def:configuration-space-carrier}
A $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
X=(I,M,T,U,Q,A,D,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\FinSetUp$ index row naming the finite ordered tuple positions.
\item $M$ is the ambient $\MetricUp$ row and $T$ is the compatible $\TopologyUp$ row used for open-neighbourhood reads.
\item $U$ is the tuple row, assigning each displayed index in $I$ a point admitted by the ambient carrier.
\item $Q$ is the $\SetLikeUp$ membership row recording that every tuple coordinate belongs to the ambient space and to the displayed configuration window.
\item $A$ is the pairwise apartness row: for each distinct pair of indices in $I$, it displays positive separation or an equivalent collision-refusal witness.
\item $D$ is the diagonal-refusal row, recording that no admitted read of $U$ factors through the collision diagonal.
\item $W$ is the finite observation-window row exposing the open neighbourhoods and separation thresholds read by downstream consumers.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the finite index row, ambient metric and topology rows, tuple row, membership row, apartness row, diagonal-refusal row, window row, provenance row, and local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the same finite configuration rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$, $\FinSetUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\SetLikeUp$, $\BraidGroupUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConfigurationSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
No coordinate supplies an unordered quotient, a completed path space, compactification boundary, global section of a covering map, or host equality of point tuples.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ConfigurationSpace NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:configuration-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$ carrier $X=(I,M,T,U,Q,A,D,W,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ConfigurationSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite index row, ambient metric and topology rows, tuple row, set-membership row, pairwise apartness row, collision-diagonal refusal row, observation window, componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming row. Every consumer-facing read must display $I,M,T,U,Q,A,D,W$ before the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ are consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:configuration-space-carrier}. The finite mathematical surface is exhausted by the displayed rows $I,M,T,U,Q,A,D,W$; the remaining rows only transport, replay, source, and name that surface.

The index and membership obligations are finite. The row $I$ supplies the tuple positions through $\FinSetUp$, while $Q$ records ambient and window membership through $\SetLikeUp$, so no host powerset or infinite family of coordinates is available.

The topology and metric obligations are likewise displayed. The rows $M,T,W$ expose only the neighbourhood and separation data read in the current window, and $A,D$ record the pairwise apartness and diagonal refusal used by the configuration read. Thus the NameCert surface stops at the finite collision-free tuple packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ConfigurationSpace collision nonescape]
\label{thm:configuration-space-collision-nonescape}
Every accepted $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$ read refuses collision escape through the finite route
$$
\FinSetUp,\MetricUp,\TopologyUp,\SetLikeUp
  \longrightarrow U,Q,A,D,W
  \longrightarrow H,C,P,N .
$$
In particular, if two displayed indices are distinct in $I$, every downstream read of their coordinates must pass through the apartness row $A$ and the diagonal-refusal row $D$ before it can be replayed as a configuration-space observation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a read of two displayed tuple positions. Distinctness is visible only through the finite index row $I$, and the corresponding coordinate membership is visible only through the tuple and membership rows $U,Q$.

The collision-refusal step is forced by the carrier order. The pairwise apartness row $A$ supplies the separation witness for the two indices, and $D$ records that the read does not factor through the collision diagonal.

Finally replay through $W,H,C,P,N$. These rows can expose neighbourhoods, transport the same finite evidence, and name the packet, but they cannot add a missing collision witness or replace $A,D$ by host tuple equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ConfigurationSpace finite product route]
\label{thm:configuration-space-finite-product-route}
Every product-facing consumer of an accepted $\ConfigurationSpaceUp$ packet receives the ordered finite tuple through the $\FinSetUp$ index spine and the ambient $\MetricUp$ and $\TopologyUp$ rows, with $\SetLikeUp$ membership and pairwise apartness displayed before any $\BraidGroupUp$ strand-boundary read is available. The route exports a finite ordered configuration packet and does not claim unordered quotient configurations, braid isotopy completeness, compactification, or path lifting.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the product request through $I$ and $U$. The finite index spine determines the ordered tuple slots, and the tuple row supplies the coordinate reads without constructing an ambient host product object.

Next expose the ambient rows. The metric and topology data in $M,T$ and the membership row $Q$ place each coordinate inside the displayed ambient window; the apartness and diagonal-refusal rows $A,D$ then certify collision-free status for every displayed pair.

Only after those rows are visible can a strand-boundary consumer read the packet through the $\BraidGroupUp$ provenance in $P$. That boundary sees ordered, collision-free strands, not an unordered quotient, a completed braid isotopy theorem, or a compactified configuration space.
\end{proof}
