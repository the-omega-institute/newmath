Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\CauchyModulusThinningUp$ packet records the finite route by which an explicit Cauchy modulus is thinned to a monotone cofinal schedule before a regular rational or real-name consumer reads it. It sits over the modulus surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchymodulus-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular rational handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a non-quotient thinning certificate; it supplies no selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[Cauchy modulus thinning carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-modulus-thinning-carrier}
A $\CauchyModulusThinningUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
X=(M,T,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the source $\CauchyModulusUp$ row, carrying the original explicit modulus request and its finite threshold answers.
\item $T$ is the monotone thinning route, recording the retained threshold schedule and the finite proof that each retained threshold dominates the source answer needed at the same dyadic depth.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger, recording the dyadic precision, scale, and comparison rows used to read the source and thinned schedules.
\item $W$ is the retained $\StreamNameUp$ window opened at the thinned threshold.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row obtained from the retained window and dyadic ledger.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $M,T,D,W,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the modulus, thinning, tolerance, window, regular handoff, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the source modulus through the thinned threshold, dyadic ledger, retained window, regular handoff, and real seal.

\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyModulusThinningUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,T,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected representatives, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy modulus thinning NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-modulus-thinning-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyModulusThinningUp$ carrier $X=(M,T,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyModulusThinningUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the ten displayed rows, source-modulus exposure through $M$, monotone thinning dominance through $T$, dyadic tolerance exactness through $D$, retained stream-window visibility through $W$, regular rational handoff through $R$, real-seal nonescape through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-modulus-thinning-carrier}. The analytic rows are exactly $M,T,D,W,R,E$, and the structural rows are exactly $H,C,P,N$.

The source-modulus and thinning obligations are read first. Row $M$ supplies the explicit threshold answer, while row $T$ records the retained monotone schedule and its finite dominance comparison against the source answer at the displayed dyadic depth.

The tolerance and window obligations then force the read through $D$ and $W$. The dyadic ledger fixes the precision scale, and the retained stream window is opened only at the thinned threshold named by $T$.

Finally the regular and real-facing rows are downstream. The row $R$ is obtained from the retained window and tolerance ledger, and the seal row $E$ is reachable only after that handoff. Since $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route and the carrier lists no quotient, selected limit, choice schedule, host equality, or ambient completeness row, the displayed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:cauchy-modulus-thinning-real-handoff}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted packet and read the source modulus. The row $M$ answers the displayed dyadic request, while $T$ records a monotone retained threshold that dominates the source answer for that request.

Read the dyadic and stream rows next. The ledger $D$ fixes the tolerance scale, and the stream window $W$ is opened only at the retained threshold. Therefore a regular rational consumer cannot bypass the thinning route.

\end{proof}
