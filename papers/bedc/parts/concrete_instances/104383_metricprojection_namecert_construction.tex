\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MetricProjectionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-metricprojection-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Hilbert projection theory and constructive convex analysis, the $\MetricProjectionUp$ packet records the nearest-point surface for a displayed closed convex subset of a Hilbert carrier as finite BEDC data. It sits after the Hilbert source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hilbert-namecert}, the closed convex source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the metric distance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the complete metric limit surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, and the real-name readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the projection route without importing a host nearest-point selector, quotient equality of points, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient real completeness.

\begin{definition}[MetricProjection carrier]\label{def:metric-projection-carrier}
A $\MetricProjectionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $H$ is the $\HilbertUp$ source row, including the inner-product carrier, norm ledger, and induced metric route.
\item $C$ is the closed convex source row, recording the admitted subset, convex-combination stability, and closedness boundary over the Hilbert source.
\item $D$ is the metric distance ledger, exposing only displayed $\MetricUp$ distance comparisons between the external point, subset witnesses, and candidate endpoints.
\item $I$ is the located infimum budget for the squared-distance values over the displayed closed convex source.
\item $W$ is the approximating minimizing window, consisting of finite candidates, tolerances, and the monotone comparison schedule used to approach the located infimum.
\item $E$ is the projection endpoint row, recording the carried subset point admitted as the limit of the minimizing window.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $H,C,D,I,W,E,R,P,N$.
\item $R$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay from distance comparisons through located infimum, minimizing windows, endpoint admission, and closest-point reads.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\HilbertUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricProjectionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The local classifier compares accepted carriers only by these displayed rows. It has no coordinate for a host projection map, selected representative, quotient equality of Hilbert points, ambient choice principle, or global completeness theorem outside the named sources.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MetricProjection NameCert obligations]\label{thm:metric-projection-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\MetricProjectionUp$ carrier $Q=(H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MetricProjectionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier exposure for the Hilbert source, closed convex source, metric distance ledger, located infimum budget, approximating minimizing window, projection endpoint, componentwise transport, replay route, package provenance, and local naming row. Every projection-facing consumer must read the endpoint through $H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N$ in that order of dependency, and no consumer receives a host nearest-point selector, quotient representative, trichotomy decision, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem from the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier exposure is projection from \autoref{def:metric-projection-carrier}. The tuple lists the Hilbert source, closed convex source, metric distance ledger, located infimum budget, minimizing window, endpoint, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming row as its only coordinates.

The projection route is finite. Distance reads pass through $D$, lower-bound and approximation reads pass through $I$ and $W$, and endpoint admission passes through $E$ after the closed convex source row $C$ and complete metric source are displayed.

The remaining rows preserve the same route. Transport $T$ moves only the displayed coordinates, replay $R$ consumes them in the stated dependency order, and $P,N$ record provenance and local naming. Since the carrier lacks rows for host selectors, quotient representatives, trichotomy, countable choice, and ambient real completeness, those objects cannot be exported by the local NameCert.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricProjection closest-point nonescape]\label{thm:metric-projection-closest-point-nonescape}
Every accepted closest-point read exported by a $\MetricProjectionUp$ carrier factors through the displayed located infimum budget $I$, approximating minimizing window $W$, and projection endpoint row $E$ over the Hilbert, closed convex, metric, complete metric, and real readback sources. Thus a consumer can use the carried endpoint only as a finite BEDC projection endpoint, not as a host nearest-point selector or an unconstrained choice of representative.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MetricProjectionUp.MetricProjectionCarrier\_closest\_point\_nonescape}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted closest-point read. By \autoref{thm:metric-projection-namecert-obligations}, the read reaches the local naming row only through $H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N$.

The rows $I$ and $W$ provide the located lower bound and finite approximation route for squared distances. The endpoint row $E$ is admitted only after the minimizing window is replayed over the closed convex source and the complete metric limit surface.

Consequently the exported point is tied to the displayed finite route. Changing the endpoint by appealing to a host projection map, a selected representative, or a choice principle is not a move inside the carrier, so such data cannot escape through the $\MetricProjectionUp$ packet.
\end{proof}

The endpoint row of a $\MetricProjectionUp$ packet consumes $\LocatedSetUp$ only after the located distance route has displayed the parent $\MetricUp$ row, MetricSubspace admission, located-distance ledger, rational windows, $\RealUp$ seal, and $\LocatedMetricUp$ handoff; see \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}.

\begin{theorem}[MetricProjection endpoint separation from LocatedSet coordinates]\label{thm:metricprojection-locatedset-endpoint-separation}
A projection endpoint exported by an accepted $\MetricProjectionUp$ carrier cannot be read as a $\LocatedSetUp$ coordinate. It is supplied only as the consumer endpoint row $E$ after the $\LocatedSetUp$ route has displayed the parent metric row, MetricSubspace admission, located-distance ledger, rational windows, $\RealUp$ seal, and $\LocatedMetricUp$ handoff.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MetricProjectionUp.MetricProjectionCarrier\_locatedset\_endpoint\_separation}
\begin{proof}
Use the carrier route of \autoref{def:metric-projection-carrier}. The endpoint row $E$ appears after the Hilbert, closed convex, metric, located-infimum, and minimizing-window rows have been displayed, and it is replayed only through $T,R,P,N$ as a projection-facing endpoint.

The LocatedSet route supplies the sibling distance data before this endpoint is read. Its parent metric row, MetricSubspace admission, located-distance ledger, rational windows, $\RealUp$ seal, and $\LocatedMetricUp$ handoff are source rows for the projection consumer, not coordinates of the endpoint itself.

Therefore a consumer cannot invert the endpoint row into a $\LocatedSetUp$ coordinate. The only accepted read is the forward projection route: first the located-set distance surface, then the $\MetricProjectionUp$ endpoint row $E$ as the consumer endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricProjection carrier stability obligation]\label{thm:metric-projection-obligation-carrier-stability}
Let $Q=(H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N)$ and $Q'=(H',C',D',I',W',E',T',R',P',N')$ be accepted $\MetricProjectionUp$ carriers whose displayed Hilbert source, closed convex source, metric distance ledger, located infimum budget, minimizing window, endpoint row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows agree componentwise through the classifier of \autoref{def:metric-projection-carrier}. Then every accepted projection-facing read through $Q$ transports to the corresponding read through $Q'$ without adding a host nearest-point selector, quotient representative, trichotomy decision, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the carrier inventory of \autoref{def:metric-projection-carrier}. The classifier compares exactly $H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N$, so any transported read must preserve those rows componentwise.

Apply \autoref{thm:metric-projection-namecert-obligations}. The local NameCert route reads carrier admission, distance ledgers, located infimum budget, minimizing window, endpoint, transport, replay, provenance, and naming in the displayed order.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:metric-projection-closest-point-nonescape}. Since the endpoint can escape only through $I,W,E$ and the structural rows, classifier stability cannot introduce a selector, quotient representative, trichotomy decision, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness row.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricProjection located-infimum replay obligation]\label{thm:metric-projection-obligation-located-infimum-replay}
For an accepted $\MetricProjectionUp$ carrier $Q=(H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N)$, every closest-point replay that consumes the located infimum budget must first read the Hilbert row $H$, closed convex row $C$, metric distance row $D$, and located infimum row $I$, then replay the minimizing window $W$ before the endpoint row $E$ can be admitted. The replay is the route displayed by \autoref{thm:metric-projection-namecert-obligations} and remains inside the LocatedSet endpoint-separation boundary of \autoref{thm:metricprojection-locatedset-endpoint-separation}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:metric-projection-namecert-obligations}. They place $H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N$ in dependency order, with distance comparison and infimum data preceding the endpoint.

The located infimum row $I$ is not an endpoint. It supplies the lower-bound and approximation budget that $W$ replays through finite minimizing windows over the displayed closed convex source.

Use \autoref{thm:metricprojection-locatedset-endpoint-separation}. The LocatedSet route contributes parent metric, subspace, distance, rational-window, Real-seal, and located-metric source rows before the projection endpoint is read, so the replay cannot collapse into a LocatedSet coordinate or hidden closest-point selector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricProjection endpoint admission obligation]\label{thm:metric-projection-obligation-endpoint-admission}
For an accepted $\MetricProjectionUp$ carrier $Q=(H,C,D,I,W,E,T,R,P,N)$, the endpoint row $E$ is admitted only after the minimizing-window row $W$ has replayed the located-infimum route over $H,C,D,I$ and after the LocatedSet source route has supplied its endpoint-separation boundary. Any consumer of $E$ must then pass through $T,R,P,N$ before using the endpoint as a projection-facing row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier and apply \autoref{thm:metric-projection-obligation-located-infimum-replay}. The rows $H,C,D,I,W$ are the only route by which the endpoint can be reached from the Hilbert, convex, metric, and located-infimum data.

Next apply \autoref{thm:metricprojection-locatedset-endpoint-separation}. It separates the projection endpoint from the LocatedSet coordinate route and forces the source handoff to remain upstream of $E$.

Finally use \autoref{thm:metric-projection-closest-point-nonescape}. The endpoint consumer must pass through transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows $T,R,P,N$, so endpoint admission remains a finite BEDC packet boundary and not a host projection principle.
\end{proof}
