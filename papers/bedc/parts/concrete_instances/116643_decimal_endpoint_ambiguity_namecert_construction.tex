\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-decimal-endpoint-ambiguity-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real approximation and the
classical terminating-decimal convention, the $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$
packet records the finite boundary where a terminating decimal window and a
repeating-nine window present the same endpoint route. It sits after
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-decimalexpansion-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-decimal-endpoint-normalization-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-signed-decimal-expansion-namecert}, and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-decimal-normal-form-namecert}, and it
hands all real-facing reads through
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only the
finite endpoint ambiguity surface for inspected decimal windows; it does not
assert uniqueness of all decimal expansions, select a host real
representative, or quotient arbitrary decimal streams.

\begin{definition}[DecimalEndpointAmbiguity carrier]
\label{def:decimal-endpoint-ambiguity-carrier}
A $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(D,E,S,M,Q,R,Y,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DecimalExpansionUp$ source ledger for the inspected
  finite decimal prefixes.
\item $E$ is the $\DecimalEndpointNormalizationUp$ endpoint row that records
  the terminating side and the repeating-nine side inside the same window.
\item $S$ is the $\SignedDecimalExpansionUp$ sign and zero-window row, when
  the endpoint ambiguity is read through a signed presentation.
\item $M$ is the $\RealDecimalNormalFormUp$ normal-form route reached after
  the endpoint row has been displayed.
\item $Q$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row attached to the same
  endpoint window.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row produced from the normalized
  endpoint window.
\item $Y$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $D,E,S,M,Q,R$
  have been exposed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the decimal source,
  endpoint-normalization, signed-window, normal-form, dyadic, regular,
  real-seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the decimal window through
  endpoint normalization, normal-form readback, dyadic tolerance, regular
  handoff, and the real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$,
  $\DecimalExpansionUp$, $\DecimalEndpointNormalizationUp$,
  $\SignedDecimalExpansionUp$, $\RealDecimalNormalFormUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$,
  $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and
  $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted endpoint-ambiguity packets only through
$D,E,S,M,Q,R,Y,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for global decimal uniqueness,
quotient stream equality, selected representatives, arbitrary real-name
trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DecimalEndpointAmbiguity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:decimal-endpoint-ambiguity-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$ carrier
$$
A=(D,E,S,M,Q,R,Y,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp}$ surface consists exactly
of carrier admission for the displayed rows, endpoint-pair exposure through
$E$, signed-window compatibility through $S$, normal-form routing through
$M$, dyadic tolerance exposure through $Q$, regular rational handoff through
$R$, terminal $\RealUp$ seal exposure through $Y$, componentwise $\hsame$
transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
Every consumer of the terminating-versus-repeating-nine boundary must read
that finite surface before treating the endpoint as real-facing data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from
\autoref{def:decimal-endpoint-ambiguity-carrier}. The visible decimal
coordinates are exactly $D,E,S,M,Q,R,Y$, while $H,C,P,N$ carry transport,
replay, provenance, and local naming.

Endpoint-pair exposure is local to $E$. The terminating side and the
repeating-nine side are compared only because the endpoint-normalization row
lists them inside the same inspected finite window.

Signed-window compatibility uses $S$ only as a displayed signed presentation
of that same endpoint window. It cannot introduce a second magnitude stream or
an endpoint selector outside the packet.

The real-facing route is ordered by $M,Q,R,Y$. Normal form is read first,
then dyadic tolerance, then the regular rational handoff, and only then the
terminal real seal. The structural rows preserve this finite route, so no
global uniqueness, quotient equality, chosen representative, trichotomy,
countable-choice schedule, or completeness row can be consumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DecimalEndpointAmbiguity real normal-form route]
\label{thm:decimal-endpoint-ambiguity-real-normal-form-route}
Every $\RealUp$ read of an accepted $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$ packet
factors through the finite route
$$
\DecimalExpansionUp,\ \DecimalEndpointNormalizationUp,\ \SignedDecimalExpansionUp,\ \RealDecimalNormalFormUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \RealUp .
$$
The route records the terminating and repeating-nine windows as finite
endpoint data before the normal-form, dyadic, regular-rational, and real-seal
rows are public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted packet $A=(D,E,S,M,Q,R,Y,H,C,P,N)$. The source row
$D$ supplies only finite decimal prefixes, and $E$ supplies the endpoint
normalization row for the same inspected window.

The signed row $S$ is read before the normal-form route $M$. Thus positive,
negative, and zero-window presentations are attached to the displayed
endpoint row rather than to an independent stream representative.

Now read the arithmetic handoff rows. The dyadic tolerance row $Q$ and the
regular rational row $R$ are reached from the normal-form route, and the
terminal real seal $Y$ is downstream of both.

Finally apply the structural rows $H,C,P,N$. They transport, replay, source,
and name the same finite endpoint surface, so the real read is a route through
the displayed packet rather than a proof of decimal uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DecimalEndpointAmbiguity nonescape]
\label{thm:decimal-endpoint-ambiguity-nonescape}
An accepted $\DecimalEndpointAmbiguityUp$ packet exports no global decimal
uniqueness theorem, quotient stream equality, selected endpoint
representative, arbitrary real-name trichotomy, countable-choice schedule, or
ambient $\RealUp$ completeness witness. Every endpoint read remains inside
the displayed rows $D,E,S,M,Q,R,Y,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier inventory. The only endpoint ambiguity coordinate is
$E$, and it is tied to the finite decimal source $D$ and signed-window row
$S$.

The normal-form and real-facing rows $M,Q,R,Y$ are downstream of that finite
endpoint row. They can transport an already displayed terminating or
repeating-nine window to a regular rational or real seal, but they cannot
select representatives for arbitrary decimal streams.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ do not add carrier data. They preserve,
replay, source, and name the displayed route. Since the excluded objects are
not coordinates of $A$, no accepted consumer can read them from the packet.
\end{proof}
