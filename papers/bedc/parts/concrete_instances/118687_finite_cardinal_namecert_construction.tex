\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteCardinalUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-cardinal-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Dedekind and Cantor on finite equipotence, the $\FiniteCardinalUp$ packet records cardinal comparison only through displayed finite bijection and counting ledgers. It sits after the membership discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-setlike-namecert}, the natural-number endpoint of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nat-namecert}, the Kuratowski finite-set presentation of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kuratowski-finite-set-namecert}, and the finite-net consumer route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-hyperspace-finite-net-namecert}. The packet names finite cardinality without importing unrestricted cardinal arithmetic, quotient sets, chosen representatives, powersets, or choice.

\begin{definition}[Finite cardinal carrier]
\label{def:finite-cardinal-carrier}
A $\FiniteCardinalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(S,T,n,L,R,B,Q,\Gamma,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source finite-object row, read through $\SetLikeUp$ and $\KuratowskiFiniteSetUp$, exposing membership, finite generation, and the inspected element names.
\item $T$ is the target initial-segment row over $\NatUp$, naming the displayed positions $0,\ldots,n-1$ as finite endpoints rather than as a host ordinal quotient.
\item $n$ is the cardinality readback row, a named natural-number endpoint whose only public meaning is the length of the displayed target segment $T$.
\item $L$ is the forward ledger, listing for each displayed source element its target position in $T$.
\item $R$ is the reverse ledger, listing for each displayed target position the source element in $S$ that occupies it.
\item $B$ is the bijection ledger, recording the two finite round-trip checks $R(L(s))=s$ for displayed source entries and $L(R(i))=i$ for displayed target entries.
\item $Q$ is the cardinality-use ledger, forcing every consumer of the number $n$ to pass through $S,T,L,R,B$ before citing the cardinality row.
\item $\Gamma$ is the local classifier, comparing accepted packets only by source membership rows, target segment rows, the named natural endpoint, the two ledgers, the round-trip checks, and the consumer route.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $S,T,n,L,R,B,Q,\Gamma,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through
$$
S,T,n \longrightarrow L,R,B \longrightarrow Q,\Gamma .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteCardinalUp$, $\SetLikeUp$, $\KuratowskiFiniteSetUp$, $\NatUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteCardinalUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for unrestricted cardinal arithmetic, quotient extensionality of subsets, selected representatives, host powersets, or choice-based enumeration.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite cardinal NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-cardinal-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteCardinalUp$ carrier
$$
F=(S,T,n,L,R,B,Q,\Gamma,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteCardinalUp}$ surface consists exactly of source finite-object admission, initial-segment admission, natural-number cardinality readback, forward ledger exposure, reverse ledger exposure, finite round-trip verification, cardinality-use routing, classifier stability, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming. A consumer receives the public cardinality only along the route
$$
S,T,n \longrightarrow L,R,B \longrightarrow Q,\Gamma,H,C,P,N .
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $F$. The rows $S,T,n$ are the only entries that name the source finite object, the target initial segment, and the natural-number readback.

The rows $L$ and $R$ display the two directions of the finite comparison. The row $B$ records the two round-trip checks on exactly the displayed source entries and target positions, so no ambient quotient or selected enumeration is available outside the packet.

The consumer route $Q$ and classifier $\Gamma$ force every cardinality read to preserve the same finite ledgers. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only those rows, so the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite cardinal classifier stability]
\label{thm:finite-cardinal-classifier-stability}
Let $F=(S,T,n,L,R,B,Q,\Gamma,H,C,P,N)$ and $F'=(S',T',n',L',R',B',Q',\Gamma',H',C',P',N')$ be accepted $\FiniteCardinalUp$ carriers. If the source rows, target segment rows, named natural endpoints, forward ledgers, reverse ledgers, round-trip ledgers, and consumer routes are componentwise $\hsame$, then $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$ classify the same finite cardinality packet. Conversely, a classifier agreement for $\FiniteCardinalUp$ exposes precisely those displayed rows as its comparison surface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the forward direction, read the componentwise $\hsame$ data through $H$ and $H'$. The classifier rows are defined over the source row, target segment, natural endpoint, both ledger directions, their round-trip checks, and the consumer route, so agreement on those coordinates gives the same classifier read.

For the reverse direction, open the classifier rows $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$. By the carrier definition, they have no coordinates other than $S,T,n,L,R,B,Q$ and their primed counterparts. Therefore classifier agreement can expose only those displayed rows, together with the structural transport and replay rows that preserve them.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite cardinal bijection ledger]
\label{thm:finite-cardinal-bijection-ledger}
Every accepted $\FiniteCardinalUp$ carrier exports cardinality as a finite bijection ledger. The number $n$ may be cited only after the packet displays the forward assignments $L$, reverse assignments $R$, and the round-trip row $B$ over the finite source row $S$ and the initial segment $T$. Thus the packet supplies a constructive finite-cardinality interface for finite-net, compactness, and bounded-family consumers, but it does not supply unrestricted cardinal arithmetic, quotient-set equality, selected representatives, host powersets, or choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the accepted carrier and use \autoref{thm:finite-cardinal-namecert-obligations}. The public route to $n$ passes through $S,T,L,R,B$ before it reaches any consumer ledger.

The bijection row $B$ contains both finite round-trip checks, so each source element and each target position is accounted for inside the displayed packet. A compact finite-net consumer such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-hyperspace-finite-net-namecert} can therefore cite the finite count as a ledger-backed size row.

Since the carrier omits unrestricted cardinal arithmetic, quotient extensionality, selected representatives, powersets, and choice, the exported statement is exactly the finite bijection ledger, not an ambient cardinal theory.
\end{proof}
