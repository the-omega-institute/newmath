Following Billingsley and Prokhorov, the $\PortmanteauUp$ packet records the weak-convergence test surface that becomes visible only after tight probability data have already been exposed. It sits after the probability source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert}, the pushforward distribution row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-distribution-namecert}, the event-surface measure row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-measure-namecert}, the real comparison boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the tight probability handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-prokhorov-tight-probability-namecert}, and the compact metric test surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, with probe admission routed through the continuous-map surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}. The chapter names a finite BEDC test packet for bounded-continuous probes; it does not assert weak compactness, choose subsequences, use countable choice, export host measures, quotient events, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Portmanteau carrier]
\label{def:portmanteau-carrier}
A $\PortmanteauUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
W=(P,D,M,T,B,R,A,E,H,C,Q,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $P$ is the $\ProbSpaceUp$ source row carrying the probability surface.
\item $D$ is the $\DistributionUp$ row whose pushforward reads supply the tested laws.
\item $M$ is the $\MeasureUp$ event-surface row through which measured test preimages are inspected.
\item $T$ is the $\ProkhorovTightProbabilityUp$ handoff row already exposing tightness data before any weak-convergence test is read.
\item $B$ is the bounded-continuous test row, recorded as a displayed $\mathsf{BoundedContinuousTest}$ surface over the compact or metric test carrier.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ comparison row for the two finite test expectations or inequalities observed by the packet.
\item $A$ is the transport row aligning probability, distribution, measure, tightness, bounded-continuous test, and real-comparison reads.
\item $E$ is the finite endpoint or tolerance ledger that records which bounded-continuous probe and real comparison window are being inspected.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for every displayed row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of source admission, pushforward readback, measure inspection, tightness exposure, bounded-continuous test evaluation, real comparison, and tolerance readback.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PortmanteauUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\DistributionUp$, $\MeasureUp$, $\ProkhorovTightProbabilityUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PortmanteauUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for weak compactness, selected subsequences, countable choice, host measures, quotient events, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Portmanteau NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:portmanteau-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PortmanteauUp$ carrier $W=(P,D,M,T,B,R,A,E,H,C,Q,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\PortmanteauUp}$ surface consists exactly of six obligation groups:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the probability source, distribution pushforward, event-surface measure, tightness handoff, bounded-continuous test, real comparison, transport, tolerance, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item tightness-before-testing exposure, requiring each weak-convergence test read to display the $\ProkhorovTightProbabilityUp$ row $T$ before the bounded-continuous probe is consumed;
\item bounded-continuous test admission, requiring the probe row $B$ to be read through the displayed compact or metric test surface rather than through a host function space;
\item real comparison exactness, requiring the comparison row $R$ to be read through $\RealUp$ and the tolerance ledger $E$;
\item distribution and measure compatibility, requiring $D$ and $M$ to name the same tested event or pushforward observation under the transport row $A$;
\item non-escape from weak compactness, selected subsequences, countable choice, host measures, quotient events, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{enumerate}
No additional NameCert obligation is stored outside these finite rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:portmanteau-carrier}. The tuple lists exactly the probability, distribution, measure, tightness, bounded-continuous test, real comparison, transport, tolerance, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The tightness side is already finite. A consumer must inspect $T$ before the probe row $B$ can be evaluated, so the weak-convergence test is downstream of the Prokhorov tightness handoff rather than a source of compactness by itself.

The comparison side is real-name data. The row $R$ and tolerance ledger $E$ provide the displayed real comparison, while $D,M,A$ keep pushforward and measured-event observations on the same route.

Since no carrier coordinate stores weak compactness, selected subsequences, countable choice, host measures, quotient events, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, the six displayed groups exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ContinuousMap Portmanteau forward link]
\label{thm:continuousmap-portmanteau-forward-link}
Every bounded-continuous test row $B$ admitted by an accepted
$\PortmanteauUp$ carrier $W=(P,D,M,T,B,R,A,E,H,C,Q,N)$ factors through a
$\ContinuousMapUp$ probe row together with the displayed $\CompactMetricUp$ or
$\MetricUp$ source surface and the $\RealUp$ comparison rows before any
weak-convergence test is read. Thus Portmanteau-facing probe admission is a
finite route through continuous-map, compact-or-metric, and real-comparison
rows, not a read from a host function space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:portmanteau-carrier}. The bounded
continuous probe is stored only as the row $B$, and the provenance row $Q$
names the continuous-map, compact-or-metric, and real-comparison dependencies
used to read it.

Apply the bounded-continuous test admission group in
\autoref{thm:portmanteau-namecert-obligations}. It requires the probe row to be
read through the displayed compact or metric test surface before the
Portmanteau test can consume it.

The real comparison group then routes the evaluated probe through $R$ and the
tolerance ledger $E$. Transport and replay through $A,H,C$ preserve the same
finite rows.

Therefore the forward link cannot introduce a host function space, quotient
event surface, selected subsequence, weak compactness theorem, or ambient
$\RealUp$ completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Portmanteau tightness-before-testing scope]
\label{thm:portmanteau-tight-test-scope}
Every accepted $\PortmanteauUp$ carrier $W=(P,D,M,T,B,R,A,E,H,C,Q,N)$ exposes the tightness handoff row $T$ before the bounded-continuous test row $B$ and the $\RealUp$ comparison row $R$ can be consumed by weak-convergence tests. The accepted test read is therefore a finite route through probability, distribution, measure, tightness, bounded-continuous probe, and real-comparison rows, not a route through weak compactness, selected subsequences, host measures, quotient events, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:portmanteau-carrier}. The tightness coordinate is the row $T$, and the test-facing coordinates $B$ and $R$ occur downstream from it in the displayed packet.

Apply \autoref{thm:portmanteau-namecert-obligations}. Its tightness-before-testing group requires each weak-convergence test read to display $T$ before the bounded-continuous probe is consumed.

Apply \autoref{thm:continuousmap-portmanteau-forward-link} to the probe row. It routes $B$ through the continuous-map, compact-or-metric, and real-comparison surfaces before the Portmanteau test consumes it.

Thus a weak-convergence consumer can read $B$ and $R$ only after tightness has already been exposed. Since no carrier coordinate stores weak compactness, selected subsequences, host measures, quotient events, or ambient Real completeness, the accepted scope is exactly the displayed finite test route.
\end{proof}
