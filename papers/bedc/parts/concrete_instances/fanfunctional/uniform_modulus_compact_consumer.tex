\begin{theorem}[FanFunctional uniform-modulus compact consumer]
\label{thm:fanfunctional-uniform-modulus-compact-consumer}
Any $\CompactMetricUp$ or $\UniformModulusUp$ consumer of an accepted $\FanFunctionalUp$ packet must read $\CantorSpaceUp$ prefix rows, $\FanTheoremUp$ finite-bar depth, $\UniformContinuityUp$ pointwise tolerance rows, $\CompactMetricUp$ finite-net data, and the extracted $\UniformModulusUp$ row through the finite packet
$$
\Phi=(C,F,\varepsilon,B,D,W,M,H,K,P,N).
$$
Here $C$ records Cantor prefix rows, $F$ records the finite bar supplied by $\FanTheoremUp$, $\varepsilon$ records the pointwise tolerance request, $B$ records the bar-depth boundary, $D$ records the extracted finite depth, $W$ records the witness windows, $M$ records the compact finite-net and uniform-modulus handoff, and $H,K,P,N$ record componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. The consumer exports no host functional, choice-selected branch, open-cover compactness, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the existing $\FanFunctionalUp$ NameCert obligations. They admit a finite packet only by displaying Cantor prefix rows, the FanTheorem bar row, tolerance data, witness windows, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Read the root finite-bar depth next. The root bar-depth exactness theorem in the FanFunctional chapter forces every modulus consumer to use the finite depth displayed by the FanTheorem rows rather than a selected branch or host functional.

The compact and uniform-modulus consumers are downstream of that depth. Compact finite-net data and the extracted $\UniformModulusUp$ row are therefore read through the same packet $\Phi$, with $M$ as the handoff row and $H,K,P,N$ preserving, replaying, sourcing, and naming the route.

Since no coordinate of $\Phi$ stores a host functional, branch selector, open-cover compactness proof, or $\RealUp$ completeness theorem, none of those objects can enter the compact-uniform consumer handoff.
\end{proof}
