\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DecidableBarUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-decidable-bar-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Brouwer on bars over spreads and Bishop--Bridges on finite binary
inspection in constructive analysis, the $\DecidableBarUp$ packet names the
finite decidable-bar row used by fan arguments before any real-completion
consumer reads it. It sits beside the finite fan surface of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fantheorem-namecert}, the binary prefix carrier
of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cantorspace-namecert}, and the finite
observation gateway of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}.
The packet records finite prefix windows, decidable bar hits, unary depth
ledgers, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$
provenance, and a local $\NameCert$ row. It does not select an infinite branch,
assert countable choice, supply open-cover compactness, or close ambient
$\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Decidable bar carrier]
\label{def:decidable-bar-carrier}
A $\DecidableBarUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(C,S,W,R,D,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C$ is the $\CantorSpaceUp$ finite binary-prefix row inspected by the bar.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ observation row naming the finite prefix window
read by the current consumer.
\item $W$ is the finite prefix-window ledger, listing the inspected binary
prefixes and their parent-prefix inclusions.
\item $R$ is the decidable bar-hit row, recording for each displayed prefix
whether the bar predicate has fired inside the finite window.
\item $D$ is the unary depth ledger bounding all inspected prefixes and bar-hit
checks.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $C,S,W,R,D,T,P,N$.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the finite route
$$
C \longmapsto S \longmapsto W \longmapsto R \longmapsto D .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DecidableBarUp$, $\CantorSpaceUp$,
$\StreamNameUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DecidableBarUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $C,S,W,R,D,H,T,P,N$. It
has no coordinate for selected infinite branches, countable choice, open-cover
compactness, host Cantor-space compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Decidable bar NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:decidable-bar-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DecidableBarUp$ carrier
$$
B=(C,S,W,R,D,H,T,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DecidableBarUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier
admission for the displayed Cantor prefix row, StreamName window row, finite
prefix-window ledger, decidable bar-hit row, unary depth ledger, componentwise
$\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local
naming. A consumer may read these rows as finite bar data, but no obligation is
stored outside the displayed packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:decidable-bar-carrier}. The
accepted tuple exposes exactly the Cantor prefix row, stream observation row,
prefix-window ledger, bar-hit row, unary depth ledger, transport row, replay row,
provenance row, and local name.

The bar obligations are finite because $W,R,D$ are displayed rows. The window
row lists the prefixes under inspection, the hit row gives a decidable answer on
that displayed list, and the depth ledger bounds the readback.

The structural rows $H,T,P,N$ preserve that same finite packet. Since the
carrier has no further coordinate, the local certificate cannot store a selected
infinite branch, a countable-choice schedule, an open-cover compactness witness,
or an ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Decidable bar stream-window route]
\label{thm:decidable-bar-stream-window-route}
Every $\StreamNameUp$ consumer reading an accepted $\DecidableBarUp$ packet must
first expose the finite route
$$
\CantorSpaceUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto W \longmapsto R
\longmapsto D .
$$
Thus the public bar read is a finite prefix-window computation preserved by
$\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows, not an infinite branch
selection or a host-level choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier projection. The stream observation row $S$ is downstream
of the Cantor prefix row $C$, and the prefix-window ledger $W$ is downstream of
that displayed stream observation.

The decidable bar-hit row $R$ is read only over the prefixes listed by $W$.
The unary depth ledger $D$ records the bound under which those hits are
available, so the route remains finite.

Finally, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,T,P,N$
preserve the same finite route. They do not add a selector for an infinite
binary branch or a countable family of choices.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Decidable bar real-completion boundary]
\label{thm:decidable-bar-real-completion-boundary}
If a $\RealUp$ or completion-facing consumer cites an accepted
$\DecidableBarUp$ packet, the citation can use only the finite Cantor prefix
row, StreamName window, prefix-window ledger, decidable hit row, unary depth
ledger, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$
provenance, and local $\NameCert_{\DecidableBarUp}$ row. The citation cannot be
read as selected branch data, countable choice, open-cover compactness, quotient
equality of real names, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:decidable-bar-namecert-obligations}. It identifies the full
public surface of the packet with the finite rows $C,S,W,R,D,H,T,P,N$.

Apply \autoref{thm:decidable-bar-stream-window-route}. Any real-facing or
completion-facing read must pass through the finite StreamName window and the
decidable bar-hit ledger before it can cite the packet.

Coordinate exhaustion gives the boundary. No row records a chosen infinite
branch, a countable-choice selector, an open-cover compactness proof, quotient
equality of real names, or a completeness witness for $\RealUp$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Decidable bar finite-branch induction]
\label{thm:decidable-bar-finite-branch-induction}
Every induction over an accepted $\DecidableBarUp$ packet is induction over the
displayed finite prefix-window ledger $W$ and its unary depth ledger $D$. At each
stage the consumer exposes only the current Cantor prefix row, StreamName
observation row, decidable bar-hit row, componentwise $\hsame$ transport,
displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the displayed ledger $W$. The empty window exposes no hidden branch
data; it carries only the structural rows already present in the accepted
packet.

For a nonempty window, read the head prefix through $C$ and $S$, inspect its
decidable hit row in $R$, and retain the unary depth bound in $D$. The induction
hypothesis applies to the remaining displayed prefixes because the carrier
stores them in the same finite ledger.

Transport and replay through $H$ and $T$ preserve the component currently under
inspection, while $P$ and $N$ preserve provenance and local naming. The induction
therefore never leaves the finite BEDC packet and cannot produce a selected
infinite branch, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or real completeness.
\end{proof}
