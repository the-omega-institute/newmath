\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-real-apartness-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp$ packet records strict real separation as finite positive witness data rather than as a trichotomy principle. It sits over the Real carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the regular rational rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the StreamName windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the positive real boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-positivereal-namecert}. Its role is to make the constructive constraint of \autoref{sec:roadmap-l10-constructive-constraints} usable by later order and completion consumers without importing quotient real equality, host equality, selected limits, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient completeness for $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[ConstructiveRealApartness carrier]
\label{def:constructive-real-apartness-carrier}
A $\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(X,Y,R_X,R_Y,S_X,S_Y,D,\delta,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ and $Y$ are the two $\RealUp$ seal rows being separated.
\item $R_X$ and $R_Y$ are the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback rows used to inspect the two real names.
\item $S_X$ and $S_Y$ are the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window rows from which the readbacks are taken.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger that records the finite dyadic comparison scale.
\item $\delta$ is the $\PositiveRealUp$ lower-bound witness for the displayed separation.
\item $W$ is the finite apartness window asserting that the rational readbacks at the displayed scale are separated by the positive lower bound named by $\delta$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the real seals, regular readbacks, stream windows, dyadic ledger, positive witness, apartness window, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay that opens the two StreamName windows, reads the RegSeqRat rows, checks the dyadic comparison ledger, and hands the positive witness to the Real-facing apartness surface.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\PositiveRealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for quotient real equality, host real equality, a selected representative limit, countable choice, trichotomy, or completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ConstructiveRealApartness NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:constructive-real-apartness-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp$ carrier
$$
A=(X,Y,R_X,R_Y,S_X,S_Y,D,\delta,W,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp}$ surface consists of carrier admission for the displayed rows, paired RealUp seal exposure through $X,Y$, regular readback exposure through $R_X,R_Y$, finite StreamName window exposure through $S_X,S_Y$, dyadic comparison exactness through $D$, positive lower-bound exposure through $\delta$, apartness-window readback through $W$, componentwise $\hsame$ stability through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:constructive-real-apartness-carrier}. The Real-facing rows are exactly $X,Y$, and the only inspection rows are the regular readbacks, stream windows, dyadic comparison ledger, positive witness, and apartness window displayed in the tuple.

The certificate reads separation constructively. The witness $\delta$ is a positive lower-bound row, while $W$ ties that row to the finite rational readbacks obtained from $S_X,S_Y$ through $R_X,R_Y$ and checked against $D$.

The transport, replay, provenance, and local name rows preserve the same finite route. Since no further carrier coordinate is present, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ConstructiveRealApartness finite readback]
\label{thm:constructive-real-apartness-finite-readback}
Every accepted $\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp$ read is witnessed by the finite route
$$
\StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \PositiveRealUp,\ \RealUp .
$$
The two Real-facing rows become apart only through the displayed StreamName windows, regular rational readbacks, dyadic comparison ledger, positive lower-bound row, and apartness window.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:constructive-real-apartness-namecert-obligations}. They force each accepted read to expose $S_X,S_Y$ before $R_X,R_Y$, then the dyadic ledger $D$, the positive witness $\delta$, and the apartness window $W$.

Only after those rows have been displayed can the Real-facing rows $X,Y$ be read as apart. The continuation row $C$ records the same order, and the package row $P$ adds provenance rather than a new comparison principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ConstructiveRealApartness non-trichotomy boundary]
\label{thm:constructive-real-apartness-nontrichotomy-boundary}
An accepted $\ConstructiveRealApartnessUp$ carrier exports a positive finite apartness witness and does not export quotient real equality, host real equality, selected limits, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness. In particular, the packet cannot be read as a decision procedure for arbitrary comparison of the two RealUp rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. Its content rows are the two RealUp seals, regular readbacks, finite StreamName windows, dyadic ledger, positive witness, and apartness window.

The finite readback theorem \autoref{thm:constructive-real-apartness-finite-readback} identifies the only route from these rows to an apartness claim. That route supplies a displayed positive witness, not a three-way case split and not a host equality test.

Since the tuple contains no row for quotient equality, selected representatives, choice, trichotomy, or completeness, no accepted consumer can recover those principles from the packet.
\end{proof}
