\begin{definition}[Poincare--Miranda finite carrier]
\label{def:poincare-miranda-finite-carrier}
A $\PoincareMirandaFiniteUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(Q,F,S,D,I,L,B,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ is the finite cube row, carrying the displayed coordinate list, opposite face pairs, dyadic side windows, and the finite incidence table for the cube boundary.
\item $F$ is the face-sign packet. For each displayed coordinate face it records the finite positive-side and negative-side sign rows read from the carried continuous map.
\item $S$ is the boundary-sign stability row, requiring each face sign to remain stable under displayed $\hsame$ transport, dyadic refinement, and the finite window reads used by $F$.
\item $D$ is the subdivision ledger, listing the dyadic cells, their face incidence, inherited sign rows, and the refinement route by which a sign conflict is localized.
\item $I$ is the compact interval handoff row. It records that every coordinate edge and retained one-dimensional slice is read through the $\CompactIntervalFixedPointUp$ and $\ContinuousIntervalImageUp$ surfaces before a Real-facing zero read is public.
\item $L$ is the Brouwer-style labelled-cell row, routing the cube-face sign conflict through the finite labelled-subdivision discipline used by $\BrouwerFixedPointUp$.
\item $B$ is the zero-cell refusal ledger. It records that any claimed zero cell must expose $Q,F,S,D,I,L$ first and that every attempt to read a zero without those rows is outside the certificate.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row opened for cube coordinates, face probes, dyadic cells, interval slices, and zero-cell readback.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular readback row attached to those windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for the displayed candidate zero cell, available only after $Q,F,S,D,I,L,B,W,R$ have been replayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the cube row, face signs, stability row, subdivision ledger, interval handoff, labelled-cell row, refusal ledger, windows, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a zero-cell request back through $E,R,W,B,L,I,D,S,F,Q$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PoincareMirandaFiniteUp$, $\BrouwerFixedPointUp$, $\CompactIntervalFixedPointUp$, $\ContinuousIntervalImageUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PoincareMirandaFiniteUp}$ row.

The carrier compares accepted packets only through these finite coordinates. It has no coordinate for a selected host zero, an ambient cube theorem, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, countable choice, Lean verification, or bridge checking.

\begin{theorem}[Poincare--Miranda finite NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:poincare-miranda-finite-namecert-obligations}

$$

$$
the local $\NameCert_{\PoincareMirandaFiniteUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, boundary-sign stability, subdivision-ledger exposure, compact interval handoff, Brouwer-style labelled-cell handoff, zero-cell refusal, finite-window readback, Real-seal nonescape, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay, provenance, and local naming. \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

The visible packet lists the cube row $Q$, face-sign packet $F$, stability row $S$, subdivision ledger $D$, interval handoff $I$, labelled-cell row $L$, refusal ledger $B$, finite windows $W$, regular readback $R$, Real seal $E$, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Boundary-sign stability is read through $F$ and $S$. The opposite-face signs are not semantic predicates on a host cube; they are displayed rows tied to the finite face incidence table in $Q$, and $S$ records their preservation under the dyadic refinements and $\hsame$ transports used by the packet.

The subdivision obligation is the ledger $D$. It lists the dyadic cells and inherited face incidences before any sign conflict can be localized, while $L$ routes the resulting labelled-cell read through the finite Brouwer-style surface rather than through an ambient fixed-point theorem.

The compact interval handoff is the row $I$. Coordinate edges and retained one-dimensional slices are exposed through the compact interval fixed-point and continuous interval image packets before their finite windows and regular rational readbacks are consumed.

Finally $B,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$ enforce nonescape. The refusal ledger rejects any zero-cell read that bypasses the displayed rows; the window, readback, and seal rows expose the candidate as a finite Real-facing read; and the structural rows only transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Thus no host zero selector, quotient equality, open-cover compactness, countable choice, Lean-side verification, or bridge datum is added.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
