\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\SequentialClosureUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-sequentialclosure-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, \emph{Constructive Analysis}, the $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet records the finite route by which a displayed sequence-limit witness may be consumed as closure evidence without importing host topological closure. It sits over the topology surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the finite windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the located-set boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}, and the sequence-consumer interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sequentialcontinuity-namecert}. The packet is a finite NameCert surface for certified sequence-limit closure; it is not an ambient closure operator, not a compactness theorem, not quotient stream equality, not arbitrary subsequence choice, and not completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[SequentialClosure carrier]
\label{def:sequential-closure-carrier}
A $\SequentialClosureUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the ambient $\TopologyUp$ row, including the named neighbourhood indices and the classifier transport available to topology-facing consumers.
\item $M$ is the ambient $\MetricUp$ row over the same visible carrier, including the distance classifier and metric comparison rows used when neighbourhood tests are read metrically.
\item $S$ is the displayed subset or $\LocatedSetUp$ row whose admitted points form the source surface for the sequence.
\item $Q$ is the sequence source row. It records a finite $\StreamNameUp$ schedule of admitted sequence entries over $S$ together with the point-name rows inspected by the packet.
\item $L$ is the candidate limit row, naming the target point over the common topology and metric source without asserting that it is host-closure equivalent to $S$.
\item $U$ is the neighbourhood and metric test ledger. Each displayed request records either a topology neighbourhood row around $L$ or a metric tolerance row around $L$, together with the sequence-entry test that answers it.
\item $W$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window ledger naming the inspected sequence windows and the index budgets consumed by $U$.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row translating the displayed metric tolerance windows into regular rational readback data before the Real distance boundary is consulted.
\item $A$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row certifying the accepted distance comparison downstream of $R$ and $U$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the topology row, metric row, subset or located-set row, sequence source, candidate limit, test ledger, StreamName windows, RegSeqRat handoff, Real seal, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a closure-consumer request through $T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\TopologyUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\LocatedSetUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SequentialClosureUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for host topological closure, arbitrary subsequence choice, quotient stream equality, open-cover compactness, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or a detached $\SequentialContinuityUp$ theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ carrier $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\SequentialClosureUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the ambient topology, ambient metric, subset or located-set row, sequence source, candidate limit, neighbourhood and metric tests, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat handoff, Real seal, transport, continuation, provenance, and local naming row;
\item neighbourhood-test stability, requiring each topology or metric request around $L$ to replay through the displayed test ledger $U$ over the same $T,M,S,Q,L$ rows;
\item sequence-window coverage, requiring every accepted closure read to expose the finite StreamName windows $W$ that name the inspected sequence entries and index budgets;
\item limit-row handoff, requiring the RegSeqRat row $R$ and Real seal $A$ to be read downstream of the displayed metric test ledger before a distance conclusion is exported;
\item classifier transport, requiring all $\hsame$ comparison of closure packets to preserve the topology, metric, subset, sequence, limit, test, window, handoff, seal, continuation, provenance, and local NameCert rows componentwise;
\item non-escape from the host closure boundary, excluding host topological closure, arbitrary subsequence choice, quotient stream equality, open-cover compactness, and detached $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:sequential-closure-carrier}. The projection exposes exactly the topology row, metric row, subset or located-set row, sequence source, candidate limit, test ledger, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat handoff, Real seal, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Neighbourhood-test stability is forced by the continuation row $C$. A closure-facing request reaches a neighbourhood or metric conclusion only by replaying the common $T,M,S,Q,L$ source rows and then consulting the displayed ledger $U$.

Sequence-window coverage is the next step in the same replay. The ledger $W$ is the only place where inspected sequence windows and index budgets are stored, so an accepted read cannot replace the displayed sequence by an arbitrary subsequence or by quotient stream equality.

The limit-row handoff is downstream of those windows. The RegSeqRat row $R$ translates the metric tolerance read into regular rational data, and the Real seal $A$ certifies the accepted distance comparison only after $U$ and $W$ have been consumed.

Finally, componentwise transport by $H$ preserves every displayed coordinate, while $P$ and $N$ package only that finite route. Since host topological closure, arbitrary subsequence choice, quotient stream equality, open-cover compactness, and detached Real completeness have no carrier row, no continuation edge, and no provenance entry, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure sequence-limit handoff]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-sequence-limit-handoff}
Let $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet. Every closure consumer of the displayed candidate limit factors through
$$
T,M,S,Q,L \longmapsto U,W \longmapsto R,A \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer receives closure evidence only through the displayed sequence windows, the RegSeqRat handoff, the Real seal, the topology and metric tests, and the local NameCert rows; no host closure operator, arbitrary sequence selection, quotient stream equality, compactness principle, or $\RealUp$ completeness theorem is exported by the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a closure consumer of the candidate limit row $L$. Carrier admission gives access to $L$ only together with the common topology row $T$, metric row $M$, subset or located-set row $S$, and sequence source row $Q$.

Replay the continuation row $C$. The consumer must pass through the neighbourhood and metric test ledger $U$ and then through the finite StreamName window ledger $W$, so the inspected sequence entries and their index budgets remain displayed throughout the read.

The metric side of the request then factors through $R$ and $A$. The RegSeqRat handoff records the regular rational readback for the tolerance window, and the Real seal records the accepted distance comparison after the topology and metric tests have already been displayed.

Transport, provenance, and local naming through $H,P,N$ preserve the same dependency order. This proves the stated factorization and leaves no available row from which a consumer could extract host closure, arbitrary sequence selection, quotient stream equality, compactness, or detached completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure RegSeqRat limit route]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-regseqrat-limit-route}
Let $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet. Any metric-side closure read of the candidate limit row $L$ must expose the finite window ledger $W$, translate the requested tolerance through the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row $R$, and seal the resulting distance comparison through the $\RealUp$ row $A$. Thus the packet exports a sequence-limit route only as displayed RegSeqRat/Real data downstream of $T,M,S,Q,L,U,W$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the packet carrier. The rows $T,M,S,Q,L$ name the ambient topology, ambient metric, source row, sequence source, and candidate limit, while $U$ records the neighbourhood and metric requests that a closure consumer may ask.

The metric request cannot bypass the finite windows. The continuation row $C$ first exposes the StreamName windows $W$, so the inspected sequence entries and index budgets remain visible before any distance comparison is read.

The handoff then passes through $R$ and $A$. The row $R$ records the regular rational readback for the displayed tolerance, and the row $A$ seals the corresponding Real comparison. Transport by $H$ and packaging by $P,N$ preserve this order, so no detached Real completeness or host limit principle is exported.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure LocatedSet stability]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-located-set-stability}
Let $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet whose source row $S$ is read as a $\LocatedSetUp$ surface. If a second packet is componentwise $\hsame$ to $K$ on $T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,C,P,N$, then admitted sequence entries, located-set membership reads, RegSeqRat handoffs, and Real seals are transported through the same $S$ row. The stability claim is only this finite LocatedSet-preserving replay.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the componentwise $\hsame$ data recorded by $H$. It identifies the topology and metric rows, the located-set source row $S$, the displayed sequence row $Q$, the candidate limit row $L$, and the test and window ledgers $U,W$.

Membership stability is read before the limit handoff. Each accepted sequence entry is admitted through the same located-set source surface $S$, and the transported packet can only replay those displayed entries because no arbitrary subsequence or external closure row appears in the carrier.

Finally transport the metric side through $R$ and $A$. The RegSeqRat and Real rows are downstream of the same located-set and sequence windows, so the transported closure read preserves located-set admission while adding no compactness, quotient stream equality, or detached completeness assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure topology handoff]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-topology-handoff}
For an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, every topology-facing closure consumer receives the candidate limit row through the finite handoff
$$
T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N .
$$
Neighbourhood requests are answered through $U$ over the common topology row $T$, metric requests are answered through $M$ and then $R,A$, and the exported closure evidence is repacked by the local $\NameCert_{\SequentialClosureUp}$ row $N$ without a host closure operator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a topology-facing request around the candidate limit $L$. The carrier admits $L$ only beside the ambient topology row $T$, metric row $M$, source row $S$, and displayed sequence row $Q$.

The request ledger $U$ determines how the consumer may proceed. A purely neighbourhood-facing read stays over $T,S,Q,L,U,W$, while a metric-facing read also consumes the distance comparison route through $M,R,A$.

The final packaging is local. Transport by $H$, replay by $C$, provenance by $P$, and naming by $N$ repack the same finite rows as $\NameCert_{\SequentialClosureUp}$ evidence. Since the packet has no coordinate for host topological closure, compactness, arbitrary sequence selection, or $\RealUp$ completeness, the topology and metric handoff exports only the displayed closure route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure root sequence carrier]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-root-sequence-carrier}
Let $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet. A sequence-closure carrier read is available only after the packet displays the $\LocatedSetUp$ or subset source row $S$, the $\MetricUp$ row $M$, the $\StreamNameUp$ row $Q$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$, the $\RealUp$ seal $A$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and the local $\NameCert_{\SequentialClosureUp}$ row $N$ in the same finite $\BHist$ carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:sequential-closure-carrier}. The sequence row $Q$ is not a detached stream; it is read beside the source row $S$, metric row $M$, test ledger $U$, window ledger $W$, handoff row $R$, and Real seal $A$.

Use \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-namecert-obligations} to identify the local NameCert obligations and \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-sequence-limit-handoff} to force every closure consumer through the displayed sequence-limit route. The RegSeqRat limit route of \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-regseqrat-limit-route} supplies the rational readback boundary for metric requests.

The located-set stability theorem \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-located-set-stability} keeps source membership and admitted sequence entries over the same $S$ row, while \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-topology-handoff} repacks the topology-facing read through $H,C,P,N$. Hence the carrier read is available only with the listed finite $\BHist$ rows displayed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure root limit ledger]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-root-limit-ledger}
For an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the limit witness ledger is finite: all accepted reads of the candidate limit row $L$ pass through $U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N$. The packet refuses selected subsequences, quotient stream equality, countable choice, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness as limit-witness sources.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The finite ledger is visible in the carrier definition: requests are stored in $U$, inspected sequence windows in $W$, rational readback in $R$, Real comparison in $A$, and structural packaging in $H,C,P,N$.

Apply \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-sequence-limit-handoff} for the dependency order from $T,M,S,Q,L$ to $U,W,R,A$ and then to $H,C,P,N$. Apply \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-regseqrat-limit-route} for the metric side of the same ledger.

The refusal clauses are forced by absence of carrier coordinates. Neither selected subsequences nor quotient stream equality appear in $Q$ or $W$, countable choice has no row in $U$ or $C$, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness is not part of the seal $A$. The NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-namecert-obligations} therefore close only the displayed finite ledger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SequentialClosure root topology transport]
\label{thm:sequential-closure-root-topology-transport}
Let $K=(T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\SequentialClosureUp$ packet. Any topology-facing transport of the closure read preserves the same sequence-limit rows $T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A$ under componentwise $\hsame$ and displayed $\Cont$ replay, and then repackages the transported read through $P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the componentwise $\hsame$ row $H$. By \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-located-set-stability}, transport preserves the source row $S$, admitted sequence entries, RegSeqRat handoffs, and Real seals along the same displayed coordinates.

Replay is controlled by $C$. The handoff theorem \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-sequence-limit-handoff} keeps the sequence-limit route over $T,M,S,Q,L,U,W,R,A$, and the topology handoff theorem \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-topology-handoff} states that neighbourhood-facing consumers receive no host closure operator.

Finally $P$ and $N$ repackage the transported rows as the local NameCert surface described in \autoref{thm:sequential-closure-namecert-obligations}. Thus topology transport preserves the same finite sequence-limit ledger rather than creating a second closure witness.
\end{proof}
