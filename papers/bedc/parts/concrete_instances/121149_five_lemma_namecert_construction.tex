\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiveLemmaUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-five-lemma-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Mac Lane's homological algebra and the standard abelian-category
diagram chase, the $\FiveLemmaUp$ packet records the finite route that turns two
exact rows, five compatible vertical maps, and four local isomorphism-style
witnesses into the middle vertical classifier witness. It sits over the
abelian-category carrier of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-abeliancat-namecert} and consumes the same
exact-row and boundary discipline used by the snake-lemma packet of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-snake-lemma-namecert}. The packet names a
bounded chase surface; it does not import element representatives, quotient
object equality, ambient diagram extensionality, or an unbounded search oracle.

\begin{definition}[Five Lemma carrier]
\label{def:five-lemma-carrier}
A $\FiveLemmaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(A,R_0,R_1,V,L,M,Q,C,T,H,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the $\AbelianCatUp$ source row exposing the additive
kernel-cokernel surface in which both exact rows are read.
\item $R_0$ and $R_1$ are the upper and lower exact-row ledgers, each carrying
five displayed object rows, four displayed horizontal morphism rows, and the
local image-kernel containment rows used by the chase.
\item $V$ records the five displayed vertical morphisms and the four
commutative squares aligning $R_0$ with $R_1$.
\item $L$ records the two left-side comparison rows used to move witnesses
across the first and second vertical maps.
\item $M$ records the two middle-neighbour comparison rows used to pass from
the second and fourth vertical maps to the third vertical map.
\item $Q$ records the two right-side comparison rows used to close the chase
through the fourth and fifth vertical maps.
\item $C$ is the finite chase ledger. It lists the kernel entry, exact-row
transport, square transport, image-kernel handoff, cokernel exit, and middle
classifier witness in bounded order.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from $A,R_0,R_1,V,L,M,Q$ to the
chase ledger $C$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the abelian-category
source, exact rows, vertical squares, comparison rows, chase ledger, replay,
provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiveLemmaUp$, $\AbelianCatUp$,
$\SnakeLemmaUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiveLemmaUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted five-lemma packets only through these rows. It
contains no coordinate for selected elements outside the displayed histories,
hidden kernel representatives, quotient representatives, host-object equality,
or a completed ambient diagram category.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Five Lemma NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:five-lemma-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiveLemmaUp$ carrier
$$
F=(A,R_0,R_1,V,L,M,Q,C,T,H,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FiveLemmaUp}$ surface consists exactly of the
abelian-category source row $A$, the upper and lower exact-row ledgers
$R_0,R_1$, vertical-square compatibility through $V$, left comparison through
$L$, middle-neighbour comparison through $M$, right comparison through $Q$, the
finite chase ledger $C$, displayed replay through $T$, componentwise transport
through $H$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:five-lemma-carrier}. The rows
$A,R_0,R_1,V$ expose the abelian-category source, the two exact rows, and the
four commutative squares before any middle-map conclusion is available.

The comparison rows $L,M,Q$ then record precisely which neighbouring vertical
maps may be used by the chase. These rows are displayed certificate data, not
ambient claims about all diagrams in the host category.

Read the chase ledger $C$ next. Its entries are bounded by the five displayed
vertical maps and the four displayed squares, so every witness passed through
the exact rows is accounted for by a named row of the packet.

Finally $T,H,P,N$ keep the same surface stable under replay, componentwise
history equality, provenance, and local naming. Since no hidden representative,
host-object equality, or unbounded diagram search appears among the carrier
coordinates, the listed rows exhaust the certificate obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Five Lemma finite diagram chase]
\label{thm:five-lemma-finite-diagram-chase}
Every downstream consumer of an accepted $\FiveLemmaUp$ packet reads the middle
vertical classifier witness only through the displayed route
$$
\AbelianCatUp \longmapsto (R_0,R_1,V)
\longmapsto (L,M,Q) \longmapsto C \longmapsto (T,H,P,N).
$$
Thus the consumer receives one finite diagram-chase packet of abelian-category
source rows, exact-row ledgers, vertical-square compatibility rows, comparison
rows, bounded chase entries, replay, transport, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the source row $A$ and the two exact rows $R_0,R_1$. The vertical
compatibility row $V$ makes the four squares visible, so the chase can move
between the two rows only at named square entries.

Use $L,M,Q$ to expose the neighbouring comparison data. These rows delimit the
classical five-lemma assumptions as finite certificate coordinates: left-side
transport, middle-neighbour transport, and right-side exit data are all
displayed before the middle conclusion is read.

The ledger $C$ then lists the bounded sequence of chase moves. Exactness supplies
the image-kernel handoffs, square compatibility transports each handoff across
the vertical maps, and the comparison rows provide the permitted entries and
exits.

Replaying through $T$ and transporting through $H$ preserves the same finite
route. The provenance and naming rows $P,N$ therefore export the middle witness
as a NameCert packet rather than as an ambient element chase.
\end{proof}
