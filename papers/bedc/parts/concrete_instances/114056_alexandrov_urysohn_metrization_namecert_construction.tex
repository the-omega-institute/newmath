Following Alexandrov--Urysohn metrization, the $\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp$ packet records only the finite BEDC certificate by which countable basis windows and separation witnesses synthesize a metric-radius readback. It sits after the open-row discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, beside the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the finite-window schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular-rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the complete separable metric boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-polishspace-namecert}. The packet names a displayed basis-to-metric route; it does not assert global choice, arbitrary open-cover metrization, quotient topology, selected embeddings, or host-level general metrization.

\begin{definition}[Alexandrov--Urysohn metrization carrier]
\label{def:alexandrov-urysohn-metrization-carrier}
An $\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(T,B,S,R,W,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row, recording the finite open-membership observations available to the packet.
\item $B$ is the countable basis-window row, recording a finite $\StreamNameUp$ schedule of basis names, incidence witnesses, and window bounds.
\item $S$ is the separation-witness row, recording the displayed point-open and disjoint-closure tests consumed by the metric synthesis.
\item $R$ is the metric-radius synthesis row, assigning dyadic rational radius ledgers to the basis windows and separation witnesses.
\item $W$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, exposing the regular-rational distance observations produced from $R$.
\item $Q$ is the refusal row, recording that global choice, arbitrary open covers, quotient topology, selected embeddings, and host-level metrization are absent from the packet.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for topology, basis-window, separation, radius synthesis, readback, refusal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from $\TopologyUp$ and basis windows through metric-radius synthesis into $\MetricUp/\MetricSpaceUp$ and $\PolishSpaceUp$ consumers.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\PolishSpaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a choice-selected global basis, an arbitrary open-cover refinement theorem, a quotient topology, a host embedding into sequence space, or a classical all-spaces metrization principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Alexandrov--Urysohn metrization NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:alexandrov-urysohn-metrization-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp$ carrier $A=(T,B,S,R,W,Q,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp}$ surface consists exactly of topology-source admission through $T$, countable basis-window scheduling through $B$, separation-witness inspection through $S$, metric-radius synthesis through $R$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback through $W$, refusal of non-displayed classical data through $Q$, componentwise $\hsame$ stability through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:alexandrov-urysohn-metrization-carrier}. The topology source, basis windows, separation witnesses, radius ledger, readback, refusal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows are exactly $T,B,S,R,W,Q,H,C,P,N$.

Read the basis data through $B$ before metric synthesis. The finite $\StreamNameUp$ schedule exposes only displayed basis windows and incidence witnesses, so no global basis choice or arbitrary open-cover selection is available.

Next read the separation witnesses through $S$ and the radius ledger through $R$. The metric-facing observation is synthesized from those displayed rows and then exposed as the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $W$ before any $\MetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, or $\PolishSpaceUp$ consumer can inspect it.

Finally $Q,H,C,P,N$ refuse absent classical data, preserve the displayed rows, replay the route, source the packet, and name it locally. Since the carrier contains no quotient-topology coordinate, selected embedding, or host-level general metrization theorem, the NameCert obligations are exactly the finite basis-to-metric route stated above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Alexandrov--Urysohn finite basis-to-metric route]
\label{thm:alexandrov-urysohn-finite-basis-to-metric-route}
Every $\PolishSpaceUp$ or $\MetricSpaceUp$ consumer of an $\AlexandrovUrysohnMetrizationUp$ packet must first read the $\TopologyUp$ row, finite basis-window schedule, separation-witness row, metric-radius synthesis ledger, $\StreamNameUp$ schedule, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\BHist$ carrier, componentwise $\hsame$, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row. The consumer receives a finite certificate for the displayed basis-to-metric handoff, not a quotient topology, arbitrary open-cover theorem, selected global basis, or host-level general metrization result.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:alexandrov-urysohn-metrization-namecert-obligations}. They force topology admission, basis-window scheduling, separation witnesses, radius synthesis, readback, refusal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming to remain rows of one finite packet.

The metric consumer then reads $R$ and $W$ after $T,B,S$. Thus the distance observations are regular-rational readbacks from displayed basis and separation data, not host real distances selected from an external metric.

For a PolishSpace consumer, replay the same metric-facing row through $C$ and provenance $P$. Completeness and separability consumers may inspect the handoff only as a routed finite NameCert surface over the sibling $\MetricUp/\MetricSpaceUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, and $\RegSeqRatUp$ rows.

The refusal row $Q$ records the boundary. No global choice, arbitrary open-cover compactness, quotient topology, selected embedding, or host-level metrization theorem is exported by this packet.
\end{proof}
