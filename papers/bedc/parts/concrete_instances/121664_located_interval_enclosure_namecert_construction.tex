\chapter{Located Interval Enclosure NameCert Construction}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-located-interval-enclosure-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real intervals, the $\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp$ packet records a finite enclosure of a displayed real-name read by dyadic endpoints before any nested-interval or completion consumer may use it. It consumes the dyadic endpoint arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, finite observation windows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, regular rational readback from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the dyadic interval-cell route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-net-namecert}. The packet names a displayed enclosure row; it does not assert interval compactness, choose a completed point, identify host real equality, invoke countable choice, or claim ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Located interval enclosure carrier]
\label{def:located-interval-enclosure-carrier}
A $\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(L,U,O,W,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ and $U$ are $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint rows for the lower and upper dyadic bounds.
\item $O$ is the endpoint order row, displaying that the lower endpoint precedes the upper endpoint inside the same dyadic tolerance ledger.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite observation window inspected before the enclosure is admitted.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback row attached to the same window and dyadic tolerance budget.
\item $S$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for the named real read enclosed by $L,U,W,R$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the endpoints, order row, containment witness, stream window, readback row, Real seal, replay row, provenance row, and local name.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the window request through the endpoint, containment, readback, seal, transport, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $L,U,O,W,R,S,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host real equality, quotient representatives, selected limits, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Located interval enclosure carrier extraction]
\label{thm:located-interval-enclosure-carrier-extraction}
Every accepted $\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp$ read exposes the dyadic endpoint rows $L,U$, the endpoint order row $O$, the $\StreamNameUp$ finite window $W$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $R$, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $S$, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ before an enclosure judgment is public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:located-interval-enclosure-carrier}. The tuple lists exactly the endpoint, order, window, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

The sibling routes named in the introduction supply those rows only as displayed finite data. Dyadic endpoints and the interval-net cell are read before the stream window and regular readback, and the Real seal is reached only after those finite rows have been displayed.

Since the classifier has no coordinate beyond $L,U,O,W,R,S,H,C,P,N$, no public enclosure read can bypass this extraction route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Located interval enclosure endpoint-refinement transport]
\label{thm:located-interval-enclosure-endpoint-refinement-transport}
Let $E=(L,U,O,W,R,S,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp$ carrier. If refined dyadic endpoints $L',U'$ preserve the endpoint order row and the containment witness through the same $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ cell, then the enclosure transports to
$$
E'=(L',U',O,W,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
without changing the stream window, regular readback, Real seal, replay, provenance, or local name.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the endpoint refinement through the dyadic endpoint rows and the interval-net cell. The hypotheses keep the order and containment witness attached to the same displayed dyadic route.

Apply the componentwise $\hsame$ transport row $H$. It carries endpoint replacement while leaving the finite observation window, regular readback, Real seal, replay, provenance, and local name on their original rows.

The transported carrier therefore remains an accepted enclosure packet. Because no host real equality or quotient coordinate is present, the transport is only the displayed endpoint-refinement route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Located interval enclosure Real-seal nonescape]
\label{thm:located-interval-enclosure-real-seal-nonescape}
Every $\RealUp$-facing consumer of an accepted $\LocatedIntervalEnclosureUp$ packet factors through the displayed route
$$
L,U,O,W,R,S,H,C,P,N .
$$
The consumer receives a sealed real-name read inside dyadic enclosure rows, but it receives no host real equality, quotient representative, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, interval compactness theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with carrier extraction, \autoref{thm:located-interval-enclosure-carrier-extraction}. It forces the dyadic endpoints, order row, finite window, regular readback, and terminal seal to be visible before any enclosure judgment is public.

Use endpoint-refinement transport, \autoref{thm:located-interval-enclosure-endpoint-refinement-transport}, for the only permitted endpoint variation. Refinement preserves the displayed rows and cannot introduce a new host-side coordinate.

Finally read the refusal inventory in the classifier of \autoref{def:located-interval-enclosure-carrier}. Since the accepted packet has no coordinate for the excluded objects, the Real-facing route cannot escape the finite enclosure surface.
\end{proof}
