\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CompletionRecursionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-completion-recursion-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\CompletionRecursionUp$ packet records the eliminator surface by which a displayed dense source, a target completeness route, and a uniformly continuous seed map produce a completion-facing extension row. It sits beside the induction surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-completion-induction-namecert}, the dense-extension route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completion-dense-extension-namecert}, the uniformly continuous extension packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniform-continuous-extension-namecert}, the regular-Cauchy universal property of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-completion-universal-property-namecert}, and the Real completion universal property of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-completion-universal-property-namecert}. The packet names a finite recursor route; it does not assert a host quotient completion, selected representatives, arbitrary function extensionality, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.

\begin{definition}[Completion recursion carrier]
\label{def:completion-recursion-carrier}
A $\CompletionRecursionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(S,D,T,U,E,I,Q,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the dense source row whose names are admitted before any completed point is inspected.
\item $D$ is the density row, read through $\CompletionDenseExtensionUp$, recording approximation windows from completed points back to source names.
\item $T$ is the target completion row that receives the recursively defined output.
\item $U$ is the $\UniformContinuousExtensionUp$ modulus row for the seed map on $S$.
\item $E$ is the extension ledger that evaluates source windows and target Cauchy windows by the same tolerance request.
\item $I$ is the $\BishopCompletionInductionUp$ induction handoff, exposing the finite proof route that recursor values are determined by dense probes.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegularCauchyCompletionUniversalPropertyUp$ comparison row.
\item $W$ is the $\RealCompletionUniversalPropertyUp$ consumer row, used only after $S,D,T,U,E,I,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, density, target, modulus, extension, induction, universal-property, Real-consumer, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the recursor request through $S,D,T,U,E,I,Q,W,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over the five completion anchors, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompletionRecursionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for a host quotient completion, selected dense representative, unrestricted function extensionality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completion recursion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:completion-recursion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompletionRecursionUp$ carrier
$$
R=(S,D,T,U,E,I,Q,W,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CompletionRecursionUp}$ surface consists exactly of dense-source admission, density-window exposure, target-completion visibility, uniform-continuity modulus routing, extension-ledger replay, completion-induction handoff, regular-Cauchy universal-property comparison, Real-completion consumer exposure, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The source, density, target, modulus, extension, induction, universal-property, and Real-consumer rows are the only analytic rows available to a recursor consumer.

Read $S,D,T,U,E$ first. These rows bind the seed map to dense source windows and target Cauchy windows before any completed value is exported.

Next read $I,Q,W$. The induction row supplies the finite determination route, the regular-Cauchy universal-property row supplies the comparison surface, and the Real-completion row is exposed only as a downstream consumer.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since the excluded host quotient, selected representative, extensionality, choice, and ambient-completeness objects have no carrier coordinate, the listed clauses exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Completion recursion universal route]
\label{thm:completion-recursion-universal-route}
Every consumer that reads a $\CompletionRecursionUp$ packet as a completion eliminator must pass through the dense-source row $S$, density row $D$, target-completion row $T$, uniform-continuity row $U$, extension ledger $E$, induction handoff $I$, regular-Cauchy comparison row $Q$, Real-completion consumer row $W$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. The route exports a displayed recursor surface, not a host completion quotient or selected representative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:completion-recursion-namecert-obligations}. They force the consumer to display the source, density, target, modulus, extension, induction, comparison, and Real-facing rows before the recursor output is available.

The completion-induction handoff is the determination step. It says that values on completed names are read through dense probes and the extension ledger, not through a selected representative.

The universal-property rows then expose comparison and Real-facing consumption only over that displayed route. Therefore the consumer receives the finite recursor surface and none of the excluded host objects.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Completion recursion substitution law]
\label{thm:completion-recursion-substitution-law}
Suppose two accepted $\CompletionRecursionUp$ packets share the same displayed source row $S$, density row $D$, target row $T$, uniform-continuity row $U$, extension ledger $E$, induction handoff $I$, universal-property rows $Q,W$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$ up to the componentwise $\hsame$ transport named by their carriers. Then every recursor consumer obtains the same displayed completion route through those rows. The comparison is internal to the packet and does not use function extensionality outside the displayed $\hsame$ data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the two carriers and read their $H$ rows. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport identifies the displayed source, density, target, modulus, extension, induction, comparison, Real-consumer, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Use displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$ for each packet. A recursor request follows the same finite route through $S,D,T,U,E,I,Q,W$ after transport, so the observable completion route is stable under the named carrier comparison.

No external function equality is introduced. The substitution law is only the packet-internal statement that a consumer following the displayed rows cannot distinguish two carriers with the same transported route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Completion recursion public seed]
\label{thm:completion-recursion-public-seed}
The carrier and obligations of \autoref{def:completion-recursion-carrier} and \autoref{thm:completion-recursion-namecert-obligations} seed a public $\CompletionRecursionUp$ surface for downstream $\RealUp$ and regular-Cauchy completion chapters: a consumer may read dense-source admission, uniform-continuity routing, induction handoff, universal-property comparison, and Real-completion consumption, but it may not read closed quotient equality, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient completeness from this packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Combine the universal route of \autoref{thm:completion-recursion-universal-route} with the substitution law of \autoref{thm:completion-recursion-substitution-law}. The first theorem exposes the recursor route to completion consumers, while the second keeps substitutions internal to componentwise transport and displayed replay.

The public surface is therefore exactly the finite route named by the carrier. Real and regular-Cauchy consumers may inspect the route, but the carrier has no coordinate that could provide any excluded host endpoint.
\end{proof}
