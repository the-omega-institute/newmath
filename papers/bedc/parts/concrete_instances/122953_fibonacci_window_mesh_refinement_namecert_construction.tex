The Fibonacci-hat Weil window realizes the mesh-refinement branch of the log-hat density problem for the finite Weil shadow. For each $m\geq 3$ the window is
$$
[-\log F_{m+2},\log F_{m+2}]
$$
and the hat spacing is
$$
h_m=\frac{\log F_{m+2}}{F_m}.
$$
The intended geometric condition is strict refinement,
$$
h_{m+1}<h_m.
$$
Because the core Lean certificate is mathlib-free and contains no logarithm interface, the formal side records the exact integer comparison obtained by exponentiating the logarithmic inequality. Namely, monotonicity of the logarithm gives the chain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\log F_{m+3}}{F_{m+1}}<\frac{\log F_{m+2}}{F_m}
&\Longleftrightarrow F_m\log F_{m+3}<F_{m+1}\log F_{m+2}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow \log(F_{m+3}^{F_m})<\log(F_{m+2}^{F_{m+1}})\\
&\Longleftrightarrow F_{m+3}^{F_m}<F_{m+2}^{F_{m+1}} .
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the finite certificate is the pure natural-number inequality
$$
F_{m+3}^{F_m}<F_{m+2}^{F_{m+1}},
$$
checked for the displayed Fibonacci-window range together with the window-growth comparisons
$$
F_{m+2}<F_{m+3}.
$$
These two families are the finite bricks behind the necessary geometric behavior: the mesh tends toward zero while the logarithmic window tends toward infinity. They are necessary conditions for the totality of the log-hat test system in the Weil space.

\begin{theorem}[Fibonacci-window mesh refinement bricks]
\label{thm:fibonacci-window-mesh-refinement-bricks}
For $m=3,4,5,6,7$ the integer mesh-refinement comparisons
$$
F_{m+3}^{F_m}<F_{m+2}^{F_{m+1}}
$$
hold, and the window-growth comparisons
$$
F_{m+2}<F_{m+3}
$$
hold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Lean certificate unfolds the Fibonacci recursion at the listed indices and proves each strict natural-number comparison by an explicit slack equality. For example, the first mesh comparison is
$$
8^2=64<125=5^3.
$$
The remaining comparisons are the same kind of integer certificates:
$$
13^3<8^5,\qquad 21^5<13^8,\qquad 34^8<21^{13},\qquad 55^{13}<34^{21}.
$$
No analytic logarithm theorem is used inside Lean; the logarithmic bridge above is the paper-side interpretation of the integer certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Density frontier for the Fibonacci-window Weil packet]
\label{thm:fibonacci-window-density-frontier}
The mesh refinement and window growth are necessary conditions for the totality of the log-hat test system, but they are not a density theorem and not a proof of RH.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The finite Weil shadow has a visible numerical decay law: the minimum eigenvalue of the inspected matrices behaves like
$$
\min\text{-eig}(W^{(m)})\sim \frac{C}{F_m^2}
$$
with $C$ approximately stable in the inspected range. This means that the positive-definiteness margin disappears at the same scale as $1/F_m^2$. Certification therefore becomes more expensive as the Fibonacci window grows: exact interval verification must keep rational entry enclosures fine enough to resolve a margin of order $F_m^{-2}$.

This is not a cosmetic numerical issue. Floating quadrature can certify the early windows, but by $m=7$ the expected margin is below the ambient floating error floor of the naive computation. A serious certificate must use interval-certified rational entries and exact lower bounds of the form
$$
W^{(m)}\succeq \delta_m I.
$$
The mesh refinement and window growth are necessary conditions for the totality of the log-hat test system, but the full fundamentality statement -- that finite Weil positivity across all windows forces RH -- is entangled with the fine distribution of the zeros and is essentially equivalent to RH itself. This packet records the exact finite mesh-geometry bricks and the honest frontier, not a density theorem.
\end{proof}
