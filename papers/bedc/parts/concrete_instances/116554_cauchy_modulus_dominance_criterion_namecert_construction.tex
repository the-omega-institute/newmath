It sits over the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the base modulus schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchymodulus-namecert}, the completion-admission surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletioncriterion-namecert}, and the terminal Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a finite dominance criterion for Real and Completion consumers; it is not a quotient stream equality, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, trichotomy split, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness assertion.

\begin{definition}[Cauchy modulus dominance criterion carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-modulus-dominance-criterion-carrier}
A $\CauchyModulusDominanceCriterionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(S,Q,D,M_0,M_1,B,E,H,C,P,N)

\begin{enumerate}

\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback row attached to the displayed windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger carrying the dyadic precision requests shown to the consumer.
\item $M_0$ is the base $\CauchyModulusUp$ row assigning a finite tail threshold to each displayed dyadic request.
\item $M_1$ is the dominating $\CauchyModulusUp$ row for the same displayed requests, with each shown threshold at least as far out as the corresponding threshold in $M_0$.
\item $B$ is the local dominance-criterion row, recording that $M_1$ dominates $M_0$ on exactly the dyadic requests displayed by $D$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached by the same Real and Completion route after $S,Q,D,M_0,M_1,B$ have been displayed.

The carrier has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected tail functions, selected limits, countable choice, host sequence equality, host real equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy modulus dominance criterion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-modulus-dominance-criterion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyModulusDominanceCriterionUp$ carrier
$$
K=(S,Q,D,M_0,M_1,B,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyModulusDominanceCriterionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier exposure for the displayed packet, finite-window exposure through $S$, regular rational readback through $Q$, dyadic tolerance exposure through $D$, base-modulus exposure through $M_0$, dominating-modulus exposure through $M_1$, dominance exposure through $B$, terminal $\RealUp$ seal exposure through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed continuation replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier in \autoref{def:cauchy-modulus-dominance-criterion-carrier}. Each obligation names one coordinate of the same finite $\BHist$ packet, and the local naming row $N$ records that these coordinates form the certificate surface.

The analytic part is read in the displayed order. A dyadic request first enters the ledger $D$, then reads the base threshold $M_0$ and the dominating threshold $M_1$, and then checks the dominance row $B$ for that displayed request. The stream windows $S$ and regular readback $Q$ are therefore attached to the same finite request route.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name this route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected tails, selected limits, countable choice, host equality, trichotomy, or ambient completeness, none of those objects can be exported by the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:cauchy-modulus-dominance-criterion-real-seal-route}

$$
The replacement preserves the terminal seal row $E$ and exports no quotient stream row, selected limit row, countable-choice row, host equality row, trichotomy row, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

Thus replacing $M_0$ by $M_1$ cannot introduce a hidden schedule, selected tail, or quotient stream comparison; it merely moves the finite threshold farther along the already displayed route.

Finally read the provenance and replay rows. They attach the dominance route to the completion-admission surface and the terminal Real seal without adding another carrier coordinate. Hence the Real seal route remains the one displayed in the theorem statement, and all excluded host or ambient principles remain outside the packet.
\end{proof}
