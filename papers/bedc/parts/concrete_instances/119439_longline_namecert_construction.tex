\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LongLineUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-longline-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Munkres and Kelley on the long line, the $\LongLineUp$ packet records the classical lexicographic order-topology boundary as finite BEDC NameCert data. It sits beside the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the order interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-order-namecert}, and the total-order comparison surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totalorder-namecert}. The packet names inspected well-order prefixes, interval cells, lexicographic comparisons, order-topology neighbourhoods, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming without importing a host ordinal, uncountable choice, quotient point equality, selected representatives, or a compactness theorem.

\begin{definition}[Long line carrier]
\label{def:long-line-carrier}
A $\LongLineUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(S,I,O,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the well-order-prefix schedule. Each inspected prefix is recorded as a finite named stage together with its predecessor and successor requests; the schedule is a displayed row of prefix evidence, not a host ordinal.
\item $I$ is the interval-cell row. It records the unit-cell coordinate inside the displayed prefix stage, the left and right boundary requests, and the local interval membership tests needed by neighbourhood consumers.
\item $O$ is the lexicographic comparison row. It compares two displayed points first by the prefix schedule $S$ and then by the interval-cell coordinate $I$, exposing the comparison only through the order rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-order-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totalorder-namecert}.
\item $T$ is the order-topology neighbourhood row. It records finite open rays and finite open intervals generated from $O$ and exports them to the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the prefix schedule, interval cells, lexicographic comparisons, order-topology neighbourhoods, provenance, replay, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a neighbourhood or comparison request through $S,I,O,T$ and then through the structural rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LongLineUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\OrderUp$, $\TotalOrderUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LongLineUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,I,O,T,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host ordinal, an uncountable choice principle, quotient point equality, selected representatives, a completed open-set lattice, or a compactness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[LongLine NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:long-line-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LongLineUp$ carrier $L=(S,I,O,T,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LongLineUp}$ surface consists exactly of the well-order-prefix schedule $S$, interval-cell row $I$, lexicographic comparison row $O$, order-topology neighbourhood row $T$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, package provenance $P$, and local naming $N$. Every topology- or order-facing consumer must expose these rows before reading a long-line point comparison, interval membership test, open ray, or open interval.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.LongLineUp.LongLineCarrier\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:long-line-carrier}. The only prefix evidence is $S$, and the only within-prefix coordinate evidence is $I$.

The order-facing read is then forced through $O$. It first reads the displayed prefix schedule and then the interval-cell coordinate, so comparison data cannot be replaced by a host ordinal comparison or by quotient point equality.

The topology-facing read is forced through $T$. Its open rays and intervals are generated from the displayed lexicographic comparison row and are exported only through the topology surface cited above.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no row stores uncountable choice, selected representatives, a completed open-set lattice, or compactness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LongLine lexicographic order route]
\label{thm:long-line-lexicographic-order-route}
Every $\LongLineUp$ consumer that reads a point comparison or an order-topology neighbourhood factors through the finite route
$$
S,I \longrightarrow O \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow H,C,P,N .
$$
The route exposes the order comparison inherited from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-order-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totalorder-namecert} before it exposes the topology read of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}. It exports no host ordinal, no uncountable choice, no quotient point equality, no selected representatives, and no compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the displayed point data. A point read has only the prefix schedule $S$ and interval-cell row $I$, so there is no separate representative or host ordinal from which a consumer can read comparison data.

Next read the lexicographic row $O$. It is the sole comparison row and therefore the only route to order-facing conclusions.

Then read the order-topology row $T$. Open rays and open intervals are generated from $O$, so a topology consumer reaches the open-neighbourhood surface only after the displayed lexicographic order route is visible.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name that same route. They do not add uncountable choice, quotient point equality, selected representatives, a completed open-set lattice, or compactness evidence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LongLine prefix-neighbourhood exactness]
\label{thm:long-line-prefix-neighbourhood-exactness}
Any accepted $\LongLineUp$ open-ray or interval-neighbourhood read must display the well-order-prefix schedule $S$, interval-cell row $I$, lexicographic comparison row $O$, order-topology row $T$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$ from the carrier
$$
L=(S,I,O,T,H,C,P,N).
$$
It cannot replace prefix evidence with a host ordinal, uncountable choice, quotient point equality, selected representative, completed open-set lattice, or compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:long-line-carrier}. The prefix schedule $S$ and interval-cell row $I$ are the only source rows from which a long-line point read can obtain prefix and within-prefix evidence.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:long-line-namecert-obligations}. They require every topology- or order-facing consumer to expose $S,I,O,T,H,C,P,N$ before reading point comparison, interval membership, open rays, or open intervals.

Now compose with the lexicographic order route of \autoref{thm:long-line-lexicographic-order-route}. The comparison row $O$ is read after $S,I$, and the order-topology row $T$ is read after $O$, so a neighbourhood read cannot bypass the displayed prefix route.

The refused endpoints are absent from the finite packet. Since $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name the same rows, they do not add host ordinals, choice, quotient equality, representatives, completed open lattices, or compactness evidence.
\end{proof}
