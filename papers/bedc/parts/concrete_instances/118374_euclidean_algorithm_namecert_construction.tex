\begin{proof}

Read the budget and terminal rows next. The row $B$ is the only coordinate that records strict descent, and the row $T$ is the only coordinate that records the terminal zero-remainder step and its divisor output.

\begin{theorem}[Euclidean algorithm descent budget]
\label{thm:euclidean-algorithm-descent-budget}
Every accepted $\EuclideanAlgorithmUp$ carrier exposes termination only through the finite budget row $B$. If a nonterminal step of $S$ has divisor row $d$ and nonzero remainder row $r$, then the budget ledger records a strict $\NatUp$ comparison from the value attached to $r$ below the value attached to $d$. The terminal row $T$ may be read only after this finite descent ledger reaches a displayed zero-remainder step.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct over the finite step ledger $S$. In the base case, the first displayed step is read with its divisor, quotient, remainder, and local division evidence, and the budget row $B$ supplies the comparison attached to that step.

For the induction step, assume the current nonterminal row exposes a nonzero remainder. The next row of $S$ uses that remainder as the new divisor row, and $B$ records the strict $\NatUp$ descent from the old divisor budget to the new divisor budget.

Because the induction ranges only over the displayed finite ledger, it cannot appeal to a host well-ordering or to an unlisted search procedure. The terminal divisor row $T$ is available exactly when the next inspected remainder is the displayed zero row, and the Bezout row $Z$ can then replay the reversed finite ledger without adding another termination principle.
\end{proof}
