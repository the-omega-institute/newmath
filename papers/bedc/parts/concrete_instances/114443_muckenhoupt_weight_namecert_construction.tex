Following Muckenhoupt, Stein, and Grafakos, the $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$ packet records the finite $A_p$ weight surface used by weighted maximal-function arguments. It sits after the maximal-function carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hardy-littlewood-maximal-namecert}, the finite covering ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-vitali-cover-namecert}, the measure and regular-measure rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-measure-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-radon-measure-namecert}, and the real and dyadic readback surfaces of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}. The packet names the displayed local-average rows required to inspect an $A_p$ window without importing a general weighted norm theorem, Lebesgue differentiation, choice-selected covers, or ambient measure completion.

\begin{definition}[Muckenhoupt weight carrier]
\label{def:muckenhoupt-weight-carrier}
A $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
W=(X,M,R,Q,B,A,D,L,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the metric-source row, recording the carried points and the finite ball names read by the weight request.
\item $M$ is the $\MeasureUp$ row, refined when needed through $\RadonMeasureUp$ regular finite-measure windows.
\item $R$ is the positive-radius and dyadic-scale row, read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparisons for the inspected balls.
\item $Q$ is the exponent row. It records the displayed $p$ and conjugate-budget comparisons used by the $A_p$ ledger.
\item $B$ is the finite ball-window row, listing the balls on which weight and reciprocal-power averages are read.
\item $A$ is the weight-average row. For every displayed ball it records the finite $\RealUp$ readback of the weight average.
\item $D$ is the reciprocal-power average row, recording the finite readback needed for the conjugate side of the $A_p$ test.
\item $L$ is the $A_p$ ledger row. It stores the finite comparison budget showing that the product of the displayed averages stays below the named local constant on each inspected ball.
\item $E$ is the maximal-consumer boundary row, recording which $\HardyLittlewoodMaximalUp$ request may read the weight packet and which rows remain hidden from that consumer.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,M,R,Q,B,A,D,L,E,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
\MeasureUp,\RadonMeasureUp,\DyadicRatCoreUp,\RealUp
\longmapsto
A,D,L
\longmapsto
\MuckenhouptWeightUp .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$, $\HardyLittlewoodMaximalUp$, $\FiniteVitaliCoverUp$, $\MeasureUp$, $\RadonMeasureUp$, $\RealUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MuckenhouptWeightUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $X,M,R,Q,B,A,D,L,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a global weighted norm theorem, a Lebesgue differentiation theorem, a choice-selected cover, quotient function equality, or an ambient measure-completion object.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Muckenhoupt weight NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:muckenhoupt-weight-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$ carrier
$$
W=(X,M,R,Q,B,A,D,L,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\MuckenhouptWeightUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, measure and Radon-window visibility, dyadic radius comparison, exponent-budget visibility, ball-window coverage, weight-average readback, reciprocal-power average readback, $A_p$ ledger comparison, maximal-consumer boundary exposure, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:muckenhoupt-weight-carrier}. The source, measure, dyadic-radius, exponent, ball-window, average, reciprocal-average, ledger, and consumer-boundary rows are the only analytic rows visible to the local certificate.

Measure visibility is read through $M$ and, for regular finite windows, through the $\RadonMeasureUp$ provenance in $P$. Radius and scale comparisons are read through $R$, so the local averages in $A$ and $D$ are attached to displayed finite ball windows rather than to a host family of balls.

The $A_p$ obligation is exactly the ledger row $L$. It compares the displayed weight average and reciprocal-power average against the local bound named for the same ball window and exponent budget.

Finally $E,H,C,P,N$ expose the maximal-consumer boundary, transport, replay, source, and local name for the same finite route. Since no coordinate stores a global weighted norm theorem, Lebesgue differentiation, a choice-selected cover, quotient function equality, or measure completion, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Muckenhoupt weight maximal consumer boundary]
\label{thm:muckenhoupt-weight-maximal-consumer-boundary}
Every $\HardyLittlewoodMaximalUp$ consumer of an accepted $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$ packet reads only the displayed finite rows $M,R,Q,B,A,D,L,E,H,C,P,N$. The consumer may inspect finite weighted averages, reciprocal-power averages, and the $A_p$ comparison ledger on named ball windows, but it does not receive a general weighted norm theorem, a Lebesgue differentiation theorem, a choice-selected cover, quotient function equality, or ambient measure completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the boundary row $E$. It records the exact maximal-function request allowed to inspect the weight packet and points that request back to the same ball windows $B$, dyadic-radius row $R$, and measure row $M$.

The numerical data available to the consumer are the finite readbacks $A$ and $D$ together with the ledger $L$. These rows certify only the displayed $A_p$ comparison on inspected balls, and the replay row $C$ keeps the route inside $\MeasureUp$, $\RadonMeasureUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ readbacks.

The structural rows $H,P,N$ transport, source, and name the same packet. Because the carrier lacks coordinates for norm inequalities, differentiation, selected covers, quotient equality, and measure completion, the maximal consumer boundary exports exactly the finite rows stated above.
\end{proof}
