Following Birkhoff's projective metric on cones and standard positive-operator treatments, the $\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp$ packet records the finite cone-ratio surface by which two positive cone elements are compared before a contraction theorem consumes the result. It sits over the positive-cone carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-positive-cone-namecert}, the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the real-name readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the contraction consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-birkhoff-contraction-namecert}. The packet names a finite projective-distance ledger; it is not a quotient of cone rays, an ambient topological cone, a supremum-infimum oracle, or a fixed-point theorem.

\begin{definition}[Birkhoff projective metric carrier]\label{def:birkhoff-projective-metric-carrier}
A $\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(K,x,y,U,L,\rho,d,M,G,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\PositiveConeUp$ source row, fixing the cone surface and the scalar-order comparison used by every ratio read.
\item $x$ and $y$ are two displayed positive cone elements in the same $K$ row.
\item $U$ is the finite upper-ratio ledger, listing the displayed scalar witnesses for $x\leq u\,y$ that the packet is allowed to inspect.
\item $L$ is the finite lower-ratio ledger, listing the displayed scalar witnesses for $\ell\,y\leq x$ that the packet is allowed to inspect.
\item $\rho$ is the $\RealUp$ scalar readback row for the accepted upper and lower ratio bounds.
\item $d$ is the $\MetricUp$ projective-distance row, storing only the displayed logarithmic ratio readback produced from $U,L,\rho$.
\item $M$ is the metric-surface compatibility row, requiring the distance read to be symmetric on the displayed pair and zero only through the local projective-identification ledger.
\item $G$ is the $\BirkhoffContractionUp$ consumer handoff row, exposing $K,x,y,U,L,\rho,d$ as the finite Hilbert-projective metric surface later used by contraction budgets.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the cone source, two positive elements, ratio ledgers, scalar readbacks, projective-distance row, metric compatibility, consumer handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a projective-distance request through $K,x,y,U,L,\rho,d,M,G,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp$, $\PositiveConeUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BirkhoffContractionUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,x,y,U,L,\rho,d,M,G,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an unstated cone quotient, hidden supremum or infimum, ambient norm, fixed point, eigenvector, non-displayed operator, or host real equality outside the finite ratio ledgers.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff projective metric NameCert obligations]\label{thm:birkhoff-projective-metric-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp$ carrier
$$
B=(K,x,y,U,L,\rho,d,M,G,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the $\PositiveConeUp$ source row, the two displayed positive elements, finite upper- and lower-ratio ledgers, $\RealUp$ scalar readbacks, the $\MetricUp$ projective-distance row, the metric-compatibility row, the $\BirkhoffContractionUp$ consumer handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.

Every downstream projective-metric read must first display the common cone source and both ratio ledgers. The read may then consume $\rho$ and $d$ as finite scalar and metric rows, but it cannot replace them with a completed quotient of rays, a hidden extremal ratio, a host topological cone, or a contraction conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:birkhoff-projective-metric-carrier}. The rows $K,x,y$ fix a single positive-cone source and two elements in that source before any ratio comparison can be read.

The rows $U$ and $L$ are finite ledgers. They display only the upper witnesses $x\leq u\,y$ and lower witnesses $\ell\,y\leq x$ that the packet records, so no hidden supremum, infimum, or ambient order oracle is available.

The scalar row $\rho$ and metric row $d$ are downstream of those ledgers. The compatibility row $M$ keeps the projective-distance read inside the displayed metric surface, and $G$ exposes exactly the same data to the $\BirkhoffContractionUp$ consumer.

Transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$ preserve the same finite row inventory. Since the classifier has no coordinate outside $K,x,y,U,L,\rho,d,M,G,H,C,P,N$, every accepted NameCert obligation is one of the listed rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff projective metric ratio nonescape]\label{thm:birkhoff-projective-metric-ratio-nonescape}
An accepted $\BirkhoffProjectiveMetricUp$ packet exports no projective distance unless the common $\PositiveConeUp$ source row, both positive elements, finite upper-ratio ledger, finite lower-ratio ledger, $\RealUp$ scalar readback, and $\MetricUp$ projective-distance row are all displayed in the same carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier inventory. The distance row $d$ is admitted only after the source row $K$, element rows $x,y$, ratio ledgers $U,L$, and scalar readback row $\rho$ have been displayed.

The replay row $C$ follows the same order, and the handoff row $G$ exposes the distance to $\BirkhoffContractionUp$ only with the source, elements, ledgers, and readback still attached.

Thus no public read can detach $d$ from the finite ratio evidence that produced it. Quotient-ray language, hidden extremal ratios, and host completeness claims remain outside the packet.
\end{proof}
