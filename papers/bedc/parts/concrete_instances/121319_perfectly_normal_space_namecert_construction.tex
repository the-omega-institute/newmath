\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-perfectly-normal-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Engelking and Munkres on perfectly normal spaces, the $\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp$ packet records the classical separation surface where every displayed closed row is presented as a zero set and every displayed open row is presented as the complementary cozero set. It sits above the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the closed-pair separation route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, the separation-function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}, the zero/cozero boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-zero-set-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cozero-set-namecert}, and the continuous-map surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}. The packet names only finite displayed rows for this traditional point-set condition; it does not import host powersets, arbitrary function spaces, selected complements, quotient equality of functions, or ambient real completeness.

\begin{definition}[Perfectly normal space carrier]
\label{def:perfectly-normal-space-carrier}
A $\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
P=(T,N,F,Z,O,U,M,H,C,K,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row, carrying the open-set and closed-set observation surface.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ row, exposing the closed-pair neighborhood separation route already named in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}.
\item $F$ is the finite ledger of displayed closed rows that the packet will expose as zero sets.
\item $Z$ assigns to each closed row in $F$ a $\ZeroSetUp$ witness and the corresponding continuous real-valued function row.
\item $O$ is the open/cozero handoff row, recording that the complement of each displayed closed row in $F$ is consumed as a $\CozeroSetUp$ row.
\item $U$ is the $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ boundary row, tying the zero-set and cozero-set witnesses to the separation-function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}.
\item $M$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ row for the displayed real-valued functions used by $Z$ and $O$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the topology, normal-space, closed-row, zero-set, cozero-set, Urysohn-boundary, continuous-map, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a closed-row request through $T,N,F,Z,O,U,M$.
\item $K$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\NormalSpaceUp$, $\ZeroSetUp$, $\CozeroSetUp$, $\UrysohnLemmaUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for a host powerset of closed subsets, arbitrary complement selection, unrestricted function-space equality, choice over separating maps, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Perfectly normal space NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:perfectly-normal-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp$ carrier
$$
P=(T,N,F,Z,O,U,M,H,C,K,L),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\PerfectlyNormalSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of the finite rows that admit the topology source, the normal-space closed-pair route, the displayed closed rows, their zero-set witnesses, the complementary cozero-set handoff, the Urysohn function boundary, the continuous-map rows, componentwise $\hsame$, displayed $\Cont$, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every public read of perfect normality must pass through these rows before exporting a perfectly normal space name.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:perfectly-normal-space-carrier}. The accepted packet displays the topology source $T$, normal-space route $N$, closed-row ledger $F$, zero-set witnesses $Z$, cozero handoff $O$, Urysohn boundary $U$, continuous-map row $M$, and the structural rows $H,C,K,L$.

Closed-as-zero-set exposure is read through $F$ and $Z$. Each displayed closed row has its zero-set witness before any consumer may treat the closed row as part of the perfectly normal surface.

The open/cozero handoff is read through $O$. Since $O$ is attached to the same closed rows and the same continuous-map data, an open-row consumer receives only the displayed complementary cozero route, not an ambient complement selector.

The Urysohn and Tietze-facing nonescape condition is enforced by $U$ and $M$. A downstream separation-function or extension reader must pass through the normal-space row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, the Urysohn route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}, and the displayed $\ContinuousMapUp$ row before using the zero/cozero certificate.

Finally the componentwise $\hsame$ row, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming rows preserve this finite route. They provide stability, source accounting, and local naming, while adding no host powerset, choice principle, quotient function equality, or ambient completeness datum.
\end{proof}
