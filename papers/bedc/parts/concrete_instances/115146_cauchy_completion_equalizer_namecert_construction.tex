Following Bishop and Bridges and Bourbaki/Kelley completion terminology, the $\CauchyCompletionEqualizerUp$ packet records the finite boundary where two displayed reads of a Cauchy completion are compared before any quotient completion equality is invoked. It sits after the finite stream-window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the public completion reflector route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletionreflector-namecert}. The packet names an equalizer surface for two completion reads; it does not identify quotient classes, select representatives, import host equality, or assert ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Cauchy completion equalizer carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-completion-equalizer-carrier}
A $\CauchyCompletionEqualizerUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(S,Q,D,R,A,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ row listing the finite observation windows used by both completion reads.
\item $Q$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row through which the two reads are compared at each requested window.
\item $D$ is the displayed source regularity row, read through $\RegSeqRatUp$ and carrying the rational Cauchy data common to both sides.
\item $R$ is the $\CauchyCompletionReflectorUp$ row exposing the completion-facing handoff without presenting a quotient carrier or selected host limit.
\item $A$ is the first displayed completion read, obtained only after $S,Q,D,R$ have been exposed.
\item $B$ is the second displayed completion read over the same $S,Q,D,R$ boundary.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the stream, dyadic, regular-rational, reflector, two read, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a comparison request through $S,Q,D,R,A,B,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyCompletionEqualizerUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CauchyCompletionReflectorUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyCompletionEqualizerUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through the displayed rows $S,Q,D,R,A,B,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, host equality, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy completion equalizer NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-completion-equalizer-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyCompletionEqualizerUp$ carrier $E=(S,Q,D,R,A,B,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyCompletionEqualizerUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, shared stream-window exposure through $S$, shared dyadic tolerance exposure through $Q$, regular-rational source exposure through $D$, reflector handoff through $R$, the two displayed completion reads $A,B$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, local naming through $N$, and non-escape from quotient completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, host equality, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or bridge checking.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:cauchy-completion-equalizer-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes exactly the stream, dyadic, regular-rational, reflector, two read, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The equalizer read is shared before it is compared. Both $A$ and $B$ are downstream of the same finite windows $S$, tolerance row $Q$, regular-rational source row $D$, and reflector row $R$.

The comparison is therefore a displayed $\BHist$ and $\NameCert$ route, not a quotient-completion equation. Since the carrier contains no row for selected representatives, host equality, countable choice, ambient completeness, or bridge checking, the listed rows exhaust the local obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy completion equalizer reflector kernel]
\label{thm:cauchy-completion-equalizer-reflector-kernel}

$$
\StreamNameUp,\ \DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \CauchyCompletionReflectorUp \longmapsto A,B .
$$
Consequently the equalizer kernel available to the consumer is the displayed agreement boundary over $S,Q,D,R,A,B,H,C,P,N$, not quotient completion equality, selected representatives, countable choice, host equality, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or bridge checking.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Open an accepted carrier $E=(S,Q,D,R,A,B,H,C,P,N)$. The only completion-facing handoff row is $R$, and both displayed reads $A$ and $B$ are stored after the shared stream, dyadic, and regular-rational rows.

The reflector-facing consumer must therefore inspect $S,Q,D,R$ before either read is available. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve the same boundary, while $P$ and $N$ source and name only that boundary.

No further kernel data can be read from the packet. The missing quotient, representative-selection, host-equality, choice, completeness, and bridge coordinates cannot be reconstructed from the displayed rows, so the consumer receives exactly the finite equalizer surface.
\end{proof}
