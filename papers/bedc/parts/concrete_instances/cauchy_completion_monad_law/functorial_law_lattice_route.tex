\begin{lemma}[CauchyCompletionMonadLaw functorial law lattice route]
\label{lem:cauchy-completion-monad-law-functorial-law-lattice-route}
Every accepted $\CauchyCompletionMonadLawUp$ law read first consumes the functorial completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletionfunctor-namecert} and the universal-property route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completionuniversalproperty-namecert}. Only after those rows are displayed may the unit, bind, idempotence, and associativity rows of $L=(M,U,I,B,S,D,R,H,C,P,N)$ be read as monad-law data. The packet therefore records a law lattice over the public functorial completion dependency, not a host-level completion equality or a quotient-completion shortcut.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-completion-monad-law-carrier}. The rows $M,U,I,B,S,D,R,H,C,P,N$ are the monad-law surface, while the functorial completion packet supplies the public source-metric, regular-Cauchy, completion-seal, unit-and-bind, universal-property, classifier, transport, provenance, and local naming route.

Apply \autoref{thm:cauchy-completion-monad-law-functor-dependency-route}. It forces the completion functor and universal-property rows to be read before the law rows are consumed by a completion-facing client.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:cauchy-completion-monad-law-namecert-obligations} and \autoref{thm:cauchy-completion-monad-law-unit-associativity-boundary}. These replay unit, bind, idempotence, and associativity through the same finite stream, dyadic, regular-rational, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows, so the law lattice remains attached to the displayed functorial dependency.
\end{proof}
