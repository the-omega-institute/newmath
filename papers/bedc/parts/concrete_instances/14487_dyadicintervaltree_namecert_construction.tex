\chapter{Dyadic interval tree NameCert construction}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicintervaltree-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges and Moore--Kearfott--Cloud interval analysis, the $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet records a finite tree-shaped dyadic bisection ledger for the open Real and Completion route. It sits between the branch construction of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicbisection-namecert}, the subdivision frontier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicsubdivision-namecert}, the rational containment surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rationalintervalrefinement-namecert}, the nested-window route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nestedinterval-namecert}, and the dyadic mesh interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicmesh-namecert}. The packet is a finite refinement witness that can be consumed by $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ window proofs; it is not an infinite interval tree, a host subset of $\RealUp$, or a selected completed point.

\begin{definition}[DyadicIntervalTree carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-interval-tree-carrier}
A $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the root rational interval row, including the ordered endpoint cells and the root containment certificate used by downstream rational-window consumers;
\item $D$ is the unary depth row naming the finite height inspected by the packet;
\item $B$ is the finite branch ledger, whose entries list exactly the retained left or right child decisions below the root;
\item $F$ is the frontier ledger, a finite family of $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ rows recording the leaf cells visible after the branch ledger has been applied;
\item $M$ is the $\DyadicMeshUp$ cell row that supplies the common dyadic scale for the frontier cells and consumer windows;
\item $Q$ records the $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment rows, one for each retained parent-child interval relation named by $B$ and $F$;
\item $NW$ is the $\NestedIntervalUp$ route row obtained by threading the retained branch prefix from the root row $R$ through the containment rows $Q$;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the root row, unary depth row, branch ledger, subdivision frontier, mesh cells, rational-containment rows, nested route, continuation rows, provenance row, and local naming row;
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay routes by which a consumer rereads the root interval, each dyadic child row, the frontier cells, the containment rows, and the nested route without changing the finite branch ledger;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$, $\DyadicBisectionUp$, $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$, $\DyadicMeshUp$, $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$, $\NestedIntervalUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalTreeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an infinite interval tree, a host subset of $\RealUp$, a Dedekind cut, a selected completed point, open-cover compactness, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[DyadicIntervalTree classifier]
\label{def:dyadic-interval-tree-classifier}
For accepted carriers
$$
T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)
\quad\text{and}\quad
T'=(R',D',B',F',M',Q',NW',H',C',P',L'),
$$
the $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ classifier compares only the displayed rows of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-tree-carrier}. It accepts precisely when the root rational interval rows, unary depth rows, finite branch ledgers, $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ frontier rows, $\DyadicMeshUp$ cell rows, $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment rows, $\NestedIntervalUp$ route rows, $\hsame$ transports, $\Cont$ replays, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ rows agree componentwise. It does not compare ambient sets of real points, infinite branches, Dedekind cuts, selected limits, or completeness witnesses.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalTreeUp}$ obligations are exhausted by carrier exposure, classifier exposure, root rational interval exposure, unary depth exactness, finite branch-ledger exactness, $\DyadicBisectionUp$ child-row exposure, $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ frontier exposure, $\DyadicMeshUp$ cell exposure, $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment exposure, $\NestedIntervalUp$ route exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local naming row $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-tree-carrier}. The root, depth, branch, frontier, mesh, containment, and nested-route obligations are exactly the rows $R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW$.

The classifier obligation is the displayed comparison of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-tree-classifier}. Because the comparison is componentwise, it cannot inspect any ambient real subset, infinite branch, Dedekind cut, selected completed point, compactness principle, or completeness witness.

The remaining obligations are structural. The $\hsame$ row $H$ transports the same displayed rows, the $\Cont$ row $C$ replays only the named reads, and the $\Pkg$ row $P$ records the finite dependency surface. The local row $L$ then names precisely this packet, so the listed rows exhaust the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree refinement induction]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-refinement-induction}
Let $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet. Any $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ window proof that consumes the tree factors through finite induction on the branch ledger $B$: the base step reads the root rational interval row $R$, and each branch step reads one displayed $\DyadicBisectionUp$ child row, the corresponding $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment row in $Q$, the updated $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ frontier row in $F$, and the same $\DyadicMeshUp$ cell scale $M$. The induction terminates at the unary depth row $D$ and exports only the nested route $NW$ through $H,C,P,L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use induction on the finite branch ledger $B$. In the empty ledger case, the consumer reads the root rational interval row $R$ and the mesh row $M$; the nested route $NW$ is the root window with the displayed transports and replay rows attached.

For a nonempty ledger, split off the first child decision. The corresponding $\DyadicBisectionUp$ child row supplies the retained child interval, the matching $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ row in $Q$ records containment inside the current parent, and the $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ row in $F$ records the visible frontier after this step.

Apply the induction hypothesis to the remaining finite ledger. The unary depth row $D$ bounds the number of repetitions, while $H$ and $C$ keep each transported and replayed row componentwise identical to the displayed packet data.

Thus every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ window proof reaches the same finite nested route $NW$ through $P$ and $L$. No step introduces an infinite tree, a host subset of $\RealUp$, a Dedekind cut, a selected completed point, open-cover compactness, or ambient real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[DyadicIntervalTree level scope]
\label{def:dyadic-interval-tree-level-scope}
For an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$, the \emph{level scope} at a displayed depth prefix is the finite read surface consisting of the root interval row $R$, the unary depth prefix of $D$, the branch-ledger entries of $B$ already consumed, the visible frontier rows of $F$, the common mesh row $M$, the containment rows of $Q$ reached by that prefix, and the corresponding nested-route prefix of $NW$. Its structural boundary is transported by $H$, replayed by $C$, recorded by $P$, and named by $L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree branch coverage]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-branch-coverage}
Let $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet. Every retained branch row in $B$ is backed by a displayed $\DyadicBisectionUp$ child row and a matching $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment row in $Q$, and both rows lie in the level scope of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-tree-level-scope} at the branch prefix where the decision is read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the finite branch ledger $B$. In the empty case there is no retained branch row, so the assertion is exhausted by the root level scope.

For a nonempty branch ledger, read its first retained child decision. The carrier rows require the corresponding dyadic child to be exposed among the branch data and require the parent-child containment to be recorded in $Q$.

Apply the induction hypothesis to the remaining branch suffix. The transport and replay rows $H$ and $C$ preserve the same child and containment reads at each later prefix, while $P$ and $L$ keep those reads inside the local packet. Thus every retained branch row is covered by the displayed bisection and rational-containment evidence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree leaf locality]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-leaf-locality}
Let $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet. Every leaf frontier read in $F$ is local to the displayed $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ frontier and the common $\DyadicMeshUp$ row $M$: a consumer that reads a leaf may use only the subdivision row, the mesh row, the branch prefix that reaches it, and the corresponding containment row in $Q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proceed by induction on the branch prefix that reaches the leaf. The root case reads the root frontier at the mesh scale $M$.

At a child step, the retained branch row selects one displayed dyadic child, and the corresponding containment row in $Q$ shows that the child is still inside the parent interval. The frontier row in $F$ records that child as a visible leaf only at the mesh scale supplied by $M$.

The structural rows $H,C,P,L$ transport, replay, package, and name those same rows. They add no ambient subset of $\RealUp$ and no completed point, so the leaf read remains local to the displayed subdivision and mesh data.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree window exhaustion]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-window-exhaustion}
Let $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet. Any $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ window consumer receives only the displayed finite nested route $NW$: its available reads are the root interval, the consumed branch prefix, the matching containment rows, the visible frontier rows, the common mesh row, and the structural transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use induction on the branch ledger $B$. The base window is the root interval row $R$ together with the mesh row $M$ and the root nested-route prefix.

At each successor branch, the consumer reads one retained child decision, one containment row from $Q$, and the corresponding frontier update from $F$. The level scope of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-tree-level-scope} records exactly these finite reads.

The induction stops at the unary depth row $D$. Since the carrier has no coordinate for an infinite tree, a host subset of $\RealUp$, a Dedekind cut, a selected completed point, or ambient real completeness, the exported $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ window can only be the finite route $NW$ together with $H,C,P,L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree frontier exhaustion]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-frontier-exhaustion}
Let $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet. The frontier row $F$ exhausts exactly the leaves named by the finite branch ledger $B$: every visible leaf in $F$ is obtained by following a prefix recorded in $B$ from the root row $R$, and every terminal prefix of $B$ has its displayed $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ leaf in $F$ with the common mesh row $M$ and containment evidence in $Q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proceed by induction on the finite branch ledger $B$. In the empty case, the only terminal prefix is the root row $R$, and the frontier row $F$ is the root frontier at mesh scale $M$.

For a nonempty ledger, split off the first retained child decision. The corresponding $\DyadicBisectionUp$ child row and $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment row in $Q$ identify the successor interval, while the $\DyadicSubdivisionUp$ row in $F$ records the visible leaf created at that step.

Apply the induction hypothesis to the remaining branch ledger. The rows $H$ and $C$ transport and replay the same root, branch, frontier, mesh, containment, and nested-route data, so no extra leaf can enter $F$ and no terminal branch prefix can be omitted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree mesh refinement functoriality]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-mesh-refinement-functoriality}
For an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$, every refinement read preserves the common $\DyadicMeshUp$ row $M$ through the $\RationalIntervalRefinementUp$ containment route $Q$ and the $\NestedIntervalUp$ route $NW$. Thus a $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer sees the same mesh scale when it reads a parent-child containment row, a frontier leaf, or the final nested-window route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First read the mesh row $M$ before any refinement row. The classifier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-tree-classifier} compares $M$ componentwise, so accepted packets cannot substitute a different dyadic scale during a consumer read.

Next inspect the containment row $Q$. Each retained parent-child interval relation named by $B$ is recorded with the same mesh row $M$, and each corresponding frontier row in $F$ is displayed at that scale.

Finally read the nested route $NW$. Since $NW$ is obtained by threading the retained branch prefix through the containment rows $Q$, the componentwise transport $H$ and replay $C$ carry the same $M$ into the $\NestedIntervalUp$ route. The provenance and local naming rows $P,L$ then export the mesh-preserving refinement as the packet surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalTree branch-ledger depth bound]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-tree-branch-ledger-depth-bound}
Let $T=(R,D,B,F,M,Q,NW,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\DyadicIntervalTreeUp$ packet. Every consumer induction over the branch ledger $B$ terminates at the unary depth row $D$: each branch step consumes one displayed child decision, one containment row in $Q$, and one frontier update in $F$, and no consumer can extend the induction beyond the depth named by $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the unary depth row $D$ together with the finite branch ledger $B$. At depth zero, the branch ledger has no further retained child decision available to a consumer, so the induction stops at the root row $R$.

At a successor depth, consume one branch decision from $B$. The rows $Q$ and $F$ provide the matching containment and frontier update, while $M$ fixes the dyadic scale used by that step.

The remaining induction is bounded by the predecessor depth row. The structural rows $H,C,P,L$ preserve this bounded ledger under transport, replay, provenance, and local naming, so no $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer can manufacture an additional branch step outside $D$.
\end{proof}
