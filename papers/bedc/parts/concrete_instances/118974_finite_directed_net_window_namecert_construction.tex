\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp$}

\origin{human}

Following Moore and Smith on nets, the $\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp$ packet records the finite cofinal net window that an L10 completion route may inspect before it reads a completion-facing boundary. It sits over the directed-index carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-directedset-namecert}, refines the net rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-moore-smith-net-namecert}, feeds the Cauchy-net completion corridor of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchynetcompletion-namecert}, and presents the same finite readback style consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a displayed finite window and handoff object; it is not a subnet selector, quotient net, cofinal-choice functional, selected limit, host equality assertion, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[Finite directed net window carrier]\label{def:finite-directed-net-window-carrier}
A $\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
W=(D,M,\Omega,U,T,S,R,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DirectedSetUp$ index substrate, exposing the inspected addresses, preorder comparisons, finite request windows, and upper-window witnesses.
\item $M$ is the $\MooreSmithNetUp$ source row whose tail ledger is being inspected.
\item $\Omega$ is the finite retained window of directed addresses used by the completion request.
\item $U$ records, for every displayed finite subwindow of $\Omega$, an upper witness still visible through $D$ and the Moore--Smith tail ledger.
\item $T$ is the cofinal touch row, stating that every completion-facing tail request named by the route has a displayed successor request meeting $\Omega$ through the same directed substrate.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ observation row obtained by listing the retained finite window as an explicit schedule.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the scheduled window.
\item $A$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $D,M,\Omega,U,T,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the directed substrate, Moore--Smith source, retained window, upper witnesses, cofinal touch row, schedule, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a completion request through the finite route
$$
D,M,\Omega,U,T \longmapsto S \longmapsto R \longmapsto A,H,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp$, $\DirectedSetUp$, $\MooreSmithNetUp$, $\CauchyNetCompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports only this finite completion-facing window. It has no coordinate for an infinite cofinal family, a selected subnet, quotient-net equality, a selected limit, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite directed net window NameCert obligations]\label{thm:finite-directed-net-window-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp$ carrier $W=(D,M,\Omega,U,T,S,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following finite obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the directed substrate, Moore--Smith source, retained finite window, upper witnesses, cofinal touch row, StreamName schedule, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming row;
\item directed-window readability, requiring every retained address in $\Omega$ and every comparison used by $U$ to be read through the displayed $\DirectedSetUp$ row $D$;
\item Moore--Smith tail compatibility, requiring the retained window and its upper witnesses to be visible in the $\MooreSmithNetUp$ tail ledger $M$;
\item finite cofinal touch, requiring each completion-facing tail request to pass through the displayed touch row $T$ before any scheduled observation or readback is consumed;
\item completion readback order, requiring the route $D,M,\Omega,U,T$ to be displayed before the $\StreamNameUp$ row $S$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $A$;
\item ledger refusal for infinite cofinal families, selected subnets, quotient-net equality, selected limits, countable choice, host real equality, and ambient completeness.
\end{enumerate}
No local NameCert obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:finite-directed-net-window-carrier}. The displayed packet exposes $D,M,\Omega,U,T,S,R,A$ as analytic rows and $H,C,P,N$ as structural rows.

Directed-window readability follows from the role of $D$. A retained address in $\Omega$ is usable only as an inspected directed address, and each upper witness in $U$ is certified by the displayed comparison rows already present in the directed substrate.

Moore--Smith tail compatibility is the role of $M$. The retained finite window is not an external subnet; it is the finite part of the Moore--Smith tail ledger being handed to the completion route.

The cofinal touch row $T$ is then read before any numerical schedule. It records the finite successor request that meets the retained window for the particular completion-facing demand under inspection, without selecting a global cofinal family.

Finally the scheduled exit is ordered through $S,R,A$. Transport and replay preserve the same rows through $H$ and $C$, while $P$ and $N$ source and name the packet. Since none of the refused objects appears as a carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite directed net window completion handoff]\label{thm:finite-directed-net-window-completion-handoff}
Every $\CauchyNetCompletionUp$ consumer of an accepted $\FiniteDirectedNetWindowUp$ packet reads the finite route
$$
\DirectedSetUp,\;\MooreSmithNetUp,\;\Omega,\;U,\;T \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
Thus an L10 completion route may inspect a finite cofinal net window as displayed BEDC data before the completion boundary is consumed, while quotient nets, selected subnets, selected limits, countable choice, host equality, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness remain outside the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:finite-directed-net-window-namecert-obligations}. It first forces the consumer to read the directed substrate $D$, Moore--Smith source $M$, retained window $\Omega$, upper witnesses $U$, and cofinal touch row $T$.

The completion-facing route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchynetcompletion-namecert} can then consume the same finite directed data as a boundary request. The packet supplies a displayed window and touch row, not a selected subnet or quotient of nets.

The numerical handoff is downstream of the directed window. The finite schedule $S$ is read as a $\StreamNameUp$ row, the regular rational readback $R$ follows that schedule, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $A$ is inspected only afterward.

Componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming remain $H,C,P,N$. Since the carrier has no coordinate for choice, selected limits, quotient equality, host equality, or ambient completeness, no such object can bypass the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}
