Following Bishop--Bridges signed-digit real-name practice, the $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$ packet records a finite stream interface in which each observed dyadic digit is signed, locally normalized, and routed toward the regular rational and Real-name layers without quotienting streams. It sits beside the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a signed-digit real-name carrier, not a host real, selected limit, quotient stream class, trichotomy oracle, or ambient completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic signed-digit stream carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-signed-digit-stream-carrier}
A $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(W,A,B,M,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $W$ is the signed dyadic digit stream window row, listing the inspected positions and their digits in $\{-1,0,1\}$.
\item $A$ is the digit-admissibility row, checking that every displayed digit lies in the signed dyadic alphabet and is attached to the requested dyadic place.
\item $B$ is the carry and normalization budget row, recording the finite carry radius and local overlap bounds used to compare neighbouring windows.
\item $M$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row that schedules the same finite observation as a stream-name request.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row, reading the normalized signed-window prefix as a regular rational approximation.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row, available only after $W,A,B,M,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $W,A,B,M,Q,E$ and the local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from signed dyadic windows through normalization, stream-name scheduling, regular rational readback, and the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $W,A,B,M,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected representatives, host real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic signed-digit stream NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-signed-digit-stream-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$ carrier $S=(W,A,B,M,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp}$ surface consists exactly of signed-window admission, digit-admissibility checking, carry and normalization budget exposure, $\StreamNameUp$ window scheduling, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, terminal $\RealUp$ seal routing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-signed-digit-stream-carrier}. The signed-window row $W$, admissibility row $A$, carry budget row $B$, stream-name row $M$, regular readback row $Q$, and Real seal row $E$ are the only analytic coordinates of an accepted packet.

The normalization budget is finite. It records how signed digits may carry across neighbouring displayed places before the window is read as a regular rational approximation, so the handoff cannot bypass $A$ or $B$.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name exactly the same finite route. Since the carrier contains no quotient stream, selected representative, host real equality, trichotomy, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness coordinate, none of those endpoints belongs to the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic signed-digit stream RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:dyadic-signed-digit-stream-regseqrat-handoff}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$ packet must first expose the signed dyadic window $W$, digit-admissibility row $A$, carry and normalization budget row $B$, and $\StreamNameUp$ window row $M$. The resulting handoff reaches $\RegSeqRatUp$ through $Q$ and reaches $\RealUp$ through $E$ only as the finite route
$$
W,A,B,M \longmapsto Q \longmapsto E,H,C,P,N .
$$
It is not a quotient-stream construction, selected host limit, host real equality, trichotomy principle, countable-choice route, or ambient Real completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:dyadic-signed-digit-stream-namecert-obligations}. They force every consumer-facing read to expose the signed-window, admissibility, normalization-budget, stream-window, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The regular rational handoff is downstream of normalization. A displayed signed digit may contribute to neighbouring dyadic places only through the finite budget $B$, so the readback row $Q$ is read from $W,A,B,M$ together, not from a raw stream of host digits.

Finally the Real seal row $E$ is consumed after $Q$. Transport and replay preserve the same route through $H$ and $C$, with $P$ and $N$ recording provenance and local naming. The excluded endpoints require coordinates absent from the packet and cannot be exported by this handoff.
\end{proof}
