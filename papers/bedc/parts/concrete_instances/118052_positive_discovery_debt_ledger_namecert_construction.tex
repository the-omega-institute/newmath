The packet names the debt-accounting surface used by a positive discovery claim; it does not by itself certify classifier novelty, positive net information, global interpretability quality, hidden semantic truth, or a prose-only discovery verdict.

\begin{definition}[Positive discovery debt ledger carrier]
\label{def:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-carrier}
A $\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(E_0,E_1,J,W,D_c,D_l,D_s,D_v,D_i,D_g,Q,U,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E_0$ is the source explanation-state row before the proposed discovery transition.
\item $E_1$ is the target explanation-state row after the proposed transition.
\item $J$ is the classifier-novelty witness row, naming the common source and changed classifier judgment.
\item $W$ is the public cost-protocol row for benefit weights, score weights, debt weights, source scope, target scope, and not-claimed boundary.
\item $D_c,D_l,D_s,D_v,D_i,D_g$ are the critical-debt rows for certificate debt, ledger debt, semantic debt, verification debt, intervention debt, and generalization debt.
\item $Q$ is the net-information readback row inherited from the explanation-complexity information surface under the same protocol row $W$.
\item $U$ is the downstream-use ledger row recording which classifier, cost, and debt rows are actually consumed by later reports.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for explanation states, novelty witness, cost protocol, critical-debt rows, net-information readback, downstream-use ledger, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through classifier novelty, cost-protocol inspection, critical-debt accounting, net-information readback, downstream-use inspection, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp$, $\PositiveInterpretabilityClassifierUp$, $\ClassifierNoveltyWitnessUp$, $\DiscoveryPipelineCalculusUp$, $\ExplanationComplexityInformationUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for hidden benefit weights, uncounted critical debt, classifier novelty without $J$, positive net information without $Q$, downstream use without $U$, or a positive-discovery verdict without the displayed protocol and debt rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive discovery debt ledger NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp$ carrier $L=(E_0,E_1,J,W,D_c,D_l,D_s,D_v,D_i,D_g,Q,U,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp}$ surface consists exactly of before-state admission, after-state admission, classifier-novelty witness exposure, public cost-protocol exposure, six critical-debt rows, net-information readback, downstream-use ledger exposure, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-carrier}. The before and after states are $E_0,E_1$, the classifier-shift evidence is $J$, and the public accounting convention is $W$.

The debt surface is the six-row family $D_c,D_l,D_s,D_v,D_i,D_g$. These rows separate certificate, ledger, semantic, verification, intervention, and generalization debt before any positive discovery claim may consume the ledger.

The net-information and downstream-use rows are $Q,U$. They ensure that the cost protocol and debt rows are read by the same route that later reports consume.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no other coordinate exists, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cost protocol critical-debt route]
\label{thm:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-cost-protocol-route}
Every accepted $\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp$ read that supports a positive discovery classification must expose the public cost-protocol row $W$ and all six critical-debt rows $D_c,D_l,D_s,D_v,D_i,D_g$ before it may consume the net-information row $Q$ or the downstream-use ledger $U$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-namecert-obligations}. They place $W$ and the six debt rows in the admitted surface before replay can certify the packet.

Read $Q$ only after that accounting surface is present. The row $Q$ compares benefit against score and debt under the same protocol, so it cannot be detached from $W$ or from the critical-debt rows.

Read $U$ last. The downstream-use ledger records which displayed rows are consumed by later reports; it cannot witness use for a hidden protocol or an omitted debt row.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Debt ledger separation from classifier verdict]
\label{thm:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-verdict-separation}
An accepted $\PositiveDiscoveryDebtLedgerUp$ packet supplies debt-accounting evidence for the positive-discovery route, but it does not itself classify the transition as $\PositiveInterpretabilityClassifierUp$. A downstream positive classifier must still read the classifier-novelty witness, positive net-information row, public cost protocol, debt rows, and not-claimed boundary through its own carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \autoref{thm:positive-discovery-debt-ledger-cost-protocol-route}. The ledger packet exposes the cost protocol, critical debts, net-information readback, and downstream-use rows as an auditable accounting surface.

The positive interpretability classifier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-positive-interpretability-classifier-namecert} has its own carrier and acceptance route. The debt ledger here names the accounting rows that route may consume; it is not a replacement for the classifier's verdict row.

Thus the packet can support a certified-discovery route by making cost and debt public, while still refusing any positive-discovery classification that skips the classifier surface.
\end{proof}
