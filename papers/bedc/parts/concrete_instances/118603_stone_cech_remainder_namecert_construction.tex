\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\StoneCechRemainderUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-stone-cech-remainder-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Gillman--Jerison and Engelking on Stone--Cech compactification remainders, the $\StoneCechRemainderUp$ packet records the compactification-boundary object read from a displayed $\StoneCechCompactificationUp$ source without forming a host set difference or quotient compactification equality. It sits after the Stone--Cech compactification carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stone-cech-compactification-namecert}, the topology rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the compact rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}, and the compact-metric witness discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. The packet names only the local boundary ledger for the complement-facing read; it does not assert an ambient corona space, select ultrafilters, identify quotient points, or import standard open-cover compactness.

\begin{definition}[Stone--Cech remainder carrier]
\label{def:stone-cech-remainder-carrier}
A $\StoneCechRemainderUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(B,D,C,Q,T,K,H,L,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the $\StoneCechCompactificationUp$ source row, including the bounded-continuous-probe windows and compact-target seal rows already named in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stone-cech-compactification-namecert}.
\item $D$ is the dense embedding and source-object row, recording the displayed source carrier, its generated topology, and the embedding route into the compactification packet.
\item $C$ is the compactification boundary row, naming the boundary-facing read attached to $B$ and $D$ without identifying it with a host complement.
\item $Q$ is the local complement and remainder ledger, recording which finite probes see the source row and which finite probes are routed to the remainder-facing boundary row.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ row for the generated neighbourhood and closure tests used by the boundary ledger.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactUp$ and $\CompactMetricUp$ compact-target row consumed by the compactification source and boundary readback.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compactification source, dense embedding, boundary row, complement ledger, topology row, compact row, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $L$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the dense source route through the Stone--Cech compactification row to the boundary and remainder ledger.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\StoneCechRemainderUp$, $\StoneCechCompactificationUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\StoneCechRemainderUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for host set difference, quotient compactification equality, an ultrafilter selector, ambient corona-space equality, selected boundary points, or standard open-cover compactness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stone--Cech remainder NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:stone-cech-remainder-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\StoneCechRemainderUp$ carrier $R=(B,D,C,Q,T,K,H,L,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\StoneCechRemainderUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, $\StoneCechCompactificationUp$ source exposure through $B$, dense embedding and source-object exposure through $D$, compactification boundary exposure through $C$, local complement ledger exposure through $Q$, $\TopologyUp$ and $\CompactUp$ row exposure through $T,K$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $L$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:stone-cech-remainder-carrier}. The only analytic rows are the Stone--Cech compactification source, dense embedding, boundary row, complement ledger, topology row, and compact row.

The source row $B$ is read through the existing compactification certificate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stone-cech-compactification-namecert}. The embedding row $D$ and boundary row $C$ then display the compactification-boundary route, while $Q$ records the finite local complement ledger used by remainder-facing probes.

Finally inspect $H,L,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the same displayed packet. Since none of them stores host set difference, quotient compactification equality, ultrafilter choice, ambient corona equality, selected boundary points, or standard open-cover compactness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stone--Cech remainder boundary non-escape]
\label{thm:stone-cech-remainder-boundary-nonescape}
Every boundary-facing consumer of an accepted $\StoneCechRemainderUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
B,D,T,K \longmapsto C,Q \longmapsto H,L,P,N .
$$
Thus the remainder boundary is exposed only through the displayed Stone--Cech compactification source, dense embedding, topology and compact rows, local complement ledger, componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming. It exports no host set difference, quotient compactification equality, ultrafilter selector, ambient corona-space equality, selected boundary point, or standard open-cover compactness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier of \autoref{def:stone-cech-remainder-carrier}. A boundary-facing read has access to $C$ and $Q$ only as rows of the displayed tuple, so it cannot begin from a host complement or an external corona-space object.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:stone-cech-remainder-namecert-obligations}. They require the compactification source row $B$, dense embedding row $D$, topology row $T$, and compact row $K$ to be exposed before the boundary row and complement ledger are public.

The structural rows $H,L,P,N$ preserve that route. Transport is componentwise, replay follows the displayed continuation, provenance cites the same source packets, and local naming adds no extra coordinate. Therefore every boundary read remains inside the finite route $B,D,T,K \longmapsto C,Q \longmapsto H,L,P,N$.
\end{proof}
