Following Bishop and Bridges, a regular Cauchy object is read through explicit moduli and finite tails before any completion seal is consumed. The $\RegularCauchySieveUp$ packet records that reading as a finite directed-tail sieve for the L10 Real and Completion route, sitting between the dyadic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite request windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational sequence readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[Regular Cauchy sieve carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-sieve-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchySieveUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(D,W,Q,L,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row naming the finite dyadic scale at which the tail is inspected.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row listing the requests whose indices lie beyond the displayed tail bound.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular-sequence readback row reached from the same dyadic tolerance and stream window.
\item $L$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row, readable only after $D,W,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $T$ is the directed finite-tail sieve row: every accepted refinement replaces $D,W,Q,L$ by a later finite window over the same route and records the inclusion of the older window into the later one.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the dyadic tolerance, window, regular-sequence readback, Real seal, sieve refinement, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from dyadic tolerance through StreamName window, RegSeqRat readback, directed-tail refinement, Real seal, transport, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchySieveUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these nine rows. It has no coordinate for a selected infinite subsequence, quotient-stream equality, host limit, ambient completeness principle, or hidden modulus choice outside the directed finite-tail sieve.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy sieve NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-sieve-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchySieveUp$ carrier
$$
S=(D,W,Q,L,T,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchySieveUp}$ surface consists exactly of dyadic tolerance exposure through $D$, finite StreamName window exposure through $W$, RegSeqRat readback through $Q$, RealUp seal exposure through $L$, directed finite-tail refinement through $T$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-sieve-carrier}. The rows $D,W,Q,L$ expose the L10 route in dependency order: dyadic tolerance, finite request window, regular rational readback, and Real seal.

Read the sieve row $T$ next. It is directed because any two displayed finite tails may be refined by a later finite window over the same dyadic, StreamName, RegSeqRat, and RealUp route; the packet records that later window as another finite row rather than selecting an infinite subsequence.

The transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows preserve only this displayed route. Since no coordinate stores a host limit, quotient stream, ambient completeness rule, or hidden modulus, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}
