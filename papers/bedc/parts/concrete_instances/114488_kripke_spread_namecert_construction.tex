\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\KripkeSpreadUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-kripke-spread-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kripke on intuitionistic models and Brouwer on spreads, the $\KripkeSpreadUp$ packet records finite spread inspection along the L10 countable-name route. It sits beside the binary prefix surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cantorspace-namecert}, the finite-bar discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fantheorem-namecert}, the finite branching carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finitely-branching-tree-namecert}, and the countable-name consumers of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}. The packet exposes only finite nodes, finite successor ledgers, prefix persistence, and readback windows; it is not weak Koenig path selection, countable choice, quotient stream equality, or completed Real data.

\begin{definition}[Kripke spread carrier]
\label{def:kripke-spread-carrier}
A $\KripkeSpreadUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(W,N,B,A,S,Q,O,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $W$ is the finite world-prefix ledger, listing the inspected nodes and their prefix order.
\item $N$ is the decidable node-admission row, recording which displayed prefixes belong to the spread.
\item $B$ is the finite branching row, recording the displayed successor family for each inspected node.
\item $A$ is the persistence row, requiring every displayed successor to retain the admitted prefix data of its predecessors.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window schedule through which countable-name reads inspect the spread.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row available only after the finite StreamName windows have been displayed.
\item $O$ is the refusal row excluding weak Koenig path selection, countable choice, quotient stream equality, unbounded branch search, and completed $\RealUp$ closure.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for worlds, node admission, finite branching, persistence, StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, refusal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite spread-inspection request through $W,N,B,A,S,Q,O,H,P,L$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KripkeSpreadUp$, $\CantorSpaceUp$, $\FanTheoremUp$, $\FinitelyBranchingTreeUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KripkeSpreadUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate asserts exactly that these finite rows form one spread-inspection packet. It has no coordinate for a selected infinite branch, a total path functional, countable choice, quotient stream equality, ambient compactness, or completed real closure.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kripke spread NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:kripke-spread-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\KripkeSpreadUp$ carrier $K=(W,N,B,A,S,Q,O,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\KripkeSpreadUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite world-prefix ledger, node-admission row, finite branching row, persistence row, StreamName window schedule, RegSeqRat readback row, refusal row, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every L10 countable-name consumer must read the finite route
$$
W,N,B,A,S,Q,O,H,C,P,L
$$
before extracting StreamName or RegSeqRat data, and no obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:kripke-spread-carrier}. The displayed finite coordinates are precisely $W,N,B,A,S,Q,O,H,C,P,L$.

The world, admission, branching, and persistence rows give the spread-inspection content. They record inspected prefixes, admitted nodes, displayed successors, and predecessor retention without creating an infinite path.

The StreamName and RegSeqRat rows are downstream. A countable-name consumer first opens the finite window schedule $S$, then reads the regular-rational row $Q$ attached to that window.

Finally $O,H,C,P,L$ keep the refusal boundary, transport, replay, provenance, and local name attached to the same packet. Since the carrier has no further coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kripke spread prefix non-escape]
\label{thm:kripke-spread-prefix-nonescape}
Every accepted $\KripkeSpreadUp$ consumer that inspects StreamName or RegSeqRat data must remain inside the finite route
$$
W,N,B,A,S,Q,O,H,C,P,L .
$$
The consumer may reuse finite Cantor-space prefix observations, finite FanTheorem bar data, finite branching-tree rows, StreamName windows, and RegSeqRat readback data, but it receives no weak Koenig path selector, no countable-choice functional, no quotient stream equality, no unbounded branch search, and no completed $\RealUp$ closure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a StreamName or RegSeqRat read of an accepted carrier. The carrier exposes countable-name data only through the finite window row $S$ and the readback row $Q$.

The prefix rows $W,N,B,A$ remain visible before that read. They supply admitted nodes and displayed successors as finite inspection data, not as a completed branch through the spread.

The refusal row $O$ is part of the same packet. It blocks any reinterpretation of the finite route as weak Koenig path selection, countable choice, quotient stream equality, unbounded branch search, or completed real closure.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,L$ preserve that boundary. Thus every consumer stays inside the finite prefix route and none of the excluded principles is exported.
\end{proof}
