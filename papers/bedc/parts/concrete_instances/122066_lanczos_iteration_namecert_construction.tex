\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LanczosIterationUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-lanczos-iteration-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Lanczos, the $\LanczosIterationUp$ packet records the finite three-term recurrence data used to build a Krylov basis and a tridiagonal projection from one symmetric matrix row, one inner-product row, and one seed-vector row. It sits after the Krylov carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-krylov-subspace-namecert}, the matrix carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, the inner-product carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-innerproduct-namecert}, and the spectral theorem surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-spectraltheorem-namecert}. The packet names only the finite iteration certificate; it does not assert floating-point stability, Ritz-value convergence, host diagonalization, or a global equality between the tridiagonal row and the original matrix.

\begin{definition}[LanczosIteration carrier]
\label{def:lanczos-iteration-carrier}
A $\LanczosIterationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(K,M,I,q_0,m,Q,A_{\mathrm{diag}},B_{\mathrm{sub}},T,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\KrylovSubspaceUp$ row generated by one matrix action and one displayed seed vector.
\item $M$ is the $\MatrixUp$ row for the symmetric action inspected by the iteration.
\item $I$ is the $\InnerProductUp$ row used to normalize vectors and test orthogonality inside the displayed Krylov span.
\item $q_0$ is the initial normalized seed row, read as the first admitted vector of the Krylov packet.
\item $m$ is the finite step bound.
\item $Q=(q_0,\ldots,q_m)$ is the displayed vector ledger.
\item $A_{\mathrm{diag}}=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_m)$ is the diagonal coefficient ledger.
\item $B_{\mathrm{sub}}=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)$ is the subdiagonal coefficient ledger.
\item $T$ is the tridiagonal matrix row with diagonal $A_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and symmetric subdiagonal $B_{\mathrm{sub}}$.
\item $R$ is the finite residual ledger recording that each next vector is generated from the previous two vectors and the same matrix action before normalization.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for all displayed rows.
\item $C$ records $\Cont$ replay from the seed request through the recurrence, coefficient, residual, and tridiagonal ledgers.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LanczosIterationUp$, $\KrylovSubspaceUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\InnerProductUp$, $\SpectralTheoremUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LanczosIterationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an infinite Krylov limit, a numerical roundoff model, an eigenvalue convergence theorem, a host orthogonal projector, or a hidden spectral decomposition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[LanczosIteration NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lanczos-iteration-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LanczosIterationUp$ carrier
$$
L=(K,M,I,q_0,m,Q,A_{\mathrm{diag}},B_{\mathrm{sub}},T,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\LanczosIterationUp}$ surface is exhausted by six obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the Krylov row $K$, matrix row $M$, inner-product row $I$, seed row $q_0$, step bound $m$, vector ledger $Q$, coefficient ledgers $A_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{sub}}$, tridiagonal row $T$, residual row $R$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$;
\item same-action recurrence, requiring every residual entry of $R$ to use the same matrix row $M$ and the previous two displayed vector rows of $Q$;
\item inner-product normalization, requiring each admitted vector and coefficient read to pass through the same $\InnerProductUp$ row $I$;
\item tridiagonal assembly, requiring $T$ to be reconstructed only from $A_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{sub}}$;
\item Krylov compatibility, requiring every vector in $Q$ and every residual in $R$ to remain a displayed finite read of the Krylov packet $K$;
\item ledger exhaustion, requiring any spectral, quadrature, conjugate-gradient, or Ritz-value consumer to cite only the displayed rows before using the packet.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation for $\LanczosIterationUp$ is stored outside those six rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:lanczos-iteration-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes the Krylov, matrix, inner-product, seed, step, vector, coefficient, tridiagonal, residual, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

The recurrence obligation is read from $R$. Each residual row records a finite application of the same matrix row to the current vector, subtracts the two displayed coefficient contributions, and admits the next vector only through the same inner-product normalization surface.

The tridiagonal matrix is not an independent host object. It is assembled from $A_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{sub}}$, so any read of $T$ factors through the two finite coefficient ledgers.

Finally $K,H,C,P,N$ provide the boundary checks. The Krylov row supplies finite iterate compatibility, the transport and replay rows preserve the displayed ledgers, and the provenance row names the spectral theorem only as downstream background rather than as a convergence claim. Therefore the six listed obligations exhaust the certificate surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LanczosIteration Krylov handoff]
\label{thm:lanczos-iteration-krylov-handoff}
Every accepted $\LanczosIterationUp$ carrier has a canonical handoff to the $\KrylovSubspaceUp$ packet obtained by forgetting the coefficient, tridiagonal, and residual rows and retaining the rows
$$
(K,H,C,P,N).
$$
This handoff preserves the finite seed, matrix-action, span, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming reads exposed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-krylov-subspace-namecert}; it does not add an Arnoldi basis, a spectral approximation theorem, or a host diagonalization claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read $K$ from the carrier of \autoref{def:lanczos-iteration-carrier}. By construction $K$ is already the finite Krylov packet generated by the same seed and matrix action used by the recurrence.

Forgetting $A_{\mathrm{diag}}$, $B_{\mathrm{sub}}$, $T$, and $R$ removes the Lanczos-specific coefficient and tridiagonal data. The rows $H$ and $C$ still transport and replay the remaining Krylov reads componentwise, while $P$ and $N$ keep the provenance and local naming boundary visible.

The handoff therefore exposes exactly the finite Krylov certificate needed by downstream consumers and no additional spectral or host-level assertion.
\end{proof}
