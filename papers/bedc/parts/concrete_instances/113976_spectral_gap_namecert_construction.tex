\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\SpectralGapUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-spectral-gap-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kato, the $\SpectralGapUp$ packet records spectral separation as a finite witnessed gap for bounded-operator and real-norm rows. It generalizes the matrix-level certificate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-dimensional-spectral-gap-namecert} while staying anchored in the operator surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedlinearoperator-namecert}, the norm ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-norm-namecert}, and the endpoint seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only displayed separation data; it does not assert a spectral theorem, resolvent calculus, compactness of the spectrum, or a hidden eigenvector selection principle.

\begin{definition}[SpectralGap carrier]
\label{def:spectral-gap-carrier}
A $\SpectralGapUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(T,B,N,R,S,\Delta,W,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the source $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$ row naming the bounded operator whose spectrum-facing read is under inspection.
\item $B$ is the boundedness ledger for $T$, including the operator-bound row and the finite replay path by which downstream consumers may inspect it.
\item $N$ is the $\NormUp$ row used to read operator and vector bounds through the displayed norm carrier.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ row naming the real endpoint surface on which separation bounds and positive-radius witnesses are sealed.
\item $S$ is the finite spectral-side row listing the inspected spectral cluster, complementary spectral window, and allowed localization probes.
\item $\Delta$ is the positive gap row: a displayed real-norm lower bound separating $S$ from its complement by a finite witness.
\item $W$ is the finite witness row recording the endpoint comparisons, norm comparisons, and probe windows that certify the gap row $\Delta$.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for bounded-operator, norm, real, spectral-side, gap, witness, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from the bounded-operator row through norm and real comparisons to the gap witness.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\SpectralGapUp$, $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$, $\NormUp$, $\RealUp$, $\FiniteDimensionalSpectralGapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SpectralGapUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,B,N,R,S,\Delta,W,H,C,P,L$. It has no coordinate for a global spectrum object, an unbounded operator, a chosen eigenbasis, a host Hilbert-space equality, a quotient of operators, or ambient classical spectral decomposition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[SpectralGap NameCert obligation surface]
\label{thm:spectral-gap-namecert-obligation-surface}
For an accepted $\SpectralGapUp$ carrier $G=(T,B,N,R,S,\Delta,W,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\SpectralGapUp}$ surface consists exactly of five rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed bounded-operator, norm, real, spectral-side, gap, witness, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item bounded-operator dependency through $T,B$, requiring every operator read to pass through $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$ before any gap witness is consumed;
\item norm and real separation through $N,R,\Delta,W$, requiring the positive gap to be read as a finite real-norm inequality;
\item finite-dimensional compatibility through the sibling $\FiniteDimensionalSpectralGapUp$ route whenever the inspected packet specializes to a displayed finite-dimensional window;
\item no-escape from global spectral theorems, hidden eigenvector selection, host operator quotients, unbounded-operator data, and ambient classical decomposition.
\end{enumerate}
Thus every downstream consumer of the gap must read
$$
T,B,N,R,S,\Delta,W,H,C,P,L
$$
as one finite $\BHist$ packet before exporting the local certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The bounded-operator rows $T,B$ are the only source of operator data, while the norm and real rows $N,R$ are the only source of numerical comparison data.

The positive separation is then read from $\Delta$ and $W$. These rows give a finite lower-bound witness and the endpoint, norm, and probe comparisons that certify it; they do not call on a global spectrum object.

For finite-dimensional windows, the packet factors through the sibling surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-dimensional-spectral-gap-namecert}. That route supplies the specialized matrix-level gap read, while the present carrier keeps the general bounded-operator and norm rows visible.

Finally $H,C,P,L$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no excluded host object appears among the coordinates, the five displayed rows exhaust the local $\NameCert_{\SpectralGapUp}$ obligation surface.
\end{proof}
