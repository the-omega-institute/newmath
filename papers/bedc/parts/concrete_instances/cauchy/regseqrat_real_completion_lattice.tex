\begin{theorem}[Cauchy RegSeqRat--Real completion lattice]
\label{thm:cauchy-regseqrat-real-completion-lattice}
Every completion-facing $\CauchyUp$ read factors through the displayed sibling lattice
$$
\StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \MetricCompletionUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
before any completion consumer may read a limit row. The StreamName windows are the observation boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}; the regular rational tails are the readback boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}; the metric-completion request is the comparison boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}; and the terminal seal is the Real boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The route exports no quotient stream, selected representative, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the hub through its displayed child routes. The finite-window dependency of \autoref{thm:cauchy-displayed-regseqrat-route} and the RegSeqRat--StreamName dependency lattice already force every Real-facing Cauchy read to expose $\StreamNameUp$ windows before it consumes regular rational readback rows.

Next apply the regular-source completion route of \autoref{thm:cauchy-regseqrat-metric-completion-dependency-route}. The $\MetricCompletionUp$ boundary receives the same displayed $\RegSeqRatUp$ tail budget, so a completion consumer cannot jump directly from a Cauchy name to a limit row.

Finally use the metric-completion forward lattice. It places the terminal $\RealUp$ seal downstream of the metric-completion comparison, with no quotient stream, selected representative, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host equality, or ambient completeness coordinate. Thus the public completion read is exactly the four-surface lattice displayed in the statement.
\end{proof}
