\begin{theorem}[Cauchy top-level completion lattice route]
\label{thm:cauchy-top-level-completion-lattice-route}
Every $\CauchyUp$ completion-facing or $\RealUp$-facing consumer of the top-level hub factors through finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance rows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ rows before it can use any completion boundary. The route is read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the Bishop regular completion surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-regular-cauchy-completion-namecert}; it exports no quotient stream, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a completion-facing read of the hub. The hub is only an ordered list of child routes, so the consumer first meets the finite StreamName window rows and the DyadicRatCore tolerance rows named by the displayed siblings, not a hidden completion object.

The regular rational readback is then routed through $\RegSeqRatUp$ before the terminal $\RealUp$ seal is visible. Structural replay through $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ preserves the same ordered rows rather than adding a quotient stream or selected endpoint.

Finally the Bishop regular completion surface may consume the already displayed route. Since every completion-facing coordinate has passed through the sibling lattice, no remaining hub coordinate can supply choice, host equality, or an ambient completeness theorem.
\end{proof}
