\begin{theorem}[Cauchy forward Real-completion route]
\label{thm:cauchy-forward-real-completion-route}
Every $\CauchyUp$ consumer that reaches a Real-completion reading must pass through the finite $\StreamNameUp$ route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular-rational completion rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal $\RealUp$ completion and seal rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The route may use the Cauchy kernel predicate only as a displayed finite observation condition; it cannot treat $\CauchyUp$ as an isolated kernel predicate that already supplies a selected limit, quotient stream equality, host real equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a completion-facing consumer of the $\CauchyUp$ hub. The hub exposes finite sequence and stream observation rows before any completion packet can read the predicate.

Follow the forward route through the named siblings. The consumer first reads $\StreamNameUp$ windows, then the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular-rational completion handoff, and finally the $\RealUp$ completion and seal rows.

The structural rows preserve only that displayed route. Since no coordinate in the hub supplies selected limits, quotient stream equality, host real equality, or ambient completeness, the consumer cannot bypass $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ by reading Cauchy as an isolated kernel predicate.
\end{proof}
