\begin{theorem}[Cauchy top-level sibling route]
\label{thm:cauchy-top-level-sibling-route}
The top-level $\CauchyUp$ hub routes ordinary Cauchy evidence, $\CauchySequenceSpaceUp$ sequence-surface data, completion algebra, and tail-dominance readback through its sibling chapters before any Real or Completion consumer reads a terminal seal. The route consumes the finite observation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the Bishop regular completion consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-regular-cauchy-completion-namecert}. It thereby keeps Cauchy, sequence-space, completion-algebra, and tail-dominance surfaces on sibling NameCert routes rather than on a hidden hub coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the hub as an ordered sibling route. Its ordinary Cauchy and tail-dominance files supply local Cauchy evidence, while the displayed RegSeqRat and metric-completion dependency files point outward to the Real and Completion lattice.

The finite-window side is supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}. Those siblings provide observation windows and tolerance rows before a regular rational or real-seal consumer can inspect the Cauchy surface.

The completion-facing side then passes through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-regular-cauchy-completion-namecert}. Thus the Cauchy hub exports only a horizontal dependency route among displayed sibling chapters, with no quotient stream, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{proof}
