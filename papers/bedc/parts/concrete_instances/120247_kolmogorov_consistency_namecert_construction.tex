\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\KolmogorovConsistencyUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-kolmogorov-consistency-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kolmogorov, the $\KolmogorovConsistencyUp$ packet records finite
index-family probability rows together with their marginal restriction ledgers.
It sits between the finite-window surface of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kolmogorov-extension-finite-window-namecert},
the conditional-law interface of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-conditional-distribution-namecert}, the
located tightness route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-tightness-namecert},
and the tight-probability handoff of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-prokhorov-tight-probability-namecert}. The packet
names finite consistency data only; it does not assert an infinite extension,
choose a projective limit, or identify host measure-space quotients.

\begin{definition}[KolmogorovConsistency carrier]
\label{def:kolmogorov-consistency-carrier}
A $\KolmogorovConsistencyUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(I,F,M,\rho,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the finite index-family row;
\item $F$ is the finite family of probability rows over the displayed index
windows;
\item $M$ records the marginal maps from a larger displayed window to a smaller
displayed window;
\item $\rho$ is the restriction ledger comparing each larger-window probability
row with the smaller-window row named by $F$;
\item $L$ is the finite consistency ledger collecting all displayed
restriction checks;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $I,F,M,\rho,L,C,P,N$;
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay of finite-window restriction
reads;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KolmogorovConsistencyUp$,
$\KolmogorovExtensionFiniteWindowUp$, $\RegularConditionalDistributionUp$,
$\RealTightnessUp$, $\ProkhorovTightProbabilityUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$,
$\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KolmogorovConsistencyUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for an infinite product measure, a selected
projective-limit object, quotient event equality, or a host measure-space
completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[KolmogorovConsistency obligation surface]
\label{thm:kolmogorov-consistency-obligation-surface}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:kolmogorov-consistency-carrier}, the local
$\NameCert_{\KolmogorovConsistencyUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier
admission for $K=(I,F,M,\rho,L,H,C,P,N)$, finite probability-row exposure through
$F$, marginal-map exposure through $M$, restriction-ledger exposure through
$\rho$, consistency-ledger exposure through $L$, componentwise transport through
$H$, replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming
through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows. The finite probability family is visible only as the
indexed row $F$, and every comparison between windows is visible only through
$M,\rho,L$.

The structural rows then bind the same packet. The row $H$ transports the
finite family and all restriction ledgers componentwise, while $C$ replays only
the displayed finite-window restriction reads. The package and naming rows
$P,N$ expose the packet as a Kolmogorov consistency surface without adding any
host product-measure object.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[KolmogorovConsistency marginal ledger]
\label{thm:kolmogorov-consistency-marginal-ledger}
Every accepted $\KolmogorovConsistencyUp$ marginal read from a displayed larger
window to a displayed smaller window factors through the same rows
$$
I,F,M,\rho,L,H,C,P,N.
$$
The read first exposes the two finite windows in $I$, then the probability rows
in $F$, then the marginal map in $M$, and then the restriction comparison in
$\rho$ as recorded by $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the index-family row $I$ and the probability-family row $F$. A
marginal read has no accepted source until both finite windows and their
probability rows have been displayed.

Apply the marginal-map and ledger rows. The row $M$ names the finite restriction
map, $\rho$ names the comparison with the smaller-window row, and $L$ records
that this comparison belongs to the displayed consistency ledger.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$ to transport, replay, package, and name the same finite
route. None of these structural rows introduces an infinite extension, quotient
event equality, or selected projective-limit witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[KolmogorovConsistency public certificate]
\label{thm:kolmogorov-consistency-public-certificate}
The public $\KolmogorovConsistencyUp$ certificate is exactly the finite route
through the index-family row, probability-family rows, marginal-map rows,
restriction ledger, consistency ledger, componentwise transport, displayed
continuation replay, package provenance, and local NameCert. Downstream
probability consumers receive this finite certificate before any request for
the stronger surfaces named by
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kolmogorov-extension-finite-window-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-conditional-distribution-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-tightness-namecert}, or
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-prokhorov-tight-probability-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the obligation surface to identify the complete public row set. It exposes
only $I,F,M,\rho,L,H,C,P,N$ and therefore supplies a finite consistency
certificate rather than an ambient measure-space construction.

Use the marginal-ledger theorem for every smaller-window read. It forces each
consumer to pass through the displayed finite windows, probability rows,
marginal map, and restriction comparison before any downstream probability
surface can inspect the packet.

The sibling references are then read as consumers, not as extra coordinates of
the certificate. They may consume the finite consistency route, but the packet
itself does not claim full infinite extension, choice of projective limits, or
host measure-space quotient equality.
\end{proof}
