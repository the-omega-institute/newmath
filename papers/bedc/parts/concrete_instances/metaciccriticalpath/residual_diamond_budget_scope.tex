\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC critical-path residual diamond budget scope]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-diamond-budget-scope}
Let
$$
K=(S,N,O,U,D,H,C,P,L)
$$
be an accepted $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ packet. A candidate-mediated residual diamond read may enter the public critical-path route only after the packet exposes the candidate schedule, residual substitution budget, local join ledger, confluence checker factorization, and L10 source rows on the same route through $S,N,O,U,D,H,C,P,L$. The read is therefore a finite budget scope for the displayed packet; it is not a global strong-normalization theorem, global confluence theorem, beta-conversion decision procedure, quotient term equality, selected limit, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle, or kernel consistency claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the residual-substitution diamond budget of \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-substitution-diamond-budget}. It places residual substitution, candidate-mediated strong normalization, residual-budget socket data, and L10 source rows on one accepted packet route before a diamond or decidability consumer may read the handoff.

Next use the candidate residual budget interface, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-residual-budget-interface}. This forces the candidate rows and the four L10 source faces $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ to remain visible before a critical-path endpoint is read.

Apply the residual-diamond local-confluence frontier together with the candidate-SN confluence checker factorization, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-diamond-local-confluence-frontier} and \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-sn-confluence-checker-factorization}. These theorems supply the local join ledger and checker factorization while keeping the checker inside the same finite packet route.

The displayed rows exhaust the residual diamond budget scope. Since no row carries global SN, global confluence, beta-decidability, quotient equality, selected limits, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or full consistency, the public MetaCICCriticalPath route can consume only the shown finite budget.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Candidate-mediated residual diamond L10 handoff]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-residual-diamond-l10-handoff}
Let
$$
K=(S,N,O,U,D,H,C,P,L)
$$
be an accepted $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ packet. Any accepted route that consumes the residual diamond budget and the candidate-SN confluence checker must expose the four L10 source faces $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ before exporting local confluence or decidability consumers. The handoff exports no global strong-normalization theorem, global confluence theorem, beta-decidability procedure, quotient term equality, selected limit, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle, or full consistency claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-diamond-budget-scope}. It already places the residual substitution budget, candidate schedule, local join ledger, confluence checker factorization, and L10 source rows on one accepted packet route.

Use the candidate residual budget interface, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-residual-budget-interface}. This keeps the candidate-mediated rows and the four L10 faces visible before a critical-path endpoint is read.

Now apply the checker factorization, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-sn-confluence-checker-factorization}, together with the residual-diamond local-confluence frontier, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-diamond-local-confluence-frontier}. These results route local confluence and decidability consumers through the displayed checker and local join ledger rather than through a detached global theorem.

Thus any accepted residual-diamond handoff must first display $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ on the same finite packet. Since none of the rows in $K$ carries global SN, global confluence, beta-decidability, quotient term equality, selected limits, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or full consistency, those exports remain outside the handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC residual diamond L10 locality obstruction]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-diamond-l10-locality-obstruction}
Let
$$
K=(S,N,O,U,D,H,C,P,L)
$$
be an accepted $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ packet. If the residual-diamond route for $K$ lacks any one of the four L10 faces $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, or $\RealUp$, then the route cannot export the candidate-mediated local-confluence consumer or the candidate-mediated decidability consumer. The obstruction is local to the displayed packet route; it is not a global failure of strong normalization, global confluence, beta-conversion decidability, quotient term equality, selected limits, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or kernel consistency.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-diamond-budget-scope}. It puts the residual substitution budget, candidate schedule, local join ledger, checker factorization, and L10 source rows on one finite route before any residual-diamond consumer can read the packet.

Now use \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-residual-diamond-l10-handoff}. That handoff requires the four source faces $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ before local confluence or decidability can be exported.

The same dependency is visible in the named candidate residual budget interface and in the candidate-SN confluence checker factorization, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-residual-budget-interface} and \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-sn-confluence-checker-factorization}. Removing any one L10 face breaks the displayed route from candidate budget to checker output, so the consumer has no finite packet coordinate from which to export local confluence or decidability.
\end{proof}
