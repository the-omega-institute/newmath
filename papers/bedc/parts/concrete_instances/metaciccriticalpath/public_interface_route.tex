\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC critical-path public interface]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface}
Every public $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ consumer route is the finite union of the L10 four-object status rows, the candidate-mediated strong-normalization frontier rows, the confluence and decidability budget rows, and the residual discharge rows already displayed by the chapter. The interface may expose $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ source status; CandidateMediatedSN and CandidateSNConfluenceBoundary readiness; confluence, finite-observation, and decidability budgets; and the residual discharge socket. It does not claim full normalization, full subject reduction, host consistency, or bridge closure.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MetaCICCriticalPathUp.MetaCICCriticalPathPublicInterface}
\begin{proof}
First project the L10 status surface. Theorems~\ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-l10-four-object-exit-certificate}, \ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-l10-status-bridge-obligation}, and \ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-l10-four-face-closure-readiness} expose the four object rows and keep them as separate finite source records.

Next project the candidate-mediated frontier. Theorems~\ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-sn-frontier}, \ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-frontier-handoff-totality}, and \ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-frontier-obstruction-nonescape} present CandidateMediatedSN and its obstruction boundary as displayed $\BHist$ route data rather than as a global normalization theorem.

Then project the confluence and decidability budgets. Theorems~\ref{thm:metaciccriticalpath-confluence-decidability-budget}, \ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-sn-confluence-budget}, and \ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-confluence-decidability-handoff-exhaustion} keep finite-observation confluence, candidate SN, and decidability uses on their visible budget rows.

Finally project the residual discharge surface through \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-budget-discharge-frontier} and \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-budget-socket-route}. The finite union of these projections is the public interface route, and finite $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ projection adds no full normalization, full subject reduction, host consistency, or bridge closure claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC critical-path residual public handoff]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-public-handoff}
Every public $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ consumer that reads residual-discharge data must pass the read through the residual budget socket and the candidate-mediated frontier rows of \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface} before it claims any confluence or decidability budget consumption. The handoff may expose the residual-budget, substitution-boundary, closed-premise, local-diamond, candidate-mediated strong-normalization, confluence-frontier, and decidability-budget rows as displayed public data. It does not discharge full normalization, full subject reduction, host consistency, full confluence, or beta-conversion decidability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the public interface theorem. It already separates the residual discharge rows from the candidate-mediated frontier rows and from the confluence and decidability budget rows.

Now route the residual read through the socket. Theorems~\ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-budget-discharge-frontier} and~\ref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-budget-socket-route} require residual-budget rows, substitution-boundary rows, closed-premise ledger rows, and local-diamond rows before any discharge socket may be treated as available.

Next attach the candidate-mediated frontier. The public interface proof cites the candidate-mediated strong-normalization frontier and its handoff and obstruction rows, so a public consumer must keep those rows visible while reading the residual socket.

Finally consume the confluence and decidability budget only after the residual and candidate rows have both been displayed. The confluence-decidability budget, SN confluence budget, and handoff-exhaustion rows remain budget rows; they do not supply full normalization, full subject reduction, host consistency, full confluence, or beta-conversion decidability.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MetaCICCriticalPathUp.MetaCICCriticalPathResidualPublicHandoff}

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC public interface frontier readiness]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface-frontier-readiness}
A public $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ consumer may read CandidateMediatedSN, CandidateSNConfluenceBoundary, finite-observation confluence, decidability budgets, and residual discharge sockets only as readiness rows over the same displayed finite route of \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface}. Before claiming closed subject reduction, full confluence, beta-conversion decidability, or host consistency, the consumer must cite a separate subject-reduction row and the matching discharge row for that global claim.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MetaCICCriticalPathUp.MetaCICCriticalPathPublicInterfaceFrontierReadiness}
\begin{proof}
Project the public interface theorem. It already displays the L10 source rows, candidate-mediated frontier rows, confluence and decidability budgets, and residual discharge sockets as a finite union of carrier-visible routes.

Readiness is preserved because each named frontier row remains attached to the same $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ coordinates. CandidateMediatedSN and CandidateSNConfluenceBoundary are therefore usable as frontier evidence, while finite-observation confluence and decidability budgets remain budget rows rather than full metatheorems.

The global claims require a further row. Since the public interface theorem explicitly refuses full normalization, full subject reduction, host consistency, and bridge closure, a consumer cannot obtain closed subject reduction, full confluence, beta-conversion decidability, or host consistency without citing the separate subject-reduction and discharge surfaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC public interface gate]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface-gate}
A $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ consumer passes the public interface gate only when its bridge-facing read cites the public interface theorem, the residual public handoff, and the frontier-readiness row in that order. Passing the gate records that the consumer has displayed the L10 four-object rows, CandidateMediatedSN frontier, confluence and decidability budgets, and residual budget socket; it records no full normalization theorem, no full subject-reduction theorem, no host-consistency theorem, no full-confluence theorem, no beta-conversion decidability theorem, and no bridge closure.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MetaCICCriticalPathUp.MetaCICCriticalPathPublicInterfaceGate}
\begin{proof}
The gate first checks the row inventory against \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface}. Missing one of the L10 source rows, the candidate-mediated frontier rows, the confluence or decidability budget rows, or the residual discharge rows leaves the public interface unreadable.

It then checks residual order through \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-residual-public-handoff}. The residual socket must be read before any confluence or decidability budget consumption is advertised, and the candidate-mediated frontier must remain attached to that read.

It finally checks readiness through \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-public-interface-frontier-readiness}. Readiness is a public-row status over the displayed finite route, so the gate cannot convert it into full normalization, full subject reduction, host consistency, full confluence, beta-conversion decidability, or bridge closure.
\end{proof}
