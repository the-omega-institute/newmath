\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC confluence residual frontier route]
\label{thm:metacic-criticalpath-confluence-residual-frontier-route}
Every confluence residual frontier read of an accepted $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ packet consumes the same candidate-mediated strong-normalization row and the same L10 route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\quad
\StreamNameUp,\quad
\RegSeqRatUp,\quad
\RealUp .
$$
Consequently residual substitution and critical-pair reads cannot bypass the finite four-object seal before reading the normal-form boundary or the confluence-decidability handoff.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Read the candidate-mediated SN frontier theorem together with \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-frontier-normal-form-discharge}. Both place the candidate row, normal-form endpoint, discharge socket, handoff boundary, and four L10 rows on the same finite frontier.

Use \autoref{thm:metacic-criticalpath-candidate-mediated-sn-l10-readback} and \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-frontier-regseqrat-handoff}. These force any Real-facing or Completion-facing residual read to pass through the displayed dyadic, stream, regular-rational, and Real rows.

Finally apply the confluence-decidability consumer boundary, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-confluence-decidability-consumer-boundary}. Residual substitution and critical-pair consumers can read the handoff only after the candidate SN frontier and L10 route are present, so no residual route escapes to full confluence, closed subject reduction, beta-decidability, quotient equality, selected limits, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}

Next read the confluence residual frontier route above. It forces residual substitution and critical-pair consumers to consume the same candidate-mediated strong-normalization row and the same four L10 source locks before the handoff can be inspected.

\begin{theorem}[MetaCIC SN discharge frontier]
\label{thm:metacic-critical-path-sn-discharge-frontier}
Let
$$
K=(S,N,O,U,D,H,C,P,L)
$$
be an accepted $\MetaCICCriticalPathUp$ packet. A candidate-mediated strong-normalization route may hand a normal-form consumer to the discharge frontier only while the subject-reduction socket $D$ and obstruction row $O$ remain visible. The confluence residual route, decidability handoff, and L10 readback rows may transport $S,N,U,H,C,P,L$, but they do not close subject reduction, fill $D$, erase $O$, or export global confluence, beta-conversion decidability, quotient term equality, host equality, selected limits, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or kernel consistency.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the candidate SN boundary and the normal-form discharge theorem, \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-sn-boundary} and \autoref{thm:metacic-critical-path-candidate-mediated-frontier-normal-form-discharge}. They place the normal-form consumer on the finite rows $S,N,U,D,H,C,P,L$ together with the L10 faces.

Next use subject-reduction socket readiness, \autoref{thm:metacic-criticalpath-subject-reduction-socket-readiness}. It permits inspection of the normal-form discharge only after $D$ is displayed as a socket, not as a closed subject-reduction proof.

Finally apply the confluence residual frontier route, \autoref{thm:metacic-criticalpath-confluence-residual-frontier-route}. Confluence, decidability, and L10 readback keep the same finite frontier visible, so they cannot discharge $D$ or erase $O$ without a separate closed subject-reduction row.
\end{proof}
