Following Darboux and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\DarbouxIntegrableUp$ packet separates integrability evidence from the $\DarbouxIntegralUp$ value packet. It sits between the Darboux criterion of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-darboux-criterion-namecert}, the finite lower and upper sum rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lower-darboux-sum-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-upper-darboux-sum-namecert}, the finite Riemann--Darboux criterion of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riemann-darboux-criterion-finite-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the value-level Darboux integral route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-darbouxintegral-namecert}. The chapter records a finite predicate-level carrier for integrability only; it does not select an integral value, quotient integrands by host equality, import measure completion, select a partition by choice, or assert ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Darboux integrable carrier]\label{def:darboux-integrable-carrier}
A $\DarbouxIntegrableUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(F,P,L,U,G,M,Q,R,H,C,V,N)
$$
with the following rows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $F$ is the bounded function row exposed only through finite interval and value observations.
  \item $P$ is the finite partition row used by the Darboux criterion.
  \item $L$ is the $\LowerDarbouxSumUp$ row over $F$ and $P$.
  \item $U$ is the $\UpperDarbouxSumUp$ row over the same $F$ and $P$.
  \item $G$ is the oscillation-gap row comparing $U$ and $L$ at the displayed tolerance request.
  \item $M$ is the tolerance-modulus row that chooses later partition requests only through finite data.
  \item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the gap estimates.
  \item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row available after the regular rational readback.
  \item $H$ is componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $F,P,L,U,G,M,Q,R$.
  \item $C$ is displayed $\Cont$ replay for the same route.
  \item $V$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DarbouxIntegrableUp$, $\DarbouxCriterionUp$, $\LowerDarbouxSumUp$, $\UpperDarbouxSumUp$, $\RiemannDarbouxCriterionFiniteUp$, $\DarbouxIntegralUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
  \item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DarbouxIntegrableUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these twelve rows. It has no coordinate for a selected integral value, host integrand equality, measure completion, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Darboux integrable NameCert obligations]\label{thm:darboux-integrable-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DarbouxIntegrableUp$ carrier $I=(F,P,L,U,G,M,Q,R,H,C,V,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DarbouxIntegrableUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed finite packet, common-partition compatibility for $L$ and $U$, oscillation-gap exactness through $G$, tolerance-modulus coverage through $M$, $\DarbouxCriterionUp$ and $\RiemannDarbouxCriterionFiniteUp$ handoff, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback through $Q$, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing through $R$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $V$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:darboux-integrable-carrier}. The rows $F,P,L,U,G,M,Q,R,H,C,V,N$ are the whole certificate surface, so every obligation must begin by reading one of those rows.

The common-partition obligation uses $P$ before either sum row is consumed. The lower and upper sums therefore compare the same bounded function row $F$ on the same finite partition, and the oscillation gap $G$ is a row over that shared surface.

The tolerance-modulus row $M$ supplies the finite requests used by the Darboux and Riemann--Darboux criterion packets. Once those requests are displayed, $Q$ gives regular rational readback for the gap estimates and $R$ supplies the terminal Real seal.

Finally, $H,C,V,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Because the carrier has no coordinate for a selected integral value, quotient function equality, measure completion, selected partitions by choice, or ambient completeness, those objects cannot be exported by the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Darboux integrable oscillation classifier]\label{thm:darboux-integrable-oscillation-classifier}
An accepted $\DarbouxIntegrableUp$ packet classifies integrability by the displayed oscillation-gap route
$$
\DarbouxCriterionUp \longmapsto \LowerDarbouxSumUp \longmapsto \UpperDarbouxSumUp \longmapsto \RiemannDarbouxCriterionFiniteUp .
$$
Every classifier read must use the bounded function row, partition row, lower and upper sum rows, oscillation gap, tolerance modulus, regular rational readback, and Real seal of \autoref{def:darboux-integrable-carrier}. It cannot read integrability from the value-level $\DarbouxIntegralUp$ packet alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:darboux-integrable-namecert-obligations}. They force the lower and upper Darboux rows to share the same bounded function and partition rows before the gap row is inspected.

The oscillation classifier is then the finite route through $G$ and $M$. The row $G$ records the upper-minus-lower comparison, while $M$ records the tolerance requests under which the comparison is reusable by Darboux and Riemann--Darboux criteria.

The readback rows $Q$ and $R$ only seal this displayed classifier route. Since $\DarbouxIntegralUp$ is named only in provenance as a downstream value consumer, the predicate-level classifier is independent of any selected integral value.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Darboux integrable Real handoff]\label{thm:darboux-integrable-real-handoff}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, or $\DarbouxIntegralUp$ consumer of an accepted $\DarbouxIntegrableUp$ packet receives the integrability evidence only through the displayed finite route
$$
F\longmapsto P\longmapsto (L,U)\longmapsto G\longmapsto M\longmapsto Q\longmapsto R .
$$
The handoff exports integrability-as-carrier data without exporting a chosen integral value, quotient integrand equality, measure completion, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the oscillation classifier theorem \autoref{thm:darboux-integrable-oscillation-classifier}. It identifies the route that any integrability consumer must read before the predicate can be used.

Next use the NameCert obligations. The regular rational and Real rows are downstream of the same gap and modulus rows, so a $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer cannot bypass $F,P,L,U,G,M$ when reading $Q$ or $R$.

For a $\DarbouxIntegralUp$ consumer, the packet supplies only the predicate-level evidence needed before a value packet may be read. It does not add a coordinate for the value itself, so the handoff remains a finite integrability carrier rather than a value certificate.
\end{proof}
