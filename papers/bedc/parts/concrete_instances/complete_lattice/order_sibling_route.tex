\begin{theorem}[Complete lattice order sibling route]
\label{thm:complete-lattice-order-sibling-route}
Every $\CompleteLatticeUp$ fixed-point read or family-boundary read factors first through the finite order sibling route
$$
\SetLikeUp,\PosetUp,\LatticeUp \longmapsto \CompleteLatticeUp .
$$
The $\SetLikeUp$ membership rows expose the displayed family, the $\PosetUp$ row supplies the antisymmetric comparison surface, and the $\LatticeUp$ row supplies the meet and join operations available before the complete-lattice schedules are read. Only after those sibling rows are visible may a consumer expose the complete-lattice join schedule, meet schedule, monotone-map row, or fixed-point boundary row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:complete-lattice-carrier}. The certified-family row $F$ is read as displayed membership data, so every family-boundary consumer begins with the $\SetLikeUp$ surface.

The order row $O$ is not a free comparison relation. It is read through the antisymmetric $\PosetUp$ surface and the meet--join $\LatticeUp$ surface named in the carrier, so fixed-point consumers must expose those sibling rows before using $J,M,T$, or $B$.

The join and meet schedules are therefore complete-lattice rows over an already visible membership and order route. They do not replace the sibling route by an ambient powerset, unrestricted supremum operator, quotient representative, or selected ordinal iteration.
\end{proof}
