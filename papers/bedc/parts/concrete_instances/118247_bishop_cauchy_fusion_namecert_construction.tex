\begin{definition}[Bishop Cauchy fusion carrier]
\label{def:bishop-cauchy-fusion-carrier}
A $\BishopCauchyFusionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$

$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ and $B$ are the two $\RegSeqRatUp$ source rows whose regular rational observations are to be fused.
\item $W_A$ and $W_B$ are the paired $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window rows through which the two sources are inspected.
\item $D$ is the shared $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ budget row, recording the single dyadic tolerance service used by both windows.
\item $F$ is the $\CauchyTailModulusFusionUp$ row that binds the two source tails to the shared budget before a seal-facing read is allowed.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row produced only after $A,B,W_A,W_B,D,F$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the two sources, paired windows, shared budget, fusion row, Real seal, continuation row, package row, and local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a requested tolerance through the two windows, the shared budget, the fusion row, and then to the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopCauchyFusionUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\CauchyTailModulusFusionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCauchyFusionUp}$ row.

The carrier exposes only this finite two-witness route. It has no row for quotient stream equality, selected host limits, host real equality, countable-choice schedules, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop Cauchy fusion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-cauchy-fusion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopCauchyFusionUp$ carrier
$$
K=(A,B,W_A,W_B,D,F,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCauchyFusionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the two $\RegSeqRatUp$ source rows, paired $\StreamNameUp$ windows, one shared $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ budget, one $\CauchyTailModulusFusionUp$ row, one terminal $\RealUp$ seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every seal-facing read must keep both source rows and both window rows attached to the same budget row $D$ before $F$ and $R$ are consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-cauchy-fusion-carrier}. Rows $A,B,W_A,W_B,D,F,R$ are the analytic route, and rows $H,C,P,N$ provide transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Carrier admission reads the two regular sources and paired windows first. The budget obligation then identifies $D$ as the only dyadic tolerance row available to both windows, so the two branches cannot be read through private tolerance services.

The fusion obligation is the next step. Row $F$ is consumed only after $A,B,W_A,W_B,D$ are displayed, and the Real seal $R$ is downstream of that same fusion row.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ preserve the route as finite NameCert data. Since quotient streams, selected host limits, host equality, countable choice, trichotomy, and ambient completeness are absent from the carrier, the listed obligations exhaust the local surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop Cauchy fusion single-window handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-cauchy-fusion-single-window-handoff}
Every accepted $\BishopCauchyFusionUp$ packet exposes a single shared observation window for the two-witness fusion route: a consumer may inspect $W_A$ and $W_B$ only through the common dyadic budget $D$ and the fusion row $F$. Thus the handoff from two $\RegSeqRatUp$ sources to the terminal $\RealUp$ seal is a single finite route through
$$
A,\ B,\ W_A,\ W_B,\ D,\ F,\ R,
$$
not two independent Real-seal routes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-cauchy-fusion-namecert-obligations}. They keep both source rows attached to the paired stream windows and require one shared dyadic budget row $D$ before the fusion row is available.

The handoff is therefore controlled by $D$. Any read of $W_A$ or $W_B$ that reaches $F$ has already passed through the same budget service, so the consumer receives one displayed fusion route rather than two detached source reads.

The terminal Real seal $R$ is downstream of $F$. Because no second Real-seal coordinate appears in the carrier, the handoff remains a single finite route through the displayed rows.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:bishop-cauchy-fusion-real-seal-nonescape}
