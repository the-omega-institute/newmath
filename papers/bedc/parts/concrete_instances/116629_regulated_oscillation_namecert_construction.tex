\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegulatedOscillationUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges on regulated functions and finite oscillation control, the $\RegulatedOscillationUp$ packet records the displayed oscillation windows by which a regulated real-valued function is read over a finite interval cover. It sits after the regulated-function schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulated-function-namecert}, the oscillation-modulus boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-oscillation-modulus-namecert}, the compact-interval oscillation-modulus packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-oscillation-modulus-namecert}, the dyadic interval-cover row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-cover-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names finite oscillation data for regulated-integration and compact-interval consumers; it does not assert open-cover compactness, quotient equality of functions, selected extrema, a uniform modulus chosen from an ambient theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Regulated oscillation carrier]
\label{def:regulated-oscillation-carrier}
A $\RegulatedOscillationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(F,O,C,D,R,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is the $\RegulatedFunctionUp$ source row, exposing the regulated approximation schedule whose oscillation is being read.
\item $O$ is the $\OscillationModulusUp$ row, carrying the requested cellwise oscillation budget.
\item $C$ is the $\CompactIntervalOscillationModulusUp$ row, binding the oscillation budget to a displayed compact interval window.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$ row, listing the finite dyadic interval cover on which the oscillation cells are inspected.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ readback row for the cellwise bounds and endpoint values exposed by the packet.
\item $H,T,P,N$ are componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedOscillationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares carriers only through $F,O,C,D,R,H,T,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host function quotient, selected extremum, arbitrary open cover, unbounded refinement search, or ambient real-completeness principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regulated oscillation NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regulated-oscillation-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegulatedOscillationUp$ carrier $Q=(F,O,C,D,R,H,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegulatedOscillationUp}$ surface consists of exactly five analytic rows before the structural rows are read: the regulated-function source $F$, oscillation-modulus row $O$, compact-interval oscillation row $C$, dyadic interval-cover row $D$, and terminal Real readback $R$. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve only this displayed surface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regulated-oscillation-carrier}. The analytic rows are precisely $F,O,C,D,R$, and the structural rows are $H,T,P,N$.

The source row $F$ supplies the regulated approximation schedule. The row $O$ attaches the requested oscillation budget to that schedule, while $C$ restricts the read to the displayed compact interval window.

The dyadic cover row $D$ supplies the finite cells on which the oscillation bounds are inspected. The terminal Real row $R$ records the cellwise bound and endpoint readback only after the first four analytic rows are visible.

Since no further analytic coordinate occurs in the packet, $H,T,P,N$ can only transport, replay, source, and name the same five-row surface. The listed rows therefore exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regulated oscillation finite partition nonescape]
\label{thm:regulated-oscillation-finite-partition-nonescape}
Every regulated-integration or compact-interval consumer of an accepted $\RegulatedOscillationUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
\RegulatedFunctionUp \longrightarrow \OscillationModulusUp \longrightarrow \CompactIntervalOscillationModulusUp \longrightarrow \DyadicIntervalCoverUp \longrightarrow \RealUp
$$
before it may use any oscillation bound. The consumer receives a finite partition-and-readback surface, not open-cover compactness, quotient equality of functions, selected extrema, unbounded refinement search, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:regulated-oscillation-namecert-obligations}. They force every consumer to expose the regulated source, oscillation budget, compact-interval window, dyadic interval cover, and Real readback before the structural rows may replay the request.

The sibling chapters provide those rows in order: \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulated-function-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-oscillation-modulus-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-oscillation-modulus-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-cover-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. Thus the consumer reads a displayed finite route rather than a detached compactness or function-extensionality theorem.

The exclusions follow from the carrier inventory. No row stores an arbitrary open cover, selected extremum, quotient function equality, unbounded refinement search, or ambient real-completeness assertion, so the finite partition route cannot export any of them.
\end{proof}
