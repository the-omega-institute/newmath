\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopTotallyBoundedCompletionNetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-totally-bounded-completion-net-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on totally bounded metric spaces and constructive completion, the $\BishopTotallyBoundedCompletionNetUp$ packet records the finite-net surface that a completion consumer may read before any completed point is sealed. It sits after the broad completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallyboundedcompletion-namecert}, the finite-net carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the Cauchy-filter basis of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-basis-namecert}, the dense embedding row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completionembedding-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The certificate is a finite NameCert surface between total-bounded finite nets and completion readback; it is not open-cover compactness, quotient completion, selected limit construction, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Bishop totally bounded completion net carrier]
\label{def:bishop-totally-bounded-completion-net-carrier}
A $\BishopTotallyBoundedCompletionNetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(X,T,N,F,E,R,Q,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source metric row whose distance witnesses are accepted by the total-bounded and completion-facing ledgers.
\item $T$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row, recording for each displayed positive tolerance a closed $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ of centers, membership rows, and rational distance bounds.
\item $N$ is the Bishop refinement row. It relates a finer tolerance request to a previously displayed finite net without replacing the packet by an open cover or a selected subnet.
\item $F$ is the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ row generated from $T$ and $N$.
\item $E$ is the $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$ row that inserts source observations into the completion boundary.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row used when completion names are inspected by finite rational windows.
\item $Q$ records the completion-readback seal that lets a downstream $\RealUp$ consumer read only after $F,E,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H,C,P,L$ are, respectively, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance over the cited sibling chapters, and the local $\NameCert_{\BishopTotallyBoundedCompletionNetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only by replaying these rows. No coordinate exports open-cover compactness, quotient representatives, selected limits, countable choice, or an ambient completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop totally bounded completion net NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-totally-bounded-completion-net-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopTotallyBoundedCompletionNetUp$ carrier $B=(X,T,N,F,E,R,Q,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for those rows, finite-net persistence through $T,N$, Cauchy-filter-basis generation through $F$, dense completion insertion through $E$, rational readback through $R$, completion-to-Real sealing through $Q$, and componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:bishop-totally-bounded-completion-net-carrier}. The listed rows are the whole visible packet, so every obligation must be read from them.

Finite-net persistence is forced by $T$ and $N$. A tolerance request first reads the finite centers, membership constructors, distance witnesses, and rational bounds of $T$; refinement then follows the displayed Bishop row $N$.

The completion readback is downstream of that finite-net data. The row $F$ builds the Cauchy-filter basis, $E$ embeds source observations into the completion boundary, $R$ supplies regular rational windows, and $Q$ seals the readback for Real-facing consumers. The remaining rows only transport, replay, source, and name the same route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop totally bounded completion net Real-seal handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-totally-bounded-completion-net-real-seal-handoff}
Every Real-facing completion consumer of an accepted $\BishopTotallyBoundedCompletionNetUp$ packet receives the displayed route
$$
X,T,N \longmapsto F \longmapsto E \longmapsto R \longmapsto Q \longmapsto H,C,P,L .
$$
Thus the consumer reads the original total-bounded finite-net ledger before the completion and Real seal rows, and it cannot substitute open-cover compactness, quotient completion, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness for that route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite-net side. The source row $X$ supplies the metric witnesses, while $T$ and $N$ display the finite centers and refinement requests in Bishop style.

Next pass to completion readback. The basis row $F$ is generated from the finite-net ledger, the embedding row $E$ places source observations at the completion boundary, and the regular readback row $R$ is the only rational-window route available before the terminal seal $Q$.

Finally use the transport and provenance rows. Since $H,C,P,L$ only preserve and name the displayed packet, the Real-facing consumer has no coordinate from which to read an open cover, quotient representative, selected limit, choice schedule, or ambient completeness claim.
\end{proof}
