\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MetrizableSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-metrizable-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Kelley-style topology, the $\MetrizableSpaceUp$ packet records the finite BEDC handoff by which a displayed topology may expose a metric whose ball-neighbourhood rows answer the same local open requests. It sits between the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the metric-space surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-space-namecert}, the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ distance readback route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a metrizable-space interface, not a Urysohn metrization theorem, open-cover compactness, quotient equality, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[MetrizableSpace carrier]
\label{def:metrizable-space-carrier}
A $\MetrizableSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Z=(T,M,B,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row, carrying open-row classifiers, point-neighbourhood requests, and finite membership reads.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ metric row over the same displayed point carrier.
\item $B$ is the ball-neighbourhood basis row. It records, for each displayed point and tolerance request, the metric ball row whose inclusion and refinement reads answer the topology row $T$.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row used when a distance request is inspected by finitely many named observations.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational distance stream generated from $W$ and the metric row $M$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for the distance value, reachable only after $M,B,W,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for topology rows, metric rows, ball-basis rows, distance windows, regular readback, Real seals, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a topological neighbourhood or distance request through $T,M,B,W,R,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetrizableSpaceUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetrizableSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for Urysohn separation hypotheses, open-cover compactness, quotient point equality, selected metric representatives, countable-choice schedules, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MetrizableSpace NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:metrizable-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MetrizableSpaceUp$ carrier $Z=(T,M,B,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MetrizableSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of topology carrier admission, metric row admission, ball-neighbourhood basis visibility, finite distance-window inspection, $\RegSeqRatUp$ distance readback, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. No local obligation is stored outside those finite rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metrizable-space-carrier}. The topology source, metric row, ball-basis row, distance window, regular readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming row are the only displayed coordinates.

The topological side is read through $T$ and $B$. A neighbourhood request must first name the open-row classifier in $T$ and then read the ball-basis witness that relates the displayed metric ball to that topology row.

The metric side is read through $M,W,R,E$. A distance request cannot reach a Real value until the metric row has been displayed, the finite window has been inspected, the regular rational readback has been produced, and the terminal Real seal has been exposed.

Rows $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no remaining coordinate stores the excluded objects, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetrizableSpace metric-topology handoff]
\label{thm:metrizable-space-metric-topology-handoff}
Every topology-facing or metric-facing consumer of an accepted $\MetrizableSpaceUp$ packet factors through
$$
\TopologyUp,\quad \MetricSpaceUp,\quad \StreamNameUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp .
$$
The topology read receives ball-neighbourhood basis rows tied to $T$, while the metric read receives distance windows, regular readback, and a terminal Real seal tied to $M$. The handoff exports no Urysohn metrization theorem, open-cover compactness, quotient equality, selected representative, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the topology-facing request. The carrier exposes topology only through $T$, and the basis row $B$ is the only row that answers neighbourhood requests by metric balls.

For a metric-facing request, read $M$ before the distance window $W$. The window is then converted by $R$ into a regular rational distance readback and sealed by $E$ as the only Real-facing endpoint.

Transport and replay through $H$ and $C$ preserve the same route, while $P$ and $N$ record provenance and local naming. The carrier contains no row for the excluded global theorems or selectors, so the handoff is exactly the finite BEDC route displayed above.
\end{proof}
