\begin{theorem}[Regular real tail equivalence transitive finite-window route]
\label{thm:regular-real-tail-equivalence-transitive-window}
Let
$$
T_{01}=(R_0,R_1,W_{01},D_{01},Z_{01},Q_{01},E_{01},H_{01},C_{01},P_{01},N_{01})
$$
and
$$
T_{12}=(R_1,R_2,W_{12},D_{12},Z_{12},Q_{12},E_{12},H_{12},C_{12},P_{12},N_{12})
$$
be accepted $\RegularRealTailEquivalenceUp$ packets whose middle $\RegSeqRatUp$ source row is the same displayed finite row. Then their same-real reads compose through a common $\StreamNameUp$ finite window, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledgers $D_{01},D_{12}$, the $\NullSequenceUp$ zero-tail rows $Z_{01},Z_{12}$, the $\RegularCauchyComparisonUp$ routes $Q_{01},Q_{12}$, and the downstream $\RealUp$ seal rows. The composed route is still a finite carrier-local certificate from $R_0$ to $R_2$; it introduces no quotient streams, host equality, selected representatives, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project both carriers using \autoref{def:regular-real-tail-equivalence-carrier}. The shared middle source is visible as the same displayed $R_1$ row, while each packet exposes its own finite window, dyadic tolerance ledger, zero-tail row, comparison route, Real seal, and structural rows.

Synchronize the finite windows by the $\StreamNameUp$ rows. The two packets may be read by one consumer only after $W_{01}$ and $W_{12}$ are displayed, so the middle source is inspected through finite observation data rather than by selecting a representative stream.

Compose the zero-tail and comparison ledgers through that shared middle source. The rows $D_{01},Z_{01},Q_{01}$ carry the first tail comparison and $D_{12},Z_{12},Q_{12}$ carry the second, so the only available transitive evidence is their displayed dyadic, null-sequence, and regular-comparison data.

Finally re-seal through $\RealUp$. The seal rows $E_{01}$ and $E_{12}$ are downstream of the same finite route, and transport, replay, provenance, and local naming keep the composed read inside the two accepted packets. Since neither carrier contains quotient streams, host equality, selected representatives, choice, or ambient completeness, none can appear in the composed certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular real tail transitive window coverage]
\label{thm:regular-real-tail-transitive-window-coverage}
Every completion-facing consumer of a composed
$\RegularRealTailEquivalenceUp$ route must expose both source packets'
$\StreamNameUp$ finite windows, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledgers,
$\NullSequenceUp$ zero-tail rows, $\RegularCauchyComparisonUp$ routes, and
terminal $\RealUp$ seal rows before it may read the composed same-real
certificate. The composed certificate is therefore a finite coverage route over
the two displayed packets, not a quotient-stream equality, representative
selection, choice schedule, or ambient completeness endpoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the transitive finite-window route of
\autoref{thm:regular-real-tail-equivalence-transitive-window}. It already
forces the shared middle source, both finite windows, both tolerance ledgers,
both zero-tail rows, both regular-comparison routes, and both Real seals to stay
visible in the composed read.

Project the two carriers through
\autoref{def:regular-real-tail-equivalence-carrier}. The rows $W_{01}$ and
$W_{12}$ expose the source windows, $D_{01}$ and $D_{12}$ expose the dyadic
tolerance data, $Z_{01}$ and $Z_{12}$ expose the zero-tail witnesses, and
$Q_{01}$ and $Q_{12}$ expose the regular Cauchy comparison routes.

Only after those rows are displayed can the terminal seals $E_{01}$ and
$E_{12}$ be read. Since the carrier presents no quotient stream, selected
representative, choice schedule, or ambient completeness row, a
completion-facing consumer receives exactly the displayed coverage route before
the composed same-real certificate.
\end{proof}
