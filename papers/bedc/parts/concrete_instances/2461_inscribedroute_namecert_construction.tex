\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\InscribedRouteUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-inscribedroute-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\InscribedRouteUp$ surface isolates the reusable proof route carried by an inscription event. It names the finite path from a visible gap through a public statement, a displayed proof route, an accepted record row, and a downstream consumer, while leaving private discovery, host trust, and unrecorded choice outside the packet.

It consumes the inscription-event carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-inscriptionevent-namecert} and the visible gap ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-inscription-gap-ledger-namecert}. The source scope and visible gap rows come from those sibling certificates, while this chapter packages the route, acceptance row, downstream consumer row, and public $\mathsf{GAP}$ ledger as the reusable route certificate.

\begin{definition}[InscribedRoute carrier]
\label{def:inscribed-route-carrier}
An $\InscribedRouteUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the prior $\BHist$ source scope from which the route is read;
\item $G$ is the visible gap row naming the local theorem, certificate, or consumer boundary that still requires a route;
\item $M$ is the named statement row, fixing the public binders, hypotheses, and conclusion of the reusable route;
\item $R$ is the proof-route row, recording the displayed constructor, projection, inversion, transitivity, symmetry, or transport steps that move from $S$ and $G$ to $M$;
\item $A$ is the acceptance row, recording that the route is admitted as public record data;
\item $U$ is the downstream consumer row naming the theorem site, citation, audit surface, or certificate boundary that may read the route;
\item $L$ is the public $\mathsf{GAP}$ ledger row tying the visible gap, named statement, route, acceptance row, and consumer row to the same finite route packet;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, route-checking data, $\mathsf{GAP}$ ledger data, and local certificate dependencies;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\InscribedRouteUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier for accepted carriers compares only $S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N$ and their displayed $\hsame$ and $\Cont$ transports. It has no coordinate for hidden psychology, host trust, or an unrecorded route selector.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[InscribedRoute NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:inscribed-route-namecert-obligations}
The local $\NameCert_{\InscribedRouteUp}$ surface for the carrier of \autoref{def:inscribed-route-carrier} consists of five finite obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission: every accepted route exposes exactly the packet $Q=(S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N)$;
\item route classifier stability: public comparison is restricted to the source scope, visible gap, named statement, proof route, acceptance row, downstream consumer, ledger, package provenance, local naming row, and displayed $\hsame$ and $\Cont$ routes;
\item proof-route determinacy: the route row $R$ reaches $M$ from $S$ and $G$ only through the displayed $\Cont$ path and the matching $\hsame$ rows;
\item acceptance-ledger exactness: the acceptance row $A$ and $\mathsf{GAP}$ ledger row $L$ record exactly the admitted route and the downstream consumer boundary that may cite it;
\item non-escape: the packet exports no hidden psychology, host trust predicate, or unrecorded choice of route.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.InscribedRouteUp.InscribedRouteNameCertObligations}
\leanvariant{BEDC.Derived.InscribedRouteUp.InscribedRouteCarrier\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:inscribed-route-carrier}. The source scope, visible gap, named statement, proof route, acceptance row, downstream consumer, ledger, package provenance, and local naming row are all displayed before the route can be consumed.

Classifier stability and proof-route determinacy are read from the displayed comparison surface. The classifier has access only to $S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N$, and the route from $R$ to $M$ is replayed through the recorded $\Cont$ path together with the matching $\hsame$ rows.

Acceptance-ledger exactness follows because $A$ and $L$ are coordinates of the same finite packet. The accepted route and the downstream consumer boundary are therefore ledgered in public data rather than inferred from a host-side process.

The non-escape clause is immediate from the carrier inventory. No row stores hidden psychology, host trust, or an unrecorded choice, so none of these can be exported by the local $\NameCert_{\InscribedRouteUp}$ surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[InscribedRoute carrier admission]
\label{thm:inscribed-route-carrier-admission}
Every accepted $\InscribedRouteUp$ carrier exposes exactly the rows
$$
S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N
$$
before any route consumer can read it. No accepted carrier has an additional coordinate for hidden host trust, private route choice, or external proof memory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:inscribed-route-carrier}. The tuple displayed there is exactly $Q=(S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N)$, so acceptance can expose only those nine rows.

The classifier sentence following the row inventory confirms that public comparison is restricted to the same rows and their displayed $\hsame$ and $\Cont$ transports. Since the carrier lists no further coordinate, hidden host trust, private route choice, and external proof memory cannot be admitted as carrier data.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[InscribedRoute continuation obligation]
\label{thm:inscribed-route-continuation-obligation}
For every accepted $\InscribedRouteUp$ packet, the route row $R$ reaches the named statement row $M$ through the displayed $\Cont$ path and the matching $\hsame$ rows recorded in the same carrier. The continuation obligation does not use an unrecorded route selector or a host-side proof search.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the proof-route determinacy clause of \autoref{thm:inscribed-route-namecert-obligations}. It states that $R$ reaches $M$ from $S$ and $G$ only through the displayed $\Cont$ path and matching $\hsame$ rows.

Carrier admission supplies the ambient packet $Q=(S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N)$. Because $R$, $M$, $P$, and the displayed transport data are all rows of the same packet, the continuation can be replayed by the recorded data before the downstream consumer $U$ reads it.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[InscribedRoute NameCert ledger obligation]
\label{thm:inscribed-route-namecert-ledger-obligation}
For every accepted $\InscribedRouteUp$ packet, the acceptance row $A$ and the public $\mathsf{GAP}$ ledger row $L$ bind the same downstream consumer row $U$. A consumer may cite the route only when acceptance, ledger entry, named statement, and route row are displayed in the same finite packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the acceptance-ledger exactness clause of \autoref{thm:inscribed-route-namecert-obligations}. It identifies $A$ and $L$ as coordinates of the same route packet and requires them to record the admitted route and downstream consumer boundary.

Now project the carrier. The rows $M,R,A,U,L$ occur together in $Q$, so the named statement, route, acceptance row, consumer row, and public ledger row are not separate host facts. The downstream consumer can therefore cite the route only through the single displayed packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[InscribedRoute public export]
\label{thm:inscribed-route-public-export}
For every accepted $\InscribedRouteUp$ packet, the public reusable route exported to a downstream consumer is exactly the displayed finite route
$$
S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N.
$$
The export makes the source scope, visible gap, named statement, proof route, acceptance row, downstream consumer row, public ledger, provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row visible together; it exports no hidden host trust predicate, private route selector, or external proof memory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission in \autoref{thm:inscribed-route-carrier-admission} identifies the accepted packet with the displayed tuple $S,G,M,R,A,U,L,P,N$. Thus any public export begins with exactly those rows.

The NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:inscribed-route-namecert-obligations} bind the proof-route determinacy and acceptance-ledger exactness clauses to that same tuple. The continuation obligation of \autoref{thm:inscribed-route-continuation-obligation} then replays $R$ to $M$ only through the displayed $\Cont$ path and matching $\hsame$ rows.

Finally \autoref{thm:inscribed-route-namecert-ledger-obligation} ties the acceptance row $A$, downstream consumer row $U$, and public ledger row $L$ together. Therefore the public consumer can cite the route precisely as the finite BEDC packet above, and no unrecorded host-side route data are exported.
\end{proof}
