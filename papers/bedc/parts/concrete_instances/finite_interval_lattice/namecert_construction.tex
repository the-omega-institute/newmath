It sits after the dyadic rational arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the dyadic interval surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicinterval-namecert}, the finite stream-window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the located interval carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-real-interval-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet gives L10 Real and Completion routes a visible finite meet-join substrate; it is not an ambient interval-domain completeness theorem, not an open-cover compactness principle, and not a quotient real equality row.

\begin{definition}[Finite interval lattice carrier]
\label{def:finite-interval-lattice-carrier}
A $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint and radius ledger used by all interval cells.
\item $I$ is the finite list of $\DyadicIntervalUp$ cells with displayed lower and upper endpoints.
\item $L$ is the located-interval source row, read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-real-interval-namecert}.
\item $J$ is the finite join table: every listed pair of cells either has a displayed least containing cell or a displayed refusal row.
\item $M$ is the finite meet table: every listed pair of cells either has a displayed greatest common subcell or a displayed empty-intersection refusal row.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row that records which endpoint and cell observations are inspected.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row reached from the displayed endpoint windows.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row exposed only after $D,I,L,J,M,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transports for all visible interval-lattice rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replays from dyadic endpoint reads to lattice cells, meet-join tables, stream windows, regular readback, and Real sealing.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\DyadicIntervalUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an infinite interval lattice, selected limit point, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval lattice NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-interval-lattice-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, dyadic endpoint ledger exposure, finite dyadic interval cell exposure, located-interval source exposure, finite join and meet tables, stream-window inspection, regular rational readback, Real seal nonescape, componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming. Every consumer of the lattice must read the displayed rows before using it in dyadic-net, located-interval, compact-window, or continuity arguments.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:finite-interval-lattice-carrier}. The packet exposes a finite tuple of dyadic endpoint rows, interval cells, located interval data, meet-join tables, window rows, readback rows, seal rows, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

The lattice operations are finite because $J$ and $M$ are displayed tables over the listed cells $I$. A consumer may inspect only the table entry or refusal row already stored in the packet, and the dyadic endpoint ledger $D$ supplies the comparisons used by those entries.

The analytic handoff then passes through $S$, $R$, and $E$. The stream row records finite endpoint observations, the regular rational row records the accepted readback from those windows, and the Real row seals the same route without adding a selected limit, quotient equality, or ambient completeness coordinate.

Finally, $H$, $C$, $P$, and $N$ preserve the displayed rows as BEDC certificate data. Thus every obligation is discharged through finite $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows, and no host interval lattice or open-cover compactness theorem can enter the local surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval lattice endpoint order]
\label{thm:finite-interval-lattice-endpoint-order}
Let $Q=(D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$ carrier. Every listed dyadic interval cell exposed by $I$ must display its lower and upper endpoint comparison through the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row $D$ before any meet or join table entry in $M$ or $J$ can be read as a lattice operation.

Consequently $J$ and $M$ are public only as endpoint-ordered finite tables over the cells listed in $I$. They cannot supply lattice operations for L10 Real or Completion consumers when the endpoint-order evidence from $D$ is absent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-interval-lattice-carrier}. The row $D$ is the dyadic endpoint and radius ledger, while $I$ is the finite list of dyadic interval cells with displayed lower and upper endpoints.

Read a cell from $I$. Its lower and upper endpoint comparison is not a property of an ambient interval lattice; it is a displayed dyadic comparison supplied by $D$ and carried with the cell row.

The finite join table $J$ and meet table $M$ are indexed by the cells already listed in $I$. A table entry can therefore be interpreted only after the endpoint order of the two source cells and the candidate result cell has been exposed through $D$.

If that endpoint-order evidence is hidden, the carrier still contains symbols named $J$ and $M$, but they no longer certify meet or join operations for the visible interval cells. Hence L10 Real and Completion consumers must read $D$ and $I$ before using the meet and join tables.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval lattice Real-completion route]
\label{thm:finite-interval-lattice-real-completion-route}
Let $Q=(D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$ carrier. Every $\RealUp$ or $\CompletionUp$ consumer of a meet or join read from $M$ or $J$ must pass through the finite route
$$
D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer first reads dyadic endpoint rows, finite interval-cell rows, the located interval source, the meet and join tables, StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, the Real seal, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row before reading completion-facing data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-interval-lattice-carrier}. The tuple exposes the dyadic endpoint ledger $D$, finite interval cells $I$, located source row $L$, meet and join tables $M,J$, stream windows $S$, regular readback $R$, Real seal $E$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Apply the endpoint-order theorem \autoref{thm:finite-interval-lattice-endpoint-order}. It forces every meet or join table entry to read the lower and upper endpoint comparisons from $D$ through the listed cells $I$ before the entry can serve as a lattice operation.

The located source and analytic handoff then remain downstream of the same finite route. The row $L$ supplies the located interval source, $S$ records the inspected endpoint windows, $R$ supplies the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and $E$ exposes the terminal $\RealUp$ seal.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only those displayed rows. Therefore a $\RealUp$ or $\CompletionUp$ consumer cannot read completion-facing data from the lattice unless the dyadic endpoints, interval cells, located source, stream windows, regular readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert rows have already been exposed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval lattice refinement induction]
\label{thm:finite-interval-lattice-refinement-induction}
Let $Q=(D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$ carrier. Suppose a displayed subdivision-depth row refines each listed cell of $I$ by finitely many dyadic subcells whose endpoint comparisons are read through the same $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row $D$. Then the meet and join tables refine by induction over the displayed depth: at every finite stage the refined tables remain indexed by dyadic endpoint rows from $D$, expose only $\StreamNameUp$ windows from $S$, preserve $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback through $R$, and pass the terminal $\RealUp$ seal only through $E$.

Consequently a $\RealUp$ or $\CompletionUp$ consumer of the refined lattice must still read the route
$$
D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N
$$
before using any refined meet, refined join, or refined endpoint cell.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the displayed subdivision-depth row. At depth zero the refined table is the original finite table of \autoref{thm:finite-interval-lattice-real-completion-route}, so all meet and join reads already pass through $D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$.

For the successor step, split each currently displayed cell into its finitely listed dyadic subcells. The endpoint-order theorem \autoref{thm:finite-interval-lattice-endpoint-order} forces the lower and upper endpoint comparisons of every subcell to be read through $D$ before a table entry can be interpreted.

The finite meet and join rows are then rebuilt table-entry by table-entry. Each entry either displays the least containing refined cell, displays the greatest common refined subcell, or records the corresponding refusal row already governed by $J$ and $M$.

Finally the analytic readback is unchanged. The stream windows remain the finite windows in $S$, the rational readback remains the row $R$, and the terminal real-name exposure is still sealed by $E$. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming in $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route, so the induction cannot introduce an infinite lattice, selected limit, quotient equality, or ambient completion principle.
\end{proof}
