\begin{theorem}[Finite interval lattice dyadic-net forward link]
\label{thm:finite-interval-lattice-dyadic-net-forward-link}
Every accepted $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$ interval packet supplies the finite interval-index lattice consumed by the dyadic interval net route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-net-namecert}. For a carrier
$$
Q=(D,I,L,J,M,S,R,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the dyadic-net consumer reads endpoint data from $D$, interval cells from $I$, located interval support from $L$, and finite order operations from $J,M$ before it can use the lattice as an index surface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-interval-lattice-carrier}. The dyadic endpoints are supplied by $D$, the finite interval cells by $I$, and the located interval source by $L$.

Apply the endpoint-order theorem \autoref{thm:finite-interval-lattice-endpoint-order}. It makes each cell and each table entry dependent on the displayed endpoint comparisons rather than on an ambient interval domain.

Use the Real-completion route \autoref{thm:finite-interval-lattice-real-completion-route}. The rows $J,M$ are finite tables over the same listed cells, and $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only those rows.

Thus a dyadic interval net may consume the finite interval-index lattice only through the displayed packet $D,I,L,J,M,H,C,P,N$. No infinite interval lattice, quotient real equality, selected limit point, or host compactness principle is exported.
\end{proof}
