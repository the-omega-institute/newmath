Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\BishopCutUp$ packet records a constructive two-sided cut as finite located lower and upper information before any quotient-real completion or Dedekind completeness principle is available. It consumes the located-cut surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedcut-namecert}, the lower-real row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lowerreal-namecert}, the upper-real envelope of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-upper-real-envelope-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the stream-name windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular-rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The chapter names only the finite cut carrier and its local handoffs; it does not claim a completed real quotient, trichotomy, countable choice, or a Dedekind-completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[BishopCut carrier]
\label{def:bishop-cut-carrier}
A $\BishopCutUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(L,U,E,D,S,R,X,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedCutUp$ row recording the finite located lower and upper tests visible in the current window.
\item $U$ is the paired $\LowerRealUp$ and $\UpperRealEnvelopeUp$ row that separates lower information from upper-refusal information.
\item $E$ is the envelope-consistency row linking the located-cut ledger to the lower and upper presentations.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row used for rational comparison inside the displayed window.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ observation-window row.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular-rational handoff row assembled from $D$ and $S$.
\item $X$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal available only after $L,U,E,D,S,R$ have been replayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the located cut, lower-real row, upper-envelope row, dyadic tolerance row, stream window, regular readback, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a cut request through $L,U,E,D,S,R,X,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopCutUp$, $\LocatedCutUp$, $\LowerRealUp$, $\UpperRealEnvelopeUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCutUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The accepted carrier has no coordinate for quotient reals, global Dedekind cuts, trichotomy, countable choice, selected decimal expansions, or host completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BishopCut NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-cut-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopCutUp$ carrier $K=(L,U,E,D,S,R,X,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCutUp}$ surface consists of carrier admission for the displayed located-cut row, lower-real and upper-envelope rows, dyadic tolerance ledger, stream window, regular-rational handoff, terminal real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming row. Every accepted read factors through those rows and refuses quotient reals, global Dedekind completeness, trichotomy, countable choice, selected decimal expansions, and host-completeness data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-cut-carrier}. The visible rows are exactly $L,U,E,D,S,R,X,H,C,P,N$, so the local NameCert can admit only those finite rows.

The analytic part begins with the located-cut row $L$ and the lower-real and upper-envelope row $U$. The envelope-consistency row $E$ binds those presentations before dyadic tolerance data in $D$ and stream-window data in $S$ are read.

The Real-facing part is downstream. The row $R$ repacks the displayed window as regular-rational readback, and $X$ seals only that readback. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route without adding a quotient, choice, trichotomy, decimal-selection, or completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopCut located window]
\label{thm:bishop-cut-located-window}
Every located-window read of an accepted $\BishopCutUp$ packet exposes the route
$$
\LocatedCutUp \longrightarrow \LowerRealUp,\UpperRealEnvelopeUp
  \longrightarrow \DyadicRatCoreUp \longrightarrow \StreamNameUp
  \longrightarrow \RegSeqRatUp \longrightarrow \RealUp .
$$
The read is therefore local to $L,U,E,D,S,R,X,H,C,P,N$ and cannot bypass the located-cut ledger by invoking a quotient real, a completed Dedekind cut, trichotomy, countable choice, or host completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a located-window request. By \autoref{def:bishop-cut-carrier}, the request must first inspect $L$ and the paired lower and upper rows in $U$.

The finite comparison data is then forced through $E,D,S$. The envelope-consistency row keeps the lower and upper presentations attached to the located-cut ledger, while the dyadic and stream rows determine the precision window actually in scope.

Finally the handoff row $R$ and the seal row $X$ supply regular-rational and Real-facing readback. Apply \autoref{thm:bishop-cut-namecert-obligations} to see that $H,C,P,N$ preserve this route and that no refused host object has a carrier coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopCut Real handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-cut-real-handoff}
For every accepted $\BishopCutUp$ packet, any $\RealUp$ consumer receives a terminal seal only after the located-cut row, lower-real row, upper-real envelope, dyadic tolerance ledger, stream-name window, and regular-rational readback have all been displayed. The handoff is a finite NameCert route from constructive cut data to a real-name seal, not a claim of Dedekind completeness or quotient-real equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the terminal seal request. The carrier definition makes $X$ the only $\RealUp$ row, and it is listed after $L,U,E,D,S,R$.

Use \autoref{thm:bishop-cut-located-window} to identify the required upstream route. A Real-facing consumer has already passed through the located-cut ledger, lower and upper rows, dyadic tolerance data, stream window, and regular readback before it sees $X$.

The structural rows cannot strengthen the result. They only transport, replay, source, and name the same finite handoff, so the theorem exports a real-name seal while leaving completeness, quotient equality, trichotomy, and choice outside the packet.
\end{proof}
