\begin{theorem}[Candidate decidability trace checker-schedule precedence]
\label{thm:candidate-decidability-trace-checker-schedule-precedence}

A consumer may therefore inspect a decidability candidate only through the displayed local-checker schedule and its finite ledger rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier projection. The rows $\Gamma,t,A$ name the local request, and $S$ is the first row that records how the request is checked.

The judgement row $J$ and bounded comparison row $B$ depend on that schedule. Without the syntax-node order, context extensions, same-term reads, and conversion subcalls listed in $S$, neither row has a displayed local-checker route.

The refusal ledger $F$ is also downstream of $S$. It records which schedule branches fail before any decidability read is exported, so it cannot be detached from the schedule that produced those branches.

The handoff rows $R,N,C$ bind the same finite trace to later strong-normalisation and confluence work. Thus every accepted consumer first reads $S$, and no unrestricted decidability theorem or proof-search oracle is introduced.
\end{proof}
