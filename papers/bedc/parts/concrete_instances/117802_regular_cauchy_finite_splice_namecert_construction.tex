\begin{definition}[Regular Cauchy finite splice carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-finite-splice-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyFiniteSpliceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(k,X,Y,W,D,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $k$ is the unary splice-depth row naming the last replaced prefix index.
\item $X$ is the original $\RegSeqRatUp$ stream row whose tail regularity is being retained.
\item $Y$ is the replacement finite-prefix row, read only through displayed cells at indices $0,\ldots,k$.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row that aligns the original and replacement observations at the splice boundary.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger for the displayed prefix cells and the retained tail comparison.
\item $R$ is the tail $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row consumed after depth $k$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the splice depth, original row, replacement prefix, window, dyadic ledger, tail readback, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the finite splice request through $k,X,Y,W,D,R,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyFiniteSpliceUp$, $\RegularCauchyShiftUp$, $\FinitePrefixStreamUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyFiniteSpliceUp}$ row naming exactly this finite splice packet.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $k,X,Y,W,D,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected host limits, countable choice, host real equality, ambient Real completeness, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy finite splice NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-finite-splice-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchyFiniteSpliceUp$ carrier $S=(k,X,Y,W,D,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyFiniteSpliceUp}$ surface consists of five finite obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item splice-depth exposure: the unary row $k$ is visible before any replacement cell is read;
\item prefix replacement locality: the replacement row $Y$ supplies only the displayed cells at indices $0,\ldots,k$;
\item boundary-window alignment: the $\StreamNameUp$ row $W$ and dyadic ledger $D$ expose the finite splice boundary before the retained tail is consumed;
\item tail readback preservation: the row $R$ reads the post-$k$ tail as regular rational data downstream of the $\RegularCauchyShiftUp$ route;
\item structural locality: the rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve exactly the same splice packet under transport, replay, provenance, and naming.
\end{enumerate}
The obligation surface may cite the finite prefix certificate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-prefix-stream-certificate-namecert} and the regular Cauchy shift route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-shift-namecert}; it cannot add quotient stream equality, selected host limits, countable choice, host real equality, ambient Real completeness, or a bridge certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-finite-splice-carrier}. The finite analytic rows are exactly the splice depth $k$, the source row $X$, the replacement prefix $Y$, the window $W$, the dyadic ledger $D$, and the retained tail readback $R$.

Read the finite prefix certificate and the regular Cauchy shift route. The prefix certificate bounds the replacement cells by $k$, while the shift route ensures that all post-$k$ reads are taken from the displayed tail rather than from an external selector.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ then repack the same finite data under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Since no excluded principle is a coordinate of the carrier or a result of the cited sibling routes, no local NameCert obligation can export quotient stream equality, selected host limits, countable choice, host real equality, ambient Real completeness, or bridge checking.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy finite splice tail stability]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-finite-splice-tail-stability}
Let $S=(k,X,Y,W,D,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RegularCauchyFiniteSpliceUp$ carrier. Every Real or Completion-facing consumer of the spliced name reads the post-$k$ tail by the displayed route
$$
X,Y,k \longmapsto W \longmapsto D \longmapsto R \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus finite prefix replacement preserves the regular Cauchy tail route after depth $k$: the consumer may inspect the replacement prefix and the retained tail readback, but it obtains no quotient equality of streams, no host sequence equality, no selected limiting value, no countable-choice tail selector, and no ambient completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-finite-splice-namecert-obligations}. They force the splice depth, replacement prefix, boundary window, dyadic ledger, and retained readback to remain visible before any downstream real-name use.

Use the finite prefix certificate for the cells up to $k$. Those cells are the only part of the name supplied by $Y$, so the replacement cannot affect a post-$k$ read except through the displayed boundary rows $W$ and $D$.

Use the regular Cauchy shift route for the tail after $k$. The readback row $R$ is downstream of that shift route and therefore preserves the regular tail as displayed RegSeqRat data.

Finally replay the structural rows $H,C,P,N$. They transport and name the same finite route, so the Real or Completion-facing consumer receives a stable tail route and none of the excluded quotient, equality, choice, limit-selection, or completeness principles.
\end{proof}
