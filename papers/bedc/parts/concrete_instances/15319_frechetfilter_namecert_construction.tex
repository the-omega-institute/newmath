\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FrechetFilterUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-frechetfilter-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Kelley, and in the Bishop--Bridges constructive reading of filters, the $\FrechetFilterUp$ packet names the cofinite-tail filter interface used when a sequence tail is read as a finite BEDC filter surface. It refines the finite tail-neighborhood packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-tail-filter-namecert}, records the filter-base rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-filterbase-namecert}, reads the finite schedule rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and hands the same tail route to the sequential-completion interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sequentiallycompletemetric-namecert} before any regular rational readback or Real-facing completion consumer can inspect the route. The chapter records only the finite cofinite-tail carrier and its handoff rows, not Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient completion.

\begin{definition}[FrechetFilter carrier]
\label{def:frechet-filter-carrier}
A $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $U$ is the unary threshold row. It names the displayed tail bound after which the filter request may read sequence data.
\item $T$ is the cofinite tail-neighborhood row. It records finite complements and the tail containment checks supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-tail-filter-namecert}.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row whose finite windows are inspected only after $U$ and $T$ have been consumed.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ row whose tolerance requests are read only through the displayed stream windows and tail threshold.
\item $B$ is the $\FilterBaseUp$ row. It packages the cofinite subsets as a finite filter-base surface and records the common-tail meet operation used by downstream consumers.
\item $Q$ is the $\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff row. It receives the cofinite-tail neighborhood surface after the metric and filter-base rows have been displayed.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the displayed stream windows and tail budgets.
\item $A$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row available only to Real and completion-facing consumers after $U,T,S,M,B,Q,R$ have been replayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for thresholds, tail neighborhoods, StreamName schedule rows, Metric rows, FilterBase rows, CauchyFilter handoff rows, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a filter request through the unary threshold, cofinite tail neighborhood, StreamName schedule, Metric row, FilterBase row, CauchyFilter handoff, RegSeqRat readback, and then the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FrechetFilterUp$, $\FiniteTailFilterUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FrechetFilterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice tail selection, or ambient completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FrechetFilterUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the unary threshold, cofinite tail-neighborhood row, StreamName schedule row, Metric row, FilterBase row, CauchyFilter handoff row, RegSeqRat readback row, Real seal, transport, continuation, provenance, and local naming row;
\item tail-neighborhood admission, requiring every filter request to pass through $U$ and $T$ before it can inspect a stream window;
\item cofinal-tail stability, requiring any two displayed thresholds over the same cofinite-tail surface to have a common displayed tail through the FilterBase meet row $B$;
\item convergence-consumer handoff, requiring the displayed cofinite-tail surface to be the only row exported to $\CauchyFilterUp$ and then to sequential-completion consumers;
\item readback and seal order, requiring $S$ and $R$ to be consumed before the Real seal $A$ can be read;
\item ledger refusal, excluding Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice selectors, and ambient completion.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:frechet-filter-carrier}. The projection exposes exactly the unary threshold, cofinite tail-neighborhood row, StreamName schedule, Metric row, FilterBase row, CauchyFilter handoff, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Tail-neighborhood admission is forced by the continuation row $C$. A filter request reaches the schedule row $S$ only after it has replayed through the unary threshold $U$ and the cofinite tail row $T$, so the packet has no independent infinite-tail coordinate.

The FilterBase row $B$ supplies the common-tail meet for any pair of displayed thresholds over that same cofinite surface. The Cauchy-source row $Q$ is then the only row exported to $\CauchyFilterUp$, and the same route is the handoff offered to sequential-completion consumers.

The readback row $R$ and Real seal $A$ sit downstream of the same route, while $H$ preserves componentwise transport and $P,N$ package only that finite carrier. Hence no Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limit, countable-choice selector, or ambient completion coordinate remains available.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter tail-neighborhood handoff]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-tail-neighborhood-handoff}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. Every displayed cofinite-tail neighborhood read factors through the finite route
$$
U,T \longmapsto S,M,B \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the $\FiniteTailFilterUp$ source supplies the unary threshold and finite-complement rows before the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule, $\MetricUp$ tolerance row, and $\FilterBaseUp$ meet row are allowed to serve any tail window.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a tail-neighborhood consumer. Carrier admission gives the threshold row $U$ and the cofinite tail-neighborhood row $T$ as explicit coordinates of the same $\BHist$ packet.

Replay $C$. The request first reads the unary threshold, then the finite complement and tail-containment data in $T$, and only then reaches the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row $S$, the $\MetricUp$ tolerance row $M$, and the $\FilterBaseUp$ meet row $B$.

Transport by $H$ preserves this order under classifier movement, while $P$ and $N$ name the same local packet. Therefore a tail-neighborhood read cannot bypass the finite threshold and finite-complement surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter common-tail refinement]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-tail-common-refinement}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ and $F'=(U',T',S',M',B',Q',R',A',H',C',P',N')$ be two accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carriers over the same finite-tail filter row. Then the two unary threshold requests admit a displayed common tail threshold $W$ read through that finite-tail row, with $W$ above both $U$ and $U'$ by BEDC row comparison. Every $\StreamNameUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ consumer of the two requests factors through the same finite cofinite-tail surface
$$
U,U',T \longmapsto W \longmapsto S,M,B,Q,R,A .
$$
The refinement exports neither quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice tail selectors, nor ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the two carriers and read their unary threshold rows $U$ and $U'$ over the shared finite-tail filter row. Since the thresholds are displayed unary $\NatUp$ rows, the finite-tail row supplies the BEDC row comparison needed to choose the larger displayed threshold as $W$.

Replay both requests through their continuation rows. The request from $F$ reaches the shared tail surface by first consuming $U$ and $T$, while the request from $F'$ reaches it by first consuming $U'$ and the same finite-tail row. The common threshold $W$ refines both routes, so any tail window admitted after $W$ is admitted for both original requests.

Now apply the handoff route of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-neighborhood-handoff}. A $\StreamNameUp$ consumer reads only the finite cofinite-tail surface determined by $W$ before inspecting schedule data.

For Cauchy and Real-facing consumers, apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-source-boundary} and \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route}. These routes force the $\MetricUp$ row, $\FilterBaseUp$ row, $\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and $\RealUp$ seal to factor through the same common finite tail. Hence the refinement cannot expose quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice selectors, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter Cauchy-source boundary]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-source-boundary}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. Every $\CauchyFilterUp$ source read exported from the packet factors through
$$
U,T,S,M,B \longmapsto Q \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
The Cauchy-filter consumer receives a cofinite-tail source built from unary thresholds, cofinite tail neighborhoods, displayed StreamName schedule rows, Metric tolerance rows, and FilterBase meet rows, with no quotient filter equality, countable choice, or selected limit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from a Cauchy-source request. The carrier exposes the handoff row $Q$, but only as a row downstream of $U,T,S,M,B$.

The continuation row $C$ replays the request through the unary threshold and the cofinite tail-neighborhood row before the schedule, metric, and filter-base rows can be inspected. This replay is exactly the finite cofinite-tail surface that $Q$ hands to $\CauchyFilterUp$.

Since $H$ transports the same coordinates and $P,N$ name no additional source, the packet exports only the displayed cofinite-tail source boundary. Quotient filter equality, countable-choice selection, selected limits, and ambient completion are outside the carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter Cauchy-filter export]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-filter-export}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. Every exported $\CauchyFilterUp$ read factors through
$$
U,T,S,M,B,Q,H,C,P,N .
$$
The export is therefore a displayed cofinite-tail Cauchy-filter source rather than quotient filter equality, countable-choice tail selection, a selected limit, or an ambient completion object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:frechet-filter-carrier}. The CauchyFilter handoff row $Q$ is present only after the unary threshold, cofinite tail-neighborhood, StreamName schedule, Metric tolerance, and FilterBase meet rows have been displayed.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-namecert-obligations}. They identify $Q$ as the sole convergence-consumer handoff and require ledger refusal for quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice selectors, and ambient completion.

Finally replay the tail route through \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-neighborhood-handoff} and the common-tail refinement of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-common-refinement}. The exported read remains anchored in the same finite cofinite-tail surface, with $H,C,P,N$ supplying transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter RegSeqRat tail readback]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-regseqrat-tail-readback}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row exported by $F$ is downstream of the cofinite tail threshold $U$, the cofinite tail-neighborhood row $T$, the schedule row $S$, and the FilterBase meet row $B$. No readback consumer can inspect $R$ as an independent regular rational stream.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier definition. The row $R$ is not a source coordinate; it is the readback row attached to the displayed stream windows and tail budgets.

Use tail-neighborhood handoff to force each stream window through $U$ and $T$ before $S$ is visible. Then use common-tail refinement to show that simultaneous tail requests are compared by a displayed common threshold and by the FilterBase meet row $B$.

The NameCert obligations place readback and seal order after the convergence-consumer handoff. Hence the $\RegSeqRatUp$ consumer receives only a tail-indexed readback row, not an independent stream selector or a completed real endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter completion nonescape]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-completion-nonescape}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. The packet exports no quotient filter equality, selected limit, countable-choice tail selector, $\RealUp$ completeness, or ambient completion. Every completion-facing read must pass through the displayed route
$$
U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N .
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the ledger refusal clause in \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-namecert-obligations}. It excludes quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice selectors, and ambient completion from the local NameCert surface.

Attach the Cauchy-filter export theorem above. It forces any completion-facing request to receive its Cauchy source through $U,T,S,M,B,Q,H,C,P,N$ rather than through an external filter object.

Finally apply the Real and completion consumer route of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route}. The route reaches $A$ only after $Q$ and $R$ have been consumed, so the Real-facing seal is a finite read boundary and not a proof of $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter Real and completion consumer route]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. Every Real or completion-facing consumer of the Frechet filter route factors through
$$
U,T,S,M,B,Q,R \longmapsto A \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer reads unary thresholds, cofinite tail neighborhoods, StreamName schedule rows, Metric tolerance rows, FilterBase meet rows, the CauchyFilter handoff, and RegSeqRat readback before reaching the Real seal. The route does not claim Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a Real or completion-facing consumer. Carrier admission gives access to $A$ only as the seal row downstream of the threshold, tail-neighborhood, schedule, CauchyFilter handoff, and readback rows.

Replay $C$ along the displayed route. The request consumes $U$ and $T$, inspects the schedule row $S$, reads the metric and filter-base rows $M,B$, hands the cofinite-tail source through $Q$, and then reads the regular rational row $R$.

Only after those rows have been consumed can the seal row $A$ be reached. Componentwise transport by $H$ and provenance through $P,N$ preserve the same dependency order, so the packet supplies a finite consumer route rather than Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient completion.
\end{proof}
