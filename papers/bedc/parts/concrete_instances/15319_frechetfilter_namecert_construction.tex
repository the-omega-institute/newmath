\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FrechetFilterUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-frechetfilter-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Kelley, and in the Bishop--Bridges constructive reading of filters, the $\FrechetFilterUp$ packet names the cofinite-tail filter interface used when a sequence tail is read as a finite BEDC filter surface. It refines the finite tail-neighborhood packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-tail-filter-namecert}, records the filter-base rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-filterbase-namecert}, reads the finite schedule rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and hands the same tail route to the sequential-completion interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sequentiallycompletemetric-namecert} before any regular rational readback or Real-facing completion consumer can inspect the route. The chapter records only the finite cofinite-tail carrier and its handoff rows, not Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient completion.

\begin{definition}[FrechetFilter carrier]
\label{def:frechet-filter-carrier}
A $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $U$ is the unary threshold row. It names the displayed tail bound after which the filter request may read sequence data.
\item $T$ is the cofinite tail-neighborhood row. It records finite complements and the tail containment checks supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-tail-filter-namecert}.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row whose finite windows are inspected only after $U$ and $T$ have been consumed.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ row whose tolerance requests are read only through the displayed stream windows and tail threshold.
\item $B$ is the $\FilterBaseUp$ row. It packages the cofinite subsets as a finite filter-base surface and records the common-tail meet operation used by downstream consumers.
\item $Q$ is the $\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff row. It receives the cofinite-tail neighborhood surface after the metric and filter-base rows have been displayed.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the displayed stream windows and tail budgets.
\item $A$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row available only to Real and completion-facing consumers after $U,T,S,M,B,Q,R$ have been replayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for thresholds, tail neighborhoods, StreamName schedule rows, Metric rows, FilterBase rows, CauchyFilter handoff rows, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a filter request through the unary threshold, cofinite tail neighborhood, StreamName schedule, Metric row, FilterBase row, CauchyFilter handoff, RegSeqRat readback, and then the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FrechetFilterUp$, $\FiniteTailFilterUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FrechetFilterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice tail selection, or ambient completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FrechetFilterUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the unary threshold, cofinite tail-neighborhood row, StreamName schedule row, Metric row, FilterBase row, CauchyFilter handoff row, RegSeqRat readback row, Real seal, transport, continuation, provenance, and local naming row;
\item tail-neighborhood admission, requiring every filter request to pass through $U$ and $T$ before it can inspect a stream window;
\item cofinal-tail stability, requiring any two displayed thresholds over the same cofinite-tail surface to have a common displayed tail through the FilterBase meet row $B$;
\item convergence-consumer handoff, requiring the displayed cofinite-tail surface to be the only row exported to $\CauchyFilterUp$ and then to sequential-completion consumers;
\item readback and seal order, requiring $S$ and $R$ to be consumed before the Real seal $A$ can be read;
\item ledger refusal, excluding Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limits, countable-choice selectors, and ambient completion.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:frechet-filter-carrier}. The projection exposes exactly the unary threshold, cofinite tail-neighborhood row, StreamName schedule, Metric row, FilterBase row, CauchyFilter handoff, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Tail-neighborhood admission is forced by the continuation row $C$. A filter request reaches the schedule row $S$ only after it has replayed through the unary threshold $U$ and the cofinite tail row $T$, so the packet has no independent infinite-tail coordinate.

The FilterBase row $B$ supplies the common-tail meet for any pair of displayed thresholds over that same cofinite surface. The Cauchy-source row $Q$ is then the only row exported to $\CauchyFilterUp$, and the same route is the handoff offered to sequential-completion consumers.

The readback row $R$ and Real seal $A$ sit downstream of the same route, while $H$ preserves componentwise transport and $P,N$ package only that finite carrier. Hence no Real completeness, quotient filter equality, selected limit, countable-choice selector, or ambient completion coordinate remains available.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter carrier admission obligation]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-carrier-admission-obligation}
For every accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the rows $U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N$ are the complete admitted source surface for the local $\NameCert_{\FrechetFilterUp}$ packet. In particular every $\FiniteTailFilterUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, transport, continuation, provenance, and local naming read is admitted only through those displayed rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:frechet-filter-carrier}. Its displayed coordinates are exactly the unary threshold, cofinite tail-neighborhood, schedule, metric, filter-base, handoff, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-namecert-obligations}. The carrier-admission clause lists the same rows and the final clause says that no NameCert obligation is stored outside them.

The later handoff and consumer-route theorems use only this admitted surface. In particular \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-neighborhood-handoff}, \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-source-boundary}, and \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route} all replay through subroutes of $U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N$, so no extra source coordinate is available to any consumer.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter cofinal-tail stability obligation]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-cofinal-tail-stability-obligation}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. If two accepted cofinite-tail requests are based on the same finite-tail row $T$, then their displayed thresholds refine through the $\FilterBaseUp$ meet row $B$ before any $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row or $\MetricUp$ tolerance row is read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:frechet-filter-carrier}. The rows $U$ and $T$ present the unary threshold and cofinite tail-neighborhood data, while $B$ is the displayed row where the common-tail meet is stored.

Use \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-namecert-obligations}. Its cofinal-tail stability clause requires two displayed thresholds over the same cofinite-tail surface to pass through the common displayed tail supplied by $B$.

The route is then the one isolated by \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-neighborhood-handoff} and \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-common-refinement}. Those theorems place $S$ and $M$ downstream of $U,T,B$, so schedule and metric consumers cannot read before the FilterBase meet has refined the two requests.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}

The request from $F$ reaches the shared tail surface by first consuming $U$ and $T$, while the request from $F'$ reaches it by first consuming $U'$ and the same finite-tail row. The common threshold $W$ refines both routes, so any tail window admitted after $W$ is admitted for both original requests.

Now apply the handoff route of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-neighborhood-handoff}. A $\StreamNameUp$ consumer reads only the finite cofinite-tail surface determined by $W$ before inspecting schedule data.

For Cauchy and Real-facing consumers, apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-source-boundary} and \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route}. \begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter cofinite meet associativity]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-cofinite-meet-associativity}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. For any three displayed cofinite-tail requests in the same packet, the two iterated meet routes through $B$,
$$
(U_1 \wedge U_2)\wedge U_3
\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad
U_1\wedge (U_2 \wedge U_3),
$$
determine the same displayed $\BHist$ common-tail route for downstream consumers. The associativity claim is only a route-level equality over the carrier rows; it does not assert quotient filter equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the finite closure induction of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-closure-induction} to the ordered triple of displayed cofinite-tail requests. Both parenthesizations consume the same three requests through the same $\FilterBaseUp$ meet row $B$.

Use \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-common-refinement} at each binary meet. In each parenthesization the final threshold is the displayed common tail above all three inputs, so the downstream route begins with the same finite cofinite-tail surface.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-filterbase-meet-obligation}. Since schedule, metric, CauchyFilter, RegSeqRat, Real, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows are read only after $B$, associativity holds at the displayed $\BHist$ route level and does not create quotient filter equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter sequential-completion handoff]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-sequential-completion-handoff}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier. Every $\SequentiallyCompleteMetricUp$ consumer of $F$ receives only the rows
$$
U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N .
$$
In particular the handoff exports a displayed cofinite-tail source, regular rational readback, Real-facing seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming, but no selected limit and no ambient completion witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the Cauchy-source boundary of \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-source-boundary}. It sends any convergence-facing request through $U,T,S,M,B$ before the Cauchy-filter handoff row $Q$ is visible.

Next apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route}. The Real and completion-facing route reaches the readback row $R$ and the seal row $A$ only after the Cauchy-filter handoff has been consumed.

Finally use \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-completion-nonescape}. Since the carrier has no coordinate for a selected limit or ambient completion object, a sequential-completion consumer receives only the displayed finite rows named in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter tail meet monotone refinement]
\label{thm:frechetfilter-tail-meet-monotone-refinement}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier.
For any two displayed cofinite-tail requests read from the finite-tail row
$T$, the $\FilterBaseUp$ meet row $B$ refines them monotonically: enlarging
either unary threshold only enlarges the displayed common-tail threshold, and
every downstream $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row, $\MetricUp$ tolerance row,
$\CauchyFilterUp$ handoff row, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, and $\RealUp$ seal
continues to factor through the same finite cofinite-tail route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The rows $U,T,B$ are the only rows where threshold data,
finite complements, and common-tail meets are displayed before schedule or
metric consumers can read later coordinates.

Apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-tail-common-refinement} to the two
cofinite-tail requests. The common threshold is read through the same finite
tail row, and enlarging either input threshold can only move the displayed
common tail farther along that row.

Now replay downstream consumption through
\autoref{thm:frechet-filter-filterbase-meet-obligation}. Since $S,M,Q,R,A$
are all downstream of $B$, the monotone refinement preserves their route and
does not introduce quotient filter equality, a selected tail, countable
choice, a selected limit, or ambient completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter Cauchy-filter basis cofinal route]
\label{thm:frechetfilter-cauchy-filter-basis-cofinal-route}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier.
Every Cauchy-filter-basis consumer of the packet reads the same cofinal tail
route
$$
U,T \longmapsto B \longmapsto S,M,Q \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
The consumer may use the $\FilterBaseUp$ meet row to refine finite tails, but
it cannot replace the displayed basis by quotient filter equality, selected
representatives, countable-choice tail selection, or an ambient completion
object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the Cauchy-source boundary of
\autoref{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-source-boundary}. It places every
$\CauchyFilterUp$ source read after $U,T,S,M,B$ and before the structural rows
$H,C,P,N$.

Use \autoref{thm:frechetfilter-tail-meet-monotone-refinement}. Any basis
refinement demanded by the Cauchy consumer is a refinement through the same
cofinite-tail meet row $B$, so the basis remains cofinal in the displayed
finite-tail surface.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-cauchy-filter-export}. The export
is the row $Q$ attached to that same route, not a quotient filter or selected
representative. The absent quotient, selection, choice, and completion data
have no carrier coordinates.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter sequential-completion tail exhaustion]
\label{thm:frechetfilter-sequential-completion-tail-exhaustion}
Let $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier.
The sequential-completion handoff exhausts the tail ledger: every
$\SequentiallyCompleteMetricUp$ read consumes the unary threshold row, the
cofinite-tail row, the FilterBase meet row, the StreamName schedule row, the
Metric tolerance row, the Cauchy-filter handoff, the regular rational
readback, the Real seal, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$, with no remaining
tail coordinate outside the displayed packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-sequential-completion-handoff}. It
states that a sequential-completion consumer receives only
$U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N$.

Insert the cofinal basis route from
\autoref{thm:frechetfilter-cauchy-filter-basis-cofinal-route}. This identifies
the Cauchy-filter input to the completion consumer with the same finite
cofinite-tail route through $U,T,B,S,M,Q,H,C,P,N$.

Complete the route with \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-regseqrat-tail-readback}
and \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-completion-nonescape}. The readback row $R$
and seal row $A$ are downstream of the exhausted tail ledger, so the handoff
cannot export quotient filter equality, a selected limit, countable choice, a
selected representative, or ambient completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FrechetFilter tail-ledger refusal]
\label{thm:frechet-filter-tail-ledger-refusal}
For every accepted $\FrechetFilterUp$ carrier $F=(U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local tail ledger refuses quotient filter equality, countable-choice tail selection, selected limits, ambient completion, and $\RealUp$ completeness. Each refused conclusion is absent because it is not one of the carrier coordinates $U,T,S,M,B,Q,R,A,H,C,P,N$ and is not produced by the displayed handoff theorems.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier definition and the NameCert obligations. The carrier contains threshold, tail-neighborhood, schedule, metric, filter-base, Cauchy-filter, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows; the obligation list explicitly records the ledger refusal.

Use the Cauchy-source boundary and sequential-completion handoff above. Both routes preserve the same finite row order and never introduce quotient filter equality, a countable-choice selector, or a selected limit.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:frechet-filter-real-completion-consumer-route}. The Real-facing seal is downstream of the finite readback route, so it is not a proof of $\RealUp$ completeness and cannot be inflated into ambient completion.
\end{proof}
