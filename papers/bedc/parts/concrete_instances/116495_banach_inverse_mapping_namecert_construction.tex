\begin{definition}[Banach inverse-mapping carrier]
\label{def:banach-inverse-mapping-carrier}
A $\BanachInverseMappingUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(X,Y,F,I,M,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source $\BanachUp$ row, including the displayed norm, metric, regular-Cauchy, complete-metric, and source-window ledgers used before an inverse request is admitted.
\item $Y$ is the target $\BanachUp$ row, including the displayed norm, metric, regular-Cauchy, complete-metric, and target-window ledgers used by output-side consumers.
\item $F$ is the $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$ graph row from $X$ to $Y$, carrying endpoint action, linearity, graph compatibility, norm-bound data, and bounded-operator provenance over the same endpoints.
\item $I$ is the bijection and inverse-graph row: its injective side routes equal target observations through the source classifier of $X$, and its surjective side records target windows with displayed source preimage windows.
\item $M$ is the inverse-modulus ledger. For each target tolerance and target-window row, it exposes a finite source tolerance, an operator-bound comparison, and the open-image or graph route needed before the inverse row is public.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,Y,F,I,M$ and for the structural rows that carry inverse-modulus requests.
\item $C$ records $\Cont$ replay from an inverse request through the source and target Banach rows, the bounded-linear graph, the bijection row, the inverse-modulus ledger, and the sibling graph and complete-metric routes.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BanachInverseMappingUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$, $\InverseMappingTheoremUp$, $\FrechetSpaceUp$, $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$, $\BanachFixedPointUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BanachInverseMappingUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an external inverse function, unrestricted operator quotient, selected source representative, nonconstructive open-map principle, or completeness datum not already displayed by $X$ and $Y$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Banach inverse-mapping NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:banach-inverse-mapping-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\BanachInverseMappingUp$ carrier $Q=(X,Y,F,I,M,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BanachInverseMappingUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the complete source and target rows, bounded linear bijection graph exposure through $F$ and $I$, inverse-modulus exposure through $M$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. Every inverse-facing consumer must read the same displayed $X,Y,F,I,M$ rows before a bounded inverse row is public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The source and target rows are $X$ and $Y$, the bounded graph row is $F$, the bijection and inverse-graph row is $I$, and the inverse-modulus ledger is $M$.

The NameCert obligations are rowwise. Endpoint agreement requires $F$, $I$, and $M$ to read the same Banach source and target rows. Transport by $H$ preserves those endpoint rows, while replay by $C$ exposes them in the order used by inverse-facing consumers.

The sibling routes do not add hidden coordinates. The Frechet and locally convex chapters provide neighbouring complete linear-topology reads, the fixed-point and continuous-map chapters provide endpoint and graph disciplines, and the complete-metric chapter supplies only the displayed completeness surface already present in $X$ and $Y$.

Thus the local NameCert surface is exactly the finite route carried by $Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Banach inverse-mapping complete source route]
\label{thm:banach-inverse-mapping-complete-source-route}
Every public read of a $\BanachInverseMappingUp$ inverse-modulus row must pass through the complete source row $X$ and complete target row $Y$ before it can inspect $F$, $I$, or $M$. In particular, a consumer cannot replace the Banach source route by a merely locally convex, Frechet, continuous-map, or fixed-point sibling route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier definition places $X$ and $Y$ before the bounded graph, bijection, and inverse-modulus rows. Any replay of the inverse request through $C$ therefore starts from the displayed Banach endpoints.

The sibling references are dependency routes rather than substitutes. $\FrechetSpaceUp$ and $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$ can supply neighbouring linear-topology data, $\ContinuousMapUp$ can inspect graph continuity, and $\BanachFixedPointUp$ can inspect fixed-point endpoints, but none of those rows contains both Banach endpoint completeness ledgers for this operator.

Hence an accepted inverse-modulus read must expose the complete source and target rows before it reaches $F$, $I$, or $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Banach inverse-modulus non-escape]
\label{thm:banach-inverse-mapping-inverse-modulus-nonescape}
For an accepted $\BanachInverseMappingUp$ carrier, the inverse-modulus ledger $M$ cannot be read as a host inverse or as a selected preimage function. It is public only as a finite route from target tolerance rows through $Y,I,F,X,H,C,P,N$ to displayed source-window and operator-bound data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the inverse-modulus row $M$. Its entries are tolerance requests, source windows, norm-bound comparisons, and graph or open-image handoffs; no entry is a host inverse function or an unrestricted selector.

Surjective information is carried by $I$ and boundedness information by $F$. The ledger $M$ can combine those rows only after the source and target Banach rows have been displayed, and transport and replay preserve that same finite order through $H$ and $C$.

Finally $P$ and $N$ record provenance and local naming for the displayed route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for selected representatives or quotient operator equality, the inverse-modulus data cannot escape into a host inverse object.
\end{proof}
