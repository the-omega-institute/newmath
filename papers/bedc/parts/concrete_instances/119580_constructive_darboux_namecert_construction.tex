\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConstructiveDarbouxUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real analysis, the $\ConstructiveDarbouxUp$ packet records the finite interval-refinement surface behind the intermediate-value route for a continuous real-valued map on a located interval. It consumes the located interval carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}, the continuous-map and modulus rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the sign-bracket and bisection route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intermediatevalue-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the finite Darboux-style interval refinement data used by L10 real-completion consumers; it does not import quotient real equality, trichotomy, host real primitives, a selected root, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Constructive Darboux carrier]
\label{def:constructive-darboux-carrier}
A $\ConstructiveDarbouxUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(I,F,M,B,R,E,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\LocatedIntervalUp$ row, carrying the displayed endpoint windows and interval-cell ledger.
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ row for the inspected map on the interval.
\item $M$ is the continuity-modulus row attached to $F$ and to the requested rational output tolerance.
\item $B$ is the interval-refinement row, storing the finite nested bracket, midpoint, and sign-change data inherited from the $\IntermediateValueUp$ route.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the endpoint values, midpoint values, and tolerance comparisons opened by $B$.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after the interval, continuity, refinement, sign, and readback rows are visible.
\item $S$ is the sign-change and readback row, recording the displayed negative-side and positive-side value witnesses used by the refinement.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the interval, map, modulus, refinement, readback, Real seal, sign, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an intermediate-value request through $I,F,M,B,R,E,S,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConstructiveDarbouxUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\IntermediateValueUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveDarbouxUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $I,F,M,B,R,E,S,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient classes of real names, real trichotomy, host real equality, an unrestricted zero search, a selected root, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive Darboux obligation basis]
\label{thm:constructive-darboux-obligation-basis}
For every accepted $\ConstructiveDarbouxUp$ carrier $D=(I,F,M,B,R,E,S,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveDarbouxUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, located-interval admission, continuous-map admission, continuity-modulus exposure, finite interval-refinement exposure, sign-change and readback exposure, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:constructive-darboux-carrier}. The accepted packet displays one located interval row, one continuous-map row, one modulus row, one finite refinement row, one regular-rational readback row, one Real seal row, one sign-change row, and the structural rows.

The interval and map rows are the entry data. The located interval row $I$ fixes the endpoint windows and interval cells, while $F$ and $M$ expose only a displayed continuity-modulus route for the requested tolerance.

The refinement and sign rows are then finite. Row $B$ records the retained bracket and midpoint route, row $S$ records the sign-change/readback witnesses, and row $R$ supplies regular rational comparisons before the terminal seal $E$ is read.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since no other coordinate is present, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface and exclude quotient equality, trichotomy, host real primitives, selected roots, and ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive Darboux scope]
\label{thm:constructive-darboux-scope}
Every $\RealUp$ or intermediate-value consumer of an accepted $\ConstructiveDarbouxUp$ packet must first read the finite route
$$
\LocatedIntervalUp,\ \ContinuousMapUp,\ M,\ B,\ S,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RealUp .
$$
The route supplies interval-refinement, continuity-modulus, sign-change/readback, terminal Real seal, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and local $\NameCert$ rows. It does not assert quotient real equality, real trichotomy, host real equality, a selected root, a host intermediate-value theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the obligation basis in \autoref{thm:constructive-darboux-obligation-basis}. It orders any consumer read through the located interval, continuous-map, continuity-modulus, refinement, sign, readback, Real seal, and structural rows.

The intermediate-value sibling contributes only the displayed bisection and sign-bracket route. Thus a consumer can inspect $B$ and $S$ as finite interval-refinement evidence, but it cannot replace them with a host theorem, a trichotomy decision, or an unbounded zero search.

The regular-rational and Real rows are terminal for this packet. Row $R$ supplies finite comparisons for endpoint and midpoint values, while $E$ seals the resulting Real-facing read only after $I,F,M,B,S$ have been displayed.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Therefore every consumer remains inside the finite Darboux packet and receives none of the excluded host or quotient data.
\end{proof}
