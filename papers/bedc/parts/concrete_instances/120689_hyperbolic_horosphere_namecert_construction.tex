Following classical hyperbolic geometry, the $\HyperbolicHorosphereUp$ packet realizes the level-set side of the observer-expansion vision of \autoref{ch:visions-hyperbolic-phase-geometry-observer-expansion} as finite NameCert data. It sits after the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-metric-namecert}, the Busemann boundary route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-busemann-boundary-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}. The packet names a displayed boundary point and Busemann level surface; it does not introduce a host ideal boundary quotient, a completed horoball compactification, a selected geodesic representative, or a physical observer trajectory.

\begin{definition}[Hyperbolic horosphere carrier]
\label{def:hyperbolic-horosphere-carrier}
A $\HyperbolicHorosphereUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(M,B,L,R,O,E,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\HyperbolicMetricUp$ metric-read row for the source observations.
\item $B$ is the $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$ boundary-point and Busemann-read row.
\item $L$ is the displayed Busemann level row naming the horosphere slice.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ level-seal row used to compare the displayed Busemann value with $L$.
\item $O$ is the observer-window row, recording which finite metric and Busemann observations are available.
\item $E$ is the non-escape row excluding ideal-boundary quotient equality, completed horoball compactification, selected geodesic representatives, and physical observer motion.
\item $T$ is the transport row preserving the boundary point, Busemann level, metric readback, observer window, and Real seal under componentwise readback.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for metric, boundary, level, seal, window, non-escape, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through metric admission, Busemann boundary readback, level comparison, observer-window inspection, Real sealing, and non-escape.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\HyperbolicHorosphereUp$, $\HyperbolicMetricUp$, $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\HyperbolicHorosphereUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports horosphere data only through $M,B,L,R,O,E,T,H,C,P,N$. It contains no coordinate for a host boundary quotient, a global foliation of hyperbolic space, a completed compactification, a selected geodesic ray, or an observer-substance reading.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic horosphere NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hyperbolic-horosphere-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\HyperbolicHorosphereUp$ carrier
$$
S=(M,B,L,R,O,E,T,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\HyperbolicHorosphereUp}$ surface consists exactly of hyperbolic metric admission, Busemann boundary admission, displayed level exposure, Real seal comparison, observer-window exposure, non-escape refusal, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hyperbolic-horosphere-carrier}. The metric row $M$ and Busemann boundary row $B$ expose the source and boundary data before any horosphere read is made.

The rows $L$ and $R$ form the level surface: $L$ names the displayed Busemann level, while $R$ gives the Real-facing comparison boundary for that level. The observer-window row $O$ records the finite observations available to a consumer.

The refusal and structural rows $E,T,H,C,P,N$ preserve this same route and exclude boundary quotients, compactification, selected geodesics, and observer-substance readings. Hence the displayed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic horosphere Busemann route]
\label{thm:hyperbolic-horosphere-busemann-route}
Every public read from an accepted $\HyperbolicHorosphereUp$ carrier to a Busemann-level statement factors through the $\HyperbolicMetricUp$ row $M$, the $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$ row $B$, the displayed level row $L$, the $\RealUp$ seal row $R$, and the observer-window row $O$ before the transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows are consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the metric and Busemann source rows first. The metric read $M$ supplies the finite hyperbolic distance observations, and $B$ supplies the boundary point together with the Busemann readback route.

The level row $L$ is interpreted only through the Real seal row $R$ and the observer-window row $O$. This makes the horosphere a displayed level surface rather than a quotient boundary object.

The rows $T,H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same Busemann-level route. Since the carrier contains no global compactification or selected geodesic coordinate, every Busemann read must factor through the displayed rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic horosphere non-escape]
\label{thm:hyperbolic-horosphere-nonescape}
An accepted $\HyperbolicHorosphereUp$ carrier cannot export host ideal-boundary quotient equality, completed horoball compactification, a selected geodesic representative, global horosphere foliation, or physical observer motion. All exported data are confined to
$$
M,B,L,R,O,E,T,H,C,P,N .
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier inventory of \autoref{def:hyperbolic-horosphere-carrier}. The only geometric source rows are the metric row $M$, the Busemann boundary row $B$, the level row $L$, the Real seal row $R$, and the observer-window row $O$.

The non-escape row $E$ records the excluded host readings, while $T,H,C,P,N$ preserve only the displayed finite route. No carrier coordinate names a boundary quotient, compactification, selected geodesic, global foliation, or observer motion.

Therefore a consumer can extract only the finite horosphere rows and their replay/provenance data. The excluded host objects have no row from which they can be read.
\end{proof}
