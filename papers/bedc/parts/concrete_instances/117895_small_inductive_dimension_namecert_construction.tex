\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$}

Following Hurewicz--Wallman and Engelking, the $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$ packet records the boundary-recursive small inductive dimension as finite BEDC separation and cover data. It sits beside the finite cover-order carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-coveringdimension-namecert}, the Lebesgue refinement route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lebesgue-covering-dimension-namecert}, the topology source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the normal-space separation packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, and the finite refinement carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-cover-refinement-namecert}. The packet names only displayed boundary neighborhoods, finite boundary-cover rows, refinement comparisons, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not assert a host inductive-dimension theorem, arbitrary open-cover compactness, selected separators outside the carrier, or ambient quotient topology equality.

\begin{definition}[Small inductive dimension carrier]
\label{def:small-inductive-dimension-carrier}
A $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(X,N,B,R,F,L,H,C,P,A)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row, exposing the finite point, open-neighborhood, closed-set, and boundary observations inspected by the packet.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ separation row, recording the closed-pair separation route used to choose a displayed neighborhood before its boundary is inspected.
\item $B$ is the boundary-cover row, recording the finite $\BHist$ boundary entries whose own dimension read is one step lower than the surrounding packet.
\item $R$ is the boundary-recursion ledger, recording that each boundary read is consumed only through the displayed topology and normal-space rows.
\item $F$ is the $\FiniteCoverRefinementUp$ comparison row, recording the finite refinement handoff used when boundary covers are compared with cover-order packets.
\item $L$ is the dimension-neighbor comparison row, recording the finite comparison with $\CoveringDimensionUp$ and $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp$ without identifying the three dimension notions.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for topology, normal-space, boundary, recursion, refinement, comparison, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from a topology-neighborhood request through normal-space separation, boundary cover inspection, finite refinement comparison, and dimension-neighbor handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\NormalSpaceUp$, $\CoveringDimensionUp$, $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp$, $\FiniteCoverRefinementUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $A$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SmallInductiveDimensionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through the displayed rows $X,N,B,R,F,L,H,C,P,A$. It has no coordinate for a host dimension theorem, arbitrary open-cover compactness, a selected separating family outside the packet, quotient-topology equality, countable choice, or an ambient boundary oracle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Small inductive dimension NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:small-inductive-dimension-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$ carrier
$$
S=(X,N,B,R,F,L,H,C,P,A),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\SmallInductiveDimensionUp}$ surface consists exactly of topology-source admission through $X$, normal-space separation exposure through $N$, finite boundary-cover admission through $B$, boundary-recursion exactness through $R$, finite refinement comparison through $F$, covering-dimension and Lebesgue-covering comparison through $L$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:small-inductive-dimension-carrier}. The source, separation, boundary, recursion, refinement, comparison, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows are precisely the displayed coordinates of the finite packet.

The boundary-recursive part is read from $X,N,B,R$. A consumer first sees the topology source, then the normal-space separation row, and only then the boundary-cover rows whose lower-dimensional reads are stored by the recursion ledger.

The finite comparison part is read from $F,L$. These rows permit comparison with finite cover-refinement, covering-dimension, and Lebesgue-covering packets, but they do not identify those packets with the small inductive dimension carrier.

Finally $H,C,P,A$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since none of the refused host objects is a carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Small inductive dimension boundary route]
\label{thm:small-inductive-dimension-boundary-route}
Every boundary-recursive read of an accepted $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$ packet must factor through
$$
\TopologyUp,\ \NormalSpaceUp,\ B,\ R,\ H,\ C,\ P,\ A
$$
before a lower-dimensional boundary claim is consumed. The route exposes a displayed neighborhood-separation row and a finite boundary-cover row; it exports no selected separating family outside the carrier, no host boundary oracle, no quotient-topology equality, and no countable-choice schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the topology source row $X$. It supplies the displayed point, open-neighborhood, closed-set, and boundary observations available to the packet.

Use the normal-space separation row $N$ next. The boundary that enters the recursion ledger is admitted only after the separation route is visible, so a consumer cannot replace it by an unrecorded host separator.

The rows $B$ and $R$ then record the finite boundary-cover entries and their lower-dimensional reads. Structural rows $H,C,P,A$ preserve, replay, source, and name this route, so the boundary claim remains inside the displayed packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Small inductive dimension cover comparison]
\label{thm:small-inductive-dimension-cover-comparison}
Every finite-cover comparison exported by an accepted $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$ packet must read the route
$$
\FiniteCoverRefinementUp,\ \CoveringDimensionUp,\ \LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp
\longmapsto
F,L,H,C,P,A .
$$
The comparison is a finite handoff from boundary-recursive rows to cover-refinement and cover-order rows. It does not identify small inductive dimension with covering dimension, does not claim equality with Lebesgue covering dimension, and does not import arbitrary open-cover compactness or a host dimension theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:small-inductive-dimension-namecert-obligations}. They require the topology source, normal-space separation row, finite boundary-cover row, and recursion ledger before any comparison is exported.

Read the finite refinement row $F$. It supplies only the displayed refinement handoff, so cover comparison is routed through $\FiniteCoverRefinementUp$ rather than through arbitrary open-cover compactness.

Finally inspect the neighbor comparison row $L$. It permits cover-order and Lebesgue-covering consumers to see the same finite boundary-refinement route while keeping $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$, $\CoveringDimensionUp$, and $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp$ as distinct packet names. The structural rows $H,C,P,A$ then transport, replay, source, and name the comparison without adding a host theorem.
\end{proof}
