\begin{theorem}[Finite product uniform-modulus NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-product-uniform-modulus-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteProductUniformModulusUp$ carrier
$$
K=(T,C,M,F,L,D,R,E,U,H,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteProductUniformModulusUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite product compactness admission, compact metric product exposure, product-metric triangle routing, continuous-map coordinate modulus exposure, finite coordinate lower-bound folding through Rat and Dyadic tolerance rows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, $\UniformModulusUp$ export, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-product-uniform-modulus-carrier}. The product and compactness rows $T,C,M$ expose the finite product source and its metric route.

The continuous-map and coordinate modulus rows $F,L$ provide the local tolerance data. The fold row $D$ is the only row that combines the finitely many coordinate lower bounds into one product tolerance.

The readback, Real seal, and export rows $R,E,U$ then present the tolerance-only uniform-modulus handoff. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same packet, and the carrier has no hidden coordinate for the excluded product or choice principles.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite product uniform-modulus export]
\label{thm:finite-product-uniform-modulus-export}
Every $\UniformModulusUp$ consumer of an accepted $\FiniteProductUniformModulusUp$ packet reads the product modulus through the route
$$
\TychonoffFiniteProductUp,\ \CompactMetricProductUp,\ \CompactMetricUp,\ \ProductMetricUp,\ \ContinuousMapUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RealUp,\ \UniformModulusUp .
$$

No consumer may replace this route by arbitrary product compactness, a selected finite-net oracle, quotient function equality, or an external modulus selector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite product compactness rows $T$ and $C$. They expose a finite compact product route and do not supply an arbitrary product theorem.

Use the product-metric row $M$ together with the continuous-map row $F$. Projection and triangle reads are kept visible while the coordinate local moduli are inspected through $L$.

Fold the coordinate tolerances through $D$ and read the resulting comparisons through $R$ and $E$. This produces one product tolerance inside the same finite packet.

Finally export through $U,H,P,N$. The $\UniformModulusUp$ row is therefore a handoff from displayed product, compactness, coordinate-modulus, lower-bound, readback, Real-seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows, not an external selector.
