Following Bolzano and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\BolzanoTheoremUp$ packet records the finite zero-crossing certificate used by Real and Completion consumers when a continuous real-valued map changes sign on a displayed interval. It sits after the bisection route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intermediate-value-bisection-namecert}, the Darboux surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-darboux-theorem-namecert}, the continuous-map carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only the displayed interval, endpoint-sign, continuity-window, bisection, Darboux, tolerance, readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert rows; it does not import host completeness, trichotomy, excluded middle, selected roots, quotient real equality, or an ambient theorem about arbitrary functions.

\begin{definition}[Bolzano theorem carrier]
\label{def:bolzano-theorem-carrier}
A $\BolzanoTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Z=(I,F,L,U,B,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the closed interval row over the real seal surface, displaying the two endpoints and the interval membership window inspected by the theorem packet;
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ row for the real-valued map whose zero-crossing read is being requested;
\item $L$ and $U$ are endpoint-sign rows, recording opposite sign evidence at the two displayed endpoints through finite rational readbacks;
\item $B$ is the $\IntermediateValueBisectionUp$ row that stores the finite bisection schedule used to narrow the sign-changing interval;
\item $D$ is the $\DarbouxTheoremUp$ row that licenses the intermediate-value route only through displayed interval data and continuity windows;
\item $Q$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger for the bisection mesh, endpoint comparisons, and zero-window request;
\item $E$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row together with the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for the displayed zero candidate;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval, map, endpoint-sign, bisection, Darboux, tolerance, readback, replay, provenance, and naming rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the continuous-map row, the endpoint-sign rows, the bisection schedule, the Darboux route, the dyadic tolerance ledger, and the real readback seal;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BolzanoTheoremUp$, $\IntermediateValueBisectionUp$, $\DarbouxTheoremUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BolzanoTheoremUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports no selected root independent of the displayed bisection route, no host real completeness theorem, no quotient equality of real names, no trichotomy principle, and no arbitrary intermediate-value theorem beyond the finite rows in $Z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bolzano theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bolzano-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:bolzano-theorem-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\BolzanoTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of five obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for $Z=(I,F,L,U,B,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$;
\item source exactness, requiring the interval row, continuous-map row, and endpoint-sign rows to be displayed before any zero-crossing read is consumed;
\item bisection and Darboux exactness, requiring $B$ and $D$ to expose the finite interval-refinement route and its intermediate-value handoff;
\item tolerance and readback stability through the dyadic ledger $Q$, regular rational readback, real seal $E$, componentwise transport $H$, and replay row $C$;
\item non-escape, excluding host completeness, excluded middle, trichotomy, selected roots, quotient real equality, and arbitrary function-space theorems outside $I,F,L,U,B,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:bolzano-theorem-carrier}. The tuple lists the interval, map, endpoint-sign, bisection, Darboux, tolerance, readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

The source-exactness row reads the interval and map before any zero-crossing claim is public. The endpoint signs are finite readbacks attached to the same displayed interval, so they cannot be replaced by a host order principle or an unstated trichotomy theorem.

The bisection and Darboux rows supply the constructive route. The bisection schedule narrows the sign-changing interval through finite steps, while the Darboux row records that the intermediate-value handoff is consumed only through the displayed continuity windows.

Finally $Q,E,H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route through tolerance accounting, rational readback, real sealing, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since no other coordinate occurs in the carrier, the listed non-escape clauses exhaust the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bolzano theorem intermediate-value route]
\label{thm:bolzano-theorem-ivp-route}
Every accepted $\BolzanoTheoremUp$ consumer that reads a zero-crossing claim must pass through the finite route
$$
\ContinuousMapUp,\quad \IntermediateValueBisectionUp,\quad \DarbouxTheoremUp,\quad \DyadicRatCoreUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp .
$$
In carrier notation this is the route through $F,B,D,Q,E,H,C,P,N$ after the interval and endpoint-sign rows $I,L,U$ have been displayed. The route exports a zero-window certificate for the named packet only; it exports no selected root, host completeness theorem, quotient real equality, trichotomy, excluded middle, or arbitrary intermediate-value theorem for all continuous maps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted zero-crossing read. The carrier has no direct root-selection coordinate, so the consumer must first read the displayed interval $I$, continuous map $F$, and endpoint-sign rows $L,U$.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bolzano-theorem-namecert-obligations}. They force the bisection row $B$ and Darboux row $D$ to appear before the zero-window read can be consumed.

The dyadic tolerance ledger $Q$ and readback seal $E$ then supply the finite comparison data visible to Real and Completion consumers. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that same route, so the export remains the displayed Bolzano packet rather than a host theorem or a selected root.
\end{proof}
