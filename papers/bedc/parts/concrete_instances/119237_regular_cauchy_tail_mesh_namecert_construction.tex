Following Bishop and Bridges on regular Cauchy reals, the $\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp$ packet records the finite shared-tail mesh read when several regular rational names are compared before a $\RealUp$ handoff. It sits over the finite stream schedules of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the cofinal shared-window route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cofinal-tail-mesh-namecert}. The packet names a regular Cauchy tail mesh; it does not quotient streams, select limits, invoke trichotomy, import choice, or assert ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Regular Cauchy tail mesh carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-tail-mesh-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(S,R,D,Q,T,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window schedule used by every participating regular name.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback row attached to those windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used for every mesh comparison.
\item $Q$ is the cofinal shared-tail row read through $\CofinalTailMeshUp$.
\item $T$ is the regular Cauchy tail row recording the common precision tail on which the readbacks are compared.
\item $W$ is the Real-facing window row exposed after $S,R,D,Q,T$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $S,R,D,Q,T,W$ and for the structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from each finite precision request through the stream windows, regular readbacks, dyadic ledger, cofinal tail row, regular tail row, and Real-facing window.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CofinalTailMeshUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through the displayed rows $S,R,D,Q,T,W,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, a selected regular Cauchy representative, a trichotomy decision, countable choice, a host cofinal subsequence, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy tail mesh NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-tail-mesh-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp$ carrier $M=(S,R,D,Q,T,W,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, StreamName window scheduling, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance accounting, CofinalTailMesh shared-tail admission, regular Cauchy tail comparison, Real-facing window handoff, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-tail-mesh-carrier}. The visible inventory is $S,R,D,Q,T,W,H,C,P,N$, and every row has a fixed role in the finite mesh.

The stream and rational rows are read first. Row $S$ opens the inspected finite windows, while $R$ supplies the regular rational readback for those same windows. The row $D$ records the dyadic tolerance ledger against which the participating readbacks are compared.

Next the shared-tail data is displayed. Row $Q$ imports only the cofinal tail-mesh route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cofinal-tail-mesh-namecert}, and row $T$ records the regular Cauchy tail comparison used by this packet. The window $W$ is downstream of those rows, so a Real-facing consumer cannot read it before the finite mesh has been exposed.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since the carrier contains no quotient stream equality, selected representative, trichotomy decision, countable choice, host cofinal subsequence, or ambient completeness coordinate, those objects are not local obligations of the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy tail mesh Real handoff]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-tail-mesh-real-handoff}
Every $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\RegularCauchyTailMeshUp$ carrier receives the finite route
$$
S \longrightarrow R \longrightarrow D \longrightarrow Q \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow W \longrightarrow H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer reads StreamName windows, RegSeqRat tails, a DyadicRatCore tolerance ledger, the CofinalTailMesh shared-tail row, and the regular Cauchy tail comparison before any terminal Real seal may be consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier definition. The Real-facing row is $W$, and it appears only after the stream schedule, regular rational readback, dyadic ledger, cofinal shared-tail row, and regular Cauchy tail row have been displayed.

Apply \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-tail-mesh-namecert-obligations}. It makes the route through $S,R,D,Q,T$ part of the local NameCert surface, so a Real consumer cannot replace the route by an external representative, a chosen limit, or a host completeness proof.

The transport and replay rows preserve the same finite order for downstream consumers, while $P$ and $N$ name the provenance and local certificate. The handoff is therefore the displayed mesh route rather than a quotient completion or a choice-based selection of a tail.
\end{proof}
