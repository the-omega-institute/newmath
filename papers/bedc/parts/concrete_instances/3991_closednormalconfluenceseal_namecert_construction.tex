\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-closednormalconfluenceseal-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ packet turns the normal-source branch of
\autoref{ch:metacic-confluence-audit-map} into finite BEDC data. It is placed
after the typed reduction and preservation surfaces of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-typedreductionnormalform-namecert} and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subjectreduction-namecert}: the present packet
records the closed normal source, two visible beta-star routes, their displayed
join, and the boundary row that prevents this normal-source seal from being
read as full closed Church--Rosser.

\begin{definition}[Closed normal confluence seal carrier]
\label{def:closed-normal-confluence-seal-carrier}
A $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Z=(t,n,r_1,r_2,j,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $t$ is the closed MetaCIC term row serving as the source of both
beta-star routes;
\item $n$ is the beta-normality witness row for $t$;
\item $r_1$ is the first displayed $\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$ route from $t$ to a
target row;
\item $r_2$ is the second displayed $\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$ route from $t$ to a
target row;
\item $j$ is the join row supplied by the normal-source confluence theorem,
recording the common target and the two residual beta-star routes into it;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transports for the source,
normality, route, join, provenance, and local certificate rows;
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ routes by which consumers read the
closedness witness, the normality witness, the two beta-star routes, and the
join row;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over MetaCIC closed terms, beta normality,
$\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$, normal-source confluence, the closed-substitution
compatibility obstruction, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and sibling
$\NameCert$ packets;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for arbitrary closed-source confluence, no proof
of $\mathrm{BetaParallelSubstitutionCompatibility}$, no closed parallel diamond
for all closed terms, and no hidden subject-reduction or normalization oracle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal normal source]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-normal-source}
Every accepted $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ carrier exposes confluence only
from the closed beta-normal source row $t$. A consumer may read $t,n,r_1,r_2,j$
through $H,C,P,N$ and obtain the displayed join of the two beta-star routes,
but it cannot use the packet as a confluence theorem for an arbitrary closed
source.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealNormalSource}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of
\autoref{def:closed-normal-confluence-seal-carrier}. The source row $t$ and the
normality witness row $n$ are not auxiliary decoration: they are the hypotheses
that select the normal-source theorem named in
\autoref{ch:metacic-confluence-audit-map}.

The routes $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the only beta-star routes exposed by the packet,
and the join row $j$ is read only as their common normal-source join. The
transport and route rows $H$ and $C$ replay this finite surface without adding
new sources.

The sibling packets provide the typed-route and preservation discipline around
the read. \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-typedreductionnormalform-namecert}
keeps terminal route access finite, while
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subjectreduction-namecert} keeps target
membership reads behind the displayed preservation boundary.

Since no coordinate supplies an arbitrary closed-source theorem, a closed
parallel diamond, or a substitution-compatibility discharge, the accepted seal
cannot be repacked into full closed Church--Rosser.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal join-window inversion]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-join-window-inversion}
For every accepted $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ carrier
$Z=(t,n,r_1,r_2,j,H,C,P,N)$, any consumer that reads the join row $j$ must first
expose the closed source row $t$, the beta-normality row $n$, and both displayed
$\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$ routes $r_1,r_2$. Conversely, the row $j$ exports only
the common target and residual routes attached to those two displayed routes.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealJoinWindowInversion}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of
\autoref{def:closed-normal-confluence-seal-carrier}. The join coordinate $j$ is
not an independent global confluence field; it is listed after the closed source
$t$, the beta-normality witness $n$, and the displayed routes $r_1,r_2$.

Apply \autoref{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-normal-source}. It fixes the
available source as the displayed closed beta-normal row and keeps both
beta-star routes visible before the join is read.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only this
finite window. Therefore a consumer of $j$ recovers precisely the common target
and residual routes attached to $r_1,r_2$, while arbitrary closed
Church--Rosser, closed parallel diamond, substitution compatibility, subject
reduction, and normalization remain outside the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal public export]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-public-export}
The public export of an accepted $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ packet is
exactly the displayed closed beta-normal source row $t$, the beta-normality
witness row $n$, the two beta-star route rows $r_1,r_2$, the join row $j$, and
the structural surface $H,C,P,N$ for componentwise $\hsame$ transport,
displayed $\Cont$ replay, package provenance, and local naming. This export
contains no arbitrary closed Church--Rosser theorem, no
$\mathrm{BetaParallelSubstitutionCompatibility}$ discharge, no closed parallel
diamond, and no subject-reduction or normalization oracle.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealPublicExport}
\begin{proof}
Start with the normal-source theorem
\autoref{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-normal-source}. It identifies the
source of the export as the displayed closed beta-normal row $t$ together with
the normality witness $n$ and the two beta-star route rows $r_1,r_2$.

The join row $j$ is therefore read only as the join for those two displayed
routes. The transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve
that finite surface for public consumers without adding a new source or a
substitution theorem.

The carrier of \autoref{def:closed-normal-confluence-seal-carrier} has no
coordinate for arbitrary closed Church--Rosser, closed parallel diamond,
subject reduction, or normalization. Hence the public export is exactly the
finite normal-source seal and not any of the excluded global endpoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal substitution boundary]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-substitution-boundary}
The $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ carrier separates the available
normal-source join from the substitution-compatibility boundary. Agreement on
$t,n,r_1,r_2,j,H,C,P,N$ records the checked closed-normal confluence read, but
it does not produce $\mathrm{BetaParallelSubstitutionCompatibility}$,
$\mathrm{BetaParallelDiamondClosed}$, or arbitrary closed-term confluence for
$\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealSubstitutionBoundary}
\begin{proof}
Read the provenance row $P$. It names the checked normal-source confluence
surface and also names the closed-substitution compatibility obstruction
recorded by \autoref{ch:metacic-confluence-audit-map}.

These two entries have different roles. The normal-source entry justifies the
join row $j$ for the displayed closed beta-normal source, while the obstruction
entry keeps the substitution interface visible as a boundary rather than as a
field filled by the carrier.

The structural rows $H,C,N$ preserve this separation. They transport and replay
the finite seal rows, but no row in the carrier contains the substitution
compatibility proof, the closed parallel diamond, or the general closed
star-confluence endpoint.

Therefore the packet is a local confluence seal for closed normal sources. It
is not a certificate for the substitution-sensitive branch that would be needed
to close arbitrary closed-term Church--Rosser.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal route stability]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-route-stability}
If two accepted $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ carriers agree componentwise
along the displayed $\hsame$ row, every permitted $\Cont$ replay of the
normal-source route for one carrier transports to the corresponding replay of
the other carrier.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealRouteStability}
\begin{proof}
Read the componentwise transport row $H$. It identifies the closed source,
normality witness, route rows, join row, provenance row, and local certificate
row only along the displayed $\hsame$ surface.

Now replay through $C$. Each permitted continuation reads the same finite
coordinates in the same order: closedness and normality first, then the two
beta-star routes, and finally the join row. Transport through $H$ therefore
sends any such read to the corresponding read of the transported packet.

The proof uses no external confluence theorem beyond the already displayed
normal-source join. Stability is a property of the finite replay route, not a
new Church--Rosser endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal scoped route]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-scoped-route}
Every permitted consumer route out of an accepted
$\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ carrier factors through the displayed closed
source $t$, beta-normality row $n$, two $\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$ rows $r_1,r_2$,
join row $j$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay
$C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, local $\NameCert_{\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp}$
row $N$, and the single-carrier alignment surface named by
BEDC.Derived.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp.ClosedNormalConfluenceSealTasteGate\_single\_carrier\_alignment.
No such route bypasses this finite chain to obtain arbitrary closed
Church--Rosser data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted carrier and project the rows listed in
\autoref{def:closed-normal-confluence-seal-carrier}. The only source available
to a consumer is the closed row $t$, and the only normality evidence available
for that source is $n$.

The replay then factors through the two displayed beta-star rows $r_1,r_2$ and
the join row $j$. The transport row $H$ can move the route only componentwise,
and the continuation row $C$ can replay only the already displayed closed
source, normality, route, and join coordinates.

The provenance row $P$ and local certificate row $N$ bind this route to the
same NameCert packet. The TasteGate single-carrier alignment identifies the
accepted carrier surface used by the route, so a consumer cannot change carrier
shape while preserving acceptance.

Thus the permitted route is exactly the finite route through the displayed
closed source, normality row, beta-star rows, join row, transport, replay,
provenance, local naming, and TasteGate alignment. Nothing in the factorization
supplies substitution compatibility, a closed parallel diamond, or full closed
Church--Rosser.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed normal confluence seal kernel scope]
\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-kernel-scope}
The kernel scope of the $\ClosedNormalConfluenceSealUp$ packet is exactly the
BEDC infrastructure $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ together
with the MetaCIC surfaces for closed-term rows, beta-normality rows,
$\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$ rows, typed reduction, and subject reduction. The scope
does not include substitution compatibility, a closed parallel diamond, a
normalization oracle, or full closed Church--Rosser.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier coordinates. The structural rows are $H,C,P,N$, and their
ambient BEDC primitives are precisely $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and
$\NameCert$.

The computational rows are $t,n,r_1,r_2,j$. They name a closed MetaCIC source, a
beta-normality witness, two $\mathrm{BetaStarStep}$ routes, and the displayed
join supplied for that normal source. The sibling typed-reduction and
subject-reduction chapters delimit how these rows may be read by consumers.

No remaining coordinate names substitution compatibility, a closed parallel
diamond, arbitrary normalization, or a general Church--Rosser theorem. The
scope is therefore the displayed BEDC infrastructure plus the listed MetaCIC
surfaces, and nothing beyond them.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:closed-normal-confluence-seal-boundary-nonescape}

Church--Rosser boundary named by the carrier. The packet can expose the local
normal-source seal, but it cannot escape to the missing closed parallel diamond
or arbitrary closed-term confluence endpoint.
\end{proof}
