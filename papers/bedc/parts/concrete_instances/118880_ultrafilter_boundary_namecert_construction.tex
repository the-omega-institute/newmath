\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\UltrafilterBoundaryUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-ultrafilter-boundary-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Kelley filter terminology, the $\UltrafilterBoundaryUp$ packet records the finite boundary at which a filter or completion argument would have to leave BEDC's displayed rows in order to invoke a maximal filter. It sits beside the constructive refusal surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-ultrafilter-boundary-namecert} and the choice boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-countable-choice-boundary-namecert}, and it is read by L10 completion routes such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-completion-criterion-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchyfilter-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completion-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}. The packet is only a finite refusal boundary; it is not an ultrafilter existence theorem, not a Boolean-prime-ideal theorem, and not a selected completion shortcut.

\begin{definition}[Ultrafilter boundary carrier]
\label{def:ultrafilter-boundary-carrier}
An $\UltrafilterBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
U=(F,B,Q,L,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is the inspected filter row, recording the finite base members and refinement requests exposed by a Cauchy-filter or completion-facing argument.
\item $B$ is the finite Boolean-branch ledger, listing the finite subset or complement tests explicitly requested before a maximality claim would be needed.
\item $Q$ is the quotient-refusal row, recording that no quotient Boolean algebra representative or host equality of filters is exported.
\item $L$ is the L10 handoff row, tying any completion-facing read to $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\CompletionUp$, and $\StreamNameUp$ windows before the boundary is consumed.
\item $R$ is the ultrafilter-refusal row. It records that the packet does not produce a maximal filter, an ultrafilter extension, a prime ideal, a countable-choice schedule, or a host-side selector.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $F,B,Q,L,R,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from finite filter and completion rows to the refusal boundary.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\UltrafilterBoundaryUp$, $\ConstructiveUltrafilterBoundaryUp$, $\CountableChoiceBoundaryUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\CompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\UltrafilterBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $F,B,Q,L,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for maximality, quotient completion equality, ultrafilter existence, or hidden choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite boundary route]
\label{thm:ultrafilter-boundary-finite-route}
For every accepted $\UltrafilterBoundaryUp$ carrier $U=(F,B,Q,L,R,H,C,P,N)$, every completion-facing filter read is routed through the displayed filter row $F$, finite Boolean-branch ledger $B$, quotient-refusal row $Q$, L10 handoff row $L$, refusal row $R$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. The packet exports no maximal-filter extension, no ultrafilter selector, no quotient-filter equality, and no host completion theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:ultrafilter-boundary-carrier}. The only positive filter data are the finite inspected row $F$, the finite branch ledger $B$, and the L10 handoff row $L$.

The refusal rows $Q$ and $R$ record the missing endpoints. They prevent a consumer from rereading finite branch data as quotient Boolean equality, maximality, an ultrafilter extension, or a selected completion object.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same displayed rows as transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since the carrier has no further coordinate, every completion-facing filter read stays inside the finite boundary route.
\end{proof}
