\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CollectionwiseNormalUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-collectionwise-normal-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Engelking and Kelley on collectionwise normal spaces, the $\CollectionwiseNormalUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface in which a displayed discrete family of closed rows receives pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods and a locally finite refinement ledger. It sits above the closed-pair separation route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, beside the locally finite cover discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-paracompact-namecert}, and before separation-function consumers such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}. The packet strengthens the normal-space and paracompact lattice by naming the finite familywise separation route; it does not import arbitrary choice of neighborhoods, host powersets, open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity weights, or an ambient point-set theorem.

\begin{definition}[Collectionwise normal carrier]
\label{def:collectionwise-normal-carrier}
A $\CollectionwiseNormalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(T,N,D,O,L,P,U,H,C,Z)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row on which the closed-family, open-neighborhood, and cover-refinement observations are read.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ separation row, exposing the closed-pair route used for each displayed member of the family.
\item $D$ is the discrete closed-family ledger, recording the finite family window and the local discreteness observations visible to the packet.
\item $O$ is the disjoint open-neighborhood ledger, assigning to every displayed closed row in $D$ an open row over $T$ and recording pairwise disjointness inside the finite window.
\item $L$ is the locally finite refinement ledger, read through the paracompact cover surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-paracompact-namecert}.
\item $P$ is the paracompact handoff row that keeps locally finite refinement data visible without selecting a subcover from an arbitrary host family.
\item $U$ is the $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ handoff row, available only after the displayed normal-space and disjoint-neighborhood rows have been read.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the topology, normal-space, discrete-family, disjoint-neighborhood, locally finite refinement, handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the discrete closed-family request through the normal-space route, disjoint open-neighborhood ledger, locally finite refinement ledger, paracompact handoff, and Urysohn handoff.
\item $Z$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CollectionwiseNormalUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,N,D,O,L,P,U,H,C,Z$. It has no coordinate for host-indexed families, selected complements, arbitrary choice of separating neighborhoods, open-cover compactness, quotient topology equality, or partition-of-unity data.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Collectionwise normal NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:collectionwise-normal-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CollectionwiseNormalUp$ carrier $Q=(T,N,D,O,L,P,U,H,C,Z)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CollectionwiseNormalUp}$ surface consists exactly of topology-source admission, normal-space separation exposure, discrete closed-family exposure, disjoint open-neighborhood exactness, locally finite refinement exposure, paracompact handoff, Urysohn handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, and local naming. No obligation is discharged by a selected family of host neighborhoods, arbitrary open-cover compactness, a host powerset, quotient topology equality, or partition-of-unity weights.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:collectionwise-normal-carrier}. The rows $T,N,D,O,L,P,U,H,C,Z$ are the whole local surface.

The familywise separation rows are $N,D,O$. The normal-space route supplies closed-pair separation data, the discrete-family row fixes the finite family window, and the open-neighborhood ledger records the disjoint neighborhoods visible to the packet.

The refinement and handoff rows are $L,P,U$. They route locally finite cover data through the paracompact sibling and expose the Urysohn-facing handoff only after the separation rows are visible.

Transport, replay, and local naming are exhausted by $H,C,Z$. Since none of the excluded host objects is a carrier coordinate, the listed rows are exactly the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Collectionwise normal paracompact handoff]
\label{thm:collectionwise-normal-paracompact-handoff}
Every locally finite refinement read exported by an accepted $\CollectionwiseNormalUp$ packet must pass through the displayed route
$$
\NormalSpaceUp,\ D,\ O \longmapsto \ParacompactUp \longmapsto \UrysohnLemmaUp .
$$
The route exposes the closed-family separation data, disjoint open-neighborhood rows, paracompact locally finite refinement ledger, and Urysohn-facing handoff as finite BEDC rows; it does not select arbitrary subcovers, choose host neighborhoods, or import open-cover compactness as a primitive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the normal-space and discrete-family rows. They determine which displayed closed rows are being separated and keep the family window visible before any refinement read occurs.

Read the disjoint open-neighborhood ledger next. It supplies only the finite neighborhood rows stored in the carrier and does not choose a host family of separators.

The locally finite refinement row then passes through the paracompact handoff. A separation-function consumer may continue to the Urysohn sibling only after the normal-space, disjoint-neighborhood, and refinement rows have been displayed.

The structural rows preserve this same order under transport and replay. Because no coordinate stores arbitrary subcovers, selected neighborhoods, or open-cover compactness, the handoff remains a finite BEDC dependency route.
\end{proof}
