\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DistanceToCompactUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-distance-to-compact-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive metric analysis, the $\DistanceToCompactUp$ packet records the finite certificate surface for the standard distance from a point to a compact metric source. It sits over the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the compact finite-net data of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the located comparison function of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-distance-function-namecert}, the displayed endpoint rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-metric-projection-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite compact-distance certificate only; it does not choose a nearest point, import open-cover compactness, quotient metric equality, countable choice, or ambient metric completeness.

\begin{definition}[Distance to compact carrier]
\label{def:distance-to-compact-carrier}
A $\DistanceToCompactUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(X,K,L,F,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\MetricUp$ point-data row naming the ambient metric source and the queried point.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ finite-net row naming the compact metric source by displayed finite covers and tolerance schedules.
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedDistanceFunctionUp$ comparison row that answers point-to-compact distance requests by located real comparisons.
\item $F$ is the $\FiniteMetricProjectionUp$ witness table, recording finite net candidates and the endpoint rows used only as witnesses for bounds.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row turning the displayed comparison windows into a regular rational name.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the exported distance value after the regular readback is replayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric point data, compact finite-net data, located comparison, finite witness table, regular readback, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a distance request through $X,K,L,F,R,E$ and then through the structural rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DistanceToCompactUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\LocatedDistanceFunctionUp$, $\FiniteMetricProjectionUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DistanceToCompactUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier accepts only packets whose distance value is read through $X,K,L,F,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a nearest-point choice, open-cover compactness proof, quotient metric equality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient metric completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Distance to compact NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:distance-to-compact-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DistanceToCompactUp$ carrier $D=(X,K,L,F,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DistanceToCompactUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the metric point row, compact finite-net row, located-distance comparison, finite witness table, regular rational readback, real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item compact-source exposure, requiring $K$ to be read through the finite-net surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert} before any distance value is exported;
\item located comparison exactness, requiring $L$ to consume the located function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-distance-function-namecert};
\item finite witness discipline, requiring $F$ to provide only finite projection witness rows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-metric-projection-namecert}, not a global nearest-point selector;
\item regular readback and real sealing, requiring $R$ and $E$ to pass through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert};
\item componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming for exactly the displayed packet.
\end{enumerate}
No local obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:distance-to-compact-carrier}. The public rows are exactly $X,K,L,F,R,E,H,C,P,N$, so carrier admission exposes only metric point data, compact finite-net data, located comparison, finite witness rows, regular readback, and the terminal real seal.

The compact-source and located-comparison obligations are forced by the replay order. A distance request first reads $X$ and the finite-net row $K$, then uses $L$ to compare the requested point against the compact source through displayed located-distance windows.

The finite witness table $F$ contributes only finite candidate rows. It can bound and certify the readback route, but it has no coordinate that selects a nearest point in the compact source.

Finally $R$ and $E$ seal the value through the regular rational and Real rows, while $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same row family under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Since the carrier has no further coordinate, the listed obligations exhaust the NameCert surface and none of the excluded principles can be projected from it.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Distance to compact located readback]
\label{thm:distance-to-compact-located-readback}
Every accepted $\DistanceToCompactUp$ packet $D=(X,K,L,F,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ exports a located distance value only through the displayed route
$$
X,K,L,F \longmapsto R,E \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus downstream projection and Hausdorff-distance consumers receive a regular rational readback and a real seal for the distance-to-compact value, while nearest-point choice, open-cover compactness, quotient metric equality, countable choice, and ambient metric completeness remain outside the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the compact finite-net row $K$ and the located comparison row $L$. Together they supply the finite tolerance windows and located comparisons needed to answer a distance request without appealing to an open-cover theorem or a completed ambient metric.

Next read the finite witness table $F$. The witness rows certify the displayed bounds used by the comparison route, but the endpoint evidence remains a finite table row and is not retyped as a nearest-point choice.

Now replay the readback rows. The regular rational row $R$ records the tolerance-indexed value route, and the Real seal $E$ is readable only after the metric, compact-source, comparison, and finite-witness rows have been displayed.

Finally transport and replay through $H,C,P,N$. The same packet is delivered to projection and Hausdorff-distance consumers, and row exhaustion excludes quotient metric equality, countable choice, ambient completeness, and any hidden nearest-point principle.
\end{proof}
