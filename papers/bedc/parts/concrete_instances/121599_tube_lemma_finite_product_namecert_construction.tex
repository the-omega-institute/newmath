\label{def:tube-lemma-finite-product-carrier}

$$

\item $X$ is the finite family of $\TopologyUp$ rows indexed by $I$.
\item $K$ is the finite family of $\CompactUp$ slice rows for the compact coordinates.
\item $O$ is the $\ProductTopologyUp$ open-neighbourhood row containing the displayed compact product slice.
\item $U$ is the finite family of coordinate product-neighbourhood rows read from $O$.
\item $V$ is the resulting finite tube-neighbourhood row in the remaining product coordinates.

The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,X,K,O,U,V,F,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for countable products, arbitrary product compactness, quotient equality of product points, selected bases, or a detached ambient open-cover selector.

\begin{theorem}[Finite product tube lemma NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:tube-lemma-finite-product-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\TubeLemmaFiniteProductUp$ carrier $T_{\mathrm{fin}}=(I,X,K,O,U,V,F,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\TubeLemmaFiniteProductUp}$ surface is exhausted by finite-index admission, topology-row exposure for the indexed factors, compact-slice exposure through $K$, product-open exposure through $O$, coordinate product-neighbourhood exposure through $U$, tube-neighbourhood exposure through $V$, finite-cover refinement through $F$, coordinatewise routing through $R$ to $\TubeLemmaUp$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:tube-lemma-finite-product-carrier}. The finite index row $I$ bounds the product coordinates, while $X$ and $K$ expose only the displayed topology rows and compact slice rows attached to those coordinates.

The product-neighbourhood data then pass through $O,U,V,F$. The row $O$ supplies the finite product-open neighbourhood, $U$ records the coordinate neighbourhood family read from it, $F$ records the finite-cover refinement, and $V$ is the resulting tube-neighbourhood row.

Finally the route $R$ reuses the ordinary tube packet coordinatewise, and $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same finite data under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Since no carrier coordinate names countable products, arbitrary product compactness, quotient product equality, selected bases, or a detached open-cover selector, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite product compact route]
\label{thm:tube-lemma-finite-product-compact-route}
Every accepted $\TubeLemmaFiniteProductUp$ carrier routes a compact finite product slice through the displayed chain
$$
I,X,K\longmapsto O\longmapsto U\longmapsto F\longmapsto V\longmapsto R .
$$
Thus a downstream compact-product or uniform-continuity consumer must first inspect the finite index row, the factor topology rows, the compact slice rows, the product-open row, the coordinate neighbourhood family, the finite-cover refinement row, and the coordinatewise $\TubeLemmaUp$ route before it can read the finite tube neighbourhood.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier projection supplies the finite source rows $I,X,K$. These rows determine which coordinates participate in the compact product slice and prevent any appeal to countable or arbitrary products.

The neighbourhood construction is forced through $O,U,F,V$. Product openness is exposed first, the coordinate neighbourhood family is then read from that product row, and the finite-cover refinement row chooses the finite tube data used by $V$.

The final row $R$ records that each coordinate fibre is routed through the ordinary $\TubeLemmaUp$ packet. Transport, replay, provenance, and naming do not introduce further compactness principles, so the displayed chain is the complete compact route available to downstream consumers.
\end{proof}
