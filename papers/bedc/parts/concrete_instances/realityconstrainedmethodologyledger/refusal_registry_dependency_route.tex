\begin{theorem}[Reality-constrained methodology refusal-registry dependency]
\label{thm:reality-constrained-methodology-ledger-refusal-registry-dependency}
Every accepted $\RealityConstrainedMethodologyLedgerUp$ packet whose refusal, denial, or upgrade row is consumed as methodology output must first pass through the finite refusal packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-refusalregistry-namecert}. A downstream reader may then consume the methodology row as a displayed refusal boundary, but it may not read denial as silence, package provenance as authority, or missing upgrade evidence as permission to export.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the methodology-ledger root and refusal rows. The root obligation surface, authority unblock rows, and route-unblock packet expose methodology scope, denial, upgrade, provenance, and local naming as finite ledger coordinates rather than as ambient policy claims.

Apply the export-control surface of $\RefusalRegistryUp$. Its blocked-claim, refusal-entry, gate, cannot-claim, status, provenance, transport, package, and local NameCert rows require an explicit refusal packet before a consumer can treat a blocked route as a valid refusal boundary.

Compose the two packets through the existing ledger route. The methodology ledger supplies the local denial and upgrade coordinates, while $\RefusalRegistryUp$ supplies the refusal export control. Therefore the consumer receives a named refusal boundary and not silence, hidden authority, or an unregistered upgrade route.
\end{proof}
