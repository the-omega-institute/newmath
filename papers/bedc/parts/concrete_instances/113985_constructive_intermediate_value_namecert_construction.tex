\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-intermediate-value-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$ packet records the finite bisection surface by which a continuous real-valued map on a located interval exposes an intermediate-value bracket without invoking a host intermediate-value theorem. It sits after the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the located-real comparison surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedreal-namecert}, the continuous-map route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, and the bisection method packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bisection-method-namecert}. The packet gives L10 Real and Completion readers a finite sign-window route, not a selected root, quotient real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Constructive intermediate-value carrier]
\label{def:constructive-intermediate-value-carrier}
A $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
V=(I,F,S,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the located interval row, recording displayed endpoint names, endpoint apartness, rational or dyadic enclosure windows, and the source interval orientation;
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ row, recording the finite modulus, graph, and value-readback data for the displayed map on the interval row $I$;
\item $S$ is the sign-apartness window, recording endpoint sign evidence and the retained crossing condition available at the current finite bisection depth;
\item $B$ is the bisection schedule row, recording the requested depth, midpoint reads, retained subintervals, and branch evidence consumed through the $\BisectionMethodUp$ route;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the interval, map, sign window, bisection schedule, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite intermediate-value request through continuous-map readback, sign-apartness inspection, bisection refinement, and terminal $\RealUp$ seal exposure;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$, $\RealUp$, $\LocatedRealUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BisectionMethodUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp}$ row binding the paper-visible name to the finite packet.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a host intermediate-value theorem, a choice-selected root, trichotomy on arbitrary reals, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive intermediate-value NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:constructive-intermediate-value-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$ carrier
$$
V=(I,F,S,B,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the located interval row $I$, continuous-map row $F$, sign-apartness window $S$, bisection schedule $B$, componentwise transport $H$, replay route $C$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:constructive-intermediate-value-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes only the displayed interval, map, sign, bisection, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The analytic rows are $I,F,S,B$. The located interval supplies the endpoint windows, the continuous-map row supplies finite value readbacks, the sign row records the crossing evidence, and the bisection row records retained branches at finite depth.

The structural rows are $H,C,P,N$. They transport the same packet, replay the finite request, record provenance, and bind the local name. Since no other coordinate exists, every NameCert obligation is one of these rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive intermediate-value bisection route]
\label{thm:constructive-intermediate-value-bisection-route}
Every accepted $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$ consumer that reads an intermediate-value bracket must factor through the finite route
$$
I \longrightarrow F \longrightarrow S \longrightarrow B \longrightarrow P \longrightarrow N
$$
before any $\RealUp$-facing seal is inspected. The route may expose retained intervals, midpoint values, sign windows, and regular real-name readbacks, but it cannot supply a selected root, a host existence theorem, trichotomy, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First read the located interval row $I$. It fixes the endpoint windows and the interval orientation before the map can be evaluated.

Next read the continuous-map row $F$ and sign-apartness row $S$. The map values are available only through the displayed finite graph and modulus data, and the crossing evidence is recorded as endpoint and retained-window sign data rather than as a host order case split.

Now apply the bisection schedule row $B$. Each retained branch is a finite refinement of the displayed interval and sign rows, so the consumer receives a bracketed route, not a choice-selected root.

Finally pass through $P$ and $N$. Provenance and local naming attach the route to the packet boundary, while the excluded root-selection, trichotomy, quotient, choice, and completeness principles have no row to occupy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive intermediate-value L10 scope]
\label{thm:constructive-intermediate-value-l10-scope}
Every L10 Real or Completion consumer of an accepted $\ConstructiveIntermediateValueUp$ packet may use it only as a finite-window bisection certificate over $\RealUp$, $\LocatedRealUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, and $\BisectionMethodUp$ rows. The scope closes the local route from displayed interval endpoints and continuous-map readbacks to retained bisection windows; it does not close the host intermediate-value theorem, trichotomy, selected roots, countable choice, quotient real equality, or $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:constructive-intermediate-value-namecert-obligations}. They require the interval, map, sign, bisection, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows to be visible before a consumer can read the packet.

Apply the bisection route of \autoref{thm:constructive-intermediate-value-bisection-route}. It orders every intermediate-value read through $I,F,S,B,P,N$ and keeps the Real-facing seal downstream of finite retained-window evidence.

Thus the L10 consumer receives exactly the displayed finite-window certificate. Any claim requiring host IVT, trichotomy, a selected root, countable choice, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness lies outside the packet scope.
\end{proof}
