\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ActiveReadingGateUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-active-reading-gate-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet realizes the single-active-reading governance surface of \autoref{ch:visions-philosophy-auditable-generativity} as a finite NameCert carrier. It reads the current-reading discipline through the machine-interface ledger and sits beside the registry export surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-registry-export-surface-namecert}, the registry layer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-registrylayer-namecert}, the claim registry layer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-claimregistrylayer-namecert}, the audit transcript envelope of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-audit-transcript-envelope-namecert}, and the writing-item audit packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-writingitemaudit-namecert}. Those siblings expose registry export, registry admission, claim admission, transcript replay, and writing-item legality; this carrier binds the unique active reading before any downstream consumer may export a prose, registry, or machine-facing route.

\begin{definition}[Active reading gate carrier]
\label{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}
An $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the target row, naming the theorem, definition, registry entry, writing item, machine-facing target, or package surface whose reading is being exported.
\item $A$ is the active-reading row, recording the single current reading allowed for $T$.
\item $R$ is the retired-reading row, recording displaced readings as non-exportable audit data rather than as active alternatives.
\item $B$ is the blocking or refusal row, recording the public refusal that blocks a second active reading for the same target.
\item $E$ is the export row, recording the only route through which the active reading may leave the gate.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for target, active reading, retired reading, blocking row, export row, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the target row through the active-reading and blocking rows to the export row.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ActiveReadingGateUp$, $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\RegistryLayerUp$, $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$, $\AuditTranscriptEnvelopeUp$, $\WritingItemAuditUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ActiveReadingGateUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a parallel active reading, hidden target alias, export that bypasses the blocking row, audit transcript without replay, writing item without the current reading, or local name outside the finite packet $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-obligation-surface}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\ActiveReadingGateUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$; target admission through $T$; active-reading uniqueness through $A$ and $B$; retired-reading exposure through $R$; registry and blocking coverage through $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\RegistryLayerUp$, and $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$; export non-escape through $E$; and transport, replay, provenance, and naming stability through $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ActiveReadingGateUp.ActiveReadingGateCarrier\_obligation\_surface}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}. The rows $T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N$ are the only coordinates available to the local certificate.

The target and reading rows supply the governance surface. Row $T$ fixes the object being read, row $A$ supplies the unique current reading, row $R$ records displaced readings only as audit data, and row $B$ blocks any attempt to export a second current reading.

The registry siblings provide the public routes. $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$ controls export, $\RegistryLayerUp$ keeps package rows finite, and $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$ prevents a claim consumer from treating the retired row as a second accepted claim.

Rows $E,H,C,P,N$ make that boundary stable. They expose the single export route, preserve the displayed rows componentwise, replay the route through the blocking surface, record package provenance, and name the packet. No remaining coordinate can create a parallel active reading or bypass the refusal row.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate export uniqueness obligation]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-export-uniqueness-obligation}
For every accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$, the export row $E$ is unique as an active-reading export for the target row $T$. Any registry, claim, transcript, or writing-item consumer must read $E$ through the current row $A$ and the blocking row $B$, with $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\RegistryLayerUp$, $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$, $\AuditTranscriptEnvelopeUp$, and $\WritingItemAuditUp$ acting only on that displayed route.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ActiveReadingGateUp.ActiveReadingGateCarrier\_export\_uniqueness}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}. The only active-reading row is $A$, the only retired-reading row is $R$, and the only export row is $E$.

Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-obligation-surface}. It routes registry export through $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, registry admission through $\RegistryLayerUp$, claim admission through $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$, transcript replay through $\AuditTranscriptEnvelopeUp$, and writing-item legality through $\WritingItemAuditUp$.

The blocking row $B$ sits between $A$ and $E$. Since $H$ transports only displayed rows, $C$ replays only the displayed route, and $P,N$ only record provenance and local naming, no sibling route can introduce a second active export for the same target.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate ledger closure obligation]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-ledger-closure-obligation}
For every accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$, the ledger for target, active reading, retired reading, blocking, export, replay, provenance, and local naming is closed by the displayed rows $R,E,L,H,C,P,N$, where $L$ denotes the visible registry and claim ledger induced by $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\RegistryLayerUp$, $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$, $\AuditTranscriptEnvelopeUp$, and $\WritingItemAuditUp$. No consumer may replace that ledger by an export outside $E$ or by a retired row outside $R$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ActiveReadingGateUp.ActiveReadingGateCarrier\_ledger\_closure}
\begin{proof}
Read $L$ as the finite ledger route exposed by the sibling chapters named in the provenance row $P$. The route records registry export, registry admission, claim admission, transcript replay, and writing-item legality before any public consumer sees an export.

Rows $R$ and $E$ are therefore not free coordinates. The retired row $R$ is held as audit data, while $E$ is the only public export row admitted by the ledger route $L$.

Rows $H,C,P,N$ close the ledger under transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. They can preserve or replay the displayed route, but they cannot add a hidden active reading, registry bypass, claim bypass, transcript bypass, writing-item bypass, or second local name.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate provenance stability obligation]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-provenance-stability-obligation}
For every accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$, provenance row $P$ is stable under componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$ and displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$ precisely for the rows $R,E,L,H,C,P,N$ over the routes $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\RegistryLayerUp$, $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$, $\AuditTranscriptEnvelopeUp$, and $\WritingItemAuditUp$. A transported or replayed packet preserves the same active-reading export boundary and cannot turn provenance into a new active row.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ActiveReadingGateUp.ActiveReadingGateCarrier\_provenance\_stability}
\begin{proof}
Start from the provenance row $P$. It names the active-reading gate together with the registry export surface, registry layer, claim registry layer, audit transcript envelope, writing-item audit route, and the BEDC structural rows.

Componentwise $\hsame$ transport in $H$ preserves the rows $T,A,R,B,E$ and the induced ledger route $L$. Displayed $\Cont$ replay in $C$ follows the same target, active-reading, blocking, and export chain.

Thus a transported or replayed packet has the same public export boundary. The provenance row can certify the route already present in $G$, but it has no coordinate that can promote $R$ or any sibling ledger entry into a second active reading.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate single export route]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-single-export-route}
Every registry, claim, transcript, or writing-item consumer of an accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet must read the target row $T$ through the active-reading row $A$ and the blocking row $B$ before the export row $E$. Such a consumer cannot export a parallel active reading for the same target.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted packet
$$
G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N).
$$
By \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-obligation-surface}, the export row $E$ is available only as part of the displayed carrier containing $T,A,R,B,H,C,P,N$.

A registry or claim consumer therefore reaches $E$ through the unique active row $A$ and the refusal row $B$. The retired row $R$ remains visible, but it is read as non-exportable audit data rather than as a second claim route.

Transcript and writing-item consumers receive the same route through the provenance row $P$, which names \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-audit-transcript-envelope-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-writingitemaudit-namecert}. Since $H$ only transports displayed fields and $C$ only replays the displayed route, neither structural row can turn $R$ into a second active row.

Thus every accepted export factors through $T,A,B,E$, with $R$ blocked from active export. A packet exporting both $A$ and $R$ as active readings for the same target leaves $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ and is refused by $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate ledger non-escape]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-ledger-nonescape}
Every accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ consumer read factors through
$$
T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N.
$$
Consequently a retired reading cannot become a second active export, and no hidden target alias, registry bypass, claim bypass, transcript bypass, writing-item bypass, or local name outside the displayed carrier is available.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}. The packet exposes exactly the target row $T$, active-reading row $A$, retired-reading row $R$, blocking row $B$, export row $E$, transport row $H$, replay row $C$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$.

Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-obligation-surface}. It makes active-reading uniqueness, retired-reading exposure, registry coverage, export non-escape, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming the displayed local obligations.

Use the single-export route of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-single-export-route}. Since every public export reaches $E$ through $T,A,B$, the retired row $R$ remains audit data and cannot become a parallel active export.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate scope grounding]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-scope-grounding}
The $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ scope is grounded in the displayed $\BHist$ rows $T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, the registry-export siblings, the claim-registry siblings, the audit transcript envelope, the writing-item audit route, and the local $\NameCert_{\ActiveReadingGateUp}$ row $N$. No accepted read has a scope coordinate outside that finite surface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier of \autoref{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}. Its rows name the target, active reading, retired reading, blocking ledger, export route, transport, replay, provenance, and local certificate.

The obligation surface of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-obligation-surface} grounds the registry and claim sides through $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\RegistryLayerUp$, and $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$. It also keeps the audit transcript envelope and writing-item audit route visible through the provenance row $P$.

Apply ledger non-escape from \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-ledger-nonescape} and the single-export route from \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-single-export-route}. The same finite rows that admit the packet also bound every consumer read, so no hidden target alias or bypass route can supply an additional scope coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate single-active obstruction]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-single-active-obstruction}
For every accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet
$$
G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the blocking row $B$ rejects any consumer route that presents two active readings for the same target row $T$. The rejected route may mention retired-reading data in $R$, but it cannot classify $R$ as a second active row beside $A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:active-reading-gate-carrier}. The target row $T$ and the active-reading row $A$ are single displayed coordinates, and the blocking row $B$ is attached to the same target.

Use the export uniqueness obligation of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-export-uniqueness-obligation}. Any public route to the export row $E$ passes through $A$ and then through $B$ before a registry, claim, transcript, or writing-item consumer can read it.

The retired row $R$ remains visible as audit data. Since $H$ transports only the displayed carrier rows and $C$ replays the displayed route, neither structural row can reclassify $R$ as another active reading for $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate retired-reading nonescape]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-retired-reading-nonescape}
For every accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$, the retired-reading row $R$ remains visible in the audit surface and cannot be erased by export. Every consumer that reads the export row $E$ still factors through the displayed carrier containing $R$, even though $R$ is not active.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-obligation-surface}. It lists retired-reading exposure through $R$ as part of the local $\NameCert_{\ActiveReadingGateUp}$ surface.

Apply the ledger closure obligation of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-ledger-closure-obligation}. The ledger route records $R$ and $E$ together before any public consumer sees the export, so export cannot remove the retired-reading coordinate from the visible packet.

Finally use ledger non-escape from \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-ledger-nonescape}. Every accepted consumer read factors through $T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N$, and therefore the retired row remains audit-visible while the blocking row prevents it from becoming active.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Active reading gate export scope]
\label{thm:active-reading-gate-export-scope}
Every export route of an accepted $\ActiveReadingGateUp$ packet reaches a consumer only through the target row $T$, active-reading row $A$, blocking row $B$, export row $E$, componentwise transport row $H$, displayed replay row $C$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$. The retired row $R$ remains visible to audit readers, but the public export scope is exactly the route
$$
T,A,B,E,H,C,P,N.
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier $G=(T,A,R,B,E,H,C,P,N)$. The only public export coordinate is $E$, and it is tied to the target and active-reading rows by the blocking row.

Use the single export route of \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-single-export-route}. It forces registry, claim, transcript, and writing-item consumers to read $T,A,B,E$ in the displayed order before any public export is available.

The structural rows then bound the rest of the route. Componentwise transport uses $H$, replay uses $C$, provenance uses $P$, and local naming uses $N$. The retired row $R$ stays in the audit packet by \autoref{thm:active-reading-gate-retired-reading-nonescape}, but it is not part of the active export chain.
\end{proof}
