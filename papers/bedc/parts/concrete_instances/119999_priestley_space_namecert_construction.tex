\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\PriestleySpaceUp$}

Following Priestley and Davey--Priestley, the $\PriestleySpaceUp$ packet records the ordered topological carrier used by Priestley duality as finite BEDC NameCert data. It sits after the meet--join order surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lattice-namecert}, the bounded endpoint rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bounded-lattice-namecert}, the indexed-open carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the clopen ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stoneduality-namecert}, and the duality-theorem packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-priestley-duality-namecert}. The chapter separates the ordered topological carrier from the representation theorem: it names compact ordered clopen-upset observations, not selected prime filters, an ultrafilter construction, a powerset topology, quotient lattice equality, or a choice principle.

\begin{definition}[Priestley space carrier]
\label{def:priestley-space-carrier}
A $\PriestleySpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(O,T,K,U,D,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $O$ is the bounded distributive-order row, read through $\LatticeUp$ meet--join observations and $\BoundedLatticeUp$ endpoint rows.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ indexed-open row, listing the finite open-neighbourhood observations available to the carrier.
\item $K$ is the Stone-facing clopen ledger inherited from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stoneduality-namecert}, restricted to displayed clopen rows rather than host subsets.
\item $U$ is the clopen-upset row, recording which displayed clopen rows are upward closed for the order observations in $O$.
\item $D$ is the Priestley separation row: for displayed ordered observations whose order comparison is separated, $D$ names a clopen upset that contains the upper observation and excludes the lower observation.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $O,T,K,U,D,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an order/topology query through the clopen-upset and separation rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PriestleySpaceUp$, $\LatticeUp$, $\BoundedLatticeUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\StoneDualityUp$, $\PriestleyDualityUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PriestleySpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The local classifier compares accepted packets only through $O,T,K,U,D,H,C,P,N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Priestley space NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:priestley-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PriestleySpaceUp$ carrier $S=(O,T,K,U,D,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\PriestleySpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of bounded lattice order admission through $O$, topology admission through $T$, Stone clopen readback through $K$, clopen-upset classification through $U$, Priestley separation through $D$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed continuation replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. The certificate exports no ultrafilter construction, selected prime filters, powerset topology, quotient lattice equality, host spectral space, or choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:priestley-space-carrier}. The rows $O,T,K,U,D$ are the only order, topology, clopen, upset, and separation coordinates; $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name those rows.

The order rows are read through the lattice and bounded-lattice siblings, while the topological rows are read through the topology sibling. The clopen ledger $K$ is supplied by the Stone-duality interface as displayed clopen data, and $U$ restricts those rows to the upward-closed observations visible from $O$.

The separation row $D$ then records only the finite Priestley condition exposed by the packet. Componentwise transport and displayed continuation preserve the same finite route under $H$ and $C$, and $P,N$ package and name it.

Since no listed coordinate stores ultrafilters, selected prime filters, powerset opens, quotient lattice equality, host spectral points, or a choice principle, those objects are outside the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Priestley space Stone-order handoff]
\label{thm:priestley-space-stone-order-handoff}
Every Stone-duality or Priestley-duality consumer of an accepted $\PriestleySpaceUp$ carrier must read the ordered topological handoff through
$$
\LatticeUp,\ \BoundedLatticeUp \longrightarrow \TopologyUp,\ \StoneDualityUp \longrightarrow O,T,K,U,D,H,C,P,N .
$$
The handoff exposes a bounded order row, indexed-open topology row, Stone clopen ledger, clopen-upset classifier, and finite separation row before any duality theorem packet may consume the carrier. It does not assert a host Priestley representation theorem, an ultrafilter construction, selected prime filters, a powerset topology, quotient lattice equality, or choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the order side. The rows $O$ and $D$ make the bounded distributive order and finite separation requests visible before a consumer can treat a clopen row as an order-sensitive observation.

Read the topological side next. The row $T$ supplies indexed opens, $K$ supplies the Stone-facing clopen ledger, and $U$ records the clopen-upset restriction for the displayed order. These rows are the ordered topological carrier surface, not the full duality theorem.

Finally use the structural rows $H,C,P,N$. They transport, replay, source, and name the same handoff for Stone-duality and Priestley-duality consumers. Because the carrier contains no ultrafilter, selected prime-filter, powerset-topology, quotient-equality, host-representation, or choice coordinate, the downstream handoff remains finite BEDC data.
\end{proof}
