\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalCover bridge export]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-cover-bridge-export}
Every accepted $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$ bridge export is the finite route isolated in \autoref{thm:dyadicintervalcover-finite-cover-bridge-scope}: the dyadic endpoint and mesh rows $L,U,M$, radius budget $R$, finite cover-membership rows $V$, $\StreamNameUp$ window row $W$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$, $\RealUp$ seal row $A$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. No bridge consumer receives a selected finite subcover, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, host compactness, countable choice, or ambient completion coordinate from the export.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the bridge scope theorem \autoref{thm:dyadicintervalcover-finite-cover-bridge-scope}. It names the endpoint rows $L,U$, mesh row $M$, radius budget $R$, finite cover-membership rows $V$, stream window row $W$, regular rational readback row $Q$, Real seal row $A$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$ before any bridge consumer can inspect the cover export.

The present theorem adds no carrier coordinate beyond that list. Its export is therefore the projection that forgets the internal proof route and keeps exactly the displayed endpoint, mesh, radius, membership, stream, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Since the source bridge scope excludes selected finite subcovers, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, host compactness, countable choice, and ambient completion, the projected export excludes them as well. A consumer can only replay the finite route already isolated by the bridge scope.
\end{proof}
