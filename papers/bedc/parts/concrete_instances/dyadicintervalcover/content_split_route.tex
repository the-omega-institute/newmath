\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalCover bridge handoff]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-cover-bridge-handoff}
Every bridge-facing consumer of the public finite-cover export of \autoref{thm:dyadicintervalcover-public-finite-cover-export} must preserve the displayed $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint rows, mesh row, cover-membership ledger, $\StreamNameUp$ window, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seal, $\BishopIntervalCompactUp$ handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ rows. The handoff is the finite public-cover route itself; it does not claim bridge checking, open-cover compactness, selected finite subcovers, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the public finite-cover export. It identifies the available public surface as the displayed rows $L,U,M,R,V,W,Q,A,H,C,P,N$ together with the $\BishopIntervalCompactUp$ handoff.

Use the scoped Real-seal and nonescape obligations, \autoref{thm:dyadicintervalcover-real-seal-scope} and \autoref{thm:dyadicintervalcover-real-seal-nonescape-obligation}. They keep every bridge-facing read downstream of the dyadic endpoints, mesh, membership, window, readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for open-cover compactness, selected finite subcovers, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient completion, a bridge-facing consumer can preserve only the displayed finite-cover route.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.DyadicIntervalCoverUp.DyadicIntervalCoverBridgeHandoff}

\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalCover content route preservation]
\label{thm:dyadicintervalcover-content-split-route}
Every Real-facing consumer that uses the bridge handoff of \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-cover-bridge-handoff} preserves the same $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, and interval-cover rows exposed by the public finite-cover export. Thus the moved route remains the displayed finite-cover NameCert route through dyadic endpoints, mesh and cover-membership data, stream windows, regular rational readback, Real sealing, interval compactness handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the public finite-cover export of \autoref{thm:dyadicintervalcover-public-finite-cover-export}. It names the public route through $L,U,M,R,V,W,Q,A,H,C,P,N$, with dyadic endpoints, mesh and membership data, stream windows, regular readback, the Real seal, and the interval-compactness handoff all displayed.

Then apply the bridge handoff of \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-cover-bridge-handoff}. A bridge-facing consumer must preserve exactly that displayed route and cannot replace it with open-cover compactness, selected finite subcovers, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient completion.

The Real-facing part is therefore still the same finite route: $L,U$ give the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint surface, $W$ gives the $\StreamNameUp$ window, $Q$ gives the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $A$ gives the $\RealUp$ seal, and $M,R,V,H,C,P,N$ preserve the interval-cover carrier and NameCert structure.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.DyadicIntervalCoverUp.DyadicIntervalCoverContentSplitRoute}
