\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalCover bridge finite route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-cover-bridge-finite-route}
Every bridge consumer of an accepted $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$ packet factors through the finite endpoint rows, mesh row, radius ledger, finite membership row, $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seal, $\BishopIntervalCompactUp$ handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row before any standard interval-cover interpretation is read. The bridge route exports only those displayed finite rows; it exports no open-cover compactness, selected finite subcover, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier $I=(L,U,M,R,V,W,Q,A,H,C,P,N)$. The rows $L,U$ are the endpoint data, $M$ is the mesh row, $R$ is the radius ledger, and $V$ is the finite membership row.

Read the window and seal rows next. The $\StreamNameUp$ windows $W$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $Q$, and $\RealUp$ seal $A$ are downstream of the endpoint, mesh, radius, and membership rows.

Use the existing compact handoff and bridge export surface. The $\BishopIntervalCompactUp$ handoff is read only after those finite rows, while $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route.

Thus any standard interval-cover interpretation available to a bridge consumer is a readback of $L,U,M,R,V,W,Q,A,H,C,P,N$. Since no excluded compactness, selector, quotient, choice, or completion coordinate occurs in the carrier, none can be exported by the bridge route.
\end{proof}
