Following Euler and Poincare, the $\EulerCharacteristicUp$ packet records the finite alternating-simplex ledger by which a finite simplicial complex receives its Euler characteristic. It sits over the finite face inventory of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-simplicialcomplex-namecert}, the finite chain rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-simplicial-chain-namecert}, the homology provenance of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-homology-namecert}, the natural-number row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nat-namecert}, and the integer row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-int-namecert}. The packet names only a finite count-and-transport certificate; it does not import quotient chain complexes, selected triangulation equivalence, host cardinality, or infinite CW-complex semantics.

\begin{definition}[Euler characteristic finite-complex carrier]
\label{def:euler-characteristic-finite-complex-carrier}
An $\EulerCharacteristicUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(K,S,Q,A,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the source $\SimplicialComplexUp$ row, including the finite simplex inventory, face relation, and dimension grading.
\item $S$ is the simplex-count ledger. For each displayed dimension $n$, it records the $\NatUp$ count of $n$-simplices admitted by $K$.
\item $Q$ is the finite-support row stating that only finitely many entries of $S$ are nonzero, so the alternating sum is a finite ledger.
\item $A$ is the $\IntUp$ alternating-sum row, recording the signed total $\sum_n (-1)^n S_n$ by finite integer addition.
\item $B$ is the subdivision ledger boundary row. It records the finite count changes produced by a displayed elementary subdivision and the matching cancellation rows for faces inserted on both sides of the alternating sum.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the simplicial complex row, simplex-count rows, finite-support row, integer sum row, subdivision ledger, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a request for the Euler characteristic through $K,S,Q,A,B,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\EulerCharacteristicUp$, $\SimplicialComplexUp$, $\FiniteSimplicialChainUp$, $\HomologyUp$, $\NatUp$, $\IntUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\EulerCharacteristicUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,S,Q,A,B,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host cardinality, quotient chain-complex equality, an unrestricted homotopy-invariance theorem, selected triangulation equivalence, or an infinite CW-complex.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Euler characteristic subdivision ledger boundary]
\label{thm:euler-characteristic-subdivision-ledger-boundary}
For every accepted $\EulerCharacteristicUp$ carrier $E=(K,S,Q,A,B,H,C,P,N)$, a displayed elementary subdivision of the finite $\SimplicialComplexUp$ row may be consumed by an Euler-characteristic read only through the subdivision ledger boundary $B$. The row $B$ pairs the inserted simplex-count changes in adjacent dimensions, transports them through $H$, replays the same finite route through $C$, and leaves the integer alternating-sum row $A$ with the same $\IntUp$ value. Consequently every $\HomologyUp$ or finite-chain consumer that reads the Euler characteristic receives a finite count ledger, not a quotient homology computation, host cardinality argument, or infinite complex invariant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:euler-characteristic-finite-complex-carrier}. The simplex source $K$ supplies a finite dimension-graded inventory, while $S$ records the $\NatUp$ count in each displayed dimension and $Q$ states that the alternating sum is finite.

Read the integer row $A$. It is built from the finite list $S$ by alternating signs and $\IntUp$ addition, so a consumer can inspect only the displayed ledger
$$
\sum_n (-1)^n S_n .
$$

Now apply the subdivision row $B$. An elementary subdivision changes the finite simplex counts only by the inserted rows recorded in $B$, and those inserted rows are paired across adjacent dimensions with opposite signs in $A$.

Transport and replay preserve that pairing. The $\hsame$ row $H$ carries the same simplex-count and cancellation rows to classified replacements, and $C$ replays the Euler-characteristic request through $K,S,Q,A,B$ in the same order.

Finally read provenance. The $\FiniteSimplicialChainUp$ and $\HomologyUp$ entries in $P$ identify the downstream consumers of the finite ledger, but they do not add quotient chain groups, selected homotopy data, host cardinality, or infinite CW-complex rows. Thus the subdivision boundary is a local finite ledger statement about $A$, not an ambient invariance theorem.
\end{proof}
