\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\KakeyaSetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-kakeya-set-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Besicovitch and Kakeya on direction-rich plane sets, the $\KakeyaSetUp$ packet records only the finite BEDC geometry needed to name a displayed direction-window and segment-incidence carrier. It sits over the terminal real-name surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the metric certificate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the dyadic interval rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-real-interval-namecert}, and the incidence bookkeeping of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-incidence-algebra-namecert}. The packet is a finite naming certificate for direction-window geometry; it does not claim measure zero, the Kakeya conjecture, Besicovitch set existence, ambient planar completeness, or a host set-theoretic construction.

\begin{definition}[KakeyaSet carrier]
\label{def:kakeya-set-carrier}
A $\KakeyaSetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(D,W,S,I,M,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is a finite direction-window row, listing named rational or dyadic direction samples and the tolerance cells that cover the inspected direction interval.
\item $W$ is the window ledger, recording the finite observation windows in which the direction samples and segment endpoints are read.
\item $S$ is the segment row, assigning to each displayed direction sample one finite segment name with endpoint rows over $\RealUp$.
\item $I$ is the segment-incidence row, recording which sampled direction, endpoint pair, and window row belong to the same displayed segment.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ comparison row used to read endpoint distance and segment length only through displayed metric data.
\item $R$ is the real-name handoff row, tying every endpoint coordinate and tolerance cell to the $\RealUp$ and $\DyadicRealIntervalUp$ sibling surfaces.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for direction windows, segment rows, incidence rows, metric comparisons, real-name handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a direction request through the finite window, segment-incidence row, metric row, and real-name handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KakeyaSetUp$, $\RealUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\DyadicRealIntervalUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KakeyaSetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $D,W,S,I,M,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for outer measure, null sets, arbitrary direction choice, infinite unions, host subsets of the plane, or any assertion that a Besicovitch set exists.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[KakeyaSet NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:kakeya-set-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\KakeyaSetUp$ carrier
$$
K=(D,W,S,I,M,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\KakeyaSetUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite direction-window admission for $D$, finite observation-window display for $W$, segment-row admission for $S$, segment-incidence exactness for $I$, metric comparison display for $M$, real-name and dyadic-interval handoff for $R$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, sibling provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

No KakeyaSet obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The finite direction-window row $D$ and the observation ledger $W$ are the only rows from which directions may be read, so the packet never introduces an arbitrary direction choice.

The segment data are then read through $S$ and $I$. These rows bind each sampled direction to a displayed segment endpoint pair and to the window in which that pair is observed.

Finally the metric and real-name handoff rows $M$ and $R$ keep all endpoint and length comparisons inside the sibling Real, dyadic-interval, and metric surfaces. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route, so the local certificate contains no measure-theoretic or host set-theoretic coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[KakeyaSet direction-window route]
\label{thm:kakeya-set-direction-window-route}
Every consumer that reads a $\KakeyaSetUp$ packet as direction-rich geometry must first expose the finite route
$$
D,W,S,I,M,R,H,C,P,N .
$$
The direction-window row and segment-incidence row are public before any geometric consumer may compare directions or endpoints. The route exports finite incidence geometry only; it does not assert measure zero, the Kakeya conjecture, Besicovitch set existence, arbitrary direction selection, or a completed host subset of the plane.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier of \autoref{def:kakeya-set-carrier}. The only direction data are the finite direction samples and tolerance cells stored in $D$, with their read windows stored in $W$.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:kakeya-set-namecert-obligations}. They require the segment row $S$ and incidence row $I$ before any consumer can attach a segment to a direction window.

The metric row $M$ and real-name handoff row $R$ then route endpoint and length reads through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-real-interval-namecert}. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that same finite path, leaving no coordinate from which a measure-theoretic or completed-plane claim could be read.
\end{proof}
