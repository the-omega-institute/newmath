\begin{theorem}[Poincare disk geodesic projection hub sibling route]
\label{thm:poincare-disk-geodesic-projection-hub-sibling-route}
Every hub-level $\PoincareDiskGeodesicProjectionUp$ read passes through the child carrier of \autoref{def:poincare-disk-geodesic-projection-carrier} and the sibling lattice
$$
\UnitDiskUp \longmapsto \HyperbolicMetricUp \longmapsto \HyperbolicGeodesicBoundaryUp \longmapsto \PoincareBoundaryFixedTransportUp \longmapsto \PoincareDiskPhaseTransportUp
$$
before projection data is public. The hub contributes no independent projection row: it routes the public read to disk points, metric comparison, geodesic-boundary data, boundary-fixed transport, phase transport, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the hub through its children. The carrier child supplies the packet
$$
G=(D,B,M,T,J,H,C,P,N),
$$
so every displayed projection row already belongs to \autoref{def:poincare-disk-geodesic-projection-carrier}.

Apply \autoref{thm:poincare-disk-geodesic-projection-namecert-obligations} and \autoref{thm:poincare-disk-geodesic-projection-sibling-lattice}. The disk row $D$ is anchored in $\UnitDiskUp$, the metric comparison row $M$ in $\HyperbolicMetricUp$, the boundary row $B$ in $\HyperbolicGeodesicBoundaryUp$, the transport row $T$ in $\PoincareBoundaryFixedTransportUp$, and the outgoing consumer in $\PoincareDiskPhaseTransportUp$.

Thus a hub-level read reaches public projection data only after the same child carrier and sibling lattice have been displayed. The top-level chapter is only a routing surface; the substantive horizontal edge is supplied here, at child level, through $\UnitDiskUp$, $\HyperbolicMetricUp$, $\HyperbolicGeodesicBoundaryUp$, $\PoincareBoundaryFixedTransportUp$, and $\PoincareDiskPhaseTransportUp$.

Since the hub has no carrier coordinate of its own, it cannot bypass the child route.
\end{proof}
