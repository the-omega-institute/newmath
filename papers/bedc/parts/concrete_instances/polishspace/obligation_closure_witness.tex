\begin{theorem}[Polish-space complete-separable obligation frontier]
\label{thm:polishspace-complete-separable-obligation-frontier}
For every accepted $\PolishSpaceUp$ carrier $P=(M,K,D,S,R,W,H,C,G,N)$, the obligation frontier for complete-separable reads is exactly the complete-metric row $K$, the separable-metric row $D$, the $\StreamNameUp$ window row $S$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal route read through $W$, and the structural rows $H,C,G,N$ over the common metric row $M$. The frontier is the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:polish-space-complete-separable-obligation-surface}, \autoref{thm:polish-space-streamname-density-scope}, and \autoref{thm:polish-space-regseqrat-real-readback-scope}; it has no coordinate for a selected dense set, quotient completion equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the complete-separable obligation surface. The rows $M,K,D,S,R,W,H,C,G,N$ are the whole displayed packet available to the complete and separable consumers.

Apply the StreamName density scope and the RegSeqRat real readback scope. They place finite stream windows and rational readback between the common metric source and every Real-facing completion or density read.

The structural rows only preserve and name this same packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for the excluded host objects, the complete-separable obligation frontier is exactly the displayed surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polish-space stream realization obligation]
\label{thm:polishspace-stream-realization-obligation}
Every $\PolishSpaceUp$ obligation read that realizes a separable dense schedule must expose the $\StreamNameUp$ row $S$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$, and the observation ledger $W$ before either the $\CompleteMetricUp$ row $K$ or the $\SeparableMetricUp$ row $D$ is consumed as public data. The realization is the carrier-local route named by \autoref{thm:polishspace-root-countable-dense-cauchy-readback}, \autoref{thm:polishspace-regular-cauchy-root-window}, and \autoref{thm:polish-space-streamname-density-scope}, not a host enumeration, selected dense sequence, or countable-choice schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the root Cauchy-basis carrier. Its dense reads are displayed by the shared rows $M,S,R,B,A,H,C,P,N$ before the complete and separable faces are consumed.

Use the countable dense Cauchy readback and regular-Cauchy root window. They force dense realization through scheduled windows and regular rational readback, not through an unscheduled host enumeration.

Finally apply the StreamName density scope for the complete-separable obligation carrier. The same stream-readback discipline survives in the $P=(M,K,D,S,R,W,H,C,G,N)$ surface, so no selected dense sequence or countable-choice schedule is exported.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polish-space real completion consumer boundary]
\label{thm:polishspace-real-completion-consumer-boundary}
Any $\RealUp$-facing completion consumer of an accepted $\PolishSpaceUp$ carrier must pass through the complete-metric row $K$, the separable-metric row $D$, the $\StreamNameUp$ row $S$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$, the real-readback ledger $W$, and the structural rows $H,C,G,N$ over the common metric row $M$. The boundary is read through \autoref{thm:polishspace-real-regseqrat-readback-nonescape}, \autoref{thm:polishspace-real-regseqrat-nonescape}, and \autoref{thm:polish-space-complete-separable-obligation-surface}; it exports no ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, selected limit, quotient completion equality, host topology, or separate real-name source.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted Polish carrier and the complete-separable obligation surface. The only Real-facing rows available to a completion consumer are the StreamName, RegSeqRat, and observation rows attached to the common metric source.

Apply the two real-readback nonescape theorems. They force every Real-facing read through the regular-rational window and rule out an alternate real-name or ambient-completeness source.

The complete and separable rows remain tied to the same metric packet by transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Hence a real completion consumer receives only the displayed rows named in the statement.
\end{proof}
