Following Bishop and Bridges, a located interval subdivision is a finite mesh of interval cells whose endpoints are read through located real names and whose rational and dyadic windows remain visible at every handoff. The $\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp$ packet sits after the located interval carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert} and the rational subdivision carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rational-interval-subdivision-namecert}. It supplies compact-interval, finite-net, and uniform-modulus consumers with a displayed subdivision route through $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\RationalIntervalSubdivisionUp$, $\DyadicIntervalUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$, without importing open-cover compactness, choice-selected subdivisions, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Located interval subdivision carrier]
\label{def:located-interval-subdivision-carrier}
A $\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(L,R,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the source $\LocatedIntervalUp$ row, exposing the lower and upper located endpoint names, rational containment ledger, dyadic refinement rows, $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readbacks, and terminal $\RealUp$ seals.
\item $R$ is the finite $\RationalIntervalSubdivisionUp$ row obtained from a rational cell of $L$.
\item $D$ is the componentwise $\DyadicIntervalUp$ mesh ledger, recording the dyadic cells refining the rational subdivision windows.
\item $W$ is the located endpoint-window row. It attaches the lower and upper located real endpoint windows of $L$ to the subdivision cells named by $R$ and $D$.
\item $Q$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the finite mesh, retaining the stream windows and regular rational comparisons used by each inspected endpoint.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $L,R,D,W,Q$ have all been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the located interval row, rational subdivision row, dyadic mesh, endpoint windows, stream and regular-rational readbacks, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a subdivision request through located endpoint inspection, rational subdivision admission, dyadic cell refinement, stream-window readback, regular-rational comparison, and real-seal handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\RationalIntervalSubdivisionUp$, $\DyadicIntervalUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $L,R,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an open-cover compactness proof, an infinite family of cover refinements, a choice-selected subdivision, quotient real equality, selected endpoint representatives, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Located interval subdivision NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:located-interval-subdivision-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp$ carrier $S=(L,R,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp}$ surface consists exactly of source located-interval exposure through $L$, rational subdivision admission through $R$, dyadic mesh compatibility through $D$, located endpoint-window stability through $W$, stream and regular-rational readback through $Q$, $\RealUp$ seal handoff through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:located-interval-subdivision-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes one located interval row, one rational subdivision row, one dyadic mesh ledger, one endpoint-window row, one stream and regular-rational readback row, one real seal, and the structural rows.

The located interval row is read first. It supplies the endpoint names, rational containment cell, dyadic refinements, stream windows, regular-rational readbacks, and real seals already certified by $\LocatedIntervalUp$.

The subdivision data are finite. The row $R$ lists the rational cells, while $D$ refines those cells by dyadic interval rows. The endpoint-window row $W$ ties each cell back to the lower and upper located real windows of $L$.

Finally $Q,E,H,C,P,N$ expose the readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming route. Since no carrier coordinate names an open cover, an infinite refinement family, a chosen subdivision, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Located interval subdivision dyadic mesh handoff]
\label{thm:located-interval-subdivision-dyadic-mesh-handoff}
Every finite-net, compact-interval, or uniform-modulus consumer of an accepted $\LocatedIntervalSubdivisionUp$ packet must factor through the displayed route
$$
\LocatedIntervalUp \longmapsto \RationalIntervalSubdivisionUp \longmapsto \DyadicIntervalUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
Concretely, the consumer first reads the source located interval row $L$, then the rational subdivision row $R$, then the dyadic mesh ledger $D$, then the located endpoint windows $W$, then the stream and regular-rational readbacks $Q$, and only then the real seal $E$, with transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the source located interval. By \autoref{def:located-interval-subdivision-carrier}, every accepted subdivision read must expose $L$ before any mesh cell is consumed.

Attach the rational subdivision row. It gives a finite ordered rational cell family, and the dyadic mesh row $D$ refines those cells componentwise through $\DyadicIntervalUp$ rather than through an unbounded refinement choice.

Now read the endpoint windows and rational readbacks. The row $W$ keeps the subdivision cells tied to the located endpoint windows of $L$, while $Q$ records the $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ data needed before a $\RealUp$ seal is visible.

The final handoff is the row $E$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Thus a consumer that reaches a finite-net, compact-interval, or uniform-modulus read without displaying the stated chain is outside the accepted packet.
\end{proof}
