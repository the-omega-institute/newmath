\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ArtinReesUp$}

Following Atiyah--Macdonald on the Artin--Rees lemma, the $\ArtinReesUp$ packet records the finite filtration interface by which an ideal-adic filtration on a displayed module is compared with the induced filtration on a displayed submodule. It sits after the ring carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert}, the ideal carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ideal-namecert}, the module carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-module-namecert}, and the Noetherian module surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-noetherian-module-namecert}. The packet names only carrier rows, filtration rows, stabilization bounds, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not import quotient-module classification, selected generators, ambient Noetherian induction, or host equality of filtrations.

\begin{definition}[Artin--Rees carrier]
\label{def:artin-rees-carrier}
An $\ArtinReesUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\mathsf{ArtinRees}(R,I,M,L,F,A,H,Q,P,N).
$$
The row $R$ is the $\RingUp$ source surface, $I$ is the $\IdealUp$ row over that ring, $M$ is the ambient $\ModuleUp$ row, and $L$ is the displayed submodule row read inside the same module classifier. The row $F$ records the finite ideal-adic filtration windows $I^nM$ and $I^nM\cap L$ visible to the packet. The row $A$ is the Artin--Rees stabilization bound, asserting that after a displayed finite stage the induced filtration on $L$ is read through the same ideal power route. The row $H$ is componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the ring, ideal, module, submodule, filtration, and stabilization rows; $Q$ is displayed $\Cont$ replay; $P$ is $\Pkg$ provenance; and $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ArtinReesUp}$ row.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Artin--Rees NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:artin-rees-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ArtinReesUp$ carrier $\mathsf{ArtinRees}(R,I,M,L,F,A,H,Q,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ArtinReesUp}$ surface consists exactly of source exposure for $\RingUp$, $\IdealUp$, $\ModuleUp$, and $\NoetherianModuleUp$; submodule exposure through $L$; ideal-adic filtration exposure through $F$; finite stabilization access through $A$; componentwise transport through $H$; displayed replay through $Q$; provenance through $P$; and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed packet of \autoref{def:artin-rees-carrier}. The rows $R,I,M,L$ fix the algebraic sources before any filtration comparison is read.

The filtration row $F$ exposes only the finite windows named by the packet, and the stabilization row $A$ gives the finite stage at which the induced filtration on the submodule is read through the same ideal power route. This is the Artin--Rees obligation surface, not a quotient-module classifier or selected generator list.

Transport $H$ and replay $Q$ preserve those rows componentwise. Provenance $P$ and local naming $N$ then repack the same finite inventory as the local certificate, so no hidden Noetherian induction or host filtration equality enters the obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Artin--Rees module-ideal consumer]
\label{thm:artin-rees-module-ideal-consumer}
Every downstream algebraic consumer that cites $\ArtinReesUp$ must first read the same ring row $R$, ideal row $I$, module row $M$, submodule row $L$, filtration row $F$, stabilization row $A$, transport row $H$, replay row $Q$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$. The consumer may use the packet as a finite module--ideal stabilization certificate, but it receives no selected basis, quotient-module classification, maximal-submodule choice, or ambient equality of filtrations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:artin-rees-namecert-obligations}. They force every read to expose the ring, ideal, module, Noetherian module, and submodule sources before the filtration rows are available.

The rows $F$ and $A$ provide the only stabilization data available to a module--ideal consumer. Since they are displayed rows inside the same packet, the consumer cannot bypass them through a host Artin--Rees theorem or through quotient-module classification.

Finally, $H,Q,P,N$ preserve the route as transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Thus the downstream consumer receives exactly the finite stabilization certificate and nothing outside the displayed carrier.
\end{proof}
