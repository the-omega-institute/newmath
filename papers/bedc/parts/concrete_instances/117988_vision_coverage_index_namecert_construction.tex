The $\VisionCoverageIndexUp$ packet realizes the top-level vision routing surface of \autoref{ch:capstones} as finite BEDC data. It records which exploratory source label is covered by which concrete-instance target, which marker row witnesses the coverage, and which replay/provenance rows make the route auditable. The packet is an index certificate for vision coverage accounting; it is not a new mathematical theorem about the source chapters, not a semantic endorsement of every vision paragraph, and not a replacement for the concrete-instance chapters named by its rows.

\begin{definition}[Vision coverage index carrier]
\label{def:vision-coverage-index-carrier}
A $\VisionCoverageIndexUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
V=(S,T,M,L,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the finite source-label row naming a vision chapter, section, or routing paragraph.
\item $T$ is the concrete-target row naming the realized chapter label under \texttt{concrete\_instances/}.
\item $M$ is the marker ledger row containing the displayed \texttt{\textbackslash concretizedIn} route from $S$ to $T$.
\item $L$ is the local coverage ledger, recording whether the row is root-index coverage, sibling coverage, bridge-facing coverage, or replay-only coverage.
\item $R$ is the replay row that lets an audit reread the target chapter without relying on cached coverage counts.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source labels, target labels, marker rows, coverage ledgers, replay rows, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from source label to target label through the marker ledger and the replay row.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\VisionCoverageIndexUp$, the referenced vision source, the referenced concrete-instance target, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\VisionCoverageIndexUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,T,M,L,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for hidden coverage totals, cached root counts, semantic approval of source prose, or a target chapter that lacks a concrete label.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Vision coverage index NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:vision-coverage-index-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\VisionCoverageIndexUp$ carrier $V=(S,T,M,L,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\VisionCoverageIndexUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the source-label row $S$, concrete-target row $T$, marker ledger $M$, coverage ledger $L$, replay row $R$, componentwise transport $H$, displayed replay $C$, package provenance $P$, and local naming row $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:vision-coverage-index-carrier}. The coverage data visible to the certificate are exactly the source label, target label, marker ledger, local coverage ledger, and replay row.

The marker row binds source to target. It records the displayed concretization route and therefore cannot be replaced by a cached count or by prose that merely resembles a coverage claim.

The replay row keeps the certificate auditable. It points back to the target chapter so that a consumer can inspect the finite NameCert rows named by $T$.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since the carrier has no hidden total or semantic-approval coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Vision coverage replay handoff]
\label{thm:vision-coverage-index-replay-handoff}
Every coverage consumer of an accepted $\VisionCoverageIndexUp$ packet factors through
$$
S \longmapsto M \longmapsto T \longmapsto R \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus a vision source is counted as concretely covered only through a displayed marker ledger and a replayable concrete-instance target; the packet exports no hidden coverage count, no semantic endorsement of the source prose, and no target without a concrete chapter label.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:vision-coverage-index-namecert-obligations}. They require the source label, target label, marker ledger, coverage ledger, and replay row before any coverage consumer reads the packet.

The marker ledger $M$ is the only source-to-target edge. The target row $T$ is therefore not inferred from the source title or from a cached index; it is displayed by the packet.

The replay row $R$ makes the target inspectable. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that route under transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming.

Consequently coverage accounting is finite BEDC data routed through the displayed marker and replay rows.
\end{proof}
