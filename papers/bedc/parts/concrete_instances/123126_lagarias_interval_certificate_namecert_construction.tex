\begin{definition}[Lagarias interval certificate carrier]
\label{def:lagarias-interval-certificate-carrier}
A $\LagariasIntervalCertificateUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$

$$
Here $n$ is a positive $\NatUp$ index, $q$ is the precision budget, $a,b$ are rational endpoints, $D$ is the divisor-sum row for $\sigma(n)$, $H$ is the harmonic-sum row for $H_n$, $E$ is the exp-log approximation row for $\exp(H_n)\log(H_n)$, $B$ is the $\mathsf{LagRHS}$ enclosure row proving $a\leq \mathsf{LagRHS}(n)\leq b$, $X$ is the rational comparison row witnessing the upper-bound comparison used by a counter event, $T$ is componentwise $\hsame$ transport, $C$ is displayed $\Cont$ replay, $P$ is $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LagariasIntervalCertificateUp$, $\NatUp$, $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, and $\Cont$, and $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LagariasIntervalCertificateUp}$ row.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lagarias interval certificate NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lagarias-interval-certificate-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted carrier $L=(n,q,a,b,D,H,E,B,X,T,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LagariasIntervalCertificateUp}$ surface consists exactly of the positive index and precision rows, rational endpoint rows, divisor-sum row, harmonic-sum row, exp-log approximation row, $\mathsf{LagRHS}$ enclosure row, comparison row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the accepted packet in row order. The index and precision rows determine the finite arithmetic request, while the endpoint, divisor, harmonic, exp-log, enclosure, and comparison rows provide the certificate payload.

Transport, replay, provenance, and the local name attach those rows to one finite packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for an exact real-number oracle, an unbounded host computation, or an uninspected arithmetic predicate, the displayed rows exhaust the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}

The comparison row $X$ compares those displayed rational bounds. Therefore a public counter event can be produced only after the packet exposes $B$ and $X$ on the same transport, replay, provenance, and local-name route.
\end{proof}
