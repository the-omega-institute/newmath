Following Herbrand's theorem as a proof-search interface for first-order logic, the $\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp$ packet records only the finite disjunction certificate surfaced by a displayed syntactic proof-search route. It sits over the first-order syntax rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-firstorder-namecert}, the model-theory boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-modeltheory-namecert}, the typed MetaCIC example gallery of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metacictypedexamplesgallery-namecert}, the GroundCompiler audit packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-groundcompiler-audit-packet-namecert}, and the local NameCert discipline used by the proof-tree siblings \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-natural-deduction-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sequent-calculus-namecert}. The packet names a finite certificate surface; it does not claim host completeness, infinite proof search, semantic truth in all models, choice, or quotient proof equality.

\begin{definition}[Finite Herbrand disjunction carrier]
\label{def:finite-herbrand-disjunction-carrier}
A $\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
H=(F,D,W,S,Q,R,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is the $\FirstOrderUp$ formula row containing the prenex source formula, free-variable ledger, quantifier-prefix ledger, and finite term-source alphabet;
\item $D$ is the finite disjunct inventory, listing the ground instances admitted into the Herbrand disjunction;
\item $W$ is the witness-term and substitution row, attaching each disjunct in $D$ to a displayed tuple of terms and a substitution readback from $F$;
\item $S$ is the syntactic checker row for the displayed disjunction, read through natural-deduction, sequent-calculus, or MetaCIC-facing proof rows only as finite proof-search data;
\item $Q$ is the proof-search route row, recording the finite branch order, branch closures, and checker calls that produced $D,W,S$;
\item $R$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source formula, disjunct inventory, witness-term rows, checker route, proof-search route, replay row, provenance row, and local naming row;
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the source formula through $Q$ and then through $D,W,S,R,P,N$;
\item $C$ is the consumer handoff row exposing the certificate to $\MetaCICTypedExamplesGalleryUp$ and $\GroundCompilerAuditPacketUp$ only after the finite disjunct inventory and checker route are visible;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp$, $\FirstOrderUp$, $\ModelTheoryUp$, $\MetaCICTypedExamplesGalleryUp$, $\GroundCompilerAuditPacketUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $F,D,W,S,Q,R,T,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for semantic completeness, truth in every structure, infinite proof search, selected model construction, choice, quotient proof equality, or a host proof checker outside the displayed rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Herbrand disjunction NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-herbrand-disjunction-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp$ carrier $H=(F,D,W,S,Q,R,T,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp}$ surface consists exactly of first-order formula exposure through $F$, finite disjunct inventory exposure through $D$, witness-term and substitution exposure through $W$, checker exposure through $S$, proof-search route exposure through $Q$, componentwise transport through $R$, displayed replay through $T$, downstream certificate handoff through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-herbrand-disjunction-carrier}. The source syntax is $F$, the finite Herbrand inventory is $D$, and the witness-term data are exactly the substitution rows in $W$.

The checker and search data are also displayed. The row $S$ records the finite checker calls, while $Q$ records the branch order and branch closures that led to the disjunction.

Transport, replay, and consumer exposure are then forced through $R,T,C$. The package row $P$ records the cited FirstOrder, ModelTheory, MetaCIC gallery, GroundCompiler, and NameCert dependencies, and $N$ names only this displayed packet.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for semantic completeness, truth in every structure, infinite proof search, selected model construction, choice, quotient proof equality, or a host proof checker outside the displayed rows, no local NameCert obligation can export those principles.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Herbrand disjunction non-escape]
\label{thm:finite-herbrand-disjunction-nonescape}
Every accepted $\FiniteHerbrandDisjunctionUp$ consumer reads the Herbrand certificate only through the finite route
$$
F\longmapsto Q\longmapsto(D,W,S)\longmapsto(R,T,C,P,N).
$$
The consumer may cite the route as a displayed finite disjunction certificate for a first-order proof-search branch, but it may not read the packet as host completeness, infinite proof search, model-theoretic truth, choice, quotient proof equality, or an ambient proof checker.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:finite-herbrand-disjunction-namecert-obligations}. They force every accepted read to expose the source formula, the finite disjunct inventory, the witness-term substitutions, the checker row, and the proof-search route before any downstream consumer sees the packet.

The consumer handoff row $C$ can then pass the certificate to MetaCIC or GroundCompiler-facing rows only after the finite inventory and checker route have been replayed through $T$ and transported through $R$.

The non-escape clauses are the non-coordinates of \autoref{def:finite-herbrand-disjunction-carrier}. Because none of host completeness, infinite proof search, model-theoretic truth, choice, quotient proof equality, or an ambient proof checker appears in $H$, no accepted consumer can project such data from the packet.
\end{proof}
