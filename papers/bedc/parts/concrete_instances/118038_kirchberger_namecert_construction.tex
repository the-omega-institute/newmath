Following Kirchberger's finite convex separation theorem, the $\KirchbergerUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface for detecting separation of two finitely displayed point families by checking bounded small subfamilies. It sits over the affine-coordinate carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-affinespace-namecert}, the convex-set carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the polytope row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-polytope-namecert}, and the finite-dimensional separating-functional row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-dimensional-hahn-banach-separation-namecert}. \begin{definition}[Kirchberger finite separation carrier]
\label{def:kirchberger-finite-separation-carrier}
A $\KirchbergerUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(A,C,D,P,S,L,E,H,T,Q,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the $\AffineSpaceUp$ coordinate row, fixing the finite-dimensional affine window in which the two point families are read.
\item $C$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ row, recording the two displayed convex hull requests generated by the finite point families.
\item $D$ is the $\PolytopeUp$ row, naming the finite hull presentations and their face or vertex ledgers.
\item $P$ is the point-family row, listing the two color classes and the finite subfamily windows inspected by the certificate.
\item $S$ is the small-subset obligation row, recording the bounded subfamilies whose convex hulls must be separated.
\item $L$ is the local separation row supplied by $\FiniteDimensionalHahnBanachSeparationUp$ for each displayed small-subset request.
\item $E$ is the exactness row, asserting that every accepted small-subset request is routed through the same affine, convex, polytope, and local-separation data.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $A,C,D,P,S,L,E,T,Q,N$.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the finite point-family, subset, convex-hull, and separation rows.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KirchbergerUp$, $\AffineSpaceUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\PolytopeUp$, $\FiniteDimensionalHahnBanachSeparationUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KirchbergerUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a selected global separating hyperplane, quotient convex hull equality, ambient compactness, infinite choice over subfamilies, or an unbounded Helly-style search.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kirchberger finite subset obligations]
\label{thm:kirchberger-finite-subset-obligations}
For every accepted $\KirchbergerUp$ carrier $K=(A,C,D,P,S,L,E,H,T,Q,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\KirchbergerUp}$ surface consists exactly of affine-coordinate admission, convex-hull readback, polytope finite-hull presentation, two-color point-family exposure, small-subset obligation accounting, finite-dimensional local separation, exactness through the same displayed rows, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:kirchberger-finite-separation-carrier}. The rows $A,C,D,P,S,L,E,H,T,Q,N$ are the complete visible inventory.

The affine, convex, and polytope rows $A,C,D$ fix the ambient finite-dimensional window and the finite hull presentations. The point-family and small-subset rows $P,S$ then identify the bounded subfamilies whose separation must be displayed.

The local separation row $L$ is read only through $\FiniteDimensionalHahnBanachSeparationUp$, and the exactness row $E$ binds those local reads to the same subfamily requests. Transport, replay, provenance, and naming through $H,T,Q,N$ preserve that finite route, so no excluded global or quotient object is exported.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kirchberger NameCert nonescape]
\label{thm:kirchberger-namecert-nonescape}
An accepted $\KirchbergerUp$ packet exports no selected global separating hyperplane, no quotient equality of convex hulls, no ambient compactness theorem, no infinite subfamily choice schedule, and no unbounded Helly-family search. Every public read factors through the displayed affine window, convex and polytope hull rows, two-color point-family row, small-subset obligation ledger, finite-dimensional local-separation row, exactness row, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier coordinates. They list only the finite affine, convex, polytope, point-family, subset-obligation, local-separation, exactness, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

A global separation read must therefore pass through the bounded subfamily ledger $S$ and the local separation row $L$. The carrier has no coordinate from which a host hyperplane, quotient convex hull identity, or infinite family selection can be read.

Thus any downstream convexity consumer receives Kirchberger's finite obstruction surface as NameCert data. The excluded principles require rows absent from \autoref{def:kirchberger-finite-separation-carrier}, so they cannot escape the packet.
\end{proof}
