\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BergeMaximumUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-berge-maximum-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Berge's maximum theorem and Aubin--Frankowska set-valued analysis, the $\BergeMaximumUp$ packet records the compact-parameter, compact-valued feasible graph, and continuous real objective data needed to read the maximum-value function through finite BEDC rows. It sits over the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the continuous-map carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the upper-hemicontinuity boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-upperhemicontinuity-namecert}, and the scalar finite-net readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-extremevalue-namecert}. The packet names a maximum-value ledger, not a selected optimizer or host choice principle.

\begin{definition}[Berge maximum carrier]
\label{def:berge-maximum-carrier}
A $\BergeMaximumUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(K_0,F,G,V,M,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K_0$ is the compact parameter-source row, read through the compact metric certificate;
\item $F$ is the compact-valued feasible-graph row, including the finite graph windows used by the upper-hemicontinuity boundary;
\item $G$ is the continuous $\RealUp$-valued objective row on the displayed feasible graph;
\item $V$ is the finite-net value-window ledger for objective reads over each displayed feasible fibre;
\item $M$ is the maximum-value comparison row, obtained by folding the finite $\RealUp$ value windows;
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $K_0,F,G,V,M$ and the local naming rows;
\item $C$ stores the displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact parameter windows, feasible graph rows, objective reads, and finite value windows to the maximum-value comparison;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over the same compact-parameter and value-window packet;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BergeMaximumUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier keeps the selected-argmax socket outside the exported rows: consumers may read the value ledger $M$, but no row chooses an optimizer from a feasible fibre.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Berge maximum NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:berge-maximum-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:berge-maximum-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\BergeMaximumUp}$ surface consists of five finite obligations: carrier admission for $K$; upper-hemicontinuity and compact-value boundary for $F$ over $K_0$; continuous objective $\RealUp$ sealing for $G$; finite-net maximum-value readback through $V$ and $M$; and refusal of selected argmax data, open-cover compactness, and choice outside the displayed packet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-net maximum-value attainment route]
\label{thm:berge-maximum-finite-net-attainment}
Every accepted $\BergeMaximumUp$ packet exports its maximum-value ledger by a finite $\ProbeBundle$ fold over compact nets and $\RealUp$-sealed objective values. The exported row is the value comparison $M$ together with its $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ boundary; no exported row selects an optimizer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the compact parameter row $K_0$ and feasible graph row $F$ through the compact metric and upper-hemicontinuity certificates. These rows provide finite feasible-fibre windows for the current parameter read, rather than an ambient open-cover compactness theorem.

Next replay the continuous objective row $G$ through the $\RealUp$ seal. Each finite feasible-window read contributes a value window in $V$, and the comparison row $M$ is obtained by folding those displayed real value windows.

Finally transport the folded value row through $H$, replay it through $C$, and record its provenance in $P$ and local name in $N$. The fold exposes a maximum-value ledger, while the selected-argmax request remains outside $K_0,F,G,V,M,H,C,P,N$.
\end{proof}
