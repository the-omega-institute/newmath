\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\UniformLipschitzModulusUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-uniform-lipschitz-modulus-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop--Bridges constructive metric analysis and Kelley-style uniform-space bookkeeping, the $\UniformLipschitzModulusUp$ packet records the textbook passage from a displayed Lipschitz bound to a uniform modulus as finite certificate data. It reads the Lipschitz modulus route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lipschitz-modulus-namecert}, the constant handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lipschitz-constant-namecert}, and the surrounding metric, real, dyadic, uniform-modulus, and uniform-continuity rows. The chapter names the carrier that makes this handoff local to BEDC; it is not a host continuity theorem, a quotient-map equality, a hidden reciprocal selection, or an ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[Uniform Lipschitz modulus carrier]\label{def:uniform-lipschitz-modulus-carrier}
A $\UniformLipschitzModulusUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(X,Y,F,K,L,E,D,U,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source $\MetricUp$ row and $Y$ is the target $\MetricUp$ row.
\item $F$ is the $\LipschitzMapUp$ graph row whose source and target reads use $X$ and $Y$.
\item $K$ is the displayed $\LipschitzConstantUp$ row for the same source, target, and graph data.
\item $L$ is the accepted $\LipschitzModulusUp$ row that turns the displayed bound into a tolerance route.
\item $E$ is the positive target-tolerance row, read through $\RealUp$ before any source-tolerance request is exported.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ division and rounding ledger used by $K,L,E$.
\item $U$ is the exported $\UniformModulusUp$ row.
\item $W$ is the $\UniformContinuityUp$ consumer row that may read $U$ only after $X,Y,F,K,L,E,D$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for metric rows, graph rows, bound rows, modulus rows, tolerance rows, dyadic ledger rows, uniform-continuity consumers, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a target-tolerance request through $E,K,L,D,U,W$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\UniformLipschitzModulusUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\LipschitzMapUp$, $\LipschitzConstantUp$, $\LipschitzModulusUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\UniformContinuityUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\UniformLipschitzModulusUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier accepts only packets with these rows. It has no coordinate for host continuity, quotient function equality, implicit reciprocal choice, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient metric or real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Lipschitz modulus NameCert obligations]\label{thm:uniform-lipschitz-modulus-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\UniformLipschitzModulusUp$ carrier $Q=(X,Y,F,K,L,E,D,U,W,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\UniformLipschitzModulusUp}$ surface consists exactly of five finite obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the two metric rows, Lipschitz graph row, displayed constant row, Lipschitz modulus row, positive tolerance row, dyadic division and rounding ledger, exported uniform modulus, uniform-continuity consumer, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item source-target alignment, requiring $F,K,L$ to read the same $X$ and $Y$ before any tolerance conversion is exported;
\item tolerance route exactness, requiring $E$ to be displayed before $D$ and requiring $D$ to be the only division and rounding ledger used by $U$;
\item uniform-consumer handoff, requiring every $\UniformModulusUp$ or $\UniformContinuityUp$ read to pass through $K,L,E,D,U$ in that order;
\item ledger non-escape, excluding host continuity, quotient map equality, hidden reciprocal choice, selected limits, countable choice, and ambient completeness.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:uniform-lipschitz-modulus-carrier}. The displayed packet exposes exactly the metric, graph, constant, Lipschitz-modulus, tolerance, dyadic, exported-modulus, consumer, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

The source-target alignment obligation follows from the shared positions of $X$ and $Y$ in $F,K,L$. A consumer cannot read a bound or modulus row for a different metric pair because no such coordinate occurs in $Q$.

The tolerance and handoff obligations use the same finite route as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lipschitz-modulus-namecert} and the displayed constant handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lipschitz-constant-namecert}. The positive tolerance $E$ is read before the dyadic ledger $D$, and $U$ is the only exported $\UniformModulusUp$ row available to $W$.

The refusal clause is an inventory check on the carrier. None of the displayed rows supplies host continuity, quotient equality, hidden reciprocal selection, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Lipschitz modulus consumer boundary]\label{thm:uniform-lipschitz-modulus-consumer-boundary}
Let $Q=(X,Y,F,K,L,E,D,U,W,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\UniformLipschitzModulusUp$ carrier. Every downstream $\UniformModulusUp$ or $\UniformContinuityUp$ consumer receives the source-tolerance route only through the displayed Lipschitz constant $K$, the Lipschitz modulus row $L$, the positive target tolerance $E$, the dyadic ledger $D$, and the exported row $U$. The consumer boundary is therefore a finite BEDC handoff rather than a rereading of a host Lipschitz proof.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The only graph row is $F$, the only displayed constant row is $K$, and the only Lipschitz-modulus row is $L$; all three read the same source and target metric rows.

The target-tolerance request is $E$. It reaches a source-tolerance request only through the dyadic division and rounding ledger $D$, so no hidden reciprocal or selected real approximation can replace the finite arithmetic row.

The exported row $U$ is then the sole $\UniformModulusUp$ coordinate available to $W$. Componentwise transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve this order, while $P$ and $N$ record the same finite packet.
\end{proof}
