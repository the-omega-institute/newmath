\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LebesgueNumberMeshUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-lebesgue-number-mesh-namecert}
\origin{human}

The $\LebesgueNumberMeshUp$ packet is grounded in the Lebesgue number lemma for compact metric spaces: a finite cover of a compact metric source yields a positive mesh radius whose balls refine the cover. In BEDC form the packet records only finite cover mesh, compact-net, local-radius, rational lower-bound, and uniform-modulus handoff data. It realizes the compact-uniform-continuity route of \autoref{ch:visions-compact-uniform-continuity} after the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the continuous-map local-radius source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the uniform-modulus consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformmodulus-namecert}, and the rational lower-bound fold surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert}. The packet is not a host compactness theorem, a chosen open-cover subcover, a least radius, quotient map equality, countable choice, or ambient metric completion.

\begin{definition}[Lebesgue number mesh carrier]
\label{def:lebesgue-number-mesh-carrier}
A $\LebesgueNumberMeshUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(K,V,G,O,B,Q,U,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row whose finite net is being inspected.
\item $V$ is the finite cover mesh row, recording the cover cells and the mesh-to-cover incidence data.
\item $G$ is the compact-net $\ProbeBundle$ row whose probes name the finite centers used to test the cover.
\item $O$ is the local radius ledger, assigning to each displayed probe the cover radius admitted by the local continuity or cover-membership read.
\item $B$ is the $\RatUp$ lower-bound fold over the finitely many positive local radii.
\item $Q$ is the mesh-refinement witness saying that the folded rational bound refines the cover mesh.
\item $U$ is the $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff row exposed only after $K,V,G,O,B,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for compact source, cover mesh, compact net, local radii, rational fold, refinement, uniform handoff, replay, provenance, and local name.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact-net exposure through local-radius reads, rational lower-bound folding, mesh refinement, and uniform-modulus handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LebesgueNumberMeshUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\RatUp$, $\ProbeBundle$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueNumberMeshUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through this finite mesh and lower-bound surface.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lebesgue number mesh NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lebesgue-number-mesh-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LebesgueNumberMeshUp$ carrier $L=(K,V,G,O,B,Q,U,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueNumberMeshUp}$ surface consists exactly of compact-metric source admission through $K$, finite cover mesh exposure through $V$, compact-net $\ProbeBundle$ exposure through $G$, local radius ledger exposure through $O$, $\RatUp$ lower-bound folding through $B$, mesh-refinement exposure through $Q$, $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff through $U$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:lebesgue-number-mesh-carrier}. The compact source $K$, finite cover mesh $V$, and compact-net row $G$ are displayed before any lower-bound or uniform-modulus row is available.

Read the radius data next. The local radius ledger $O$ attaches one positive rational radius to each displayed probe, and the $\RatUp$ fold $B$ produces the common lower bound from that finite family. The mesh-refinement row $Q$ records that this bound makes each inspected mesh ball land inside a cover cell.

The handoff row $U$ is therefore downstream of finite cover, compact-net, local-radius, and rational-fold data. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route, so the packet exports no host compactness theorem, selected subcover, least-radius principle, quotient map equality, choice functional, or ambient completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lebesgue number mesh uniform-modulus extraction]
\label{thm:lebesgue-number-mesh-uniform-modulus-extraction}
Every compact-uniform-continuity consumer of an accepted $\LebesgueNumberMeshUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
K,V,G,O,B,Q,U,H,C,P,N
$$
before consuming the exported $\UniformModulusUp$ row. The extracted modulus is the tolerance-only handoff obtained from finite cover mesh data, compact-net probes, local radii, and a rational lower-bound fold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:lebesgue-number-mesh-namecert-obligations}. It places $U$ after the compact source, finite cover mesh, compact net, local radii, rational fold, and mesh-refinement witness.

The extraction is a finite fold. Each probe in $G$ contributes one radius row in $O$, and the $\RatUp$ row $B$ folds these finitely many positive radii into a common lower bound. Row $Q$ then records that this bound refines the finite cover mesh.

Only after those rows are exposed may a compact-uniform-continuity consumer read $U$ as a $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff. Since the carrier contains no host compactness, cover selector, least-radius operator, quotient equality, choice, or ambient completion coordinate, the exported modulus is exactly the finite mesh route described above.
\end{proof}
