\begin{theorem}[Arzela--Ascoli finite-modulus equicontinuity forward anchor]
\label{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-equicontinuity-forward-anchor}
Every $\ArzelaAscoliFiniteModulusUp$ finite-modulus read first consumes the $\EquicontinuityUp$ family-window and shared-radius rows before it exports compact finite-subfamily data. In carrier terms, the read must expose the equicontinuity compact source, continuous-family ledger, tolerance row, shared radius row, uniform-modulus handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-equicontinuity-namecert}; only then may the Arzela--Ascoli finite-modulus packet read its compact finite-subfamily, diagonal, modulus, and selector-facing rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the existing finite-modulus rows through the equicontinuity anchor of \autoref{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-equicontinuity-anchor}. That route already places the $\EquicontinuityUp$ compact source, family ledger, tolerance, shared radius, uniform-modulus handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming before any finite-modulus consumer is public.

Use \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-equicontinuity-namecert} to identify the family-window and shared-radius rows as substantive sibling data rather than optional annotations. The finite-modulus packet can therefore export compact finite-subfamily data only after those rows have been consumed.

Finally apply the downstream dependency routes of this chapter, which read finite subfamilies, diagonal data, compactness-facing handoffs, and selector rows as Arzela--Ascoli consumers of the equicontinuity family surface. This proves the forward anchor without adding a second source for compactness or equicontinuity.
\end{proof}
