\begin{theorem}[Arzela--Ascoli sibling route export]
\label{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-sibling-route}
Every selector-facing export from an accepted $\ArzelaAscoliFiniteModulusUp$ packet factors through the displayed sibling route
$$
\CompactMetricUp,\quad \EquicontinuityUp,\quad \CompactUniformContinuityUp,\quad \UniformModulusUp,\quad \StreamNameUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp,\quad \DyadicRatCoreUp.
$$
The export may read compact finite nets, equicontinuity bounds, compact-uniform-continuity transfer, tolerance-to-precision ledgers, finite stream windows, regular rational readback, the terminal real seal, and dyadic tolerance arithmetic; it does not obtain a selector row from host compactness, selected subsequence choice, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the forward lattice of \autoref{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-forward-lattice}. It places compact metric finite-net data, continuous-map inspection, finite common modulus rows, stream windows, regular rational readback, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, and dyadic tolerance arithmetic before any selector-facing handoff is public.

The sibling routes refine that lattice. The compact-metric, equicontinuity, compact-uniform-continuity, and uniform-modulus dependency theorems force $\CompactMetricUp$, $\EquicontinuityUp$, $\CompactUniformContinuityUp$, and $\UniformModulusUp$ rows to be displayed before the finite-modulus export can be consumed.

The L10 readback rows are then fixed by the same carrier. $\StreamNameUp$ supplies finite windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ supplies regular rational schedules, $\RealUp$ seals the terminal values, and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ supplies the tolerance arithmetic. Since the carrier has no coordinate for host compactness, selected subsequence choice, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness, every selector-facing export factors through the stated sibling route.
\end{proof}
