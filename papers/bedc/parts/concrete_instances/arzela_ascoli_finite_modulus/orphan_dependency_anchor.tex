\begin{theorem}[Arzela--Ascoli sibling lattice repair]
\label{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-sibling-lattice-repair}
Every selector-facing export from an accepted $\ArzelaAscoliFiniteModulusUp$ packet consumes the sibling rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactuniformcontinuity-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-equicontinuity-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, namely
$$
\CompactMetricUp,\quad \CompactUniformContinuityUp,\quad \EquicontinuityUp,\quad \StreamNameUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp,\quad \DyadicRatCoreUp
$$
before any finite-modulus selector row is public. The export therefore reads compact source data, compact-uniform continuity data, equicontinuity discipline, finite stream windows, regular-rational readback, the terminal real-name seal, and dyadic tolerance arithmetic as separate certificate rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-carrier}. Its selector-facing surface already exposes the compact source row, finite window row, regular rational readback, real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Apply the sibling lattice routes recorded in \autoref{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-forward-lattice}, \autoref{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-compactmetric-forward-reference}, \autoref{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-compact-uniform-repair}, and \autoref{thm:arzela-ascoli-finite-modulus-sibling-dependency-route}. These routes place $\CompactMetricUp$, $\CompactUniformContinuityUp$, and $\EquicontinuityUp$ before the exported selector row.

The same carrier supplies $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, the $\RealUp$ seal, and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ arithmetic. Since the export is obtained only after those displayed rows are read, no selector-facing consumer can bypass the horizontal dependency lattice.
\end{proof}
