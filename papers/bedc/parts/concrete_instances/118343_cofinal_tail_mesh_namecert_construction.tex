\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CofinalTailMeshUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-cofinal-tail-mesh-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on regular Cauchy completion, the $\CofinalTailMeshUp$ packet records the shared tail-window mesh used when several regular rational names are read before a Real or Completion consumer may inspect a terminal seal. It sits over the finite stream schedules of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular Cauchy tail-meet route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-tail-meet-namecert}, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and completion-facing Cauchy routes. The packet names a common cofinal window surface; it does not quotient streams, select limits, invoke countable choice, or import ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[CofinalTailMesh carrier]
\label{def:cofinal-tail-mesh-carrier}
A $\CofinalTailMeshUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(S,R,D,M_0,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule family naming the finite windows inspected for every participating regular Cauchy name.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to those windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used by every mesh comparison.
\item $M_0$ is the regular Cauchy tail-meet row, recording the common cofinal tail at which the named reads are compared.
\item $W$ is the shared cofinal window row consumed before any $\RealUp$ or completion-facing seal is read.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $S,R,D,M_0,W$ and for the structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for opening the stream windows, reading regular rational tails, checking dyadic tolerances, and handing the shared window to downstream consumers.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CofinalTailMeshUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, regular Cauchy completion routes, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CofinalTailMeshUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through the displayed rows $S,R,D,M_0,W,H,C,P,N$. No coordinate stores quotient stream equality, a selected limit, a host cofinal subsequence, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CofinalTailMesh NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cofinal-tail-mesh-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CofinalTailMeshUp$ carrier $M=(S,R,D,M_0,W,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CofinalTailMeshUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, StreamName window scheduling, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance comparison, regular Cauchy tail-meet admission, shared cofinal window handoff, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every consumer must read the stream schedules and dyadic ledger before it can consume the shared window row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cofinal-tail-mesh-carrier}. The visible inventory is $S,R,D,M_0,W,H,C,P,N$, and each row has a fixed role in the mesh.

The stream and rational rows are read first. The schedule row $S$ opens the finite windows, while $R$ supplies regular rational readback for those same windows.

The dyadic and tail-meet rows then bind the comparison. The row $D$ supplies the tolerance ledger, and $M_0$ records the common regular Cauchy tail at which the participating reads are compared before $W$ exposes the shared cofinal window.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since quotient streams, selected limits, host cofinal subsequences, countable choice, and ambient completeness have no carrier coordinate, they are not NameCert obligations of the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CofinalTailMesh Real-completion route]
\label{thm:cofinal-tail-mesh-real-completion-route}
Every Real or Completion consumer of an accepted $\CofinalTailMeshUp$ carrier receives the finite route
$$
S \longrightarrow R \longrightarrow D \longrightarrow M_0 \longrightarrow W \longrightarrow H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer reads finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat tails, a DyadicRatCore tolerance ledger, a regular Cauchy tail meet, and one shared cofinal window before any terminal $\RealUp$ or completion seal can be consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from carrier admission in \autoref{def:cofinal-tail-mesh-carrier}. The only completion-facing row is the shared window $W$, and it is stored after the stream schedule, regular rational readback, dyadic ledger, and tail-meet rows.

Apply \autoref{thm:cofinal-tail-mesh-namecert-obligations}. It requires the stream schedules and dyadic ledger to be read before the shared window handoff, so a Real-facing read cannot bypass $S,R,D,M_0$.

The replay row $C$ and transport row $H$ preserve the same route for downstream consumers, while $P$ and $N$ name the provenance and local certificate. Therefore the route exports a finite mesh and not a quotient completion, selected representative, countable-choice tail, or ambient complete-space witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CofinalTailMesh nonescape]
\label{thm:cofinal-tail-mesh-nonescape}
Any accepted $\CofinalTailMeshUp$ read is exhausted by $S,R,D,M_0,W,H,C,P,N$. It cannot produce quotient equality of streams, a selected regular Cauchy representative, a global cofinal subsequence, countable choice, a completed host limit, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier definition gives the complete coordinate list. A consumer may inspect the finite stream schedule, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance ledger, regular Cauchy tail meet, shared cofinal window, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

The Real-completion route of \autoref{thm:cofinal-tail-mesh-real-completion-route} keeps every value-facing handoff inside that list. Its only terminal-facing row is $W$, and $W$ is reached through $S,R,D,M_0$.

The excluded objects would require additional carrier coordinates or an external selection principle. Since neither appears in $M=(S,R,D,M_0,W,H,C,P,N)$, the accepted read remains within the finite NameCert packet.
\end{proof}
