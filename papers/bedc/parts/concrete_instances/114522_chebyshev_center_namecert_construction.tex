\begin{definition}[Chebyshev center carrier]
\label{def:chebyshev-center-carrier}
A $\ChebyshevCenterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Z=(M,K,V,L,R,S,C,A,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the source $\MetricUp$ row, naming the admitted points, distance comparisons, triangle ledger, and closed-ball tests used by the packet.
\item $K$ is the compact metric row, recording the finite cover and total-boundedness observations consumed by radius comparisons.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ row, exposing the displayed convex subcarrier and the admitted affine-combination operations.
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedSetUp$ row, recording located membership and distance-to-set comparisons for the candidate set.
\item $R$ is the located-radius row, listing the finite radius requests, comparison tolerances, and scalar bounds read through $\RealUp$.
\item $S$ is the center-candidate row, naming the inspected candidates and the ball-containment observations certified for each candidate.
\item $C$ is the compact-radius ledger. It records that every displayed finite cover and center-candidate comparison is evaluated through $M,K,L,R,S$ before a radius bound is read.
\item $A$ is the admissible-center row, exposing the local witness that a candidate is read as an accepted center only after the displayed radius ledger has been inspected.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for metric, compact, convex, located, radius, center-candidate, radius-ledger, admissible-center, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$

\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ChebyshevCenterUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\MetricProjectionUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\LocatedSetUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ChebyshevCenterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,K,V,L,R,S,C,A,H,T,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host minimizer, selected center outside the displayed candidates, quotient compactness, ambient Hilbert projection theorem, separating functional, choice schedule, or global uniqueness claim.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Chebyshev center NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:chebyshev-center-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ChebyshevCenterUp$ carrier
$$
Z=(M,K,V,L,R,S,C,A,H,T,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ChebyshevCenterUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, metric-source admission, compact finite-cover exposure, convex-carrier admission, located-set readback, located-radius comparison, center-candidate visibility, compact-radius ledger exactness, admissible-center exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:chebyshev-center-carrier}. The rows $M,K,V,L,R,S,C,A,H,T,P,N$ are the visible inventory, so any local consumer first sees the metric, compact, convex, located, radius, candidate, ledger, admissible-center, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

Metric and compact admission are read from $M,K$. The packet can inspect distance comparisons and finite-cover data, but it cannot replace them with a host compactness theorem or a selected minimizing sequence.

Convex and located data are read from $V,L$. These rows expose the set in which candidates are tested and the located comparisons used to read distances to the displayed set.

The radius and center obligations are then read from $R,S,C,A$. A center is accepted only through the displayed compact-radius ledger, so the certificate records a finite radius comparison surface rather than an ambient minimizer.

Finally $H,T,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same rows. Since the carrier has no coordinate for quotient compactness, a selected center outside $S$, ambient convex duality, choice, \texttt{Classical.choice}, or \texttt{Quot.sound}, none of those objects can be exported by the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Chebyshev compact-radius ledger]
\label{thm:chebyshev-center-compact-radius-ledger}
Every $\ChebyshevCenterUp$ radius read factors through the displayed compact metric, located-set, and center-candidate rows before it reaches a scalar bound:
$$
M,K,V,L \longmapsto R,S \longmapsto C,A \longmapsto H,T,P,N .
$$
Thus a public consumer receives finite compact-cover comparisons, located-radius requests, and admissible-center evidence before any center or radius datum is visible. The route does not assert uniqueness of centers, existence of a host minimizer, selected projection, quotient compactness, or ambient convex duality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a radius-facing consumer of $Z$. The consumer cannot read a scalar radius directly, because the carrier places the metric row $M$, compact row $K$, convex row $V$, and located row $L$ before any radius or candidate row.

The located-radius row $R$ and center-candidate row $S$ are then inspected together. Radius comparisons are meaningful only for the displayed candidate and set data, so the route remains local to the packet.

The compact-radius ledger $C$ and admissible-center row $A$ bind those comparisons to finite cover observations and ball-containment tests. This is the finite NameCert surface corresponding to the minimal-enclosing-ball question.

Transport, replay, provenance, and naming through $H,T,P,N$ preserve the same route. Since no further coordinate exists, the packet exports neither a selected minimizer nor a host compact convex optimization theorem.
\end{proof}
