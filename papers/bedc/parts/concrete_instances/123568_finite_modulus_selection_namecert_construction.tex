\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteModulusSelectionUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges on compact metric uniform continuity, the $\FiniteModulusSelectionUp$ packet records the finite-net step that turns displayed compact-metric coverage and pointwise continuity data into a selected uniform modulus row without leaving the finite substrate. It sits after the compact-metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the continuous-map surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the finite epsilon-net carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-epsilon-net-namecert}, the dyadic comparison core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite window names of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The carrier is a generic L10 finite-net modulus-selection handoff; it is not arbitrary open-cover compactness, countable choice, a selected subcover theorem, quotient real equality, or ambient real completeness.

\begin{definition}[Finite modulus selection carrier]
\label{def:finite-modulus-selection-carrier}
A $\FiniteModulusSelectionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(K,F,E,D,W,R,M,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row exposing the compact-metric finite-net or coverage budget consumed by the selection.
\item $F$ is the displayed map or finite family row, read through $\ContinuousMapUp$ data before any modulus row is inspected.
\item $E$ is the $\FiniteEpsilonNetUp$ row naming the finite centers, coverage ledger, and mesh witness used by the modulus read.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row, recording the dyadic slack, comparison budget, and tolerance split attached to the request.
\item $W$ records the finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows opened under the joint $E,D$ budget.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the displayed stream windows.
\item $M$ is the selected modulus row, visible only after $K,F,E,D,W,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact source, map or family row, finite net, dyadic tolerance, stream windows, rational readback, selected modulus, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a tolerance request through $K,F,E,D,W,R,M,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteModulusSelectionUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\FiniteEpsilonNetUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteModulusSelectionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,F,E,D,W,R,M,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for arbitrary open-cover compactness, countable choice, selected subcovers, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite modulus selection NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-modulus-selection-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteModulusSelectionUp$ packet $S=(K,F,E,D,W,R,M,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteModulusSelectionUp}$ surface consists exactly of compact-source admission, map or family visibility, finite epsilon-net exposure, dyadic tolerance routing, finite stream-window admission, regular rational readback, selected-modulus exposure, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay, package provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-modulus-selection-carrier}. The analytic rows are $K,F,E,D,W,R,M$, and the structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

The compact source and map rows are read first. They determine which compact metric source and which displayed map or family are allowed to receive a modulus request.

The finite-net and tolerance rows then constrain the request. The row $E$ exposes the finite centers and coverage ledger, while $D$ fixes the dyadic comparison budget before any stream window can be opened.

The stream and regular-rational rows are downstream of that finite budget. Thus $W$ and $R$ must be visible before $M$ can be used as a selected modulus row.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no excluded compactness, choice, subcover, quotient, or completeness coordinate occurs in the carrier, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite modulus selection dyadic-net route]
\label{thm:finite-modulus-selection-dyadic-net-route}
Every uniform-continuity consumer of an accepted $\FiniteModulusSelectionUp$ packet reads the selected modulus through the finite route
$$
\CompactMetricUp \longmapsto \ContinuousMapUp \longmapsto \FiniteEpsilonNetUp \longmapsto \DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
In packet coordinates, no modulus read reaches $M$ until the finite net $E$, dyadic tolerance $D$, finite stream window $W$, regular rational readback $R$, and terminal $\RealUp$ seal have been exposed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the obligation theorem. It forces compact-source admission and map or family visibility before the selected modulus row is public.

The finite selection step is then mediated by $E$ and $D$. A consumer that has not read the finite epsilon-net centers and dyadic tolerance budget has not fixed the finite mesh through which the modulus is selected.

The Real-facing read is also finite. The stream window $W$ and regular rational readback $R$ are consumed before the terminal Real seal, and only then can the selected modulus row $M$ be replayed by $H,C,P,N$.

Thus every uniform-continuity consumer factors through the finite net, dyadic tolerance, stream window, RegSeqRat readback, and Real seal before it uses a modulus. The route exports no arbitrary open-cover compactness, countable-choice selector, selected subcover, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
