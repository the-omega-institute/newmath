\begin{theorem}[CriticalLineWitness bridged route status]
\label{thm:critical-line-witness-bridged-route-status}
The bridged route status for an accepted $\CriticalLineWitnessUp$ packet is the finite zeta-source and fixed-strip readback lattice already exposed by the hub children: the $\ZetaUp$ source route, zero-strip source rows, fixed-strip tuple, source-modulus readback, standard finite-tuple bridge rows, refusal budget, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. A bridge-facing consumer may inspect only that lattice and its displayed refusal boundary. It receives no proof of $\mathsf{RH}$, no analytic continuation theorem, no zero-free strip theorem, no zero-search procedure, no host complex equality, no quotient real equality, and no standard-model bridge beyond the named finite route.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CriticalLineWitnessUp.CriticalLineWitnessCarrier\_bridged\_route\_status}
\begin{proof}
Read the zeta-source side first. The hub children expose the $\ZetaUp$ source, zero-strip source rows, source-modulus rows, fixed-strip tuple, and fixed-strip readback package before any bridge-facing row is visible.

Next apply the fixed-strip StdBridge route totality and componentwise StdBridge transport theorems, \autoref{thm:critical-line-witness-fixed-strip-stdbridge-route-totality} and \autoref{thm:critical-line-witness-componentwise-stdbridge-transport}. They identify the bridge-facing read with the finite image, readback, transport, taste-gate, and refusal rows of the displayed packet.

Finally use the RH-boundary StdBridge nonescape theorem \autoref{thm:critical-line-witness-rh-boundary-stdbridge-nonescape}. It places RH, analytic-continuation, zero-search, host-equality, quotient-equality, and completeness demands on the same refusal boundary, so the bridged status is a finite route record rather than any of the excluded analytic claims.
\end{proof}
