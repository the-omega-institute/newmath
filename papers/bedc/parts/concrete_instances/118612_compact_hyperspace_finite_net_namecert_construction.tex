\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp$}

Following Blaschke and Hausdorff, the $\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp$ packet records the finite-net interface by which compact subsets of a compact metric source are compared inside the compact hyperspace without passing through host powersets, quotient hyperspaces, selected representatives, or choice. It sits after the Hausdorff-distance interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hausdorff-metric-namecert}, the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the compact-subset ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperspace-namecert}, the compact metric hyperspace route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-metric-hyperspace-namecert}, and the Blaschke selection surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-blaschke-selection-compact-hyperspace-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[Compact hyperspace finite-net carrier]
\label{def:compact-hyperspace-finite-net-carrier}
A $\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(X,H,D,E,U,S,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row carrying the metric, finite-net, total-boundedness, and completion data of the ambient compact metric object.
\item $H$ is the $\HyperspaceUp$ compact-subset ledger over $X$.
\item $D$ is the $\HausdorffMetricUp$ bidirectional distance ledger for displayed compact-subset names.
\item $E$ is the tolerance-indexed finite-net family in the hyperspace: each entry records a positive tolerance, finitely many compact-subset names, and Hausdorff coverage witnesses for the inspected compact-subset rows.
\item $U$ is the finite-union row, recording the finite union of selected compact-subset names and its displayed compact-subset readback through $H$.
\item $S$ is the stability ledger showing that Hausdorff finite-net matching is preserved when finitely many accepted compact-subset rows are united.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,H,D,E,U,S,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through
$$
X,H,D,E \longmapsto U,S .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp$, $\HausdorffMetricUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\HyperspaceUp$, $\CompactMetricHyperspaceUp$, $\BlaschkeSelectionCompactHyperspaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier compares accepted packets only through $X,H,D,E,U,S,T,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host subsets, powerset quotients, open-cover compactness, selected dense families, selected Hausdorff minimizers, or ambient hyperspace completion.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact hyperspace finite-net NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-hyperspace-finite-net-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp$ carrier
$$
F=(X,H,D,E,U,S,T,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following five finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item source admission, requiring $X$ to expose the compact metric finite-net and completion rows before any hyperspace net is public;
\item compact-subset admission, requiring every named compact subset in $H$ and every finite-net center in $E$ to be read through the same $\HyperspaceUp$ ledger;
\item Hausdorff finite-net matching, requiring $D$ and $E$ to display bidirectional finite coverage at each requested tolerance;
\item finite-union stability, requiring $U,S$ to keep finite unions inside the same compact-subset ledger and to preserve the displayed Hausdorff finite-net matching;
\item structural exhaustion, requiring $T,C,P,N$ to transport, replay, source, and name only the displayed finite route.
\end{enumerate}
No local NameCert obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The source row $X$ is the only entry point for the compact metric, finite-net, total-boundedness, and completion data, and the compact-subset ledger $H$ is the only entry point for hyperspace names.

Read the Hausdorff and finite-net rows next. The row $D$ supplies bidirectional distance witnesses, while $E$ lists finitely many compact-subset centers and coverage witnesses at the requested tolerances. Both rows are tied to $X$ and $H$, so the matching cannot range over a host powerset.

The finite-union row $U$ and stability ledger $S$ are then read as the only union-facing coordinates. They certify that finite unions of accepted centers remain named compact-subset rows and that the Hausdorff finite-net matching is preserved under that finite operation.

Finally $T,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same displayed route. Since the carrier has no additional coordinate for host subsets, quotient hyperspaces, selected dense families, selected minimizers, or ambient hyperspace completion, the five rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact hyperspace finite-net finite-union stability]
\label{thm:compact-hyperspace-finite-net-finite-union-stability}
Let $F=(X,H,D,E,U,S,T,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\CompactHyperspaceFiniteNetUp$ carrier. If a finite family of compact-subset rows is matched by the Hausdorff finite-net row $E$ at a displayed tolerance, then the finite union named by $U$ is matched at the same hyperspace read through the stability ledger $S$. The stability uses only the $\HausdorffMetricUp$ ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hausdorff-metric-namecert}, the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the hyperspace ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperspace-namecert}, the compact metric hyperspace route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-metric-hyperspace-namecert}, and the Blaschke finite-selection surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-blaschke-selection-compact-hyperspace-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite family named in $E$. Each member is a displayed compact-subset row in $H$, and each matching witness is a bidirectional Hausdorff read through $D$ over the compact metric source $X$.

Apply the finite-union row $U$. It records the union as another compact-subset row over the same source, rather than as a host subset or quotient object.

Now use the stability ledger $S$. For each requested tolerance, the matching witnesses for the finitely many members are replayed through the same Hausdorff ledger, and the union read is accepted because every constituent row is already displayed in $H$.

The structural rows keep the operation inside the packet. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport and $\Cont$ replay preserve $X,H,D,E,U,S$, while $P,N$ source and name the route. Thus finite-union stability is a finite NameCert consequence, not an open-cover compactness theorem or a choice-selected hyperspace argument.
\end{proof}
