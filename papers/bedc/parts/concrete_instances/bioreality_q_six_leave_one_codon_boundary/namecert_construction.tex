The carrier BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary records the finite boundary packet for the claim h1.leave.one.codon.out.  The packet is intentionally narrow: it does not assert a universal genetic-code theorem, and it does not infer unseen codon-level effects.  It only packages the measured leave-one-codon boundary shape witnessed by the given finite data.

\begin{definition}[BioReality Q-six leave-one-codon boundary carrier]
\label{def:bioreality-q-six-leave-one-codon-boundary-carrier}
A BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary carrier is a finite NameCert packet with six numerical coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 0,\\
B &= 5,\\
H &= 8,\\
P &= 8,\\
R &= 1.0,\\
T &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $A$ is the anchor count, $B$ is the boundary count, $H$ is the high-coverage closure count, $P$ is the predictable count, $R$ is the recovery rate, and $T$ is the above-threshold recovery rate.
\end{definition}

The decisive feature of the packet is the contrast between no anchors and a nonempty boundary.  The anchor coordinate is exactly $0$, while the boundary coordinate is exactly $5$.  Thus the certificate is not a seed-anchor claim: its content is a five-point boundary observation under leave-one-codon removal.

\begin{definition}[Leave-one-codon boundary profile]
\label{def:leave-one-codon-boundary-profile}
The leave-one-codon boundary profile of BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary is the ordered tuple
$$
\begin{gathered}
(\text{anchor count},\text{boundary count},\text{high closure coverage},\text{predictable count},\text{recovery rate},\text{threshold recovery})\\
= (0,5,8,8,1.0,1.0).
\end{gathered}
$$
A profile is boundary-realized when its boundary count is positive and its recovery rate is total.  BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary is boundary-realized because $5>0$ and $1.0=1$.
\end{definition}

The two eight-count coordinates play different roles.  The high-coverage closure count is $8$, which records the size of the closure side of the packet.  The predictable count is also $8$, which records that the predictable side reaches the same finite size.  The equality $H=P=8$ is the internal balance condition used by this NameCert chapter; it is not converted into a claim about any codon not present in the packet.

\begin{theorem}[Finite boundary witness]
\label{thm:bioreality-q-six-leave-one-codon-boundary-witness}
BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary carries a finite leave-one-codon boundary witness with no anchors, five boundary points, eight high-coverage closure points, eight predictable points, and total recovery.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The carrier data are the six displayed coordinates
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 0, & B &= 5, & H &= 8,\\
P &= 8, & R &= 1.0, & T &= 1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The absence of anchors follows from $A=0$.  The presence of a boundary follows from $B=5$.  The closure and prediction sizes are both eight, since $H=8$ and $P=8$.  Total recovery and above-threshold recovery follow from $R=1.0$ and $T=1.0$.  These six equalities are finite data equalities, so the NameCert packet is closed over exactly the stated witness.
\end{proof}

The threshold coordinate is not an auxiliary decoration.  Because the recovery rate above threshold is $1.0$, the packet records that every counted recovery event lies on the accepted side of the threshold test.  The chapter therefore certifies a boundary packet whose recovery side is total both before and after the threshold filter.  It still makes no claim about which biological mechanism produced the five boundary points.

\begin{theorem}[Threshold-stable recovery]
\label{thm:bioreality-q-six-leave-one-codon-threshold-stable-recovery}
For BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary, ordinary recovery and above-threshold recovery coincide at total recovery.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The recovery rate is $R=1.0$, and the recovery rate above threshold is $T=1.0$.  Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
R=T=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The conclusion is only this equality of the two finite packet coordinates.  No additional threshold distribution, codon identity list, or external biological model is used.
\end{proof}
