\begin{definition}[BioReality Q-six leave-one-codon boundary carrier]
\label{def:bioreality-q-six-leave-one-codon-boundary-carrier}
A BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary carrier is a finite packet
$$
\begin{aligned}
B=(A,D,H,P,R,T)
\end{aligned}
$$
whose public coordinates are fixed by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=0, & D&=5, & H&=8,\\
P&=8, & R&=1.0, & T&=1.0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $A$ is the anchor-count row, $D$ is the boundary-count row, $H$ is the high-coverage closure row, $P$ is the predictable-count row, $R$ is the recovery-rate row, and $T$ is the above-threshold recovery-rate row.
\end{definition}

The packet is therefore not an anchor certificate. The anchor side is empty because $A=0$, while the boundary side is nonempty because $D=5$. The NameCert content is the five-boundary-point observation under leave-one-codon removal, together with the two eight-count rows and the two total-recovery rows. Nothing in the carrier records a codon identity list, a p-value, or a generator that extrapolates beyond these six displayed coordinates.

\label{def:bioreality-q-six-leave-one-codon-boundary-profile}
The boundary profile of a BioRealityQSixLeaveOneCodonBoundary packet is the ordered tuple
$$
\begin{gathered}
(\text{anchor count},\text{boundary count},\text{high closure coverage},\text{predictable count},\text{recovery rate},\text{threshold recovery})\\

It is boundary-realized when the boundary count is positive and the recovery coordinate is total. The displayed packet is boundary-realized because $5>0$ and $1.0=1$.
\end{definition}

The two eight-count readings are separate rows. The high-coverage closure coordinate is $8$, and the predictable-count coordinate is also $8$. The equality $H=P=8$ is an internal finite-balance reading of this packet. It is not a statement that every possible codon deletion has been inspected, nor a statement that a missing codon name can be recovered from the packet.

\begin{aligned}
