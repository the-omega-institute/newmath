\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\OreLocalizationUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-ore-localization-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Ore localization of noncommutative rings, the $\OreLocalizationUp$ packet records the finite denominator calculus needed to read fractions $s^{-1}r$ or $rs^{-1}$ without treating localization as generic parameter transport. It sits after the multiplicative monoid surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-monoid-namecert}, the ring operation rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert}, and the ideal-facing absorption surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ideal-namecert}. The packet names an admissible denominator set, left and right Ore squares, cancellation witnesses, fraction rows, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not import a host quotient ring, a selected common denominator outside the ledger, commutativity, or a classical total ring of fractions.

\begin{definition}[OreLocalization carrier]
\label{def:ore-localization-carrier}
An $\OreLocalizationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(R,S,O,C,F,D,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the ambient $\RingUp$ row, including the additive, multiplicative, and distributive operation data read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert};
\item $S$ is the denominator $\MonoidUp$ row, naming the multiplicatively closed set used by the localization route;
\item $O$ is the Ore-square ledger, listing the displayed left and right common-multiple squares used to move a numerator past a denominator;
\item $C$ is the cancellation ledger, recording the denominator-side cancellation witnesses allowed by the same Ore surface;
\item $F$ is the finite fraction row, whose entries are displayed numerator-denominator pairs and not host quotient classes;
\item $D$ is the denominator ledger, recording which denominator row is consumed by each fraction, Ore square, cancellation step, and multiplication handoff;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the ring, denominator, Ore-square, cancellation, fraction, denominator-ledger, replay, provenance, and local naming rows;
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for denominator admission, Ore-square formation, fraction multiplication, cancellation, and consumer readback;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\OreLocalizationUp$, $\RingUp$, $\MonoidUp$, $\IdealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\OreLocalizationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $R,S,O,C,F,D,H,T,P,N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[OreLocalization NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:ore-localization-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:ore-localization-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\OreLocalizationUp}$ surface consists exactly of ambient ring admission, denominator-monoid admission, left and right Ore-square exposure, denominator-side cancellation exposure, finite fraction-row admission, denominator-ledger readback, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay, provenance, and local naming. Every accepted localization read must pass through these rows before a fraction multiplication or denominator handoff is public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $L=(R,S,O,C,F,D,H,T,P,N)$. The ring and denominator rows are visible first, so every numerator and denominator read is grounded in the displayed $\RingUp$ and $\MonoidUp$ certificates.

The Ore-square and cancellation rows are $O$ and $C$. They provide the finite common-multiple and cancellation witnesses used to compare displayed fraction rows; no commutative shortcut or host quotient equality is available.

The fraction and denominator ledgers are $F$ and $D$. They record which denominator is consumed at each multiplication, comparison, and handoff step.

Finally, $H,T,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite data. Since the packet has no coordinate for a host localization object, selected representatives outside the ledger, total quotient rings, or ambient ideal equality, these rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[OreLocalization denominator ledger]
\label{thm:ore-localization-denominator-ledger}
In an accepted $\OreLocalizationUp$ carrier $L=(R,S,O,C,F,D,H,T,P,N)$, every public fraction row in $F$ has its denominator named in $D$, every Ore-square witness in $O$ consumes denominator rows from $D$, and every cancellation witness in $C$ returns to the same denominator ledger. A consumer cannot cite a localized product, comparison, or handoff unless the relevant denominator route is displayed in $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the fraction row $F$ together with the denominator ledger $D$. The carrier admits fractions only as displayed numerator-denominator pairs, so each fraction coordinate has a corresponding denominator entry.

Use the Ore-square ledger $O$. A common-multiple square is accepted only when the denominator rows it moves across are already listed in $D$.

Use the cancellation ledger $C$ for the final handoff. Cancellation may shorten or compare displayed fraction rows, but it does not create a denominator outside the ledger. The transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows $H,T,P,N$ preserve the same denominator route for downstream consumers.
\end{proof}
