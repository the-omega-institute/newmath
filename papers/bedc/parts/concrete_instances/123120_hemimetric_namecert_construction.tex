\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\HemiMetricUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-hemimetric-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kelley and Nachbin on directed topology and quasi-uniform structures, the $\HemiMetricUp$ packet records the finite directed-distance surface in which zero distance need not identify points and no reverse-distance symmetry is supplied. It sits between the directed-distance carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-quasi-metric-namecert}, the enriched directed-distance route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lawvere-metric-namecert}, the ordinary metric-space reader of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-space-namecert}, the indexed-open topology surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, and the completion consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformcompletion-namecert}. The packet names only source, point, directed $\RealUp$ distance, zero diagonal, forward triangle, directed-ball, provenance, transport, replay, and local naming rows; it does not assert symmetry, separated point equality, quotient metric equality, selected limits, open-cover compactness, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[HemiMetric carrier]
\label{def:hemimetric-carrier}
A $\HemiMetricUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
H=(S,X,D,Z,T,B,Q,L,M,U,R,K,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source-carrier row naming the traditional hemimetric context and the finite observation window in which directed-distance requests are admitted.
\item $X$ is the point row, recording admitted point names and point classifiers without identifying points by zero distance.
\item $D$ is the directed $\RealUp$-valued distance row, recording each oriented request $d(x,y)$ independently from any reverse request.
\item $Z$ is the zero-diagonal row, recording $d(x,x)=0$ as a displayed boundary condition on admitted point rows.
\item $T$ is the forward triangle ledger, recording finite comparisons from $d(x,z)$ through the ordered pair $d(x,y)$ and $d(y,z)$.
\item $B$ is the directed-ball row, recording finite requests $B^+(x,\varepsilon)$ with displayed center, radius, and incoming point data.
\item $Q$ is the $\QuasiMetricUp$ provenance row, exposing the nearest directed-distance sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-quasi-metric-namecert}.
\item $L$ is the $\LawvereMetricUp$ provenance row, exposing the enriched directed-distance sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lawvere-metric-namecert}.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ reflected-reader row, available only when a consumer explicitly asks for the ordinary metric-space sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-space-namecert}.
\item $U$ is the $\TopologyUp$ and $\UniformCompletionUp$ handoff row, exposing directed balls to topology and completion readers through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformcompletion-namecert}.
\item $R$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source, point, directed-distance, zero-diagonal, forward-triangle, directed-ball, provenance, handoff, replay, and local naming rows.
\item $K$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from consumer requests through the same source, point, distance, ball, sibling, topology, completion, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ is the local carrier classifier row comparing accepted packets only through $S,X,D,Z,T,B,Q,L,M,U,R,K,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\HemiMetricUp$, $\QuasiMetricUp$, $\LawvereMetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\UniformCompletionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\HemiMetricUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier has no coordinate for symmetry, separated point equality, quotient distance, selected completion limits, host topology, open-cover compactness, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[HemiMetric NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hemimetric-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\HemiMetricUp$ carrier
$$
H=(S,X,D,Z,T,B,Q,L,M,U,R,K,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\HemiMetricUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the source row, point row, directed $\RealUp$-valued distance row, zero-diagonal row, forward triangle ledger, directed-ball row, $\QuasiMetricUp$ and $\LawvereMetricUp$ provenance rows, $\MetricSpaceUp$ reflected-reader row, $\TopologyUp$ and $\UniformCompletionUp$ handoff row, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, classifier row, package provenance, and local naming row.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.HemiMetricUp.HemiMetricCarrier\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hemimetric-carrier}. The tuple displays the source, point, directed-distance, zero-diagonal, forward-triangle, and directed-ball rows before any sibling or completion-facing read is available.

The directed-distance rows are asymmetric by inventory. The row $D$ records an oriented request, while no carrier coordinate turns that request into a reverse-distance equality or separated point identity.

The sibling and consumer rows are also displayed. The rows $Q,L,M,U$ expose the quasi-metric, Lawvere-metric, metric-space, topology, and uniform-completion routes as named reads rather than as hidden host structures.

Finally $R,K,C,P,N$ transport, replay, classify, source, and name the same finite packet. Since the excluded symmetry, quotient, selection, compactness, completeness, and bridge data do not appear among the rows, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[HemiMetric directed-ball handoff]
\label{thm:hemimetric-directed-ball-handoff}
Let
$$
H=(S,X,D,Z,T,B,Q,L,M,U,R,K,C,P,N)
$$
be an accepted $\HemiMetricUp$ carrier. Every $\TopologyUp$ or $\UniformCompletionUp$ consumer that asks for a directed ball reaches it only through
$$
S,X,D,Z,T \longmapsto B \longmapsto Q,L,M,U \longmapsto R,K,C,P,N .
$$
The handoff may cite $\QuasiMetricUp$, $\LawvereMetricUp$, and reflected $\MetricSpaceUp$ provenance, but it receives no symmetry, no quotient point equality, no selected completion limit, no host topology, and no ambient completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the accepted carrier. A directed-ball request first reads the source and point rows $S,X$ and then the oriented distance row $D$.

The zero-diagonal row $Z$ and forward triangle ledger $T$ are displayed before the ball row $B$ is exported. Thus the consumer sees the hemimetric laws as finite directed rows rather than as consequences of an ordinary symmetric metric.

Only after $B$ is visible can the request pass to the sibling and handoff rows $Q,L,M,U$. These rows expose the quasi-metric, Lawvere-metric, reflected metric-space, topology, and uniform-completion routes without adding a hidden quotient or selected limit.

The structural rows $R,K,C,P,N$ preserve the same route by transport, replay, classification, provenance, and local naming. Therefore every topology or completion consumer receives precisely the displayed directed-ball handoff and none of the excluded host data.
\end{proof}
