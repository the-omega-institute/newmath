\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DoldKanUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-dold-kan-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Dold and Kan on the equivalence between simplicial abelian groups and nonnegatively graded chain complexes, the $\DoldKanUp$ packet records the finite BEDC naming surface for the normalized-chain handoff. It sits after the face-and-degeneracy ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-simplicialset-namecert}, the chain comparison route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-eilenberg-zilber-namecert}, the cycle-boundary consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-homology-namecert}, and the additive exactness rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-abeliancat-namecert}. The packet names only displayed simplicial-object rows, normalized-chain rows, abelian-category rows, homology readback, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and a local naming row; it does not select quotient representatives, assert a host categorical equivalence, import ambient derived-category machinery, or choose an unbounded normalization procedure.

\begin{definition}[Dold-Kan carrier]
\label{def:dold-kan-carrier}
A $\DoldKanUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(S,A,N,C,H,E,R,T,Q,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\SimplicialSetUp$ source row, specialized to the visible simplicial abelian-object window inspected by the packet.
\item $A$ is the $\AbelianCatUp$ ambient additive row in which kernels, cokernels, sums, and zero morphisms are read.
\item $N$ is the normalized-chain row, recording the finite intersection of displayed degeneracy kernels and the quotient-free normalized endpoint used by the consumer.
\item $C$ is the chain-complex row whose differential is read from the displayed face maps and whose square-zero check is routed through the same finite window.
\item $H$ is the $\HomologyUp$ readback row reached only after the normalized-chain and differential rows have been displayed.
\item $E$ records the Eilenberg-Zilber compatibility row consumed from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-eilenberg-zilber-namecert} when tensor or product consumers ask for a chain-level comparison.
\item $R$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the simplicial-object, abelian-category, normalized-chain, differential, homology, comparison, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $T$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite simplicial window through normalization, differential readback, homology handoff, and comparison rows.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance row over $\DoldKanUp$, $\SimplicialSetUp$, $\AbelianCatUp$, $\HomologyUp$, $\EilenbergZilberUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $P$ records the public dependency boundary: every accepted read passes through the sibling chapters named above before a consumer may inspect the normalized-chain handoff.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DoldKanUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The local classifier compares accepted carriers only through the displayed tuple $S,A,N,C,H,E,R,T,Q,P,L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dold-Kan NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dold-kan-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DoldKanUp$ carrier $D=(S,A,N,C,H,E,R,T,Q,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DoldKanUp}$ surface consists exactly of simplicial-object admission, abelian-category source admission, normalized-chain row admission, differential readback, homology handoff, Eilenberg-Zilber compatibility, componentwise $\hsame$ stability, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, dependency-boundary exposure, and local naming. A consumer cannot read the normalized-chain handoff without first exposing the $\SimplicialSetUp$, $\AbelianCatUp$, $\HomologyUp$, and $\EilenbergZilberUp$ sibling surfaces named by the carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier through \autoref{def:dold-kan-carrier}. The source rows $S$ and $A$ expose the finite simplicial-object window and the additive ambient rows before any normalized chain row is reachable.

Next read $N$ and $C$. The normalized-chain endpoint is tied to the displayed degeneracy-kernel window, and the differential row is tied to the displayed face maps in the same packet. The homology row $H$ can therefore be consumed only after those rows have been displayed.

Finally inspect $E,R,T,Q,P,L$. The comparison row $E$ supplies the Eilenberg-Zilber sibling route, while transport, replay, provenance, dependency boundary, and local naming keep all reads on the same finite carrier. Since no coordinate supplies host equivalence, quotient representative choice, derived-category machinery, or unbounded normalization, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dold-Kan normalized-chain handoff]
\label{thm:dold-kan-normalized-chain-handoff}
Every accepted $\DoldKanUp$ packet exports its normalized-chain handoff through the finite route
$$
\SimplicialSetUp,\ \AbelianCatUp \longmapsto N,C \longmapsto \HomologyUp \longmapsto \EilenbergZilberUp \longmapsto R,T,Q,P,L .
$$
The route reads finite face and degeneracy rows, additive kernel and zero rows, the normalized-chain endpoint, the differential row, homology readback, Eilenberg-Zilber comparison, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming before any downstream chain or homology consumer receives the handoff.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier rows $S$ and $A$. They are the only source rows for the simplicial object and abelian ambient data, so a normalized-chain request first opens those finite windows.

Use the normalized-chain and chain rows $N$ and $C$. These rows display the degeneracy-kernel intersection and the differential readback before the homology row $H$ can be inspected.

Attach the comparison and structural rows. The Eilenberg-Zilber row $E$ is downstream of the chain window, and $R,T,Q,P,L$ transport, replay, source, bound, and name the same packet. Thus the handoff remains finite and cannot be replaced by a host Dold-Kan equivalence, a quotient-chain representative, or an unbounded normalization oracle.
\end{proof}
