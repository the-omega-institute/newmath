Following Hurewicz--Wallman and Engelking on dimension theory, the $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp$ packet records the finite boundary-recursion route that compares Lebesgue covering dimension with the small and large inductive dimension surfaces. It sits over the topology source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the normal separation row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, the finite-cover refinement row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-cover-refinement-namecert}, the covering-dimension packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-coveringdimension-namecert}, the small inductive dimension packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-small-inductive-dimension-namecert}, and the large inductive dimension packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-large-inductive-dimension-namecert}. The chapter names a displayed finite induction route; it does not import a host powerset of covers, arbitrary open-cover compactness, quotient equality of closed boundaries, or a global dimension theorem.

\begin{definition}[Lebesgue covering dimension induction carrier]
\label{def:lebesgue-covering-dimension-induction-carrier}
A $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(T,C,S,I,N,F,B,H,R,P,Z)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row on which open covers, closed boundaries, and refinement windows are read.
\item $C$ is the $\CoveringDimensionUp$ row recording the displayed finite order bound for a finite cover window.
\item $S$ is the $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$ boundary-decomposition row for the inspected closed neighbourhood frontier.
\item $I$ is the $\LargeInductiveDimensionUp$ boundary-refinement row used to compare the closed frontier with the surrounding cover window.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ separation row that turns a displayed closed boundary request into a finite neighbourhood-separation ledger.
\item $F$ is the $\FiniteCoverRefinementUp$ row recording the finite refinement and order-control data consumed by the induction step.
\item $B$ is the boundary-recursion ledger, storing the predecessor-dimension request, boundary carrier, and finite descent witness.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the topology, cover, inductive-dimension, normal-space, refinement, boundary-recursion, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
\TopologyUp,\ \NormalSpaceUp,\ \FiniteCoverRefinementUp
\longmapsto
\CoveringDimensionUp,\ \SmallInductiveDimensionUp,\ \LargeInductiveDimensionUp
\longmapsto B .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\NormalSpaceUp$, $\FiniteCoverRefinementUp$, $\CoveringDimensionUp$, $\SmallInductiveDimensionUp$, $\LargeInductiveDimensionUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $Z$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,C,S,I,N,F,B,H,R,P,Z$. It has no coordinate for arbitrary host open covers, selected infinite refinements, quotient equality of boundary carriers, completed lattices of closed sets, or external dimension equivalence theorems.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lebesgue covering dimension induction NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lebesgue-covering-dimension-induction-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp$ carrier $L=(T,C,S,I,N,F,B,H,R,P,Z)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp}$ surface consists exactly of topology-source exposure, covering-dimension order-bound exposure, small-inductive boundary exposure, large-inductive boundary-refinement exposure, normal-space separation exposure, finite-cover-refinement exactness, boundary-recursion descent, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:lebesgue-covering-dimension-induction-carrier}. The analytic rows are $T,C,S,I,N,F,B$, and the structural rows are $H,R,P,Z$.

The boundary recursion is not read from a global dimension theorem. The rows $S$ and $I$ expose the inductive boundary surfaces, $N$ supplies the finite normal-separation ledger, and $F$ records the finite refinement data needed for the covering-dimension comparison.

The replay row $R$ orders these dependencies through the topology and normal-space sources before the boundary-recursion ledger is consumed. Transport, provenance, and local naming preserve the same displayed rows.

Since no excluded host object is a carrier coordinate, the listed rows are exactly the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lebesgue covering dimension induction boundary route]
\label{thm:lebesgue-covering-dimension-induction-boundary-route}
Every boundary-recursion read exported by an accepted $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionInductionUp$ packet must pass through
$$
\TopologyUp,\ \NormalSpaceUp,\ \FiniteCoverRefinementUp
\longmapsto
\CoveringDimensionUp,\ \SmallInductiveDimensionUp,\ \LargeInductiveDimensionUp
\longmapsto
B,H,R,P,Z .
$$
The route names a finite comparison between cover-order control and inductive boundary descent; it is not a proof of arbitrary covering-dimension equivalence, not a quotient boundary theorem, and not a host open-cover compactness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the topology row $T$ and the normal-space row $N$. These rows determine the displayed open and closed boundary windows on which the dimension comparison is allowed to speak.

Read the finite-cover refinement row $F$ next. It supplies only the finite refinement and order-control data stored in the packet, so the argument cannot choose an arbitrary refinement outside the ledger.

Now compare the three dimension rows. The covering-dimension row $C$ supplies the order-bound surface, while the small and large inductive rows $S,I$ supply boundary-decomposition and boundary-refinement surfaces for the recursion ledger $B$.

Finally transport and replay the route through $H,R,P,Z$. The exported object is the finite boundary-recursion packet, so it cannot be reread as host open-cover compactness, quotient equality of boundaries, or a global dimension-equivalence theorem.
\end{proof}
