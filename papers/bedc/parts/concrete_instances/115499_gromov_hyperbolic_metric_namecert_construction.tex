Following Gromov's hyperbolic metric spaces and the four-point formulation of coarse negative curvature, the $\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp$ packet records a finite metric carrier whose hyperbolicity is read through displayed distance comparisons and bounded four-point slack. It sits after the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the length-space route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-length-space-namecert}, the geodesic metric surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-geodesic-metric-namecert}, and the existing hyperbolic metric packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-metric-namecert}; its boundary-facing consumers include \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-busemann-boundary-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-busemann-boundary-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gromov-boundary-namecert}. The packet names only finite four-point, geodesic, and boundary-handoff rows, not a host geodesic space, selected boundary compactification, quotient of rays, or ambient completion theorem.

\begin{definition}[Gromov hyperbolic metric carrier]
\label{def:gromov-hyperbolic-metric-carrier}
A $\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(M,L,E,\Delta,Q,S,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ distance-observation row.
\item $L$ is the $\LengthSpaceUp$ path-length readback row when a length route is present.
\item $E$ is the $\GeodesicMetricUp$ geodesic witness row when geodesic segments are displayed.
\item $\Delta$ is the nonnegative hyperbolicity-bound row used by the four-point comparisons.
\item $Q$ is the four-point comparison ledger over displayed quadruples.
\item $S$ is the stability row recording that enlarging the displayed bound keeps the same accepted metric source.
\item $B$ is the boundary-handoff row available to $\BusemannBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$, and $\GromovBoundaryUp$ consumers.
\item $H,C,P,N$ are componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,L,E,\Delta,Q,S,B,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a selected geodesic model, quotient ideal boundary, host compactification, or ambient completion principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Gromov hyperbolic metric NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:gromov-hyperbolic-metric-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp$ carrier $G=(M,L,E,\Delta,Q,S,B,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp}$ surface consists exactly of metric-source admission, optional length-space readback, optional geodesic witness exposure, hyperbolicity-bound admission, four-point comparison exposure, bound-stability reading, boundary-handoff exposure, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Every downstream consumer must factor through
$$
M,L,E,\Delta,Q,S,B,H,C,P,N
$$
before it reads a hyperbolic metric datum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:gromov-hyperbolic-metric-carrier}. The metric source $M$ is visible before any four-point comparison is inspected, and the length and geodesic rows $L,E$ are displayed as route data rather than as a host geodesic space.

The bound row $\Delta$ and comparison ledger $Q$ provide the Gromov-hyperbolic content. They inspect only finite quadruples and the displayed distance observations, so the packet records hyperbolicity as BEDC comparison data.

The stability and boundary rows $S,B$ then route the same accepted metric surface to boundary consumers. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only those displayed rows, leaving no coordinate for selected geodesic models, quotient boundaries, host compactifications, or ambient completion theorems.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gromov hyperbolic metric four-point stability]
\label{thm:gromov-hyperbolic-metric-four-point-stability}
Every accepted $\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp$ carrier remains accepted when a consumer replaces the displayed hyperbolicity-bound row $\Delta$ by a transported row $\Delta'$ whose finite comparison ledger dominates the same quadruple reads. The accepted route is still
$$
M,L,E,\Delta',Q,S,B,H,C,P,N,
$$
and the replacement does not add a host proof of global curvature, a selected geodesic realization, a quotient boundary, or ambient metric completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the four-point ledger $Q$. It is indexed only by displayed metric observations from $M$, with optional length and geodesic support read through $L$ and $E$.

The stability row $S$ records the admissible transport from $\Delta$ to $\Delta'$ for those same finite quadruple reads. Since the row changes only the displayed bound, the metric source and the comparison ledger remain the same BEDC route.

Finally apply the structural rows $H,C,P,N$. They replay the transported bound and preserve the boundary handoff $B$, but they do not introduce a host curvature theorem, selected geodesic model, quotient boundary, or completion object.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gromov hyperbolic metric geodesic boundary route]
\label{thm:gromov-hyperbolic-metric-geodesic-boundary-route}
Every boundary-facing consumer of an accepted $\GromovHyperbolicMetricUp$ carrier must read the $\MetricUp$ source row $M$, the length or geodesic rows $L,E$ when present, the hyperbolicity bound $\Delta$, the four-point ledger $Q$, and the boundary-handoff row $B$ before it can use the packet in $\BusemannBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$, or $\GromovBoundaryUp$. The handoff exports finite metric and comparison rows, not a completed ideal boundary, quotient of rays, selected geodesic representative, or host compactification.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

Next expose $\Delta$ and $Q$. These rows are the only hyperbolicity evidence available to the packet, so a boundary consumer cannot bypass the finite four-point comparison surface.

The boundary handoff $B$ is then consumed by the Busemann and Gromov boundary chapters named above. Because $B$ is transported only through $H,C,P,N$, the export remains a finite metric comparison route and not a completed ideal boundary, quotient of rays, selected geodesic representative, or host compactification.
\end{proof}
