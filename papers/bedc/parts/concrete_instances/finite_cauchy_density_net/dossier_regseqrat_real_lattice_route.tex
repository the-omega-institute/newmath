\begin{theorem}[Finite Cauchy density net RegSeqRat Real lattice route]
\label{thm:finite-cauchy-density-net-regseqrat-real-lattice-route}
Every $\FiniteCauchyDensityNetUp$ consumer that exports a density-net handoff first reads the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular readback surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the $\RealUp$ seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the $\RealSeparabilityUp$ density route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-separability-namecert}. The exported handoff is therefore a finite lattice route through regular rational reads, dyadic tolerances, Real naming, and separability density data, not a detached dense-set theorem or an ambient completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the finite density-net packet and read the dependency rows before the export row. The regular rational observations are supplied by the $\RegSeqRatUp$ surface, while the mesh tolerances are supplied by $\DyadicRatCoreUp$.

Next read the Real-facing rows. The $\RealUp$ seal identifies the named Real boundary available to the consumer, and $\RealSeparabilityUp$ supplies the density route that licenses a separability-facing handoff.

Since these four rows are read before export, the consumer receives a displayed lattice dependency. No coordinate in the route supplies an independent dense-subset theorem, selected representative, quotient equality, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
