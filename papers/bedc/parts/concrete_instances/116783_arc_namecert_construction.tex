\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ArcUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-arc-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Munkres and Bishop--Bridges, an arc is read as a certified image of a closed interval under a displayed continuous map. The $\ArcUp$ packet is anchored by the closed interval source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closedboundedinterval-namecert}, the map graph and modulus rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the compact-metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the connected-space boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-connected-space-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It records the interval-image route through finite BEDC rows; it is not a host subset of a topological space, a quotient path image, or a selected point-set equality.

\begin{definition}[Arc carrier]
\label{def:arc-carrier}
An $\ArcUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(I,M,K,C,R,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$ source row, naming the endpoint interval, order witnesses, and endpoint real seals inspected by the packet.
\item $M$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ row, recording the displayed map from $I$ into the target metric carrier together with the finite continuity or modulus ledger used for image reads.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ target-source row through which finite nets, distance tests, and compact image windows are exposed.
\item $C$ is the $\ConnectedSpaceUp$ image-continuity row, recording the connected interval-image boundary visible to continuum-facing consumers.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ readback row for endpoints, radii, modulus budgets, and target-distance seals.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the interval, map, compact-metric, connectedness, real-readback, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from interval requests through map evaluation, compact-metric windows, connected-image reads, and real seals.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ArcUp$, $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ConnectedSpaceUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ArcUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,M,K,C,R,H,T,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host subset equality, quotient path images, arbitrary open-cover compactness, selected representatives, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Arc NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:arc-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ArcUp$ carrier $A=(I,M,K,C,R,H,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ArcUp}$ surface consists exactly of closed-interval source admission through $I$, displayed continuous-map admission through $M$, compact-metric image-window admission through $K$, connected-image exposure through $C$, real endpoint and tolerance readback through $R$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $T$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

Every arc-facing consumer must read these rows before it may treat the packet as interval-image data. No obligation is discharged by a host point-set topology theorem, quotient equality of images, selected image representatives, arbitrary open covers, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:arc-carrier}. The closed interval row $I$ and the continuous-map row $M$ are the only source rows through which an interval image can be read.

The compact and connected image faces are then finite rows of the same packet. The row $K$ supplies compact-metric windows and distance tests for the target image, while $C$ records the connected-image boundary consumed by continuum-facing readers. The row $R$ supplies the endpoint and tolerance seals needed by both faces.

Transport and replay remain componentwise. The rows $H$ and $T$ move and expose exactly the interval, map, compact-metric, connectedness, and real-readback rows, while $P$ and $N$ record provenance and local naming.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for host subset equality, quotient image equality, arbitrary open-cover compactness, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient real completeness, the NameCert obligations are exhausted by the displayed rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Arc interval image nonescape]
\label{thm:arc-interval-image-nonescape}
Every public interval-image read of an accepted $\ArcUp$ packet factors through the displayed route
$$
\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp
\longmapsto
\ContinuousMapUp
\longmapsto
\CompactMetricUp
\longmapsto
\ConnectedSpaceUp
\longmapsto
\RealUp
$$
inside the rows $I,M,K,C,R,H,T,P,N$. The route exposes a closed interval, a displayed continuous map, compact-metric image windows, connected-image data, and real tolerance seals; it exports no host image subset, quotient path-image equality, selected representative, open-cover compactness theorem, or ambient real-completeness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier and read $I$ before $M$. The packet names an interval-image object only after the closed interval and displayed continuous map are both visible.

Next read $K,C,R$. Compact-metric image windows, connected-image data, and real tolerance seals are downstream of the same map row, so a consumer cannot replace them by a detached host subset or by a quotient image object.

Finally use $H,T,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the finite interval-image route without adding another image coordinate. Therefore every public interval-image read remains inside the displayed BEDC rows.
\end{proof}
