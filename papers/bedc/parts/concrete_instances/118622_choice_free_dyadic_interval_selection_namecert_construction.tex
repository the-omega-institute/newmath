\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real analysis, the $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$ packet records the finite dyadic interval-selection surface used when a Real or completion route needs a displayed local interval but must not import a hidden countable-choice operator. It sits between the dyadic rational core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readbacks of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seals of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the located interval carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}. The packet names only a finite request ledger, a displayed dyadic candidate family, a local selection row, a located-interval handoff, structural transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not choose an infinite subsequence, a global Cauchy selector, a completed real endpoint, or a countable family of intervals.

\begin{definition}[Choice-free dyadic interval selection carrier]
\label{def:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-carrier}
A $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(Q,D,W,R,E,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ is the finite request row. It records the inspected tolerance, endpoint-side flag, and consumer route that asked for a dyadic interval witness.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ candidate ledger. It supplies only the finitely displayed dyadic endpoints and containment comparisons named by $Q$.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row for the inspected real-name or completion-name prefixes.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational approximants exposed by those windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for the inspected endpoint names, with no selected completed real.
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedIntervalUp$ handoff row, packaging the displayed dyadic endpoints as a located interval cell when the consumer asks for interval-shaped output.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $Q,D,W,R,E,L,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the interval-selection request through the finite dyadic, window, readback, Real-seal, and located-interval rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier accepts a packet only through the displayed tuple $Q,D,W,R,E,L,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for countable choice, dependent choice, a selected infinite branch, a global interval selector, a completed endpoint value, or a quotient of real names.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Choice-free dyadic interval selection obligations]
\label{thm:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-obligations}
For every accepted $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$ carrier
$$
S=(Q,D,W,R,E,L,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp}$ obligation surface has exactly five finite rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed packet $S$ and its finite request row $Q$;
\item dyadic candidate exposure through $D$ together with window and rational readback exposure through $W$ and $R$;
\item terminal real-name sealing through $E$ and located-interval handoff through $L$;
\item componentwise transport and displayed replay through $H$ and $C$;
\item provenance and local naming through $P$ and $N$.
\end{enumerate}
No additional obligation asks for a countable selection function, an infinite chain of interval choices, a quotient-real endpoint, or a host-completion choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-carrier}. Its accepted rows are exactly $Q,D,W,R,E,L,H,C,P,N$, and the classifier compares only these displayed components by $\hsame$.

The computational part of the obligation surface is exhausted by $Q,D,W,R,E,L$. The request row fixes one finite inspection, the dyadic core supplies the displayed endpoint candidates, the stream and regular rational rows expose the requested prefixes, the Real row seals the inspected endpoint names, and the located interval row repacks the same finite dyadic cell.

The structural part is exhausted by $H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no row contains a function selecting intervals for all tolerances or all request histories, the local certificate cannot smuggle countable choice into the Real or completion-facing route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Choice-free dyadic interval selection finite route]
\label{thm:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-finite-route}
Every accepted $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$ read factors through the finite route
$$
Q \longmapsto D \longmapsto W,R,E \longmapsto L \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
The route exports only the inspected request, displayed dyadic candidates, finite stream windows, regular rational readbacks, terminal Real seals, located-interval handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. It exports no countable selector, dependent-choice tree, completed real endpoint, or global interval-selection oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted consumer of an interval-selection read. By \autoref{def:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-carrier}, the consumer enters through the finite request row $Q$ and reaches dyadic data only through $D$.

Apply \autoref{thm:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-obligations}. The only rows downstream of $D$ are the finite window and readback rows $W,R$, the terminal seal row $E$, and the located-interval handoff $L$ before the structural rows are inspected.

The sibling packets $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, and $\LocatedIntervalUp$ provide their own finite NameCert surfaces. None adds a selected infinite interval family or a host choice operator to this packet. Hence every accepted read remains on the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Choice-free dyadic interval selection nonescape]
\label{thm:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-nonescape}
An accepted $\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp$ consumer cannot obtain a $\LocatedIntervalUp$ handoff or a $\RealUp$ endpoint seal without first displaying $Q,D,W,R,E$ as appropriate to the request. In particular, any route that produces an interval cell by choosing a member of an infinite dyadic family is outside the local $\NameCert_{\ChoiceFreeDyadicIntervalSelectionUp}$ surface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the accepted carrier through \autoref{def:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-carrier}. The located-interval output occurs only in $L$, and $L$ is downstream of the finite request, dyadic candidate, window, readback, and Real-seal rows.

Use \autoref{thm:choice-free-dyadic-interval-selection-finite-route}. The route places $Q,D,W,R,E$ before $L$ and then proceeds only to $H,C,P,N$, so the carrier has no independent interval-output entry point.

The structural rows can only transport, replay, source, and name the same displayed packet. They do not create a choice function over all tolerances, all histories, or all dyadic refinements.
\end{proof}
