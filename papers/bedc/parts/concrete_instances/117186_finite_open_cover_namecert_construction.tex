\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteOpenCoverUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-open-cover-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Kelley on finite open covers, the $\FiniteOpenCoverUp$ packet records a cover whose indexing family is already finite before any compactness or subcover-selection consumer inspects it. It sits between the general open-cover surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-open-cover-namecert}, the open-set rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the compact-metric source rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the finite-subcover criterion of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-subcover-criterion-namecert}, and the dyadic interval cover carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-cover-namecert}. The packet is not a selected subcover and not an open-cover compactness theorem; it is the finite carrier row through which already finite cover data is named.

\begin{definition}[Finite open-cover carrier]
\label{def:finite-open-cover-carrier}
A $\FiniteOpenCoverUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(I,U,M,R,K,D,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the finite index row, listing exactly the cover indices available to the current consumer.
\item $U$ is the $\TopologyUp$ open-set row, assigning an open certificate to each displayed index of $I$.
\item $M$ is the membership and coverage row, tying each covered point read to one displayed index and one open row.
\item $R$ is the finite refinement row, comparing finite cover windows by finite index maps and inclusion witnesses.
\item $K$ is the compact-source compatibility row, read only when a $\CompactMetricUp$ consumer supplies a compact metric source.
\item $D$ is the dyadic-interval handoff row, present only when the finite cover is displayed through a $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$ endpoint, mesh, or membership ledger.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for index, open-set, membership, refinement, compact-source, dyadic-handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite cover request through $I,U,M,R,K,D,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteOpenCoverUp$, $\OpenCoverUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\FiniteSubcoverCriterionUp$, $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteOpenCoverUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,U,M,R,K,D,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an arbitrary open-cover compactness theorem, a selected finite subcover, a host powerset of opens, quotient cover equality, countable choice, or ambient compactness outside the displayed finite rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite open-cover NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-open-cover-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteOpenCoverUp$ carrier $F=(I,U,M,R,K,D,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteOpenCoverUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite index, topology open-set, membership, refinement, compact-source, dyadic-handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows; finite-index exactness through $I$; open-set exactness through $U$; coverage and refinement exactness through $M$ and $R$; compact-metric compatibility through $K$; dyadic-interval handoff through $D$; and non-escape through $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-open-cover-carrier}. The rows $I,U,M,R$ expose the only finite cover window, open predicates, coverage witnesses, and refinement data available to the packet.

The compatibility rows are downstream. A compact-metric consumer reads $K$ only after the finite cover rows are displayed, while a dyadic-interval consumer reads $D$ only as the finite endpoint, mesh, or membership handoff already named by $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that same finite route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for arbitrary open-cover compactness, selected subcovers, host powersets, quotient cover equality, countable choice, or ambient compactness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite open-cover dyadic interval handoff]
\label{thm:finite-open-cover-dyadic-interval-handoff}
Every $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$ consumer that reads a $\FiniteOpenCoverUp$ packet must display the finite index row $I$, the $\TopologyUp$ open row $U$, the membership row $M$, the refinement row $R$, and the dyadic handoff row $D$ before any interval-cover endpoint, mesh, or membership read is exported. The handoff factors through the finite route
$$
\FiniteOpenCoverUp
\longrightarrow
\DyadicIntervalCoverUp
\longrightarrow
\FiniteSubcoverCriterionUp
$$
only as displayed cover data. It exports no selected finite subcover, open-cover compactness theorem, host compactness principle, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite cover rows of \autoref{def:finite-open-cover-carrier}. The interval consumer first sees $I,U,M,R$, so the cover window is finite before any dyadic endpoint or mesh information is consulted.

Read the dyadic handoff through $D$. This row may point to the $\DyadicIntervalCoverUp$ endpoint, mesh, or membership ledger, but it does so only after the finite open-cover rows have been displayed and transported by $H$.

The finite-subcover criterion can then consume the displayed cover data as a finite route. The packet contains no selector row and no compactness theorem row, so the handoff cannot export selected subcovers, host compactness, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
