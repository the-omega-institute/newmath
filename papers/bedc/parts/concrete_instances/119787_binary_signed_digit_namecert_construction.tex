\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BinarySignedDigitUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-binary-signed-digit-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on signed-digit real representations, the $\BinarySignedDigitUp$ packet records the finite binary signed-digit substrate used before a signed-digit real name is read as a regular rational or sealed as a $\RealUp$ object. It sits below the signed-digit real expansion of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-signed-digit-real-expansion-namecert}, the dyadic signed-digit stream of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-signed-digit-stream-namecert}, the dyadic arithmetic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names finite digit rows and dyadic readback data; it is not a quotient of infinite streams, a selected expansion, trichotomy, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[BinarySignedDigit carrier]
\label{def:binary-signed-digit-carrier}
A $\BinarySignedDigitUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(D,W,Q,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the binary signed-digit row, listing only finite words over the digit alphabet $\{-1,0,1\}$ and the dyadic place attached to each displayed digit.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row that schedules the visible signed-digit prefixes.
\item $Q$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ signed dyadic readback ledger, recording the finite partial sum, carry normalization, endpoint value, and tolerance radius for each displayed prefix.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row, reading the signed dyadic ledger as regular rational data.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row available only after the digit, window, dyadic, and regular-readback rows have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for digit rows, windows, signed dyadic readback, regular readback, Real sealing, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a digit-window request through dyadic readback, regular rational handoff, and Real sealing.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BinarySignedDigitUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\SignedDigitRealExpansionUp$, $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BinarySignedDigitUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $D,W,Q,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It contains no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected infinite expansions, host real equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BinarySignedDigit NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:binary-signed-digit-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BinarySignedDigitUp$ carrier
$$
B=(D,W,Q,R,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BinarySignedDigitUp}$ surface consists exactly of digit-row admission, finite $\StreamNameUp$ window exposure, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ signed dyadic readback, $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff, terminal $\RealUp$ seal routing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:binary-signed-digit-carrier}. Its analytic rows are precisely the finite digit row $D$, the stream-window row $W$, the signed dyadic readback ledger $Q$, the regular rational handoff $R$, and the Real seal $E$.

The digit and window obligations are the route through $D$ and $W$. A consumer can inspect only the displayed finite signed-digit prefixes, with each digit attached to its dyadic place.

The arithmetic and readback obligations are the route through $Q$ and $R$. The dyadic ledger records partial sums, carry normalization, endpoint values, and tolerance radii before the regular rational handoff is available.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name exactly the same finite route. Since no other coordinate is present, the refused endpoints cannot be projected from an accepted packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BinarySignedDigit window induction]
\label{thm:binary-signed-digit-window-induction}
Let
$$
B=(D,W,Q,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
be an accepted $\BinarySignedDigitUp$ carrier. Every accepted extension of a displayed binary signed-digit prefix by one digit remains in the same carrier route only after the enlarged prefix is recorded in $D$, the enlarged window is recorded in $W$, and the corresponding signed dyadic readback is recorded in $Q$. Thus prefix growth is a finite window induction through displayed rows, not a selected infinite expansion or quotient stream equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proceed by induction on the finite displayed prefix window. The base window reads the empty prefix through $D$ and $W$, and its signed dyadic readback is the base row in $Q$.

For the successor step, append one admitted digit to the visible prefix. The digit row $D$ records the digit and dyadic place, while $W$ records the enlarged inspection window.

The dyadic readback ledger $Q$ then normalizes the enlarged partial sum and records its endpoint and radius. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ keep the route inside the same finite carrier.

Finally $R,E,P,N$ read the resulting regular rational handoff, Real seal, provenance, and local name. No step introduces an infinite representative, quotient equality, choice schedule, trichotomy branch, host real equality, or completeness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BinarySignedDigit dyadic readback]
\label{thm:binary-signed-digit-dyadic-readback}
Every $\SignedDigitRealExpansionUp$, $\DyadicSignedDigitStreamUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\BinarySignedDigitUp$ packet reads the finite route
$$
D,W \longmapsto Q \longmapsto R \longmapsto E,H,C,P,N .
$$
The row $Q$ is the unique local passage from binary signed-digit windows to signed dyadic endpoint and tolerance data, so downstream signed-digit and completion-facing consumers receive a finite dyadic readback packet rather than a host real, quotient stream class, selected expansion, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:binary-signed-digit-namecert-obligations}. It requires the digit row and StreamName window before any signed dyadic readback can be consumed.

Use the window induction theorem \autoref{thm:binary-signed-digit-window-induction}. Each finite prefix extension reaches $Q$ only through the displayed digit and window rows, so $Q$ is attached to a visible finite prefix.

The regular rational row $R$ and the Real seal row $E$ are downstream of $Q$. The signed-digit real expansion and dyadic signed-digit stream chapters can therefore consume the packet only as the same finite dyadic readback route.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve this route under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Since the carrier has no coordinate for the excluded endpoints, no downstream consumer can recover them from the packet.
\end{proof}
