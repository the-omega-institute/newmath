The $\FiniteEpsilonNetSelectorUp$ packet records that finite selector surface for a $\CompactMetricUp$ object: it reads the $\MetricSpaceUp$ carrier, a $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ positive-radius ledger, finite $\StreamNameUp$ observation windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ rational readback, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal before exporting any selected net row. \begin{definition}[Finite epsilon-net choice carrier]\label{def:finite-epsilon-net-choice-carrier}
A $\FiniteEpsilonNetSelectorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(K,M,\Lambda,W,Q,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row whose compact presentation supplies finite observation data.
\item $M$ is the underlying $\MetricSpaceUp$ row, keeping the metric carrier and distance readback visible.
\item $\Lambda$ is the $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ row that provides the positive radius ledger used by the net request.
\item $W$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window family naming the candidate centers inspected by the selector.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to those windows.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row used to compare displayed radii and metric distances.
\item $S$ is the selected finite epsilon-net row, recorded only after $K,M,\Lambda,W,Q,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact source, metric row, Lebesgue-number ledger, windows, rational readback, Real seal, selected-net row, replay, provenance, and local name.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite radius request through $K,M,\Lambda,W,Q,R,S,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteEpsilonNetSelectorUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteEpsilonNetSelectorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for an arbitrary open cover, a choice-selected subcover, quotient metric equality, host compactness, countable choice, or a selected completion object.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite epsilon-net choice NameCert obligations]\label{thm:finite-epsilon-net-choice-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteEpsilonNetSelectorUp$ carrier
$$
E=(K,M,\Lambda,W,Q,R,S,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteEpsilonNetSelectorUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, compact source exposure through $K$, metric readback through $M$, finite positive-radius exposure through $\Lambda$, finite window exposure through $W$, rational readback through $Q$, Real comparison through $R$, selected-net export through $S$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-epsilon-net-choice-carrier}. The analytic rows are $K,M,\Lambda,W,Q,R,S$, and the structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

The compact source and metric rows are read first. The selector therefore receives a displayed compact metric presentation rather than an arbitrary cover or host compactness assertion.

Next the finite Lebesgue-number row $\Lambda$ fixes the positive-radius ledger. The windows $W$, rational readback $Q$, and Real seal $R$ are downstream of that ledger, so the selector row $S$ can record only centers inspected through the displayed finite data.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since no carrier coordinate stores open-cover choice, quotient metric equality, host compactness, countable choice, or a selected completion object, the listed rows exhaust the certificate surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite epsilon-net compact handoff]\label{thm:finite-epsilon-net-choice-compact-handoff}

$$

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations. They make $K,M,\Lambda,W,Q,R,S,H,C,P,N$ the complete public surface of the accepted selector packet.

The positive-radius step is forced through $\Lambda$. A consumer cannot read $S$ until the compact source, metric row, and finite Lebesgue-number ledger have already determined the displayed radius budget.

The window and readback rows then determine which finite centers are visible. The $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ rows provide the finite observations, and the $\RealUp$ seal records the displayed metric comparison.

Thus the selected-net row is a finite handoff from compact metric data to Real-facing consumers. Because the carrier lacks open-cover choice, quotient metric equality, host compactness, countable choice, and selected completion coordinates, no stronger compactness principle is exported.
\end{proof}
