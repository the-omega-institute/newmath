\chapter{The Golden-Mean Shift Equivalence: A Finite Presentation-Invariance Certificate}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-window6-golden-mean-shift-equivalence}
\origin{ai}

This chapter records a finite strong-shift-equivalence certificate for the golden-mean subshift zeta. The certificate does not change the dynamical system; it changes the finite presentation from the two-state transfer matrix to a three-state edge-expansion matrix and verifies that the reciprocal zeta denominator and the periodic trace window are unchanged.

\section{The Rectangular Factors}
\label{sec:window6-golden-mean-shift-equivalence-factors}

Let
$$
R=\begin{pmatrix}
1&y&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then the two rectangular products are
$$
RS=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&y\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
=T_y,
\qquad
SR=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&y&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&y&0
\end{pmatrix}
=E_y.
$$
Thus $R$ and $S$ exhibit the two-state golden-mean transfer presentation $T_y$ and the three-state edge-expansion presentation $E_y$ as the two sides of an elementary strong-shift-equivalence datum.

\section{The Presentation-Invariance Certificate}
\label{sec:window6-golden-mean-shift-equivalence-certificate}

The determinant side gives the same reciprocal zeta polynomial in both finite presentations:
$$
\det(I_2-tT_y)=\det(I_3-tE_y)=1-t-yt^2.
$$
The trace side gives the same weighted periodic-point window. For $1\le n\le 8$, the Lean certificate computes the powers of $E_y$ by genuine $3\times3$ matrix multiplication over $\mathbb Z[y]$ and verifies
$$
\operatorname{tr}(E_y^n)=C_n(y),
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_1(y)&=1,\\
C_2(y)&=1+2y,\\
C_3(y)&=1+3y,\\
C_4(y)&=1+4y+2y^2,\\
C_5(y)&=1+5y+5y^2,\\
C_6(y)&=1+6y+9y^2+2y^3,\\
C_7(y)&=1+7y+14y^2+7y^3,\\
C_8(y)&=1+8y+20y^2+16y^3+2y^4.
\end{aligned}
$$

\begin{theorem}[Golden-mean strong-shift-equivalence certificate]
\label{thm:window6-golden-mean-shift-equivalence-certificate}
The rectangular matrices $R$ and $S$ satisfy $RS=T_y$ and $SR=E_y$; the two finite presentations have the common reciprocal zeta denominator $1-t-yt^2$; and the trace window $\operatorname{tr}(E_y^n)=C_n(y)$ is checked for $1\le n\le 8$ by explicit polynomial matrix multiplication.
\end{theorem}

The point is local and finite: the determinant and trace data of the golden-mean SFT zeta survive the elementary presentation change from $T_y$ to its edge-expansion presentation $E_y$. In this sense it is a finite witness for the Williams presentation-invariance principle, while remaining independent content from the determinant calculation itself.
