\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\NakayamaLemmaUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-nakayama-lemma-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Atiyah--Macdonald and Matsumura, the $\NakayamaLemmaUp$ packet names
the finite commutative-algebra handoff by which an ideal action trapped in the
Jacobson-radical direction forces a finitely generated module witness to
collapse. In BEDC form it sits after the $\NameCert_{\ModuleUp}$ carrier of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-module-namecert}, the $\NameCert_{\IdealUp}$
carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ideal-namecert}, the ring and
commutative-ring rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert} and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-commring-namecert}, and the finite-generation
surface of $\NameCert_{\NoetherianModuleUp}$
(\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-noetherian-module-namecert}). The certificate
records only the displayed algebraic rows needed for the lemma-shaped
non-escape argument; it does not import quotient-module equality, host module
equality, full local algebra, or a standard bridge.

\section{Carrier}
\label{sec:nakayama-lemma-carrier}

\begin{definition}[Nakayama lemma carrier]
\label{def:nakayama-lemma-carrier}
A $\NakayamaLemmaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\mathsf{Nak}(M,R,C,I,F,J,A,H,Q,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$, the $\ModuleUp$ source surface for the module whose generators are
being inspected;
\item $R$, the ambient $\RingUp$ scalar row read by the module action;
\item $C$, the $\CommRingUp$ specialization used by the ideal-action rows;
\item $I$, the $\IdealUp$ source surface acting on $M$;
\item $F$, a finite-generator ledger for the displayed module endpoints,
read through the $\NoetherianModuleUp$ finite-generation surface;
\item $J$, a Jacobson-radical containment row for the ideal action;
\item $A$, the action-collapse row recording that the ideal action carries
each displayed generator back into the generated submodule boundary;
\item $H$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the module, scalar, ideal,
finite-generation, radical-containment, and action-collapse rows;
\item $Q$, displayed $\Cont$ replay for the finite generator and ideal-action
checks;
\item $P$, $\mathsf{Pkg}$ provenance for the module, ring, commutative-ring,
ideal, finite-generation, radical, and action rows;
\item $N$, the local $\NameCert_{\NakayamaLemmaUp}$ row binding these rows as
one certificate.
\end{enumerate}
The packet contains no quotient-module equality, no host equality of module
carriers, no local-ring structure theorem, and no chosen maximal ideal.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Nakayama NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:nakayama-lemma-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\NakayamaLemmaUp$ carrier
$\mathsf{Nak}(M,R,C,I,F,J,A,H,Q,P,N)$, the local
$\NameCert_{\NakayamaLemmaUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission
for the displayed rows, source compatibility with $\ModuleUp$, $\RingUp$,
$\CommRingUp$, $\IdealUp$, and $\NoetherianModuleUp$, finite-generation access
through $F$, radical-containment access through $J$, ideal-action collapse
through $A$, componentwise classifier transport through $H$, displayed
continuation replay through $Q$, provenance non-escape through $P$, and local
naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:nakayama-lemma-carrier}. The projection
exposes exactly the module row, scalar ring row, commutative specialization,
ideal row, finite-generation ledger, radical-containment row, action-collapse
row, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The source compatibility obligations are read componentwise from the first
four rows, with $F$ tied to the $\NoetherianModuleUp$ finite-generation surface.
The radical and action rows are separate finite ledgers, so the certificate
does not need a host quotient module or a selected maximal ideal to state the
handoff.

Transport and replay preserve only these rows. Thus $H$, $Q$, $P$, and $N$
record movement, finite observation, provenance, and local naming without
exporting quotient equality, host module equality, or full local algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nakayama finite-generation handoff]
\label{thm:nakayama-lemma-finite-generation-handoff}
Every accepted $\NakayamaLemmaUp$ carrier exports finite-generation evidence
only through the displayed ledger $F$ over the $\ModuleUp$ row $M$ and the
$\NoetherianModuleUp$ finite-generation surface. Downstream algebra consumers
may reuse this evidence together with $R,C,I,H,Q,P,N$, but they cannot read a
chosen basis, a quotient-module representative, or a host equality of modules
from the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read $F$ from the carrier. It is attached to the module row $M$ and to the
finite-generation surface already provided by $\NoetherianModuleUp$, so it is
the only generator ledger available to a consumer.

The scalar and ideal rows $R,C,I$ give the algebraic context in which the
generators are inspected. They do not choose a basis or identify quotient
representatives; they only supply the displayed ring, commutative-ring, and
ideal surfaces.

Componentwise transport through $H$, replay through $Q$, provenance through
$P$, and local naming through $N$ keep the finite-generation row attached to
the same packet. Hence the handoff is finite-generation evidence, not host
module equality or quotient-module data.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nakayama nilpotent radical non-escape]
\label{thm:nakayama-lemma-nilpotent-radical-nonescape}
In an accepted $\NakayamaLemmaUp$ carrier, radical-action information is
confined to the displayed rows $J$ and $A$: $J$ records the
Jacobson-radical containment surface for the ideal row $I$, and $A$ records
the finite action-collapse check over the module row $M$. Any nilpotent or
radical-action read transported by $H$ and replayed by $Q$ remains inside the
same $M,R,C,I,F,J,A,H,Q,P,N$ packet and cannot escape into a local-algebra
oracle, a maximal-ideal selector, quotient-module equality, or host module
equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the radical-facing part of the packet. The row $J$ is the only
radical-containment source, and $A$ is the only row that connects the ideal
action back to the finite module generator boundary.

The surrounding algebraic rows keep this read finite. The $\ModuleUp$,
$\RingUp$, $\CommRingUp$, and $\IdealUp$ sources specify the carrier and
action surfaces, while $F$ supplies the finite-generation ledger needed by the
collapse argument.

Transport and replay do not add structure. The rows $H$ and $Q$ move and
replay the displayed radical and action ledgers componentwise, with $P$ and
$N$ recording provenance and local naming. Therefore every radical-action read
stays inside the named packet and exports none of the excluded host algebra.
\end{proof}
