Following Bishop and Bridges and Kelley, the $\CompletionModulusExtractorUp$ packet records the finite extraction surface by which completion-facing data exposes a modulus row before any completed object is consumed. It sits beside \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-basis-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}: those chapters supply the metric-completion, filter-basis, regular rational, dyadic tolerance, finite-window, and real-seal rows, while this chapter names the extractor that keeps the modulus route finite and inspectable.

\begin{definition}[Completion modulus extractor carrier]
\label{def:completion-modulus-extractor-carrier}
A $\CompletionModulusExtractorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(M,B,S,R,D,W,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the source $\MetricCompletionUp$ row naming the completion-facing request.
\item $B$ is the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ row that records the finite basis through which the source is inspected.
\item $S$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window schedule opened by the extractor.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the displayed windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used to compare the extracted regular observations.
\item $W$ is the extracted modulus row, recording for each admitted precision a finite window and dyadic tolerance witness.
\item $L$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $B,S,R,D,W$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source, basis, window, readback, dyadic, modulus, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a completion request through the same finite extractor rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompletionModulusExtractorUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompletionModulusExtractorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier accepts exactly packets carrying these rows. It exports no quotient completion, selected limit, countable-choice selector, host real equality, hidden completeness principle, or modulus not displayed in $W$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completion modulus extractor NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:completion-modulus-extractor-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompletionModulusExtractorUp$ carrier
$$
E=(M,B,S,R,D,W,L,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CompletionModulusExtractorUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, completion-request readback, basis readback, finite-window admission, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance visibility, extracted-modulus visibility, terminal real sealing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every completion-facing consumer must read the extracted modulus row $W$ through
$$
M,B,S,R,D,W,L,H,C,P,N
$$
and through no hidden completion or choice coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:completion-modulus-extractor-carrier}. The source row $M$ names the completion-facing request, while $B,S,R,D$ expose the finite basis, window, regular readback, and dyadic tolerance data from which a modulus can be read.

Inspect the extracted row $W$. It is the only coordinate whose role is to present the precision-to-window and precision-to-tolerance schedule, so any consumer asking for modulus data must pass through that displayed row.

Finally read $L,H,C,P,N$. The terminal real seal is downstream of the modulus display, and the structural rows transport, replay, source, and locally name the same finite route. Since no coordinate of $E$ stores a quotient completion, selected limit, countable-choice selector, host equality, or hidden completeness principle, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Completion modulus extractor finite route]
\label{thm:completion-modulus-extractor-route}
Every $\MetricCompletionUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\CompletionModulusExtractorUp$ packet reads the finite route
$$
\MetricCompletionUp \longmapsto \CauchyFilterBasisUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto W \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
implemented by the displayed rows $M,B,S,R,D,W,L,H,C,P,N$. The route exposes modulus data as finite NameCert evidence; it does not select a completed point, quotient Cauchy names, invoke countable choice, prove host real equality, or assert ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with $M$ and $B$. The completion request is admitted only through the displayed metric-completion row, and the Cauchy-filter basis row fixes the finite observations the extractor may open.

Next inspect $S,R,D,W$. The stream windows determine the finite observations, the regular rational row records the readback, the dyadic ledger supplies tolerance comparison, and $W$ packages those data as the visible modulus row.

The final rows $L,H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route for Real-facing consumers. They seal the terminal real row, transport displayed components, replay the finite request, record provenance, and name the local packet. None of these rows adds quotient data, a selected point, choice, host equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
