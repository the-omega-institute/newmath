\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ResidualSetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-residual-set-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Baire, Oxtoby, and Bishop--Bridges, the $\ResidualSetUp$ packet records the positive side of a constructive Baire argument: a residual evidence carrier is accepted only when the dense-open schedule and the meagre-complement ledger are both displayed as finite BEDC rows. It sits beside the meagre-set carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-meagre-set-namecert}, the nowhere-dense component carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nowhere-dense-namecert}, the dense-open schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-baire-dense-open-namecert}, and the Baire-category theorem packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-baire-category-theorem-namecert}. The packet names residual evidence; it does not choose a generic point, assert arbitrary countable intersections, or import a host category theorem.

\begin{definition}[ResidualSet carrier]
\label{def:residual-set-carrier}
A $\ResidualSetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(T,D,M,N,B,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the ambient topology or complete-metric context in which the residual read is made.
\item $D$ is the $\BaireDenseOpenUp$ dense-open schedule row whose finite requests are visible to the Baire argument.
\item $M$ is the $\MeagreSetUp$ complement ledger, decomposed through the $\NowhereDenseUp$ component rows named by the meagre packet.
\item $N$ records the nowhere-dense component interface read from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nowhere-dense-namecert}.
\item $B$ is the $\BaireCategoryTheoremUp$ handoff row certifying that the displayed dense-open and meagre-complement evidence are the rows consumed by the Baire route.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $T,D,M,N,B,C,P,L$.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay of the dense-open schedule, meagre-complement ledger, Baire handoff, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ResidualSetUp$, $\BaireDenseOpenUp$, $\MeagreSetUp$, $\NowhereDenseUp$, $\BaireCategoryTheoremUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ResidualSetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a chosen generic point, an unrestricted set-theoretic complement, a host countable-intersection principle, or a classical comeagre-subset theorem outside the displayed BEDC rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ResidualSet Baire handoff]
\label{thm:residual-set-baire-handoff}
Every accepted $\ResidualSetUp$ packet read by a Baire-category consumer factors through the displayed dense-open schedule $D$, meagre-complement ledger $M$, nowhere-dense component interface $N$, and Baire handoff row $B$. Thus a residual conclusion is handed to downstream constructive Baire arguments only as the positive face of the same finite evidence that records the meagre complement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:residual-set-carrier}. The only analytic rows are the dense-open schedule, meagre-complement ledger, nowhere-dense component interface, and Baire handoff.

Read the sibling packets in order. \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-baire-dense-open-namecert} supplies the finite dense-open requests, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-meagre-set-namecert} supplies the complement ledger, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nowhere-dense-namecert} supplies the component interface used by that ledger.

The Baire-category row $B$ can therefore export residual evidence only through those displayed rows. Since $R$ has no coordinate for a selected generic point or host countable intersection, the handoff is the finite BEDC residual carrier and nothing stronger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ResidualSet NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:residual-set-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ResidualSetUp$ carrier $R=(T,D,M,N,B,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ResidualSetUp}$ surface consists exactly of ambient context admission through $T$, dense-open schedule exposure through $D$, meagre-complement exposure through $M$, nowhere-dense component visibility through $N$, Baire-category handoff through $B$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the displayed tuple. Each accepted row of the residual packet is one of $T,D,M,N,B,H,C,P,L$, and the structural rows only transport, replay, source, and name those coordinates.

The negative side is exhausted by $M$ and $N$: the meagre ledger supplies the complement surface, while the nowhere-dense component rows record the finite decomposition interface consumed by that ledger. The positive side is exhausted by $D$ and $B$: the dense-open schedule supplies the Baire requests and the Baire-category handoff records the route by which the residual read reaches a consumer.

No listed coordinate stores a generic point, an arbitrary complement, or a host countable-intersection theorem. The local NameCert obligations are therefore precisely the displayed rows and their structural support.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ResidualSet dense G-delta boundary]
\label{thm:residual-set-dense-gdelta-boundary}
Every accepted $\ResidualSetUp$ packet whose dense-open schedule and meagre-complement ledger are displayed through a $\DenseGDeltaUp$ carrier factors through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dense-g-delta-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-g-delta-set-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bairecategory-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-baire-category-theorem-namecert} before a residual consumer reads the positive side.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:residual-set-carrier} as $R=(T,D,M,N,B,H,C,P,L)$. The residual packet exposes its dense-open schedule through $D$, its meagre-complement ledger through $M$, its nowhere-dense interface through $N$, and its Baire handoff through $B$.

When the positive side is displayed through a $\DenseGDeltaUp$ carrier, read that carrier's $D,M,N,B$-facing route as the corresponding countable open schedule, dense-open schedule, Baire route, and residual handoff. The route first passes through the dense $G_\delta$ certificate, then through the $\GDeltaSetUp$ schedule, then through the Baire-category surface, and then through the Baire-category theorem packet.

The consumer therefore receives residual evidence only after the positive dense-open face has been displayed. No step chooses a generic point, forms a host intersection, imports countable choice, or identifies quotient sets outside the displayed BEDC rows.
\end{proof}
