\begin{definition}[MetricCompletionFilter carrier]
\label{def:metric-completion-filter-carrier}
A $\MetricCompletionFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(X,d,F,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X,d$ is the source metric row accepted by the $\MetricCompletionUp$ surface;
\item $F$ is the $\CauchyFilterUp$ evidence row for the displayed completion-facing filter;
\item $B$ is the $\FilterBaseUp$ finite-base row refining the requested filter neighborhoods;
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row used to read the base tails;
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for regular rational tail data;
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal attached to the displayed readback;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport of the metric, Cauchy-filter, base, window, readback, and seal rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from metric-completion requests through Cauchy-filter evidence, finite base refinement, StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, and the Real seal;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricCompletionFilterUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CauchyFilterUp$, $\FilterBaseUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricCompletionFilterUp}$ row.

The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a quotient completion class, selected filter limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient real completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFilter tail-base refinement]
\label{thm:metric-completion-filter-tail-base}
Every completion-facing read of an accepted $\MetricCompletionFilterUp$ carrier
$$
M=(X,d,F,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
must expose the $\CauchyFilterUp$ row $F$, the $\FilterBaseUp$ row $B$, the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row $S$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$ before it may be consumed as completion data. The finite base row $B$ refines each displayed filter request to a tail window read through $S$ and $R$, so the completion handoff is the displayed tail-base route rather than a quotient-class representative or a selected limit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-filter-carrier}. The metric row and Cauchy-filter row are visible before any completion-facing read reaches the base, window, readback, or seal rows.

Use the finite row projection through $B,S,R$. The $\FilterBaseUp$ row refines each displayed filter request to a named base tail, the $\StreamNameUp$ row fixes the finite request window, and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row reads that window as regular rational data.

The terminal $\RealUp$ seal is downstream of this readback. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay preserve the same tail-base route, while $\Pkg$ provenance and the local $\NameCert$ row name the packet.

Thus every completion-facing consumer receives only the displayed Cauchy-filter, finite-base, StreamName, RegSeqRat, and Real-seal rows. No quotient class, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness theorem is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFilter obligation surface]
\label{thm:metric-completion-filter-obligation-surface}
For every accepted $\MetricCompletionFilterUp$ carrier
$$
M=(X,d,F,B,S,R,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the visible completion-filter obligation surface is the finite $\BHist$ inventory consisting of the metric row $(X,d)$, Cauchy-filter row $F$, filter-base row $B$, $\StreamNameUp$ window $S$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $R$, $\RealUp$ seal $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and local $\NameCert_{\MetricCompletionFilterUp}$ naming row $N$. No completion obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-filter-carrier}. The analytic rows are exactly $(X,d),F,B,S,R,E$, and the structural rows are exactly $H,C,P,N$.

Apply tail-base refinement from \autoref{thm:metric-completion-filter-tail-base}. It forces every completion-facing read to pass through the Cauchy-filter row, finite base row, finite StreamName window, regular rational readback, and terminal Real seal.

The remaining rows transport, replay, source, and locally name that same route. Since the carrier has no further coordinate, quotient completion classes, selected filter limits, countable-choice schedules, and ambient completeness cannot be part of the local obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFilter RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:metric-completion-filter-regseqrat-handoff}
Every accepted $\MetricCompletionFilterUp$ read that exports tail data to a completion-facing consumer must pass from the $\FilterBaseUp$ row $B$ and $\StreamNameUp$ window $S$ through the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$ before any $\RealUp$ seal is visible. The handoff therefore records regular rational tail data as finite BEDC rows, with $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and $\NameCert$ naming carried along the same route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \autoref{thm:metric-completion-filter-tail-base}. The theorem already places $B,S,R$ between the Cauchy-filter row and the terminal seal.

Read the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:metric-completion-filter-obligation-surface}. It identifies $R$ as the unique regular rational readback row in the carrier, so a consumer cannot replace it by a selected filter limit or host completion representative.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same handoff under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Thus the RegSeqRat handoff is the displayed route through $B,S,R$ and not an ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MetricCompletionFilter Real-seal boundary]
\label{thm:metric-completion-filter-real-seal-boundary}
The terminal $\RealUp$ seal row $E$ of an accepted $\MetricCompletionFilterUp$ carrier is available only after the metric row, Cauchy-filter row, filter-base row, StreamName window, and RegSeqRat readback have been displayed and transported through $H,C,P,N$. A Real-facing consumer may therefore read $E$ only as the end of the finite completion-filter route, not as quotient completion equality, a selected limit, a countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the tail-base theorem \autoref{thm:metric-completion-filter-tail-base}. It places the seal row downstream of $F,B,S,R$ for every completion-facing read.

Apply \autoref{thm:metric-completion-filter-regseqrat-handoff}. The regular rational readback row is already displayed before the seal, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ carry the same route.

Finally use the carrier projection from \autoref{def:metric-completion-filter-carrier}. The carrier has no coordinate for quotient completion equality, selected limits, countable-choice schedules, or ambient Real completeness, so the Real seal is confined to the finite route just described.
\end{proof}
