\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\TopologistBroomUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-topologist-broom-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Steen and Seebach and Engelking, the $\TopologistBroomUp$ packet records the topologist's broom as finite BEDC topology and metric boundary data. It sits beside the comb-space packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-comb-space-namecert}, the indexed-open surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the distance-law carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the finite observation windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names one handle row, finitely inspected broom hairs, a handle-accumulation row, topology classifier rows, metric handoff rows, and local naming without importing a host plane subset equality, local connectedness theorem, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[TopologistBroom carrier]
\label{def:topologist-broom-carrier}
A $\TopologistBroomUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(H,A,S,T,M,E,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $H$ is the handle row. It records the inspected real-axis handle windows and the $\RealUp$ endpoint seals used by those windows.
\item $A$ is the broom-hair row. It stores finitely many hair indices, their rational slope or height windows, and the displayed attachment data tying each inspected hair to the handle.
\item $S$ is the handle-accumulation row. It records only finite evidence that the displayed hair windows approach the handle through visible $\StreamNameUp$ observations.
\item $T$ is the topology classifier row. It names the basic-neighbourhood tests for handle points, hair points, and handle-near observations through the $\TopologyUp$ carrier.
\item $M$ is the metric handoff row. It records the finite $\MetricUp$ distance windows comparing displayed handle, hair, and accumulation observations after their $\RealUp$ readbacks are present.
\item $E$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $H,A,S,T,M,C,P,N$ and the local packet shape.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay through
$$
H,A,S,T,M \longmapsto E,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\TopologistBroomUp$, $\CombSpaceUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\TopologistBroomUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $H,A,S,T,M,E,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host subset equality, local connectedness, selected infinite hair families, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[TopologistBroom NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:topologist-broom-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\TopologistBroomUp$ carrier
$$
B=(H,A,S,T,M,E,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\TopologistBroomUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the handle windows, broom-hair rows, handle-accumulation rows, topology classifier rows, metric handoff rows, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:topologist-broom-carrier}. The rows $H,A,S$ display the handle, the finitely inspected hairs, and the accumulation evidence before any topology or metric consumer can read the packet.

Next read $T$ and $M$. The topology classifier is finite because it only inspects the displayed neighbourhood tests, and the metric handoff is finite because every distance row is tied to the shown real-name and stream-window data.

Finally use $E,C,P,N$. Transport is componentwise, replay follows the displayed rows, provenance names the source packets, and the local NameCert row closes the finite carrier surface. Since no carrier coordinate stores host subset equality, local connectedness, selected infinite hairs, open-cover compactness, or ambient Real completeness, those objects cannot enter the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[TopologistBroom comb-space boundary]
\label{thm:topologist-broom-comb-space-boundary}
Every topology-facing or metric-facing read of an accepted $\TopologistBroomUp$ packet factors through the same finite dependency boundary as the $\CombSpaceUp$ packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-comb-space-namecert}:
$$
\TopologyUp,\quad
\MetricUp,\quad
\RealUp,\quad
\StreamNameUp .
$$
The broom packet changes the visible shape row from comb teeth to broom hairs, but a consumer still receives only displayed neighbourhood tests, finite distance-window handoffs, real-name seals, and stream-window accumulation evidence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the handle and hair rows $H,A$. They expose the finite real-axis windows and inspected broom hairs, so both topology and metric reads are tied to displayed observations rather than a host subset.

Use the accumulation row $S$ before reading either classifier. The handle-near behaviour is visible only through the finite stream windows recorded in the packet, matching the role of the accumulation-spine row in the $\CombSpaceUp$ boundary.

The topology row $T$ then supplies neighbourhood tests, while the metric row $M$ supplies distance-window handoffs. Both rows depend on the same displayed handle, hair, accumulation, real-name, and stream data.

The structural rows $E,C,P,N$ preserve and name this route. Because the carrier has no coordinate for host subset equality, local connectedness, selected infinite hairs, open-cover compactness, or ambient completeness, the comb-space boundary remains a dependency boundary rather than a stronger topology theorem.
\end{proof}
