\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\GronwallFiniteOdeUp$}

Following Gronwall and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\GronwallFiniteOdeUp$ packet records the finite dyadic surface by which a displayed ordinary-differential-equation row is bounded before any Real-facing consumer reads an estimate. It sits after the ODE source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ode-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic arithmetic ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the Gronwall envelope of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gronwall-inequality-namecert}, and the ODE comparison route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ode-comparison-namecert}. The packet names a finite constructive bound surface; it is not host existence, a selected solution curve, quotient path equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Gronwall finite ODE carrier]
\label{def:gronwall-finite-ode-carrier}
A $\GronwallFiniteOdeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(M,T,L,E,R,D,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following rows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the finite mesh row, displaying the inspected time nodes and successor windows.
\item $T$ is the time-window row that bounds the interval on which the displayed ODE source is read.
\item $L$ is the Lipschitz row, exposing the dyadic local constant and the source window in which it is valid.
\item $E$ is the Euler or Picard comparison row that records the finite stepwise approximation route.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational approximation and error sequence.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger carrying every tolerance, mesh width, Lipschitz coefficient, and accumulated error bound.
\item $B$ is the bound row. It displays the Gronwall majorant read by downstream ODE or Real consumers.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the mesh, time, Lipschitz, comparison, readback, dyadic, bound, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the finite bound route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\GronwallFiniteOdeUp$, $\OdeUp$, $\GronwallInequalityUp$, $\OdeComparisonUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\GronwallFiniteOdeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,T,L,E,R,D,B,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host solution theorem, selected flow, quotient path equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Gronwall finite ODE NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:gronwall-finite-ode-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\GronwallFiniteOdeUp$ carrier $G=(M,T,L,E,R,D,B,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\GronwallFiniteOdeUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following five rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed tuple $M,T,L,E,R,D,B,H,C,P,N$;
\item mesh exposure, requiring every time step and successor window to be read through $M$ and $T$;
\item bound stability, requiring the Lipschitz row $L$, comparison row $E$, dyadic ledger $D$, and bound row $B$ to remain componentwise stable under $\hsame$ transport;
\item regular-Cauchy handoff, requiring every exported approximation and error bound to pass through $R$ before any $\RealUp$ seal is read;
\item no-escape from host completeness, selected solution curves, quotient path equality, trichotomy, countable choice, or any estimate not already displayed in $D$ and $B$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:gronwall-finite-ode-carrier}. Carrier admission is exactly the displayed tuple $M,T,L,E,R,D,B,H,C,P,N$ and the local naming row $N$ attached to it.

The mesh exposure row is forced by $M$ and $T$. A consumer can inspect only the listed time nodes and their successor windows, so the route cannot appeal to a host interval theorem or an unbounded solution domain.

The bound-stability row then reads $L,E,D,B,H$. The Lipschitz data and the Euler or Picard comparison row determine which dyadic entries may be used, the ledger $D$ records all tolerances and accumulated errors, the bound row $B$ is the only majorant export, and $H$ preserves these rows componentwise.

Finally the regular-Cauchy handoff uses $R$, while $C$ and $P$ replay and source the same finite route. Since no carrier coordinate names completeness, choice, selected solutions, quotient paths, or trichotomy, the five rows exhaust the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gronwall finite ODE dyadic bound surface]
\label{thm:gronwall-finite-ode-dyadic-bound}
Every accepted $\GronwallFiniteOdeUp$ packet exports an ODE estimate only through the dyadic bound row $B$ after the mesh row $M$, time window $T$, Lipschitz row $L$, Euler or Picard comparison row $E$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$, and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ ledger $D$ have been exposed. The packet exports no separate analytic estimate, selected solution curve, quotient equality of paths, countable-choice schedule, or ambient-completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted carrier $G=(M,T,L,E,R,D,B,H,C,P,N)$. The only estimate-bearing coordinates are the comparison row $E$, dyadic ledger $D$, and bound row $B$.

Read the mesh and time rows first. They restrict the computation to the displayed finite windows, so an estimate consumer cannot widen the route by invoking a host ODE existence theorem.

Next read the Lipschitz and comparison rows. The row $L$ supplies the local coefficient data, while $E$ records the finite Euler or Picard comparison route whose accumulated error is written in $D$.

The exported bound is then exactly $B$, with $R$ repacking the rational data before any $\RealUp$ consumer sees it. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name this same route, and no remaining coordinate can export another estimate surface.
\end{proof}
