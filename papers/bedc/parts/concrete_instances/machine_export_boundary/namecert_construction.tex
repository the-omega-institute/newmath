\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-machine-export-boundary-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ packet realizes the machine-facing export side of \autoref{sec:visions-philosophy-machine-interface} as one finite NameCert boundary. It sits beside the registry export surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-registry-export-surface-namecert}, the machine-readable audit interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-machine-readable-audit-interface-namecert}, the cannot-claim export gate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cannot-claim-export-gate-namecert}, and the certificate-preserving morphism route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-namecertmorphism-namecert}. Those siblings expose registry status, audit readback, refusal, and morphism preservation separately; this packet binds target, audit, refusal, transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert rows before a machine export may be read.

\begin{definition}[Machine export boundary carrier]
\label{def:machine-export-boundary-carrier}
A $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$ row, recording the exported, blocked, horizon-facing, or formal-target registry status.
\item $T$ is the machine target row, recording the exact machine-facing target that may read the export.
\item $A$ is the $\MachineReadableAuditInterfaceUp$ row, recording the schema, checker, audit report, and non-evidence boundary for the export.
\item $F$ is the $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$ refusal row, recording any blocked claim that must remain visible before export.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for registry status, target, audit, refusal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from registry lookup to target routing, audit readback, refusal inspection, and local NameCert exposure.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$, $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\MachineReadableAuditInterfaceUp$, $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$, $\NameCertMorphismUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MachineExportBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for an unregistered export, unchecked theorem evidence, refused-claim erasure, hidden transport, replay outside the displayed continuation row, provenance outside $P$, or a local name not carried by $N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Machine export boundary certified-use route]
\label{thm:machine-export-boundary-certified-use-route}
Any accepted $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ packet exporting a machine-facing boundary must route certified-use reads through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-certifieduseprocess-namecert}, through the registry/export boundary rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-registry-export-surface-namecert}, and through the replayable audit-transcript rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-audit-transcript-envelope-namecert} before the boundary can be consumed as an export interface. Concretely, for $E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)$, the rows $R,A,C,P,N$ identify registry status, audit transcript readback, replay, provenance, and local naming, while $T$ and $F$ keep the machine target and refusal rows visible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:machine-export-boundary-carrier}. The registry row $R$ is the visible $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$ input, the target row $T$ is the machine-facing export input, the audit row $A$ is the machine-readable audit route, and the refusal row $F$ is the cannot-claim boundary.

The certified-use read is therefore not available from $T$ alone. It must pass through $\CertifiedUseProcessUp$ so that public use is read as a process row, then through $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$ so that exported, blocked, horizon-facing, or formal-target status remains visible.

The audit-transcript row is supplied by the replay path through $A$ and $C$, with provenance recorded in $P$. Local naming through $N$ may be consumed only after those certified-use, registry, audit-transcript, and export-interface rows have been displayed.

Thus the export boundary cannot be consumed as a bare machine target or audit-only row. It is consumed only through the certified-use route, registry/export boundary, audit-transcript replay, structural transport, provenance, and local NameCert rows already present in the carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Machine export boundary NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:machine-export-boundary-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted packet $E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MachineExportBoundaryUp}$ obligations are exhausted by registry export admission through $R$, target exposure through $T$, audit readback through $A$, refusal preservation through $F$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.MachineExportBoundaryUp.MachineExportBoundary\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:machine-export-boundary-carrier}. The registry export row $R$, target row $T$, audit row $A$, and refusal row $F$ are displayed before a machine consumer reaches local naming.

Read the sibling routes in order. The registry export surface fixes the public status, the machine-readable audit interface fixes the audit report and non-evidence boundary, and the cannot-claim export gate keeps refused rows visible.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, package, and name the same export route, with $\NameCertMorphismUp$ present only as provenance for certificate-preserving handoff. Since the carrier has no hidden export or proof-producing report coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Machine export boundary registry route]
\label{thm:machine-export-boundary-registry-route}
Every machine export read from an accepted $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ packet must pass through $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\MachineReadableAuditInterfaceUp$, $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$, and $\NameCertMorphismUp$ before the local $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ naming row is consumed. In particular, the registry status route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-registry-export-surface-namecert} is a required sibling surface for any machine-facing export read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)$. The first row $R$ is the registry export surface, so a machine read begins with a displayed registry status rather than with a local machine target.

Next inspect $A$ and $F$. The machine-readable audit interface fixes the audit report and non-evidence boundary, while the cannot-claim export gate preserves any refused claim that must remain visible.

The morphism route is then carried in $P$ as certificate-preserving provenance before $N$ is read as the local name. The transport and replay rows $H,C$ preserve this sibling route, so the packet exposes no direct machine export that skips registry status, audit readback, refusal visibility, or NameCert morphism provenance.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:machine-export-boundary-sibling-route}
Every accepted machine export route
$$
E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)
$$
is a sibling-lattice consumer of $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\MachineReadableAuditInterfaceUp$, $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$, $\NameCertMorphismUp$, and $\MachineInterfaceBoundaryUp$. Hence the route cannot introduce unregistered export, unchecked theorem evidence, or refusal erasure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)$. The registry row $R$, audit row $A$, refusal row $F$, and provenance row $P$ are displayed before the local naming row $N$.

Apply the registry route of \autoref{thm:machine-export-boundary-registry-route}. It forces every machine-facing export read through $\RegistryExportSurfaceUp$, $\MachineReadableAuditInterfaceUp$, $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$, and $\NameCertMorphismUp$ before the boundary can be named locally.

The broader $\MachineInterfaceBoundaryUp$ sibling is preserved by the target row $T$ together with transport $H$ and replay $C$. Those rows keep the same displayed export route and do not add unregistered export, unchecked theorem evidence, or refusal erasure, so the accepted packet is exactly the stated sibling-lattice consumer. Consequently, downstream bounded-diamond and standard-bridge consumers must cite the displayed export route $E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)$ rather than a bare machine target row.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:machine-export-boundary-interface-forward-link}
Every $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ export route is a specialization of the $\MachineInterfaceBoundaryUp$ route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-machine-interface-boundary-namecert} after registry export, audit readback, refusal preservation, and $\NameCertMorphismUp$ provenance have been displayed. Concretely, for an accepted packet
$$
E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N),
$$
the rows $R,A,F,P$ expose registry status, machine-readable audit, cannot-claim refusal, and certificate-preserving provenance before the target row $T$ and local name row $N$ are consumed. Thus the export boundary cannot be read as a machine-interface boundary unless it has first passed through the displayed export ledger.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $E=(R,T,A,F,H,C,P,N)$. The registry export row $R$, audit row $A$, refusal row $F$, and provenance row $P$ are displayed before the local export name $N$.

The broader interface route supplies the machine-facing boundary, while this packet supplies the export specialization. The target row $T$ selects the machine consumer, and the rows $R,A,F,P$ add registry export, audit readback, refusal preservation, and certificate-preserving provenance.

Finally $H$ and $C$ preserve the same displayed route under transport and replay. Hence the accepted export packet is a specialization of $\MachineInterfaceBoundaryUp$ only after the export ledger has been shown, and no direct interface read can bypass those rows.
\end{proof}
