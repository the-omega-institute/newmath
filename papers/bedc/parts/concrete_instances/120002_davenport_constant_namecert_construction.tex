Following Davenport's zero-sum constant tradition in finite abelian groups, the $\DavenportConstantUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface by which a displayed sequence over a finite abelian group is inspected for a nonempty zero-sum subsequence or for a bounded refusal ledger. It sits after the group operation rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-group-namecert}, the commutative group source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-abgroup-namecert}, the unary length and index budget of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nat-namecert}, and the finite sequence spine of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-list-namecert}. The packet names only the displayed finite additive-combinatorics data; it is not an ambient pigeonhole principle, a quotient group theorem, a choice principle over subsequences, or a host-side extremal existence oracle.

\begin{definition}[Davenport constant carrier]
\label{def:davenport-constant-carrier}
A $\DavenportConstantUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Z=(G,A,L,S,W,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the finite $\AbGroupUp$ source, read through the $\GroupUp$ operation, inverse, identity, associativity, commutativity, and equality rows.
\item $A$ is the finite sequence row over the displayed group source. It is a $\ListUp$ spine whose entries are group elements, not an unordered host multiset.
\item $L$ is the $\NatUp$ length row, recording the inspected sequence length, the bounded index set, and the threshold comparison used by the Davenport-constant request.
\item $S$ is the partial-sum ledger. It records the finite prefix sums and the contiguous or subsequence index witnesses inspected by the packet.
\item $W$ is the zero-sum witness or refusal row. In the witness case it names a nonempty displayed sublist and a proof that its transported group sum is the identity; in the refusal case it records the finite inspected obstruction rows available below the threshold.
\item $R$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the group source, sequence spine, length row, partial sums, witness or refusal row, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $H$ records classifier comparison only through the visible group operation, list entries, length budget, partial-sum ledger, and zero-sum witness or refusal row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a zero-sum request through $G,A,L,S,W,R,H$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DavenportConstantUp$, $\AbGroupUp$, $\GroupUp$, $\NatUp$, $\ListUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DavenportConstantUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $G,A,L,S,W,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an external enumeration of all subsequences, a quotient group equality, an unbounded choice of witnesses, or a host theorem asserting the optimal numerical value of the Davenport constant.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Davenport constant NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:davenport-constant-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DavenportConstantUp$ carrier
$$
Z=(G,A,L,S,W,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DavenportConstantUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite abelian group source admission, displayed sequence membership, unary length budgeting, partial-sum ledger construction, zero-sum witness or refusal exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, classifier comparison through displayed rows, $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:davenport-constant-carrier}. Its coordinates are precisely the finite abelian group row, the list spine, the unary length budget, the partial-sum ledger, the witness-or-refusal row, transport, classifier, replay, provenance, and local naming.

The algebraic obligations are inherited in order. The $\AbGroupUp$ source supplies the commutative operation and identity rows through the underlying $\GroupUp$ packet, while the $\ListUp$ spine supplies only displayed finite entries. The $\NatUp$ row bounds the inspected indices and fixes the length comparison before any zero-sum read is attempted.

The additive-combinatorics obligation is local to the ledger. The partial sums in $S$ are finite sums over the displayed entries, and $W$ either exposes a nonempty selected sublist with transported sum equal to the identity or records the finite refusal rows available below the displayed threshold.

Transport, classifier comparison, replay, provenance, and naming preserve the same rows. Since the carrier stores no external subsequence enumeration, quotient equality, choice functional, or host-side optimality theorem, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Davenport constant zero-sum handoff]
\label{thm:davenport-constant-zero-sum-handoff}

$$

It does not supply quotient group equality, a global subsequence-choice functional, a host extremal theorem, or ambient completeness data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from an accepted carrier $Z=(G,A,L,S,W,R,H,C,P,N)$. A consumer can read group addition only through $G$, sequence data only through $A$, and the inspected bound only through $L$.

The zero-sum decision surface is then read from $S$ and $W$. The ledger $S$ lists the finite partial sums and inspected index data, while $W$ names the displayed witness or the bounded refusal rows. No later coordinate can introduce an unlisted subsequence or an unbounded selector.

Finally $R,H,C,P,N$ transport, compare, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Thus the handoff is exactly the displayed zero-sum route through the finite abelian group, length, list, and ledger rows, and not an ambient theorem outside the packet.
\end{proof}
