Following Bishop and Bridges on compact metric spaces and Banach's fixed-point theorem, the $\CompactCompleteFixedPointUp$ packet records the finite certificate route by which a contraction on a compact complete metric source exports a fixed-point orbit and a completion-facing endpoint. The packet sits after the complete metric surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, the compact metric surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the continuous-map graph row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the contraction packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-contraction-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It is a NameCert reconstruction of the classical compact-complete fixed-point handoff, not a selected global choice function or a host-space equality principle.

\begin{definition}[Compact complete fixed-point carrier]
\label{def:compact-complete-fixed-point-carrier}
A $\CompactCompleteFixedPointUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(K,M,T,B,O,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ row naming the source by finite covers, displayed metric data, and compactness witnesses.
\item $M$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ row naming the same source as a completion-facing metric packet.
\item $T$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ graph row for the self-map being iterated.
\item $B$ is the $\BanachContractionUp$ row recording the contraction modulus and the Picard-step boundary.
\item $O$ is the finite orbit ledger, exposing the inspected Picard iterates and their contraction-budget comparisons.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the Cauchy orbit estimates.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal reached after the regular readback and completion boundary are displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $K,M,T,B,O,Q,E,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay of compactness, completeness, continuity, contraction, orbit, readback, and endpoint handoff rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactCompleteFixedPointUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BanachContractionUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactCompleteFixedPointUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,M,T,B,O,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for selected fixed points, quotient orbit equality, host metric completion equality, countable choice, or ambient compactness beyond the displayed $\CompactMetricUp$ and $\CompleteMetricUp$ rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact complete fixed-point NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-complete-fixed-point-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:compact-complete-fixed-point-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\CompactCompleteFixedPointUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $F=(K,M,T,B,O,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$, compact metric source exposure through $K$, complete metric boundary exposure through $M$, continuous self-map replay through $T$, contraction data through $B$, finite orbit ledger exposure through $O$, regular rational readback through $Q$, terminal real seal through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted packet to the eleven displayed rows. The rows $K,M,T,B,O,Q,E$ are the only mathematical content rows, while $H,C,P,N$ provide transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Read the source rows first. The row $K$ supplies compact metric data by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, and $M$ supplies the completion-facing metric boundary by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}. These rows provide finite compact and complete surfaces without adding a separate host-space equality row.

Read the map and orbit rows next. The row $T$ is a $\ContinuousMapUp$ self-map row, $B$ is the contraction row supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-contraction-namecert}, and $O$ is the finite Picard-orbit ledger. Thus every endpoint request must pass through a displayed contraction-budget comparison before it reaches the readback row.

Finally read $Q$ and $E$. The row $Q$ turns the orbit estimates into a $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular readback, and $E$ seals the endpoint at the $\RealUp$ boundary. Since the carrier has no further endpoint coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the NameCert obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact complete fixed-point Cauchy orbit route]
\label{thm:compact-complete-fixed-point-cauchy-orbit-route}
Every accepted $\CompactCompleteFixedPointUp$ consumer that exports a fixed-point endpoint must first expose the route
$$
\CompactMetricUp,\ \CompleteMetricUp,\ \ContinuousMapUp,\ \BanachContractionUp,\ O,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RealUp .
$$
The route presents the endpoint as a compact-complete contraction orbit with a regular Cauchy readback and a terminal real seal. It exports no selected global fixed-point function, quotient equality of orbits, host completion equality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with carrier admission from \autoref{def:compact-complete-fixed-point-carrier}. The public packet displays exactly the rows $K,M,T,B,O,Q,E,H,C,P,N$.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:compact-complete-fixed-point-namecert-obligations}. They force any endpoint read to pass through compactness, completeness, continuous self-map replay, contraction data, and the finite orbit ledger before the regular readback can be consumed.

The readback and endpoint boundary are then fixed by $Q$ and $E$. Because selected fixed points, quotient orbit equality, host completion equality, countable choice, and ambient real completeness are not coordinates of the carrier, they cannot be repacked by the handoff rows $H,C,P,N$.
\end{proof}
