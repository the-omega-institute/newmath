Following Lebesgue and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\LebesgueNumberFunctionUp$ packet records the finite cover-to-radius ledger used when a compact metric source supplies displayed cover rows and downstream uniform-continuity consumers need explicit positive radii. It sits after the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the finite-cover Lebesgue-number surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-cover-lebesgue-number-namecert}, the Lebesgue-number lemma route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lebesgue-number-lemma-namecert}, the compact-uniform modulus extractor of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-uniform-modulus-extractor-namecert}, the rational positivity row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert}, and the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}. The packet names a finite function ledger assigning radii to displayed cover entries; it does not assert open-cover compactness, choose a cover refinement from an ambient theorem, or hide a modulus inside host analysis.

\begin{definition}[LebesgueNumberFunction carrier]
\label{def:lebesgue-number-function-carrier}
A $\LebesgueNumberFunctionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(K,U,C,L,R,D,W,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the compact metric source row, read through $\CompactMetricUp$ and carrying the finite compactness data available to the packet;
\item $U$ is the finite cover row, listing the displayed cover entries to which radii may be assigned;
\item $C$ is the compact finite-cover and Lebesgue-number row, routing the cover through the finite-cover Lebesgue-number and Lebesgue-number lemma surfaces;
\item $L$ is the function ledger assigning a displayed positive radius to each cover entry that has been admitted by $U$ and $C$;
\item $R$ is the rational tolerance row, recording positivity and comparison data for each ledger radius through $\RatUp$;
\item $D$ is the dyadic tolerance row, recording endpoint and refinement budgets through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$;
\item $W$ is the finite witness-window row, recording the compact windows on which the radius assignment is checked;
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transports for $K,U,C,L,R,D,W$ and for the structural rows;
\item $T$ stores displayed transport and replay routes for cover entries, radius reads, and compact-window witnesses;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over the compact metric source, finite cover row, Lebesgue-number rows, rational and dyadic tolerances, witness windows, transports, and local naming evidence;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueNumberFunctionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier is exactly this finite packet. It has no coordinate for an arbitrary open-cover compactness principle, a selected refinement outside the displayed cover, a host real-valued radius function, countable choice, or an ambient uniform-continuity theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[LebesgueNumberFunction NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lebesgue-number-function-namecert-obligations}
The local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueNumberFunctionUp}$ surface consists of carrier exposure for $K,U,C,L,R,D,W,H,T,P,N$, finite cover admission for every entry read by $L$, positive-radius ledger stability through $R$ and $D$, witness-window replay through $W$ and $T$, componentwise transport through $H$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:lebesgue-number-function-carrier}. The only analytic rows are the compact metric source, finite cover, compact finite-cover route, radius ledger, rational and dyadic tolerance rows, and witness windows.

Read the cover and radius obligations together. A ledger entry in $L$ is accepted only for a cover entry already displayed by $U$ and certified through $C$, while $R$ and $D$ record the positivity and tolerance data for the assigned radius.

The remaining rows bind the ledger to BEDC structure. Witness windows $W$ are replayed by $T$, transported by $H$, recorded by $P$, and named by $N$, so the NameCert surface has no hidden radius function or ambient compactness proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LebesgueNumberFunction finite-cover handoff]
\label{thm:lebesgue-number-function-finite-cover-handoff}
Every accepted $\LebesgueNumberFunctionUp$ packet hands a finite cover row from $U$ through the compact finite-cover and Lebesgue-number row $C$ before any consumer reads a radius assignment in $L$. The handoff preserves the same compact metric source $K$, rational tolerance row $R$, dyadic tolerance row $D$, witness-window row $W$, transport rows $H,T$, provenance row $P$, and local name $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the compact metric source and finite cover rows $K$ and $U$. They fix the source and the displayed cover entries before the packet exposes any radius.

Use the compact finite-cover and Lebesgue-number row $C$. It is the only route from the cover entries to the positive-radius ledger, so a consumer cannot read $L$ before the finite-cover handoff has occurred.

Finally preserve the structural rows. The rational and dyadic tolerance rows, finite witness windows, transports, replay routes, provenance, and local name are carried with the same cover entry, giving a handoff rather than a detached radius table.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LebesgueNumberFunction uniform-modulus boundary]
\label{thm:lebesgue-number-function-uniform-modulus-boundary}
A $\CompactUniformModulusExtractorUp$ or uniform-continuity consumer may read an accepted $\LebesgueNumberFunctionUp$ packet only as the finite ledger $K,U,C,L,R,D,W,H,T,P,N$. It receives the displayed cover-to-radius assignments and witness windows, but receives no open-cover compactness principle, no selected host modulus, no quotient real equality, no countable-choice schedule, and no ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the finite-cover handoff first. It supplies the compact source, displayed cover entries, Lebesgue-number route, radius ledger, tolerance rows, and witness windows as one finite packet.

A uniform-modulus consumer may reuse those rows, but only through the transports, replay routes, provenance, and local naming data. Thus the consumer receives a named finite ledger, not a function chosen from outside the packet.

The excluded principles require coordinates absent from the carrier: open-cover compactness, host modulus selection, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness. Since none of these coordinates appears in $F$, they remain outside the uniform-modulus boundary.
\end{proof}
