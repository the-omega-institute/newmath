\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RealUniformLimitUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-real-uniform-limit-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, uniform limits of continuous real-valued maps are obtained from explicit Cauchy moduli and finite readback windows, not from an ambient function-space completion or a choice-selected limit function. The $\RealUniformLimitUp$ packet records this theorem as a finite NameCert surface over the real-name interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the finite windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the continuity carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuous-namecert}, and the uniform-modulus surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformmodulus-namecert}. It is a Real and Completion bridge seed: a downstream consumer may read the limit only through the displayed uniform Cauchy family, rowwise continuity data, shared modulus ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

\begin{definition}[RealUniformLimit carrier]
\label{def:real-uniform-limit-carrier}
A $\RealUniformLimitUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
U=(X,F,W,R,M,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the named source-domain row on which all functions in the family are read.
\item $F$ is a finite row family of $\ContinuousUp$ maps whose values are sealed as $\RealUp$ names.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window schedule for the values of every row of $F$ at every displayed source request.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from those windows, with one regular rational stream for each displayed value read.
\item $M$ is the $\UniformModulusUp$ ledger giving a common Cauchy threshold for all displayed source requests and all sufficiently late rows of $F$.
\item $L$ is the limit-read row: it records the candidate $\RealUp$ value read at a source request only after $W,R,$ and $M$ have supplied the shared uniform Cauchy evidence.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source row, row family, stream windows, regular readbacks, uniform modulus ledger, limit-read row, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a source request through the continuous-map row, the stream windows, the regular readback, the uniform modulus ledger, and the limit-read row.
\item $P$ records $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RealUniformLimitUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\ContinuousUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RealUniformLimitUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a host function-space completion, pointwise-only convergence, a selected subsequence, quotient equality of streams, countable choice, or an ambient completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RealUniformLimit NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:real-uniform-limit-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\RealUniformLimitUp$ carrier $U=(X,F,W,R,M,L,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RealUniformLimitUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed source row, $\RealUp$-valued continuous-map family, $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readbacks, $\UniformModulusUp$ ledger, limit-read row, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every limit read must expose the common modulus ledger before it may expose the $\RealUp$ limit value.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:real-uniform-limit-carrier}. The packet exposes only the rows $X,F,W,R,M,L,H,C,P,N$, so every local read begins with the displayed source, family, window, readback, modulus, and limit data.

The constructive route is uniform rather than pointwise-only. For a displayed tolerance and source request, the window row $W$ and readback row $R$ expose the late values of the $\ContinuousUp$ rows in $F$, while the ledger $M$ supplies one threshold valid for the whole displayed source scope.

The structural rows then preserve that route. The transport table $H$ compares packets componentwise, the replay row $C$ reruns the same finite route for a consumer, and $P,N$ source and name the certificate. Since no omitted coordinate stores a host completion, selected subsequence, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness theorem, no such datum can be exported by the NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RealUniformLimit continuity handoff]
\label{thm:real-uniform-limit-continuity-handoff}
Every completion-facing or continuity-facing consumer of an accepted $\RealUniformLimitUp$ packet must read the $\ContinuousUp$ row family $F$, the $\StreamNameUp$ window schedule $W$, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $R$, and the $\UniformModulusUp$ ledger $M$ before it can use the limit row $L$ as a $\RealUp$-valued continuous map. The handoff route is therefore
$$
X,F,W,R,M \longmapsto L,H,C,P,N .
$$
It exports the constructive uniform-limit theorem only through the displayed finite rows, and not through pointwise convergence alone, host function-space completeness, countable choice, quotient stream equality, or a selected limit representative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:real-uniform-limit-namecert-obligations}. They force every limit read to pass through the displayed family, windows, readbacks, and shared modulus ledger.

Continuity is handed off rowwise and then uniformly. Each row of $F$ supplies its $\ContinuousUp$ replay for the displayed source request, while $W$ and $R$ expose the corresponding $\RealUp$ value names as regular rational readbacks.

The ledger $M$ is the uniform step. It supplies the common late threshold used to compare all displayed rows at the requested tolerance, so the candidate limit row $L$ is read only after the same threshold covers the whole source scope.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the handoff. A consumer that bypasses $F,W,R,$ or $M$ is outside the accepted packet, and a consumer that asks for pointwise-only convergence, a host completion, countable choice, quotient stream equality, or a selected representative asks for data not carried by the certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RealUniformLimit ledger exactness]
\label{thm:real-uniform-limit-ledger-exactness}
Every accepted $\RealUniformLimitUp$ limit read is ledgered by the single packet $X,F,W,R,M,L,H,C,P,N$. The same ledger records the Real-valued continuous rows, stream windows, regular rational readbacks, shared uniform Cauchy threshold, limit-read row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it records no pointwise-only convergence row, host limit selector, quotient stream class, countable-choice schedule, or ambient function-space completion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the carrier of \autoref{def:real-uniform-limit-carrier}. Its analytic rows are exactly $X,F,W,R,M,L$, and its structural rows are exactly $H,C,P,N$.

The NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:real-uniform-limit-namecert-obligations} make these rows the complete public surface. A limit read has to display the continuous row family, the value windows, the rational readbacks, and the uniform ledger before the limit row is available.

The continuity handoff of \autoref{thm:real-uniform-limit-continuity-handoff} then identifies the consumer route through that same packet. Since the packet has no additional coordinate, the excluded pointwise, host-completion, selector, quotient, and choice data cannot be recovered from an accepted read.
\end{proof}
