\begin{theorem}[Constructive ultrametric completion Cauchy-completion dependency]
\label{thm:constructive-ultrametric-completion-cauchy-completion-dependency}
Every $\ConstructiveUltrametricCompletionUp$ completion read first passes through the ultrametric carrier of \autoref{def:constructive-ultrametric-completion-carrier}, then through the metric-completion and separated-completion rows already named by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-separatedcompletion-namecert}. The strong-triangle and dyadic-radius rows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ultrametricspace-namecert} remain visible before any completion interface is consumed, so the completion handoff is a carrier-local route rather than a direct appeal to quotient completion, selected representatives, host equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the constructive ultrametric completion carrier of \autoref{def:constructive-ultrametric-completion-carrier}. Its source row, dyadic radius ledger, strong-triangle row, Cauchy route, and separated handoff are already displayed before any completion interface row appears.

Then read the metric-completion and separated-completion siblings. They supply the named boundary rows that the handoff must pass through, while the ultrametric-space chapter keeps the strong-triangle and dyadic-radius rows visible. The route therefore stays local to the carrier and does not appeal to quotient completion, selected representatives, host equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
