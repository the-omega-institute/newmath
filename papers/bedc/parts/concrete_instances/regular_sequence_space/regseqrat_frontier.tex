\begin{theorem}[RegularSequenceSpace RegSeqRat frontier]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-space-regseqrat-frontier}
Let $S=(X,T,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ carrier. Any Real-completion consumer of $S$ reaches the terminal $\RealUp$ seal only through the displayed $\StreamNameUp$ schedule $T$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ regularity rows $Q$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance surface $D$, and $\RealUp$ seal $R$, with transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows $H,C,P,N$ still visible. The packet exports no quotient of sequences, choice-selected subsequence, hidden representative selector, or ambient Real completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-sequence-space-carrier}. The value-facing route exposes the schedule row $T$, the regular rational readback row $Q$, the dyadic tolerance row $D$, the terminal seal $R$, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

Use the sibling route of \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-sibling-route}. It forces completion-facing consumers to read the StreamName, RegSeqRat, DyadicRatCore, and Real rows in that order before any value-facing data are public.

Next use the Real and Completion dependency route of \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-real-completion-dependency-route}. It keeps the finite-tail and tail-filter rows upstream of the same $T,Q,D,R$ route and blocks any detour through quotient stream equality or selected limits.

Finally replay $H,C,P,N$. Componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the displayed frontier and add no coordinate for sequence quotients, choice-selected subsequences, hidden representatives, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness. Hence the RegSeqRat frontier is the only route from the accepted packet to Real-completion consumers.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RegularSequenceSpace RegSeqRat frontier tail seal]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-space-regseqrat-frontier-tail-seal}
Let $S=(X,T,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ carrier. A $\RealUp$-completion consumer must expose the finite-tail row, tail-filter row, $\StreamNameUp$ schedule $T$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ regularity row $Q$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row $D$, $\RealUp$ seal $R$, componentwise transport $H$, continuation replay $C$, provenance $P$, and local naming row $N$ before reading the packet as a completed regular sequence. The tail seal is therefore a displayed RegSeqRat frontier obligation and not a quotient stream, selected representative, choice-selected subsequence, host real equality, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the RegSeqRat frontier theorem, \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-regseqrat-frontier}. It places $T,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N$ on the only value-facing route available to Real-completion consumers.

Use the Real and Completion dependency route of \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-real-completion-dependency-route}. It keeps the finite-tail and tail-filter rows upstream of the same $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ route.

Apply the sibling route theorem, \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-sibling-route}. It prevents a completion-facing consumer from skipping the displayed stream schedule, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerance row, terminal seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Thus the accepted packet can be read as a completed regular sequence only after the tail seal has exposed the finite-tail and tail-filter rows together with $T,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N$. Since no carrier coordinate stores quotient stream equality, selected representatives, choice-selected subsequences, host real equality, or ambient completeness, the seal is exactly the displayed RegSeqRat frontier boundary.
\end{proof}
