\begin{theorem}[RegularSequenceSpace finite shift stability]\label{thm:regular-sequence-space-shift-stability}
Let $S=(X,T,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ carrier. Every finite shift of the displayed $\StreamNameUp$ schedule transports through the same $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row $D$, tail-filter ledger, terminal $\RealUp$ seal $R$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. Thus finite shift stability is a displayed route inside the regular sequence space packet, not a quotient-stream argument, selected-limit argument, countable-choice argument, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use finite tail-depth transport, \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-tail-window-transport}. A finite shift of the schedule is a finite tail-depth extension of the displayed $\StreamNameUp$ window, so the read remains on the same $T,Q,D,R,H,C,P,N$ route.

Apply tail-diagonal projection, \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-tail-diagonal-projection}. Any two shifted tail-filter reads feeding the same regular rational or Real consumer are projected back to one displayed StreamName window before the value-seal row is public.

Finally use the RegSeqRat Real seal route of \autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-regseqrat-real-seal-route}. The shifted read reaches $\RealUp$ only after the $\RegSeqRatUp$ classifier row and the visible structural rows have stayed attached to the accepted packet.
\end{proof}
