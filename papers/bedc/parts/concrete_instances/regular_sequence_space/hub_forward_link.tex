\begin{theorem}[RegularSequenceSpace hub forward link]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-space-hub-forward-link}
Every $\RegularSequenceSpaceUp$ packet exposed by the hub routes a
completion-facing read through at least one substantive L10 sibling before the
terminal value seal is public. Concretely, a consumer must keep the displayed
$\StreamNameUp$ finite window, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$
finite-cell route, $\CompleteMetricLimitUniquenessUp$ uniqueness boundary, and
terminal $\RealUp$ seal visible before treating the packet as a completion
input. The hub therefore exports a forward lattice edge to existing L10
completion siblings, not a kernel-only carrier island, quotient stream
equality, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness
principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the sibling route of
\autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-sibling-route}. It forces every
completion-facing read through the displayed $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$,
$\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows before the local packet exports
value-facing data.

Use the common-window completion route of
\autoref{thm:regular-sequence-space-common-window-completion-route}. The same
read must expose the finite-cell route of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-net-namecert} after the common
window and before the terminal seal, so the hub points to a concrete L10
finite-net sibling.

Finally route uniqueness through
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-complete-metric-limit-uniqueness-namecert}.
The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the displayed window, readback,
finite-cell, uniqueness, and Real-seal route, and no coordinate in the carrier
adds quotient stream equality, selected limits, countable choice, or ambient
completeness.
\end{proof}
