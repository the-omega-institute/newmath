The $\BishopRealIntervalHalvingUp$ packet isolates that operation as L10 NameCert data after the dyadic carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the stream naming surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational sequence packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the constructive real packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the located-real surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedreal-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[BishopRealIntervalHalving carrier]
\label{def:bishop-real-interval-halving-carrier}
A $\BishopRealIntervalHalvingUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(L,U,M,S,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the lower dyadic endpoint row;
\item $U$ is the upper dyadic endpoint row;
\item $M$ is the dyadic midpoint row, read as the exact average of $L$ and $U$ inside the dyadic carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert};
\item $S$ is the selected-half row, choosing either the lower half $[L,M]$ or the upper half $[M,U]$ from displayed located evidence;
\item $D$ is the dyadic tolerance row recording the radius before and after one halving step;
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the selected endpoint sequence;
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row, asserting that the selected half remains inside the same constructive-real envelope;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint, midpoint, selected-half, tolerance, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local certificate rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ routes for endpoint inspection, midpoint replay, selected-half handoff, tolerance decrease, regular-sequence readback, real-seal replay, and consumer use;
\item $P$ is the provenance row over the cited dyadic, stream-name, regular-sequence, real, and located-real siblings;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRealIntervalHalvingUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for trichotomy, Heine--Borel compactness, quotient-real equality, global choice of nested intervals, or countable choice. It records one finite bisection step with all endpoints, tolerance, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows visible.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BishopRealIntervalHalving NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-real-interval-halving-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-real-interval-halving-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRealIntervalHalvingUp}$ surface consists of five finite obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item midpoint exactness: $M$ is displayed as the dyadic midpoint of the endpoint rows $L$ and $U$;
\item selected-half containment: the selected row $S$ names either $[L,M]$ or $[M,U]$ and remains contained in the original located interval;
\item dyadic radius decrease: the tolerance row $D$ records the halved radius of the selected interval;
\item Real seal nonescape: the seal row $E$ keeps the selected half inside the same $\RealUp$ envelope read through $R$;
\item provenance exhaustion: the rows $H,C,P,N$ replay only the dyadic endpoint, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real, LocatedReal, transport, continuation, package, and local NameCert sources displayed in $K$.
\end{enumerate}
The obligations produce a finite bisection certificate, not an interval-completeness theorem, trichotomy principle, Heine--Borel theorem, quotient-real construction, uniform search over arbitrary predicates, or countable-choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The midpoint obligation is read directly from the $M$ row together with the dyadic endpoint rows $L$ and $U$. Since all three rows are part of the finite packet, no external real-number quotient or hidden completion operation is required to name the midpoint.

The selected-half and radius obligations are the rows $S$ and $D$. The selected half is one of the two displayed dyadic subintervals, and the tolerance row records that this subinterval has the halved radius.

The readback and seal obligations are the rows $R$ and $E$. The regular rational sequence readback supplies the finite endpoint data consumed by the constructive-real seal, while the seal records nonescape inside the same real envelope.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ exhaust the transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows. They cite only the dyadic, stream-name, regular-sequence, real, located-real, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ surfaces already displayed in the packet, so the certificate exports a single finite halving step and none of the refused global principles.
\end{proof}
