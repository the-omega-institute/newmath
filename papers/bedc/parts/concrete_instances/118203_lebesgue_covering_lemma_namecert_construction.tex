\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp$}

Following Lebesgue and Bishop--Bridges compact metric analysis, the $\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp$ packet records the finite compact-cover route that turns displayed compact metric data into a finite positive-radius witness. It sits between the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the metric-space rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the rational and dyadic tolerance surfaces of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite-radius consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-lebesgue-number-namecert}, and compact-uniform-continuity consumers such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-uniform-continuity-modulus-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-uniform-modulus-certificate-namecert}. The packet names only finite cover data, radius comparisons, componentwise transport, and local provenance; it is not open-cover compactness, a selected subcover principle, countable choice, or an ambient theorem about all metric spaces.

\begin{definition}[Lebesgue covering lemma carrier]
\label{def:lebesgue-covering-lemma-carrier}
A $\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(K,M,U,F,R,D,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row, exposing a named compact metric packet only through finite compact-cover data.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ distance row whose point and ball readings are available to the cover test.
\item $U$ is the finite cover row: a finite list of named cover centers, cover radii, and membership tests read from the compact source.
\item $F$ is the finite subcover witness already displayed by $U$; it is a finite list coordinate, not a host-selected subcover.
\item $R$ is the positive radius row, recorded through $\RatUp$ and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparisons.
\item $D$ is the finite radius-bound ledger asserting that every point read through $K,M$ lies in a cover member whose displayed radius is bounded below by the same positive row $R$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact source, metric distance, finite cover, subcover list, rational radius, dyadic ledger, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a point read through $K,M$ to the finite cover row $U$, the finite list row $F$, the radius row $R$, and the bound ledger $D$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for arbitrary open covers, a host-chosen subcover, countable choice, quotient metric equality, ambient compactness, or a global Heine--Borel theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lebesgue covering lemma NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:lebesgue-covering-lemma-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp$ carrier $L=(K,M,U,F,R,D,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp}$ surface consists exactly of five obligation families:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the compact metric source, metric distance row, finite cover row, finite subcover list, positive radius row, and dyadic radius-bound ledger;
\item cover exposure, requiring each point read from $K,M$ to enter the finite list row $F$ only through the displayed cover row $U$;
\item finite positive-radius certification through $R,D$, with all comparisons recorded through $\RatUp$ and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$;
\item componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay through $H$ and $C$;
\item $\Pkg$ provenance and local naming through $P,N$, including the handoff to $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ and compact-uniform-continuity consumers.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation is stored outside these rows, and no obligation selects a subcover from an arbitrary open cover.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:lebesgue-covering-lemma-carrier}. The only cover data are $U$ and the finite list coordinate $F$, so carrier admission and cover exposure are read from displayed finite rows.

The radius witness is read next. Rows $R$ and $D$ provide the positive rational or dyadic lower-bound data and the finite ledger saying that each point read through $K,M$ reaches a cover member with that bound.

The structural rows preserve the same finite route. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ move the cover, list, radius, and ledger rows componentwise, while $P$ and $N$ source and name the packet without adding open-cover compactness or a choice-based selector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lebesgue covering lemma finite radius bound]
\label{thm:lebesgue-covering-lemma-finite-radius-bound}
Every finite-cover consumer of an accepted $\LebesgueCoveringLemmaUp$ packet factors through the displayed route
$$
\CompactMetricUp,\MetricSpaceUp\longrightarrow U,F\longrightarrow R,D\longrightarrow \FiniteLebesgueNumberUp .
$$
In particular, compact-uniform-continuity consumers may read the shared positive radius only after the finite cover row, finite list row, rational or dyadic radius row, and bound ledger have all been exposed. The route does not provide arbitrary open-cover compactness, a host-selected subcover, countable choice, quotient metric equality, or ambient completeness for $\RealUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer read of $L=(K,M,U,F,R,D,H,C,P,N)$. The compact metric and metric-space rows $K,M$ supply only the point and distance data needed to inspect the displayed finite cover.

Next read the finite cover route. The row $U$ exposes the cover members, $F$ records the finite list actually used by the packet, and $R,D$ give the shared positive-radius bound through rational and dyadic comparisons.

Finally hand the same rows to $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ or to a compact-uniform-continuity consumer. Since $P$ names no arbitrary open cover, host selector, countable-choice schedule, quotient metric equality, or ambient completeness row, every accepted consumer remains inside the finite radius route.
\end{proof}
