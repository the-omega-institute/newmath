Following Méray and Bishop--Bridges on Cauchy presentations of the real line, the $\MerayCauchyRealUp$ packet records the historical construction as finite displayed name data rather than as a quotient of Cauchy sequences. It sits after the Cauchy-real presentation of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-real-presentation-namecert}, the quotient-free boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-reals-without-quotient-namecert}, the stream-window carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet gives the Real and Completion phase a traditional source row whose public reads remain finite all the way to the $\RealUp$ seal.

\begin{definition}[Méray Cauchy-real carrier]
\label{def:meray-cauchy-real-carrier}
A $\MerayCauchyRealUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(A,W,R,D,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the displayed Cauchy source row. It records the rational Cauchy presentation used by the historical construction, with its modulus and tolerance requests shown as finite data.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window service row. It exposes exactly the inspected prefixes of the source and no infinite stream equality.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row. It converts the displayed windows into the regular rational sequence data consumed by downstream real-name readers.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger shared by the Cauchy source, window reads, regular readback, and terminal seal.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal, exposed only after $A,W,R,D$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the Cauchy source, windows, readback, tolerance ledger, seal, replay, provenance, and local name rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay along the finite route
$$
A,W,R,D \longmapsto E,H,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MerayCauchyRealUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MerayCauchyRealUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through the nine displayed rows. It has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, selected representatives, host real equality, countable choice, trichotomy, or an ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Méray Cauchy-real NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:meray-cauchy-real-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MerayCauchyRealUp$ carrier $M=(A,W,R,D,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MerayCauchyRealUp}$ surface consists exactly of the finite carrier row, displayed Cauchy-source admission, StreamName window locality, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance accounting, RealUp seal ordering, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. No obligation is stored outside those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:meray-cauchy-real-carrier}. The tuple lists the Cauchy source, finite window service, regular rational readback, dyadic ledger, terminal seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The analytic rows are then read in order. The source row $A$ supplies only displayed rational Cauchy data, $W$ exposes the finite windows inspected from it, $R$ records the regular readback of those windows, and $D$ accounts for every tolerance used before the seal.

The structural rows add no extra primitive. The $\hsame$ row transports the displayed coordinates, the $\Cont$ row replays the same route, and $P,N$ source and name the packet. Since quotient classes, selected representatives, host equality, choice, trichotomy, and ambient completeness are not coordinates of $M$, the listed rows exhaust the local certificate surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Méray Cauchy-real RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:meray-cauchy-real-regseqrat-handoff}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\MerayCauchyRealUp$ packet must first read the displayed Cauchy source row, the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, the componentwise $\hsame$ transport, the displayed $\Cont$ replay, the $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row. The handoff exports no quotient stream equality, selected Cauchy representative, host real equality, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:meray-cauchy-real-namecert-obligations}. They force every accepted read to stay inside $A,W,R,D,E,H,C,P,N$.

The pre-seal route is explicit. A RegSeqRat consumer receives $R$ only as the readback of the displayed windows $W$ over the Cauchy source $A$, with every tolerance charged to $D$.

The Real-facing route is downstream of the same readback. The seal row $E$ is available only after $A,W,R,D$ have been displayed and replayed through $H,C,P,N$. Thus the historical Cauchy-real presentation hands off to regular rational and real-name consumers without adding any quotient, selector, host equality, choice, trichotomy, or completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}
