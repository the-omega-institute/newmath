$$

\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite schedule row for reading rational observations.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback row over the displayed schedule.
\item $T$ is the Bishop tail-test row recording that later displayed reads stay within the dyadic tolerance demanded by $D$.
\item $U$ is the $\RegularCauchyLimitUniquenessUp$ comparison row, available only when two displayed candidate-limit reads are compared through the same regular route.

\begin{theorem}[Bishop regular real limit NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-regular-real-limit-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopRegularRealLimitUp$ carrier $L=(D,S,R,T,U,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopRegularRealLimitUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, dyadic tolerance visibility, finite schedule admission, regular rational readback, Bishop tail-test exposure, regular Cauchy uniqueness boundary exposure, terminal real sealing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every limit-facing read passes through
$$
D,S,R,T,U,E,H,C,P,N
$$
and through no quotient stream equality, selected limit, countable-choice selector, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-regular-real-limit-carrier}. The rows $D,S,R$ display the tolerance ledger, schedule, and regular readback before any limit-facing row is inspected.

Read the Bishop tail test next. The row $T$ is attached to the same displayed schedule and dyadic ledger, so it records only finite tail data. When two candidate-limit reads are compared, $U$ routes the comparison through $\RegularCauchyLimitUniquenessUp$ before the real seal $E$ is public.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and locally name this route. Since the excluded principles have no carrier coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local $\NameCert$ obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop regular real limit regularity induction]
\label{thm:bishop-regular-real-limit-regularity-induction}
Every accepted $\BishopRegularRealLimitUp$ packet supports induction on displayed finite schedules: the base read is the first $\StreamNameUp$ window checked against $D$, and the induction step appends one later window whose $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback satisfies the same Bishop tail-test row $T$. The induction exports only the preserved route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RegularCauchyLimitUniquenessUp,\ \RealUp
$$
and no quotient, selector, choice, host equality, or ambient completeness data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The base case is the displayed schedule row $S$ read against the dyadic ledger $D$. Its regular rational readback is the row $R$, and the tail-test row $T$ records the admitted tolerance.

For the induction step, append one finite window to $S$ and read it through the same dyadic ledger. Regularity is preserved because the appended read is admitted only when it satisfies $T$ and therefore remains on the displayed $\RegSeqRatUp$ route.

The uniqueness and seal rows are downstream. The row $U$ compares only displayed candidate-limit reads, and $E$ is reached only through the same finite route. The excluded completion principles are absent from the carrier, so the induction cannot export them.
\end{proof}

The handoff exports finite regular real limit data and not quotient streams, selected limits, countable-choice schedules, host real equality, or full $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

Apply regularity induction to the inspected finite schedule. It preserves the route through appended windows and prevents the consumer from replacing the displayed rows by a selected representative or quotient stream.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$ to transport, replay, source, and locally name the same handoff. No excluded completion coordinate is present, so the public seal is only the displayed regular real limit route.
\end{proof}
