\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for Window-Six Weight Log-Concavity}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-window6-weight-logconcave-turan-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The window-six Fibonacci carrier records finite binary words with no adjacent true entries and reads their weight distribution through the polynomial
$$
W_m(x)=\sum_k b(m,k)x^k,
$$
where $b(m,k)$ is the number of length-$m$ admissible words with exactly $k$ true entries. Equivalently $b(m,k)=\binom{m-k+1}{k}$ on its natural support, and $\sum_k b(m,k)=F_{m+2}$. The BEDC carrier used here is the direct finite enumeration of those words, not the closed binomial formula.

\begin{definition}[Fibonacci weight coefficient]
\label{def:window6-fibonacci-weight-coefficient}
For $m,k\in\mathbb{N}$, let $b(m,k)$ be the coefficient of $x^k$ in $W_m(x)$, computed by enumerating all length-$m$ Boolean words, rejecting words with adjacent true entries, and counting the survivors with weight $k$. In Lean this coefficient is the natural number
$$
\mathsf{weightCoeff}(m,k).
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Window-six through window-ten degree-two Turan certificate]
\label{thm:window6-weight-logconcave-turan}
For the finite windows $m=6,7,8,9,10$, every internal coefficient position checked by the certificate satisfies the degree-two Turan inequality
$$
b(m,k+1)b(m,k-1)\leq b(m,k)^2.
$$
Equivalently, the inspected coefficient rows of $W_m(x)$ are log-concave. The Lean certificate covers the nontrivial internal positions
$$
\begin{aligned}
&(6,1),(6,2),\\
&(7,1),(7,2),(7,3),\\
&(8,1),(8,2),(8,3),\\
&(9,1),(9,2),(9,3),(9,4),\\
&(10,1),(10,2),(10,3),(10,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.Window6WeightLogConcaveTuran.window\_six\_to\_ten\_weight\_logconcave\_turan}

\begin{proof}
The proof is a finite certificate. Lean computes the admissible-word counts by recursive enumeration and then proves each inequality by an explicit natural-number slack. For example, the window $m=6$ row is
$$
(b(6,0),b(6,1),b(6,2),b(6,3))=(1,6,10,4),
$$
with total $21=F_8$. The two internal inequalities are witnessed by
$$
\begin{aligned}
b(6,2)b(6,0)+26&=b(6,1)^2,\\
b(6,3)b(6,1)+76&=b(6,2)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The remaining windows are discharged in the same finite manner, using only the enumerator and natural-number slack equalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-window RH criterion shape]
\label{thm:window6-weight-logconcave-rh-shape}
The certificate of \autoref{thm:window6-weight-logconcave-turan} realizes the degree-two Turan positivity shape on a Fibonacci carrier. In the classical analytic route, the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the completed xi function lying in the Laguerre--Polya class, and that route is tested by finite Jensen-polynomial hyperbolicity windows; at degree two the finite positivity condition has the Turan form
$$
a_k^2\geq a_{k-1}a_{k+1}.
$$
This chapter supplies that shape only for the displayed Fibonacci coefficients, adjacent to the Laguerre--Polya packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-laguerre-polya-namecert} and the RH roadmap of \autoref{ch:visions-riemann-hypothesis}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The statement is a scope identification. The Lean theorem supplies finite natural-number inequalities for the coefficient rows of $W_m(x)$. The Laguerre--Polya and Jensen route asks for hyperbolicity of finite polynomial windows, whose degree-two shadow is the adjacent coefficient inequality above. The carrier here is not the xi-function coefficient stream; it is the finite Fibonacci-cube weight carrier.

This is a finite-window positivity certificate on a Fibonacci carrier, not a proof of RH; it models the degree-two Turán/Jensen shape that Laguerre-Pólya (hence RH) requires.
\end{proof}
