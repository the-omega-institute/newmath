\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegularSequenceComparisonUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\RegularSequenceComparisonUp$ packet records the finite comparison surface for two regular rational stream windows. It sits between the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite observation route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational sequence packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the displayed real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the Cauchy naming surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-namecert}. The packet compares only displayed windows and tolerances; it is not a Dedekind cut, quotient real equality, selected limit, trichotomy principle, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Regular sequence comparison carrier]
\label{def:regular-sequence-comparison-carrier}
A $\RegularSequenceComparisonUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(S_0,S_1,Q_0,Q_1,D,T,M,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S_0,S_1$ are $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window rows for the two regular rational streams being compared.
\item $Q_0,Q_1$ are $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback rows attached to the two displayed windows.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ladder, displayed as finite dyadic stages
$$
\delta_0 \geq \delta_1 \geq \cdots \geq \delta_k
$$
together with the endpoint and difference comparisons consumed at each inspected stage.
\item $T$ is the termwise comparison ledger pairing the visible $Q_0$ and $Q_1$ entries at the dyadic stages listed by $D$.
\item $M$ is the modulus-window row recording which finite StreamName windows are required before a requested dyadic tolerance may be read.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row, exposed only after $S_0,S_1,Q_0,Q_1,D,T,M$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for both windows, both regular-rational readbacks, the dyadic tolerance ladder, the comparison ledger, the modulus-window row, the Real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a comparison request to the two windows, their regular readbacks, the dyadic ladder, the comparison ledger, and the Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularSequenceComparisonUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CauchyUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularSequenceComparisonUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S_0,S_1,Q_0,Q_1,D,T,M,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for Dedekind cuts, quotient real equality, selected limits, trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular sequence comparison NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-comparison-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularSequenceComparisonUp$ carrier $R=(S_0,S_1,Q_0,Q_1,D,T,M,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegularSequenceComparisonUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the two $\StreamNameUp$ windows, the two $\RegSeqRatUp$ readbacks, the finite $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ladder, the termwise comparison ledger, the modulus-window row, the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-sequence-comparison-carrier}. The two windows $S_0,S_1$, the two regular-rational readbacks $Q_0,Q_1$, and the dyadic ladder $D$ are the only source rows visible to the comparison.

Read the comparison through $T$ and $M$. The ledger $T$ pairs only entries already exposed by the two readback rows, while $M$ records the finite windows required for the requested tolerance.

Finally transport and replay the same rows through $H$ and $C$, and source them through $P,N$. Since the carrier has no further coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations and leave quotient equality, selected limits, countable choice, trichotomy, and Real completeness outside the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular sequence comparison RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:regular-sequence-comparison-regseqrat-handoff}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\RegularSequenceComparisonUp$ packet receives comparison data only through the finite route
$$
\StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
as displayed by $S_0,S_1,Q_0,Q_1,D,T,M,E,H,C,P,N$. The handoff therefore supplies a finite regular-sequence comparison window, not a quotient equality of real names, selected limit, Dedekind-cut comparison, trichotomy decision, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted packet and project its two $\StreamNameUp$ rows. The consumer cannot read a regular rational comparison until both finite windows $S_0,S_1$ have been displayed.

Next read $Q_0,Q_1$ and the dyadic tolerance ladder $D$. The $\RegSeqRatUp$ rows expose only finite regular-rational readbacks, and $D$ records the finite tolerance stages at which those readbacks are compared.

The comparison ledger $T$ and modulus-window row $M$ then expose the requested finite comparison. The terminal row $E$ is a displayed $\RealUp$ seal for this route only; it is not a completeness assertion.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Thus any RegSeqRat or Real-facing consumer receives the finite comparison route and no hidden quotient equality, selected limit, Dedekind cut, trichotomy decision, countable-choice schedule, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}
