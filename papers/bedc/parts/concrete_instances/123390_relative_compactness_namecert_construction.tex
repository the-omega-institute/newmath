\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RelativeCompactnessUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-relative-compactness-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive metric analysis, the $\RelativeCompactnessUp$ packet records the finite interface by which a displayed family inside a metric carrier is read as precompact relative to a complete or compact metric envelope. It sits between the finite-net discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the completion service of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, and the compact-metric package of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. The packet names the relative compactness data consumed by completion-facing arguments; it does not assert open-cover compactness, selected limits, quotient equality of streams, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Relative compactness carrier]
\label{def:relative-compactness-carrier}
A $\RelativeCompactnessUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(M,A,T,C,K,S,Q,H,D,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ source row, carrying the point classifier, distance readback, and metric comparison ledger.
\item $A$ is the admitted family or subset row whose points are read inside the metric carrier $M$.
\item $T$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row for $A$, exposing positive-tolerance requests, finite center bundles, membership rows, and metric coverage ledgers.
\item $C$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ completion-service row available for Cauchy reads produced from the same metric carrier.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ envelope row, used when the same total-bounded and complete data are read as a compact metric consumer surface.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ sequence-window row for finite observations of a carried stream in $A$.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regularity row that records the rational Cauchy readback used by the completion route.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric source, family row, finite nets, completion service, compact envelope, sequence windows, regularity readback, provenance, and local name.
\item $D$ records displayed $\Cont$ routes from finite-net requests to Cauchy-window extraction, from Cauchy windows to completion service, and from the relative packet to compact-metric consumers.
\item $P$ records $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RelativeCompactnessUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RelativeCompactnessUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for a host-level closure operation, an arbitrary open-cover subcover, a chosen limit point outside the displayed completion service, or quotient equality of Cauchy names.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Relative compactness NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:relative-compactness-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RelativeCompactnessUp$ carrier $R=(M,A,T,C,K,S,Q,H,D,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RelativeCompactnessUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed metric-family packet, finite-net exactness through $T$, completion handoff through $C$, compact-envelope compatibility through $K$, sequence-window readback through $S$, regular rational Cauchy readback through $Q$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed continuation routing through $D$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:relative-compactness-carrier}. The displayed rows are the metric source, the admitted family, the total-bounded finite-net surface, the complete-metric service, the compact-metric envelope, the sequence-window row, the regular-rational row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Read the finite-net row first. It supplies the tolerance-indexed center bundles and coverage ledgers for the admitted family $A$ inside $M$, using the same constructive metric data as the sibling total-boundedness chapter.

Next read the completion and compact-envelope rows. The completion service consumes Cauchy-window data, while the compact-metric envelope matches the same total-bounded and complete ingredients already exposed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. The structural rows $H,D,P,N$ keep these reads in one finite packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative compactness Cauchy-subsequence route]
\label{thm:relative-compactness-cauchy-subsequence}
Every sequence read through an accepted $\RelativeCompactnessUp$ carrier
$$
R=(M,A,T,C,K,S,Q,H,D,P,N)
$$
has its completion-facing Cauchy-subsequence read factored through the finite route
$$
M,A,T,S,Q,C,K,H,D,P,N.
$$
The finite-net row $T$ supplies the nested tolerance windows, the $\StreamNameUp$ row $S$ exposes the observed windows of the sequence, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $Q$ records the regular Cauchy readback, and the $\CompleteMetricUp$ row $C$ is the only completion service available before the $\CompactMetricUp$ envelope row $K$ is consumed. No selected host subsequence, quotient stream equality, arbitrary closure point, or ambient compactness principle is exported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the finite-net exactness in \autoref{thm:relative-compactness-namecert-obligations}. A sequence consumer can ask for a tighter tolerance only by reading the displayed $\TotallyBoundedUp$ row $T$ over the admitted family $A$ inside the metric row $M$.

Use the sequence and regularity rows $S$ and $Q$. They turn the finite tolerance windows into a displayed Cauchy readback, with every step still transported by $H$ and replayed by $D$.

Finally use the completion handoff and compact-envelope compatibility. The row $C$ is the only complete-metric service row in the carrier, and $K$ only packages the same total-bounded and complete data as a compact-metric consumer surface. Since the carrier has no host-level selected subsequence, quotient equality, arbitrary closure point, or ambient compactness coordinate, the Cauchy-subsequence read factors through exactly the displayed route.
\end{proof}
