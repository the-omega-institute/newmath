\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MetricSequentialSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-metric-sequential-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kelley and Engelking, the $\MetricSequentialSpaceUp$ packet records the classical metric-space sequentiality interface as finite BEDC data. It sits over the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the topology neighbourhood surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the sequence-limit closure route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sequentialclosure-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the displayed metric convergence route that a topology-facing sequential-closure consumer may inspect; it is not a quotient of streams, not arbitrary topological sequentiality, not a choice principle, not open-cover compactness, and not completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Metric sequential space carrier]
\label{def:metric-sequential-space-carrier}
A $\MetricSequentialSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(D,T,W,R,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the metric source row, recording the visible carrier, distance classifier, zero-distance boundary, and finite tolerance comparisons consumed from $\MetricUp$.
\item $T$ is the topology neighbourhood row, recording the neighbourhood names around candidate limits and the topology-facing reads supplied by the metric source.
\item $W$ is the sequence window row, recording the finite $\StreamNameUp$ schedules, inspected indices, and admitted entries used by convergence consumers.
\item $R$ is the regular rational readback row, translating each displayed metric tolerance request into $\RegSeqRatUp$ data before any real comparison is read.
\item $L$ is the limit row, recording the candidate limit point, the neighbourhood requests around it, and the metric tolerance ledger sealed through $\RealUp$.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric source, topology neighbourhoods, sequence windows, regular rational readback, limit row, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from a sequential-closure request through $D,T,W,R,L$ without adding a host equality or subsequence-selection route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\SequentialClosureUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MetricSequentialSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for selected subsequences, quotient streams, arbitrary topological sequentiality, open-cover compactness, detached host equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Metric sequential space NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:metric-sequential-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MetricSequentialSpaceUp$ carrier $M=(D,T,W,R,L,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MetricSequentialSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of the metric source row $D$, topology neighbourhood row $T$, sequence window row $W$, regular rational readback row $R$, limit row $L$, componentwise transport row $H$, displayed replay row $C$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the finite $\BHist$ carrier. The metric source $D$ supplies the distance classifier and tolerance comparisons, while the topology row $T$ supplies the neighbourhood names that a sequential-closure consumer may ask to read.

The sequence data are displayed before any limit read. Windows are stored in $W$, rational readback in $R$, and the candidate limit ledger in $L$, so convergence evidence cannot be produced by a detached stream or a host-selected subsequence.

Rows $H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, package, and name exactly those displayed rows. Since the carrier contains no additional coordinate for quotient streams, arbitrary topological sequentiality, choice, compactness, host equality, or $\RealUp$ completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Metric sequential space sequential-closure handoff]
\label{thm:metric-sequential-space-sequential-closure-handoff}
Let $M=(D,T,W,R,L,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\MetricSequentialSpaceUp$ packet. Every $\SequentialClosureUp$ consumer of the metric candidate limit factors through
$$
D,T,W,R,L \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus topology-facing sequential closure receives the displayed metric convergence route, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, Real-sealed limit row, and local NameCert packaging, and receives no host closure operator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a topology-facing closure request. Carrier admission exposes the metric source $D$ and neighbourhood row $T$ before the request can inspect any sequence or limit data.

Replay through $C$ then forces the request to consume the finite windows $W$, the regular rational readback $R$, and the limit ledger $L$. This is the same finite route needed by the $\SequentialClosureUp$ surface, but it is anchored in the metric classifier rather than in an ambient closure operator.

Transport by $H$ and packaging by $P,N$ preserve the displayed route. Hence the exported handoff is the stated finite factorization, with no separate topological closure, quotient stream equality, selected subsequence, compactness principle, or real-completeness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Metric sequential space nonescape]
\label{thm:metric-sequential-space-nonescape}
An accepted $\MetricSequentialSpaceUp$ packet cannot export selected subsequences, quotient stream equality, arbitrary topological sequentiality, open-cover compactness, host equality of metric points, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness from its sequentiality interface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the carrier rows of \autoref{def:metric-sequential-space-carrier}. The sequence interface is the finite window row $W$, the metric tolerance interface is the readback row $R$, and the limit interface is the displayed row $L$ sealed through the available Real comparison data.

Apply \autoref{thm:metric-sequential-space-namecert-obligations}. The local NameCert row names only $D,T,W,R,L,H,C,P,N$, and \autoref{thm:metric-sequential-space-sequential-closure-handoff} shows that every closure consumer passes through those rows in order.

The refused principles have no $\BHist$ row, no $\hsame$ transport coordinate, no $\Cont$ replay edge, no $\Pkg$ provenance entry, and no local $\NameCert$ clause. The packet therefore exposes metric sequentiality only as the displayed finite BEDC route.
\end{proof}
