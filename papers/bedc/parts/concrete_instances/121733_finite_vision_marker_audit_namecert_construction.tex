\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-vision-marker-audit-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$ packet turns the marker-ledger discipline of \autoref{ch:capstones} into finite NameCert data. It reads the visions index as a source-label surface, points to concrete-instance targets through displayed chapter labels, records the marker ledger, replays the audit readback, and carries provenance for the route that connects inspiration-only vision material to mature concrete packets. It is a finite audit object for the existing marker surface, not a replacement for the target chapters it names.

\begin{definition}[Finite vision marker audit carrier]
\label{def:finite-vision-marker-audit-carrier}
A $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
V=(S,T,L,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source-label row naming the vision chapter or vision index site being audited.
\item $T$ is the concrete-target row naming the concrete-instance chapter label claimed by the marker.
\item $L$ is the finite marker-ledger row recording the source label, target label, marker text, and local position.
\item $A$ is the audit-readback row that checks source-label agreement, target-label agreement, and marker-ledger agreement in one finite pass.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source label, target label, ledger, audit readback, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay that reads $S$, then $T$, then $L$, and finally the readback row $A$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$, the visions index, the concrete-instance target chapter, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier stores only finite marker data and route readback. It does not import the mathematical content of the target chapter, rewrite a vision file, or certify any theorem beyond the marker route itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite vision marker audit NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-vision-marker-audit-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$ carrier $V=(S,T,L,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp}$ surface consists of source-label exposure through $S$, concrete-target exposure through $T$, marker-ledger exposure through $L$, audit-readback exactness through $A$, componentwise $\hsame$ stability through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-vision-marker-audit-carrier}. The source label, target label, marker ledger, audit readback, transport, replay, provenance, and name are the only rows available to an accepted consumer.

The readback row $A$ is downstream of $S,T,L$ and checks exactly those labels and the marker ledger. The continuation row $C$ fixes the same order, while $H$ transports the rows componentwise.

The package row $P$ names the visions index and the concrete target without importing the target chapter's theorem content. Therefore the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite vision marker audit readback failure]
\label{thm:finite-vision-marker-audit-readback-failure}
An accepted $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$ carrier rejects any marker read whose source label or concrete-target label differs from the displayed rows $S$ and $T$. The marker ledger row $L$ and audit-readback row $A$ must agree on the same pair of labels before the route can be consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:finite-vision-marker-audit-namecert-obligations}. They make $S,T,L,A$ visible and force the audit readback to occur only after the source and target rows are displayed.

If a candidate read changes the source label, it no longer transports componentwise through the $S$ row. If it changes the target label, it no longer transports componentwise through the $T$ row.

The ledger and readback rows therefore agree only for the displayed pair. A mismatched source or target label leaves the accepted carrier surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite vision marker audit TasteGate obligations]
\label{thm:finite-vision-marker-audit-tastegate-obligations}
The TasteGate surface for $\FiniteVisionMarkerAuditUp$ requires Lean to provide local $\mathsf{Nontrivial}$ and $\mathsf{FieldFaithful}$ instances over the same carrier rows $S,T,L,A,H,C,P,N$. Nontriviality separates a marker with matching source and target labels from a candidate with a mismatched label, and field faithfulness preserves the source-label, target-label, ledger, and audit-readback order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier of \autoref{def:finite-vision-marker-audit-carrier} supplies the only rows on which the taste gate may act. A nontrivial boundary is visible because a matched marker read and a mismatched marker read differ at $S$ or $T$ before any audit readback is accepted.

Field faithfulness is the requirement that every faithful read preserve $S,T,L,A$ and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ in the displayed order. Dropping the ledger or replacing the audit readback by a detached target theorem would create data outside the carrier.
\end{proof}
