\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$}

Following the common-carrier programme of \autoref{ch:visions-unified-reality-common-carrier-projection} and the completion-boundary analysis of \autoref{sec:unified-reality-core-commonality-completion-boundary}, the $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$ packet records the finite route by which shared carrier rows are allowed to reach completion-facing readback. It reads the dyadic scale surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a completion boundary for a common carrier; it does not assert a universal object, quotient completion, selected representative, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Common-carrier completion-boundary carrier]
\label{def:common-carrier-completion-boundary-carrier}
A $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(G,O,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the common-carrier source row, exposing only the shared generated carrier and its public classifier boundary.
\item $O$ is the object-specific observable ledger, recording which public rows of the carrier are being tested for completion-facing faithfulness.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row used to measure finite completion windows.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row generated from $G,O,D$.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the same stream windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ or completion-seal row reached only after $D,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, observable ledger, dyadic tolerance, stream window, regular readback, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from common-carrier observation through completion-facing readback and back to the same carrier rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $G,O,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an unlicensed fourth observable, a universal completed carrier, quotient representatives, countable choice, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Common-carrier boundary NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:common-carrier-completion-boundary-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(G,O,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the common source $G$, observable-ledger admission for $O$, dyadic tolerance admission for $D$, stream-window generation through $S$, regular rational readback through $R$, terminal sealing through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:common-carrier-completion-boundary-carrier}. The displayed tuple gives the only rows available to the local certificate, so the NameCert surface can expose only $G,O,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$.

Read the completion-facing rows in order. The observable ledger $O$ chooses which public rows of the common source are under test, the dyadic row $D$ fixes the finite tolerance scale, the stream row $S$ supplies the windows, and the regular readback $R$ is attached to those windows before the terminal seal $E$ can be inspected.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. Since none of the excluded objects appears in the carrier, the obligations cannot export a universal completed carrier, a quotient representative, countable choice, or ambient real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common-carrier completion nonescape]
\label{thm:common-carrier-completion-boundary-nonescape}
Every completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
G,O,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N .
$$
In particular, a consumer moving from a shared carrier row toward $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ readback cannot bypass the observable ledger $O$, the dyadic tolerance row $D$, or the stream window $S$, and cannot replace them by an unledgered fourth observable, quotient completion, selected representative, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:common-carrier-completion-boundary-namecert-obligations}. They force every completion-facing read to expose the common source, observable ledger, dyadic tolerance, stream windows, regular readback, terminal seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

Use the core-completeness boundary of \autoref{thm:unified-reality-core-completeness-excludes-fourth-observables}. Any row that is not a core readout must be carried as a displayed extension, bridge, boundary, or refusal. Here the boundary row is exactly $O,D,S,R,E$ over the common source $G$.

Finally inspect the carrier. Since no coordinate stores quotient completion, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, none of those objects can be read from the accepted packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common-carrier regular readback handoff]
\label{thm:common-carrier-completion-boundary-regular-readback}
Let $Q=(G,O,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\CommonCarrierCompletionBoundaryUp$ carrier. Any $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ read from $Q$ is tied to the same common source row $G$ and observable ledger $O$ through the dyadic tolerance row $D$ and stream window $S$. The handoff is therefore a displayed completion boundary, not a claim that the common carrier itself is a completed universal object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read $D$ as the finite dyadic tolerance row admitted over the common source and observable ledger. It supplies the scale for the stream windows, but it does not choose a completed object.

The stream windows $S$ are displayed before the regular readback $R$ and terminal seal $E$ can be consumed. Thus every completion-facing read remains attached to the same $G,O,D,S$ route.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve those rows. A readback that omits $O$, $D$, or $S$ would use a coordinate absent from \autoref{def:common-carrier-completion-boundary-carrier}, so it is not a read of the accepted packet.
\end{proof}
