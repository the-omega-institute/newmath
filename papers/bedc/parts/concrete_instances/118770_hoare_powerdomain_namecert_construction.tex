\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\HoarePowerdomainUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-hoare-powerdomain-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Hoare and Smyth domain-theoretic powerdomain semantics, the $\HoarePowerdomainUp$ packet records the lower powerdomain side of finite nondeterministic observation as a hit-oriented NameCert surface. It sits below the open-set classifier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-scott-topology-namecert}, the order carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-poset-namecert}, and the compact-family comparison route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-vietoris-topology-namecert}. The packet gives domain-topology consumers a lower hit window without importing an upper powerdomain, a convex powerdomain law, ideal completion, or host-level set equality.

\begin{definition}[Hoare powerdomain carrier]
\label{def:hoare-powerdomain-carrier}
A $\HoarePowerdomainUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
H=(D,O,K,L,W,R,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the underlying domain row, read through the ordered carrier surface of $\PosetUp$.
\item $O$ is the Scott-open observation row, read through $\ScottTopologyUp$.
\item $K$ is the compact-or-finite lower witness row, comparable with the hyperspace route of $\VietorisTopologyUp$.
\item $L$ is the lower hit predicate row: an observation is public only when the packet displays a witness that the lower set meets the inspected open.
\item $W$ is the finite hit-window ledger listing the inspected open rows and the associated witnesses.
\item $R$ records monotone readback along the displayed order row of $D$.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $D,O,K,L,W,R,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the domain row through Scott opens, lower hits, finite windows, order readback, transport, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\HoarePowerdomainUp$, $\ScottTopologyUp$, $\PosetUp$, $\VietorisTopologyUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\HoarePowerdomainUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these rows. It has no coordinate for upper misses, convex combination, ideal completion, saturated-set equality, arbitrary unions of host subsets, or a quotient by extensional set equality.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hoare powerdomain NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hoare-powerdomain-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\HoarePowerdomainUp$ carrier $H=(D,O,K,L,W,R,T,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\HoarePowerdomainUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the domain row, Scott-open observation row, compact-or-finite lower witness row, lower hit predicate row, finite hit-window ledger, monotone readback row, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Every downstream domain-topology consumer must read those rows before treating the packet as a lower powerdomain object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hoare-powerdomain-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes the domain row, Scott-open row, compact-or-finite witness row, lower hit predicate, finite hit-window ledger, monotone readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Read the lower observation route first. The domain row $D$ supplies the ordered carrier surface, while $O$ restricts observations to displayed Scott opens and $K$ supplies the finite or compact witness data that can meet such opens.

The hit information is then forced through $L,W,R$. The row $L$ records lower hit admission, $W$ lists the inspected windows and witnesses, and $R$ keeps the readback monotone along the displayed order.

Finally $T,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no coordinate stores upper misses, convex powerdomain operations, ideal completion, arbitrary host subsets, or set-extensional quotient data, the listed rows exhaust the local certificate surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hoare lower hit window]
\label{thm:hoare-powerdomain-lower-hit-window}
Every $\ScottTopologyUp$, $\PosetUp$, or $\VietorisTopologyUp$ consumer of an accepted $\HoarePowerdomainUp$ carrier must read the finite lower-hit route
$$
\PosetUp
\longmapsto
\ScottTopologyUp
\longmapsto
\VietorisTopologyUp
\longmapsto
\HoarePowerdomainUp .
$$
Thus the public observation is a displayed lower hit of an inspected Scott open, not an upper miss, a convex powerdomain comparison, a completed ideal, or a host subset equality claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the ordered carrier. The consumer receives $D$ before any lower-powerdomain observation is available, so the packet is anchored in the displayed $\PosetUp$ surface rather than in host subset equality.

The open observation is then a $\ScottTopologyUp$ row. The lower hit predicate can be read only against such an inspected open, and the compact-or-finite witness row supplies the visible meet data comparable with $\VietorisTopologyUp$.

The finite window ledger $W$ prevents the hit from becoming an arbitrary host-level union or a hidden ideal completion. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same lower-hit packet for downstream consumers, so the only public observation is the stated finite route.
\end{proof}
