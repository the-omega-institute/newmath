\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-compact-regular-cover-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive compactness, the $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$ packet records finite cover refinement as explicit NameCert data over a compact metric carrier. It sits beside the compact-metric route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the metric-space source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the rational radius discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert}, the Lebesgue-number handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-lebesgue-number-namecert}, and the $\UniformModulusUp$ consumer used by \autoref{ch:visions-compact-uniform-continuity}. The packet exposes a finite regular refinement route for compact-uniform-continuity and Lebesgue-number style arguments without claiming open-cover compactness, selected subcovers, quotient point equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Bishop compact regular cover carrier]
\label{def:compact-regular-cover-carrier}
A $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
R=(K,M,Q,\mathcal{P},C,L,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row, exposing only the compact finite-cover inspection surface.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ distance row used to compare centers, radii, and refinement witnesses.
\item $Q$ is the $\RatUp$ positive-radius row, naming the rational radius ledgers used by cover and refinement checks.
\item $\mathcal{P}$ is the finite $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ cover row, listing centers, rational radii, and membership witnesses for the displayed finite cover.
\item $C$ is the regularity row, requiring every bundled ball to carry a smaller rational core still routed through $M,Q,\mathcal{P}$.
\item $L$ is the refinement ledger row, recording the finite parent map from a refined $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ back to the displayed cover.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the compact source, metric source, rational radii, cover bundle, regularity row, refinement ledger, replay row, provenance row, and local naming row.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of finite cover refinement over the same bundle.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\RatUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no open-cover compactness object, selected subcover functional, quotient point equality, countable choice, hidden Lebesgue-number selector, or ambient completion row.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop compact regular cover NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-regular-cover-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$ carrier $R=(K,M,Q,\mathcal{P},C,L,H,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp}$ surface consists exactly of compact-source admission, metric-distance admission, positive rational radius admission, finite $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ cover admission, regular core exposure, refinement-ledger readback, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. The route is confined to
$$
\CompactMetricUp,\quad \MetricSpaceUp,\quad \RatUp,\quad \UniformModulusUp,\quad \mathsf{ProbeBundle}.
$$
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier in \autoref{def:compact-regular-cover-carrier}. The only source rows visible to a cover-refinement consumer are $K,M,Q,\mathcal{P}$, so every cover read starts from compact metric data, metric comparisons, positive rational radii, and a finite bundle.

The regularity row $C$ and refinement ledger $L$ are attached to that same finite bundle. They can shrink a displayed radius and record a parent map for the refined bundle, but they cannot introduce an external subcover or an unlisted center.

The structural rows $H,T,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since none of the excluded compactness, choice, quotient, selector, or completion objects occurs in the carrier, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop compact regular cover refinement induction]
\label{thm:compact-regular-cover-refinement-induction}
Every finite refinement read of an accepted $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$ carrier is obtained by primitive induction over the displayed $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ spine. At the empty spine the refinement ledger is the empty ledger. At a successor probe, the read appends one regular core row from $C$, one positive $\RatUp$ radius comparison from $Q$, and one parent entry in $L$, then replays the same route through $H,T,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the finite $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ row $\mathcal{P}$. The empty bundle has no cover entry and therefore supplies the empty refinement ledger.

For the successor step, read the next bundled center and rational radius through $M$ and $Q$. The regularity row $C$ supplies the smaller core for that entry, and $L$ records the parent map back to the original cover row.

Transport and replay are componentwise. The rows $H,T,P,N$ attach the successor entry to the same accepted carrier, so the induction never leaves the finite bundle or asks for a selected subcover.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop compact regular cover nonescape]
\label{thm:compact-regular-cover-nonescape}
Every compact-uniform-continuity or Lebesgue-number consumer that reads a $\BishopCompactRegularCoverUp$ packet receives only the finite route
$$
K,M,Q,\mathcal{P},C,L,H,T,P,N .
$$
In particular, a downstream $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff may use the displayed regular refinement ledger, but it cannot import open-cover compactness, countable choice, selected representatives, quotient point equality, a hidden Lebesgue-number selector, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the source rows $K,M,Q,\mathcal{P}$. They expose compact metric data, metric comparisons, positive rational radii, and the finite cover bundle before any refinement is consumed.

Read the regular core and refinement ledger through $C$ and $L$. These rows only relate displayed bundled balls to displayed refined cores, so they provide the finite cover-refinement boundary needed by compact-uniform-continuity and Lebesgue-number consumers.

Finally use $H,T,P,N$ to transport, replay, source, and name the route. The carrier has no coordinate for the excluded compactness, choice, quotient, selector, representative, or completion objects, so no downstream handoff can escape the displayed finite packet.
\end{proof}
