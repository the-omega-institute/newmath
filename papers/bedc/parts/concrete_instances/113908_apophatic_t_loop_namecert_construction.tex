\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ApophaticTLoopUp$}

The $\ApophaticTLoopUp$ packet realizes the apophatic $T$-loop discipline of \autoref{subsec:philosophy-apophatic-t-loops} as finite NameCert data. It sits beside the host-source refusal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hostprimitiveleakage-namecert}, the export refusal gate of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cannot-claim-export-gate-namecert}, the claim-entry layer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-claimregistrylayer-namecert}, the external supply boundary of \autoref{sec:external-supply-boundary-problem}, and the negative certificate boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-negativecertificateboundary-namecert}. The packet names the records-side return through a gap inscription while keeping the far-end supply position outside the carrier.

\begin{definition}[Apophatic T-loop carrier]
\label{def:apophatic-t-loop-carrier}
An $\ApophaticTLoopUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(H,K,M,R,G,I,L,E,N,H_s,C,P,Q)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $H$ is the records-side history row from which the loop read begins.
\item $K$ is the classifier row naming the displayed commitment consumed by the history.
\item $M$ is the reality-constrained meaning row attached to that classifier read.
\item $R$ is the reality-constraint row whose source is not discharged by the closed records.
\item $G$ is the gap row exposing the unclosed $\mathsf{GAP}_T$ question without internalizing its far end.
\item $I$ is the inscription row, recording the local records-side return point.
\item $L$ is the loop ledger, recording that the return reaches a successor or refinement of the records-side history rather than $\mathsf{T}$ itself.
\item $E$ is the external-supply-boundary row, tying the unclosed source to an accepted boundary form without importing an external primitive.
\item $N$ is the negative-certificate-boundary row, recording the cannot-internalize and cannot-export refusals.
\item $H_s$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $H,K,M,R,G,I,L,E,N$ and the local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from records through classifier, meaning, reality constraint, gap exposure, inscription, loop ledger, boundary rows, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ApophaticTLoopUp$, $\HostPrimitiveLeakageUp$, $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$, $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$, $\NegativeCertificateBoundaryUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $Q$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ApophaticTLoopUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these displayed rows. It has no coordinate for the far-end supply position, host primitive content, total truth, object-layer $\mathsf{T}$, exportable cannot-claim discharge, or an erased gap.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Apophatic T-loop NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:apophatic-t-loop-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ApophaticTLoopUp$ carrier
$$
T=(H,K,M,R,G,I,L,E,N,H_s,C,P,Q),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ApophaticTLoopUp}$ surface consists exactly of records-side history exposure through $H$, classifier exposure through $K$, reality-constrained meaning exposure through $M$, reality-constraint exposure through $R$, gap exposure through $G$, local inscription exposure through $I$, loop-ledger exposure through $L$, external-supply-boundary exposure through $E$, negative-certificate-boundary exposure through $N$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H_s$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $Q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:apophatic-t-loop-carrier}. Its content rows are the records-side history, classifier, reality-constrained meaning, reality constraint, gap, inscription, loop ledger, external boundary, and negative boundary.

The records-facing route follows \autoref{thm:philosophy-apophatic-t-loop}. A history read may return through classifier use, meaning, reality constraint, gap exposure, and local inscription, but the return is a records-side successor or refinement rather than an object-layer copy of $\mathsf{T}$.

The refusal rows fix the boundary. $\HostPrimitiveLeakageUp$ blocks imported host primitives, $\CannotClaimExportGateUp$ blocks overstrong export, $\ClaimRegistryLayerUp$ stores the bounded claim row, and $\NegativeCertificateBoundaryUp$ records the cannot-internalize certificate. These rows name the boundary without discharging the far-end supply position.

Finally $H_s,C,P,Q$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since the carrier has no additional coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Apophatic T-loop gap inscription handoff]
\label{thm:apophatic-t-loop-gap-inscription-handoff}
Every accepted $\ApophaticTLoopUp$ consumer that reads the loop ledger $L$ must pass through the gap row $G$, the local inscription row $I$, the external-supply-boundary row $E$, and the negative-certificate-boundary row $N$. The handoff records a records-side return; it does not internalize the far-end supply position, import host primitive leakage, export an overstrong claim, erase the claim registry, or close the negative certificate boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with \autoref{thm:apophatic-t-loop-namecert-obligations}. The loop ledger is exposed only after the records-side history, classifier, meaning, reality constraint, gap, inscription, and boundary rows are visible.

Read the gap and inscription rows together. The gap row $G$ names the unclosed question, while $I$ names the local records-side return point. Thus the handoff is a visible inscription in the history, not a carrier field for the far-end supply position.

Now inspect the refusal siblings. The external-supply-boundary row $E$ keeps the source outside the generated packet, and the negative-certificate-boundary row $N$ records the cannot-internalize refusal. Host primitive leakage and cannot-claim export are therefore blocked by the same route that names the loop.

The structural rows $H_s,C,P,Q$ preserve this ordering. They can replay the displayed handoff, but they cannot add a host primitive, erase the claim registry, close the gap, or turn the inscription into $\mathsf{T}$ itself.
\end{proof}
