\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\WeilUniformityUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-weil-uniformity-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Weil and Kelley on the left and right uniformities of a topological group, the $\WeilUniformityUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface on which the two entourage directions are read together. It sits between the entourage rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformspace-namecert}, the multiplication and inversion continuity rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topgroup-namecert}, the open-neighbourhood source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, and the completion-facing consumers of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completion-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniform-complete-space-namecert}. The packet explains why Raikov-style completion and topological-group completion require a two-sided uniformity surface rather than an anonymous uniform-space row.

\begin{definition}[Weil uniformity carrier]
\label{def:weil-uniformity-carrier}
A $\WeilUniformityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
W=(G,T,U,L,R,M,I,C_L,C_R,Q,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the underlying group-object row whose multiplication and unit are read only through the topological-group packet.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ neighbourhood row supplying the open identity-neighbourhood requests.
\item $U$ is the $\UniformSpaceUp$ source row exposing finite entourage requests, diagonal rows, symmetry rows, and composition refinements.
\item $L$ is the left-entourage ledger generated by rows of the form $x^{-1}y$ lying in a displayed identity neighbourhood.
\item $R$ is the right-entourage ledger generated by rows of the form $xy^{-1}$ lying in a displayed identity neighbourhood.
\item $M$ records the $\TopGroupUp$ multiplication-continuity transport for the left and right ledgers.
\item $I$ records the $\TopGroupUp$ inversion-continuity transport exchanging the left and right ledgers.
\item $C_L$ is the $\CompletionUp$ consumer row that can read Cauchy data only after the left ledger is displayed.
\item $C_R$ is the $\UniformCompleteSpaceUp$ consumer row that can read completion data only after the right ledger is displayed.
\item $Q$ records the comparison boundary with $\HausdorffUniformityUp$ when separated completion or uniqueness consumers require diagonal separation.
\item $V$ records the two-sided completion handoff used by Raikov-style consumers without selecting a quotient completion point.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for all displayed rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay for neighbourhood admission, left and right entourage generation, multiplication transport, inversion transport, completion handoff, and separated comparison.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\WeilUniformityUp$, $\UniformSpaceUp$, $\TopGroupUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\CompletionUp$, $\UniformCompleteSpaceUp$, $\HausdorffUniformityUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\WeilUniformityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The local classifier compares accepted carriers only through $G,T,U,L,R,M,I,C_L,C_R,Q,V,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host topological group, a chosen completion, quotient equality of Cauchy filters, ambient uniform completeness, or a one-sided replacement for the two-sided uniformity route.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Weil uniformity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:weil-uniformity-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\WeilUniformityUp$ carrier
$$
W=(G,T,U,L,R,M,I,C_L,C_R,Q,V,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\WeilUniformityUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, identity-neighbourhood access, left-entourage generation, right-entourage generation, multiplication transport, inversion transport, completion-facing handoff, separated-uniform comparison, two-sided completion provenance, componentwise $\hsame$ stability, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. A completion or topological-group consumer must read both $L$ and $R$ before it may inspect $C_L$, $C_R$, or $V$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier through \autoref{def:weil-uniformity-carrier}. The rows $G,T,U$ supply the group, topology, and ambient uniform-space requests, while $L$ and $R$ are distinct displayed ledgers for the left and right entourages.

Read the topological-group transport rows. Multiplication continuity in $M$ keeps products compatible with both ledgers, and inversion continuity in $I$ exchanges the two directions instead of allowing one direction to stand for the other.

The completion-facing rows $C_L,C_R,Q,V$ occur only after the two ledgers have been displayed. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same packet, so the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weil uniformity topological-group handoff]
\label{thm:weil-uniformity-topgroup-handoff}
Every $\TopGroupUp$ consumer of an accepted $\WeilUniformityUp$ packet reads multiplication and inversion through the finite route
$$
\TopologyUp,\ \UniformSpaceUp,\ L,\ R,\ M,\ I \longrightarrow \CompletionUp,\ \UniformCompleteSpaceUp .
$$
The handoff keeps the left and right entourage ledgers visible until completion-facing rows are reached. Consequently the packet may feed Raikov-style completion, separated-uniform comparison, and uniform-complete-space consumers, but it does not identify the carrier with a host completion, choose Cauchy representatives, assert ambient completeness, or collapse the two ledgers to a single anonymous uniform-space row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the topology and uniform-space source rows. The open identity-neighbourhood requests in $T$ and the finite entourage data in $U$ are the only source rows from which the left and right ledgers can be generated.

Use the $\TopGroupUp$ transport rows $M$ and $I$. Multiplication reads both directions through the same finite packet, while inversion swaps the direction of an entourage request and therefore requires both $L$ and $R$ to remain present.

Finally inspect $C_L,C_R,Q,V,H,C,P,N$. These rows hand the two-sided packet to completion and separated-uniform consumers while preserving componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since no host completion or quotient representative is a coordinate of the carrier, the handoff cannot bypass the displayed two-sided route.
\end{proof}
