\begin{definition}[Cauchy separable completion carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-separable-completion-carrier}
A $\CauchySeparableCompletionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(D,T,W,R,C,E,H,K,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the dense enumeration row, naming the countable dense source exposed by $\RealSeparabilityUp$.
\item $T$ is the dyadic tolerance row, recording the finite precision requests used to compare dense approximants.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row, listing exactly the stream fragments inspected for the displayed tolerance requests.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row, repacking the inspected windows as a regular Cauchy handoff.
\item $C$ is the regular Cauchy handoff row consumed by the $\MetricCompletionUp$ boundary.
\item $E$ is the completion seal, visible only after $D,T,W,R,C$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal reached from the completion seal and not from a host equality of completed names.
\item $K$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay for the dense enumeration, tolerance, window, regular readback, completion seal, and Real seal rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchySeparableCompletionUp$, $\RealSeparabilityUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchySeparableCompletionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $D,T,W,R,C,E,H,K,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient streams, selected dense points, chosen limits, host equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy separable completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-separable-completion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchySeparableCompletionUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(D,T,W,R,C,E,H,K,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CauchySeparableCompletionUp}$ surface consists exactly of dense enumeration admission, dyadic tolerance accounting, stream-window inspection, regular rational readback, regular Cauchy handoff, completion-seal discipline, Real-seal routing, componentwise transport and replay, provenance, and local naming. Every completion-facing consumer must read the displayed route
$$
\RealSeparabilityUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \MetricCompletionUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
before it may export the completed real-name boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-separable-completion-carrier}. The projection exposes the dense enumeration row, dyadic tolerance row, stream windows, regular rational readback, regular Cauchy handoff, completion seal, Real seal, and structural rows.

The route is finite at each consumer request. The dense row $D$ supplies only the displayed enumeration boundary, while $T$ supplies the finite tolerance budget used to inspect the requested approximants.

The stream and regular rows then determine the handoff surface. The window row $W$ records the inspected fragments, and $R$ repacks them as the regular Cauchy readback consumed by $C$.

Finally $E$ and $H$ are exposed only after that handoff, and $K,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since no carrier coordinate stores quotient streams, selected dense points, chosen limits, host equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient Real completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy separable completion dense regular handoff]
\label{thm:cauchy-separable-completion-dense-regular-handoff}
Every accepted $\CauchySeparableCompletionUp$ packet hands a completion-facing consumer from the dense enumeration row $D$ to the $\MetricCompletionUp$ seal only through the dyadic tolerance row $T$, the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window row $W$, and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular Cauchy readback row $R$. The handoff supplies a displayed regular Cauchy boundary; it does not supply a selected dense point, a quotient stream, a chosen limit, or host equality of completed names.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:cauchy-separable-completion-namecert-obligations}. They order every completion-facing read through the dense enumeration, tolerance, stream-window, regular readback, and handoff rows before the completion seal is available.

The dense row is only an enumeration boundary. A consumer may request finite approximants from $D$, but those requests are interpreted through the dyadic budget $T$ and the stream windows $W$.

The regular Cauchy handoff is therefore the row $R$ together with its continuation into $C$. No selected dense point or quotient stream is present in the carrier, so the completion-facing consumer receives exactly the displayed regular route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy separable completion no-choice boundary]
\label{thm:cauchy-separable-completion-no-choice-boundary}
No accepted $\CauchySeparableCompletionUp$ carrier exports Real completeness, open-cover compactness, quotient streams, selected dense points, host equality of completed names, or countable choice. Any Real-facing read must factor through the dense enumeration row, dyadic tolerance row, stream-window row, regular Cauchy handoff, completion seal, Real seal, transport and replay row, provenance row, and local NameCert row displayed in \autoref{def:cauchy-separable-completion-carrier}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \autoref{thm:cauchy-separable-completion-dense-regular-handoff}. It forces every completion-facing read through $D,T,W,R,C$ before the completion seal can be inspected.

The Real-facing seal adds no new analytic principle. It is the row $H$ reached after $E$, with $K$ transporting and replaying only the same finite dependency surface.

Thus the excluded principles are absent by carrier projection. The packet records separable completion data as finite NameCert rows and not as a choice principle, quotient construction, open-cover theorem, host equality, or ambient completeness assertion.
\end{proof}
