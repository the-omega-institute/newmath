\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BanachStoneUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-banach-stone-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Banach and Stone, the $\BanachStoneUp$ packet records the finite BEDC rows through which a compact-space homeomorphism signal is read from an isometric algebra route between scalar-valued continuous function packets. It sits after the compact naming surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}, the continuous-map carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the Banach-space packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-namecert}, the normed-space rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normedspace-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The chapter names the finite certificate route from compact topology to Banach function-space isometry; it does not assert host function-space extensionality, a global choice of points, a quotient spectrum, or an ambient theorem outside the displayed rows.

\begin{definition}[Banach--Stone carrier]
\label{def:banach-stone-carrier}
A $\BanachStoneUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(K,L,C,F,I,S,R,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the source $\CompactUp$ row, recording the finite compact-space naming surface whose closed probes are available to the route.
\item $L$ is the target $\CompactUp$ row, recording the corresponding compact-space naming surface for the codomain side.
\item $C$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ row, displaying the finite continuity probes used to compare scalar-valued reads over the two compact packets.
\item $F$ is the Banach function-space row, recording the visible $\BanachUp$ and $\NormedSpaceUp$ norm probes for the retained scalar-valued continuous functions.
\item $I$ is the isometry row, recording the finite norm-preservation comparisons accepted by the route.
\item $S$ is the spectrum-route row, recording the finite maximal-probe and point-separation observations through which compact points are read from the function-space packet.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the scalar values, norm comparisons, and point-separation reads.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for compact rows, continuous-map rows, function-space rows, isometry rows, spectrum-route rows, real seals, and structural rows.
\item $T$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact probes to continuous scalar reads, from continuous reads to norm probes, and from isometry probes to spectrum-route observations.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BanachStoneUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\NormedSpaceUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BanachStoneUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $K,L,C,F,I,S,R,H,T,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host function extensionality, quotient spectra, selected points, non-displayed maximal ideals, or an ambient Banach--Stone theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Banach--Stone NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:banach-stone-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BanachStoneUp$ carrier $B=(K,L,C,F,I,S,R,H,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BanachStoneUp}$ surface consists exactly of source and target compact admission, continuous scalar-map admission, Banach and normed-space probe exposure, finite isometry comparison, spectrum-route observation, Real sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every Banach--Stone consumer must inspect those rows before treating an isometric function-space packet as compact-topological information.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:banach-stone-carrier}. The compact rows $K,L$ and the continuous-map row $C$ provide the only topological source data, while $F,I$ provide the only function-space and isometry data.

The spectrum-route row $S$ is downstream of those rows: it can read point-separation and maximal-probe observations only after compact probes, continuous scalar reads, norm probes, and finite isometry comparisons have been displayed.

The rows $R,H,T,P,N$ then seal, transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for host function extensionality, quotient spectra, selected points, or off-packet maximal ideals, the NameCert surface is exactly the displayed row list.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Banach--Stone isometry spectrum route]
\label{thm:banach-stone-isometry-spectrum-route}
Let $B=(K,L,C,F,I,S,R,H,T,P,N)$ be an accepted $\BanachStoneUp$ carrier. Any read that extracts compact-space information from a Banach function-space isometry must first expose the source and target compact rows, the continuous scalar-map row, the Banach and normed-space probe rows, the finite isometry row, the spectrum-route row, the Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. The route can report only the displayed compact-topology signal carried by those rows; it cannot export host function-space extensionality, a quotient spectrum, a selected point map, a choice principle, or an ambient homeomorphism theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:banach-stone-namecert-obligations}. They force a consumer to read compact admission, continuous scalar-map admission, function-space probes, finite isometry comparison, spectrum observations, Real sealing, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming in one packet.

The spectrum-route row $S$ is therefore not an independent host spectrum. It is a finite observation row attached to $K,L,C,F,I$ and sealed by $R$.

Transport through $H$ and replay through $T$ preserve that same inventory, while $P$ and $N$ keep the packet named as $\BanachStoneUp$. Because the carrier omits extensional function spaces, quotient spectra, selected point maps, choice data, and ambient homeomorphism witnesses, no read through the packet can export any of those objects.
\end{proof}
