\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RHZeroWindowUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-rh-zero-window-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following the Riemann zeta zero tradition and the finite RH-route boundary
of \autoref{ch:visions-rh-route-halting-boundary}, the $\RHZeroWindowUp$
packet records bounded inspection windows for supplied zeta-zero witnesses.
It sits downstream of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-zeta-zeros-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-critical-strip-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-zeta-continuation}, and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-complex-namecert}. The packet names a finite
witness surface only: it does not prove $\mathsf{RH}$, enumerate all zeros,
decide all zero locations, or supply a global route-closure oracle.

\begin{definition}[RHZeroWindow carrier]
\label{def:rh-zero-window-carrier}
An $\RHZeroWindowUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
W=(Z,S,A,X,B,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Z$ is the supplied $\ZetaZerosUp$ zero-witness row for the inspected
  complex history.
\item $S$ is the $\CritStripUp$ row recording the critical-strip boundary
  or obstruction data visible to the window.
\item $A$ is the $\ZetaContUp$ continuation row through which the zeta value
  is read.
\item $X$ is the $\ComplexUp$ point row for the inspected source history and
  zero-value comparison.
\item $B$ is the finite bound row naming the inspected height, depth,
  precision, or source-prefix budget of the window.
\item $T$ is the provenance row tying the zero row, strip row, continuation
  row, complex point, and finite bound to the same RH-route request.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $Z,S,A,X,B,T,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for the zero, strip,
  continuation, complex, bound, and provenance rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RHZeroWindowUp$, $\ZetaZerosUp$,
  $\CritStripUp$, $\ZetaContUp$, $\ComplexUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$,
  and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RHZeroWindowUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted zero-window packets only through
$Z,S,A,X,B,T,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a proof of $\mathsf{RH}$, a
counterexample, a complete zero enumeration, host complex equality, quotient
zero-set equality, or a global zero-location decider.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RHZeroWindow NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:rh-zero-window-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RHZeroWindowUp$ carrier
$$
W=(Z,S,A,X,B,T,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RHZeroWindowUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier
admission for the displayed rows, zeta-zero source exposure through $Z$,
critical-strip exposure through $S$, continuation exposure through $A$,
complex point exposure through $X$, finite-bound exposure through $B$,
route-provenance exposure through $T$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport,
displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:rh-zero-window-carrier}.
The zero-facing rows are exactly $Z,S,A,X,B,T$, while $H,C,P,N$ are the
structural transport, replay, package, and naming rows.

The zeta-zero source is supplied, not searched for. The row $Z$ records a
zero witness already presented by $\ZetaZerosUp$, and the strip row $S$ only
records the finite critical-strip boundary visible to this same window.

The continuation and complex rows are also finite inputs. The row $A$ gives
the $\ZetaContUp$ route used for the inspected zeta value, while $X$ names
the complex-history point and zero-value comparison consumed by the window.

Finally the bound and provenance rows $B,T$ keep the read inside one finite
inspection budget. Since no other carrier coordinate is present, the packet
cannot export an RH proof, a counterexample, all zeros, host equality,
quotient zero-set equality, or a global zero-location decider.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RHZeroWindow zeta source route]
\label{thm:rh-zero-window-zeta-source-route}
Every zero-facing read of an accepted $\RHZeroWindowUp$ packet factors through
the finite route
$$
\ZetaZerosUp,\ \CritStripUp,\ \ZetaContUp,\ \ComplexUp .
$$
The finite bound row $B$ and route-provenance row $T$ must be displayed
before the packet can be consumed by an RH-route boundary argument.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted packet $W=(Z,S,A,X,B,T,H,C,P,N)$. The zero source row
$Z$ is the first public zero-facing datum, and it is read together with the
critical-strip row $S$ rather than with an ambient zero search.

The continuation row $A$ and complex row $X$ identify the value route and
the inspected point. They are finite rows of the same packet, so a consumer
cannot replace them by host analytic continuation or host complex equality.

The bound row $B$ and provenance row $T$ then fix the inspection budget and
the RH-route request. Only after those rows are exposed may the structural
rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the zero window.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RHZeroWindow nonescape]
\label{thm:rh-zero-window-nonescape}
An accepted $\RHZeroWindowUp$ packet records finite inspection windows for
zeta-zero witnesses and exports no proof of $\mathsf{RH}$, no counterexample,
no complete zero enumeration, no host complex equality, no quotient zero-set
equality, and no decision procedure for all zeros.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the packet coordinates. The only zero data are the supplied
$\ZetaZerosUp$ row $Z$, the critical-strip row $S$, the continuation row $A$,
the complex point row $X$, the finite bound row $B$, and the provenance row
$T$.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name that displayed route. They do not add a
new witness, a zero search, an equality quotient, or an RH decision datum.

Thus every accepted consumer remains inside the finite window
$Z,S,A,X,B,T,H,C,P,N$, and the excluded global conclusions are outside the
carrier surface.
\end{proof}
