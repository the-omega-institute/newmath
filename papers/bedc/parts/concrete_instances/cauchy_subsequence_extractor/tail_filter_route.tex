\begin{theorem}[CauchySubsequenceExtractor tail-filter route]
\label{thm:cauchy-subsequence-extractor-tail-filter-route}
Every accepted $\CauchySubsequenceExtractorUp$ route
$$
S=(C,Q,W,D,E,R,L,H,T,P,N)
$$
consumes the tail-filter or cofinal-subsequence surface before exporting the subsequence selector row $E$. In particular, a consumer that reads $E$ must first expose either the Cauchy sequence tail-filter route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-sequence-tail-filter-namecert} or the cofinal-subsequence filter route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cofinal-subsequence-filter-namecert}, together with the displayed source row $C$, regular readback $Q$, finite window $W$, and dyadic ledger $D$. The exported selector is therefore a tail/cofinal-filter read, not an isolated selector coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier rows of \autoref{def:cauchy-subsequence-extractor-carrier}. The extraction row $E$ is not available before the source row $C$, regular readback $Q$, finite window $W$, and dyadic tolerance ledger $D$ have been displayed.

Use the selector-lattice handoff and finite-window route, \autoref{thm:cauchy-subsequence-extractor-selector-lattice-handoff} and \autoref{thm:cauchy-subsequence-extractor-finite-window-route}. They bind the selector schedule to the same finite subsequence route that later feeds $\RealUp$ and $\RealSeparabilityUp$ consumers.

Finally attach the tail-filter siblings. A tail-filter or cofinal-subsequence read supplies the late-window discipline that makes the selector row finite and monotone; the structural rows $H,T,P,N$ then replay, transport, source, and name that same route. Since no carrier coordinate stores a free selector, countable choice, quotient stream equality, or ambient completeness, every exported selector remains downstream of the tail/cofinal filter surface.

The same route is the extractor-side input consumed by cluster-witness packets such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-sequence-cluster-witness-namecert}; the selector row can feed a regular sequence cluster witness only after the displayed tail/cofinal-filter discipline has already been read.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CauchySubsequenceExtractor regular-tail route]
\label{thm:cauchy-subsequence-extractor-regular-tail-route}
Every accepted $\CauchySubsequenceExtractorUp$ route
$$
S=(C,Q,W,D,E,R,L,H,T,P,N)
$$
that exports the selector row $E$ through a regular-tail consumer must first expose the sequence-generated tail-filter surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-sequence-tail-filter-namecert} or the regular-tail selector surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-reg-cauchy-tail-sel-namecert}. The selector row is therefore downstream of the regular tail discipline carried by $Q,W,D,R,L,H,T,P,N$; it is not a free choice schedule detached from the displayed tail and readback rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows named in \autoref{thm:cauchy-subsequence-extractor-tail-filter-route}. The source row $C$, regular readback $Q$, finite window $W$, and dyadic ledger $D$ are visible before $E$ can be read.

Attach the regular-tail siblings next. The $\CauchySequenceTailFilterUp$ packet supplies tail filters generated by displayed sequence windows, while the RegularCauchyTailSelector packet supplies a finite selected-tail witness over the same regular readback route.

The structural rows $R,L,H,T,P,N$ then replay, bind, transport, source, and name that route. Since no carrier coordinate stores an independent selector function, quotient stream equality, selected limit, countable choice schedule, or ambient completeness proof, the exported subsequence selector remains a downstream regular-tail read.
\end{proof}
