\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-interval-nested-locator-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$ packet records a finite nested-interval locator route from dyadic refinement data to stream, regular-rational, and real-seal consumers. It reads the retained-window discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nestedinterval-namecert}, the dyadic arithmetic surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the stream-window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a local L10 locator object for nested dyadic intervals; it is not a host interval-intersection theorem, a selected point, quotient stream equality, countable choice, or ambient real completeness.

\begin{definition}[BishopIntervalNestedLocator carrier]
\label{def:bishop-interval-nested-locator-carrier}
A $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(I,D,J,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the retained $\NestedIntervalUp$ row naming the inspected interval chain;
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ refinement ledger, including dyadic endpoints, dyadic radii, and the finite containment budget used by the locator;
\item $J$ is the nested-refinement row asserting that each inspected interval window is retained inside the previous displayed window;
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ row obtained from the dyadic endpoint windows and retained containment data;
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row determined by the same dyadic windows and not by an independent rational sequence;
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $S$ and $R$ have been projected;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $I,D,J,S,R,E$ and the support rows;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a locator request through the nested interval, dyadic refinement, stream-window, regular readback, and real-seal rows;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$, $\NestedIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares two carriers only through these rows and contains no coordinate for a selected limit point, quotient stream equality, host real equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BishopIntervalNestedLocator NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$ carrier $L=(I,D,J,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed nested interval, dyadic refinement ledger, containment row, stream row, regular rational readback, real seal, transports, replay routes, provenance, and local naming row; dyadic endpoint and radius exactness through $D$; retained-window nesting through $J$; $\StreamNameUp$ readback through $S$; $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback through $R$; $\RealUp$ seal delivery through $E$; componentwise transport through $H$; displayed continuation through $C$; package provenance through $P$; and local naming through $N$. No obligation exports host interval intersection, selected limits, quotient stream equality, host real equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-interval-nested-locator-carrier}. The packet displays exactly the nested interval row, dyadic refinement ledger, containment row, stream row, regular rational row, real seal, and structural rows.

The dyadic and containment obligations are row projections. The row $D$ gives the endpoint and radius budget, and $J$ states the retained-window relation between adjacent displayed intervals. Thus a locator-facing read must inspect the finite dyadic refinement data before any stream or real-facing row is available.

The stream, regular, and seal obligations are then forced by the carrier order. The rows $S$ and $R$ are read from the same dyadic windows, and $E$ is downstream of both. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that order, leaving no coordinate for the excluded host-level principles.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopIntervalNestedLocator dyadic refinement]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-dyadic-refinement}
Every accepted $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$ packet factors each locator precision request through the dyadic refinement route
$$
I,\quad D,\quad J,\quad S,\quad R.
$$
The row $D$ supplies the finite $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint and radius data, $J$ supplies retained nested containment, $S$ supplies the $\StreamNameUp$ windows, and $R$ supplies the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback. The route cannot be replaced by a selected branch, quotient stream equality, host real equality, or countable choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with carrier admission. A precision request reaches the packet only through the displayed rows $I,D,J,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$.

Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-namecert-obligations}. Dyadic endpoint and radius exactness in $D$ precede retained-window nesting in $J$, and both precede stream and regular-rational readback.

Finally use the absence clauses in \autoref{def:bishop-interval-nested-locator-carrier}. Since the carrier has no selected branch, quotient stream equality, host real equality, or countable-choice coordinate, every accepted precision request is exhausted by the displayed dyadic refinement route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopIntervalNestedLocator Real-seal route]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-real-seal-route}
Every $\RealUp$-facing read from an accepted $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$ carrier must pass through the retained $\NestedIntervalUp$ row $I$, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ refinement ledger $D$, the containment row $J$, the $\StreamNameUp$ row $S$, and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $R$ before the seal row $E$ is visible. The seal is therefore a terminal row of the displayed locator packet, not a proof of real completeness or host equality of real numbers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \autoref{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-dyadic-refinement} to identify the only route into a locator precision request. It places $I,D,J,S,R$ before any real-facing consumer can read the packet.

Now project the seal row in \autoref{def:bishop-interval-nested-locator-carrier}. The row $E$ occurs after the stream and regular-rational rows, and the support rows $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, package, and name the same route.

Thus the seal consumer receives a finite terminal row rather than an ambient completeness theorem, host real equality, selected point, or quotient stream equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopIntervalNestedLocator non-escape]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-nonescape}
Every consumer of an accepted $\BishopIntervalNestedLocatorUp$ packet is exhausted by the finite rows $I,D,J,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$. In particular, the locator route can feed $\NestedIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ sibling consumers, but it cannot escape as a selected nested-interval intersection point, a quotient stream representative, a host real equality proof, a countable-choice functional, an open-cover compactness theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the packet inventory from \autoref{def:bishop-interval-nested-locator-carrier}. The only data rows are $I,D,J,S,R,E$, and the only structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

Use the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-namecert-obligations}. They force every accepted read through the displayed dyadic refinement, nested containment, stream-window, regular-rational, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming route.

Apply the Real-seal route of \autoref{thm:bishop-interval-nested-locator-real-seal-route} for completion-facing consumers. Since the seal itself remains downstream of $I,D,J,S,R$ and no excluded coordinate is present in the carrier, the locator cannot export any of the host-level objects listed in the statement.
\end{proof}
