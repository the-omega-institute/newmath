\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegularCauchyFusionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-fusion-namecert}
\origin{human}

Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis compares real names by passing to a common regular tail rather than by choosing a quotient representative. The $\RegularCauchyFusionUp$ packet records that comparison as finite NameCert data: it reads the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the window names of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the fast-subsequence handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fastcauchysubsequence-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[RegularCauchyFusion carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-fusion-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyFusionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\Phi=(L,R,D,S,T,U,V,W,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
whose rows have the following roles:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ and $R$ are the two input regular Cauchy name rows.
\item $D$ is the shared $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used by both inputs.
\item $S$ and $T$ are the two $\StreamNameUp$ window schedules read from the inputs.
\item $U$ is the common finite refinement window where the schedules are compared.
\item $V$ is the $\FastCauchySubsequenceUp$ handoff that reaches the refinement without countable choice.
\item $W$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row consumed by the comparison.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ name seal reached from the displayed regular readback.
\item $H,C,P,N$ are componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyFusionUp}$ naming rows.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyFusion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-fusion-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\RegularCauchyFusionUp$ carrier $\Phi=(L,R,D,S,T,U,V,W,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyFusionUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite obligations for input-name admission, dyadic tolerance reuse, two stream-window reads, common-refinement exposure, fast-subsequence access, regular rational readback, Real-name sealing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. The surface exports no quotient Cauchy class, selected representative, selected limit, countable-choice functional, host real equality, trichotomy, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or standard bridge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows. The input names $L,R$, dyadic ledger $D$, stream windows $S,T$, common refinement $U$, fast-subsequence handoff $V$, readback row $W$, and terminal seal $E$ are all displayed coordinates of $\Phi$.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only those displayed coordinates. They do not add a quotient class, selected representative, host equality, choice schedule, completeness principle, or bridge row.

Thus the NameCert obligation surface is precisely the finite carrier route listed above. Any consumer asking for the refused objects must ask for a row that is absent from $\Phi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyFusion L10 sibling dependency]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-fusion-l10-sibling-dependency}
Every $\RegularCauchyFusionUp$ comparison read factors through the sibling route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\quad \StreamNameUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp,\quad \FastCauchySubsequenceUp .
$$
The dyadic ledger fixes the tolerance, the two stream-name rows expose finite windows, the fast-subsequence handoff supplies the common refinement, the regular rational row reads the refined comparison, and the Real row seals only that displayed readback.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the dyadic and stream rows. The ledger $D$ is the only tolerance coordinate, while $S$ and $T$ are the only window schedules available for the two input names.

Use the fast-subsequence handoff row $V$ to reach the common refinement $U$ without selecting an infinite subsequence. The row is finite carrier data and remains tied to the same dyadic and stream schedules.

The regular rational readback $W$ and terminal seal $E$ then consume that common refinement. Since the packet has no quotient, selected-limit, choice, or completeness row, the comparison remains inside the five sibling dependencies displayed in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RegularCauchyFusion common refinement comparison]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-fusion-common-refinement-comparison}
For any accepted $\RegularCauchyFusionUp$ carrier, both input regular Cauchy names are compared only after the common refinement row $U$ has been displayed. A downstream Real-and-Completion consumer receives the route
$$
L,R,D,S,T,U,V,W,E,H,C,P,N
$$
and may inspect finite agreement over the refinement, but it receives no quotient equality of Cauchy names and no selected representative of either input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier definition. The two input rows $L,R$ are not compared directly; they first expose their dyadic tolerance ledger and stream windows through $D,S,T$.

The refinement row $U$ is reached through the fast-subsequence handoff $V$. This gives a finite common window on which the regular rational row $W$ can read both presentations.

The Real seal $E$ receives only that regular readback. The structural rows preserve the route and do not create quotient equality, a representative selector, a selected limit, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
