\begin{theorem}[Mosco epigraphical recovery dependency route]
\label{thm:mosco-convergence-epigraphical-recovery-dependency-route}
Every $\MoscoConvergenceUp$ lower-bound or recovery consumer must read the $\GammaConvergenceUp$ route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gamma-convergence-namecert}, the epigraphical-limit route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-epigraphical-limit-namecert}, and the recovery-sequence route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-mosco-recovery-sequence-namecert} before any local $\NameCert_{\MoscoConvergenceUp}$ row is public. Thus the weak-liminf side is exposed through the lower-bound and epigraphical rows, while the strong side is exposed through the recovery sequence, and no host equivalence among Mosco, epigraphical, and $\Gamma$ convergence is exported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the Mosco carrier of \autoref{def:mosco-convergence-carrier}. The lower-bound route is visible through $F,W,L$, while the recovery route is visible through $R,C,H$.

The Gamma and epigraphical siblings consume those rows only as displayed variational data. The Mosco recovery-sequence sibling supplies the strong approximation route, so the local naming row is reached after both lower-bound and recovery dependencies have been displayed.

The structural rows $T,P,N$ then transport, source, and name the same packet. Since none of the rows stores a host theorem identifying the convergence notions, the dependency route is exactly the stated finite sibling lattice.
\end{proof}
