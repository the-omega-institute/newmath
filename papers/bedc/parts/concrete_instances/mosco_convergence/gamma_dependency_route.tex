\begin{theorem}[Mosco-to-Gamma variational dependency route]
\label{thm:mosco-to-gamma-variational-dependency-route}
Every $\Gamma$-convergence or epigraphical-limit consumer that uses a $\MoscoConvergenceUp$ packet may read the Mosco data only through the displayed carrier rows
$$
F,W,L,R,C,H,T,P,N .
$$
The weak-liminf request is therefore routed through $F,W,L$, the recovery request through $R,C$, and any completion handoff through $H$ after those two variational rows have been displayed. Consequently the sibling routes of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gamma-convergence-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-epigraphical-limit-namecert} consume a shared finite variational lattice rather than a host equivalence between Mosco, epigraphical, and $\Gamma$ convergence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the accepted Mosco carrier of \autoref{def:mosco-convergence-carrier}. Its weak-liminf side is exactly $F,W,L$, so a downstream lower-bound read cannot bypass the lower-semicontinuity row, weak-topology probes, or displayed comparison ledger.

The recovery side is separate. The row $R$ gives the strong approximation schedule and $C$ gives the convex support on which the schedule is read. These rows are the only recovery coordinates available to the variational consumers.

The completion and structural rows are downstream. The handoff $H$, transport $T$, provenance $P$, and local naming row $N$ preserve the same packet for the Gamma and epigraphical routes, but they add no host theorem identifying the convergence notions.
\end{proof}
