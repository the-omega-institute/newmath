\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-tail-triangle-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges' use of explicit Cauchy tail estimates, the $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$ packet records a finite triangle ledger for composing two tail-diameter controls into a third. It consumes the tail-diameter surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-tail-diameter-namecert}, the regular-tail composition route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-tail-composition-namecert}, the finite observation schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a NameCert for displayed tail-diameter composition; it is not ambient metric completeness, quotient equality of real names, a selected limit, countable choice, or a host triangle theorem outside the finite ledger.

\begin{definition}[CauchyTailTriangle carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-tail-triangle-carrier}
A $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(A,B,C,T_{AB},T_{BC},T_{AC},D,W,R,E,H,K,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A,B,C$ are the three tail-name rows whose finite windows are read through the accepted $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ surfaces.
\item $T_{AB}$ is the $\CauchyTailDiameterUp$ row bounding the tail distance from $A$ to $B$ after the displayed threshold.
\item $T_{BC}$ is the $\CauchyTailDiameterUp$ row bounding the tail distance from $B$ to $C$ after the displayed threshold.
\item $T_{AC}$ is the target tail-diameter row for the composed $A$ to $C$ comparison.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger that stores the two input tolerances, their displayed sum, and the target tolerance budget.
\item $W$ is the common finite tail window on which the three names and all three tail-diameter rows are simultaneously readable.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational bounds extracted from $D$ and $W$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal available only after the dyadic and regular-rational rows have been displayed.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the three names, three tail-diameter rows, tolerance ledger, common window, regular readback, real seal, provenance, and naming row.
\item $K$ stores the displayed $\Cont$ replay routes for projecting either input tail-diameter witness and for feeding them through the $\RegularCauchyTailCompositionUp$ row.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$, $\CauchyTailDiameterUp$, $\RegularCauchyTailCompositionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyTailTriangleUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $A,B,C,T_{AB},T_{BC},T_{AC},D,W,R,E,H,K,P,N$. It exports no ambient metric space, completed limit, quotient real equality, chosen modulus, countable choice, or host triangle inequality.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CauchyTailTriangle NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-tail-triangle-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(A,B,C,T_{AB},T_{BC},T_{AC},D,W,R,E,H,K,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyTailTriangleUp}$ surface consists of carrier admission for the three tail-name rows, projection of the two input $\CauchyTailDiameterUp$ witnesses, display of the composed $T_{AC}$ row, dyadic tolerance accounting through $D$, common-window readability through $W$, regular-rational readback through $R$, terminal real sealing through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, continuation replay through $K$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is the tuple of \autoref{def:cauchy-tail-triangle-carrier}. The rows $A,B,C$ bind the three names, while $T_{AB},T_{BC},T_{AC}$ bind the two input diameter witnesses and the target diameter row.

The tolerance and window obligations are the rows $D,W,R,E$. The dyadic row $D$ stores the two input budgets and their displayed sum, the window $W$ is the common tail domain where all three estimates are visible, and the rows $R,E$ route the regular rational readback into the terminal real seal.

The structural obligations are $H,K,P,N$. Transport preserves each displayed coordinate, $K$ replays the two projections and the tail-composition row, and provenance plus naming repack the same finite packet. Since no excluded host object is a coordinate, these rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CauchyTailTriangle composition]
\label{thm:cauchy-tail-triangle-composition}
Every accepted $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$ packet exposes a finite composition route
$$
T_{AB},T_{BC},D,W,R,E,K \longrightarrow T_{AC}.
$$
The route first projects the two tail-diameter witnesses, then reads the shared dyadic budget and common tail window, and only then feeds the two witnesses through the displayed $\RegularCauchyTailCompositionUp$ row to expose the $A$ to $C$ tail-diameter witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the rows $T_{AB}$ and $T_{BC}$ from the carrier. These are the only two input tail-diameter witnesses available to the packet.

Next read the shared ledger. The rows $D$ and $W$ display the finite tolerance addition and the common window on which both input estimates and the target estimate are readable; $R$ and $E$ keep the readback within regular-rational and real-seal rows.

Finally use $K$. Its continuation replay row is precisely the route from the two projected witnesses through $\RegularCauchyTailCompositionUp$ to the displayed target row $T_{AC}$, so the composition remains inside the finite packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CauchyTailTriangle RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:cauchy-tail-triangle-regseqrat-handoff}
Any $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of a $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$ packet must pass through
$$
D,W,R,E
$$
after the two input tail-diameter rows have been displayed. Thus the triangle ledger hands rational tail bounds to completion-facing consumers through the existing regular-rational and real-name surfaces, not through an untracked ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a consumer request for a rational or real bound. The carrier gives no direct bound coordinate except the dyadic ledger $D$, the common tail window $W$, the regular-rational readback $R$, and the terminal seal $E$.

The two input diameter rows must already be visible, because $D$ records the displayed sum of their budgets and $W$ is the common window on which those estimates are read.

Once $R$ and $E$ are consumed, the downstream route has only the accepted $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ surfaces. No selected limit, quotient real equality, or ambient completion theorem is introduced by the handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CauchyTailTriangle non-escape]
\label{thm:cauchy-tail-triangle-nonescape}
The public information exported by a $\CauchyTailTriangleUp$ carrier is exactly the displayed packet
$$
A,B,C,T_{AB},T_{BC},T_{AC},D,W,R,E,H,K,P,N .
$$
In particular, a consumer cannot extract ambient metric completeness, a chosen limit, quotient equality of real names, countable choice, or a host triangle inequality from the certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier coordinates. The three tail-name rows, the three tail-diameter rows, and the dyadic, window, regular-rational, and real-seal rows are all explicitly listed in \autoref{def:cauchy-tail-triangle-carrier}.

The structural rows do not add new mathematical objects. The transport row $H$ preserves displayed coordinates, the continuation row $K$ replays only the two projections and the regular-tail composition route, and $P,N$ provide provenance and local naming for the same packet.

Therefore every public read factors through the displayed rows. None of the excluded host objects is present as a coordinate or produced by the replay route.
\end{proof}
