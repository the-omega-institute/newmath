\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\HadamardBoundaryUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-hadamard-boundary-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bridson--Haefliger and Ballmann on CAT(0) and Hadamard boundaries, the $\HadamardBoundaryUp$ packet records the finite readback by which geodesic-ray, Busemann, visual-boundary, metric, and Real-seal rows expose a boundary direction without selecting a point at infinity from a host completion. It sits beside the hyperbolic metric surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-metric-namecert}, the Busemann boundary route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-busemann-boundary-namecert}, the visual boundary route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-visual-boundary-namecert}, the geodesic-flow route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hyperbolic-geodesic-flow-namecert}, and the terminal scalar seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a traditional CAT(0)/Hadamard boundary read as displayed NameCert data; it does not import a quotient boundary, selected asymptotic ray, global compactification, countable choice, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Hadamard boundary carrier]
\label{def:hadamard-boundary-carrier}
A $\HadamardBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(X,M,R,G,U,V,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the CAT(0) or Hadamard source surface, read only as finite metric and convexity observations.
\item $M$ is the $\HyperbolicMetricUp$ comparison row used for distance and thinness-style readback when a hyperbolic model supplies the visible metric window.
\item $R$ is the finite geodesic-ray window, recording initial segments and comparison requests without choosing a global representative ray.
\item $G$ is the $\HyperbolicGeodesicFlowUp$ transport row attached to the displayed ray window.
\item $U$ is the $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$ row, recording Busemann-level boundary values over the same source window.
\item $V$ is the $\HyperbolicVisualBoundaryUp$ row, recording the visual boundary face exposed by the ray and Busemann rows.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal for the scalar comparison values carried by $M,U,V$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, metric, ray, flow, Busemann, visual-boundary, Real-seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
X,R \longmapsto M,G \longmapsto U,V \longmapsto E .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\HadamardBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicMetricUp$, $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicVisualBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicGeodesicFlowUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\HadamardBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier stores no quotient ideal boundary, selected asymptotic ray, host CAT(0) compactification, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hadamard boundary NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hadamard-boundary-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\HadamardBoundaryUp$ carrier $B=(X,M,R,G,U,V,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\HadamardBoundaryUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, finite ray-window visibility, metric readback, geodesic-flow transport, Busemann boundary exposure, visual-boundary exposure, Real-seal discipline, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. The certificate refuses quotient boundary equality, selected ray representatives, host compactification, countable choice, and ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hadamard-boundary-carrier}. The displayed rows list the source surface, metric readback, finite geodesic-ray window, geodesic-flow transport, Busemann row, visual-boundary row, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The obligations follow the carrier order. A boundary-facing request first exposes the finite ray window over the source surface, then reads metric and geodesic-flow rows, then reaches Busemann and visual-boundary rows, and only then consumes the Real-valued seal.

Transport and replay do not add a second boundary object. They preserve the same rows componentwise and repeat the same route for downstream consumers.

Since no quotient boundary, selected asymptotic ray, host compactification, countable-choice schedule, or ambient completeness witness is a coordinate of the packet, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hadamard boundary finite ray-window handoff]
\label{thm:hadamard-boundary-finite-ray-window-handoff}
Every CAT(0)/Hadamard boundary read exported by an accepted $\HadamardBoundaryUp$ carrier must pass through the finite geodesic-ray row $R$, the $\HyperbolicMetricUp$ metric comparison row $M$, the $\HyperbolicGeodesicFlowUp$ transport row $G$, the $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$ row $U$, the $\HyperbolicVisualBoundaryUp$ row $V$, and the $\RealUp$ seal $E$ before any boundary consumer may inspect it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the accepted carrier $B=(X,M,R,G,U,V,E,H,C,P,N)$. The source boundary request is attached to $X$ only through the finite ray-window row $R$.

Read the metric and flow rows next. The row $M$ supplies the displayed metric comparison, while $G$ records the geodesic-flow transport attached to the same ray window.

Then consume the boundary rows $U$ and $V$. The Busemann row and visual-boundary row are the only boundary-facing coordinates of the packet, and their scalar comparisons are sealed by $E$.

The replay row $C$ records the route order, and $H,P,N$ transport, source, and name the same rows. Hence every boundary consumer sees a finite ray-window handoff rather than a selected ideal representative or host compactification.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hadamard boundary sibling nonescape]
\label{thm:hadamard-boundary-sibling-nonescape}
The sibling routes to $\HyperbolicMetricUp$, $\HyperbolicBusemannBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicVisualBoundaryUp$, $\HyperbolicGeodesicFlowUp$, and $\RealUp$ are consumed by $\HadamardBoundaryUp$ only through the carrier rows $M,U,V,G,E$ and the shared transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. Thus a Hadamard boundary read exports no global CAT(0) compactification, quotient ideal-boundary equality, selected geodesic ray, countable-choice selector, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the provenance row of \autoref{def:hadamard-boundary-carrier}. It names the sibling packets available to the boundary read, but each sibling appears as a displayed row in the same finite carrier.

Apply \autoref{thm:hadamard-boundary-finite-ray-window-handoff}. It orders metric, flow, Busemann, visual-boundary, and Real-seal reads after the finite ray-window row and before any boundary consumer can inspect the packet.

The nonescape claim is then a carrier inventory. Since $B$ contains no separate global compactification, quotient ideal-boundary equality, selected geodesic ray, choice selector, or completeness witness, none of those endpoints can be exported by a downstream consumer.
\end{proof}
