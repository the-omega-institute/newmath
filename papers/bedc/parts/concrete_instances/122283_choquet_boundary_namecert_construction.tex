\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ChoquetBoundaryUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-choquet-boundary-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop-de Leeuw and Choquet, the $\ChoquetBoundaryUp$ packet records the finite boundary surface by which a compact convex function-system read exposes peak-boundary and representing-measure evidence before any boundary representation is consumed. It sits over the compact carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}, the convex-set rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the continuous-map surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}, the scalar seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the order-vector context of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riesz-space-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert}. The packet names a boundary certificate for displayed function-system data; it is not a host Shilov boundary, a selected representing measure, a quotient state space, or an appeal to ambient compactness beyond the sibling rows.

\begin{definition}[ChoquetBoundary carrier]
\label{def:choquet-boundary-carrier}
A $\ChoquetBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
C=(K,A,P,E,R,H,T,G,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K$ is the compact source row for the inspected state space, read through the $\CompactUp$ certificate.
\item $A$ is the convex function-system row over $K$, read through $\ConvexSetUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, and the $\RealUp$ scalar seal.
\item $P$ is the peak-boundary observation row: it records the finite peak tests and exposed boundary witnesses inspected by the packet.
\item $E$ is the extremal handoff row that keeps exposed-point or extremal-state evidence visible before a boundary representation row is consumed.
\item $R$ is the representing-measure handoff row; it records only the displayed measure or barycentric route presented to the packet.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for compact source rows, function-system rows, peak observations, extremal or representing-measure handoffs, replay rows, provenance, and the local naming row.
\item $T$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay from compact source data to function-system data, then to peak observations, then to extremal or representing-measure handoff rows.
\item $G$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ChoquetBoundaryUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RieszSpaceUp$, $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ChoquetBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a host-selected boundary, Shilov minimality, quotient state equality, countable choice, a selected representing measure, or an ambient compactness principle detached from the displayed sibling certificates.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ChoquetBoundary NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:choquet-boundary-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:choquet-boundary-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\ChoquetBoundaryUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $C=(K,A,P,E,R,H,T,G,N)$, compact source exposure through $K$, convex function-system exposure through $A$, peak-boundary observation exposure through $P$, extremal handoff exposure through $E$, representing-measure handoff exposure through $R$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $T$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $G$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is tuple projection from the displayed packet $C$. The analytic rows expose the compact source, convex function-system data, peak observations, extremal evidence, and representing-measure handoff.

The structural rows are exactly $H,T,G,N$. They transport the same displayed rows, replay the same finite dependency route, record provenance for the named packets, and bind the local certificate.

No additional boundary object is available. Since the excluded principles are not carrier coordinates, the local NameCert cannot export them.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ChoquetBoundary peak route]
\label{thm:choquet-boundary-peak-route}
Every accepted $\ChoquetBoundaryUp$ carrier routes a boundary-facing read through the compact source row $K$, the convex function-system row $A$, and the peak-boundary observation row $P$ before any extremal handoff $E$ or representing-measure handoff $R$ is consumed. The route preserves the $\RealUp$ scalar seal and the $\hsame$ transport rows, and it exports no host-selected boundary, Shilov minimality claim, quotient state equality, countable-choice schedule, selected representing measure, or detached compactness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:choquet-boundary-carrier}. Its only source rows for boundary analysis are the compact row $K$, the function-system row $A$, and the peak-observation row $P$.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:choquet-boundary-namecert-obligations}. They place the extremal and representing-measure handoffs after those rows and keep the replay, transport, provenance, and local name attached to the same packet.

It remains to check non-escape. The carrier has no coordinate for the excluded host boundary, minimality, quotient, choice, selected-measure, or detached compactness principles, so a consumer can only read the displayed peak route.
\end{proof}
