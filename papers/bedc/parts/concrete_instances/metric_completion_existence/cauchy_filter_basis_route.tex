\begin{theorem}[Metric completion existence Cauchy-filter basis route]
\label{thm:metric-completion-existence-cauchy-filter-route}
Every accepted $\MetricCompletionExistenceUp$ completion seal is read only after the Cauchy-filter basis row and the separated metric reflection boundary have been displayed with the same finite completion packet. In particular, a public completion seal must expose $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\SeparatedMetricReflectionUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows before any completion consumer treats the seal as public. The route exports no quotient completion, selected representative, host limit choice, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-existence-carrier}. Its finite route already displays the source metric, Cauchy schedule, dense embedding, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance ledger, Real-facing completion seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Insert the Cauchy-filter basis boundary before the completion seal is consumed. The basis row refines the Cauchy schedule into finite filter requests, while the separated metric reflection row fixes the equality boundary through which the completion consumer may read the result.

The remaining rows are then forced in order: $\MetricCompletionUp$ supplies the completion target, $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$ supplies the dense embedding boundary, and the $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows supply the finite readback and tolerance seal. Since none of those rows contains a quotient object, selected representative, host limit selector, choice schedule, or ambient completeness principle, the public completion seal has exactly the stated dependency route.
\end{proof}
