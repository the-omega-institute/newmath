\begin{theorem}[Metric completion existence Cauchy-filter basis route]
\label{thm:metric-completion-existence-cauchy-filter-route}
Every accepted $\MetricCompletionExistenceUp$ completion seal is read only after the Cauchy-filter basis row and the separated metric reflection boundary have been displayed with the same finite completion packet. In particular, a public completion seal must expose $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$, $\SeparatedMetricReflectionUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows before any completion consumer treats the seal as public. The route exports no quotient completion, selected representative, host limit choice, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-existence-carrier}. Its finite route already displays the source metric, Cauchy schedule, dense embedding, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, DyadicRatCore tolerance ledger, Real-facing completion seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Insert the Cauchy-filter basis boundary before the completion seal is consumed. The basis row refines the Cauchy schedule into finite filter requests, while the separated metric reflection row fixes the equality boundary through which the completion consumer may read the result.

The remaining rows are then forced in order: $\MetricCompletionUp$ supplies the completion target, $\CompletionEmbeddingUp$ supplies the dense embedding boundary, and the $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows supply the finite readback and tolerance seal. Since none of those rows contains a quotient object, selected representative, host limit selector, choice schedule, or ambient completeness principle, the public completion seal has exactly the stated dependency route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Metric completion existence finite basis-read route]
\label{thm:metric-completion-existence-cauchy-filter-basis-route}
Every completion-facing read of an accepted $\MetricCompletionExistenceUp$ carrier first normalizes the Cauchy schedule into a finite $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ basis before exposing the public $\MetricCompletionUp$ seal. Only after that basis row is displayed may the consumer read the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular readback row, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger, and the terminal $\RealUp$ row attached to the same completion packet.

The basis-read route exports no quotient completion, selected representative, host limit choice, countable-choice schedule, excluded ambient completeness row, or separated metric shortcut outside the displayed basis packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:metric-completion-existence-carrier}. Its completion-facing coordinates already include the Cauchy schedule, finite readback rows, completion seal, embedding boundary, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Apply \autoref{thm:metric-completion-existence-cauchy-filter-route}. That route inserts the $\CauchyFilterBasisUp$ boundary before the completion seal is public, so the Cauchy schedule is read as finite basis data rather than as an ambient filter object.

Now expose the consumer rows in order. The $\MetricCompletionUp$ seal can be inspected only after the basis row, and the $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows are the finite observation, rational readback, tolerance, and terminal seal rows of that same packet.

The excluded rows have no coordinate in the carrier. There is no quotient completion row, selected representative row, host limit-choice row, countable-choice schedule row, or ambient completeness row from which the consumer could read additional data.
\end{proof}
