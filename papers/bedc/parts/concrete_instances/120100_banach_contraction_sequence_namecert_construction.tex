\begin{theorem}[Banach contraction sequence NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:banach-contraction-sequence-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\BanachContractionSequenceUp$ carrier $S=(K,I,M,Q,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BanachContractionSequenceUp}$ surface consists exactly of contraction-map visibility, Picard-orbit ledger visibility, residual-modulus exposure, $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff visibility, $\CompleteMetricUp$ endpoint-admission visibility, terminal $\RealUp$ seal visibility, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming for the finite packet of \autoref{def:banach-contraction-sequence-carrier}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The mathematical rows are $K,I,M,Q,R,E$, and the support rows are $H,C,P,N$.

Read $K$ and $I$ first. The contraction row supplies the self-map and contraction bound, while the Picard row supplies the finite orbit ledger and residual-distance comparisons.

Read $M,Q,R,E$ next. The modulus row is extracted from the displayed residuals, the regular rational row is generated from that modulus, the complete-metric row admits the same Cauchy data as endpoint-facing input, and the real seal is downstream of the regular handoff.

The transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows preserve only this displayed route. Since no excluded endpoint or choice object appears in the carrier, the local NameCert cannot export one.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:banach-contraction-sequence-picard-cauchy-handoff}

$$

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

The complete-metric and real-seal rows are then read as boundaries for later consumers. They receive the named regular Cauchy handoff; they do not create an endpoint selector or a host fixed-point equality.

Thus the packet is precisely the finite Picard-orbit Cauchy handoff. Any consumer asking for a fixed point must add its own endpoint and uniqueness obligations after this route.
\end{proof}
