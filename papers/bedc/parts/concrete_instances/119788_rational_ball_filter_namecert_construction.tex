Following Bishop and Bridges on neighborhood filters and constructive real approximation, the $\RationalBallFilterUp$ packet records a finite rational-ball request route for Real and Completion consumers. It sits after the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the metric-completion boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}. \begin{definition}[RationalBallFilter carrier]
\label{def:rational-ball-filter-carrier}
A $\RationalBallFilterUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(Q,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ is the rational center request row, recording the requested center and comparison budget as finite rational data.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ dyadic radius and tolerance ledger attached to the same request.
\item $W$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window row opened by the request.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row that reads the stream window as regular rational approximation data.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row available only after the rational center, dyadic ledger, stream window, and regular readback rows have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the rational request, dyadic ledger, stream window, readback, terminal seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a rational-ball request through $Q,D,W,R,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RationalBallFilterUp$, rational center data, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RationalBallFilterUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $Q,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient real equality, selected representatives, trichotomy, countable-choice schedules, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[RationalBallFilter NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:rational-ball-filter-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RationalBallFilterUp$ carrier $F=(Q,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\RationalBallFilterUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, rational center request exposure, ball-window coverage, dyadic refinement stability, regular rational readback, Real-seal nonescape, ledger exhaustion, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming for the rows $Q,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:rational-ball-filter-carrier}. The only available rows are $Q,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$, so carrier admission exposes the rational request, dyadic ledger, stream window, regular readback, terminal seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming data.

The ball-window coverage clause is read through $Q,W,R$. The rational center request opens only the stored finite StreamName windows, and the RegSeqRat row reads those windows as regular rational approximation data.

Dyadic refinement stability and ledger exhaustion are read through $D$. Refining a radius or tolerance budget changes only the displayed DyadicRatCore ledger row, and every accepted request must spend the ledger before the terminal seal can be inspected.

Finally $E,H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route. The Real-seal row is downstream of $Q,D,W,R$, while transport, replay, provenance, and local naming keep the packet inside the displayed finite data. The excluded quotient, selection, trichotomy, choice, compactness, and ambient-completeness objects are absent from the carrier, so the listed clauses exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RationalBallFilter finite-window handoff]
\label{thm:rational-ball-filter-finite-window-handoff}
Every $\RealUp$ or completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\RationalBallFilterUp$ carrier must read the rational ball request through the finite route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\;\StreamNameUp,\;\RegSeqRatUp
\longmapsto
\RealUp
$$
before terminal Real evidence is consumed. The handoff uses the displayed rows $Q,D,W,R,E,H,C,P,N$ of the same packet and cannot import quotient real equality, selected representatives, trichotomy, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted $\RealUp$ or completion-facing consumer of $F$. The carrier admits a rational center request $Q$ only together with the dyadic radius and tolerance ledger $D$.

The consumer then opens the finite window $W$ and reads it through $R$. These rows are the only route from the rational-ball request to regular rational approximation data.

Only after $Q,D,W,R$ have been displayed can the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$ be consumed. Thus a completion-facing consumer receives rational-neighborhood evidence through finite dyadic, stream, and regular-readback rows before any terminal Real evidence is available.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are confined to $H,C,P,N$. Since the carrier contains no quotient equality, representative selector, trichotomy branch, choice schedule, open-cover compactness row, or ambient-completeness row, the handoff remains a finite rational-ball route.
\end{proof}
