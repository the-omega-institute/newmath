\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-located-interval-order-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$ packet records comparison of located intervals by displayed endpoint windows and finite tolerance budgets, not by a host trichotomy principle for completed real endpoints. It sits beside the dyadic arithmetic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite observation windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the located bisection route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-interval-bisection-namecert}. The packet gives Real and Completion consumers a Bishop-style interval comparison surface without importing classical order trichotomy, open-cover compactness, quotient equality, selected limits, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[LocatedIntervalOrder carrier]
\label{def:located-interval-order-carrier}
A $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
O=(L,R,D,S,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the left located-interval endpoint row, containing the displayed lower and upper endpoint windows for the first interval.
\item $R$ is the right located-interval endpoint row, containing the displayed lower and upper endpoint windows for the second interval.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison-budget row, recording the finite dyadic tolerance at which the endpoint windows are compared.
\item $S$ is the shared $\StreamNameUp$ window schedule through which the two endpoint rows are inspected.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the same endpoint windows, followed by the terminal $\RealUp$ seal used by Real-facing consumers.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $L,R,D,S,Q$ and the structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the comparison route through the same finite rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedIntervalOrderUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an exhaustive real-order decision, open-cover compactness, quotient equality of endpoint names, selected limits, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[LocatedIntervalOrder obligation surface]
\label{thm:located-interval-order-obligation-surface}
For an accepted $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$ carrier $O=(L,R,D,S,Q,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedIntervalOrderUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the two endpoint rows, dyadic comparison-budget coverage, shared finite-window inspection, regular rational readback with terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming. Every interval-order read must consume those rows before any downstream Real or Completion consumer may inspect the comparison.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:located-interval-order-carrier}. The only interval data available to the consumer are the two displayed endpoint rows $L$ and $R$.

The comparison step is forced through $D$ and $S$. The dyadic row supplies the finite tolerance budget, while the shared stream row records exactly which endpoint windows are inspected on both sides.

The readback and sealing step is forced through $Q$. Componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are then carried by $H,C,P,N$, and none of these structural rows adds a second order principle. Thus the local NameCert surface is exhausted by the displayed packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LocatedIntervalOrder bisection compatibility]
\label{thm:located-interval-order-bisection-compatibility}
Let $O=(L,R,D,S,Q,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$ packet. If a $\LocatedIntervalBisectionUp$ consumer refines either displayed interval endpoint row by a retained finite child interval, then the interval-order read remains valid exactly when the refined endpoint windows are replayed through the same $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ budget row $D$, shared $\StreamNameUp$ window row $S$, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $Q$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$. The bisection handoff exports no selected infinite branch and no classical trichotomy of real endpoints.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the accepted order packet and the displayed bisection refinement. The bisection route supplies only a finite retained-child row, so it can replace an endpoint window only by exhibiting the corresponding finite endpoint data.

Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:located-interval-order-obligation-surface}. The order read still has to pass through $D,S,Q$ after the refinement, because those rows are the only comparison budget, observation window, and readback route in the carrier.

Finally transport the refined endpoint row through $H$ and replay the same route through $C$. Provenance and local naming remain $P,N$, so the bisection consumer receives a compatible finite comparison packet, not a hidden branch selector or a trichotomy witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LocatedIntervalOrder ledger exhaustion]
\label{thm:located-interval-order-ledger-exhaustion}
The complete ledger of an accepted $\LocatedIntervalOrderUp$ packet is the finite route
$$
L,R,D,S,Q,H,C,P,N .
$$
Every order-facing, bisection-facing, Real-facing, or Completion-facing consumer of the packet is exhausted by the displayed endpoint rows, dyadic comparison budget, shared stream window, regular rational readback, terminal seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. No consumer may read trichotomy, open-cover compactness, quotient equality, selected limits, or $\RealUp$ completeness from the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the obligation surface to account for carrier admission, finite comparison budget, shared window inspection, readback, seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming. These are exactly the rows listed in the carrier.

Use bisection compatibility for interval-refinement consumers. Any retained child interval must still replay through the same comparison-budget, window, readback, and structural rows.

The carrier contains no further coordinate. Therefore the ledger is exhausted by $L,R,D,S,Q,H,C,P,N$, and the excluded order, compactness, quotient, selected-limit, and completeness principles have no route into an accepted consumer read.
\end{proof}
