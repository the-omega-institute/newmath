\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MarkovDecisionProcessUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-markov-decision-process-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bellman and Puterman on Markov decision processes, the $\MarkovDecisionProcessUp$ packet records a finite policy-transition-reward interface before any optimality theorem or measurable selector is visible. It sits after the transition-row surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-markovchain-namecert}, the normalized probability carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert}, the Bellman recursion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dynamic-programming-namecert}, and the action-ledger surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-policy-namecert}. The packet names displayed state rows, action rows, transition rows, reward rows, policy rows, Bellman-consumer routes, transports, replay, provenance, and local naming as BEDC data; it does not import optimal policy existence, measurable selection, infinite-horizon convergence, or host stochastic-process equality.

\begin{definition}[MarkovDecisionProcess carrier]
\label{def:markov-decision-process-carrier}
A $\MarkovDecisionProcessUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(S,A,K,R,\Pi,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the finite state-observation row read through the same normalized probability source used by $\ProbSpaceUp$ consumers.
\item $A$ is the finite action row whose visible choices are read through the action-ledger discipline of $\PolicyUp$.
\item $K$ is the one-step transition row, exposed as a Markov-transition packet before any Bellman consumer can inspect the next-state law.
\item $R$ is the reward row, a finite value-facing ledger whose scalar endpoints are read as displayed $\RealUp$ names rather than as a host utility function.
\item $\Pi$ is the displayed policy row, recording which action ledger is paired with each visible state row.
\item $V$ is the Bellman-consumer boundary row, storing the finite continuation by which dynamic-programming readers consume $S,A,K,R,\Pi$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transports for the state, action, transition, reward, policy, Bellman-boundary, provenance, and local name rows.
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay by which consumers read the probability source, Markov transition, policy ledger, reward ledger, Bellman boundary, transports, provenance, and local naming row.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MarkovDecisionProcessUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\MarkovChainUp$, $\DynamicProgrammingUp$, $\PolicyUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MarkovDecisionProcessUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these displayed rows and their rowwise transports, continuation routes, package provenance, and local naming provenance.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[MarkovDecisionProcess NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:markov-decision-process-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\MarkovDecisionProcessUp$ carrier $D=(S,A,K,R,\Pi,V,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MarkovDecisionProcessUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed state, action, transition, reward, policy, Bellman-boundary, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows; source compatibility with $\ProbSpaceUp$ and $\MarkovChainUp$; policy-ledger compatibility with $\PolicyUp$; Bellman-consumer compatibility with $\DynamicProgrammingUp$; componentwise $\hsame$ stability through $H$; displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$; and non-escape from the finite packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier. Its rows name the probability-sourced state surface, action ledger, one-step transition packet, reward ledger, policy row, Bellman-facing boundary, transport, replay, package, and local certificate.

Read the dependency rows in consumer order. The normalized probability source is visible before the Markov transition row, the policy ledger is visible before an action-dependent reward row is consumed, and the dynamic-programming boundary is exposed only after $S,A,K,R,\Pi$ have been displayed.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for a selected optimizer, measurable policy selector, infinite-horizon limit, or host stochastic-process equality, the local NameCert obligations remain exactly the listed finite rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[MarkovDecisionProcess Bellman consumer boundary]
\label{thm:markov-decision-process-bellman-consumer-boundary}
Every Bellman or dynamic-programming consumer of an accepted $\MarkovDecisionProcessUp$ packet reads the value-facing boundary through the finite route
$$
S\longmapsto A\longmapsto K\longmapsto R\longmapsto \Pi\longmapsto V.
$$
The route first displays the probability and transition data of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-markovchain-namecert}, then the policy action ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-policy-namecert}, and only then the Bellman-facing continuation consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dynamic-programming-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a Bellman-facing read of the boundary row $V$. The carrier makes $V$ available only through the continuation replay that has already exposed the source state row, action row, transition row, reward row, and policy row.

The source and transition reads are finite: $S$ is attached to the probability carrier and $K$ to the Markov-transition surface. The action read is also finite: $A$ and $\Pi$ are policy-ledger rows, not an external decision rule or measurable selector.

Finally the reward row $R$ and boundary row $V$ are replayed as displayed certificate data. Thus a dynamic-programming consumer receives only the finite Bellman boundary over $S,A,K,R,\Pi,V$, with $H,C,P,N$ supplying transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Nothing in the route exports optimal policy existence, measurable selection, infinite-horizon convergence, or host stochastic-process equality.
\end{proof}
