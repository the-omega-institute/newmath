Following Scott and the domain-theoretic completion tradition, the $\DomainCompletionUp$ packet records the finite frontier object that turns rounded-domain approximation data into a completion-facing Real handoff. It sits after the interval-domain route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervaldomain-namecert}, the Scott-domain basis of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-scott-domain-namecert}, the rounded-ideal completion surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rounded-ideal-completion-namecert}, the finite stream observations of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite domain-completion route; it does not choose ideal representatives, quotient directed systems, selected limits, host order equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Domain-completion carrier]
\label{def:domain-completion-carrier}
A $\DomainCompletionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(I,S,R,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\IntervalDomainUp$ cell row naming the located interval approximants read by the completion frontier.
\item $S$ is the $\ScottDomainUp$ basis row that records the inspected finite way-below comparisons.
\item $R$ is the $\RoundedIdealCompletionUp$ row that packages the rounded directed approximation discipline.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row through which completion-facing consumers observe the approximants.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the displayed windows.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row exposed only after $I,S,R,W,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval cells, Scott-domain basis data, rounded ideals, stream windows, regular readbacks, Real seals, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a completion request through $I,S,R,W,Q,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DomainCompletionUp$, $\IntervalDomainUp$, $\ScottDomainUp$, $\RoundedIdealCompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DomainCompletionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,S,R,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for selected ideal representatives, quotient directed-system equality, host order equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Domain-completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:domain-completion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DomainCompletionUp$ carrier
$$
D=(I,S,R,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DomainCompletionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for interval-domain cells, Scott-domain basis comparisons, rounded-ideal completion data, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, Real sealing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Completion-facing reads must expose the ordered route
$$
I \to S \to R \to W \to Q \to E
$$
before any Real handoff is available, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ may only transport, replay, source, and name that route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:domain-completion-carrier}. The displayed analytic rows are $I,S,R,W,Q,E$, and the structural rows are $H,C,P,N$.

Read the domain side first. The interval-domain row $I$ supplies the located cells, the Scott-domain row $S$ supplies the inspected finite basis comparisons, and the rounded-ideal row $R$ records the directed approximation discipline before any completion-facing stream row is available.

The observation and Real handoff then pass through $W,Q,E$. The finite window row $W$ exposes the observations admitted by the rounded route, the regular rational row $Q$ supplies the readback, and the Real seal row $E$ is downstream of those displayed rows.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same packet boundary. Since no carrier coordinate stores selected ideal representatives, quotient directed systems, host order equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness, the local NameCert obligations are exhausted by the displayed route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Domain-completion Real handoff]
\label{thm:domain-completion-real-handoff}
Every $\RealUp$ or $\CompletionUp$ consumer of an accepted $\DomainCompletionUp$ carrier must read the finite completion frontier
$$
\IntervalDomainUp \to \ScottDomainUp \to \RoundedIdealCompletionUp \to \StreamNameUp \to \RegSeqRatUp \to \RealUp .
$$
The handoff exports located interval approximation, finite Scott-basis comparison, rounded directed approximation, finite observation windows, regular rational readback, Real sealing, $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ naming. It exports no selected ideal representative, quotient directed-system equality, host order equality, countable-choice selector, or ambient completion theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted carrier $D=(I,S,R,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$. Carrier projection gives the consumer access only to those displayed rows.

The completion frontier begins with the domain rows. A consumer reads located interval approximants through $I$, finite Scott-basis comparisons through $S$, and rounded directed approximation through $R$ before reaching any stream or Real-facing data.

The handoff then consumes $W,Q,E$ in order. The finite observation windows in $W$ feed the regular rational readback $Q$, and the terminal Real seal $E$ is exposed only after that readback is displayed.

The structural rows add no new mathematical primitive. Componentwise transport in $H$, replay in $C$, provenance in $P$, and local naming in $N$ carry the same finite route, so the excluded representative, quotient, host-equality, choice, and ambient-completeness data have no row from which a consumer could read them.
\end{proof}
