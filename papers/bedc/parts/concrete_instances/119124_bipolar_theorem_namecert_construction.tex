\begin{definition}[Bipolar theorem carrier]
\label{def:bipolar-theorem-carrier}
A $\BipolarTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(X,V,R,S,F,E,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ row naming the inspected convex set, its displayed membership tests, and the convex-combination ledger available to polar consumers.
\item $V$ is the $\VecSpaceUp$ row over which every vector, scalar, pairing, and finite affine-functional read is performed.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ scalar seal for all inequality levels, support comparisons, and polar-bound readbacks.
\item $S$ is the $\SupportFunctionUp$ row, supplying only the finite support-budget comparisons attached to inspected directions and witnesses.
\item $F$ is the $\FarkasLemmaUp$ row, supplying the finite alternative ledger used when a polar-bound failure is converted into a displayed multiplier obstruction.
\item $E$ is the $\SeparatingHyperplaneUp$ row, exposing the separating functional only as the finite vector, convex, scalar-level, Farkas, and witness rows already present in that sibling packet.
\item $Q$ is the polar-polar handoff row. It lists the inspected point, the displayed polar tests, the finite support-budget comparisons, and the return comparison saying that the point is admitted by the carried convex row exactly through the visible certificate.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the convex, vector, real, support, Farkas, separation, polar-polar, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from convex membership and vector pairing through support budgets, finite alternatives, separating hyperplanes, and the terminal polar-polar return.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BipolarTheoremUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\SupportFunctionUp$, $\FarkasLemmaUp$, $\SeparatingHyperplaneUp$, $\VecSpaceUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BipolarTheoremUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $X,V,R,S,F,E,Q,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an ambient dual-space completion, an unbounded family of seminorms, quotient polar equality, selected separating functionals, host closure of convex sets, or choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bipolar theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bipolar-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BipolarTheoremUp$ carrier $B=(X,V,R,S,F,E,Q,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BipolarTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of convex-set admission through $X$, vector-space admission through $V$, scalar sealing through $R$, finite support-budget exposure through $S$, finite-alternative handoff through $F$, separating-functional exposure through $E$, polar-polar return through $Q$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bipolar-theorem-carrier}. The geometric source is exhausted by $X,V,R$, so every convex, vector, pairing, and scalar-level read is tied to the displayed rows.

Read the convex-duality siblings next. The support row $S$ contributes only the finite budget surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-support-function-namecert}; the finite alternative row $F$ contributes only the multiplier or obstruction boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-farkas-lemma-namecert}; and the separation row $E$ contributes only the displayed affine-functional witness ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-separating-hyperplane-namecert}.

The polar-polar row $Q$ is therefore a handoff over already visible data. Rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that same finite packet. Since no carrier coordinate stores host closure, a completed dual space, quotient polar equality, a selector for separating functionals, or choice, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bipolar theorem polar handoff]
\label{thm:bipolar-theorem-polar-handoff}
Every downstream convex-duality consumer of an accepted $\BipolarTheoremUp$ packet must read the polar-polar return through
$$
\ConvexSetUp \longmapsto \SupportFunctionUp \longmapsto \FarkasLemmaUp \longmapsto \SeparatingHyperplaneUp
$$
over the shared $\VecSpaceUp$ and $\RealUp$ rows. The consumer receives only the finite convex row, displayed vector and scalar seals, support-budget comparisons, finite alternative boundary, separating-functional witness, polar-polar handoff row, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local name. It receives no detached bipolar theorem, host closure operation, quotient dual-space equality, selected support functional, or choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carried convex row $X$ and the shared vector and scalar rows $V,R$. These rows fix the only vector, pairing, and inequality endpoints that may be inspected by a polar consumer.

The support route is then read through $S$. By \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-support-function-namecert}, this exposes finite support-budget comparisons rather than a host supremum or a selected exposed point.

Next read the obstruction and separation rows. The $\FarkasLemmaUp$ row $F$ supplies a finite alternative boundary, and the $\SeparatingHyperplaneUp$ row $E$ packages any separating functional as displayed coefficient, scalar-level, and witness rows. Thus the polar-polar handoff $Q$ can only cite the sibling certificate surface already visible in the packet.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ preserve that route under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. Any consumer that replaces it by a detached closure theorem, completed dual-space equality, selected functional, or choice principle would require a carrier row absent from $B$.
\end{proof}
