It sits beside the dyadic arithmetic core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite observation schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The sibling route is deliberately local. $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ supplies the endpoint arithmetic and width ledger, $\StreamNameUp$ supplies the finite observation window, $\RegSeqRatUp$ supplies the regular rational readback boundary, and $\RealUp$ supplies the terminal seal only after the displayed dyadic row has been inspected.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic interval halving carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-interval-halving-carrier}
A $\DyadicIntervalHalvingUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
H=(I,D,M,L,R,W,Q,E,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the input dyadic interval row, recording left endpoint, right endpoint, and the displayed nonnegative width witness.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ arithmetic row for endpoint comparison, midpoint formation, and dyadic denominator tracking.
\item $M$ is the midpoint row computed from the displayed endpoints through $D$.
\item $L$ is the left-child row with endpoints given by the input left endpoint and $M$.
\item $R$ is the right-child row with endpoints given by $M$ and the input right endpoint.
\item $W$ is the width-halving row, exposing the equality of the two child widths with one displayed dyadic half of the input width.
\item $Q$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row for reading endpoints, midpoint, and child widths.
\item $E$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ rational readback row formed from $Q$ and $D$.
\item $T$ is the $\RealUp$ seal boundary row available only after $I,D,M,L,R,W,Q,E$ have been displayed.
\item $C$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval, arithmetic, midpoint, child, width, window, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicIntervalHalvingUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalHalvingUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports exactly one dyadic halving step. It has no coordinate for an infinite branch, open-cover compactness, quotient real equality, trichotomy, selected midpoint choice beyond the displayed dyadic row, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic interval halving NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-halving-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicIntervalHalvingUp$ carrier
$$
H=(I,D,M,L,R,W,Q,E,T,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalHalvingUp}$ surface consists exactly of input interval exposure, dyadic arithmetic exposure, midpoint exposure, left-child exposure, right-child exposure, width-halving exposure, finite-window scheduling, regular rational readback, terminal Real sealing, componentwise transport, package provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-halving-carrier}. The rows $I$ and $D$ expose the input interval and the dyadic arithmetic ledger before any child interval is read.

The midpoint and child rows are $M,L,R$. They are computed from the displayed dyadic endpoint data, and the width row $W$ records the local halving equality for those two children.

The completion-facing route is downstream from the halving rows. The finite window $Q$ is read before the regular rational row $E$, and the terminal Real seal $T$ is available only after that readback. The rows $C,P,N$ transport, source, and name the same finite route, so the listed rows exhaust the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:dyadic-interval-halving-nested-route}
Every nested interval consumer of an accepted $\DyadicIntervalHalvingUp$ carrier must read the handoff through
$$
I,D,M,L,R,W,Q,E,T,C,P,N .
$$
The packet offers one displayed dyadic bisection step that can be appended to a finite nested route, but it does not choose an infinite branch, assert interval compactness, or identify quotient real names.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the displayed input interval row $I$ and arithmetic row $D$. These rows determine the endpoint comparison and midpoint formation visible to a later nested-route consumer.

The child rows $L$ and $R$ are both present as finite data, with $W$ recording the halved width relation. A consumer may retain either displayed child in its own finite route, but the carrier itself does not provide an infinite selector.

The rows $Q,E,T$ then expose the observation, rational readback, and Real-facing boundary for that same one-step bisection. Since the structural rows $C,P,N$ preserve only this finite packet, the handoff remains a local nested-route input rather than a compactness or quotient-real principle.
