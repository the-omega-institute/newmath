\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\MonotoneClassTheoremUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-monotone-class-theorem-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Dynkin and Halmos, the $\MonotoneClassTheoremUp$ packet records the theorem-level handoff from a displayed algebra or pi-system generator to the smallest monotone class carrying the same finite BEDC event rows. It sits after the monotone-class carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-monotone-class-namecert}, the pi-lambda route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dynkin-pi-lambda-namecert}, and the generated-set boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-borel-set-namecert}. The packet names generator admission, algebra closure, increasing and decreasing sequence windows, generated-class exactness, pi-lambda handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not import a host sigma-algebra completion, a measure-extension theorem, a selected countable family, or quotient subset equality.

\begin{definition}[Monotone class theorem carrier]
\label{def:monotone-class-theorem-carrier}
A $\MonotoneClassTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(G,A,U,D,C,L,H,R,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the generator family read through $\SetLikeUp$ rows and, when the source is topological or real-facing, through the $\BorelSetUp$ generated-set boundary.
\item $A$ records algebra closure for the displayed generator rows: finite union, finite intersection, and relative complement requests are all replayed inside the same named source.
\item $U$ records increasing-sequence closure through finite observed windows and their continuation rows.
\item $D$ records decreasing-sequence closure through finite observed windows and their continuation rows.
\item $C$ is the generated monotone-class exactness row, comparing the class generated by $G$ with the class reached by $A,U,D$.
\item $L$ is the $\DynkinPiLambdaUp$ handoff row for consumers that read the theorem through a pi-lambda proof route.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $G,A,U,D,C,L$ and the structural rows.
\item $R$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay of the finite route
$$
G,A \longmapsto U,D \longmapsto C,L \longmapsto H,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MonotoneClassTheoremUp$, $\MonotoneClassUp$, $\DynkinPiLambdaUp$, $\BorelSetUp$, $\SetLikeUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MonotoneClassTheoremUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $G,A,U,D,C,L,H,R,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an ambient sigma-algebra, a host monotone-class theorem, selected sequence choices, a measure extension, quotient subset equality, or external equality of events.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone class theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:monotone-class-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:monotone-class-theorem-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\MonotoneClassTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of generator admission through $G$, algebra closure through $A$, increasing-window closure through $U$, decreasing-window closure through $D$, generated monotone-class exactness through $C$, pi-lambda handoff through $L$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $R$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. Every accepted $\MeasureUp$, $\BorelSetUp$, or $\SetLikeUp$ consumer that reads a monotone-class-theorem conclusion must expose those rows before it treats the generated class as event-visible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $M=(G,A,U,D,C,L,H,R,P,N)$. The generator row $G$ is the only source of displayed event requests, and the algebra row $A$ is the only finite Boolean closure coordinate.

Read the monotone closure rows next. Increasing requests pass through $U$, and decreasing requests pass through $D$; both rows expose only finite observed windows and named continuation data over the same generator family.

The exactness and handoff rows then determine every downstream consumer. The generated-class row $C$ compares the class reached by $A,U,D$ with the displayed monotone class, while $L$ records the pi-lambda route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dynkin-pi-lambda-namecert} without importing a separate theorem.

Finally $H,R,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for a host sigma-algebra, selected countable family, measure extension, quotient subset equality, or external event equality, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone class theorem pi-lambda handoff]
\label{thm:monotone-class-theorem-pi-lambda-handoff}
Every accepted $\MonotoneClassTheoremUp$ packet exports the handoff route
$$
G,A,U,D,C \longmapsto L \longmapsto H,R,P,N
$$
to $\DynkinPiLambdaUp$. A downstream consumer may use the pi-lambda route only after the generator row, algebra-closure row, increasing-window row, decreasing-window row, generated-class exactness row, transport row, replay row, provenance row, and local name row have all been exposed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with $G$ and $A$. They bind the displayed generator family and its finite algebra closure before any monotone or pi-lambda consumer can inspect a theorem-facing row.

Apply the monotone closure windows $U$ and $D$. These rows record increasing and decreasing sequence requests through finite observations, so the consumer cannot replace the route by a selected host sequence or an ambient sigma-algebra construction.

The exactness row $C$ is the only comparison between the displayed generated class and the monotone closure route. The handoff row $L$ then sends that finite surface to the $\DynkinPiLambdaUp$ packet already named in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dynkin-pi-lambda-namecert}.

The structural rows $H,R,P,N$ preserve the same route. Therefore the pi-lambda consumer receives a finite NameCert handoff, not a measure-extension theorem, quotient subset equality, selected countable family, or host-level event equality.
\end{proof}
